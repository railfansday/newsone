Poland’s Parliament Votes 240-202 to Let Officials Freeze Crypto Before Court Review

📅 Nov 10, 2026  |  Category: Business  |  Read Source →

Poland's Sejm passed a sanctions bill Friday that lets the interior minister freeze bitcoin and bank funds of people on a national list, and a court appeal would not pause the freeze.

Poland’s lower house of parliament voted 240-202 on Friday to let the interior minister freeze crypto assets, bank accounts and other property of people placed on a national sanctions list, before any court reviews the order.

The Sejm, Poland’s lower house, passed the Act on Restrictive Measures at its 68th sitting, with one member abstaining and 17 not voting. The Senate received the bill Monday and has 30 days under Article 121 of the Constitution to adopt, amend or reject it, after which it would go to President Karol Nawrocki.

The law, a government bill that also implements European Union sanctions rules, treats crypto assets as financial means that can be frozen, using the definition in the EU’s Markets in Crypto-Assets regulation. A freeze, as the text defines it, is meant to stop any movement, transfer, use or other change that would let the holder benefit from the assets.

The freeze applies only to people and entities the interior minister adds to a public national list, either on the minister’s own initiative or at the request of agencies that include the financial watchdog, the central bank, the financial intelligence unit and the internal security service. Grounds include supporting a country already under EU sanctions, serious human rights abuses, or initiating or financing information manipulation that poses a serious threat to Poland’s national security.

A listing on that last ground requires a showing of a real and serious threat to Poland’s interests, borders, security or elections. The bill carves out ordinary journalism, political speech and publishing, unless that activity is a knowing campaign of the kind the law describes.

The interior minister’s decision takes effect the day after it is posted in the ministry’s Public Information Bulletin and is immediately enforceable. There is no request to the minister to reconsider. A listed person may file a complaint with an administrative court, but the law says the decision remains in force until a judgment becomes final, and it sets aside the usual rule that would let a court pause a challenged order.

The measures also bar making funds or property available to listed persons and can exclude them from public contracts. In some cases, the economy minister may appoint a temporary administrator to sell frozen assets or, under a separate order, take them over for the State Treasury.

The Poland bill does not target ordinary bitcoin holders in Poland. It reaches crypto assets owned, held or under the control of people and firms on the sanctions list. Banks, exchanges and other parties face administrative fines if they fail to freeze those assets or make them available, and compliance is checked by the financial intelligence unit and the Polish Financial Supervision Authority for the firms they already oversee.

The text does not describe a way for officials to obtain the keys to coins held in self-custody. The practical enforcement path in Poland runs through exchanges, custodians and other obliged institutions that can actually stop a transfer.

The bill still has to clear the Senate and Nawrocki’s desk. The president refused to sign a separate crypto-assets market bill the Sejm passed on May 15, and lawmakers in September failed to override that veto, voting 241-198. He had also vetoed earlier versions of that market bill, arguing the rules went too far.

That earlier fight was about licensing and supervision under the EU’s crypto-assets rulebook, not about a sanctions freeze. Whether the Senate trims the pre-court freeze, and whether Nawrocki will sign a sanctions law that reaches bitcoin, is the question the bill now leaves open.

Mortgage rates ease as buyers gain leverage with concessions, ARMs

📅 Nov 10, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Mortgage rates slipped to 7.35% this week but remain high enough to keep buyers extremely payment-sensitive. Economists say hotter inflation data could delay meaningful rate relief, limiting demand to needs-based movers. In many markets, leverage is shifting toward buyers as inventory builds, prompting sellers and builders to offer larger concessions to fund temporary and permanent rate buydowns and adjustable-rate mortgages.  AI Summary

Mortgage rates eased slightly this week but stayed above 7.3%, keeping affordability tight and forcing loan officers to lean on seller concessions and adjustable-rate mortgages (ARMs) to close deals.

At HousingWire’s Mortgage Rates Center on Tuesday, 30-year fixed-rate mortgages averaged 7.35%, down from 7.38% one week earlier. Rates for 30-year jumbo loans averaged 7.56%, a 6-basis-point decline, while 30-year Federal Housing Administration (FHA) loans were up 6 bps to 7.22%.

“Since early October, rates have improved about 100 basis points, which can equate to an eighth of a point to 0.375, so that’s helping affordability,” said Shannon Hoff, a California-based mortgage broker at Hoff Home Loans.

Inflation remains a key obstacle. Data released Tuesday showed the Consumer Price Index rose 0.4% on a monthly basis in October, hotter than expected, keeping the door open for the Federal Reserve to raise interest rates at its December meeting.

“Because that’s creating uncertainty, I am seeing buyers are still extremely payment-sensitive,” Hoff said. “However, during the COVID-19 pandemic, people were having to pay $100,000 over for the listing price. If your payment is going to change $100 or $200 from where you want the rate to be, in the big picture, that’s not so worrisome.”

First American senior economist Sam Williamson said hotter inflation is likely to prolong the wait for real mortgage rate relief.

“Stronger job growth may give some households the income to move, but elevated borrowing costs raise the hurdle for everyone,” Williamson said in a statement. “Until mortgage rates ease, life moves will keep the housing market moving, while discretionary buyers wait on the sidelines.”

In this context, Optimal Blue‘s October 2026 Market Advantage mortgage data report showed that rate-lock volume fell 19% month over month and 38% year over year. But ARMs represented 17% of total lock volume, the highest share in the report going back to 2019.“ARM share reached another high, FHA gained share and first-time buyers took a larger share across the major agency programs,” Brennan O’Connell, director of data solutions at Optimal Blue, said in a statement. “For lenders, that means the opportunity is increasingly in helping borrowers find the product and payment structure that works for their circumstances.”

As fewer borrowers are able or willing to buy at today’s rates, inventory is starting to build in parts of the country. That’s giving buyers more leverage, while pushing sellers and builders to participate more heavily in financing costs.

That’s the opposite of a few years ago, when rock-bottom rates, intense competition and limited supply forced buyers to bid far above list price and waive contingencies.

“I’m licensed in nine states, I get to see various markets, and a lot of them are leaning toward the buyer,” said Gay Veale, a 30-year U.S. Air Force veteran and owner of Guidon Mortgage Group. “Because of that, there’s often seller concessions involved, and I am seeing people use those seller concessions for as many different ways to do rate buydowns or get their mortgage to be cheaper in the beginning, at least, if possible.”

Veale cited one deal in which a borrower received $58,000 in seller concessions — an unusually high figure — and locked a 6% rate. With a 3-2-1 temporary buydown, a permanent buydown and an adjustable-rate mortgage, the borrower’s effective initial rate fell to 2.75%.

“Around 75% of my clients are getting seller concessions. They’re not all that much, but at least $10,000 is an average for me,” Veale said.

Veale said most buyers using ARMs today are opting for products that provide about five years of lower rates before they reset. That roughly matches borrower expectations that rates could ease somewhat over the medium term.

She added that most of her current clients are “need-to-move” borrowers rather than investors, such as people relocating for a job, dealing with a death in the family, welcoming a new child or trying to get closer to relatives. Investment purchases are far less common than during the pandemic period, she said.

Veale said her business volume this year looks very similar to last year, an outcome she called “very atypical of the market” and not something she expects to see again under normal conditions.

In Northern California, Hoff said weaker demand is allowing some buyers to negotiate steep price cuts and substantial credits.

She pointed to a client scheduled to sign on Wednesday for a new home after securing $50,000 off the purchase price and $10,000 in seller credits. That $60,000 swing would have been unattainable in a hotter market, she said.

“That $60,000 is much more than paying a half of a point or a point higher in rate,” she said. “Not only is it the uncertainty affecting the buyers, it’s also affecting the sellers.”

Down payment dynamics have shifted as well. During the pandemic, borrowers using down payment assistance or putting 3% down struggled to compete with cash offers and large down payments. Today, Hoff said, these buyers are reappearing.

She is seeing “more first-time homebuyers jump in the market with challenged credit, down payment assistance, outside-the-box loans, bank statements. My loans are definitely a lot more challenging now.”

Fidelity Sets Retail Participation Terms for Anthropic’s Planned IPO

📅 Nov 10, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Fidelity Investments on November 10, 2026, published customer participation terms for Anthropic’s planned common-stock IPO, setting out how eligible brokerage customers can request shares. Participation requires at least $25,000 in certain Fidelity assets, but neither a request nor a confirmed order guarantees an allocation.

Fidelity expects the offer period to close at 4 p.m. ET on November 17, 2026, and Anthropic’s registration to be declared effective and the offering priced on the evening of November 18, 2026. The schedule creates two customer checkpoints: an initial request before the offer period closes and a confirmation after pricing.

Anthropic’s IPO would be its first public share sale, moving the company from private ownership to public trading. Founded in 2021, the AI research and product company develops models and tools including Claude, an assistant used for writing, analysis, coding and research.

The asset test is calculated weekly across retail accounts sharing the same name and Social Security number. Institutional assets, including annuities and 401(k) or 403(b) plan assets, do not count, although Fidelity can include other assets at its discretion.

Customers submit indications of interest through Fidelity’s IPO calendar, stating the maximum number of shares they want. Anthropic requests must start at 25 shares, rise in 25-share increments and stay at or below 1,000,000 shares. The system rejects quantities outside those limits.

Customers can increase a request until the offer period closes; afterward, they can only reduce it. Deleting the indication after the close prevents reentry. Once registration is effective and pricing has occurred, confirmation usually takes place after 7 p.m. ET on pricing night and turns the indication into an order to buy at the offer price. Customers can confirm or reduce the quantity, but cannot increase it, and confirmation is necessary to remain eligible.

Fidelity’s allocation process, called tiering, ranks participants by assets and revenue associated with their Social Security number or taxpayer identification number. Revenue includes brokerage commissions, margin interest and mutual-fund revenue from retail accounts. The size of an indication does not affect that rank; it only caps how many shares the customer can receive. Across multiple accounts, smaller requests are considered first, with submission time breaking ties.

A companion FAQ says allocated customers will pay the public offering price, which can be above or below the expected range printed in the preliminary prospectus. Fidelity may receive only a small percentage of the total offering, so even a valid request can end with a partial allocation or no shares.

Fidelity expects allocations on the morning after pricing, usually completed before 9:30 a.m. ET. Customers then receive an alert and can check whether shares were assigned.

Payment is due by settlement, generally one business day after the trade date. If the account holds sufficient cash, the purchase is debited automatically; additional funds can be supplied by check or wire. Customers cannot use a credit card or proceeds from selling the allocated shares to pay for the purchase. Failure to pay can forfeit both the shares and any gains, while the customer remains responsible for losses on the resulting sale.

Once Anthropic shares begin secondary-market trading, holders can sell at any time. Fidelity nevertheless treats a sale within the first 15 calendar days as flipping; sales from day 16 onward avoid that designation. A first flip brings a six-month restriction on future IPO participation through Fidelity, a second brings a one-year restriction, and a third results in a permanent restriction tracked by Social Security number. These penalties affect future offering access while leaving the shareholder free to make the Anthropic sale.

The practical hurdle extends beyond eligibility: confirmation keeps a request in contention, but the eventual purchase still depends on Fidelity’s share supply, the final offering price and payment at settlement.

Anthropic Announces Claude Meetings With a Google Meet Whiteboard Beta

📅 Nov 10, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Anthropic announced Claude Meetings on November 10, 2026, describing a forthcoming feature that will add Claude to calls to take notes and update documents while teams talk.

Separate Help Center documentation describes a live-whiteboard beta for Claude Meetings on Team and Enterprise plans, initially supporting Google Meet. The coming-soon label applies to Meetings as a whole, while the beta language applies specifically to live whiteboards; Microsoft Teams and Zoom are unsupported at launch.

On Team and Enterprise plans, a live whiteboard can be started from the meeting’s page in Claude. It can also be started from the meeting event page from 30 minutes before the meeting until it ends; that route adds Claude to the call. Only people who can edit the meeting can start its whiteboard, although anyone with access to the meeting can open it once started.

Participants can then speak requests, such as asking Claude to put a timeline on the board. Whiteboards accept spoken, typed and sketched input. Claude organizes the material with shapes, arrows and sections, groups related ideas, orders steps and connects dependencies, while everyone on the board can rearrange the content together live.

The board remains an editable artifact rather than a fixed summary. Users can ask Claude to regroup it by theme, turn it into a timeline or suggest missing steps for review. Every board is saved in the Artifacts tab, and Claude can convert the finished work into a document or slide deck.

Whiteboards are on by default for Team plans. Enterprise organizations start with the feature disabled until an administrator enables it under Organization settings > Artifacts. Artifacts are private until shared; on Team and Enterprise plans, links stay inside the organization unless an Owner or Primary Owner enables external sharing. Whiteboards count toward plan usage limits, and boards created during a Meetings call follow Claude Meetings pricing.

Claude Whiteboards works from the contents of the board and does not use context from connected applications. That limits the information Claude can bring into the exercise from other systems, even where those connectors are available. The feature is unavailable to organizations with HIPAA-ready configurations. In organizations with zero data retention for Claude Code, whiteboards remain available in chat and Cowork but are not covered by that retention setting and are unavailable within Claude Code.

The same overview introduces Claude Sheets, Claude Whiteboards and Claude Data, alongside expanded interactive connectors. Anthropic’s broader design is to preserve context as work changes format: an idea can move from a whiteboard to a spreadsheet or dashboard without the user restating the details. Meetings is intended to bring that approach into the call, where discussion can become material the team continues using elsewhere.

The documented beta centers on a shared visual workspace for Team and Enterprise Google Meet calls. Its value lies in producing an editable artifact during the discussion, while plan access, platform support, administrative defaults and data-retention boundaries determine where that workflow can be used.

Mosaic Raises $47M Seed to Develop Spatial Intelligence for Surgery

📅 Nov 10, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Mosaic Intelligence Labs announced on November 10, 2026 that it had emerged from stealth with $47 million in seed funding to develop sensing-enabled spatial intelligence and robotic systems for surgery, initially focusing on procedures performed through the vascular system.

The San Francisco company named 8VC, Fundomo, Lingotto Innovation and Obvious Ventures among its backers. Chief executive Ileana Pirozzi and chief technology officer Alison “Ali” Kight Mackanic founded Mosaic after meeting during their doctoral studies at Stanford. Their work alongside clinicians exposed how difficult, less-invasive procedures often remain tied to knowledge held by individual operators.

“We kept returning to one question: what would it take to scale the knowledge of the very best surgeons?” Pirozzi said. “We believe that starts with making our machines aware, and turning surgical repetition into experience we can learn from.”

Mosaic’s initial target is endovascular care. In these procedures, physicians thread flexible wires and catheters through blood vessels to reach treatment sites. Mosaic describes blood vessels as routes to virtually every organ, yet operators have no direct line of sight inside them. They use live X-ray imaging, preoperative scans and the feel of the instruments to work out where they are and what lies ahead.

The company identifies radiation exposure and outcomes that vary with operator experience as limitations of that workflow. Interpreting the available signals can take years of practice, and much of the resulting knowledge is difficult to record or hand down. The result is a mismatch: surgical access has become less invasive, while navigation still depends heavily on the perception and judgment of the person holding the instrument.

For context, Mosaic’s website compares open surgery’s 20–30 cm incision and keyhole surgery’s 1–2 cm ports with endovascular surgery’s 2 mm needle puncture, and lists stopping strokes, clearing blocked coronary arteries, repairing aneurysms and restoring blood flow to limbs among current applications. That range helps explain why Mosaic is treating vascular navigation as a reusable capability across multiple procedures.

The technical design begins by instrumenting the point where surgical tools meet anatomy. Mosaic intends to combine those sensor readings with computation that reconstructs the vascular environment from within, maintaining a three-dimensional picture of anatomy and instrument position as it changes. A robotic interface would support precise instrument control.

The learning architecture would record the environment, instrument movement and physician actions as synchronized streams. Physical models would then help the system relate what it senses to what happens when it moves. The release connects this approach to the robotics concept of a world model: a representation of an environment that helps a machine predict how its actions will change that environment.

All of these capabilities remain development goals; the announcement supplies no patient-outcome findings or clinical-performance results. The nearer objective is to assist physicians’ perception and control, while the recorded procedures are intended to become a source of cumulative learning for machines over time.

Mosaic is recruiting engineers, physicians and creatives in San Francisco. Open roles cover hardware engineering, sensing, perception, machine learning and clinical engineering, matching a technical effort that spans physical instruments, software, robotics and clinical practice.

Mosaic’s longer-term goal is to widen the treatments delivered through these vascular routes. The company says catheters can deliver drugs, energy, biologics and other therapies directly to a target site, and that more reliable navigation could expand both the range of treatments delivered through blood vessels and the settings in which clinicians can offer them.

Mosaic’s near-term product challenge and longer-term data ambition are linked: dependable navigation must work in individual procedures before those procedures can provide a useful foundation for cumulative machine learning.

While it may seem like power is the most important part of successful axe-swinging, control and technique have greater influence on success. Limbing can be more dangerous than felling because you are dealing with smaller pieces and a great deal of sharp force near your body. Often, limbing happens while you are standing on a fallen tree. The 30-year Treasury bond yield, more sensitive to geopolitical developments, rose more than 11 basis points, to 5.37%. The 10-year yield last traded above the 5% level earlier this month. In October, 10-year and 30-year yields hit 24-year highs.

One basis point equals 0.01% or 1/100th of 1%, and yields and prices move inversely to one another.

The consumer price index for October grew 0.4%, more than the 0.3% expected from economists polled by Dow Jones and down from the 0.6% growth in September.

Core CPI, which excludes volatile food and energy prices, gained 0.3%. Economists had expected a 0.2% gain on the month. The reading was in line with September's 0.3% rise.

The report came after Fed Governor Christopher Waller said he would support "more robust action" if inflation readings are higher than expected.

"In that case, I believe a 50 basis point hike in the policy rate should be considered at the December meeting," Waller said in London at a central bank conference hosted by the Bank of England. "Hiking only 25 basis points would put our credibility at risk, a risk I am not eager to take."
