Alabama Lands at No. 5 in First CFP Rankings Despite Two Losses

📅 Nov 04, 2026  |  Category: Sports  |  Read Source →

The Alabama Crimson Tide have officially earned a spot inside the top five of the first College Football Playoff rankings of the 2026 season. Despite already suffering two losses, the selection committee placed Alabama at No. 5, giving Kalen DeBoer's team an early opportunity to compete for a national championship.

At 6-2 overall and 4-2 in SEC play, Alabama has experienced an interesting season. The Crimson Tide started strong but suffered consecutive losses against Georgia and Tennessee, raising questions about their consistency and championship expectations.

However, the committee clearly believes Alabama has one of the strongest resumes in the country.

One of the biggest reasons Alabama landed at No. 5 is its impressive collection of victories. The Crimson Tide have defeated Kentucky, Florida State, South Carolina, Mississippi State and Texas A&M.

Their 45-17 victory over Kentucky was particularly important. With Kentucky sitting at No. 6 in the first CFP rankings, Alabama holds a valuable head-to-head victory over another highly ranked SEC program.

DeBoer has also helped Alabama remain competitive despite the difficult stretch in October. The Crimson Tide responded to their losses against Georgia and Tennessee with a 31-20 victory over Texas A&M.

That bounce back performance helped Alabama regain some momentum heading into November.

Alabama's next challenge will be another important SEC matchup against LSU on November 7.

The Tigers currently sit at No. 14 in the first CFP rankings, making this another opportunity for Alabama to add a quality victory to its playoff resume.

With games against Vanderbilt and Auburn still remaining, the Crimson Tide have opportunities to strengthen their position.

Can Alabama Make Another Championship Run?

Landing at No. 5 is a significant accomplishment for Alabama, especially considering its two losses.

However, the Crimson Tide cannot afford to become comfortable. The expanded 12 team playoff gives Alabama some room for error, but another loss could make its path much more difficult.

The committee has shown that it still believes in Alabama's potential. Now, DeBoer and his players must prove that ranking is deserved.

The road to the playoff is far from over, but Alabama has officially put itself in the conversation.

Michigan CB Jyaire Hill calls Michigan State rivalry a 'violent game'

📅 Nov 04, 2026  |  Category: Sports  |  Read Source →

ANN ARBOR, Mich. -- The in-state rivalry pitting Michigan football vs. Michigan State is not for the faint of heart. It's more physical than most, and more chippy. In fact, there almost always seems to be some in-game (or postgame) shenanigans between the Wolverines and Spartans.

But it's something that Michigan cornerback Jyaire Hill can't wait to be a part of.

Hill has played in three iterations of the battle for the Paul Bunyan Trophy, having not been a part of the travel party in 2023, but having played in the past three. He says that this game stands apart, because there's a different intensity to it than some others.

"Man, violent game, all four quarters," Hill said. "I'll be ready to go. See it right there, violent. Brotherhood, ready to play for your brothers, all four quarters."

What makes it violent in Hill's eyes? He says that there's an honest hatred between the two teams, and that because of that, there can be some -- extracurriculars.

"Man, I feel like everybody's just mad at each other," Hill said. "You got some players might do something after the play, some try to get in your head. You've got to be real poised into the game, real dialed in. You can't get out your zone."

Michigan is preparing for that aspect, and is not looking to retaliate, but also wants to ensure that they have their brothers' backs.

But there have been questions about head coach Kyle Whittingham, given this is his introduction to the rivalry. It had seemed initially as if he was downplaying it -- much like he appeared to do with Ohio State. But Hill -- who's played under Jim Harbaugh and Sherrone Moore before Hill -- says that he has liked Whittingham's approach thus far.

"He knows what he's doing," Hill said. "Like I said, he's real. It's like he's been here. So he did some history. He did some work on his game. So he knows what time it is."

For now, Hill is just excited to get out there. He's looking forward to the enhanced physicality, noting that it does happen for receivers and cornerbacks as well, and he is ready to take his game up a notch.

"Oh, yeah. I'm ready for that," Hill said. "Yeah, yeah, yeah. Ready to twist some helmets. Yeah, I'm ready."

Kickoff is at 7:40 p.m. EST on Saturday, with the game set to be streamed on Peacock.

NBA rescinds Luka Dončić's technical foul during win over Heat, bringing him back to 1 on the season

📅 Nov 04, 2026  |  Category: Sports  |  Read Source →

Luka Dončić's latest technical foul has been called off.

The league rescinded a technical foul that the Los Angeles Lakers star had picked up during their 138-117 win over the Miami Heat. The foul, which Dončić earned during the second quarter of Monday night's contest at Crypto.com Arena, was reviewed by the league on Tuesday.

That has brought Dončić to just a single technical foul on the season so far.

The incident on Monday night happened after Dončić reacted to a call an official made, which led to a very candid conversation between Dončić and the officials on the broadcast.

"I clapped. That's a technical? Come on now," Dončić was heard telling the official. "If it was anybody else, you know it's not a technical. Come on now."

Klay Thompson hit the free throw after the technical, which brought the Heat within five points at the time.

Dončić was asked about the technical after the game, but he did not want to get into it.

Regardless of his run-in with the officials, Dončić still finished with 42 points, 13 assists and seven rebounds in the win on Monday night. He's averaged 43.4 points per game over the last five outings, too, which included a 52-point night against the Utah Jazz late last month.

Dončić has had issues with technical fouls in the past. He was suspended for a game last season after reaching the 16-technical foul threshhold, which triggers an automatic one-game suspension. He had three straight seasons with 15 technical fouls each, too, but only had 13 during the 2024-25 campaign to braek the streak.

By comparison, two players in the league already have three technical fouls already this season. Golden State Warriors star Draymond Green is one of them after he was ejected on Monday night for an incident during their win over the Phoenix Suns. Houston's Jabarai Smith Jr. also has three.

It's still very early in the regular season, and Dončić has a long way to go before having to worry about such a penalty again. But having Monday's rescinded undoubtedly gives him extra room to work with down the stretch.

How Brian Schottenheimer is failing the Cowboys

📅 Nov 04, 2026  |  Category: Sports  |  Read Source →

Watching the Dallas Cowboys in 2026 has become a tiring experience. We are not even halfway through the season, and things are already looking bleak. Losing is never fun, but watching them underachieve has become a weekly ordeal that has turned into pure exhaustion.

Every Sunday, it's a new level of disappointment. There's talent on this team, but the leadership and tactical execution have brought about regular failures that are tough to sit through. The most recent collapse against the Arizona Cardinals serves as more evidence that head coach and play-caller Brian Schottenheimer is fundamentally not the guy to help this franchise return to glory.

The issues start with Schottenheimer's bizarre tendencies. He routinely fails to make the basic decisions that would maximize his team's probability of winning. One example is his weird habit of running quarterback sneaks down at his own goal line. Instead of trusting his playmakers or scheming an effective play, he plays scared and chooses to waste a down, putting the offense in a tough spot. Every time he does this, the Cowboys have punted three plays later.

Another example is his treatment of Brandon Aubrey. We all know that Aubrey is one of the league's most lethal kicking weapons, but Schottenheimer finds ways to turn that into a disadvantage for the Cowboys.

Rather than pressing and trying to pick up extra yards to give him a more reasonable field goal attempt, the head coach contentedly runs the ball and settles for a short gain. His confidence in Aubrey treats those situations like an automatic three points instead of working for a shorter attempt. He unnecessarily creates high-stress kicking situations that have resulted in blocks and misses.

The team also cannot stop repeating the same mistakes. They look uncoached at times, committing the same self-inflicted errors week after week. Are we sick of neutral zone infractions yet? Every week. Early and often. It's just the same penalties over and over. It's become an epidemic. Whatever the coaches are doing to discourage this practice is not working. They keep jumping early.

As a play-caller, Schottenheimer is equally frustrating. The team doesn't have much of a rushing attack, as the offense has struggled to deploy the physical ground game they so confidently promote during the summer. It's been ineffective, and there have been moments when he has completely abandoned it, essentially acknowledging defeat.

That admission might be a little easier to accept if the offense possessed a high-powered aerial attack that many believe should accompany having one of the best wide receiving duos in the league. But they don't.

For some reason, the team's most explosive receiving weapons are underutilized. Schottenheimer fails to create mismatches, and the offense becomes very stagnant at times. No pre-snap motion or creative blocking schemes. The Cowboys' offense has turned into a predictable entity that defenses can absolutely shut down at certain points in the game, and those moments are extremely harmful.

Under Schottenheimer, the Cowboys constantly invent new ways to lose football games. Against the Cardinals, it was the inability to run out the clock. Earlier in the year, against the Ravens, it was a goal-line debauchery that couldn't win the game. Both instances put the pressure back on a porous defense to come through, and sadly, they did not.

The Cowboys don't just lose. They actively engineer their own downfall through a blend of situational ineptitude and executive panic.

But if you listen to Schottenheimer's postgame press conferences, he seems completely detached from reality. His media appearances have turned into comedy shows, highlighted by delusion and a lack of awareness. He stands at the table and claims to be a problem solver, yet no problems ever get solved. He throws out tired clichés and empty promises about how things need to get cleaned up, but each week the same chaos ensues.

The Cowboys are a 3-5 football team, and sadly, it's fitting for who they are. Each week, they are favored against these bad teams because, on paper, they should be beating them. They have more talent. But none of that matters when the coaching puts them at such a disadvantage.

Star players become average, and average players become bad. So far, the evidence strongly suggests that Schottenheimer is not a head coach capable of leading an NFL team to victory, and with each new week, this is becoming more and more obvious.

Kawhi Leonard injury update: Raptors star finishes knee treatment but still out

📅 Nov 04, 2026  |  Category: Sports  |  Read Source →

Kawhi Leonard is headed back to Toronto. Getting back on the court is the next step.

The Raptors announced Tuesday, Nov. 3 that Leonard, 35, has completed treatment for swelling in his left knee and will begin the return-to-play process in Toronto. The team will update his progress in one week.

Leonard has missed five consecutive games. He felt something in the knee during pregame warmups Oct. 25 and was scratched from a loss to the Minnesota Timberwolves. An MRI showed no structural damage.

Coach Darko Rajakovic said Oct. 29 that Leonard was getting his treatment outside of Toronto. At the time, NBA insider Chris Haynes reported Leonard would miss at least three to four weeks. The Raptors didn't say Tuesday whether that window has changed.

Leonard already has injury history with the right knee. Leonard tore the ACL in his right knee in the 2021 NBA playoffs and the meniscus in the 2023 playoffs – both with the Los Angeles Clippers.

Toronto brought the two-time NBA Finals MVP back this offseason, sending Brandon Ingram, Gradey Dick and draft picks to Los Angeles. The deal was agreed to in late June, then sat until September while the NBA investigated the Clippers and Leonard for a salary cap circumvention scheme. The league fined Leonard $700,000. He signed a two-year, $115 million extension with Toronto soon after.

Leonard played in the Raptors' first two games, both wins. He averaged 19.5 points, 7.5 rebounds and 3.5 steals but shot just 32.4% from the field. Last season, he played 65 games for the Clippers and averaged 27.9 points.

The Raptors (4-3) are 2-3 without him. The next update is due Nov. 10.

Defiant Bruno Fernandes claims he wasn't dropped for publicly criticising Michael Carrick - and hits out at 'a lot of lies' in sweary interview after Man United's stunning 3-2 comeback against Roma

By WILL GRIFFEE and CHRIS WHEELER, NORTHERN SPORTS WRITER

Published: 17:42 EDT, 3 November 2026 | Updated: 20:12 EDT, 3 November 2026

Bruno Fernandes launched into an adrenaline-fuelled, X-rated rant after Man United's stunning comeback win over Roma on Tuesday night.

Having been at the centre of the storm surrounding the club's abysmal form and his undermining of manager Michael Carrick, Fernandes offered contrition for publicly criticising tactics but denied it was the reason he was dropped last week.

The club captain was benched by Carrick for United's 3-0 defeat by Bournemouth before being reintegrated against Chelsea and Roma, winning Man of the Match and scoring against the Italians.

Amazon Prime presenter Gabby Logan was forced to apologise for Fernandes' bad language after he let the emotion pour out of him in the post-match interview.

The stirring 3-2 comeback at Old Trafford on Tuesday night was the jumping-off point for Fernandes to unburden himself, saying: 'We don't need to save anyone's job.

'The club needed that win. The players needed that win. It is something we needed as a group of players and staff.

'We have been working so hard to get out of this situation together. It has been tough. There has been a lot of speculation in the media.

'The manager makes the decisions. I have to accept it. I have to go forward with that. It doesn't change the belief I have in the manager, the staff and every player at this club.

'And it doesn't change my aim to win every f***ing game that I have ahead of me.'

Carrick was given a stay of execution by club bosses at United after losing against Bournemouth when he seemed destined to be sacked.

Defeat at Chelsea appeared to push him closer to the exit door but Fernandes and Co fought back from 2-0 down on Tuesday night to give their boss and a beleaguered fanbase a much-needed lift.

Fernandes, along with Lisandro Martinez, had both publicly criticised different aspects of the club, with the Argentine targeting Jason Wilcox and Fernandes making disparaging comments about Carrick's tactics.

He also told Fernandes to 'read the room' and accused him of being an 'excuse merchant' on his podcast.

'He's gone and basically thrown his manager under the bus the week after [Lisandro] Martinez did the same,' he said.

‘It’s one win so we’re not getting too carried away, but it was an important one for us.’

When asked about his future at the club, he added: 'I don't have a crystal ball to know what happens next.

'We move on to the weekend, we've got a really important game here against Aston Villa, which we need to prepare for right.'
