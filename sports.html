Dirk Kuyt, Liverpool’s big-game hero and embodiment of the Benitez era

📅 Nov 04, 2026  |  Category: Sports  |  Read Source →

English fans can be excused for never been quite sure what to expect when a striker swaps the Eredivisie for the Premier League.

Will they be the next Robin van Persie or Ruud van Nistelrooy? Or are they being sold false goods? Think Ricky van Wolfswinkel. A star in Holland with Utrecht and then in Portugal with Sporting but the scorer of precisely one goal in England during a full season with Norwich.

Dirk Kuyt had plundered 71 goals in 101 games for Feyenoord before Liverpool pressed ‘buy’ in the summer of 2006. In theory, the Reds were getting a bargain at just £10million. A proven goalscorer and, those who had seen him in action, the very antithesis of a goal hanger.

Yes, Kuyt ran around a lot. He worked for the team. He put in a shift. Ironically, that was part of the reason why his career at Liverpool will be remembered as that of honest pro rather than superstar.

Kuyt, with those solid legs and neat mop of blonde hair, made his debut in a 2-1 home win over West Ham on August 26, 2006, coming on as a substitute to replace Peter Crouch.

That 2006-07 Liverpool team could be classed as being ‘between strikers’.

Crouch brought something different to the mix and Craig Bellamy was a classy pest with a goal or two in him, while Robbie Fowler had come back for a second stint but was more nostalgia act than genuine first-teamer so there was a goalscoring void to be filled. Was Kuyt the man?

This, of course, was the Rafa Benitez era at Anfield. His tactical wizardry had guided a genuinely mixed bag of a Liverpool team to Champions League glory in 2005, but now there was the Holy Grail of trying to win the Premier League.

Anyone and everyone can chip in on cup runs, but a title assault needs a genuine and regular goalscorer. A 20-goal haul from Kuyt and solid contributions from elsewhere could have been the winning formula.

The Dutchman took a little while to settle but scored twice in four days at the end of September, helping Liverpool to home wins against Newcastle and Spurs.

Reds fans were hopeful but cautious too. On the one hand, there was a feeling that could this be the start of something. On the other, there was a nagging feeling that he was good but not great. A seven out of 10 player.

Fans are greedy. They want superstars. They want No.9s and No.10s. Kuyt looked like what he was. A £10million signing from the Dutch league.

He scored 14 goals in that first campaign, a very respectable haul but not a game-changing one.

In the next, he managed 11 while a late flurry in Liverpool’s near-miss 2008-09 season yielded 15.

It was back to 11 in the following campaign but back up to 15 in 2009-10, a tally boosted by one very special afternoon against Manchester United.

One thing was very clear. Rafa loved him. Or rather, given that the Spaniard doesn’t really do love, Rafa saw how valuable he was to the cause. From being an out-and-out striker, Benitez turned Kuyt into the ultimate workhorse, ploughing a furrow up and down one side of a front three.

And Liverpool fans adjusted with him. The fanciful expectations of Kuyt being a world-beater had been downgraded to a different type of admiration. He wasn’t Pele, but good old Dirk would play his heart out for the club and die for the cause. And that goes a long, long way on Merseyside.

While other Reds flickered and flirted with greatness, Kuyt offered a rubber-stamped guarantee. Whatever the occasion, whatever the opponent, whatever the location, he would put in a blue-collar shift week in, week out.

That can sound patronising, but Kuyt had another trick up his sleeve which moved him into a category beyond honest trier. The man from the little coastal town of Katwijk aan See (Dutch for Clacton-on-Sea… well, not really) could score big goals in big games.

When he finally left the Big Anfield House in 2012, his ‘best bits’ were quite something:

– Scorer in the 2006-07 Champions League final against AC Milan

– Scorer of the winning penalty in the 2007 semi-final second leg against Chelsea.

– First player in 21 years to score a hat-trick against Manchester United

– Injury-time penalty to secure dramatic derby win at Everton

– Extra-time goal against Cardiff in the 2012 League Cup final

– Shootout goal against Cardiff to secure League Cup final glory

There were other important strikes against Arsenal, while he netted seven times in the 2007-08 Champions League campaign. Even the hat-trick against Manchester United had added cult value as they were all one-yard tap-ins. Those goalscoring instincts had never left him.

Not many can claim to have scored a hat-trick against Manchester United.

In many ways, Dirk Kuyt was the embodiment of the Benitez era. Tactically astute, hard-working and capable of taking it up a level on big occasions.

You don’t build a football team just by throwing 11 superstars together. Even Brazil 70 had a few in their ranks who provided solidity and balance. That was Dirk Kuyt at Liverpool. Popular, reliable and hugely valuable.

Imagine Kuyt was at his peak now. Would he make this current high-flying Liverpool team? Not as a regular starter, but Jurgen Klopp would have him as a squad player all day long.

Slight injury concern for one of the litany of superstar forwards in the squad? Two games in four days? Tough away assignment where you need to dig in but try to nick a big goal?

Former Tottenham Hotspur striker Jermain Defoe is poised to return to the dugout as assistant manager to Karl Robinson at League One strugglers Bromley. The ex-England marksman has agreed to join the south London club's coaching staff four months after his abrupt departure from National League side Woking.

According to talkSPORT, Defoe will serve as second-in-command once Robinson finalises his appointment to succeed Andy Woodman, who was dismissed on October 22. Ranked 10th on the Premier League's all-time scoring chart with 163 goals across 496 games for six clubs, the ex-forward is Tottenham's seventh-highest scorer. The pair have been tasked with rescuing the Ravens from the foot of the third tier.

Defoe has been out of work since leaving Woking in July after less than four months in charge following a falling-out with their director of football, failing to attend pre-season training before calling to confirm his exit.

Meanwhile, former Charlton Athletic, MK Dons and Oxford United boss Robinson was sacked by Salford City in June after losing the League Two play-off final. He previously tried to sign Defoe for Oxford after the striker left Rangers in 2022.

Bromley also interviewed ex-Cesena boss Ashley Cole and considered Matt Bloomfield after sacking 55-year-old Woodman, who ended the club's 132-year wait for Football League status in 2024 before guiding them to League One.

Speaking on the Inside the 72 podcast, Woodman said: "The easiest way I can sum this up is that when I got the job at Bromley, my first game was Wealdstone and my last game in the league was Leicester. I was pretty disappointed and pretty upset. It felt like I'd been misled, in numerous ways.

"To give five-and-a-half years of your life to a club and a fanbase that I really loved and bought into, where I gave everything I had, to then be told in no more than three or four minutes 'thanks'. That was it really.

"There's no other way of dressing it up. That's the truth. I'm disappointed, but that's football as we know it guys. Unfortunately it never, ever surprises us."

Robinson and Defoe will inherit a struggling squad sitting three points adrift of safety with just two wins from 10 league outings. A new era is underway at Sheffield Wednesday following the appointment of Dave Challinor as the club's new manager.

After a mixed start to life back in League One, Wednesday parted company with Henrik Pedersen last month, and it has not taken long for the South Yorkshire outfit to find his replacement.

It has been a frustrating few weeks for Wednesday on the pitch, with back-to-back away defeats at Notts County and Doncaster Rovers spelling the end of Pedersen's tenure before interim manager Matt Hamshaw oversaw consecutive home draws against Cambridge United and Blackpool.

However, while it was a disappointing afternoon for the Owls as they were held to a 1-1 draw by struggling Blackpool on Sunday, the headlines were instead dominated by the appointment of Challinor, who was confirmed as the club's new manager on the morning of the game.

Given his links to Wednesday's sporting director Simon Wilson, who he worked with previously at Stockport County, it had long been rumoured that Challinor would be taking over at Hillsborough at some stage following his departure from Edgeley Park in June.

Challinor spent almost five years in charge of the Hatters, during which time he led them from the National League to League One, and he missed out on promotion to the Championship in what proved to be his final game as his side were beaten 4-1 by Bolton Wanderers in the play-off final at Wembley.

The 51-year-old, who has also had spells with the likes of AFC Fylde and Hartlepool United, holds the remarkable record of finishing in the play-offs or higher in each of his 15 full seasons as a manager, and he arrives with Wednesday sitting ninth in the League One table, just two points from the top six.

It is fair to say that Challinor's appointment has been well-received by Wednesday fans, with many feeling that the seven-time promotion winner is the right man to lead the club back to the Championship.

Challinor's impressive track record will no doubt equally excite the Owls dressing room as they prepare to begin work under their new manager, but one player in particular may be thrilled about the change in the dugout.

Louie Barry worked under Challinor on three separate occasions at Stockport during his loan spells from Aston Villa, and the forward produced the best form of his career across his various stints at Edgeley Park.

Barry linked up with Challinor for the first time in the 2023-24 season as he joined the Hatters on a season-long loan, and he scored nine goals and provided four assists in 22 appearances to help the club to the League Two title.

After the success of his first loan, Barry returned to Stockport in the summer of 2024 ahead of their return to League One, and he took his form to another level in the third tier, netting 16 goals and registering three assists in 24 games before being recalled by Villa in January.

The Hatters secured a third loan deal for Barry in January, and while injury limited him to just 10 appearances in the second half of last season, he still managed to score four goals, including netting in the second leg of the play-off semi-final victory over Stevenage.

Barry made a promising start to his Wednesday career after his permanent move from Aston Villa this summer, scoring three goals and providing one assist in his first six appearances, but he was ruled out for three months after sustaining an ankle ligament injury in early September.

As he continues to make good progress in his recovery, it has been suggested that Barry could make his return earlier than expected after the November international break, meaning Challinor may not have to wait too long to have the 23-year-old at his disposal, which is a mouthwatering prospect for Owls supporters.

While Barry has started to debunk the theory that he cannot deliver outside of Stockport since his arrival at Wednesday, he did struggle to hit the same heights in his other loans away from Aston Villa, failing to register a single goal contribution in a combined total of 13 appearances during injury-hit spells with Hull City and Sheffield United in the Championship in 2025.

The Owls have won just two of their nine league games during Barry's absence, and they have only scored 11 goals in that time, with five of those coming in the victory over AFC Wimbledon last month, so the forward has certainly been missed.
