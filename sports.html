The 46-year-old had been out of work since leaving Burnley towards the end of the previous campaign, but has wasted no time in getting his feet back under the table and experienced an action-packed debut in the Hammers dugout by claiming a 3-3 draw at Cardiff City last Wednesday after his side had stunningly fallen to a three-goal defecit before the break in South Wales.

Real Madrid: why José Mourinho has enormous hatred for FC Barcelona

📅 Nov 09, 2026  |  Category: Sports  |  Read Source →

Tonight is Clasico! FC Barcelona welcomes Real Madrid to the Camp Nou, in a match followed by the whole world, and with a lot at stake on all sides. Beyond the historic rivalry, the Merengues must win or risk finding themselves 9 points behind their sworn enemy in the La Liga standings. There are players like Lamine Yamal or Kylian Mbappé who will also want to show their best side. But if there is a man who plays big, and who is keen to hurt his evening rival, it is José Mourinho.

And for good reason, the Portuguese coach has real hatred for FC Barcelona. And it’s reciprocal, even if the tension has weakened a little since his first sulphurous stint at the head of the Merengues. It must be said that the story of hatred between the Special One and the Catalan club had the potential to be in reality… a love story. Younger generations don’t know it, but in 1996, a young José Mourinho arrived at FC Barcelona as assistant to Bobbt Robson, whom he had known in Portugal. He then remained at the club despite the Englishman’s dismissal a year later, occupying different roles in the different staffs that followed, for example being a video analyst, then again assistant.

If many supporters culés make fun of him by calling him “the translator” – he actually took care of translating Robson’s instructions at the time – he was very appreciated internally by the players, including, at the time, young Carles Puyol, Xavi or Pep Guardiola. However, the Catalan management never really wanted to offer him a more important role, he who was rather happy, to the point where some explained that his dream was to become the coach of the first team. Which could have happened well after his departure from the club in 2000, since on several occasions, his name was announced as a potential coach of the A. In 2008, the club thought very strongly of him… But Laporta finally offered to Guardiola. Multiple disappointments which caused real scars for the Portuguese.

In 2010, before arriving on the bench at the Bernabéu, he also had a very lively Champions League semi-final against FC Barcelona, ​​with barbs at a press conference, which had already put him in the sights of the Barcelonans. And inevitably, upon his arrival, this aversion was accentuated on one side or the other. Not helped by a very complicated media context, and a rather difficult sporting context with the man who was surely the best Barça in history opposite, José Mourinho adopted an almost warlike strategy upon his arrival. The Special One had thus multiplied the media outings against the Catalan club, with accusations linked to arbitration, and this famous “Por Qué? » (“why” in Spanish, Editor’s note) after the 2011 semi-final in which he questioned the decisions of many referees who would have favored Barça in previous years.

It is also said that the famous Clasico de la manita (5-0) in November 2010 had particularly affected him on a moral level, and the presence of a Pep Guardiola praised by the public in front of him also frustrated him. On the pitch, his team also played particularly aggressively, and there was also this episode of the Spanish Super Cup in the summer of 2011, during which he put his finger in the eye of Tito Vilanova (then in Guardiola’s staff) during a scuffle. For Mourinho, creating permanent tension with Barça was also a strategy, serving to mobilize everyone madridismo behind him, and to put pressure on his rival. Since his return this summer, and after several rather peaceful weeks, he very quickly put a part back into the machine, more than ten years later.

Even if he is perhaps a little less virulent and explicit in his remarks than at the beginning of the 2010s, the Special One does not miss an opportunity to launch accusations against Barça and the arbitrations which would be favorable to it. The Negreira affair has logically been mentioned several times by the Lusitanian since the start of the season. Even if it is clear that this season, Mourinho’s speech is a little less popular with madridistas, with a good number of supporters accusing him of wanting to divert attention from the team’s real problems by talking about Barça or the referees. Tonight, at the Camp Nou, José Mourinho will meet his sworn enemy, and there is no doubt that any result other than a victory will be a huge disappointment for him…

12 Most Universally Loved Sitcom Characters Of All Time

📅 Nov 08, 2026  |  Category: Sports  |  Read Source →

Sitcoms are famous for producing a whole host of lovable characters, but there are a few that come out as clear favorites among wider audiences. The sitcom is one of the most endearing formats on TV, providing audiences with comfort watches that they can revisit time and time again, and a cast of characters who largely remain familiar throughout the show.

With that said, every sitcom character typically needs a set of flaws that make them more interesting and serve as a building block to create narratives around these individuals. And while some flaws are innocuous, there are others that can make a sitcom character insufferable.

On the flip side, there are a handful of sitcom legends who have cemented their place in audiences' hearts thanks to pretty flimsy flaws, or their charming and quirky personalities, or simply being a figure who often sees the best in others, and thus ends up having their best shine through.

With that said, the first person on this list is, of course, Ted Lasso. Played by Jason Sudeikis, Ted is a charming American man who moves to the UK to coach a football team, and while he has little to no knowledge about the sport or English customs, his endless positivity, warmth, and ability to draw out people's best serves him well in his new position.

Ted is also incredibly bright, with a high degree of emotional intelligence that allows him to tap into what his peers are feeling and connect on a level that many others struggle to replicate. Ted's charm and optimism earns him a spot as one of the most beloved characters on TV.

Next up, Phil Dunphy is remarkably similar to Ted Lasso in some ways, due to his incredible positive outlook and his warmth, but he does also possess a great deal more naïveté than Ted. Phil is clumsy, corny, and desperate to get approval from others, whether that be his wife, kids, father-in-law, or clients.

However, despite his surprising lack of self-awareness, Phil's depth of love and support for his family is the thing that makes him an undeniably endearing character that people can't help but fall in love with. Ty Burrell plays the part with incredible confidence and energy, and it all comes together in perfect harmony on the show season after season.

Matthew Perry may have been a more colorful character in life whose choices didn't always lead him down the healthiest paths, but his time as Chandler Bing on Friends will forever keep his memory alive in the hearts and minds of audiences everywhere. The interesting thing about Chandler is the fact that he is such a guarded character, always hiding behind humor and sarcasm.

However, despite his tendency to try and keep his emotions at a distance, Chandler becomes one of the most vulnerable and real characters on the show, especially in the moments he shares with Monica, or as his friendship with Joey evolves. Chandler is a beautiful soul in a bristly package, and it makes him all the more lovable.

Another character who likes to keep his emotions buried deep, Ron Swanson from Parks and Recreation, is the Director of his local Parks and Recreation department of government, which makes his unabashed contempt for all forms of politics and red tape all the more intriguing.

Ron Swanson is a simple man who enjoys simple things, and he has no respect for how government bodies are supposed to run, instead holding onto the job because he finds it to be an easy and low-effort role where almost nothing ever happens, that is until Leslie Knope sets her mind to opening a new park in Pawnee.

Nick Offerman is absolutely fantastic as Swanson, and the character remains a wonderful contradiction whose desire to not work is rivaled only by his intense loyalty to his friends and his commitment to supporting their dreams.

The late and great Catherine O'Hara left her mark on the TV landscape with her spectacular turn as Moira Rose, the matriarch of Schitt's Creek. While Moira is initially a hard woman to love or respect, and her self-absorbed and theatrical nature make her a bit of a pain, Moira's growth throughout the series is something to behold.

As the character begins to warm up to small-town living, and become someone that fans could engage with on a deeper level, it's hard to look back at those earlier moments without a tenderness and admiration for this character, who is ultimately on a journey of growth and understanding.

The 1950s sitcom, I Love Lucy, was a foundational entry in the genre that set the tone from which many other TV comedy shows would draw inspiration. Lucille Ball plays the titular lead, and brings a tremendous physical comedy that, when paired with Lucy's wacky schemes and overtly expressive face, makes her a character that is impossible not to fall in love with.

With that, the show earned its title, and the series continued to prove just how popular it was over six seasons. Today, I Love Lucy may not be the most familiar show for younger viewers, but anyone who picks up the series will ultimately find themselves captivated by the charm of Lucy Ricardo.

A few decades later, in 1985, The Golden Girls launched on TV and became an instant classic. And in no small part, that was thanks to the incredible ensemble cast that brought together four strong and vocal personalities who all had their own distinctive voice, and explored their wonderful maturing friendship.

Still, among these four charming women, Betty White's Rose Nylund stands out as one of the most lovable, thanks to her innocence, naïveté, and her ability to balance out the rest of the girls when things got heated. Rose is a literal treasure who remains one of the most beloved characters from TV, even all these decades later.

Rowan Atkinson struck gold in developing this silent character, Mr. Bean, who became a global sensation without ever saying a word. Employing a great deal of slapstick humor, intense facial expressions, and a certain innocence, Mr. Bean is a character that seems to transcend time and other barriers to engage with fanbases the world over.

He is a simple man, who sets out to complete simple tasks. However, due to his lack of awareness, and his clumsy nature, Mr. Bean often gets caught up in trouble that would never impact on typical people. But all of that simply makes him more engaging and adorable.

Andy Samberg's Jake Peralta is the embodiment of golden retriever energy in the form of an immature New York City detective. Jake is an eternally youthful and playful character who wants to get the best out of life, and is always ready to gamify his situation.

Jake also experiences profound personal growth over the course of the series, as he becomes more vulnerable and forms closer relationships with people like Captain Raymon Holt and Amy Santiago.

Community is a show that is brimming with colorful characters, but while the flaws for many of these individuals are initially overwhelming, Abed's (Danny Pudi) quirk is that he views everything through a meta lens and constantly references pop culture.

While everyone else around him embarks on a journey to become the best version of themselves, Abed is often the anchor that helps others to recognize where they are making mistakes, and course correct. He is intensely loyal, curious about human connection and boldly honest. All of which adds up to make him one of the best characters on TV.

7 Sleek Sports Cars Ranked Worst To Best By Drag Coefficient

📅 Nov 08, 2026  |  Category: Sports  |  Read Source →

Equipping a sports car with a big and powerful engine is all well and good. But if the car in question is designed like a cinder block, then it's just wasting the engine's potential. As a car speeds up, the air resistance around it increases. And a car with a sleeker design can slip through the air with less resistance, thus granting it better performance. That, in a very basic nutshell, is why aerodynamics matter.

Now, drag coefficient (Cd) isn't the be-all and end-all when it comes to measuring a car's aerodynamics as a whole. Something like drag area, which combines that metric with frontal area measurements, provides a more complete picture. Nevertheless, drag coefficient scores still provide a solid impression as to just how slippery a car is — and such figures are more readily available from automakers, too.

Sleek designs certainly aren't reserved exclusively for performance models; economy cars like Honda's little hybrid that could, the Insight, can also be marvelously slippery. But this article will be focusing on some of the sleekest sports and performance cars out from across the last century, covering old-school favorites such as the Tatra T87 with modern marvels like Volkswagen's XL1.

This Czechoslavkian masterpiece is a genuinely fascinating piece of design. Before moving onto the aerodynamic aspects of the T87, it's first worth covering what else makes it such an intriguing automobile. Motivation is sourced from a rear-engined 2.9-liter V8, which kicked out an impressive-for-the-era 85 horsepower. Back in the '40s, that was a serious amount of power to be dealing with, and Tatra ensured its effectiveness by complimenting it with a seriously slippery design.

German Zeppelins provided plenty of influence behind the T87's iconic shape, and the end result was a drag coefficient of just 0.36 Cd. This enabled the 85-horse V8 to propel the T87 to an impressive 100 mph, although the handling characteristics of a rear-engined machine with independent swing-axle suspension resulted in a fair number of accidents. Rumor has it that, due to so many Nazi officers meeting their maker in T87 accidents, the car was actually banned for use – but the accuracy of these claims has been contested over the years. Still, it all adds to the fascinating history of Tatra's aerodynamic masterpiece.

Production spanned from 1936 to 1950, albeit with a gap during the war years. Despite such a wide production window, though, just over 3,000 T87s were ever built. It proved to be a significant influence to Ferdinand Porsche, and today, it's a cherished classic that attracts collectors willing to pay over $100,000.

The Huayra replaced the awesome Zonda in 2011, and it boasted a design that was just as striking. It was a million-dollar masterpiece at launch, packing a 720-horsepower twin-turbocharged 6.0-liter V12, capable of propelling it along to a top speed of 224 mph. Aerodynamic trickery helps the Italian to remain stable at speed, with front- and rear-mounted flaps that rise and fall to alter downforce levels.

It's important to remember that a supercar like this needs to be comfortable performing through the corners as well as the straight lines. Here's where that active aero really proves its relevancy. Those four flaps allow the Huayra to transform itself depending on the given requirements. While flat, the Huayra boasts a drag coefficient of just 0.31 Cd (as proven by a Mercedes wind tunnel). But they can also flip up to assist the car with braking and keep it planted through the corners.

Much like Pagani did with the Zonda, as the Huayra began to age, the automaker began to produce numerous special editions. Among them was a rolling $7.3 million air piece dubbed the Codalunga. The svelte model sports various ducts and diffusers, plus a longtail design – which is a known method of reducing the amount of drag a car's body produces. However, the Codalunga has not been individually tested in a wind tunnel, so whether it beats the standard Huayra's 0.31 Cd isn't officially known.

While on the topic of longtail designs, it seems appropriate to mention the McLaren Speedtail. This car categorically proves the effectiveness of such a design, with a drag coefficient of just under 0.29 Cd. The British firm from Woking knows a thing or two about churning out slippery supercars, but this Speedtail is something of a caricature; it sits 3 feet longer than the F1, and it's even longer than the F1 GTR Longtail. Helping it cut through the air as precisely as possible is a narrow frontal area, numerous concealed ducts, a complete lack of rear-view mirrors, and front wheels that are almost entirely enclosed.

Of course, a sleek design still needs to be complimented with a mighty power train if impressive performance is the end goal. And with a turbocharged 4.0-liter V8 that combines its efforts with a single electric motor to produce 1,035 horsepower, the Speedtail recipe is certainly a comprehensive one. Combined with a dry weight of just 3,153 pounds, that's enough to see the Speedtail achieve a top speed of 250 mph. Plus, it'll crack 186 mph in just shy of half the time required by its forefather, the mighty F1.

As impressive as the McLaren Speedtail's design is, what's arguably far more impressive is that a $44,245 Corvette from 2005 can match the $2.25 million British hypercar's drag coefficient. With a drag coefficient of just 0.28 Cd, the C6 Chevy is surprisingly slippery. This wasn't a one-hit-wonder for Chevrolet, either, as previous generations had also proven to be remarkably aerodynamic as well. But the C6 manages to outdo them all (albeit only marginally). The C4 boasted a drag coefficient of 0.34 Cd, while the C5 sat at 0.29 Cd. It's worth noting that later generations – including the current C8 — also sit behind the C6's rating.

It's no wonder that people think the C6 is still the best Corvette to date. Regardless of whether you agree or not, it's hard to find real fault with the C6. It combined a brilliantly efficient design with supercar-rivaling performance; the most powerful iteration was the 638-horsepower ZR1, which could top 200 mph. Even the base models were serious performers thanks to an LS1 lurking beneath that low-slung hood. These cars were affordable when new, and they're still great bang-for-buck choices today, with entry-level coupes and convertibles both available for sub-$25,000 – proving that slippery sports cars don't exclusively belong in six-figure price brackets.

Electric cars and hybrids pay a lot of attention to aerodynamics, as being able to slip through the air with minimal resistance is a particularly effective way of increasing a vehicle's range. That's led to buyers of electrified vehicles becoming understandably obsessed with realistic and effective ranges. It should come as no surprise, then, that BMW's bold hybridized i8 sports a drag coefficient of just 0.26 Cd.

That drag coefficient proves that the i8's striking design isn't all about stealing the attention of passers by, in fact, it's really quite clever. The various slices and slashes between those layered body panels help to direct airflow into the car's various radiators, which allows the i8 to disregard active aero. Even the cockpit itself is teardrop-shaped to reduce drag, while the flared rear pillars help keep the visuals sweet without damaging any aerodynamic benefits.

With a turbocharged 1.5-liter inline-three engine and single electric motor tackling the task of motivation, the i8's power train recipe could not be more different from that of the aforementioned Corvette. However, just like the C6, the BMW i8 now finds itself at a very tempting price point, with early examples now changing hands for sub-$40,000.

This one's probably something of a surprise, as unlike so many of the other mentions on this list, the Nissan GT-R doesn't exactly look immensely slippery. It's not particularly low-slung, it doesn't incorporate an obvious longtail design, nothing else about it is clearly geared toward aerodynamics — plus, there's a great big spoiler adorning the trunk. However, that hasn't stopped the GT-R from being a technological marvel with a drag coefficient of only 0.26 Cd. Yep, this chunky model with heaps of boost and gaping grills matches the rating of BMW's slippery little eco-minded coupe.

The secrets of the GT-R low drag coefficient are actually somewhat hidden. It sports an almost completely flat floor, a functional diffuser which helps to channel airflow efficiency, and a slightly tapered rear overhang. Also helping to direct the air in an effective manner are what Nissan describes as "aero-blades" adorning the front fenders, while the narrow fender vents help to provide valuable downforce at the front, too.

Throughout its impressive 18-year tenure, the R35 GT-R saw the power from its twin-turbocharged 3.8-liter V6 rise to genuine supercar levels. And the clever aero tweaks it received over those years helped drivers to make the most of that strength. It came incredibly close to cracking the 7-minute barrier on the Nurburgring, peaking with a time of 7:08.679 in its 2013 Nismo guise — breaking the world record for fastest volume production car in the process.

All of the cars featured upon this list are aerodynamically impressive, and they certainly sit a long way removed from the least aerodynamic cars ever made. However, the Volkswagen XL1 stands in a class of its own. The sole purpose of this striking little hybridized-diesel inline-three-powered sports car was to be as mightily efficient as possible.

The headline number here is a drag coefficient of just 0.19 Cd. To get this number down so far, it combines many of the other engineering techniques used by other cars on this list. It very obviously sports a teardrop profile like the i8, allowing the smoothest possible path for airflow. Like the GT-R, the car's floor is completely flat, preventing the underside from pushing against the wind. And — going above and beyond the Speedtail in this regard – covered rear wheels assist the XL1 in achieving its remarkably low drag coefficient.

It wasn't just aerodynamic efficiency that Volkswagen was chasing with the XL1. It was also designed to be immensely frugal on fuel, to the point where a Honda Insight or Toyota Prius might look like a big-block Chevy from the '70s in comparison. On the European combined test cycle, Volkswagen managed to achieve a frankly mind-boggling 313 mpg, a feat helped by the model's incredibly low curb weight (courtesy of magnesium wheels and a carbon-fiber monocoque chassis).

CJ Carr running Notre Dame's two-minute offense against Miami to end the first half is all you need to know about who he is.

It's as surgical a series of plays as you'll see this season; he's calm before the snap, unpanicked after, even when he's avoiding the rush, always has his eyes downfield, and throws with the accuracy and precision that will make you a first-rounder, even if you lack the "prototypical physical tools" NFL teams covet in their franchise QBs. When you look like you've been doing this for a decade, no one cares if you can throw the ball 80 yards or run a 4.3 forty.

That drive ended with an 18-yard laser to Micah Gilbert to give the Fighting Irish a 17-3 halftime lead. And while they didn't cruise to a blowout win – credit to Darian Mensah and the Canes for battling back – it did reinforce a question I've been thinking about as we work our way through another "can't-miss" quarterback class that has, once again, missed:

Have we been evaluating the position backward?

Every fall, NFL teams (and folks in the media too) fall in love with the big, athletic, strong-armed quarterbacks who, if you squint enough, start to look like Justin Herbert or Josh Allen. And there's a reason for that. When it all comes together, it can be special. When it doesn't … well, it can be the 2026 Los Angeles Chargers.

It's why we annually watch evaluators excuse bad decision-making, chronic inaccuracy and poor processing because a QB gets off the bus looking like Will Levis or Drew Allar or if we're talking the '27 class, names like Arch Manning and LaNorris Sellers. Meanwhile, Brock Purdy, Mr. Irrelevant in 2022, and Kirk Cousins, a fourth-rounder a decade earlier, lacked those eye-popping physical traits but proved they could play quarterback at a high level in the NFL.

I'd even throw Mac Jones into the group. He put up huge numbers at Alabama and made the Pro Bowl as a rookie in New England, but his career went sideways after the Patriots decided Matt Patricia and Joe Judge should lead the offense. Jones had a pit stop in Jacksonville before finding his way to Kyle Shanahan and San Francisco, and all he did was play so well in Purdy's absence early last season that there were questions about whether he should keep the job. The point, in part, is what a smart, accurate quarterback with average physical tools can accomplish when the system plays to his strengths.

To be clear, this is less about arm strength or athleticism than how much inconsistency evaluators are willing to put up with for the upside, no matter how unlikely that realized upside might be. Put another way: are we undervaluing quarterbacks who win with anticipation, accuracy, processing and average NFL arms?

Which brings us to CJ Carr and Darian Mensah. Both should come in 6-foot-2 or 6-foot-3, so we're not talking about Bryce Young or Kyler Murray. But neither has a Patrick Mahomes fastball in their locker, either. They see the field, throw on time and with anticipation, and consistently understand where the football needs to go. Saturday gave us two very different looks at those skills.

Carr was clinical from the start. He set protections, got the ball out quickly and accurately, and once he started feeling it, was damn-near unstoppable in the way Purdy can be when he's in his bag. Again, I'd refer you to Carr running that two-minute offense as all the evidence you need.

Mensah spent much of the first half running for his life before settling down in the third quarter and drawing the Hurricanes to within seven points after one of his bread-and-butter back-shoulder throws:

For Mensah, the biggest improvement this season has been his consistency. Notre Dame's outstanding offensive line deserves some credit, certainly, but Carr looks like a different quarterback when things break down; he's more decisive, better identifies pressure and seldom looks rattled.

The ability to avoid costly mistakes separates both quarterbacks from some of their peers. Mestemaker has put the ball in harm's way seven times in 54 opportunities under pressure, compared with one apiece for Mensah and Carr. Neither has been immune to bad decisions, but both have generally understood when to test a defense and when to take what it's giving them. And as we've seen with many highly drafted quarterbacks, that matters.

There's also this idea that Carr's average arm means he's Captain Checkdown. Fun fact: Nearly a quarter of his passes this season have traveled at least 20 yards, almost twice Mensah's rate. Heading into Week 10, Carr had eight touchdowns on those attempts and only one turnover-worthy play. None of this means the next Brock Purdy should be auto-drafted ahead of the next Josh Allen. Allen is proof that betting on rare talent can pay off (even if those bets rarely work out, the allure is often too irresistible), and Bryce Young shows NFL teams will invest the No. 1 pick in a quarterback with more modest physical tools if the college tape is exceptional. And Carr and Mensah are making strong cases that they belong in that category.

Processing and anticipation don't replace physical talent. They are talents themselves, and Carr's and Mensah's performances were a reminder of how much they matter.

The question for NFL teams isn't whether either can make NFL throws. They've done that repeatedly. It's whether we've been too quick to forgive the quarterbacks who look the part, and too slow to appreciate the ones who consistently play the part.
