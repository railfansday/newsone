I Walked After Every Meal for Two Months, and My Digestion and Energy Levels Have Never Been Better

📅 Nov 13, 2026  |  Category: Sports  |  Read Source →

Experts weigh in on why sitting is the worst thing you can do after eating

It’s almost 9:00 p.m., and my work dinner in Manhattan’s SoHo neighborhood is finally coming to a close. As the people around me start to get up and call their Ubers, I head out the door to go home—on foot. After a lavish dinner, most people’s last thought is to get a brisk walk in, but for me, it’s crucial—not to hit a step count or burn calories, but as my version of a digestif, if you will.

My idea to walk after dinner came from a video I saw months ago from a TikTok nutritionist who touted a brisk 10-minute walk after meals as the key to boosting metabolism, regulating blood sugar levels, and helping your stomach digest food. My two favorite pastimes are eating and walking. I never thought the two would go hand in hand.

“Sitting briefly after eating isn’t inherently harmful, but prolonged, uninterrupted sitting can lead to a higher post-meal blood-sugar response compared with breaking it up with short walks,” says Elizabeth Sharp Edens, an internal medicine and obesity medicine physician, functional medicine practitioner, and founder of HMW Method. A 2022 meta-analysis in Sports Medicine found that light-intensity walking reduced post-meal glucose by almost 17 percent. The same study found that standing breaks after meals reduced glucose spikes by 9.5 percent on average. The TL;DR: Research says that any type of movement can improve glucose levels.

We know walking can do wonders—it can clear the mind, help you lean out, and even boost dopamine—but why is it a solution for feeling less full and avoiding afternoon crashes? “When you walk, your muscles use glucose for energy,” Edens explains. “Muscle contractions move glucose transporters called GLUT4 to the surface of muscle cells, helping draw glucose out of the bloodstream through a pathway that doesn’t depend on insulin.” Since your legs, the vehicle for walking, contain some of the biggest muscles in your body, it makes sense that walking effectively stimulates muscle contractions that move glucose transporters. “Walking breaks are a simple way to support blood sugar management while getting your steps in,” she adds.

While many rely on peptides, green powders, and “magic” supplements, walking is an easy, mindless (and free) way to improve digestion and prevent blood-sugar crashes. “Even two to five minutes after a meal can help, ideally before the glucose peak, typically 60 to 90 minutes after eating,” Kiran Krishnan, a research microbiologist, the founder and chief science officer of the Biome Learning Center, and Dose key opinion leader, adds.

I often joke after large meals that I will have to be rolled home. The good news: Walking can also reduce the overwhelming feeling of fullness. “Walking also helps the gut physically,” Krishnan says, citing a study that found that a slow walk after a meal significantly shortened the time it took the stomach to empty half its contents. “For people prone to bloating, mild [walking] cut intestinal gas retention from 45 percent to 24 percent and eased symptoms,” he adds. Plus, Krishnan says studies show reduced LDL cholesterol levels and improved liver function in those who walked after meals.

Then there’s good old gravity, says Caitlin Hosmer, a Maine-based registered dietitian and co-founder of MenoPantry. “Gravity helps too, which is part of why walking may help people who feel uncomfortably full or get reflux after meals,” she explains. “Gentle movement and staying upright help food move out of the stomach and along the digestive tract.”

Ten minutes isn’t long, but studies show it’s just as effective as a longer walk. A 2025 study published by PubMed Central found that a 10-minute walk lowered glucose measurements by 6 percent and a 30-minute walk lowered glucose levels by 5 percent, meaning just 10 minutes of walking is all you need to help reduce a post-meal glucose spike. If you can walk for 30 minutes, an hour, or even longer—that’s great. But just 10 minutes can work just as well.

What matters most is how quickly you get moving. “Glucose typically peaks 60 to 90 minutes after eating, and moving before that peak is what blunts the spike,” Hosmer says. “A walk puts your muscles to work just as glucose from the meal is flooding into the bloodstream.” At first, I questioned whether I had to walk after every meal. “Put your effort where it counts most: after your largest, most carbohydrate-rich meal,” Hosmer advises, adding that for most, that’s after dinner, but walking after all three meals is ideal.

My post-meal walks varied—some days I would walk for 30 minutes or even an hour, depending on how much time I had and where I was going next. But as I started incorporating them into my work schedule, especially on busy office days, I often had only 10 minutes to spare. When in a pinch, I would get up and walk around the office floor and, at home, do air squats, which engage the same large leg muscles as walking and can be just as effective as a post-meal walk.

It may feel strange to pencil in time to walk after every meal, but the benefits I have noticed surpass any inconvenience or awkwardness. I have fewer crashes after I eat, whereas I previously would feel lethargic after every meal. I’ve also noticed improved digestion. While this can be chalked up to a number of things (my beloved vibration plate, greens powders, or digestive enzymes), I always feel lighter and less full after a post-meal walk.

It’s also not meant to feel daunting or like a chore. “Your post-dinner walk isn’t a workout. It’s a way of helping your body handle the meal,” Hosmer says. “The one real ‘wrong way’ is skipping it because you think it has to be long or perfect. I often say something is better than nothing, and that’s especially true here. Even a few minutes of walking, started soon after you finish eating, can blunt the blood sugar rise, and it may help your digestion too.”

Germany dominate Serbia in rain-soaked Nations League clash

📅 Nov 13, 2026  |  Category: Sports  |  Read Source →

Germany have made a good start to the last Nations League phase in 2026, beating Serbia 3-1 in a rain-soaked contest. Goals from Wirtz and debutant Gruda mean that Germany still have the chance to top their Nations League group.

The unusual behind-closed-doors atmosphere has seen selected groups of children under 15 admitted alongside teachers and football coaches following UEFA sanctions against Serbia for discrimination. However, the game still delivered on the entertainment front.

Jürgen Klopp's side needed just two minutes to break the deadlock, with Kai Havertz getting on the end of a corner before his attempt was deflected into the net by Srdan Babić for an own goal.

Germany doubled their advantage in the 14th minute following a brilliant, Klopp-esque pressing sequence. Aleksandar Pavlović played a superb through ball to Havertz, whose effort was denied by goalkeeper Vanja Milinković-Savić. Florian Wirtz reacted quickest to the rebound and calmly chipped the ball into the net.

Germany dominated the remainder of the first half, enjoying 75% possession and completing 94% of their passes. Their superiority continued after the break, with Brajan Gruda capping a fantastic solo run with a clinical finish in the 50th minute to make it 3-0.

Serbia responded just six minutes later. Babić made amends for his earlier own goal by reacting quickest after Stefan Gudelj's header from a free kick was saved, converting the rebound to give the hosts a lifeline.

Joshua Kimmich thought he had restored Germany's three-goal advantage shortly afterwards with a powerful low strike from the edge of the box. However, the goal was correctly ruled out due to Kai Havertz's passive offside position.

Derry Scherhant, one of Germany's standout performers under Klopp so far, had enjoyed a relatively quiet evening but came close to scoring in the 70th minute. After cutting inside from the left, his effort beat the goalkeeper but struck the woodwork.

Serbia substitute Aleksandar Mitrović also came close to scoring, but Marc-André ter Stegen stood firm to keep his effort out. Things then went from bad to worse for the hosts as Vanja Dragojevic received a straight red card for a dangerous challenge on Germany captain Joshua Kimmich, accidentally standing on his ankle.

Dragojević was not the only Serbian to leave the pitch early. Substitute Andrija Zivković also saw red after picking up two yellow cards in quick succession following an animated exchange with the referee shortly before full-time.

Klopp used the opportunity to introduce several other Bundesliga names, including Niclas Füllkrug, Felix Nmecha and Said El Mala. However, none managed to make a significant impact during their limited time on the pitch. They could get another opportunity when Germany face the Netherlands on Monday.

The match also marked a historic first for Germany, with Kimmich and Pavlović starting together as a double pivot for the first time. The partnership showed plenty of promise and could become a regular feature under Klopp.

Finally, it was also Germany's last appearance in their long-standing partnership with Adidas. Given the convincing performance, it was certainly a fitting farewell.

Warriors' Jimmy Butler is sending himself down to the G League for a game

📅 Nov 13, 2026  |  Category: Sports  |  Read Source →

Golden State Warriors star Jimmy Butler says his first game back from his torn ACL will be in the G League.

Butler tore his ACL on Jan. 19 and faced a year-long recovery process, but has been ramping up his activity in recent weeks. In an interview with the Athletic's Nick Friedell, Butler said he is planning on playing in a game with the Santa Cruz Warriors, Golden State's affiliate in the NBA minor league, as part of his rehabilitation process.

"Well, y'all gonna get your opportunity come Santa Cruz," Butler told the Athletic. "Because I'm definitely coming down there to play. So good luck to everybody. Y'all ain't gonna get too many minutes out of me, but I'm definitely going down there. I want to hoop."

Butler even acknowledged that there would be some who may not like the idea. Butler's agent Bernard Lee took to social media to voice his displeasure at Friedell for reporting the story.

"Nick…you had to give this table legs huh?" Lee wrote on X. "I'm going to hit you with one of them when I see you."

Within the organization, the main person who would be against the idea is Mike Dunleavy, the Warriors general manager and Butler's former teammate with the Bulls. When Friedell asked Butler if he had cleared the plan with Dunleavy, Friedell wrote Butler's smile only got bigger before responding.

"I don't got to clear s-t with nobody," Butler said. "I'm going. Me and Mike will fistfight. He's gonna be like, 'Jimmy, no.' I'm gonna be like, 'Mike, yes. I'm going to G League, baby!' Y'all know where to find me. Santa Cruz, baby."

One person who is clearly in favor of it, though, is Steve Kerr. In an interview with the Athletic in Houston on Wednesday, Kerr celebrated Butler's plans.

"I think it makes such perfect sense," Kerr said. "But most guys in the league in Jimmy's position are, like, too vain to do it - and Jimmy's the opposite. I think he would love giving the fans down there a great surprise. That's amazing. I love that."

Kerr related the move to how MLB players do rehab assignments in that sport's minor leagues. But while that is quite common in baseball, there has rarely been an NBA player who has done so. Warriors stars Steph Curry and Klay Thompson have both joined the "Sea Dubs" for practices during their recovery processes, but a star of Butler's stature has never played in a G League game.

Kerr said if the Warriors schedule allows, he will be in the stands to watch Butler play in the G League. The Santa Cruz team plays their home opener Friday night and has home games all throughout December, when Butler might be getting close to ready to play.

"It may not be for long, but I'm going to play in Santa Cruz," Butler said. "I'm playing in a f-king game."

Now in Baltimore, the well-traveled Brissett prepares to fill in for another star quarterback

📅 Nov 13, 2026  |  Category: Sports  |  Read Source →

OWINGS MILLS, Md. (AP) — Jacoby Brissett's next challenge is to fill in for a two-time MVP in Baltimore.

If anyone should be used to that assignment, it's Brissett. The 33-year-old has been a journeyman for the past several seasons, but has plenty of experience replacing standout quarterbacks.

"I think he'll operate our offense at a level required to be successful. We're really excited about the addition, and he's just a very mature pro that I think will bring a leadership style to our offense from the quarterback position," Ravens coach Jesse Minter said. "He'll get the ball out on time. He'll be able to kind of run the whole thing. He'll be a leader. He'll hold guys accountable."

Brissett is set to start for the Ravens on Monday night against the Los Angeles Chargers. Baltimore traded for him after Lamar Jackson and backup Tyler Huntley both had to go on injured reserve. Now in his 11th season, Brissett is one of the game's most experienced understudies. Rarely a team's top choice entering the season, he nonetheless has started 73 games, often in place of some pretty big names.

"You are around a lot of different people, a lot of different systems, different situations, every year," Brissett said. "So just being able to be flexible, that's what's kept me in this league for so long, and this is no different."

Brissett was drafted by New England in 2016, the year Tom Brady was suspended four games in the "Deflategate" aftermath. When Jimmy Garoppolo went down with an injury, Brissett suddenly found himself starting a couple of games less than a month into his career.

Indianapolis traded for Brissett the following year, with Andrew Luck recovering from surgery. Brissett ended up starting 15 games that season. When Luck abruptly retired two years later, Brissett was next man up for Indy, again starting almost every game.

"You have to have grace for yourself, obviously, and then confidence that you wouldn't be in that position if the organization, the team, the coaches, the players didn't think that you can handle it," Brissett said. "Then obviously, you take it day by day, and I think you slow the time down while everybody else is moving fast around you."

In 2021, he made five starts for Miami in place of the injury-plagued Tua Tagovailoa. A year later, Brissett started Cleveland's first 11 games when Deshaun Watson was suspended following sexual misconduct accusations.

In 2024, Brissett was back in New England, starting the first five games while the Patriots held off on making rookie Drake Maye the No. 1 quarterback. Last year, Brissett replaced injured Kyler Murray and made 12 starts for Arizona. With Murray out of the picture this year, Brissett started the first eight games for the Cardinals before being demoted and then quickly traded.

"It was obviously a tough situation. Then you get the news like the next day or two, or whatever it is, and then you kind of have to get your mind wrapped around another situation," Brissett said. "So, it's kind of all over the place, but having those extra days kind of helped me settle my emotions down and then obviously refocusing and figuring out what the next steps were."

Now he's in a familiar role — replacing a franchise quarterback as what the team hopes will be a short-term fix. Baltimore announced Friday that Jackson's type of thumb injury typically takes six to 10 weeks to heal. Brissett's job is to keep the Ravens (4-5) in playoff contention for as long as he's needed.

"He's a guy who's played 11 years, so he's seen a lot of football," offensive coordinator Declan Doyle said. "He'll let it rip. He can really drive the ball. Those are all things that I'm really excited about with him."

Jesus Owono Banned For 12 Games After Jesus de Miguel’s Death

📅 Nov 13, 2026  |  Category: Sports  |  Read Source →

FC Andorra goalkeeper Jesus Owono has learned his punishment after he was sent off in an incident that eventually led to the death of CD Tenerife forward Jesus de Miguel.

Tenerife and Andorra met in a Spanish Segunda Division match on November 2, but the match had to be abandoned in stoppage time after De Miguel suffered a serious injury.

Jesus Owono has broken his silence after the tragic passing of CD Tenerife forward Jesus de Miguel.

The striker was looking to run onto a long ball over the top when he was met by Owono in a horrific collision. Despite making it to hospital, De Miguel's death was confirmed the next morning.

Owono was sent off for the challenge, but immediately showed his panic when he realised the severity of De Miguel's injury. The 25-year-old is reported to be struggling with the incident, which saw his fellow player die at just 30 years of age.

Spanish police opened an investigation into involuntary manslaughter, but it is believed Owono is unlikely to face legal action. He has, however, now been handed a lengthy ban from football.

According to Marca, the goalkeeper will now serve a 12-match suspension for his part in the tragic event. The Disciplinary Committee of the Royal Spanish Football Federation (RFEF) released the following statement:

"The Disciplinary Committee, as an independent body within the federation's structure, has agreed to suspend Jesús Lázaro Ferrer Owono, a player for FC Andorra, for twelve matches for his actions during the CD Tenerife – FC Andorra match. Twelve matches is the maximum penalty allowed by the Disciplinary Code for the events described in the referee's report.

"The referee's report is the only document in the disciplinary proceedings file, and the Committee must determine the sanction based solely on this information. The report describes a violent action, with the leg raised and excessive force used, while the ball was in play. In light of these circumstances, the Committee has determined that the events fall under Article 102 of the Disciplinary Code.

"Furthermore, the subsequent consequences of the action, which are also documented in the referee's report and are public knowledge, have been taken into account to increase the sanction to the maximum level permitted by the regulations. Under no circumstances may the Committee exceed the threshold expressly stipulated in the applicable article.

"The decision of the Disciplinary Committee may be appealed to the RFEF Appeals Committee within ten working days."

47 Years Later, the Best 'Peanuts' Quote About Life and Death Still Hits Just as Hard

📅 Nov 12, 2026  |  Category: Sports  |  Read Source →

You can track the evolution of the American comic strip through its typical depiction of childhood. Early 20th century strips like Ernie Bushmiller's Nancy (1933) show kids' lives as relatively prosaic, particularly given the culture and publication standards of the time. While their child protagonists might have faced some incidental frustrations, the comics page was mostly about gentle humor. Yes, Sluggo is a homeless orphan, but no, that isn't a source of angst or conflict.

Charles Schulz's Peanuts has become a sort of cultural shorthand for safe, family-friendly entertainment, but for its first decade, it was a genuinely daring comic. Schulz's kids were frustrated, unlucky, and often pointlessly cruel to one another, in a more realistic approach to kids' behavior than most comic strips risked showing at the time. Even if you don't have any particular use for Peanuts itself, you can see it as a necessary stepping stone towards the edgier mainstream comics of the 1980s and '90s, like The Boondocks or even Calvin and Hobbes.

Even at his most potentially controversial, Schulz was never interested in "very special episodes." No one in the Peanuts gang usually had to deal with anything more serious than academic underachievement. That's part of why some other comics' big swerves, like the death of Andy Lippincott in Doonesbury or the raccoon cub storyline in Calvin and Hobbes, hit as hard as they did at the time. You didn't often see the funny pages get anywhere near topics like death or mortality.

As far as Peanuts is concerned, there are two real exceptions. One is part of a weird series of comics that involve Snoopy's brothers, while the other is a month-long storyline from 1979.

In the daily Peanuts strip for July 2, 1979, Charlie Brown started to feel woozy during a baseball game and decided to just go home. This was the start of a surprisingly long plot arc, particularly for Peanuts, in which ol' Chuck landed in the hospital.

No one ever says what's wrong with Charlie Brown. He feels "woozy," then goes to the hospital for treatment, at which point he's admitted. Charlie Brown winds up staying in the hospital until July 29, at which point he's released and fully recovered. This leads into one of the high points of Peanuts' running football gags at the start of August 1979.

More importantly, Charlie Brown's illness has a strong ripple effect throughout the Peanuts gang. Sally immediately tries to steal his bedroom, Snoopy is only vaguely aware Charlie Brown's gone at all, and both Peppermint Patty and Marcie make a point of supporting him as best they can.

The character who's the most affected, however, is also the one that you might least expect. Lucy, who spends most of her panel time with Charlie Brown tearing him down, is worried for him to the point where she can't eat, sleep, or even harass Schroeder. She actually goes so far as to cry over Charlie Brown (and then wipe her tears off on Schroeder's piano).

Lucy's emotions on the subject hit their apex in the Peanuts strip for July 26, 1979. When Linus tells her that Charlie Brown doesn't appear to be getting any better, Lucy loses it.

"He's not any better? That's crazy! He's got to get better!" Lucy yells at Linus. "What's wrong with a world where someone like Charlie Brown can get sick, and then not get any better?! I need somebody to hit!"

That leads into Lucy's promise, in the next two days' strips, to actually abandon the football gag for once. It's an uncommon amount of empathy for Lucy and it gets abandoned almost as soon as it arrives, but it's also one of the most emotional moments in the daily strip. The July 26 comic doesn't even have a punchline as such. Instead, it's an understandable outburst that can resonate with anyone who's ever been in a situation where they can't do anything but wait.

Given how much of Charles Schulz's real life made it into Peanuts, it's enough to raise the question of whether Schulz himself had a relative in the hospital at some point in 1979. This particular Peanuts arc ends up as a strange outlier in the comic's history, as well as an oddly resonant brush with mortality.

Club Deportivo Guadalajara are Mexico's most decorated football club, but the story behind their popularised alternative name is steeped in history.

For supporters, broadcasters and rivals alike, they are known as 'Chivas'. It is a name that has become synonymous with their commercial identity, plastered across the Estadio Akron and shouted from Los Angeles to Monterrey.

However, 'Chivas' began as an insult. Rival fans and journalists voiced it so much that it stuck, with Guadalajara eventually adopting it. Here's where the nickname came from, and the other titles the club has picked up along the way.

Chiva is Spanish for a goat. The plural, chivas, carries connotations of skittishnes in Mexican usage. For example, animals that bolt or scatter without direction.

The related phrases attached to Guadalajara in the late 1940s, chivas locas (crazy goats) and chivas brinconas (jumping goats), were mockery of a team perceived to be running around in a random and unlogical fashion.

The top-flight in Mexico has a deep and rich history across its division, with nicknames steeped in heritage - here's all of them in one place.

The origin of this nickname dates back to September 30, 1948. On that day, the club posted a scrappy one-nil victory over Tampico.

A newspaper columnist — one that was sympathetic to city rivals Atlas — reportedly described the Guadalajara players as charging around the pitch like chivas locas — a phrase that translates roughly into 'crazy things' or 'crazy goats'.

Supporters of Atlas jumped on the name and immediately relayed it as a terrace taunt, but Guadalajara's response was the making of the nickname. Instead of pushing back or protesting, they simply leaned into it.

Incredibly, during their next city derby against Atlas, they emerged with a live goat dressed in the club's red-and-white stripes. It instantly turned it from a taunt to a beloved name, and the goat also became a permanent piece of matchday iconography.

Four other nicknames have been popularised.

El Rebaño Sagrado (The Sacred Flock) — the most-used alternative. It stems from the club's first professional title in the 1956/57 season. The story is that Cardinal José Garibi Rivera received the squad and revealed a Guadalajara shirt beneath his cassock. Then, a papal blessing from Pius XII gave journalists the religious framing that turned a herd of goats into a sacred flock.

Campeonísimo — this was reserved for the 1956-62 side that won seven league titles in a nine-year span, including four in succession, and remains the benchmark for Mexican domestic football.

Ya Merito (Almost There) — seen as the more affectionate one, applied in the early 1950s during a run of near misses before the 1957 breakthrough.

Rojiblancos — the most stereotypical, which is derived from the colour of thei kit: the red-and-white stripes.

Midfielder’s old-school attributes came to the fore during Nottingham Forest’s win against Manchester City

Modern football is more systematised and data-driven than ever but some things never change. Winning the first ball is important but the second ball – the Holy Grail for Sunday league footballers – is often more so.

Nottingham Forest’s 3-1 win against Manchester City last weekend featured a classic example. Forest led 2-1 inside the last 20 minutes but City, lifted by Rayan Cherki’s sweet half-volley, were pushing for an equaliser.

Another City attack comes down their right: after Jair Cunha’s clearing header from a deep Matheus Nunes cross, the ball loops up in the air before descending inside the home penalty area.

Ryan Yates, Forest’s old-fashioned midfield general, is being pressed from behind by the Argentina international Enzo Fernández as the ball drops. Yates stands his ground, using his strength to hold off his opponent’s muscular challenge. Fernández tries to impose himself, too, but ends up on the turf.

Not only does Yates win that crucial one-on-one, by cleverly improvising an overhead kick to shift the ball away from danger he inadvertently begins a counterattack that arguably changes the course of Forest’s season.

Liam Delap shepherds the ball to Morgan Gibbs-White. Enzo Maresca’s team are hopelessly exposed and Gibbs-White devours the space in front of him with a driving run.

He lays a pass off to Delap, who takes one touch before swerving a sensational shot into the far corner for 3-1. It is a memorable move that will surely compete for goal of the month. But none of it would have been possible without the dirty work of Yates: a show of strength and desire from the captain at the other end of the pitch.

Oliver Glasner understands the importance of the moment and sprints to celebrate with his players. After a barren scoring run Delap suddenly has two goals in one afternoon. The home fans are delirious. Twelve seconds after Yates won the duel, Forest have a two-goal cushion rather than a desperate fight to maintain their lead.

The second ball is a pleasingly old-school term but remains of paramount importance. Visit your local amateur club and you are likely to hear it being shouted as a motivational mantra.

At grassroots level it’s a reminder to teammates that finesse and creativity are all very well but it’s hard work, desire and a refusal to let your opponent get the better of you that win matches.

And it remains part of the elite game’s lexicon. Premier League managers sometimes point to an upcoming opponents’ second-ball prowess – a back-handed compliment if ever there was one. The hidden meaning: “They feed off scraps at set pieces. They don’t create from open play.”

“To be honest,” Rusk replied with amusing frankness, “I’m not sure some of them even know what second ball means.”

Managers like relying on data. They like predictable outcomes. They prefer to remove risk, reduce chaos and exert control over as much of the game as possible. But while the outcome of a one-on-one duel can never be certain, the consequences can often be far-reaching. That’s why second balls matter.

Win the first one if you can. But it’s the second ball that often makes the difference.

Mauricio Pochettino has been warned against taking the Manchester United job, by Gary Neville unless the club's hierarchy promise to back him in the January transfer window and bolster 'the worst back four in the Premier League'.

As reported by Daily Mail Sport, Pochettino is expected to be named as the new Red Devils head coach, replacing Michael Carrick. Carrick was pictured arriving at Carrington on Thursday morning before being sacked just hours later.

Director of football Jason Wilcox was pictured arriving to the club's training ground on Thursday morning too. He had been in Monaco the day before with the club’s chief executive Omar Berrada, where minority owner Sir Jim Ratcliffe ratified their decision to sack Carrick with the blessing of co-chairmen Joel and Avram Glazer.

United moved closer to putting Pochettino in charge at Old Trafford after reaching a verbal agreement with the US national team boss on Wednesday. The club then made a formal approach to US Soccer.

Upon Pochettino's expected arrival, he will join a club languishing in the relegation zone - with United 18th in the Premier League with just one win from their opening 10 matches.

United have conceded 19 goals across those 10 games - with only 14th-placed Ipswich Town (20) and bottom side Crystal Palace (24) having conceded more.

And speaking about Pochettino's pending arrival, Neville insists on the Argentine making ultimatums to the board if they want him - starting with improving the defence.

'If I was Mauricio Pochettino now, I would be literally protecting myself up to the high heaven,' he said on The Overlap.

'He'll do that with a pay-off. So do that in your contract. But secondly, he needs two defenders in January. So he's got to insist that he gets two defenders in January. He's got to insist that he gets a minimum budget next summer for the transfer we're not to be able to turn this around. He's got to insist that he gets a budget that follows.

'They have to give it to him or else he shouldn't take the job.'

Expanding further, the former United right back didn't hold back in his criticism of the current defensive options at the club.

'I mean look the back four that he finished with on Sunday was [Diogo] Dalot left back, [Noussair] Mazraoui right back, Leny Yoro and Ayden Heaven,' he said.

'That is probably the worst back four in the Premier League. I don't know a back four that is worse than that in the Premier League right now if I watch it. That is I can't believe Manchester United have ended up with that back four.'

Pochettino is currently at the US Soccer base outside Atlanta preparing for a CONCACAF Nations League quarter-final double header against Haiti, and is due to speak to the media on Friday.

It’s unclear if he will remain in charge for those games, with United said to be relaxed over the Argentine taking the team. It is possible he could stay for the first leg in Kingston, Jamaica at the weekend and then travel to Manchester.

The second leg in Cincinnati is not until the early hours of Thursday, and Pochettino may even would only take over at United on Friday with a huge game against Liverpool at Anfield 48 hours later.

It’s understood that the US Soccer Federation are ready for this contingency and have a succession plan in place should he leave.

It will cost Pochettino around £7million to buy himself out of his contract with the US Soccer Federation, with United ready to foot the bill.

The cost of bringing in his back-room team, including trusted assistants Jesus Perez and Miguel D’Agostino, will take the cost above £10m.

The 54-year-old only signed a new deal after the World Cup meaning that United could have got him for nothing in the summer.

Manchester United are closing in on a deal to appoint Mauricio Pochettino before their next game against Liverpool with Michael Carrick set to be sacked.

United must strike a deal with US Soccer, with a solution required over the United States’ next two international games in particular, while the club’s hierarchy is meeting at a Monaco summit to finalise a decision over the club’s manager.

Compensation, worth an initial $7m (£5.3m), will be a formality due to the clause in Pochettino’s existing contract, that runs until the 2030 World Cup.

While additional compensation will be required for Pochettino’s trusted coaching staff, including long-time assistant Jesus Perez.

The United States face Concacaf Nations League fixtures against Haiti during the current international break, with an away match on 14 November and a home match in Cincinnati on 18 November.

US Soccer are currently planning for Pochettino to be in charge of both games, though United will push to have Pochettino available sooner, with their next Premier League match against Liverpool at Anfield on 22 November.

Another option is potentially for Darren Fletcher to be handed an interim role once more, should a deal be unable to conclude in time.

While losing Pochettino would be a blow for the USA, The Independent understands that US Soccer has been prepared for the prospect of his exit at some stage before the 2030 World Cup, with a succession plan already in place.

Pochettino’s move back to England would be a major shift in his plans after committing to a four-year build to the 2030 World Cup, having overseen an encouraging, yet ultimately unsuccessful home World Cup this summer, ending in a heavy defeat to Belgium in the last 16.

While Pochettino admitted in September that he was “excited” for the journey to Spain, Portugal and Morocco, he conceded four years is a “long time” and that this commitment was “different”.

"We are serious people,” Pochettino told The Independent and other outlets in a wide-ranging interview. “Now it's a different thing. When we gave our word to the federation, it was about starting and finishing, after the World Cup. It's different, maybe, if you ask me now. Because now we commit to four years.

“Always it's different. Before, it was very easy to say 'OK, we start now, we prepare the team for the World Cup, we play the World Cup, and then we'll see.' [Leaving] before that, it was difficult. Because we committed before. For us, it was the moment to say 'if some club came, it's impossible'. Now it's another question.

“For us it was impossible to think about coming back, or going to another club. Because we received some calls from different clubs. But we cannot go away, first, because we didn't want to go. Always you can break a contract, say you stop, and that's it, you agree something. But in the moment I think it wasn't right for us to create all these situations and expectations.

"And now it's a four year contract, it's different. We can next season lose the Copa America or the Gold Cup, and they're not happy with us, they can sack us, or we can say, 'ok it's not in the way that we think'. Four years is different. Maybe now if someone calls in the future, it's a different, different type. We are excited, we are thinking to spend four years, I think to the next World Cup. But now it's a long period."

Fantasy Football Updated Rookie Report: Fernando Mendoza gets his shot

📅 Nov 11, 2026  |  Category: Sports  |  Read Source →

Nine weeks into the NFL season, we've seen enough from this rookie class to know who's for real, who's still finding their footing and who could make some noise down the stretch. From Carnell Tate's impressive start in Tennessee to Fernando Mendoza getting his opportunity in Las Vegas, here's my midseason check-in on 15 rookies, and what I'm expecting for the rest of the season.

Carnell Tate has been everything we hoped he would be — and then some. Through seven games, he's hauled in 34 receptions for 413 yards and a touchdown despite playing in one of the more inconsistent passing offenses in football. Cam Ward and the Tennessee Titans haven't exactly made life easy for their rookie receiver, but Tate continues to produce.

The talent is undeniable, the opportunity is there and the chemistry with Ward continues to develop. I expect Tate to build on what's already been a successful rookie campaign. He's a weekly fantasy starter with plenty of room to grow.

Jonah Coleman might be the rookie running back I'm most excited about for the second half of the season. He's rushed for 191 yards and three touchdowns while adding 13 receptions for another 128 yards. More importantly, look at how Denver is using him. He handled 21 touches in Week 9, and the coaching staff clearly trusts him.

With R.J. Harvey on injured reserve and J.K. Dobbins getting older, the opportunity is sitting right in front of Coleman. He's already shown he can contribute in the passing game and handle meaningful carries. I wouldn't be surprised if Coleman finishes as one of the most valuable rookie running backs in fantasy.

Omar Cooper Jr. missed time early in the season, but the moment he got healthy, he made his presence felt. Through four games, Cooper has 20 receptions for 306 yards and two touchdowns, including back-to-back 100-yard performances. In Week 9, he followed those outings with eight receptions for 58 yards while Garrett Wilson was sidelined.

What I've really liked is the rapport he's developing with Geno Smith. Cooper looks comfortable; he's earning opportunities and he's showing he can win in different areas of the field. Wilson's return could impact his volume, but I'm buying what we've seen. Cooper has legitimate weekly flex appeal moving forward.

KC Concepcion Jr. has quietly been one of the most impressive rookie wide receivers in football. He's sitting at 37 receptions for 437 yards and a touchdown, including 251 receiving yards over his last three games. What jumps off the screen is his ability to create after the catch. Get this young man the football in space and let him work.

The Browns haven't exactly provided consistent quarterback play, but Concepcion continues to find ways to produce. He's dynamic, explosive and already showing flashes of a player who could eventually become a Pro Bowl-caliber receiver. For fantasy purposes, he's worked his way into weekly flex consideration.

And how about Denzel Boston? The rookie has 507 receiving yards and three touchdowns on 32 receptions. Boston gives me some young Mike Evans vibes with his size, downfield ability and willingness to go make contested catches. He's already producing explosive plays despite the limitations of this Browns offense.

What's encouraging is that both Boston and Concepcion are earning significant opportunities without cannibalizing each other's production. Boston has legitimate big-play upside every single week and continues to look like one of the steals of the rookie class. I love what we've seen and expect a strong finish.

Germie Bernard's rookie season has been a little up and down, but I've seen enough to remain encouraged. He's caught 32 passes for 284 yards and a touchdown, highlighted by a phenomenal 11-reception performance in Week 6. The following two games weren't nearly as productive, but I wouldn't let that discourage you.

Bernard has established himself as a trusted option for Aaron Rodgers in a Pittsburgh offense that's been better than advertised. His role makes sense. Get him the football quickly, let him operate in space and allow his natural ability to take over. Bernard feels like a buy-low candidate with a solid second-half outlook.

I can't believe I'm putting Fernando Mendoza on this list, but here we are. The No. 1 overall pick is taking over as the Raiders' starting quarterback after Kirk Cousins was traded to Minnesota. Mendoza has attempted just eight regular-season passes, completing six for 48 yards, so we have almost nothing to evaluate.

But here's what we do know: Cousins threw 20 touchdowns in nine games and Mendoza gets to operate in that same Klint Kubiak offense featuring Brock Bowers. Listen, any quarterback throwing to Brock Bowers gets a touchdown bonus in my book. Add Ashton Jeanty, Trey Tucker and a potentially more favorable schedule down the stretch, and there's legitimate streaming appeal here. I'm not expecting Mendoza to be a league winner, but don't be surprised if he becomes fantasy relevant sooner than people think.

Jadarian Price is one of those players where the box score doesn't tell the entire story. He's rushed for 172 yards and added 83 receiving yards on 10 catches without scoring a touchdown. The production hasn't been there consistently, but every time he touches the football, you see the explosiveness. Unfortunately, injuries and ball-security issues have limited his opportunities.

With Zach Charbonnet still working his way back from a knee injury, there's a path for Price to earn a larger role. Seattle's offensive line should also improve as it gets healthier. I'm cautiously optimistic, but I need to see him string together consistent performances before trusting him in fantasy lineups.

Makai Lemon has been better than his rookie numbers might suggest. Through nine games, he's caught 37 passes for 312 yards and one touchdown, including 19 receptions for 185 yards over his last three games. The Eagles offense has been difficult to watch at times, and Lemon has dealt with inconsistent quarterback play while occasionally being asked to operate as the team's top receiver with DeVonta Smith unavailable.

What I've loved is his ability to separate, particularly against zone coverage. He's consistently finding openings and earning targets. The big fantasy performances haven't arrived yet, but the underlying usage is encouraging. There's more here than the box score suggests.

We finally got our first look at Jordyn Tyson, and while it wasn't perfect, there were encouraging signs. In his NFL debut, Tyson caught four passes for 30 yards but also lost a fumble on his first reception. I'm not worried about that. What matters is that New Orleans immediately looked to get him involved.

This Saints offense ranks among the league leaders in neutral-situation pass rate, meaning the opportunity should continue to be there. Getting Calvin Banks back should help Tyler Shough operate more comfortably behind that offensive line. The biggest question remains Tyson's health. If that hamstring holds up, I'm expecting his role to expand significantly.

Mike Washington Jr. gave us one of the more surprising rookie performances of the season when he handled 25 carries for 83 yards. Then he turned around the following week and rushed six times for -3 yards. That's been the problem. Washington has 275 rushing yards and a touchdown this season, but his role remains difficult to project.

Neither Washington nor Ashton Jeanty has consistently found success running behind this Raiders offensive line. Washington clearly has some ability, but can this coaching staff establish a defined role for him? Until that happens, he's difficult to trust. He's an interesting stash with upside if Jeanty misses time.

There's no bigger fantasy disappointment from this rookie class than Jeremiyah Love. And before anybody gets upset, I'm not putting all of that on the player. Love has rushed for 268 yards, added just 36 receiving yards and scored two total touchdowns in six games. We've seen flashes of the talent that made him such a highly regarded prospect, but the situation in Arizona has been a disaster.

The offense has struggled, the quarterback situation has deteriorated and now Love is sitting on injured reserve. If you invested a second-, third- or fourth-round fantasy pick in him, there's no way around it — it's been disappointing. The talent hasn't disappeared, but the fantasy return simply hasn't been there.

Antonio Williams is one of those rookies who could become much more relevant over the second half of the season. He's caught 26 passes for 284 yards and a touchdown, including eight receptions for 79 yards over his last two games. The problem hasn't necessarily been Williams. It's been the Commanders' offense.

Jayden Daniels continues to deal with a significant elbow injury and Washington hasn't been particularly creative offensively. We've also seen Williams have some unfortunate plays wiped away. With Terry McLaurin out, there should be additional opportunities available. He's not a weekly fantasy starter yet, but there's a path for Williams to become a useful flex option.

Emmett Johnson has quietly put together 348 yards from scrimmage and two total touchdowns, including that ridiculous 128-yard receiving performance against Denver in Week 8. We saw a 73-yard touchdown reception and a player who can absolutely make things happen with the football in his hands.

The problem? Kenneth Walker III has been phenomenal since arriving in Kansas City.

Johnson isn't taking that starting job anytime soon. But what if the Chiefs can turn him into a Jerick McKinnon-type receiving weapon? There's potential for standalone fantasy value in that role. And if Walker were to miss time, Johnson immediately becomes one of the most valuable backups in football.

Kaelon Black has 303 rushing yards and a touchdown this season, and I don't think enough people are paying attention to what he's doing. That's more rushing production than Jeremiyah Love, Mike Washington Jr. and Jadarian Price. The biggest obstacle is obvious: Christian McCaffrey.

But we've seen San Francisco utilize multiple backs at times, and keeping McCaffrey fresh has to remain a priority. Black has demonstrated that he can be an effective runner when given opportunities. The receiving game hasn't been a major part of his role, so there are limitations to his standalone fantasy value. Still, this is a player I want rostered heading into the second half of the season.

World Children's Day is a global day of action for children, run by UNICEF. In 2026, it falls on Friday, November 20. This year’s theme is “My day. My rights. My future.” It invites children to tell world leaders what kind of future they want.

The day celebrates children and the rights they’re born with. In many places, children “take over” high-profile roles in media, sports, and government for the day. Landmarks light up blue to show support for children's rights.

November 20 marks two milestones for children’s rights. On this date in 1959, the UN General Assembly adopted the Declaration of the Rights of the Child, a statement of shared ideals. The Convention sets out the rights every child has, including the right to be safe, to play, and to have their opinions taken seriously. Nearly every country has formally agreed to follow it, and it’s the most widely ratified human rights treaty in history.
