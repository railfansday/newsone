McCord Needs Run Game Help Against Jets On Sunday

📅 Nov 26, 2026  |  Category: Sports  |  Read Source →

Kyle McCord has a chance to reset his rookie season, but the path forward is narrow. The Miami Dolphins face the New York Jets on Sunday, Nov. 29, at Hard Rock Stadium. McCord just endured a brutal outing against the Bills, completing only 12 of 33 passes for 102 yards. He also threw an interception and took eight hits while getting sacked twice. That performance highlighted a team that has lost four straight games since its lone win. The Dolphins need a better plan than just hoping the quarterback improves on his own.

The offense has stalled because the run game has disappeared. Since beating the Jets on Oct. 25, Miami has failed to rush for 100 yards in any single game. This offensive drought lines up perfectly with the current four-game losing streak. McCord does not need to be a hero to give the team a chance. He simply needs protection and a running back who can move the pile. If the Dolphins cannot run the football, the rookie could face another long afternoon against a defense that will be ready.

The New York Jets remember their first meeting well, even though they lost that day. In Week 7, Miami built a 21-7 lead and held on for a 28-25 victory. In the first half, the Jets recorded 114 yards while the Dolphins amassed 231 total net yards. They also owned the clock with 21:09 of possession compared to just 8:51 for New York. Jets head coach Aaron Glenn admitted the difficulty of that start. "They punched us in the mouth in the first quarter," HC Aaron Glenn said. That lesson sticks with a team that now sits at 3-8.

Geno Smith and his offense are trying to fix their issues after a solid win against the Chargers. During his most recent start, Smith completed 17-of-21 passes for 176 yards and 3 TDs. He led long scoring drives and scored points on 5 of 6 possessions. The Jets want to start fast this time, especially since Smith grew up near the stadium. According to "We want to start fast first and foremost,", Smith, a Miramar, FL native, grew up roughly 20 minutes away from Hard Rock Stadium. The Dolphins cannot afford to let the Jets control the game early.

Dolphins coach Jeff Hafley believes the extra time in practice is helping McCord. The rookie has now started two games and faced different defenses in Indianapolis and Buffalo. Hafley sees the value in these reps as the team prepares for Sunday. "I think the reps that he's getting in practice," Hafley said. "The chemistry he's developing with the guys, whether it's the offensive line and the huddle, the wide receivers and the timing. I think that's all going to be continued improvement." The coach added that McCord is the type of guy who grows from both success and failure.

The Dolphins are not asking McCord to reinvent the wheel or make impossible plays. They just need competent quarterback play and the ball to stay in his hands. The Jets have been wildly inconsistent this season, which gives Miami a real opening. But the offense must execute better than it did against the Bills. The next game is Sunday, Nov. 29, at 1 p.m.

Cowboys vs. Eagles prediction: Dallas looks for first win streak of 2026 vs. ex-QB on Thanksgiving

📅 Nov 26, 2026  |  Category: Sports  |  Read Source →

If not listed, please contact your TV provider.

FRISCO, Texas -- The Dallas Cowboys (5-6) have the chance to do something they haven't done yet in the 2026 season against the Philadelphia Eagles (3-7) on Thanksgiving Day: win consecutive games.

The only time the Cowboys have won back-to-back games under head coach Brian Schottenheimer came back in his first season in 2025 following their Week 10 bye. That was a stretch of three victories in a row against Las Vegas Raiders (33-16), the Eagles (24-21) and the Kansas City Chiefs (31-28) on Thanksgiving Day.

After Dallas' 51-30 victory over the Tennessee Titans (1-9) on Sunday, Cowboys owner and general manager Jerry Jones has some hope that his squad can make a postseason push down the stretch. That's just days after Jones called his Cowboys "a bad team" in the wake of their 46-40 home loss against the San Francisco 49ers (9-1) in Week 10.

"It gives me hope that we can do some of the things that we wanted to do," Jones said postgame on Sunday. "We needed this kind of thing. Any team does. We needed to feel it and get a little wind under our wings, so to speak."

Quarterback Dak Prescott looks to continue his high-level play on Thanksgiving. If he replicated his Sunday performance against the Titans (325 yards passing and four touchdowns on 28-of-33 passing with a rushing touchdown), that would give Prescott 19 passing touchdowns on Thanksgiving, the record for the most Thanksgiving Day touchdowns in NFL history. Prescott's predecessor Tony Romo and Matthew Stafford currently share the record with 18 Turkey Day passing touchdowns each.

"It's a blessing. It's something that, one of my favorite games of the year," Prescott said Tuesday. "And so, just being able to put that uniform on with the John Madden seal on it, understanding the whole world's watching and how many little kids are in the position that I once was: watching the game and then ready to go outside and play my own version of it. ... I don't take it for granted."

However, there's one Cowboy who doesn't look forward to Thanksgiving: defensive tackle Quinnen Williams. The four-time Pro Bowler doesn't enjoy the holiday because it goes against his in-season training and diet regimen. Williams' go-to Thanksgiving side dish is macaroni, something that is hard to resist this time of year.

"It's during the season. I'm trying to stay lean and trim, and it's a holiday right around when real football starts to come in," said Williams. "So I'm not a big fan of Thanksgiving."

That's okay, because there will be plenty of fans ready to see Williams on the field, especially against starting Eagles quarterback and former Cowboy Joe Milton. Dallas acquired Milton in a trade with the New England Patriots in April 2025, and he worked as one of Prescott's backups until the Eagles signed him off the Cowboys' practice squad on Oct. 17. That's the same day Philadelphia placed Super Bowl MVP quarterback Jalen Hurts (blood clots) on injured reserve.

"Haha, it's good to see him," said Cowboys wide receiver CeeDee Lamb. "I hope he don't go crazy."

Prescott is cooking right now. He became the fourth player in NFL history with 300-plus passing yards and five-plus total touchdowns in consecutive games across Weeks 10 and 11, per CBS Sports Research. He joined Daunte Culpepper (2004), Tom Brady (2007) and Ben Roethlisberger (2014) in this exclusive club. After racking up 1,003 yards passing, 11 touchdowns and just a single interception over the last three weeks, Prescott now ranks second in the league in passing yards (3,049) and second in passing touchdowns (25). He also ranks eighth in touchdown-to-interception ratio (25-6) and passer rating (101.0) and fourth in expected points added (EPA) per dropback (0.21) this season.

Prescott credited his historic production to being able to play "balanced football" the last two weeks, with the Cowboys averaging the 12th-most rushing yards per game (125.5) since Week 10.

"It really just opens up the whole playbook," Prescott said Thursday. "So it's a credit to all of it just, in that sense, coming together. Guys up front being physical in the run game, which then allows them to pass block a little bit easier there. And guys outside just getting open, making my job easier."

In their 51-30 Week 11 win over the Titans, Schottenheimer provided Prescott and Co. their highest motion rate (63.5%) and play-action rate (58.3%) of the season, weeks after getting away from those staples through the first quarter of the year.

Cowboys Pro Bowl wide receivers Lamb (1,108 receiving yards, the second most in the NFL) and George Pickens (780 receiving yards, the 11th most in the NFL) going up against the Eagles All-Pro cornerback tandem of Quinyon Mitchell and Cooper DeJean will be the key matchups on Thursday. However, Mitchell's status was uncertain because of a hamstring injury. The Eagles held walkthroughs on Monday and Tuesday, and they upgraded Mitchell to limited on Tuesday after he didn't participate on Monday.

Philadelphia defensive coordinator Vic Fangio said Mitchell will be "fine" for Thanksgiving, and the Eagles listed him without a game status on the team's final injury report after he practiced fully on Wednesday, indicating he will indeed play.

When the Eagles have the football, they'll definitely be looking to play ground-and-pound football with 2024 NFL Offensive Player of the Year running back Saquon Barkley. Barkley is currently on a streak of four consecutive games with a rushing touchdown, the longest streak of his career. The Cowboys have the worst run defense in football, allowing 151.5 rushing yards per game, and they struggle against Barkley's agile running style.

Both Schottenheimer and Williams described Barkley as being a "jump cut" running back with how smoothly he can hit the hole and then glide to a different spot along the line of scrimmage and in the open field. Williams compared Barkley stylistically to Tampa Bay Buccaneers running back Bucky Irving, who went off for 165 yards and a touchdown on 21 carries in a 24-16 upset over the Cowboys in Week 5. Irving averaged a career-high 7.9 yards per carry that night at AT&T Stadium. Williams said the key is to stop Barkley's feet before he gets going.

Williams also presents a challenge for Philadelphia as he leads the NFL with 18 tackles for loss, the most in a season by a defensive tackle since 2022. He also leads all defensive tackles in quarterback pressures (40) and co-leads his position in forced fumbles (4) along with Eagles defensive tackle Jalen Carter. Yet, he feels he can still find higher levels of play with the Cowboys defense allowing 20-plus points in 18 consecutive games, the longest active streak in the league. That's the longest such streak since the 2019-2021 Jacksonville Jaguars' run of 25 straight games, per NFL Research.

Milton earned his first career win as an NFL starting quarterback on Sunday in the Eagles' 19-16 home victory over the Pittsburgh Steelers that snapped the team's seven-game losing streak. He completed 11 of his 21 throws for 141 passing yards while rushing for 7 yards on five carries. Prescott said he saw Milton develop most in Dallas on his mental side of the game and preparation as it pertains to pass protection adjustments and discernment at the line of scrimmage.

"Joe is amazing, man. The athleticism that he has, the speed that he has, the arm talent that he has, he can do it all," Williams said. "So it's super dope to be able to go against him, especially after all the laughs and things we had in the locker room during training camp practices."

The present iteration of the Eagles lacks the offensive artillery to win a duel with Prescott, Lamb and Pickens. That's especially true if Mitchell is unable to suit up on Thursday. Prescott is on a 20-game home winning streak against NFC East opponents, which is tied with Tom Brady (versus the AFC East) for the longest home winning streak by a starting quarterback against divisional opponents since the 1970 AFL/NFL merger. He'll break the tie with Brady with his 21st such victory in a row on Thanksgiving.

2026 Concacaf W Championship: What to know, how to watch as USWNT aim to punch ticket to World Cup

Just when you think you're going to settle in for a long winter's nap, there's more high-stakes women's soccer to watch. The 2026 Concacaf W Championship paves the way to the 2027 FIFA Women's World Cup in Brazil and the 2028 Summer Olympics, and eight national teams from the region will play a knockout bracket starting Friday, Nov. 27.

With World Cup and Olympic berths decided along the way, fans can watch all of the 2026 Concacaf W Championship on CBS Sports Golazo Network and Paramount+.

Here's everything you need to know about the upcoming tournament:

All times Eastern -- all games will air on Paramount+

Saturday, Dec. 5 (third-place match and final)

The Concacaf W Championship is a senior women's national team championship tournament that doubles as the region's qualifier for both the World Cup and the Olympics.

This year's edition has a new format that sends teams to the 2027 World Cup and 2028 Los Angeles Olympics. The biggest change is the quarterfinal bracket. The top two ranked teams, the United States and Canada, earned byes into the tournament. Jamaica, Costa Rica, Mexico, Panama, Haiti, and El Salvador all earned spots through the Concacaf W Qualifiers.

Back in 2022, eight finalists were placed into two groups of four, with the top two teams in each advancing into the semifinals and clinching World Cup spots. Third-place teams were given a chance to qualify in the inter-confederation playoffs. 2022 was also the first year the competition served as both a World Cup and Olympic qualifier, but only the champion and the winner of a two-leg playoff clinched Olympic berths.

This year, the group stages are gone, and a single quarterfinal will decide who qualifies directly. In 2026, the quarterfinal winners all earn a place in the 2027 World Cup, and quarterfinal losers compete in a play-in round, with the two winners earning spots in the inter-confederation playoffs in February 2027.

The two Concacaf W Championship finalists and the third-place finisher qualify for the 2028 Summer Games. The United States are hosts of the 2028 Olympics and qualify automatically, so if they finish in the top three, the fourth-place team takes the remaining Olympic spot.

The U.S. women's national team will face El Salvador for the first time in program history. El Salvador are the USWNT's first new opponent since playing Zambia in the Olympic group stage in July 2024.

The USWNT step into the competition as reigning Concacaf W Championship winners. They've won the last three tournaments and hold a record nine all-time titles. The USWNT won all five of their games in the 2022 W Championship without conceding a goal and outscored opponents 13-0. The USWNT are 106W-1D-0L in matches against teams ranked outside of FIFA's Top 25, drawing only to Costa Rica 0-0 in July 2024 in Washington, D.C.

Manager Emma Hayes named a 23-player roster for the tournament, but recently made some changes following the 2026 NWSL Championship. Trinity Rodman, who sustained a shoulder injury, has been replaced by Jaedyn Shaw, and Shaw will be replaced by Lia Godfrey on the U-23 roster.

Kansas City Current defender Laney Rouse was also ruled out of the U-23 roster and is replaced by Denver Summit FC defender Natalie Means.

El Salvador have never qualified for a Women's World Cup and will make their debut at the Concacaf W Championship. They qualified for the tournament by winning Group F with three clean sheets against Honduras, Barbados, and Trinidad & Tobago.

8 2000s Sitcom Lines That Have Aged Terribly

📅 Nov 25, 2026  |  Category: Sports  |  Read Source →

The best sitcoms of the 2000s are still some of the most beloved and rewatched shows today, with Friends and The Office finding new life in the streaming era. Sitcoms are interesting sociological windows into the eras in which they were produced, using humor to both reinforce and challenge prevailing social norms.

That’s why sitcoms often age poorly—as culture changes, so too does the line between what's funny and what's offensive. Of course, there's an important distinction between a sitcom character and the show itself being problematic. Sometimes sitcoms use a character’s outdated views to make a point, like Archie Bunker in the eternally relevant All in the Family. However, some of the most poorly aged sitcom lines aren't uttered by deliberately offensive characters, but by ostensibly nice guys audiences were encouraged to root for, like Leonard over Howard in The Big Bang Theory.

It's unreasonable to expect sitcoms that premiered upwards of two decades ago to adhere to contemporary social sensibilities. Even some of the best sitcoms of all time have their shortcomings in that regard. But what's particularly interesting about revisiting 2000s sitcoms that haven't aged well is that it's not always the most provocative jokes that have aged the worst. Sometimes, it's the seemingly innocuous lines that are the most jarring.

Part of the enduring humor of King of the Hill is the disconnect between Hank Hill's conservative sensibilities and his son's more unconventional interests. The animated sitcom frequently acknowledges that Hank is a little behind the times, but his attitude toward gender roles in "The Boy Can't Help It" feels particularly antiquated.

Hank is horrified to learn that his son Bobby is letting a girl take the initiative in their relationship, from planning their dates to picking him up. His immediate conclusion that Bobby is a girl reinforces the idea that masculinity is intrinsically tied to assuming a dominant role in romantic relationships, while femininity is synonymous with passivity.

Hank Hill is a great dad, and the episode where he gets sucked into the "Manosphere" in the King of the Hill revival proves why he's a role model.

While Hank's discomfort is itself a source of comedy, the episode's central conflict feels like something that could just as easily have been featured in an '80s sitcom, despite airing in 2009. There's something especially dated about the implication that a boy should feel emasculated rather than flattered by a girl who actively pursues him.

Sitcoms have always offered a fascinating anthropological window into evolving social norms, particularly around fatherhood. The transition from the traditional conception of fathers as breadwinners to more active and equal parenting partners is especially apparent when revisiting family sitcoms from the 2000s — look at Phil compared to Jay in Modern Family.

Ray isn't simply trying to shirk an unpleasant household chore; he's essentially rejecting the responsibilities of fatherhood while enjoying its privileges. What was once played as an exaggerated version of a relatable marital squabble leaves a particularly sour taste today, when audiences are increasingly critical of the unequal distribution of emotional and domestic labor.

Charlie Harper was unapologetically hedonistic in Two and a Half Men, and both the character and the sitcom were acutely aware of it. Alan, despite being considerably more neurotic than his brother, was often just as selfish. He simply made selfishness look a lot less fun.

Alan's shortcomings as a father are perfectly encapsulated in a moment that has since become a meme. When Judith needs Alan to take Jake for an entire week rather than just the weekend, the additional parenting responsibilities prove too much for him. Midweek, Alan can't shake the feeling that he's forgotten something, prompting Charlie to reassure him that it couldn't have been important. The episode cuts to Jake waiting to be picked up from soccer in the dark and pouring rain.

Two & a Half Men had iconic casting with Charlie Sheen and Jon Cryer. Here’s who could bring a fresh take on Chuck Lorre’s classic sitcom characters.

It's a well-executed visual gag, but the underlying joke takes Alan from an occasionally inept father to an outright negligent one. As cultural attitudes toward co-parenting have evolved, the sitcom trope of fathers being comically incapable of managing responsibilities that mothers are expected to handle without complaint has lost much of its comedic appeal.

Almost everything about Ross Geller gives contemporary audiences the ick, from his relationship with one of his students to his ill-advised attempt to kiss his cousin. While Ross's general sexism has been extensively scrutinized in the years since Friends ended, his casual homophobia is often overlooked.

In season 9, Ross and Rachel are struggling to find a suitable nanny for Emma when they meet a seemingly perfect candidate, Sandy (Freddie Prinze Jr.), who happens to be a man. However, Ross is fundamentally incapable of processing why a heterosexual man would want to be a nanny, assuming that Sandy must be gay or “at least” bisexual, one of many infamous Friends moments that aged poorly.

There's something particularly frustrating about watching a father take issue with another man's aptitude for childcare. Ross's attitude perpetuates two equally reductive stereotypes: that nurturing is an inherently feminine quality and that men who exhibit traditionally feminine qualities must be attracted to other men. It's an even worse look for Ross than his excessively whitened teeth.

Howard Wolowitz's behavior toward women is one of the more overtly problematic aspects of The Big Bang Theory, but the sitcom is generally self-aware about his sleaziness. Similarly, Sheldon's frequently offensive remarks are largely attributed to his lack of social awareness.

Leonard, meanwhile, is positioned as the group's comparatively well-adjusted nice guy, whom audiences are supposed to root for in his pursuit of Penny. However, a telling line buried in the sitcom's second episode exposes an uncomfortable similarity between Leonard and Howard.

Leonard essentially admits that being nice to Penny is advantageous because it doesn't diminish his chances of sleeping with her. So, while the character may possess more social grace than Howard, his attitude toward women is arguably just as transactional.

The line also retroactively colors much of Leonard and Penny's relationship differently. Rather than presenting a compelling alternative to the stereotypical alpha male, Leonard inadvertently embodies another problematic archetype: the self-proclaimed nice guy who views basic decency toward women as an investment with an anticipated romantic or sexual payoff.

Throughout The Office's nine-season run, the show took some big comedic swings — not all of which have aged particularly well, such as Michael Scott's racially insensitive "Ping" character. However, one of the sitcom's most distasteful jokes comes from Kelly Kapoor instead.

When Kelly is confronted about falsifying Jim and Dwight's customer survey results, she attempts to evade accountability by falsely claiming that she was raped. Michael's response implies that Kelly has used this tactic so often that it's become a trademark for her.

What's particularly uncomfortable about the exchange is how inconsequential the situation is that Kelly is trying to extricate herself from. The joke isn't simply that Kelly is willing to lie to get herself out of trouble, but that she considers an allegation of sexual assault a convenient excuse for even the most trivial workplace transgressions.

It's not reasonable to expect sitcoms from nearly a quarter of a century ago to approach sensitive subjects with the same nuance that audiences expect in 2026. However, the British workplace sitcom The IT Crowd took transphobia beyond a punchline and into an actual punch in its third-season episode, "The Speech.”

Douglas Reynholm finds a seemingly perfect romantic partner in April, with whom he shares numerous interests. However, when he realizes that April is transgender, having previously misunderstood her disclosure, their breakup escalates into a physical altercation. What makes the sequence particularly egregious is that the violence itself is played for laughs. The episode of The IT Crowd was eventually removed from Channel 4's streaming service following complaints about its transphobic content.

Even by the standards of its era, the episode's treatment of its transgender character is remarkably cruel, making Friends' notoriously insensitive treatment of Chandler's father look comparatively respectful. The comedy could have been derived entirely from Douglas's ignorance without turning violence against a transgender woman into physical comedy.

Barney Stinson’s playbook is cartoonishly problematic, but How I Met Your Mother is largely cognizant of Barney’s reprehensible behavior. Ted Mosby, conversely, is positioned as the romantic hero and audience surrogate even more explicitly than Leonard in The Big Bang Theory​​​​​​, literally narrating his romantic exploits to both his children and the audience.

However, there's something arguably more insidious about Ted's behavior because, unlike Barney, he's unwilling to acknowledge his own manipulative tendencies. For someone so preoccupied with finding love, Ted is remarkably adept at finding petty reasons to discard women who don't conform to his romantic ideals.

Ted's declaration that loving someone means never stopping is framed as a profound romantic sentiment, but it also encapsulates everything wrong with his approach to relationships. It leaves little room for respecting another person's boundaries or accepting that a relationship has run its course.

Ironically, Ted's romanticism is often the biggest impediment to his finding love. He’s perpetually chasing a fantasy rather than appreciating the actual women in his life.

Revenge may be a dish best served cold, but when your target is a giant black bear, you’d better bring a perfect plan—or you may end up as the appetizer.

That’s the premise of Jon Finkel’s latest novel, Bear Brawl, an outrageously well-thought-out mix of modern training methodology soaked with an over-the-top ’80s action movie scenario that every Stallone and Schwarzenegger film fan has wanted answered: Is it possible to plan a fight against a rogue, 450-pound bear—the same unrepentant ursid that killed Brick Bridger’s mother and left his brother in a wheelchair—and win in a hand-to-hand duel?

As an ’80s expert who speaks fluent Roadhouse, I was able to read Bear Brawl in one sitting—something I’d never done with a book before—and that’s exactly the experience Finkel was aiming for. It’s also why the book would absolutely make a solid stocking stuffer this Christmas. Picture Bear Brawl as Rambo, John Matrix, and Dalton, all trained by Mr. Miyagi.

That brings us to Brick Bridger, a beast in his own right. He’s an Arkansas-bred 6’6”, 265-pound former UFC heavyweight and two-time collegiate wrestling champion who wasn’t around the night “Macho,” the quarter-ton Colossus, killed his mother and crippled his brother. He’s been living with that guilt ever since, convinced none of it would have happened had he been there.

“He’s the perfect human specimen,” Finkel said. “Brick’s a two-time NCAA wrestling champion, with eight fights in the UFC heavyweight division before retiring because of injury. And a rogue black bear—the one night he’s late to a barbecue—kills his mom and puts his brother in a wheelchair. So now we have the man, we have the means, we have the motivation.”

Instead of spiraling, he uses his depression as fuel to take on Macho. Only with the help of several experts and a commitment to a plan does the idea of fighting the bear seem, well… still ridiculous. But somewhat more attainable than going into the woods carrying nothing but rage.

For Finkel, an M&F contributing writer and editor who witnessed fight camps with UFC legends such as Brock Lesnar, Randy Couture, and Frank Mir, the novel is more than a tribute to the ’80s action adventure genre. There’s also a bit of strategy readers can incorporate into their own goal-setting, without actually having to grapple a grizzly.

“Turn your grief or stress into a healthy challenge,” he said. “A lot of people, they’ll start triathlons. They’ll do Ironmans to get over something, to occupy their mind. In this case, it’s not an Ironman or a marathon. It’s to fight a bear.”

Whether it’s losing weight, running a marathon or staying true to a 12-week training program for a BJJ tournament, each of these will have its own set of obstacles that may appear overwhelming at certain points. Like Bridger, with the right help and strategy, even the most ridiculous of challenges can become attainable—on paper at least.

1. Choose a Goal that Gives Your Training Direction

A clear target can turn scattered and inconsistent workouts into preparation with purpose. Whatever your type of activity you’re into, select an event or milestone that excites you, then break it into manageable steps. Study its requirements, plan your training, then execute and track your progress.

“You take a task and whether it’s impossible or not—or you think it’s impossible—and you train all your energies toward it,” Finkel says. “Yours may be completing an Ironman or marathon. Brick’s was fighting a bear.”

2. Find the Obstacle—and Make a Plan for It

No matter what your skill level is, there will come a point in which your progress stalls. When it happens, don’t assume that the solution is simply to work harder. Take inventory of what may be the cause of your plateau. Is it a physical weakness? Technique? Consistency? Recovery? Brick understood the challenge and searched for an opening. For your fitness goals, it could mean hiring a coach, making program adjustments, or even giving yourself more time to build your skillset.

“You got to know your enemy, and you have to be humble,” Finkel says. “He looks until he eventually finds the Achilles’ heel. Maybe there is always a way.”

3. Work on Weak Points While Still Enhancing Your Strengths

A smart plan is a combination of making improvements while also capitalizing on your primary abilities. Brick used his own size and MMA skills as the foundation while also putting in the work in the “classroom” to search for new ways to victory. For you, build on your strengths, too, from choosing a training style that fits your abilities, then working and progressing on the secondary skills that will help get the job done.

“Lean into your strength,” Finkel says. “Brick’s not an in-his-prime Mike Tyson knocking out guys in the first round. Every fight is now a grind for him. I even did this myself—I was trying to do triathlons, and I realized the only thing I actually like is swimming. So I just became a really fast swimmer and forgot the two parts I hated. As soon as I leaned into the thing I was best at, I enjoyed it so much more. So pick one and be really good at it.”

A go-it-alone strategy heavily reliant on brute strength and past personal achievements ends in disaster for nearly all of us. For Brick, filling the gaps for this task meant incorporating experts who knew the right tendencies and could spot openings and weak spots the untrained eye would overlook. So when it comes to goal achievement, it helps to find a coach, training partner, or knowledgeable source who can add to your preparations, spot problems, and offer candid feedback.

“Brick had a zoologist and a wrestling coach explaining the ways it can’t work,” Finkel says. “Being creative is one thing, but being resourceful within that creativity can really make a project like this work.”

An outrageous goal will take longer than you expect, and there will be stretches where it looks like you’re losing. Brick’s MMA motor keeps him moving forward when every fight becomes a struggle. He keeps working his way toward that small but decisive opening he knows is there but isn’t quite visible yet.

“He doesn’t quit,” Finkel says. “Brick might not be the most technically sound guy. He might not even be the strongest. He’s the guy with the motor, the size, and the will to keep figuring things out until he can exploit the weakness. Most people’s goals die right at the point where it stops feeling good—keep grinding until it does.”

Leny Yoro’s one-match ban has been lifted by the FA after Manchester United successfully appealed against his sending-off at Liverpool on Sunday, despite Pro Ref boss Howard Webb believing it was the right decision.

Yoro was shown a straight red card by referee Sam Barrott in the eighth minute for a challenge on Alexander Isak, and United’s 10 men went on to lose 4-0 in Mauricio Pochettino’s first game in charge.

The club launched an appeal over the decision and the FA have confirmed that Yoro will now be available for Saturday’s game against Brentford at Old Trafford.

A statement read: ‘Leny Yoro’s one-match suspension has been removed following a successful claim for wrongful dismissal. The Manchester United player was sent off for denying an obvious goal scoring opportunity during their Premier League match against Liverpool on Sunday 22 November. The Independent Regulatory Commission’s written reasons for the decision will be published in due course.’

Barrott sent off Yoro for pushing Isak in the back on the edge of the penalty box, and Dominik Szoboszlai scored Liverpool’s first goal from the free kick.

However, United insisted that the Frenchman did not deny Isak a clear goalscoring opportunity because goalkeeper Senne Lammens would have reached the ball first.

Leny Yoro has seen his red card suspension overturned following an appeal by Man United

The overturned decision, contradicts the opinion of Webb who backed Barrott's on-field verdict, when speaking on the Match Officials Mic'd Up show.

'Firstly, I want to say, I think this is a really decisive decision by the referee, Sam Barrott. We know it's clearly a foul. I don't think there's any dispute about that. And we also know that it's outside the penalty area. We're thinking about the sanction, aren't we that's why we're talking about this.

'Is it a denial of an obvious goal scoring opportunity? Four considerations officials have to think about. Direction of play, it's definitely going towards the goal. It's not going out wide. Proximity from goal, well, it's just outside the penalty area. The location of other defenders. Well, there's only Yoro there. The keeper is moving back towards his own goal.

'He has to put the brakes on and possibly come back out. The reason we're talking about it is the fourth consideration, that's control or likelihood of keeping on or gaining control of the ball. Isak's got control of the ball, he's got possession, he's got the touch, he knocks it past Yoro. He's moving at pace at that moment, and a foul comes in and knocks him down.

'And there's a question mark, I guess, around whether or not he would get onto that ball before Senne Lammens. Now, in the moment, the referee looked at it and felt he would.

'That was the referee's call made on the field, and then the VAR checks that to see whether that decision made on the field was clearly and obviously incorrect, and formed the opinion that it wasn't, that if there was any doubt, then it was doubt.

'It wasn't going to be something that was clearly and obviously wrong and therefore allowed that decisive on-field decision to stand. and i think by and large, most people would see that as a correct decision, with Isak moving quickly on towards that ball. The alternative view is that Lammens might get there first, but i think it's a little bit debatable, I guess. Certainly not clearly wrong.'

Welcome to Manchester United, Mauricio. For a man who had coveted the United job for years, this was an ignominious introduction. Mauricio Pochettino got a 13-minute crash course in the craziness of his new club. His first game was lost before he made his first substitution, and that came as early as the 22nd-minute. His debut brought the joint heaviest ever defeat by a United manager in his first game in charge, and the biggest in 100 years.

“So painful,” Pochettino said. “So disappointed.” As can be the way with United of late, most things that could go wrong did. When their new head coach’s name was sung, it was by the Kop. “Poch is at the wheel,” they taunted. A first meaningful day of work showed the scale of the task to steer United in the right direction.

The team who exhibited the pace and energy of the best Pochettino sides were Liverpool, relishing the chance to compound their rivals’ problems. Florian Wirtz scored a glorious goal, Bradley Barcola kept demonstrating a sprinter’s speed and Rio Ngumoha illuminated Anfield with his precocity. Yet each was aided by United, hapless and hopeless, showing it is easier to change the manager than to reverse one of the most extraordinary declines in English football history.

It was as though Pochettino’s charges had decided to show him a snapshot of the problems: their self-destructive streak, their defensive failings, their capacity to concede in quick succession. No previous Premier League manager had seen his side both let in two goals and go down to 10 men in the first half of his opening game. Pochettino’s United accomplished that in a quarter of an hour. Suffice to say there was no honeymoon period.

The watching Sir Alex Ferguson had lost his first game in charge, 2-0 at Oxford 40 years ago. Pochettino topped that, in the wrong way. Only Lal Hilditch, in 1926, had begun his reign as badly, with another 4-0 loss.

And yet there was a continuity to United’s calamity. Meet the new boss, same as the old boss? Despite regime change, some of this was very familiar. United were two goals behind after 18 minutes against Roma, after four against Villa, after 13 against Liverpool. United got rid of Michael Carrick, kept the masterplan of going 2-0 down and discovered it has certain flaws.

This was scarcely an unlucky 13 for United. Rather, they were culpable in their own downfall. Matthijs de Ligt needlessly left Ronald Araujo’s long ball, Alexander Isak ran behind the United defence and Leny Yoro barged him over. “It was an unfair situation,” complained Pochettino. “It was clear that was not a red card.” Iraola disagreed. “It is clearly the last man, clearly a foul,” he said.

So Yoro was sent off by referee Sam Barrott and waved off by the Liverpool faithful. The punishment for United was doubled when Dominik Szoboszlai drilled his free kick past a ramshackle wall and in. Chants of “going down” rang around Anfield. It was replaced, a few minutes later, by “sacked in the morning”.

With wonderful improvisation, Wirtz hooked in a volley after Ngumoha sped past Diogo Dalot and dinked in a cross. If a criticism was that the £100m German had influenced too few major matches in his Liverpool career, he altered this, brilliantly, and ended with an assist to accompany his second goal in as many games. “He's in a lovely moment,” said Iraola.

Liverpool slowed down at the end of the first half and then sped up again after the break. Barcola’s profligate finishing had cost him Liverpool’s third goal but his extraordinary pace created it. The £106m man’s scorched-earth solo run was followed by a pass to allow Ngumoha to replace Michael Owen as Liverpool’s youngest ever scorer against United. The teenager had tormented Dalot, but he was not the only winger on the scoresheet.

Victor Munoz exchanged passes with Wirtz and curled in the fourth after Liverpool’s high pressing was shown as Virgil van Dijk robbed Joshua Zirkzee, 25 yards from the United goal. Iraola ended doing fist pumps to the Kop, Jurgen Klopp-style.

“We probably should have scored more than four,” Iraola said. Senne Lammens saved, from Isak, Alexis Mac Allister and Szoboszlai. Harry Maguire made a brilliant block to turn an Isak shot away. The Swede slashed a shot wide after De Ligt inexplicably missed the ball for a second time.

It reflected badly on the new manager. Perhaps Pochettino’s first big call was to bring in De Ligt for a first league appearance of 2026. That backfired. Meanwhile, Maguire was first dropped and then summoned.

Pochettino had to keep changing tack. As Liverpool scored four goals, United used four formations. Pochettino seemed to begin by borrowing Ruben Amorim’s 3-4-3, then it looked a 4-2-3-1. Then it was neither, with United down to 10 men. “After what happened, happened and the plan was in the bin,” said Pochettino, who went to first 4-4-1 then 5-3-1, and who tried to argue what he felt was an injustice of a red card rendered what followed rather irrelevant. “We concede four goals with 10 men, and in a circumstance after playing 80 minutes at Anfield,” he said. But the circumstances are that United are in the relegation zone, lost on Pochettino’s bow, and were walloped by their rivals.
