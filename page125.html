📅 Nov 04, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

President Donald Trump has been posting through the U.S. midterm elections, and he doesn't appear happy with the results.

Millions of Americans went to the polls on Tuesday, casting their vote to choose their members of Congress. Democrats decisively took control of both the Senate and House of Representatives in a sweeping midterm victory, one which featured several upsets. South Carolina elected Annie Andrews as its first Democratic senator in 28 years. Texas voted in James Talarico as its first Democratic senator in over 30 years. Even Kansas swung blue, with Adam Hamilton becoming the first Democrat elected to its Senate seat in 94 years.

Still, it's possible that nobody was more upset than Trump. The U.S. president characteristically took to his social media platform Truth Social on election night, expressing his displeasure with the midterm's results as they came in.

Trump's day started off well, the president posting at 12:07 p.m. ET / 9:07 a.m. PT that Guam had been called as a Republican victory. Guam has elected Republican Tony Ada as governor, taking the position from the Democrats for the first time since 2014.

"Guam, which has always been considered strongly Democrat, just had an early call for a REPUBLICAN Victory in the top spot, Governor — Congratulations to Tony Ada!" he wrote. "Also, the Republicans have the Majority in the State Senate. Wow, hopefully that’s an indication for things to come!"

However, Guam's election was separate from the U.S. midterm elections, and had no impact on the makeup of the Senate or House. Once the midterm results began rolling in, the Republicans' situation began looking less rosy.

At 4:11 p.m. ET / 1:11 p.m. PT, Trump shared a screenshot from right-wing YouTuber Benny Johnson in which he claimed that "Republicans are currently showing signs of PRESIDENTIAL-LEVEL midterms turnout already on Election Day." The president also posted that had been watching the pre-election results on Fox News, sharing a clip of commentators praising him. Republican pollster Matt Towery refuted the New York Times' prediction of a "blue surge" in the video, stating that his own polling placed both parties much more evenly matched.

Despite this, Trump began attacking Fox News by 6:36 p.m. ET / 3:36 p.m. PT, shortly after the first polls started to close.

"The FoxNews Poll, always the worst, is so insulting with the questions they ask. Every question is a hit job, and every answer is 'BULLSHIT!'" wrote the U.S. president.

Trump continued to criticise Fox News less than half an hour later at 7:00 p.m. ET / 4:00 p.m. PT, claiming that its ratings are down "because they’re spending too much time covering the Trump Deranged Dumocrats." Fox News' viewership is down 27 percent compared to the same time last year, with Fox Corp. CEO Lachlan Murdoch blaming "viewer fatigue" after "some incredible news cycle[s] the last [few] years."

"All of those people who used to watch Fox, and no longer do, get it," Trump wrote. "It’s them, not me — My numbers are better than they have ever been."

Trump's approval rating fell to a record low just prior to the midterm elections, a Reuters/Ipsos poll finding that just 31 percent of Americans approve of his performance in office.

"The new CNN Poll stated that 'TRUMP' is at 99% among Republicans," Trump continued, misinterpreting a 2024 poll in which Trump received 99 percent of the "MAGA GOP" vote specifically when put up against Democrat Kamala Harris.

"Among Republican Presidents, 'TRUMP' is at 53%, compared to Ronald Reagan at 18%, and Abraham Lincoln at 8%!," he wrote, citing a CNN poll asking Republicans which president throughout history they most admire. The same poll found that 64 percent of Democrats most admired former president Barack Obama. Without accounting for party affiliation, 30 percent chose Obama, while 19 percent named Trump.

"No wonder FoxNews Ratings are collapsing, and they’re going to go down a lot further," Trump posted, further blasting Fox Corp. for donating to Democratic candidates. Fox Corp. donates to both Republicans and Democrats. "They’re way down, not because of me, but because they are AGAINST me!"

By the time of Trump's next posts at around 10:37 p.m. ET / 7:37 p.m. PT, the midterm election results were clearly indicating a Democratic sweep.

"I wasn’t on the Ballot and if I was, we would have won IN A LANDSLIDE! Thank you for your attention to this matter," Trump wrote on Truth Social. "I'm going to become a Political Pundit, both good and bad, on the reasons these Candidates won and lost!"

The president then posted an image of a red cap with the word "veto" embroidered upon it, reminiscent of his "Make America Great Again" hats. Calling in to Fox News, Trump said he was "a little bit surprised" by the midterm election results, and stated that he intended to deal with the Democratic majority Senate and House by blocking its attempts to pass legislation.

"It's the power of veto, it's one of the stronger powers that a president has. And they'll send all the things that are insane and I'll veto them and after a couple of months of vetos they'll come over and they'll say, 'how do we get this stuff done?' and we'll work together," said Trump. "I have the power of veto. The power of veto is impervious."

The U.S. Constitution does allow presidents to refuse to sign legislation, however it is not impassable. Congress can override a president's veto if it has a two-thirds majority in both the House and Senate.

While Trump called in to speak to Fox News, he also blamed the program for the Republicans candidates' poor results. The U.S. president ended midterm election night by claiming on Truth Social that Fox News "bears responsibility for these losses by constantly and consistently putting on the Dumocrat opposition." Many former Republican voters have reported growing disillusionment with the party since Trump took office for his second term, disapproving of how he has handled issues such as the economy and the war in Iran.

Denver Broncos quarterback Bo Nix rallied in the fourth quarter with a 15-point comeback to beat the Kansas City Chiefs on Sunday. Despite leading the Broncos to a 28-24 victory, Nix's inability to drive the ball into enemy territory in the first three quarters has become the center of criticism.

On Monday, former NFL scout John Middlekauff refused to back down from his criticism of Nix. The former scout explained the issue with Nix in the first three quarters, where he has struggled to help the Broncos this season.

"I think we have to acknowledge that this goes back to college; he's not a very good pocket quarterback," Middlekauff said via 3 & OUT with John Middlekauff's X. "When he has to make a throw inside the pocket, I think you would all agree that he doesn't excel. I'm not acting like the guy's a scrub, but we have to all come to an agreement that he's the most clutch quarterback in the NFL. Well, he's literally in this situation every week because of his own undoing."

"If you could just have him for the fourth quarter, he'd be your pick," Middlekauff added. "You do not want him in the first, second, or third quarter."

In Sunday's comeback win against the Chiefs, Nix completed 15-of-26 passes for 153 yards in the first three quarters. Only seven of his 26 attempts resulted in first downs, and he was also just 1-of-8 on third-down attempts. However, in the fourth quarter, Nix went 5-of-6 for 84 yards, helping Denver erase an 11-point deficit and take the lead.

Adding to Bo Nix's reputation as the most clutch quarterback, the Broncos signal-caller has 12 fourth-quarter comebacks since entering the NFL in 2024, tied for the most by a quarterback in his first three seasons.

Sunday's win, in which Nix engineered two touchdown drives and a two-point conversion in the fourth quarter of a 28-24 victory, marked his 15th career game-winning drive in the fourth quarter or overtime. According to ProFootballTalk's Michael Smith, no other quarterback in NFL history has that many game-winning drives in his first three seasons.

Nix surpassed Dallas Cowboys quarterback Dak Prescott, who had 14 game-winning drives in his first three seasons in the NFL.

As a rookie in 2024, Nix had three game-winning drives, followed by an NFL-high seven last year, and a league-leading five this season.

Syfy Cancelled Its Ambitious Sci-Fi Series — Now It’s Prime Video’s Greatest Space Opera

📅 Nov 04, 2026  |  Category: World  |  Read Source →

After just three seasons, Syfy decided to pull the plug on an ambitious sci-fi series. Adapted from a bestselling book series, the show in question consistently landed in critics' books and even acquired a cult following. Yet, its run was cut short after three seasons. Fortunately, this is when Prime Video stepped in and renewed the series before it lasted for three more seasons.

Adapted from James S.A. Corey's acclaimed book series, The Expanse was first developed by Mark Fergus and Hawk Ostby for the Syfy Network. However, in 2018, Syfy prematurely ended the show's run in 2018. Sci-fi fans were devastated as The Expanse was easily one of the greatest additions to the genre in the 21st century.

Even some of the best sci-fi series, no matter how much potential they initially show, do not return after being canceled. Perhaps, in a parallel world, The Expanse, too, saw the same fate. Fate, however, had different plans for the series. After extensive campaigns from viewers, Amazon picked up the show and continued it for three more seasons, which cemented its "masterpiece" status.

Even in its first three seasons, The Expanse was undeniably an incredible sci-fi series. This explains why many relentlessly campaigned to bring it back. However, it was still short of becoming the masterpiece it eventually turned into. Everything from its portrayal of Newtonian physics to geopolitical conflicts between Earth, Mars, and the Belt were compelling in the first three seasons.

However, the show initially struggled with pacing issues. Especially in season 1, The Expanse felt inconsistent when it balanced Joe Miller's detective noir arc on Ceres with overarching political tension surrounding the Rocinante crew's escape. Syfy's network budget also limited the visual scope of the series, although its VFX team still did an incredible job.

Even main characters in The Expanse, like Holden, Amos, Naomi, and Alex, were yet to break free from their early archetypes in the show's initial chapters. The Expanse season 3 also had to race through Book 2 (Caliban's War) and significantly compress Book 3 (Abaddon's Gate) before ending its run. All in all, the sci-fi series was from its true potential after three installments.

The Expanse started putting its brilliance on full display after it returned for three more seasons on Prime Video. If it wasn't for its revival, it seems possible that The Expanse would have become another forgotten sci-fi series that was canceled a little too soon.

Landing on Prime Video after its revival not only allowed The Expanse to slightly extend its episode runtimes but also become more realistic. With no broadcast censors holding the show back from exploring the grit and violence of its world, The Expanse gradually became a more accurate and relatable representation of how space colonization works.

Going into season 5, The Expanse also finally got to split the Rocinante crew across different locations. This, in turn, helped the series explore and test the individual strengths and journey of its main roster. Even story beats surrounding the Ring Gate and the Marco Inaros and the Free Navy appeared much later, allowing the series to dive deeper into its portrayal of revolutionary stress and economic extinction.

All in all, if Prime Video had not given The Expanse a second chance, its legacy would have been far less long-lasting than it is today. While it is unfortunate that even Prime Video canceled the show before it could cover the complete length of its source material, it is hard not to be grateful that Syfy's cancellation did not mark the end of its run.

The Expanse is a sci-fi series set in the distant future where humanity has spread out across the solar system, but the alliances between the three most potent governing bodies have reached a state of cold war. A mixed-up crew finds themselves at the center of a dangerous intergalactic conspiracy that threatens to bring war to the colonized worlds.

Voters chose Rob Sand as Iowa's next governor, defeating first-time Republican candidate Zach Lahn.

The victory is key for Democrats ahead of the 2028 presidential election, because Iowa's early caucuses play a significant role in shaping national campaigns.

Sand, however, had kept the party at arm's length during his race. Instead, he focused on his centrist policies and stressed his humble upbringing.

"We have to have bigger hearts and minds than just our party," Sand told the crowd at a victory event. "Come January, we will be forced to work together."

He promised to work across the aisle to shape Iowa's future.

Former US President Barack Obama said the election results showed Americans wanted leaders who brought people together and offered practical solutions.

With President Donald Trump in office for another two years, Obama urged Democrats to use their gains to pursue change and "prevent further damage."

"Tonight, the American people sent a clear message — they want a politics that brings people together rather than divides them," he wrote on X.

Obama credited strong candidates across the country, including in places once considered unlikely prospects for victory. He also thanked election officials and volunteers from both parties for ensuring a smooth vote.

"Our job now is to roll up our sleeves, make changes wherever we have the power to do so, prevent further damage, and lay the groundwork for the future," he said.

In what was one of the most widely followed and closely watched races of the night, Democratic newcomer Abdul El-Sayed defeated Republican Mike Rogers in Michigan with 52% of the vote to 46%.

The win makes the 42-year-old El-Sayed the first Muslim senator in the history of the United States.

Born and raised in Michigan, El-Sayed was repeatedly targeted by attacks over his heritage and religion, including by President Trump.

Inspired to enter politics after the Flint water crisis that lasted from 2014 to 2019, El-Sayed ran on a platform of making medical care affordable, increasing taxes on billionaires, and building more housing for lower- and middle-income families.

It was one of the most expensive Senate races ever run, with tens of millions poured in from mega-donors on both sides.

"Michigan, we did it!" El-Sayed said as he addressed supporters after his win, to jubilant applause, adding that he was "honored" to have been elected.

He credited his volunteers and staff as well as their message of "Medicare for all and getting money out of politics" for his victory.

Tech mogul Elon Musk appeared to joke on his X account that his "election-interfering space lasers" did not work as Republican candidates he supported began to concede defeat.

At the same time, he commented that he was going to build data centers in space, an apparent reference to how the noise, pollution, and water consumption of data centers had been a hot topic in several congressional races.

Through funding organizations known as PACs, Musk donated $446 million to the races in Maine, Michigan, Texas, and Ohio, amongst others.

Sherrod Brown has defeated Republican Jon Husted in Ohio's special Senate election, securing a victory that had been crucial to Democrats' plan to retake the Senate.

Brown was a longtime Ohio senator until his defeat to Republican Bernie Moreno in 2024. His campaign highlighted the many corruption allegations against Husted, who had been appointed in 2025 to fill the vacant seat left when JD Vance became vice president.

Husted has been accused of being part of a bribery scandal related to his time in Ohio state politics, illegal campaign donations from a billionaire with ties to Jeffrey Epstein.

Despite the tens of millions donated to his campaign and vigorous support from President Trump, Brown won 49% of the vote to Husted's 46%.

"My pledge to all Ohioans, whether you voted for me or you didn't, is that I will always be on your side — always," Brown said in his victory speech. "I do not work for the utility companies. I do not work for data centers. I do not work for Wall Street. I work for you, I work for Ohio."

House Democratic leader Hakeem Jeffries celebrated his party's projected victory, declaring: "America is back."

The result puts him in line to become the first Black Speaker of the House and gives Democrats greater power to challenge President Donald Trump's agenda.

"Today begins the great American comeback," Jeffries told a cheering crowd at the Democrats' watch party, describing the occasion as a patriotic moment rather than a partisan one.

‘Eventually I won’t have a choice’: Car subscriptions for remote start and extra horsepower pile on top of a record $787 average payment

📅 Nov 04, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Asher Davis was vaguely aware that subscription-based car perks had “become a thing,” he said. But when the 33-year-old was thrown into the new-car market unexpectedly, after nine years of blissful ignorance, what he found disturbed him: as far as he could tell, the roster of new cars with no subscriptions attached had dwindled drastically.

“I feel like eventually I won’t have a choice,” he told Fortune.

Davis’s frustration is aimed less at any single subscription—offerings run the gamut from remote start to horsepower upgrades—than at the market’s direction.

Automakers are betting that drivers will get used to paying monthly for features built into cars they’ve already bought. Early evidence suggests consumers will tolerate some of it, but not fees for hardware they’ve already paid for, and their willingness to pay is slipping.

Edmunds doesn’t formally track the spread of subscription-based car features, according to Ivan Drury, the firm’s director of insights. But Drury knows the trend firsthand.

“I’ve got a Subaru that has the location and remote start based upon a subscription fee,” Drury told Fortune. “And my prior vehicle, a 2024 F150, had both of those for free.”

Different manufacturer, different time, he noted, but the contrast is the point. “It’s becoming more frequent and more annoying,” he said.

The appeal for automakers is straightforward. “Number one, it really is based upon the fact that they can,” Drury said. Modern vehicles are connected to the internet, which means features can be switched on and off remotely.

“It’s not like it’s a hard touch point thing,” he said, like swapping leather for cloth. “It’s the fact that there is control over some things that are actually hardwired into the vehicle, but at the same time—flip of a switch.”

The dealership, meanwhile, “hates it,” Drury said. No salesperson wants to sheepishly explain that some of the bells and whistles on a customer’s new toy sit behind a paywall.

“But they’re just the middleman,” Drury said. “It’s shifting where the money’s going, but at the end of the day, who’s bearing the cost? The customer.”

BMW briefly test-drove a subscription for heated seats, charging drivers in markets including the UK, Germany, and South Korea a monthly fee to use seat heaters already installed in their cars. The fee was never offered in the U.S., and the backlash was swift and severe enough that BMW dropped the heated-seat subscription in September 2023.

“Talk about leaving a bad taste in your mouth,” Drury said. “It’s like you bought this vehicle; it’s good to go. You should just be able to hit the button. Get that heated seat. And you paid for a BMW, and you might get yours turned off?”

The episode suggests a red line for consumers: Charging for something long considered part of the car, in a vehicle already sold at a premium, reads differently than charging for a genuinely new capability.

Peter Anderson, 36, a luxury car enthusiast who has been driving high-end cars since age 14 and a half (“thanks to the great state of North Carolina’s legal driving age requirements”), agreed. “Honestly, if the technology is something like heated seats, I don’t want to pay for something that’s already built into the car and I’ve paid for the car to be equipped with those seats,” he told Fortune.

But automakers are banking on his openness to subscriptions in general.

“I’m into [subscriptions] to an extent,” Anderson said. “If there’s an update to the software that’s capable of giving me better performance or a new feature, I’d pay to enable it.”

Anderson cited upgrades to power, handling, and driving dynamics. “Even if it was just a new mode that could be added because the software had evolved,” he said.

Volkswagen, No. 1 on the 2026 Fortune 500 Europe, is testing that appetite. In 2025 it brought its “Power on Demand” subscription for the ID.3 electric hatchback to the UK, letting owners of Pro and Pro S versions pay £16.50 (roughly $22) a month to raise output from 201 to 228 horsepower. The extra 27 horsepower is already built into the car’s hardware.

Online, some Volkswagen customers cried foul (“If you’re OK with this dystopian nonsense, you’re a sucker,” one Redditor said). Volkswagen, meanwhile, told Fortune that customer feedback “has been positive.”

The subscription push arrives as vehicle costs hit records. The average monthly payment on a financed new car reached $787 in the third quarter of 2026, up from $756 a year earlier, according to Edmunds data. The average rate on those loans has held near 7%.

Against payments like that, a $20 or $30 monthly subscription can feel almost negligible, which is exactly what makes it easy to underestimate.

Sue Helper, an economics professor at Case Western Reserve University’s Weatherhead School of Management and a former chief economist at the U.S. Commerce Department, said the monthly sticker price can be a misleading odometer.

“The important thing is to look at the total cost of owning a vehicle, not just the monthly payment,” she told Fortune. “A subscription that costs a few dollars a month may seem insignificant, but multiple fees can add up over several years. Consumers have to weigh those costs against the value they actually get from the features.”

Subscriptions aren’t inherently exploitative, Helper said. They can make sense for services that require ongoing investment, like connectivity or software updates.

Part of the problem is attention, she said. “Consumers have limited time and attention, and they may not fully account for recurring charges when they initially purchase a vehicle.”

She draws the line at charging for hardware already baked into the purchase price. “One difference between a vehicle-related subscription and a magazine subscription is that the former is tied to a very expensive investment,” Helper said. “It wouldn’t be fair to say that the $50,000 vehicle is in fact not usable if a consumer doesn’t pay an ongoing fee.”

She pointed to New York City’s “click to cancel” rule, which Mayor Zohran Mamdani’s administration put into effect Oct. 1, as a policy that can keep tactics that exploit consumer behavior from becoming “standard business practice.”

The risk isn’t only on the consumer side. “A subscription might generate additional revenue in the short term, but if customers feel they’re getting less value for their money, they may be less likely to buy from that company again,” Helper said.

“The competitive question is whether consumers see enough value in a paid feature to keep subscribing—and whether the company’s pricing strategy strengthens or weakens its appeal relative to other brands,” she added.

The market has started to answer. In S&P Global Mobility’s 2025 Connected Car Study, published in July 2025, 68% of respondents said they would pay for connected-car services, down from 86% in 2024.

Automakers are betting the decline is about presentation, not principle: that drivers who balk at a heated-seat fee will still pay for a performance upgrade or a connectivity plan if the value is clear.

Drury worries that the fees will fade into background noise, and that resistance will erode as a new norm sets in. “There’s huge backlash, but at the same time, if even anybody signs up, they make money,” he said.

“Only older people like myself are going to remember that ‘Hey, that used to be free, that used to be something normal that my car always had—and now you guys are charging for it.’”

A tip of the hat now to Danny Mills, father to the GB Olympic runner George Mills, with Mills Sr running the 2023 London Marathon in 3hr 12min 18sec at 45 years of age. Meanwhile, Milner’s run places him eighth as we approach some of football’s more serious runners.

The former Dundee United and West Ham defender Christian Dailly clocked a seriously impressive 3hr 6min 26sec at the 2013 London Marathon. “In 1990, I went from boys’ football in May to my Dundee United debut in August,” said Dailly after the race. “We’d be down at the beach, and all of a sudden, I’d be running through the sea at St Andrews or up and down the sand dunes. When I got to pre-season at Dundee United, I was flying, running away from everyone.” Dailly went into athletics coaching when his football career ended. We did not expect a goalkeepeer to be jostling for position towards the front of the pack, but the former Derby County and Sheffield Wednesday stopper Lee Grant hared around London in 3hr 0min 33sec this year.

And so we get to the hallowed ground of a sub-three hour marathon and a select group of only five. The Real Madrid legend Raúl González was clearly being cheered from the rafters as he ran his home marathon in 2hr 59min 22sec in 2019. “It was only the second marathon of my life. I wanted to break three hours, and I’m very happy with what I’ve accomplished,” he said. The Oxford United manager, Aaron Ramsey, used his relative youth at 35 to tear up the Stockholm Marathon this summer in exactly 2hr 59min. Arjen Robben was known for his blistering pace over short distances in his glittering career but he showed that he could blend speed with stamina as he raced round Rotterdam in 2023 in a time of 2hr 58min 33sec.

Before setting out on his managerial career, Luis Enrique trained like a demon for the Florence Marathon, with his hard work paying off as he came home in 2hr 58min 8sec. He has since continued his fitness regime, competing in the Frankfurt Ironman event and the Marathon des Sables.

But the medal for the fastest former footballer to run a marathon goes to Alexei Smertin. The former Chelsea and Russia utility man took endurance running seriously when he retired in 2008 and hit a peak of 2hr 48min 17sec at the Kazan Marathon in 2020, aged 45. That is an incredible pace of 6min 25sec per mile. He has since tried switching to longer distances as he has aged but believes he may still have one or two quick marathons left in him. “I’ve been leaning toward ultras. One reason is that speed declines with age,” he said recently. “I thought I’d never be able to improve my personal best [2:48:17] … [but] I have a few more years to improve my speed.”

“What is the soonest a manager has been sacked after signing a contract extension?” asks Dave Smythe.

Adam Clark comes in hot with the first suggestion: “Leam Richardson, then of Wigan Athletic, signed a three-year contract extension on 25 October 2022 and was sacked 16 days later on 10 November 2022. Wigan then recruited Kolo Touré on a three-and-a-half year contract, who subsequently lasted just nine games.”

“I’m pretty sure when Watford sacked Glenn Roeder in 1996 he’d signed a contract extension about a fortnight earlier?” suggests Daz Pearce. Good shout, Daz: Watford were not yet in their era of sacking a manager every month, but Roeder – initially appointed at Vicarage Road in 1993 – was given the boot by Watford just 10 days after signing a new one-year contract extension in February 1996. “I am sure I have learned from this experience,” said Roeder, who announced his intention to stay in the game. He would not get another managerial job for another five years, enjoying a successful stint at West Ham from 2001-03.

Not a contract extension but Kevin Cullis’s appointment at Swansea City in 1996 lasted just seven days. Cullis, who never played professionally, had only ever previously coached non-league Crawley Town’s youth team and was later jailed for fraud in 2003, and didn’t get off to the best start in Wales with Swans players reportedly ignoring his tactics at half-time against Blackpool during just his second game in charge. Cullis resigned shortly afterwards.

West Ham took too long to sack Nuno and now must trust Parker to do what he does best | Jacob Steinberg

📅 Nov 04, 2026  |  Category: Business  |  Read Source →

Forget speculation over how a Parker-led side would fare in the Premier League and let the man who has taken three clubs up do his work again

It has taken West Ham six months to come to their senses and sack Nuno Espírito Santo, but at least the decision has arrived early enough to revive their hopes of automatic promotion.

Dithering was no longer an option after the dismal performance against West Brom last Saturday.

West Ham, eighth in the Championship and four points off the top two, had to act after a second consecutive home defeat. They are in danger of slipping into a battle for a playoff spot and needed to move on from Nuno after just over a year of his confused, passive football.

Nuno did a terrible job after replacing Graham Potter as manager in September 2025. The 52-year-old had 33 games to save West Ham from relegation and failed. He has the most expensive squad in the Championship but his side have not lived up to their billing as title favourites and their stuttering form has exposed the folly of not sacking Nuno at the end of last season.

David Sullivan, then the club’s largest shareholder, wanted a change. But Daniel Kretinsky favoured continuity and looked to capitalise on the shifting power dynamics in the boardroom. Karren Brady had stepped down as vice-chair in April – Karim Virani replaced her in an interim role – and there was further upheaval when Sullivan resigned as joint-chair and a director of the club to fight what he described as “false allegations” regarding his personal conduct.

Sullivan bears most culpability for West Ham’s relegation. He still owns a 40% stake but is no longer on the board and West Ham are better off without him dictating key decisions. Equally, though, where Sullivan was not mistaken was in wanting Nuno gone. Kretinsky got his first big call wrong. At least the Czech billionaire has given Nuno’s replacement time to get the team back on track. The task falls to Scott Parker, who has been on the market since leaving Burnley last April. Parker is regarded as a “safe” appointment and has been on West Ham’s radar for a while.

Some wanted the former England midfielder in the summer and his name was mentioned again when West Ham failed to win their first three league games. Patience was wearing thin. A subsequent run of four wins in a row swiftly proved a false dawn.

West Ham have not had the right mindset. They have two wins from their past six games and have often started playing only after going a goal down. A 6-0 win against Wrexham was a highlight but victory at Lincoln by the same scoreline came with the caveat that the Imps received two red cards before half-time.

There was no identity under Nuno. West Ham are unbeaten on the road and won 1-0 at the then table-topping Swansea last month but it has been hard to tell Nuno broke the Football League transfer record with the £22m signing of the Belgium midfielder Arne Engels from Celtic. Even Jarrod Bowen, who was convinced to sign a new deal in the summer, has been off colour. Nuno’s tactics mean the winger, West Ham’s top scorer, is often crowded out.

West Ham were lopsided against West Brom. Enough was enough. Kretinsky, who is not at every game, was unimpressed when he attended West Ham’s 3-2 defeat by Southampton a week earlier. He pays attention to the views of Bowen, Tomas Soucek and Mark Noble, the technical director and former club captain.

Nuno, whose substitutions were almost as weird as his starting lineups, had lost the players. The mood darkened after the West Brom game. West Ham were easy to play through because of Nuno’s reluctance to use a holding midfielder, an issue exacerbated by his refusal to use Edson Álvarez or Soungoutou Magassa after both sought moves during the summer.

Magassa’s exile has caused disquiet. Not only is a young asset being devalued, a fragile defence is going without enough protection. West Ham have a lot of No 8s but they need a No 6. It is an area for Parker to address. He will also need to work out whether to stick with Mads Hermansen in goal, and the jury is out on summer signings.

One source questions whether West Ham targeted players equipped to handle the intensity of the Championship. Divine Mukasa, the 19-year-old midfielder, is talented but inconsistent. Manor Solomon and Edvin Austbø have flattered to deceive on the left wing. Joël Veltman, the 34-year-old Dutch right-back, has had fitness issues. Adama Boiro, a 24-year-old left‑back on loan from Athletic Bilbao, has not been seen since a cameo against Derby in early September. The three strikers signed by Nuno across two windows have toiled; Pablo Felipe, Taty Castellanos and Joël Piroe have five league goals between them this season.

Nils Koppen, the head of recruitment appointed in the summer, will have to be busy in January, but Parker will hope to have made his impact before the window opens. He is not known for expansive football but he is proven at this level. Parker won promotion with Bournemouth, Burnley and Fulham. The 46-year-old’s track record is clear and it helps that he has an association with West Ham.

Parker, whose all-action playing style made him a fan favourite, knows how difficult it can be in east London. He played in some pretty bad West Ham sides, experienced relegation in 2011 and arrives with the club in a tricky spot again.

It is not the moment to start worrying about Parker’s questionable record as a Premier League manager. What do West Ham do if they go up? Do they stick with Parker? Do they make an instant change? Well, how about they get promoted first? The rest can wait.
