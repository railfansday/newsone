Baldini: ‘Chivu and Fabregas good for Italian football, not afraid to play youth’

📅 Nov 09, 2026  |  Category: Sports  |  Read Source →

Italy Under-21 coach Silvio Baldini insists ‘there are no friendly games’ at international level, while Cesc Fabregas and Cristian Chivu ‘are good for football, because they teach and are not afraid to play youth.’

The Azzurrini secured qualification for the European Under-21 Championship, which will be played next summer.

Meanwhile, they have two prestigious friendly tests against France on Friday November 13, and Belgium on November 16.

“As I always tell the lads, there are no friendly games when you are representing the Nazionale, because when you wear the Azzurri shirt, you carry the honour and pride of our country, our culture and our roots,” said Baldini in his press conference.

“We cannot think that we are playing friendly matches. We need to give our best every time, as if it was an official competitive game.”

Baldini had for two games been the interim Italy coach when Gennaro Gattuso resigned in the wake of the World Cup play-off failure, but has returned to his main role with the Under-21 side.

“Youth is vital for our football, and I embraced this role with so much enthusiasm. I also want to thank the media, because you spoke a lot about the fact Italian football was in a tunnel, and the only way to get out of it was to make reforms and have more courage in using young players.

“The responses we’ve seen have been positive at club level. I think of Chivu at Inter and Fabregas at Como, talented coaches who are good for football, because they know how to teach players, they set rules, and are not afraid to play youth.

“This is a spell where coaches like this give a big contribution to the development of Italian football, so we are fortunate they are working here in Serie A. Now I hope that others follow their example.”

Several of the Italy Under-21 talents have been promoted to the senior squad for the final two Nations League games against France and Belgium.

They include Milan forward Alphadjo Cisse, Como’s Mattia Liberali, Parma striker Simone Lontani and Crystal Palace defender Honest Ahanor.

Brentford talent Michael Kayode had already made the step up along with Sporting midfielder Issa Doumbia and Roma’s Niccolò Pisilli.

Inside Designer Carla Fernández’s Vibrant Mexico City Home, Full of Indigenous Textiles and Art

📅 Nov 09, 2026  |  Category: Business  |  Read Source →

When the Mexico City-based writer and curator Su Wu started working on her new design book, a theme began to emerge: She kept noticing the presence of pre-Hispanic architectural and design traditions in modern Mexican interiors.

It was from this realization that Mesoamerican Modern: Ancient Ideas in Contemporary Mexican Homes was born. The book features 16 homes across Mexico—those of artists, architects, designers, and more—all shot by photographer Luis Garvan.

Wrought iron doors designed by Carla Fernández.

“When I began this project, I thought that we would be documenting a history, like the aura surrounding the artifacts in a museum, the apparitions of the past,” Wu writes in the book’s introduction. “Instead, what I’ve found is the vibrant, living presence of Indigenous ideas in Mexico.”

“In the guest bedroom, pink silk curtains dyed by Fernández cast a glow in the room. The bedspread is from her personal collection of Mesoamerican textiles, made of undyed wool in Teotitlán del Valle, Oaxaca.”—Su Wu

One space that exemplifies those ideas is Casa Flora, the colorful brutalist Mérida home and studio of the Mexican fashion designer Carla Fernández. As a designer, Fernández seeks to preserve the textile traditions of Indigenous and Mestizo communities through collaborations with artisans, bringing ancient practices to a contemporary audience. Fittingly, the home is full of Mesoamerican art and textiles.

Fernández led the renovation of the house, which dates back more than a century and now hosts workshops for Taller Flora, her design lab, which focuses on traditional Indigenous clothing techniques. (You can also stay in the home; the designer lists it for stays on Airbnb.)

Fernández’s husband, the artist Pedro Reyes, created several elements of the space, including a concrete outdoor shower, as well as some of the tiles throughout the space. His sculptures—including a prototype of Mitla Sillas, chairs inspired by the pyramids of the Mitla archaeological site in Oaxaca—are found throughout the home.

The outdoor shower designed by Pedro Reyes.

Reflecting on Fernández’s home, Wu tells Vogue: “I was very moved when writing the book with the idea of the ‘void’ in Mesoamerican architecture, and how so many architects, going back to the writings of Teodoro González de León, described the patio as a central, defining feature of the Mesoamerican home, a space that is at once enclosed and open to the elements. And in Carla’s home, I think there are many moments that exemplify this idea of the boundary between interior and exterior being not a physical one, but rather a marker of movement across psychological states.”

“A latticed window, known in Mexico as a celosia, is a central part of Mesoamerican architecture, used for cross-ventilation and to create a permeable sense of separation and shadow.”—Su Wu

Prototypes of Reyes’s Mitla Sillas chairs.

For Wu, Casa Flora is also a prime example of an idea that chef Elena Reygadas wrote about in her foreword to the book, “which is that handmade things are meant to be lived with no matter how precious, or especially if precious.”

“An equipale chair made of bent wood by the Wixárika people in Nayarit sits in front of a backstrap-loom tapestry of the Torre Latinoamericana, the iconic skyscraper in Mexico City designed by architect Augusto H. Álvarez. Made by artisans from San Juan Cancuc, Chiapas, the tapestry echoes a collection of dresses from Carla Fernández that featured historic buildings in Mexico City.”—Su Wu

I Spent My First 25 Hours In World Of Warcraft: Forever Happily Wasting Time

📅 Nov 09, 2026  |  Category: Business  |  Read Source →

World of WarcraftWorld of Warcraft: Forever

Here are some things I have done in the 25 hours I have played of World of Warcraft: Forever so far:

Sound awful? Well…maybe. When I think about how I could be spending that time finally weeding my garden, or doing all the real-life chores I keep putting off, yeah. Bit of a waste of time. Such a waste of time, in fact, that I have hardly even touched most of the new content in WoW: Forever. I did play the Skyborne in beta: their starting island is fine. A bit too modern-feeling for me, but fine. I’ve definitely encountered a few new quests mixed in here and there, and the aforementioned torch and sleeping bag missions were new as well (though I did the former in beta and the latter in Season of Discovery already). But I haven’t even touched the new dungeons. I’m months away (at my current pace) from the new raids. I’m not even a high enough level to check out the Riverlands yet. May as well be playing Classic, right?

But as I hike back and forth and up and down Azeroth, I tilt my camera around. That draw distance! I see Mt. Hyjal, misty, looming in the distance, waiting for me at max level. The sun rises and sets over plains and swamps. It filters through trees and fog. I /wave at the other travelers I meet on the road. I sit at campfires and chat with others who gather around. People buff me with their class’ spells when they see me, or resurrect me if they see my dead body so that I don’t have to struggle all the way back to it. One person yesterday finished a quest just as I was starting, and volunteered to stick around (wasting 20 more minutes of their own time) to help me finish too. Another time, the game’s layer system goofed while I was on a zeppelin and something like 200 of us all ran into Undercity at once, prompting humorous panic from the folks already inside. Today, myself and two others started an entire, lengthy conversation in our zone’s general chat while I was squeezing in some early morning fishing time, just by saying “Good morning” in hopes someone would say it back. It ended with someone telling us all silly, made-up stories about a warlock named Daniel.

Somehow, this time doesn’t feel like such a waste anymore. It’s…soothing, I think. The slowness, the friction. A lot of this was always present in WoW Classic, sure. But I think the little added touches in Foreverâthe extra quests for things like torches and sleeping bags, the campfires, the layering ensuring that my world is always packed with other peopleâare lending the experience some new magic that wasn’t there before. In the past, the slowness of Classic felt more like a product of the time we were in, because no one had streamlined MMORPGs to eliminate it yet. But Forever embraces that pace and leans into it as a design feature rather than a flaw, making those moments feel rewarding on purpose rather than on occasional accident. More often than ever, you’re put in positions where there’s nothing to do but say hello to other people and forge a brief connection, or to sit back and admire the way the new lighting plays on the ripples in the river. It’s WoW, but with mindfulness. And mindfulness is something I’ve been in desperate want of lately.

Given that, it’s been really, really hard for me to put World of Warcraft: Forever down, even in the face of another brilliant game launch happening simultaneously. In my brain, a task list has populated of things I want to do: a dozen or so quests, of course, plus that one extra quest I have to turn in on the other continent, and that sleeping bag scavenger hunt I’m in the middle of. I need to visit my class trainer back in Orgrimmar or Thunder Bluff here soon to learn some new skills, and also all the profession trainers, and also the bank. There are some dungeons I’d like to run soon. All of this backs up in my brain the same way my real-life chores list does, but with the distinguishing feature that I dread none of it. I am eager to take part in every aspect, even though over half of these will inevitably be time-consuming, even time-wasting affairs. I’ll get to level 60 eventually, but I’m in no hurry to do it. To my editors: I’m sorry I’m inevitably going to take so long with this review. But there are so many wonderful, pointless distractions between here and there, I can’t help but let myself be blissful and slightly bored for another 50 or more hours first.

Mass Effect Legendary Edition Finally Gets Co-op Back on PC

📅 Nov 09, 2026  |  Category: World  |  Read Source →

Mass Effect 3’s co-op mode put four players on a map, each with a class and a race, against waves of Cerberus, geth, Reapers, or Collectors. In the original trilogy, it was the only Mass Effect you could play with friends. When Mass Effect Legendary Edition compiled and remastered the games in 2021, that co-op mode didn’t make the cut. Project director Mac Walters said in 2021 that the team’s focus in releasing Legendary Edition was on the single-player experience…and that at some point the team had to draw the line. This year, that changed.

Legendary Edition Multiplayer, a free download from the modding team ME3Tweaks, both restores that missing co-op mode and adds new ways to play the game. After installing the mod, boot it up, and you’ll get a world of quality of life improvements, new content, and glitch fixes:

You’ll need a PC copy of Legendary Edition with an unmodded Mass Effect 3. Because the mod lives apart from your trilogy, setup takes a few more steps than a typical mod. ME3Tweaks says it won’t interact with the original game at all, so your settings, saves, and mods stay fully separate. To install it, download ME3Tweaks Mod Manager 10.0 or higher, import both the Launcher Mod and the main mod from Nexus, and click Install LMP. To import your Mass Effect 3 profile, run Legendary Edition once first, or EA App cloud sync may flag a conflict. Then launch from Steam, the EA App, or Mod Manager.

ME3Tweaks has been building toward an N7 Day mod release for months. The team has released mods on N7 Day before, including Pinnacle Station in 2021, a combat-focused DLC for the first Mass Effect that the remaster left out because its source code was lost. The team isn’t done working, either: We can look forward to at least three further content packs, including a Redemption map pack with remade multiplayer maps and one with the Rejection enemy faction.

But for now, with the restored co-op mode, we’ve got our hands full. Bring three friends, generate an invite from the pause menu, then pick a heavy weapon (or skip it for the extra ammo and speed). See how long your squad can hold the line. Co-op is back in Legendary Edition, and it’s a good thing, too. The Reapers have waves waiting.

Mass Effect Legendary Edition is available to play on PS4, PS5, Windows PC, and Xbox Series X/S. Find the latest install and joining instructions for this co-op mod on the Legendary Edition Multiplayer page.

Prime Video and Nordic distribution powerhouse Scanbox Entertainment have joined forces to develop a new slate of theatrical films and streaming series based on “Hamilton,” the bestselling Swedish espionage-thriller franchise created by author Jan Guillou.

The ambitious project, which aims to relaunch one of Scandinavia’s most enduring spy franchises for international audiences, will be produced by Swedish banner Dramanation, part of Scanbox Entertainment, alongside Germany’s Odeon Fiction, which is part of Mediawan. The deal marks a major push to capitalize on established Nordic IP across theatrical and streaming platforms. A new actor set to take on the role of Carl Hamilton, the elite Swedish intelligence officer who has previously been portrayed on screen by Stellan Skarsgård, Peter Stormare and Mikael Persbrandt.

The films and series are currently in active development, with casting, directors and other key creative attachments expected to be announced in the coming months.

Guillou’s “Hamilton” novels have sold more than 10 million copies internationally and spawned numerous film and television adaptations over the decades. The franchise follows a U.S. Navy SEAL-trained Swedish operative navigating international espionage, covert missions and shifting geopolitical alliances.

Patrick Nebout, whose credits include “Midnight Sun,” “Whiskey on the Rocks” and the previous “Agent Hamilton” television reboot starring Jakob Oftebro, will executive produce for Dramanation alongside Britta Meyermann (“Spy City,” “Deadwind,” “The Seed”) for Odeon Fiction.

“Amid ongoing global power shifts and rising chaos, Jan Guillou’s Hamilton feels more urgent and relevant than ever. He is an enduring, complex and unique Scandinavian icon with true crossover appeal worldwide,” said Nebout.

“And it’s not just a man-with-a-gun concept, there is a lot more depth, nuances and political awareness to this character. Our vision is to bring all the nuances from Guillou’s novels to the surface, and I’m thrilled to team up with Odeon and Britta Meyermann on this project.”

“The unique theatrical and streaming partnership with Scanbox Entertainment and Prime Video paves the way for many exciting iterations as we explore the vast Hamilton universe,” Nebout added.

The new adaptations will span both feature films designed for theatrical release and television series for streaming, reflecting a strategy to build a broader screen franchise around Guillou’s extensive literary universe.

Brad Morley, head of content acquisitions, Nordics, at Prime Video, said the franchise fits the streamer’s ambitions to back locally rooted stories with global appeal.

“The Hamilton franchise is exactly the kind of bold, internationally resonant storytelling we champion at Prime Video,” said Morley. “Jan Guillou created one of Scandinavia’s most compelling heroes, and we’re thrilled to bring this iconic series to a new generation of viewers alongside Scanbox, Odeon Fiction, and Dramanation.”

For Scanbox Entertainment, which is part of European distribution group Vuelta, the project represents an opportunity to leverage its theatrical distribution business alongside Prime Video’s streaming reach.

“Hamilton has been a fixture of Nordic popular culture for decades, and at Scanbox we believe the time is right to introduce him to a new audience,” said Thor Sigurjonsson, CEO of Scanbox Entertainment.

“Teaming up with Dramanation, Odeon Fiction, and Prime Video lets us give this story the scale it deserves, in cinemas and on screen at home. Jan Guillou created a character that resonates far beyond the Nordic region, and we’re committed to doing justice to that legacy.”

Annica Bellander Rune, local marketing and acquisitions manager at Scanbox, and Torben Thorup Jørgensen, the company’s chief commercial officer, will also executive produce alongside Prime Video’s Morley.

Ever since NASA's James Webb Space Telescope (JWST) started observing the early universe, it has been providing almost as many surprises as it has answers. One such surprise are the so-called Little Red Dots, cosmic objects that seemingly disappear completely before the universe is two billion years old.

New research suggests that Little Red Dots may actually be connected primordial black holes, tiny black holes born not from dying stars but rather from fluctuations in the density of matter just after the Big Bang, in the earliest moments of cosmic history. Should they exist, primordial black holes would predate stars, galactic dust particles, and elements heavier than hydrogen and helium. Now, researchers have used JWST data to test whether one particular Little Red Dot, known as blackAbell 2744-QSO1 (QSO1), could host a primordial black hole.

"A black hole is an object born from the gravitational death of a star. Primordial black holes are a very different story," study team member Valerio De Luca, of the Department of Physics and Astronomy at Johns Hopkins University (JHU) in Maryland, said in a statement.

"They come from the physics of the early universe that predates the first stars, when the universe was expanding exponentially in time and space. So the mechanisms for creating primordial black holes are different," De Luca added.

Though scientists have theorized about the existence of primordial black holes since they were first proposed by Stephen Hawking in the 1970s, no hard evidence has presented itself. One of the issues scientists have puzzled over is how to tell the difference between a primordial black hole and a "traditional" or astrophysical black hole formed from the supernova death and collapse of a massive star.

"Little Red Dots could surround astrophysical black holes, and most probably many of them do, but they could also be primordial black holes, if primordial black holes exist," De Luca said. "So the question becomes, Which ones are primordial and how can we tell?"

De Luca and colleagues turned their attention to QS01, located around 13.1 billion light-years from Earth, which is seen as it was just 700 million years after the Big Bang. What makes this Little Red Dot stand out is the fact that it is almost entirely composed of hydrogen and helium. Because these were the universe's first elements, it could be more unspoiled and primordial than other Little Red Dots.

The team modeled how a Little Red Dot like QS01 could form from a primordial black hole. That was necessary because the circumstances under which this could happen would have to be very specific.

"There are constraints on primordial black holes from cosmological observations," said team member Loris Del Grosso, also of JHU's Department of Physics and Astronomy. "There can only be so many primordial black holes in the universe, for example, and there cannot be too many of them in a given cluster."

Considering three possible scenarios, the team found that the most likely situation is a smaller primordial black hole consuming surrounding gas and energy until it grew to be the size of QSO1.

"The door was open on this theory — that Little Red Dots could be primordial black holes," Del Grosso said. "We've now cracked it open a little farther by showing some scenarios are more likely than others."

Del Grosso and crew now intend to look at the other 100 or so Little Red Dots seen by the JWST to determine how many of them could have started life as primordial black holes.

Then scientists may finally have the evidence to say these long-theorized ancient black holes actually existed.

"We don't have a clear understanding of what happens in the early universe right after the Big Bang," De Luca concluded. "If primordial black holes do exist, then something had to happen to make them in the first place, which would be a groundbreaking discovery in physics."

The team's findings were published in June in the journal Physical Review Letters.

From July, the top rate on real, long-term crypto gains will increase to 47%, minus the effects of inflation on the cost basis.

Similarly, Australians leaving the country face a hefty exit tax on departure, which may require selling a decent amount of your stack.

“They tax you on the way out. If you’ve got a lot of gains or profits you’re sitting on, they’ll catch you when you leave,” says Adrian Forza, director of Crypto Tax Australia. “Arguably it’d be bigger in the future, but it’s still difficult to stomach selling off everything to leave.”

Forza explains you also have the option of paying the Australian taxes later on, but on the full value of the gain after you sell the asset. So if you expect Bitcoin to go up, you’d be best off paying the exit tax now.

Simpson is only 27 and the prospect of locking up his crypto portfolio for forty years holds little appeal. Instead, he trades and holds crypto via a company to benefit from the 25% company tax rate.

Running a single-person company is easier than you might think, but not worth the hassle unless you have a reasonable amount of capital and intend to make a lot of trades. It requires registering the company with ASIC and paying an annual fee, paying an accountant to produce financial statements and/or an annual company tax return and mucking about with payroll software and payday superannuation contributions if you plan to pay yourself an income. You can pay yourself a dividend at any time when you need some money.

“The company structure is good if you’re wanting to cap the capital gains tax at the company tax rate,” he says, but notes there’s a big catch: when you take funds out of the company, you need to pay the difference between the 25% company tax rate and your personal tax rate. “You still need to pay your marginal income tax rates. If you’re on the top tax rate, like 47%,” he says.

You may be wondering: if you have to pay your income tax rate anyway, which will equal the CGT rate from July, what is the benefit in paying a few thousand dollars and filling out a bunch of paperwork to run a company?

Simpson explains that the key advantage is that while you are building up your crypto portfolio by trading in and out of different coins, you only pay the 25% company rate for each sale. “Instead of paying 47% because you’ve just taken the profits… then the CGT amount is only 25% — the company tax rate. So when you put that money back in, you’ve got more capital to play with.”

The 50% CGT discount has been replaced by an indexation discount, which takes into account how much inflation affects how much you paid originally for an asset.

This means that investors who buy and hold dividend stocks or index funds for a long period will receive a bigger CGT discount. So should you drop crypto and buy a bunch of ETFs so you can pay less tax?

The short answer is no: the new CGT ‘discount’ is largely an illusion, as it simply measures your real gain after inflation.

Because this is an operator announcement, the scope of that first-of-kind claim should be attributed to the participants. Tokenized equities can describe several very different products. Some are economic exposures issued by a separate intermediary; others aim to represent the issuer's shares with the rights and recordkeeping attached to conventional stock. Here the issuer-sponsored structure and transfer-agent involvement are central. They are intended to connect token holdings with the legally recognized ownership register, rather than just a derivative tracking a market price. Investors still need to know transfer restrictions, voting rights, eligibility rules and how corporate actions are administered. A blockchain token is not proof of all those protections by itself.

The reported transactions occurred in a permissioned pool with verified participants. Restricted access may be a design choice needed to satisfy securities obligations, investor eligibility and transfer controls. It may also limit liquidity and interoperability compared with the open token markets crypto users are accustomed to. The test of such infrastructure is whether these controls can function at scale without making trading and settlement more cumbersome than conventional systems.

The company referred to an SEC innovation exemption announced in September. Different venues, issuers or instruments may require their own legal analysis. Reporting on this area is especially sensitive to the distinction between an operational pilot, a live limited transaction and a broadly available retail market. This appears to be the second category: a real transaction, but with explicit participation boundaries.

Future announcements should reveal the number and variety of issuers, whether transactions settle consistently, how ownership and voting are handled, and whether participants can transfer positions between approved venues. Costs, order-book depth and operational incidents will be more revealing than another first-trade headline. As such, there was a self-aware aspect to the movie, which knew exactly what it was and wasn't about to apologize for it. The final showdown between its two stars was emblematic of its excess and was, it turns out, a lot more real than you might imagine. In fact, Carradine and Norris seemingly gave everything they had to their climactic battle and ended up injuring themselves.

"Lone Wolf McQuade" isn't the type of '80s actioner that gets mentioned alongside such legendary features as "First Blood" or "Die Hard." But it's also not the kind of movie that should be lumped in with the worst examples of the decades' bombastic nonsense. In fact, it's easily one of the best Chuck Norris movies, and in its unapologetic embrace of the uber macho ethos of the era and its reverence for the great Spaghetti Westerns, manages to turn it into a sort of paean to both the golden age of action cinema the influential films that preceded it.

That's not to say the film is some sort of artistic masterwork. This is, at the end of the day, a film in which Norris and David Carradine beat each other senseless for our entertainment. "Lone Wolf McQuade" stars the former as Texas Ranger Jim "J.J." McQuade, an ex-Marine who is the epitome of the '80s action stereotype: a lone wolf lawman who routinely flouts the rules and lives a solitary existence in the wake of his divorce. Oh, and he also literally has a pet wolf.

The film follows McQuade as he tangles with David Carradine's Rawley Wilkes, an arms dealer who also happens to be a martial arts expert. It seems Carradine's Kung Fu specialist is selling weapons to terrorists, and J.J. isn't about to let that go unpunished. When Wilkes captures his daughter, Sally McQuade (Dana Kimmell), he all but guarantees a confrontation with J.J., which is exactly what we get in the film's climactic moments. That particular battle is a protracted slog in which Norris and Carradine go all out.

In a 1990 issue of Psychotronic Video (via archive.org), David Carradine recalled his showdown with Chuck Norris in "Lone Wolf McQuade." Neither actor opted to use a stunt double, making for what sounds like a brutal on-set experience. "We worked on that fight for four days," he said. "We shot it for four days. And we were little old men by the end of it. All our old injuries came back, we got new injuries, and we were stumbling around like little old men."

According to the actor, he and his co-star needed canes and crutches by the end of shooting their fight, which in Carradine's recollection was the result of Norris wanting to outdo his famous showdown with Bruce Lee in 1972's "The Way of the Dragon." "We were actually trying to go beyond that," continued Carradine. "I think we did. I think photographically we didn't. But as far as the fight was concerned, the combination of the two styles, you know, because I was very flowing and loose, and he was very solid and hard, I think we accomplished what we set out to do."

I'm fairly confident in saying that Jim "J.J." McQuade vs. Rawley Wilkes was not, in fact, quite as memorable as Tang Lung vs. Colt. But it was an impressive ending to one of Norris' best films and reminder of the physical talent that had made Carradine a star a decade prior.
