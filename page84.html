iPad mini OLED vs. iPad mini: What's new?

📅 Nov 04, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Apple's new iPad mini gets a bunch of upgrades, but they come at a cost.

Apple doesn't update its iPad mini every year, which is why each new generation usually gets a huge performance boost. The new iPad mini is powered by the 5-core, 2nm A20 Pro SoC, which Apple says is up to 2.7 times faster in graphics performance and up to 4.5 times faster in AI workloads than the A17 Pro in its predecessor. It comes in the same three storage configurations as last time, and the 512GB model features an extra CPU core and a dual 16-core Neural Engine.

While both models support Apple Intelligence and Siri AI, the A20 Pro should be more suited to demanding on-device AI tasks. The outgoing iPad mini offered 8GB of RAM, and while Apple doesn't officially list how much memory its phones and tablets have, the new iPad mini likely has 12GB of RAM. Battery life claims haven't changed, but the new iPad mini charges faster (capable of hitting 50 percent in 30 minutes) with a compatible adapter.

Unfortunately, color options have been reduced to just Midnight and Starlight. The price has also increased: the new iPad mini costs $200 more, starting at $699 for the 128GB model. If spec'd with cellular connectivity and a nano-texture glass finish, the 512GB iPad mini will set you back a whopping $1,249 — that's iPad Pro money. We'll let you decide if it's worth it; for now, let's look at the changes Apple has made to its compact tablet offering this year.

The big iPad mini upgrade this time is the OLED panel. That makes it the only other iPad in Apple's lineup with an OLED display aside from the iPad Pro. It's roughly the same resolution as last time, but can get much brighter at up to 1,200 nits of peak HDR brightness. OLED also brings other benefits: the new iPad mini is the same height and nearly the same width, but is thinner at 5.4mm and lighter at 255 grams — likely thanks to the thinner OLED panel. In comparison, the A17 Pro iPad mini maxes out at 500 nits of SDR brightness, weighs 293 grams and is almost an entire millimeter thicker at 6.3mm.

The new iPad mini also features a slightly larger 8.5-inch display, owing to the bezels that are now 24 percent thinner. The only major letdown is the lack of ProMotion. Plenty of users on X have understandably expressed disappointment about Apple sticking with a 60Hz display on a $700 product in 2026. However, in an interview with Christopher Lawley, Apple's vice president of Hardware Engineering, Dave Pakula, said that the "display experience is about more than just a single spec" and that OLED's faster pixel response time "makes everything really smooth."

If you're coming from an older iPad mini, the display on the new one will be a huge upgrade, especially for creative workflows involving HDR content. Apple Pencil Pro support is still present, too, making the new iPad mini a compact, color-accurate canvas for artists and designers.

Despite its nearly identical design, the new iPad mini is Apple's first water-resistant iPad. One of the ways it has achieved an IP67 rating is by replacing the speaker grilles with its new Acoustic Display system. This involves tweeters placed between the glass and aluminum body, with woofers firing through the display. The result, apparently, is punchier bass, clearer treble and more volume. It's similar to the Huawei P30 Pro's acoustic display, though that particular setup was largely designed to replace the earpiece speaker for phone calls rather than deliver a full stereo speaker experience like the iPad mini.

Connectivity upgrades are also present, including Wi-Fi 7, Bluetooth 6 and Apple's new C2 cellular modem system, which it claims should deliver up to 50 percent faster cellular data speeds.  Authentication is still handled by the Touch ID-enabled power button, and while the iPad mini's optics seem largely unchanged, the front-facing camera is now positioned along the tablet's longer edge, making it better suited for video calls in landscape orientation.

The new iPad mini runs iPadOS 27 and supports Apple's most powerful on-device AI model with improved dictation and the option to adjust Siri's pace and expressiveness. While only the 512GB variant gets the extra CPU and Neural Engine cores, the 5-core A20 Pro SoC in the lower-end storage variants still delivers a substantial performance boost over the A17 Pro. With so many good AAA titles like Control and Death Stranding now available natively on the App Store, the new iPad mini is shaping up to be a portable powerhouse for gamers.

Thailand: Two Uyghurs at Risk of Return to China

📅 Nov 04, 2026  |  Category: Travel  |  Read Source →

Click to expand Image Detainees stand behind cell bars at the police immigration detention center in Bangkok, Thailand, January 21, 2019. © 2019 Sakchai Lalit/AP Photo

(Bangkok) – Thai authorities should not forcibly return two Uyghur men to China, where they face grave risks of torture and other serious abuses, Human Rights Watch said today. The two men, whose names are not public for security reasons, were transferred from prison to immigration detention in Bangkok after receiving a royal pardon, apparently in preparation for deportation.

“Previous Thai governments have kept Uyghurs in inhumane detention for years and then complied with Beijing’s demands to send them to China, where they were forcibly disappeared,” said Elaine Pearson, Asia director at Human Rights Watch. “Thai Prime Minister Anutin Charnvirakul should end forced returns to China and allow the two Uyghur men to travel to a safe third country.”

The two Uyghur men have been detained by Thai authorities since 2014, when one of them was only 14 years old. They were serving a prison sentence for offenses related to an escape attempt from immigration detention in January 2020. On October 21, 2026, the authorities transferred them from Bangkok Central Prison to the Bangkok Immigration Detention Center Suan Phlu after they had served most of their seven-year sentence. Thai Foreign Ministry officials told a parliamentary committee that in August the Chinese embassy in Bangkok demanded their return to China.

Under Thai immigration law and regulations, the two Uyghur men will be deported to China unless they object to the deportation and are resettled to a third country. Lawyers representing them told Human Rights Watch that they have already informed Thai authorities that the two do not consent to be sent to China. Thai immigration authorities have repeatedly refused to allow the United Nations refugee agency (UNHCR) access to the men to determine their refugee status.

The Thai government is obligated to respect the international law principle of nonrefoulement, barring anyone’s forcible return to a place where they would face a genuine risk of persecution, torture or other ill-treatment, or a threat to their life or other comparable serious human rights violations. Refoulement is prohibited by the UN Convention Against Torture, to which Thailand is a party, as well as customary international law. Thailand’s Prevention and Suppression of Torture and Enforced Disappearance Act prohibits deportation when there is a substantial risk of torture or ill-treatment.

In March 2014, Thai police in Songkhla province, near the Malaysia border, arrested about 220 Uyghur men, women, and children, charged them with immigration violations, and soon after transferred them to an immigration detention facility in Bangkok. In several separate incidents around the same period, the authorities arrested dozens of other Uyghurs and placed them in immigration detention facilities around the country. In July 2015, about 170 of the Uyghur women and children detained in Songkhla were released to Turkey. A week later, however, the Thai authorities forcibly transferred over 100 Uyghur men to Chinese authorities, who flew them to China.

On February 27, 2025, Thai authorities took 40 Uyghur men from immigration detention and forcibly returned them to China. Upon the men’s return, the Thai authorities participated in a Chinese government-organized propaganda trip to show some of these men with their families. Despite promises by the Thai and Chinese authorities, there has been no credible monitoring of those Uyghurs since their return.

UN human rights experts issued a statement on the one-year anniversary of the deportation and said the families of returnees “have received no communication, no confirmation of the place of detention or whereabouts, and no indication as to whether their loved ones are even alive.”

Following the deportation, UN High Commissioner for Human Rights Volker Türk said in February 2025 that it was “a clear violation of international human rights laws and standards,” and urged Thai authorities to “ensure there are no further deportations and the remaining members of the group, including potential refugees and asylum-seekers, being held in Thailand are fully protected in accordance with their obligations under international law.”

Uyghurs are Turkic-speaking Muslims, most of whom live in the Xinjiang Uyghur Autonomous Region in China’s northwest. Over the past 10 years, China’s repression of Uyghurs has increased significantly as the authorities have intensified a widespread and systematic campaign of human rights abuses against them, several amounting to crimes against humanity.

The authorities have subjected the population to arbitrary detention, unjust imprisonment, mass surveillance, forced labor, cultural and religious persecution, and severe movement restrictions. Uyghurs considered to have left China illegally are, if forcibly returned, viewed with intense suspicion and subject to arbitrary detention, interrogation, torture, and other cruel, inhuman, and degrading treatment.

“Prior to the UN Human Rights Council’s review of Thailand’s human rights record on November 9, governments should press Thailand to immediately end its abusive treatment of Uyghurs,” Pearson said. “The Thai authorities should be protecting Uyghurs fleeing persecution, not jailing them, and certainly not returning them to harm’s way.”

How Brian Schottenheimer is failing the Cowboys

📅 Nov 04, 2026  |  Category: Sports  |  Read Source →

Watching the Dallas Cowboys in 2026 has become a tiring experience. We are not even halfway through the season, and things are already looking bleak. Losing is never fun, but watching them underachieve has become a weekly ordeal that has turned into pure exhaustion.

Every Sunday, it's a new level of disappointment. There's talent on this team, but the leadership and tactical execution have brought about regular failures that are tough to sit through. The most recent collapse against the Arizona Cardinals serves as more evidence that head coach and play-caller Brian Schottenheimer is fundamentally not the guy to help this franchise return to glory.

The issues start with Schottenheimer's bizarre tendencies. He routinely fails to make the basic decisions that would maximize his team's probability of winning. One example is his weird habit of running quarterback sneaks down at his own goal line. Instead of trusting his playmakers or scheming an effective play, he plays scared and chooses to waste a down, putting the offense in a tough spot. Every time he does this, the Cowboys have punted three plays later.

Another example is his treatment of Brandon Aubrey. We all know that Aubrey is one of the league's most lethal kicking weapons, but Schottenheimer finds ways to turn that into a disadvantage for the Cowboys.

Rather than pressing and trying to pick up extra yards to give him a more reasonable field goal attempt, the head coach contentedly runs the ball and settles for a short gain. His confidence in Aubrey treats those situations like an automatic three points instead of working for a shorter attempt. He unnecessarily creates high-stress kicking situations that have resulted in blocks and misses.

The team also cannot stop repeating the same mistakes. They look uncoached at times, committing the same self-inflicted errors week after week. Are we sick of neutral zone infractions yet? Every week. Early and often. It's just the same penalties over and over. It's become an epidemic. Whatever the coaches are doing to discourage this practice is not working. They keep jumping early.

As a play-caller, Schottenheimer is equally frustrating. The team doesn't have much of a rushing attack, as the offense has struggled to deploy the physical ground game they so confidently promote during the summer. It's been ineffective, and there have been moments when he has completely abandoned it, essentially acknowledging defeat.

That admission might be a little easier to accept if the offense possessed a high-powered aerial attack that many believe should accompany having one of the best wide receiving duos in the league. But they don't.

For some reason, the team's most explosive receiving weapons are underutilized. Schottenheimer fails to create mismatches, and the offense becomes very stagnant at times. No pre-snap motion or creative blocking schemes. The Cowboys' offense has turned into a predictable entity that defenses can absolutely shut down at certain points in the game, and those moments are extremely harmful.

Under Schottenheimer, the Cowboys constantly invent new ways to lose football games. Against the Cardinals, it was the inability to run out the clock. Earlier in the year, against the Ravens, it was a goal-line debauchery that couldn't win the game. Both instances put the pressure back on a porous defense to come through, and sadly, they did not.

The Cowboys don't just lose. They actively engineer their own downfall through a blend of situational ineptitude and executive panic.

But if you listen to Schottenheimer's postgame press conferences, he seems completely detached from reality. His media appearances have turned into comedy shows, highlighted by delusion and a lack of awareness. He stands at the table and claims to be a problem solver, yet no problems ever get solved. He throws out tired clichés and empty promises about how things need to get cleaned up, but each week the same chaos ensues.

The Cowboys are a 3-5 football team, and sadly, it's fitting for who they are. Each week, they are favored against these bad teams because, on paper, they should be beating them. They have more talent. But none of that matters when the coaching puts them at such a disadvantage.

Star players become average, and average players become bad. So far, the evidence strongly suggests that Schottenheimer is not a head coach capable of leading an NFL team to victory, and with each new week, this is becoming more and more obvious.

Why Fiserv Stock Triumphed on Tuesday

📅 Nov 04, 2026  |  Category: Business  |  Read Source →

The company showed signs of a potential turnaround in its third-quarter earnings.

Fiserv (FISV +12.46%), one of the sturdier and more experienced fintechs on the market, had a Tuesday to remember as far as its equity was concerned. After it served up an encouraging quarterly earnings report in the morning, investors eagerly bought the company's shares to send them almost 13% higher across that trading session.

Fiserv's third-quarter revenue not under generally accepted accounting principles (non-GAAP, or adjusted) slid by 1% year over year to $4.85 billion. Despite the slide, that figure was essentially in line with the consensus analyst estimate.

As for profitability, adjusted net income fell more steeply, decreasing by 15% to $936 million, or $1.75 per share. Those Fiserv-tracking pundits were expecting a slightly sharper fall, since they were collectively estimating $1.72.

Fiserv said in its earnings release that the erosion in revenue it suffered in its key banking customer segment was improving notably. Clients didn't migrate away from Fiserv back-end solutions as much as they had in other recent quarters, while the company reported "major client wins" in that business.

Investors were also heartened by management's doubling of its Project Elevate cost-saving and automation target to $1 billion from $500 million. Another confidence booster was the company's news that it would resume its share repurchase program, with a goal of buying back $500 million worth of stock in the current (fourth) quarter alone.

Fiserv maintained its full-year 2026 organic revenue guidance of a slump of 1% to break-even when matched against the previous year's figure. It's also sticking to its adjusted EPS forecast of $7.20 to $7.40, which is well down from the $8.64 it posted in 2025.

While no investor likes to see declines in either trailing results or guidance, Fiserv's moves position it for renewed growth in the near future. So now feels like it might be an opportune time to buy into the company's (hopefully continuing) recovery story.

Unai Emery has closed gap between Aston Villa and Europe's elite - but rampant Barcelona proved there is still a way to go as Lamine Yamal dazzles, writes OLIVER HOLT

Published: 17:17 EDT, 3 November 2026 | Updated: 02:35 EDT, 4 November 2026

John McGinn heard the question. His eyes darted around Barcelona's packed media auditorium for a second or two on Monday night. What was it like to be about to play in the Nou Camp, the Aston Villa skipper had been asked, as if this were to be some sort of spin off from Bill and Ted's Excellent Adventure.

McGinn was careful with his answer. He did not seek to downplay the nature of the occasion or the thrill that comes with lining up in such a majestic arena. But he was determined not to play the wide-eyed ingenu. 'What our club is trying to do now,' McGinn said, 'is make this normal, make this a yearly experience.'

So when he and his team-mates walked up the flight of steps from the dressing rooms into the vast bowl of the stadium on Tuesday evening, they were here to show that they were not arrivistes. Villa is a historic club. They won this competition in 1982, ten years before Barcelona ever did. They were not here for a treat or to get patted on the back just for turning up.

It is nearly 20 years since Liverpool became the last English club to win here and nobody gave Villa too much chance of ending that drought. They are being told constantly that the odds are stacked against them and that their much-admired manager, Unai Emery, may be tempted by the entreaties of struggling 'giants' elsewhere.

That was the reason why this game early in the league phase had attained so much importance. For Villa, it was a symbol of something they aspire to. They want to get used to belonging in this company, to walking up those steps, to emerging into the stadium in the pouring rain, and staring at players of the calibre of Lamine Yamal, Rodri and Pau Cubarsi staring back at them.

It still feels as if they stand on the cusp of something bigger but they blew their shot at proving that on Tuesday night. They needed to be at their best to have a chance of beating Hansi Flick's side, which sits atop LaLiga, and they were far too naïve in defence to get close to causing a shock. They are rising but this was a step too far at this stage of their evolution.

The Nou Camp, which is still in the midst of an extensive renovation and was only about two-thirds full, was ringed by towering yellow cranes, peering down at the floodlit patch of green. From their lofty vantage point, they saw Villa get off to the worst possible start.

Only seven minutes had gone when Emery's team gift-wrapped a present for the home side. Zion Suzuki played the ball out short to Joao Gomes but Gomes panicked under pressure from the Barcelona press and the close attention of Pedri. He played the ball square without looking, straight to Yamal.

Of all the players he could have found, Gomes had to find Yamal. The Spain superstar squared up Pau Torres and then curled the ball around him with a gentle caress of his left boot. Suzuki was helpless. The ball nestled in the corner of the net. Barcelona had scored 56 goals in 14 games before this match. Extending the hand of generosity to them must have felt particularly galling for Emery.

Villa almost hit back immediately. A stinging volley from Matty Cash was deflected wide and Barcelona looked jittery at the back. Nicolas Jackson played a square ball to Johan Manzambi, who should have equalised but scuffed his first-time effort wide of Joan Garcia. Barcelona were thankful for Garcia's intervention when he raced out of his goal to dispossess Jackson as he charged on to a through ball.

Yamal was rampant. His battle with Ian Maatsen was a mismatch and Maatsen was booked after 11 minutes for hacking him down. Villa were still intent on playing themselves into trouble. Suzuki repeated his mistake when he tried to play the ball out to Joao Gomes. This time, Pedri beat him to it and the loose ball fell to Gordon. Gordon hit his shot towards the corner but Suzuki redeemed himself by diving to push it away.

Barcelona had the better of the play. They looked like scoring every time they went forward. Time and again, either Raphinha or Yamal found space to run at the Villa defence, which was barely coping. Even in that context, though, it was an open game. Villa had chances, too. Manzambi forced a fine save out of Garcia. A Jackson breakaway was thwarted by a last-ditch block.

But seven minutes after the break, Barcelona doubled their lead. Fermin Lopez played a clever ball to Raphinha, who had found a yard of space in the box. Raphinha, LaLiga's leading scorer this season, drilled a left-foot shot goalwards and it deflected off the heel of Torres, wrong-footed Suzuki and rolled over the line.

Barcelona put the game out of reach midway through the half when Joao Cancelo forced his way past McGinn on the left flank and hit a sumptuous cross into the box with the outside of his right foot. Raphinha gambled on the path of the ball, darted behind his marker and tucked the ball away.

Villa manager Unai Emery will have plenty to think about after another Nou Camp defeat

Substitute Leon Goretzka hit the face of a post for Villa late on but the game was already gone. There was no shame in losing to a team who are one of the favourites to win the competition this season but there was frustration in the way Villa had handed their opponents the advantage.

Emery looked bedraggled as he stood on the touchline in the rain, arms folded, shaking his head. This was his 15th defeat in 16 visits to the Nou Camp. As Villa's coach fights to edge his team closer to the elite of the game, this game will become just one more bad memory.
