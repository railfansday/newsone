Ex-Aston Villa scout 'not impressed' by what he's seen from Zion Suzuki, says Crystal Palace man is better

📅 Nov 04, 2026  |  Category: Sports  |  Read Source →

Crystal Palace goalkeeper Dean Henderson was the better option for Aston Villa to replace Emiliano Martinez in the summer.

That is the opinion of former senior Villa scout, Bryan King, speaking exclusively to Football Insider.

The Villans instead paid Parma £30million to sign Zion Suzuki, who has enjoyed a strong start to life in the Premier League.

The 24-year-old's first significant mistake came when he dropped Alex Iwobi's corner into his own net on Saturday, throwing away the lead as Villa drew 1-1 with Fulham.

Yet the Japan international made several other key saves in the game and has been backed to bounce back from the error.

Nottingham Forest were said to have bid for Henderson in the summer transfer window, but had their offer knocked back by Palace.

Zion Suzuki drops Alex Iwobi's corner and Fulham are level! 🫨 pic.twitter.com/KwLFMRpKqk

Suzuki has drawn some early comparisons to Andre Onana, who joined Manchester United in 2023 and is now at Turkish side Trabzonspor.

The Cameroon international caught eyes for his superb ability on the ball when he first arrived, but mistakes quickly creeped in.

"He's an Onana. That's what he is. This is a goalkeeper who makes saves look so much harder than they are, more spectacular than they are," King told Football Insider.

"He wants to concentrate on the simple job. Catching it, organising it and keeping it out of the goal."

He continued: "He's a goalkeeper who certainly hasn't impressed me. Not for the money they paid for him.

"The way he keeps goal is very similar to the one they had at Manchester United, Onana. Again, very spectacular. Making quite simple saves look more spectacular than they should have been.

"Should be doing his job without the glitz. But they've obviously seen far more of him than I have to spend that kind of money and allow somebody like Martinez to leave the football club.

"Obviously, I think the main reason Martinez went is because him and his agent were continually moaning and making problems for the management and the club. But it surprised me they replaced him with this guy.

"If I'd have been looking for a goalkeeper that kind of money, I'd have gone for Henderson at Crystal Palace."

Henderson joined Palace in 2023 and has formed a deep connection with the Eagles in that time.

He signed a new contract in September, but it remains unclear whether the offer of Champions League football would have swayed him.

"I'm delighted. It's been an amazing three years – and I'm just so happy to extend my contract," he said following the announcement.

"My best years are in front of me and I'm happy to be giving that to Crystal Palace."

‘Painful’ – Alexis Mac Allister doesn’t hold back about ‘unpleasant situation’ at Liverpool

📅 Nov 04, 2026  |  Category: World  |  Read Source →

Alexis Mac Allister has spoken out about his ‘painful, unpleasant situation’ at Liverpool in terms of not being offered a new contract.

The Argentine caused a stir in September when he said that he was ‘very sad‘ not to have been offered a new deal by the club, having seen fellow 2023 signings Ryan Gravenberch and Dominik Szoboszlai both pen contract extensions earlier this year.

The Telegraph’s Dominic King reported last month that Anfield chiefs aren’t in ‘any rush’ to begin talks over a new deal for the Reds’ number 10 (who’s contracted to June 2028), despite his resurgence in form since the start of this season.

Appearing on the Los Edul show in his homeland, Mac Allister spoke candidly about Liverpool’s decision not to offer him new terms over the past few months.

The 27-year-old said: “We’re in a situation where I have a year and a half left on my contract. Last season I was told that the club’s idea was not to renew it, at least for now, and obviously that was a painful, unpleasant situation.”

When asked if the situation was making him despondent, he replied: “Not that much, because at the end of the day, I still had – and still have – time left at this club, which is what I want most – to stay here.

“The reality is that the manager has now changed, the sporting director has changed, and new investors are coming into the club, so I think I’m now in a position where anything can happen. We’ll see what happens in the future. I’ve always said that I’m very happy here, that I really enjoy it, and we’ll see.”

Mac Allister added: “Look at Szoboszlai and Gravenberch. They’re two players who were in the same contractual situation as me, and when I was told that, they renewed their contracts.

“It was more a position taken by the club, and generally that’s how things are done. At the end of the day, as a club you don’t want to lose a lot of money, and you don’t want players leaving on free transfers either.”

That the Argentine has spoken out publicly about his contract situation once more gives a strong hint that privately he’s seething at not being offered a new deal in the same way that Szoboszlai and Gravenberch were.

Admittedly Mac Allister’s collapse in form last season may have triggered a reluctance from the club to commit to a contract extension for a player who’ll be 29 when his current terms expire, and he’s a bit older than his aforementioned midfield teammates.

However, the ex-Brighton man has been excellent in the first two months of this campaign, almost as though he’s motivated by a burning desire to earn a new deal at Anfield and show that the hierarchy have been mistaken in their reluctance to tie him down for the long-term.

The 2022 World Cup winner is currently on £180,000 per week at Liverpool, a figure exceeded by 10 of his teammates (Capology), and it could be the case that he’s seeking a higher amount than what the club are prepared to offer him.

We don’t know exactly why his situation is taking longer to resolve than those of Szoboszlai and Gravenberch, but if he keeps performing on the pitch, then surely it’s just a matter of when and not if he’s offered an extension by the Anfield hierarchy.

Zcash Is Getting a Major Upgrade in November. Could This Be a Catalyst for Further ZEC Price Gains?

📅 Nov 04, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

The red-hot cryptocurrency has a significant upgrade just days away.

Zcash (ZEC +14.01%), the largest privacy coin by market cap, is about to get a major technical upgrade around Nov. 5. The NU7 network upgrade will cut the time between blocks of transactions from 75 seconds to 25, making confirmations roughly three times faster.

Despite a rough year for the crypto market, Zcash has exploded in value, gaining 134% year to date. Let's take a closer look at the NU7 upgrade to see what it changes and whether it could drive further growth.

The main headline of the NU7 upgrade is the change in block time. Like other cryptocurrencies, Zcash records transactions on a blockchain, with each block containing groups of transactions. Blocks currently have a target confirmation time of 75 seconds, which will drop to 25 seconds after the upgrade.

This doesn't mean transactions go through in 25 seconds. Each exchange sets a required number of confirmations to fully verify a transaction, based on its risk tolerance. If an exchange waits for three confirmations, then transaction times on its platform would drop from nearly four minutes (225 seconds) to 75 seconds.

NU7 also changes Zcash's fee allocation. Starting at activation, 60% of each block's transaction fees are removed from circulation and placed in the network sustainability mechanism, a reserve that provides a security budget for the network. Miners keep the other 40% of transaction fees. Originally, 100% of block fees went to miners. Like Bitcoin, Zcash has a hard cap of 21 million coins, so the reserve prepares for a day when fees pay miners rather than newly issued coins.

In addition, the upgrade adds per-block limits on shielded transactions and shuts down Sprout, the blockchain's original privacy pool. Funds must be transferred out of Sprout before activation, or they'll become unusable.

Execution issues are the primary risk for the NU7 upgrade, especially since it's being activated on a faster schedule than usual. Zcash's rules normally call for activation to occur approximately three months after release of the first software for the new network. This provides exchanges, node operators, and miners with ample time to update their software before it expires.

NU7's deployment document waived the three-month requirement. A release candidate started running on Zcash's test network early last month, but the stable software for the main network only launched on Oct. 22. Anyone using outdated software could end up on a different chain.

The closure of the Sprout pool also presents potential issues. Any funds left there will be lost forever, and moving funds off that pool could temporarily expose transaction amounts.

NU7 is an important upgrade to the Zcash network, both shortening block times and setting aside fee revenue in a reserve. But I wouldn't expect it to have a big impact on the privacy coin's price.

Blockchain upgrades aren't surprises. They're scheduled in advance, giving traders time to buy in and often turning the actual activation dates into "sell-the-news" events. Zcash has also already been one of the most successful crypto investments this year, so an upgrade may not be enough to move the needle at this point.

For investors, it's still worth watching how activation goes. A clean switch to NU7 would be a positive sign, while a delay or glitch could weigh on sentiment. Either way, the upgrade's value is more likely to show up in how Zcash works than in its price.

One of the most closely watched elections in the U.S., the race in Michigan tested how an antiestablishment candidate on the left would fare in a perennial Midwest battleground.

El-Sayed will be the first Muslim American to serve in the Senate.

The 42-year-old former public health official defeated former Republican Rep. Mike Rogers. Rogers lost his first bid for the Senate in 2024.

Harnessing anger over the cost of living and corruption, El-Sayed galvanized young and infrequent voters by focusing on passing single-payer healthcare and curbing the influence of corporate money in politics.
