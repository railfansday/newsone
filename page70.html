<a href="https://www.fiercehealthcare.com/payers/unitedhealthcare-unveils-new-care-model-leans-community-pharmacies" hreflang="en">UnitedHealthcare's new care model leans on community pharmacies</a>

📅 2026-11-18  |  Category: Other  |  Read Source →
UnitedHealthcare's new care model leans on community pharmacies" style="max-width:100%; height:auto; border-radius:8px;" loading="lazy">

UnitedHealthcare is teaming up with CPSEN for a new program that will lean on community pharmacists to reach Medicaid patients.

The insurance giant said Wednesday that its through the UHC Pharmacy Care Hub model, pharmacists will work alongside the payer's care managers and providers to help close gaps in care and support management for chronic conditions and other needs.

Participating pharmacies will offer a range of services, including telehealth, medication management, point-of-care testing and personalized supports. The program is built on a hub-and-spoke model, where pharmacists can then help lead the patient to the right site of care or through any follow-up if needed.

These interactions can also help track patterns in emergency care use, and help patients find a more appropriate setting if their needs aren't emergent.

The program is currently available to UnitedHealthcare Medicaid members in seven states: Florida, Kansas, Michigan, Missouri, North Carolina, Tennessee and Virginia. UHC expects to add additional states throughout the year.

Part of the goal, the company said, is to tap into the trust that local pharmacists have in the communities they serve.

“Community pharmacies are trusted, local access points for care,” said Arethusa Kirk, M.D., chief medical officer for UnitedHealthcare Community & State, in the announcement. “This new model expands the services independent pharmacists can help deliver, making it easier for people—especially those facing barriers to care—to get timely support closer to home.”

UHC said that most (89%) of Americans live within five miles of a pharmacy, making these locations an easier option to access for many. This is especially true for individuals who may struggle to find an appointment with a doctor or find transportation to a visit.

For example, a participating pharmacist in North Carolina, Madeline Clark, said in the announcement that a patient came into the pharmacy for a urine test, as she had a potential urinary tract infection. The pharmacy conducted the test and sent the results virtually to a provider through UnitedHealthcare's Doctor Chat tool.

A prescription was then filled for the patient in the same visit, and she was out the door within about 30 minutes.

Clark said the patient was thankful not just for the ease of that trip to the pharmacy but for avoiding a visit to the ER as well.

"Not only was this a lower cost of care in the healthcare system space, but it also was just incredibly helpful for the patient," she said.

<a href="https://www.fiercehealthcare.com/regulatory/department-labor-moves-end-expedited-green-cards-nurses-physical-therapists" hreflang="en">Department of Labor moves to end expedited green cards for nurses, physical therapists</a>

📅 2026-11-18  |  Category: Business  |  Read Source →
Department of Labor moves to end expedited green cards for nurses, physical therapists" style="max-width:100%; height:auto; border-radius:8px;" loading="lazy">

The U.S. Department of Labor proposed new policy this week that would end fast-track green cards for healthcare professionals like nurses and physical therapists—the latest in a string of policies from the Trump administration raising the bar for foreign-born workers and students across healthcare and other industries.

Currently, nurses, physical therapists and other workers “with exceptional ability in the sciences or arts” are granted an exemption from an administrative test in the permanent labor certification program (PERM), a months-long administrative process in which an employer must prove there are no able, willing, qualified and available U.S. workers for a permanent job ahead of sponsoring a foreign worker.

The exemption, referred to Schedule A, is given to occupations where the DOL recognizes a nationwide shortage of U.S. workers. Nurses and physical therapists have been listed under Schedule A for more than 30 years.

The Department of Labor, in its newly proposed rule, said it wants to eliminate Schedule A as it cannot “demonstrate that the occupations currently listed in Schedule A are still shortage occupations.”

It cited in the proposed rule a late 2023 request for information published under the Biden administration seeking input on potentially expanding Schedule A. Despite more than 2,000 responses, DOL now says it didn’t receive the information it would need to establish a methodology for regularly reviewing and updating Schedule A occupations, and questioned whether it has the statutory authority to make such occupational exemptions in the first place.

“The elimination of Schedule A would enable the Department to better ensure that U.S. workers have an opportunity to apply for available job opportunities, and adhere to the statutory requirements to protect U.S. workers assigned to it, as intended by Congress under the [Immigration and Nationality Act].”

The proposed rule, which is open to public comment for 60 days, also includes requirements for employers to advertise their open jobs more broadly and for a longer period of time to demonstrate a good-faith effort to recruit American workers before turning to a green card sponsorship. These and other proposed changes would be the first “comprehensive” reforms to the PERM program in almost two decades, the Department of Labor said.

If finalized as-is, employers who want to sponsor professional nurses, physical therapists and other affected roles “would be required to go through the lengthier and costlier PERM labor market testing process, adding time, cost and administrative work to a pathway that hospitals, nursing homes and rehabilitation providers have historically relied on to fill persistent shortages,” Caroline Tang, an Austin shareholder at labor and employment law firm Ogletree Deakins, told Fierce Healthcare.

Industry associations as well as other sections of the government have widely projected shortages in the healthcare workforce, including nurses, for the coming years. The Heart Resources and Services Association’s National Center for Health Workforce Analysis, for instance, projects (PDF) national shortages of registered nurses through at least 2038, with a projected 8% shortage in 2028 that is more severe in non-metro areas.

The proposed policy change is among several brought by the Trump administration in line with an order from the White House to prioritize domestic hiring—though many have been stymied in the courts.

Among the most prominent is a $100,000 supplemental fee the Department of Homeland Security attached to H-1B petitions that was vacated by two separate judged, and in August was succeeded by the somewhat altered proposal of an additional H-1B petition fee of $103,265. Another policy from the same department tightening how long nonimmigrant students may remain in the country (referred to as “duration of status”) was temporary blocked hours before implementation in September and fully vacated on Monday (PDF), though the former decision is already being appealed.

The Department of Labor has also proposed increasing the prevailing wage floors for several green cards that legal experts expect will be challenged by employers that would shoulder the resulting financial burden.

📅 2026-11-18  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →
HLTH 2026: Viz.ai’s chief clinical officer on takeaways, goals of AI collab with National Rural Health Association" style="max-width:100%; height:auto; border-radius:8px;" loading="lazy">

It’s been almost seven months since artificial intelligence disease detection and care coordination platform Viz.ai entered into a partnership with the National Rural Health Association (NRHA).

The partnership aims to help rural healthcare leaders better understand and implement AI tools across systems. That's been a personal and professional passion of Viz.ai Chief Clinical Officer Andrew Ibrahim, M.D., who discussed with Fierce Healthcare at HLTH his key takeaways from the collaboration thus far and forward-looking goals as it approaches its one-year mark.

Ibrahim said that the two organizations have done three educational events for rural health systems since the partnership kicked off in April. These series aim to show rural health administrators the specifics of AI—and whether they should adopt it, he said.

He noted there’s “sometimes a very big mismatch” when smaller rural health systems onboard multiple vendors.

“We've learned [that] many rural hospitals don't have the IT expertise and infrastructure to adopt some of these tools, and so it's actually been a force function to strengthen their hub spoke relationships,” Ibrahim said.

Strengthening these relationships, according to Ibrahim, has been key.

“It's actually been beautiful to see the hub-spoke relationship strengthened with the AI tool as the conduit,” Ibrahim said. “And it gives them a reason to interact more, and a way for the rural hospitals to strengthen their capabilities.”

He continued, “there's an authenticity about the moment where it's like ‘Yeah, we do need to help each other out.’ We're all kind of figuring it out as we go.”

In addition to the educational series, Ibrahim said frameworks are in development to aid rural health systems in navigating, implementing and deploying AI—which will be built into a toolkit.

One of Ibrahim’s personal goals for the one-year mark of the partnership is to ensure any rural health administrator can understand what an AI tool is when pitched.

“I think that would be a huge milestone,” he said. “Getting to 100% of hospitals at year one would be ideal—probably unrealistic, but that's probably the ultimate goal.”

And when it comes to metrics for measuring the collaboration, Ibrahim said he's looking for numbers that show participants are “keeping more care local” and retaining their workforces.

“It would be great to be able to connect the dots [and understand whether] adoption of an AI tool give[s] rural clinicians confidence, enthusiasm, willingness to stay in their community as opposed to dropping the services and moving back,” he said.

Another initiative from Viz that has “disproportionate benefit in rural communities,” according to Ibrahim, is its new partnership with Wolters Kluwer Health to integrate the Viz.ai One platform into UpToDate Expert AI.

“I'm pretty excited about being able to bring their level of clinical decision support into our rural hospitals,” he said.

How to Plan Your Holiday Meals in a Year of Unprecedented Recalls

📅 Nov 18, 2026  |  Category: Health  |  Read Source →

Follow Healthline on our socials for all things health.

Following a series of high-profile food recalls and outbreaks this year, many Americans may be concerned about keeping their families safe from foodborne illness during the holidays.

In fact, according to a recent national survey by Orlando Health, around 60% of U.S. adults say they’re more concerned about foodborne illness this year than in previous years.

Although several major foodborne illness outbreaks have ended and their associated recalls are no longer active, food safety remains an important concern. The Centers for Disease Control and Prevention (CDC) estimates that 48 million people in the United States experience foodborne illness each year.

However, there are simple steps you can take to reduce the risk of foodborne illness when preparing holiday meals and storing leftovers.

While there were several food recalls in the United States over the summer, two of the largest involved Salmonella linked to jalapeños and Cyclospora linked to lettuce.

These outbreaks prompted widespread recalls and raised concerns about the safety of certain foods, particularly fresh produce.

Although health officials have since declared the major outbreaks over, some consumers may still be hesitant to purchase foods associated with previous recalls.

Even with this declaration, you may still feel nervous about purchasing certain foods, but experts say you don’t need to worry.

“Here in the United States, we have the safest food supply in the world. The recent recalls show that our food safety system is working,” said William Schaffner, MD, Professor, Division of Infectious Diseases at Vanderbilt University.

Michelle Routhenstein, preventive cardiology dietitian at Entirely Nourished, told Healthline that past recalls do not mean entire food categories need to be avoided. The important thing is whether the specific product you have matches a current recall, based on details such as brand, lot number, package size, and expiration date.

“You should enjoy familiar foods, including properly cooked poultry, washed fruits and vegetables, and pasteurized dairy products,” she said. “The key is checking relevant recalls and practicing safe food handling.”

“Before shopping or cooking, review FDA and USDA (U.S. Department of Agriculture) recall information and compare any relevant notices with the labels on your ingredients,” said Routhenstein.

Check the pantry and freezer, too: something purchased months ago may still be affected.

“Stay alert to any news reports about food recalls and use good food preparation practices. Those simple actions should keep your holidays happy and safe,” said Schaffner.

Consumers can sign up for food recall alerts through the FDA or visit FoodSafety.gov for recall information from both the FDA and USDA.

Schaffner emphasized the importance of following safe food-handling practices when preparing everyday meals and holiday gatherings.

“Remember the longstanding advice: keep food hot or cold. Avoid long times at room temperature,” he said.

Routhenstein advised focusing on four basics: clean, separate, cook, and chill.

Schaffner noted the importance of refrigerating leftovers promptly rather than leaving them at room temperature for extended periods. He recommends clearing the table and refrigerating leftovers soon after the meal ends.

Routhenstein reiterated that you should refrigerate leftovers within 2 hours rather than leaving them out to cool.

Divide turkey into smaller portions and transfer sides and gravy into shallow containers to help them cool quickly, she advised.

Leftovers can typically be stored in the refrigerator for 3–4 days. If you don’t plan to eat them within that time, consider freezing them.

The important thing is to keep an eye out for any new recalls, practice food safety, and enjoy your holiday meals!

Gemini 4 Hydrogen: a faster model for general purpose robotics

📅 Nov 17, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Over the past month, Gemini 4 Argon and Gemini 4 Carbon brought frontier physical reasoning to robotics: understanding 3D scenes, reconstructing real environments in simulation, practicing tasks in sim before running them on hardware, and directly controlling robot arms from camera images. Last week, we introduced Gemini 4 Hydrogen, which brings strong spatial and embodied reasoning to a much faster model built for speed and efficiency.

To help developers put Hydrogen to work, today we're launching the Robotics plugin on Google Antigravity: a set of skills for hardware onboarding, simulation and robot control turning Antigravity into a one-stop robotics workbench. Together, they enable a developer to go from a robot in a box to one that can perform a working application in an afternoon. To install the plugin, open Antigravity, go to Customizations and search for Robotics on the Build With Google shelf.

Gemini 4 Argon and Carbon showed that a general model can drive a robot arm directly, and Gemini 4 Hydrogen preserves that ability. Give it a goal in plain language and a camera view, and it plans the task, writes and runs the control code, and keeps adjusting until the job is done. On the Artificial Analysis Robotics 0.1 benchmark, Gemini 4 Hydrogen controls a real 6-axis arm through eight everyday tabletop tasks, from pouring a drink to packing blocks into a box. Hydrogen scores 58, similar to Argon at a lower cost per token.

Hydrogen brings the specialized skills of our Gemini Robotics ER 2 (Embodied Reasoning) models into the core Gemini 4 model series, and it beats them on nearly every axis. On the public ERQA benchmark, Hydrogen outperforms Gemini Robotics ER 2 on spatial reasoning tasks such as multi-view correspondence and 3D trajectory reasoning. For developers using Gemini Robotics ER 2 today, we recommend trying out Gemini 4 Hydrogen for these workloads.

A fast, capable model is only half the story. Building a robot application has always meant gluing together drivers, calibration scripts, simulators, perception code, and a UI, which requires robotics expertise and a lot of time. Accelerating development for massive Git repositories

📅 Nov 17, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Android’s source code is big – the Android 17 open source release has 1,084 Git repositories, 1.62 million files, and takes 300 GB for a built workspace. Additionally, in a typical Android source tree with hardware vendor code and first-party applications, the size increases by 2-3x. Traditionally, it took over 30 minutes to fetch Android Open Source Project (AOSP) source code and well over an hour to build on even highly capable developer workstations. As software development evolved over the last 2 years, modern coding harnesses and LLMs have accelerated the development of many projects. These tools are used today for Android operating system development, but the long sync and build times make them less efficient. Both human developers and agents need quick setup, parallel workspaces, and fast build-and-test iteration times.

We built Another Build File Service (ABFS) to address these bottlenecks. ABFS is a full suite of software across a Linux kernel filesystem module, user-space daemons, and server-side infrastructure. ABFS uses content-addressability across the entire stack to lazy-load and cache source code, developer workspaces, intermediate build files, and build results.

Today, we are excited to announce that ABFS is open source on GitHub.The impact of ABFS to Android is:

ABFS already powers parallel workflows for developers and agents in Android, but it is designed to scale to other massive Git-based projects.

In the Android build system, developers define build rules in Blueprint files (Android.bp), Soong compiles them into Ninja files, and Ninja executes the final build graph (see the Android build overview). Similar to the Make build tool, Ninja is timestamp-based. If a build step’s input file is newer than the existing output file, Ninja rebuilds the output file.

Analyzing the AOSP code for Android 17 shows the sheer disk footprint and file count involved in a single checkout and build.

In a typical Android tree that includes hardware-specific repositories, board support packages, first-party applications, form-factor-specific code, etc., the code size is 2–3 times the size of AOSP (over 3,000 Git repositories), compounded across multiple active SKU and legacy maintenance branches.

Building the Cuttlefish emulator target executes a build graph with 192,249 Ninja actions. While local incremental builds are fast once a tree is already built, every new workspace, branch switch, or ephemeral agent session starts cold.Even though typical developer changes touch only 1-10 different Git projects, someone building locally without any remote execution might waste up to 80 minutes on a 64-core machine repeating work their CI system already built. Even if a developer edits a core library like system/libbase, 97% of the build graph is identical to the CI build.

Sharing cached build outputs across CI and local machines is also tricky in a codebase of Android's age and rate of change. Build rules occasionally introduce hidden dependencies (such as passing an undeclared schema file inside raw compiler flags), over-declared inputs, or undeclared outputs. In timestamp-based builds, hidden dependencies silently produce stale incremental artifacts, forcing developers to wipe output directories and run clean builds.

ABFS makes building Android fast by replacing massive local source checkouts with a virtual, content-addressable filesystem (CASFS). ABFS streams files on demand only when a build actually touches them and utilizes shared build caching to help eliminate redundant downloads and compilation.

On the server side, Git-pushers continuously ingest new Git objects from Gerrit or other Git hosts into a horizontally scalable gRPC ABFS Content-Addressable Storage (CAS) Server that indexes Git content, workspace snapshots, and build step results. On the developer host, a local Least Recently Used (LRU) Cacheman daemon, Mount Daemon, and Tap utility connect user-space build tools to the kernel filesystem so checkouts and builds stream only what they need. See the full ABFS architecture on GitHub.

At the core of the client is a virtual filesystem called Content-Addressable Storage Filesystem (CASFS), a Linux kernel module that exposes Git SHA content hashes via extended attributes. When a file's hash is set, CASFS populates its metadata immediately and downloads file bytes via Cacheman only when needed:

Initial checkouts download only lightweight directory metadata prepopulated with Git hashes. CASFS uses Git's object model natively, allowing standard git and repo operations to work transparently.

CASFS can also tap build processes. Tapping records every file actually read, written, or deleted into lists called patches, which are stored alongside cached build outputs. Ninja-go replaces timestamp checks with two-phase content-addressable caching:

Ninja-go accelerates Ninja-based builds like Android and Chrome; other build systems can integrate with ABFS's caching APIs using the same model (see the two-phase caching walkthrough on GitHub).

Each mounted checkout attaches to a persistent workspace that eagerly syncs modified files, git state, and repo state through Cacheman to the ABFS CAS Server—keeping mounts synchronized across sessions or across multiple developer hosts.

We released ABFS as open source because we want developers, Android partners, and any large-scale Git-based project to benefit from what we have learned and be able to scale.

While Android’s Soong/Ninja pipeline was the first acceleration target, ABFS was built to be extensible to other build systems. Content-addressable build systems like Bazel map naturally onto CASFS and there is already a proof-of-concept integration with Bazel.
