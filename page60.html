Grayscale Bets $1 Million on Zcash as Privacy Coin Tumbles

Grayscale is putting money and marketing muscle behind Zcash, even as the privacy coin’s price retreats.

The asset manager said Tuesday that it plans to direct $1 million to nonprofits and initiatives that support the Zcash ecosystem. It has already awarded $100,000 each to four groups: the Zcash Foundation, policy group Pretty Good Policy for Zcash, Swiss-based Shielded Labs and Project Tachyon, a scaling effort led by one of Zcash’s co-founders.

The funding comes from fees on The Zcash ETF, which trades under the ticker ZCSH. Grayscale said it intends to reinvest all of the sponsor fees it collects from the fund, for up to its first year as an exchange-traded product, into developing and marketing the network and educating people about it.

The commitment is voluntary, and Grayscale can change or end it at any time.

Grayscale also launched Zcash Invest, an educational website and social media presence, along with a national ad campaign, “Zcash: The Next Era of Crypto,” which kicked off with a Wall Street Journal wrap. The ads will run in print, outdoor, digital, streaming and social channels, featuring a zebra motif drawn from the Zcash community.

“Financial privacy is becoming essential as more economic activity moves onchain and AI makes it easier to analyze,” said Steve Vanourny, Grayscale’s head of index.

The ETF fund listed on NYSE Arca in August, after a critical flaw in Zcash’s shielded pool sent ZEC down about 50% just three months earlier before a fix arrived in July. Grayscale later split the fund’s shares to make them more affordable for investors seeking exposure to the, at the time, red hot privacy coin.

Competition is building, too, with another Zcash ETF in the works from the Winklevoss twins and Europe’s first Zcash exchange-traded product already launched.

Grayscale’s recent push, though, lands during a rough stretch for Zcash. ZEC fell 11.3% over 24 hours to about $1,043 on Tuesday after briefly dipping below $1,000, according to CoinGecko.

It’s down roughly 19% over 30 days and well off its October highs above $1,500, though still up about 75% from a year ago. Perpetual futures open interest stands at about $2.3 billion, and Zcash previously tumbled after leverage built up during its rally.

Appear Inc. Begins Pre-Production of Solid-State Battery, Targets Mid-2027 Launch

📅 Nov 10, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Field trials complete across motorcycles, e-bikes and scooters. Pre-production underway. Limited production on track for mid-2027, with full-scale production in 2028.

SAN FRANCISCO, Nov. 10, 2026 /PRNewswire/ -- Appear Inc., a San Francisco-based developer of next-generation solid-state battery technology, today announced that it has moved from proving its technology to producing it.

The company has completed field trials on three types of electric vehicle: motorcycles, e-bikes and scooters. It has also completed advanced manufacturing trials and started pre-production manufacturing with a manufacturing partner.

Alongside this technical progress, Appear has added new distributors and strategic partners in selective established and emerging markets. The company remains on track to begin limited commercial production in mid-2027, followed by full-scale production in 2028.

Most electric vehicles today run on lithium-ion batteries, which use a liquid electrolyte to move charge between the electrodes. Solid-state batteries replace that liquid with a solid material. This is widely seen as a path to safer batteries, with no flammable liquid to leak or ignite, and to more energy in a smaller, lighter cell.

Appear's solid-state technology is built around three priorities: safety, performance and manufacturability. For motorcycles, e-bikes and scooters, where weight, range and safety matter on every ride, that combination is especially valuable.

Following its advanced manufacturing trials, Appear has moved into pre-production with its manufacturing partner and is refining its processes for volume manufacturing.

New distributors and strategic partners in selective established and emerging markets are expected to give Appear regional market access and distribution channels at launch. Partner details remain confidential.

"We are moving beyond development and validation and into commercialization," said Prashant Rurs, Founder & CEO of Appear Inc. "We remain on track to begin limited production in mid-2027 and to scale up to full production in 2028."

Appear Inc. is a San Francisco-based company developing next-generation solid-state battery technology for electric mobility and other energy storage applications.

Mick Schumacher to skip wrist operation before 2027 IndyCar season

📅 Nov 10, 2026  |  Category: Other  |  Read Source →

Mick Schumacher will head into his sophomore IndyCar campaign without undergoing surgery on his left wrist, opting instead to continue with conservative treatment and physical therapy.

The German driver, who has switched from Rahal Letterman Lanigan Racing to Meyer Shank Racing for the 2027 season, had been managing the issue since the opening round of the year in St. Petersburg.

Following a multi-car chain reaction triggered by Sting Ray Robb in the race's opening stages, a small bone fragment in his wrist chipped off when the steering wheel violently snapped back.

Although a surgical procedure appeared likely at various points during the season, Schumacher and his medical team ultimately decided against an operation for the time being.

"The most important thing is really to avoid surgery," he explained. "So we've done a lot of physiotherapy and worked intensively to get it under control. After several MRIs, we came to the conclusion that it's better to go another year without surgery."

The severity of the discomfort inside the cockpit depends heavily on the car's handling characteristics. On circuits requiring smooth steering inputs, symptoms remain minimal.

"Everything that is gentle on the hand is completely normal and totally fine. The fast, jerky movements are the ones that usually hurt the wrist. I felt it most intensely when the rear was relatively loose," he described.

This physical dynamic may well explain why Schumacher's rookie campaign yielded some of his strongest performances on ovals. Because those tracks demand precise, measured inputs rather than abrupt steering corrections, the injury might have caused fewer issues here.

Across the six oval events at Phoenix, Indianapolis, St. Louis, Nashville, and Milwaukee, he secured 97 points, qualified fourth at both Phoenix and Milwaukee, and earned Indianapolis 500 Rookie of the Year honors.

While a future operation remains an option should his condition deteriorate during the 2027 season, Schumacher is entirely focused on his upcoming transition to Meyer Shank Racing.

"We'll just see next year whether it stays good or not," he said. "If it gets worse, we'll have to operate then."

It's not that I'll turn my nose at any game that isn't running at 60 FPS or higher, filthy PC gamer that I am. But if we're talking preferences, yeah, I do wish everything ran at 60 FPS at 1440p or below. This is easy for me to say, of course. I didn't have to move mountains to get a game of this scale running at 30 FPS to begin with. It's not just about the visible sweat, intricate clothing, innumerable environmental wrinkles, granular animations, or ray-traced shadows – all the little details that people at Rockstar have presumably achieved through blood sacrifice. This might actually be the best-looking game ever made by many metrics. That, to me, is the truly important, gameplay-affecting stuff.

It all amounts to more things for that frame rate to haul, and Rockstar seemingly chose 30 FPS with all the fixins' over higher FPS with all the sacrifices. I understand and respect that. Devs should make what they want, as ludicrously complex as they want it to be. But on that scale, my taste leans heavily toward frame rate for a few reasons.

Console generational leaps are getting smaller. This generation has, as far as I'm concerned, had only two real difference-makers: M.2 NVMe SSDs that dramatically improve loading times, and the spread of performance-focused graphic settings (or performance modes). As you may have guessed, I go for performance mode every single time.

Some games benefit more from having higher frame rates than others, but maximum FPS is always my pick until I hit 60. A fast-paced martial arts game like Phantom Blade Zero would be seriously hurt by a low or inconsistent frame rate, for instance. But I'm currently playing Persona 3 Reload, and its turn-based combat and simple environments aren't really bothered as long as the frame rate is half-decent and stable. But the choice is still simple: I never care about graphics in any way that matters, and I always care about smoothness. GTA 6 isn't a frenetic wuxia fiesta, but its driving and shooting definitely push it toward the more FPS-sensitive camp in my mind.

There's a point where I lose interest in graphical improvements, and that point came at least 10 years ago. I can appreciate a cutting-edge game, whether it's GTA 6 NPCs whose calves flex with each step or a huge RPG like Cyberpunk 2077 with every setting cranked to the moon. But if AAA games eternally looked like late-gen PS4 releases, I honestly wouldn't complain. My threshold for game that looks good is comfortably below that, and an art style far removed from realism is more likely to grab me anyway. Now and forever, I'm more interested in games that run better and console gaming that feels, not necessarily looks, more like my PC experience (again, M.2s really helped with this). The real or imagined title of 'best-looking game ever made' has never meant less, and a significantly uglier version of GTA 6 would still assuredly blow my socks off. I'll tell you what would really impress me: a promise that this runs at twice the frame rate of GTA 5, a 13-year-old game.

An Ancient Virus May Hold the Secret to What Makes the Human Brain Different

📅 Nov 10, 2026  |  Category: Health  |  Read Source →

Could a virus that infected chimpanzees millions of years ago help explain how the human brain became different?

Researchers at Lund University have discovered that ancient viral DNA embedded in the chimpanzee genome silences a gene involved in early human brain development. Their study, published in Science Advances, found that the gene, LINC00662, remains active in human brain organoids, where it helps support the development of nerve cells and the connections between them.

The finding offers a way to explore how humans and chimpanzees developed different biological traits despite sharing much of their genetic makeup. Evolution can change how existing genes are used, as well as which genes a species carries.

“The virus we investigated specifically affected chimpanzees. Yet humans and chimpanzees have largely the same genetic makeup. That is why it is important to understand the differences in how genes are regulated. Our findings suggest that one gene, LINC00662, plays a significant role in early human brain development,” says Johan Jakobsson, professor of molecular neurogenetics at Lund University.

The genetic remnant belongs to PTERV1, an endogenous retrovirus. Its DNA became embedded in the chimpanzee genome during an ancient infection and has been inherited ever since. The researchers found copies of the viral sequence at numerous locations throughout chimpanzee DNA.

Those sequences are systematically shut down through methylation, a chemical process that helps regulate gene activity. At the LINC00662 location, the inherited viral insertion also keeps the neighboring gene silent.

To examine this difference during brain development, the team grew brain organoids from human and chimpanzee stem cells. These miniature, three-dimensional structures resemble brain tissue and reproduce some features of its early development.

“The gene was strongly expressed in the human organoids, but not at all in the chimpanzee organoids,” says Patricia Gerdes, a postdoctoral researcher in molecular neurogenetics at Lund University.

The researchers then tested whether the viral sequence caused the shutdown. Using the CRISPR-Cas9 gene-editing tool, they removed it from chimpanzee cells. LINC00662 became active again.

“This shows that it is in fact the viral insertion that is responsible for the gene being switched off in chimpanzees,” says Patricia Gerdes.

A separate experiment explored the gene’s role in humans. When the team used CRISPR to silence LINC00662 in human brain organoids, genes involved in nerve cell development and the formation of connections between those cells became less active.

For Jakobsson, the results show how an infection can leave a lasting genetic change across a population, while helping explain how gene regulation can protect the brain or contribute to damage. The team will next investigate whether other viral remnants influenced brain evolution and whether such sequences are linked to brain diseases.
