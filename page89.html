"My first layoff in 14 years making games"

When you purchase through links on our site, we may earn an affiliate commission. Here’s how it works.

Yet another round of layoffs has apparently gone down at Tomb Raider studio Crystal Dynamics, as developers report that they've been cut from the studio. This is at least the sixth round of layoffs since the studio last shipped an original game.

Multiple developers have posted on LinkedIn about the layoffs, as reported by Vertical Slice. Those include UI software engineer Garin R. K. Richards and senior game designer Joshua Mitchell. "After several rounds of layoffs at Crystal Dynamics, today it was my turn," Mitchell writes. "My first layoff in 14 years making games."

Also cut is senior environment technical artist Kevin Baker, who led the development of "procedural tools used extensively in the creation of Tomb Raider: Catalyst's game world," and "integrated Claude Code into procedural tool development." We already knew that Legacy of Atlantis is being made with AI, and it seems the studio is keen to use similar generative tech in Catalyst – though not keen enough to keep the people responsible for it employed, I guess.

Crystal Dynamics has not shipped a new game since the release of Marvel's Avengers in 2020. Since then, it was acquired by Embracer Group, a parent company that's undergone no shortage of financial upheaval in recent years. In 2023, Embracer insisted that Crystal Dynamics would be unaffected by the ongoing layoffs at the broader company, a few months before laying off people at the Tomb Raider studio.

Counting today's cuts, there have been five more rounds of layoffs since then. There were three sets of cuts in 2025 alone, both before and after Perfect Dark – which Crystal Dynamics had been co-developing – was cancelled at Xbox. The studio insisted it was still "fully committed" to its upcoming Tomb Raider games when it announced yet more layoffs in March 2026.

The extent of today's cuts are not yet fully clear, nor is their effect on the new games. All three developers who've posted about the layoffs so far listed Catalyst among their credits.

Jensen Huang says he would be 'honoured' to return as Nvidia CEO if board asked him

📅 Nov 17, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Jensen Huang said he would be "honoured" to return as the boss of tech giant Nvidia if the board asked him to, in his first public comments since his shock resignation.

"I created problems for the Board that I deeply regret. I own my mistakes completely," Huang said on X, days after reports he had extramarital affairs, including with an alleged Chinese spy.

He said he had an "outpouring" of calls from company employees, investors and others calling for him to return as CEO of Nvidia.

But, as the BBC reported on Monday, Nvidia's board is not considering bringing him back to lead the company.

The prospect of Huang returning at this point looks unlikely.

The company's board is investigating his conduct, which includes an alleged affair with an Nvidia executive.

The AI chip giant is set to report its financial results later on Tuesday, during which the company's interim chief executive, Collette Kress, may address the situation with Huang.

Beyond the internal Nvidia investigation, Huang's affair with a woman suspected of being an asset for Beijing is set to see the billionaire questioned before multiple committees of US lawmakers in the weeks ahead.

The Nvidia founder said he had "made serious mistakes in my personal life".

Nonetheless, Huang's statement appeared to show some hope he would be able to move past the major corporate scandal.

Huang said people have continued to "reach out to me directly as the company navigates complex technology, market, competitive, and geopolitical dynamics".

"If the board of directors decides that my return is in the best interest of our shareholders, our employees, the company, and America, I would be honoured to answer these calls and return as CEO," Huang added.

Many technology executives in recent days have praised Huang's role in the artificial intelligence boom. A petition began circulating online over the weekend calling for his reinstatement at the company.

Yet, by Monday, many executives were not openly calling for Huang's return.

Brad Gerstner, a major tech investor who knows Huang personally and also holds a significant stake in Nvidia, last week wrote an open letter saying that Nvidia could consider Huang's reinstatement.

On Monday, Gerstner said during an event hosted by Semafor that his letter had been misunderstood and the investigation into Huang's conduct needed to run its course.

While Gerstner questioned the facts around Huang's departure, he added: "I don't know to what degree he misled the board. If I was on a board I would take that very seriously. Let's be clear no one is above the law."

Jensen Huang Says He'd Be 'Honored' to Return as Nvidia CEO, Despite Board's Resistance

📅 Nov 17, 2026  |  Category: Business  |  Read Source →

Jensen Huang Says He'd Be 'Honored' to Return as Nvidia CEO, Despite Board's Resistance

Jensen Huang said Tuesday he would be willing to return as Nvidia CEO, publicly addressing the campaign to reinstate him less than a week after his abrupt departure. His offer came despite reports that the chipmaker's board has no plans to bring him back.

“If the Board of Directors decides that my return is in the best interest of our shareholders, our employees, the company, and America, I would be honored to answer these calls and return as CEO,” Huang wrote in a statement on X.

The Nvidia co-founder also apologized for the conduct that triggered his exit. “I did not live up to the standards expected of everyone at NVIDIA, and I have made serious mistakes in my personal life,” he wrote. “I created problems for the Board that I deeply regret.”

“I own my mistakes completely, and I am committed to cooperating with the Board’s independent investigation,” he added.

Over the past week, I’ve been overwhelmed by the love and support of friends, industry colleagues, and people I've never met. Thank you to everyone who has shown kindness to my family and me. It means the world to me.

I came to the United States 53 years ago, and becoming an…

— Jensen Huang (@JensenHuang) November 17, 2026

His X profile underwent a separate change around the same time. By Tuesday, Huang had removed “CEO” from his bio, which now identifies him only as “Founder of NVIDIA”; the company banner and corporate affiliation badge also disappeared.

Huang resigned last Thursday after 33 years leading the chipmaker he founded in 1993. His departure followed disclosures about extramarital relationships involving Nvidia employees and a business executive whom U.S. officials suspect of connections to Chinese intelligence. Those intelligence allegations have not been established as fact.

The board's independent investigation is examining his conduct and whether sensitive company information or national security were put at risk. Nvidia has said it has no reason to believe company secrets were compromised; chief financial officer Colette Kress is serving as interim CEO.

“Since my departure, I’ve seen an outpouring of calls from NVIDIA’s employees, investors, business partners, and customers for me to return as CEO,” Huang wrote. He said many were reaching out directly as Nvidia navigates “complex technology, market, competitive, and geopolitical dynamics.” “NVIDIA is a once-in-a-generation company,” he added.

But the board's position—at least as of Tuesday—is that directors are focused on the investigation, not reinstatement.

The company is simultaneously looking for a successor. Directors reportedly approached Arm CEO Rene Haas, a former Nvidia executive, according to the Financial Times, although Nvidia said it had not formally engaged candidates and called speculation premature.

The movement to restore Huang has nevertheless attracted powerful allies. President Donald Trump said Monday he “would certainly not mind” a comeback, while Elon Musk backed a petition demanding Huang's reinstatement that had drawn nearly 192,000 signatures by Monday.

OpenAI CEO Sam Altman offered a more qualified defense. Altman, who was briefly fired and reinstated at OpenAI in 2023, said Huang had “long been a hero of mine” and that “heroes have flaws,” but stopped short of saying Nvidia should rehire him.

Huang also made a patriotic appeal, especially considering his outsting was especially rushed not because of his affairs but because of the possibility of him leaking secrets to China. “I came to the United States 53 years ago, and becoming an American citizen is one of the greatest gifts and proudest moments of my life,” he wrote.

“NVIDIA has been my life’s work,” he continued. “Nowhere but in America could a company like NVIDIA be possible. I love our country, and I love NVIDIA.”

He closed by thanking supporters and telling his family, “I love you, and I will spend the rest of my life being worthy of yours.” Nvidia, meanwhile, is continuing its permanent CEO search, with Kress scheduled to lead the company's quarterly earnings call later today.

BMW’s first US-built EV promises 456 miles of range and ultra-fast charging

📅 Nov 17, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

The iX5 is BMW’s first fully electric vehicle built in the US, promising an estimated up to 456 miles of range, ultra-fast charging speeds, and plenty of Neue Klasse tech.

BMW officially opened its new Plant Woodruff in South Carolina on November 13, marking the start of series production of its sixth-generation (Gen6) electric vehicle batteries.

The Gen6 batteries will power BMW’s upcoming “X” electric vehicles, starting with the all-new iX5 SUV.

The iX5 is being built about 15 miles away at BMW Group Plant Spartanburg, the company’s first fully electric model to be assembled in the US.

BMW Plant Spartanburg is churning out around 70 iX5s a day following a $1.7 billion investment in South Carolina to expand the plant for EV production and build the new battery assembly plant in Woodruff.

According to Robert Engelhorn, president and CEO of BMW Manufacturing in Spartanburg, the company is confident the iX5 will attract buyers, promising 435 miles of range, ultra-fast charging, and its updated Neue Klasse technology and design.

Engelhorn told Automotive News in an interview that “there’s a clear trend globally for EVs,” adding that “We will contribute to this growth from the BMW side.”

BMW didn’t provide a specific production estimate for the iX5, but suppliers were reportedly told it would be around 60,000 EVs in 2027, down from 100,000 it initially forecasted.

“We said we would begin assembling fully electric BMW vehicles in the US by the end of 2026 – and we are delivering on that commitment,” Engelhorn said.

A company spokesperson confirmed that the iX5 will initially use battery cells from China before switching to US-sourced cells from LG Energy Solutions plant in Arizona by the end of the decade.

With production now underway, the iX5 is expected to begin arriving at US dealerships in early 2027.

The 2027 BMW iX5 60 xDrive starts at $79,800. BMW estimates the 144 kWh battery will provide up to 456 miles of range, up from the 435 miles it previously estimated.

It features BMW’s new “double-X” light signature, which combines the headlights, daytime running lights, side lights, and turn signals into one unit. The Double-X headlights come standard, but BMW said you can switch them for simpler diagonal stripes.

Last month, BMW showcased the i5’s ultra-fast-charging capabilities during a media event in Southern California with IONNA and Alpitronic.

During the demo, the iX5 60 xDrive hit a peak of 464 kW, which BMW said enables it to add an impressive up to 170 miles of range in 10 minutes.

The electric SUV features a dual-motor all-wheel-drive powertrain setup that delivers a combined 578 hp (425 kW) and 593 lb-ft of torque. BMW says it can go from 0 to 60 mph in 4.4 seconds and tow up to 6,000 lbs.

Inside, the iX5 adopts BMW’s Neue Klasse design and tech, like the smaller iX3 SUV. The setup includes a new 17.9″ infotainment touchscreen and BMW’s Panoramic Vision, which spans the bottom of the windshield. There’s also an optional 14.6″ passenger screen for a third display.

Measuring 197.2″ long, 78.7″ wide, and 68.9″ tall, BMW’s new iX5 is about the size of the Porsche Cayenne Electric.

By 2030, BMW plans to be assembling at least fully electric models at Plant Spartanburg. “This marks the beginning of the next chapter in the success story of the largest plant in our global production network,” Raymond Wittman, BMW’s head of production, said.

Following Debrecen (Hungary), Irlbach-Straßkirchen (Germany), and Shenyang (China), Woodruff is the fourth site in BMW’s production network to begin series production of the Gen6 batteries.

"Original Meaning and the Freedom of Speech and Press: Some Illustrative Examples"

📅 Nov 17, 2026  |  Category: Other  |  Read Source →

Mostly law professors | Sometimes contrarian | Often libertarian | Always independent

This short new article of mine has now been published in the Notre Dame Law Review. The Introduction:

There are many important big-picture questions about original meaning and the Free Speech, Free Press, Assembly, and Petition Clauses. This short Essay will leave those questions for others. Instead, it will offer as illustrations some specific, concrete questions—and, in some instances, answers—about original meaning and the freedom of expression. I hope these narrow illustrations can help us think about the broader questions.

From the table of contents, which should illustrate the subjects I cover:

I. Broad Evidence, and Silently Consistent Precedents A. "The Liberty of the Press Is Not Confined to Newspapers and Periodicals" B. Symbolic Expression C. Civil Liability II. Scant Evidence: Provoking a Riot III. Ambiguous Evidence: Enjoining Repetition of Libels

Whether courts should shift the constitutional law of free expression to a more originalist footing is an important and difficult question. Perhaps it's a question that should be resolved entirely as a matter of constitutional theory—maybe such an approach is required by proper interpretive principles, or maybe it's forbidden by other principles (for instance, having to do with precedent).

But it's also possible that the answer may rest in part on how practically usable an originalist framework is likely to be. And to consider that, it's helpful to have in mind some practical questions. Some are already familiar, for instance questions related to damages liability in libel cases, obscenity law, and the like. This short Essay has aimed to help supplement those familiar examples with some others that haven't been much discussed; I hope others who think about the topic can find them useful.

How To Watch The Social Reckoning At Home

📅 Nov 17, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

"The Social Reckoning" is finally hitting the home market. It will become available to buy or rent on digital starting November 24, 2026, prior to its physical media release on January 12, 2027.

In case you're unfamiliar with "The Social Reckoning" writ large, here's the gist. Almost two decades after he won an Academy Award for writing the screenplay for "The Social Network," a 2010 dramatization of the founding of Facebook directed by David Fincher, Aaron Sorkin decided to make a sequel that focuses on what Facebook has become in the 2020s. Jesse Eisenberg, who earned an Oscar nod for playing Facebook founder Mark Zuckerberg in "The Social Network," declined to return to the role and was replaced by "Succession" veteran, Emmy and Tony winner, and Sorkin's "The Trial of the Chicago 7" collaborator Jeremy Strong. With Mikey Madison, Jeremy Allen White, Bill Burr, Wunmi Mosaku, Betty Gilpin, and Billy Magnussen starring alongside Strong, the project seemed almost too big to fail before it premiered in theaters on October 9, 2026.

Fail it did, though. Though it reportedly only lost a couple million for Sony (per Deadline), the film absolutely underperformed at the box office, and its rating on Rotten Tomatoes is a middling-to-bleak 54%. So what happened here? Well, from my perspective, "The Social Reckoning" is just a bad movie ... and /Film's chief film critic Chris Evangelista agrees with me. What went wrong as Sorkin tried to make a legacy sequel to one of the best films of the 21st century? Quite a lot actually!

I'll circle back to the "Social Network" of it all shortly, but the bottom line here is that "The Social Reckoning" just ... stinks to high heaven. From the embarrassing prologue featuring Portia Doubleday as a caricature named Kayleigh (I'm not kidding) to the fact that Aaron Sorkin keeps forcing Oscar winner Mikey Madison into outfits that would make her title character from "Anora" blush (in the name of corporate espionage), I found "The Social Network" to be a uniformly dreadful watch from start to finish. Not only is the script clunky as all hell — unfortunately, an ongoing problem with many of Sorkin's recent screenplays — but the story is attempting to make some sort of larger point that doesn't just fall flat but thuds directly to the floor. We already know social media makes everyone angry and insane, and we also know that social media has contributed to right-wing radicalization. Sorkin making a movie that beats us over the head with these two ideas and also ends in a full recreation of the January 6, 2021 insurrection is just as bad as it sounds in theory.

It's also incredible to me that Sorkin managed to cast a lot of great actors and coach them into giving some of the worst performances of their careers. Madison is an enigmatic and compelling performer, and Sorkin transforms her into the robotic Facebook whistleblower Frances Haugen, a deeply unlikable person who doesn't seem to understand human behavior. Jeremy Allen White's journalist Jeff Horwitz is nothing but an exposition machine, and Jeremy Strong's Mark Zuckerberg has no interiority and sounds like Kermit the Frog. The only person who's good in this movie is Bill Burr. Think about that for a second.

"The Social Network" might be one of my very favorite movies of all time, but I can tell you, from a completely unbiased perspective, that "The Social Reckoning" is a wholly undeserving sequel to that 2010 masterpiece. It's clear now, with plenty of hindsight and movies like this, that "The Social Network" was good because of director David Fincher and despite Sorkin. To be fair to Sorkin, he basically admitted that to The Hollywood Reporter after "The Social Reckoning" got soundly panned.

Discover how we built the open Agent Host Protocol to keep your agent sessions accessible across editors, apps, phones, and the cloud.

Models are now good enough to work for hours or even days on their own. You don’t have to watch over every thing they do, but you still need to be able to check in, no matter where you are. An agent can’t live only inside one application. The work should continue even If you close a window or switch devices.

In GitHub Copilot you can start an agent session in one place and pick it up in another. This session continuity runs on the Agent Host Protocol (AHP), an open standard built first in VS Code and adopted across GitHub. Here’s how it works, what we learned building it, and how you can use it to bring your agents into your own apps and workflows.

GitHub Copilot was first built in VS Code as an extension that bundled its own harness. This gave you access to your agents in VS Code, but it tied their lifetime to the editor. Extensions are attached to a single VS Code window. Close that window, and the agent is gone. Update VS Code, and every agent stops as the application restarts. Connect to a remote machine, and you get an entirely new extension host commit-locked against the version of the VS Code workbench you have.

At the same time, the Copilot app was being developed, alongside the existing GitHub mobile apps for iOS and Android. Each product was doing its own distinct integration with cloud-based agents to work towards delivering session continuity.

Each of these integrations solved the problem for one product, but developers also still want to be able to pull their agents into their own workflows–whether that’s an editor, a terminal, a phone, or a tool they built themselves–and they can’t do that if every client needs its own one-off integration. That’s why developers needed a protocol: a standard, independently versioned language across versions of VS Code, the GitHub Copilot app, mobile apps, and any other client that might host an agent. A shared protocol means every client sees the same agents, history, and state without bespoke integrations, and you aren’t tied to one app’s idea of what an agent session is.

The protocol needed to allow multiple clients to interact with the same set of agents seamlessly. Start a session in the cloud from your phone, for example, then continue it from your laptop. It should tolerate network interruptions, reconnections, and concurrent client mutations. And it needed to be full-featured enough for you to do real work from each of your clients.

At the same time, the protocol needed to stay out of agent behavior. Unlike the domains that produced the Language Server Protocol and Debug Adapter Protocol, the world of agents is evolving quickly. That’s why AHP avoids encoding how agents work, and instead focuses on how you want to interact with them.

You also need the protocol to be simple enough to implement on your own client or server. While the full feature set would necessarily be extensive, the core protocol needed to be minimal.

The result of these requirements is the Agent Host Protocol (AHP). It is oriented around URI-identified “channels”, which are the coarsest unit of functionality in the protocol. Every server must implement the root, session, and chat channels, but can also implement more channels that provide terminal support, MCP additions, or OTel reporting, for example.

Within each channel is a reducer-based state object, which drives most of the logic for clients. The server is the arbiter of the state, and each client sends a sequence of actions that either mutate the state or trigger side effects, like sending a chat message. This provides a few very nice properties. Reconnection and replay in poor network conditions are easy: a client sends all queued actions, presents the right ‘loading’ state, and waits for the server to reconcile and acknowledge the actions before confirming them. Clients and servers are straightforward to debug. And reducer-based state also plays nicely with popular UI frameworks like React.

Adopting the Agent Host Protocol in VS Code required building a new harness and agentic stack underneath it. The Copilot Chat extension, which used to provide all of VS Code’s AI and agentic capabilities, had been growing for over three years, reaching over 400,000 lines of TypeScript, not counting tests. But, as a VS Code extension, it was structured around the lifecycle of a VS Code window and depended entirely on the VS Code extension API. Neither that lifetime nor those APIs would be available in a standalone agent host process.

As we started building the Agent Host Protocol in early 2026, the Copilot SDK was also being developed, and it made sense to leverage it as the foundation for the new agentic stack. This was a big transition. It’s easy to build a harness implementation that says hello and can edit files—we were using the agent host to develop the agent host after the first couple weeks—but it’s another thing to build one that covers the long tail of features developers had come to expect from Copilot Chat.

GitHub has had Copilot cloud agents for a couple of years, allowing developers to run tasks in the cloud from any device. While VS Code was quietly developing the Agent Host Protocol, GitHub was looking to expand that capability to coordinate sessions running on your own devices via the Copilot app, or in cloud sandboxes. This was exactly the N:M problem that AHP was built to solve, so adopting the same technology made perfect sense.

From the outset, the GitHub Agent Host had different requirements than VS Code’s implementation. VS Code runs the host locally and can drop its host on other compute over SSH, containers, WSL, or Dev Tunnels, and it is actually included as part of the VS Code Server bundle. The GitHub Agent Host needed to run in the cloud and across a much wider variety of infrastructure, environments, and security constraints.

Those differing requirements are why they’re separate implementations written in different languages: VS Code’s in TypeScript and GitHub’s in Rust. For now, having two agent hosts has been a good acid-test for the protocol itself. How well does it work when two entirely different teams and sets of agents implement the same protocol independently?

Pretty well, it turns out. Like MCP, the Agent Host Protocol provides escape hatches via custom methods and _metadata on objects that consumers can use for out-of-band signaling. After several months heads-down on our respective implementations, our first task as we worked toward unification was inventorying the ways each implementation had extended the protocol. Many of these extensions reasonably stay GitHub-specific, such as the GitHub Agent Host using metadata to link to project boards. In other cases, variance was a sign of a gap in the protocol; early versions, for example, could not accurately represent shell-type tool output that wasn’t backed by a PTY. And some were bad assumptions, like VS Code’s client assuming a particular structure for the session URIs it received from the agent host. A protocol with only one implementation isn’t truly portable; as we taught our clients and servers to talk to each other, AHP became portable in practice, not just in theory.

Today, the Agent Host Protocol provides that same continuity across all VS Code and GitHub surfaces. Agent sessions running locally in the GitHub Copilot app, in cloud sandboxes, and in VS Code all share the same underlying protocol. To see how it’s used, read about how you can work from anywhere with the new connected Copilot cloud experience.

AHP was designed to be easy to use in clients and servers. Agent Host Protocol libraries are available in Rust, TypeScript, Kotlin, Swift, .NET, and Go, and agents are able to build clients and servers with little prompting. I have a macropad sitting on my desk that reflects my agent’s state in real time, and others have built everything from smart watch apps to Kanban-style task management tools against the protocol. Whether you want a companion app for your own agents, a way to bring agent sessions into your team’s tools, or AHP support in your own editor or agent, the protocol is open for you to build on.

You can connect to an agent host you implement in the Copilot app (enable Experimental > Direct Connections, then go to Environments > Add Agent Host Protocol Host) and in VS Code (via Add Remote Agent Host in the Agents window). You can spawn a standalone version of VS Code’s agent host by running code agent host in your terminal, or see the endpoints for the one in your editor by running code agent endpoints, and samples can help you and your agent get started.

The Agent Host Protocol is an open standard, and questions and suggestions are welcome on GitHub.
