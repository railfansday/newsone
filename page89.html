A key report for where inflation stood in October comes out Tuesday. Here's what to expect

📅 2026-11-09  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Falling gasoline prices provided some relief to consumers in October, but Federal Reserve officials are increasingly concerned that inflation pressures elsewhere in the economy are proving harder to contain.

Prices at the pump declined by more than 4%, according to the Energy Information Administration, providing a respite from a surge that had added more than a dollar a gallon over the past year.

However, the consumer price index for the month, which the Bureau of Labor Statistics will release Tuesday at 8:30 a.m., is expected to show only a slight let-up.

Meanwhile, Fed officials say they are worried that inflation initially driven higher by President Donald Trump's tariffs and the Iran war is proving more persistent than expected, with price pressures emerging in sectors beyond those directly affected by the shocks.

At a time when the central bank is increasingly confident about the state of the labor market, concerns over the cost of living are what are top of mind for the Fed.

"We've been moving up to what historically we'd consider full employment, and the big problem has all been on the inflation side. We go around, we talk to people everywhere in this district, all we hear about is affordability on the business side, the cost," Chicago Fed President Austan Goolsbee said Monday during an event in his home district. "So we've got to come to terms with this cost-side and inflation-side failure."

The CPI reading for all items is expected to show an increase of 0.3%. Core inflation, which excludes volatile food and energy costs, is forecast to increase 0.2%, according to the Dow Jones consensus forecast. While both would be slower than the respective September monthly gains of 0.6% and 0.3%, they still represent further price pressures.

There will be no annual reading for October because of the government shutdown a year ago, which prevented the BLS from collecting some data and compiling a full index level. Because of the data issue, there isn't a comparable figure for the index.

Bank of America, using an assumed baseline interpolating data for the period last year, estimated the annual growth rates at 3.8% for headline and 2.7% for core, both 0.1 percentage point higher than September and well above the Fed's 2% goal.

Goolsbee said the current run on inflation is defying what normally could be expected from supply shocks and tariffs, with the current situation intensified by the hyperscaler build-out related to artificial intelligence.

"It was supposed to have gone away by now, and we're now starting to see the inflation kicking up in areas that probably weren't from oil prices and tariffs, like a bunch of services. Now this stuff with the AI data center build-out is, to my eyes, starting to look more just like old-fashioned demand overheating," he said.

The massive investment in data centers is creating demand for construction workers, electricity and equipment, potentially adding to price pressures even as AI promises longer-term productivity gains, he added.

Though the Fed in October voted to hold its benchmark interest rate in place, markets are pricing in a better than 80% probability that the central bank will raise rates by a quarter percentage point at its next meeting in December, according to CME FedWatch data.

Goolsbee, who doesn't vote on the rate-setting Federal Open Market Committee this year but will in 2027, didn't commit to which way he was leaning on additional rate hikes, though he said current conditions could warrant action.

"The conventional wisdom there is: Be aggressive up front, to never let the economy overheat, because once it gets overheated, it's extremely painful to get rid of that kind of overheating," he said. "So I think we've got to watch out in that space."

The CPI release comes with economic growth strong — 3.4% in the third quarter for gross domestic product and tracking at 3.5% for the final three months of the year, according to the Atlanta Fed — and the labor market seemingly in solid shape as well. Nonfarm payrolls expanded by a much better-than-expected 165,000 in October, while the unemployment rate edged lower to 4.1%, though inflation-adjusted wage growth has been negative.

That puts the inflation reading squarely in focus.

Bank of America said it sees tariff impacts fading but that goods prices could be pushed higher because of AI demand. Further, the firm is cautious about "sticky" services prices as well as pressures from health insurance and autos.

The bank has been hawkish in terms of Fed expectations and sees a December hike as likely so long as there isn't an escalated stock market sell-off that might dissuade the central bank.

"This will be the final inflation report before the Fed's December 8-9 meeting. If our forecast is correct, we think it would effectively lock in another 25bp hike, all else equal," BofA economist Stephen Juneau said in a client note. "Absent an indisputably soft 0.1% [month-over-month] print, we doubt the inflation data will dissuade the Fed from hiking in December. The bigger risk to our call comes from financial markets."

Speedrunners are already breaking Zelda: Ocarina of Time remake with infinite and super jumps

📅 Nov 09, 2026  |  Category: Other  |  Read Source →

Speedrunners have wasted absolutely no time breaking The Legend of Zelda: Ocarina of Time remake, discovering glitches that let Link jump infinitely, launch himself into the sky, and skip entire sections of the game.

Nintendo’s Switch 2 remake launched on November 5, bringing a host of improvements to the 1998 classic, including a dedicated jump button that gives players more control over Link’s movement.

As we highlighted in our Ocarina of Time remake review, the new mechanics make exploring Hyrule better than ever.

However, it took speedrunners just a couple of days to discover that Nintendo’s new movement system could be exploited in ways the developers almost certainly never intended.

As reported by Polygon and IGN, one of the biggest discoveries is a technique called “actor jumping,” which allows Link to launch himself absurdly high into the air.

The glitch involves standing on a moving object, such as a Magic Bean platform or movable gravestone, using an item like the Hookshot, and carefully timing the targeting and jump inputs.

When performed correctly, Link goes flying, allowing players to bypass obstacles and reach locations far earlier than intended.

In one demonstration, a speedrunner shows how the technique can be used to access the Shadow Temple shortly after obtaining the Hookshot, allowing players to bypass the Forest, Fire, and Water Temples on their way to the Shadow Temple.

This could allow players to obtain the Hover Boots early, making subsequent sections of the Forest, Fire, and Water Temples easier to navigate.

Baldini: ‘Chivu and Fabregas good for Italian football, not afraid to play youth’

📅 Nov 09, 2026  |  Category: Sports  |  Read Source →

Italy Under-21 coach Silvio Baldini insists ‘there are no friendly games’ at international level, while Cesc Fabregas and Cristian Chivu ‘are good for football, because they teach and are not afraid to play youth.’

The Azzurrini secured qualification for the European Under-21 Championship, which will be played next summer.

Meanwhile, they have two prestigious friendly tests against France on Friday November 13, and Belgium on November 16.

“As I always tell the lads, there are no friendly games when you are representing the Nazionale, because when you wear the Azzurri shirt, you carry the honour and pride of our country, our culture and our roots,” said Baldini in his press conference.

“We cannot think that we are playing friendly matches. We need to give our best every time, as if it was an official competitive game.”

Baldini had for two games been the interim Italy coach when Gennaro Gattuso resigned in the wake of the World Cup play-off failure, but has returned to his main role with the Under-21 side.

“Youth is vital for our football, and I embraced this role with so much enthusiasm. I also want to thank the media, because you spoke a lot about the fact Italian football was in a tunnel, and the only way to get out of it was to make reforms and have more courage in using young players.

“The responses we’ve seen have been positive at club level. I think of Chivu at Inter and Fabregas at Como, talented coaches who are good for football, because they know how to teach players, they set rules, and are not afraid to play youth.

“This is a spell where coaches like this give a big contribution to the development of Italian football, so we are fortunate they are working here in Serie A. Now I hope that others follow their example.”

Several of the Italy Under-21 talents have been promoted to the senior squad for the final two Nations League games against France and Belgium.

They include Milan forward Alphadjo Cisse, Como’s Mattia Liberali, Parma striker Simone Lontani and Crystal Palace defender Honest Ahanor.

Brentford talent Michael Kayode had already made the step up along with Sporting midfielder Issa Doumbia and Roma’s Niccolò Pisilli.

Inside Designer Carla Fernández’s Vibrant Mexico City Home, Full of Indigenous Textiles and Art

📅 Nov 09, 2026  |  Category: Business  |  Read Source →

When the Mexico City-based writer and curator Su Wu started working on her new design book, a theme began to emerge: She kept noticing the presence of pre-Hispanic architectural and design traditions in modern Mexican interiors.

It was from this realization that Mesoamerican Modern: Ancient Ideas in Contemporary Mexican Homes was born. The book features 16 homes across Mexico—those of artists, architects, designers, and more—all shot by photographer Luis Garvan.

Wrought iron doors designed by Carla Fernández.

“When I began this project, I thought that we would be documenting a history, like the aura surrounding the artifacts in a museum, the apparitions of the past,” Wu writes in the book’s introduction. “Instead, what I’ve found is the vibrant, living presence of Indigenous ideas in Mexico.”

“In the guest bedroom, pink silk curtains dyed by Fernández cast a glow in the room. The bedspread is from her personal collection of Mesoamerican textiles, made of undyed wool in Teotitlán del Valle, Oaxaca.”—Su Wu

One space that exemplifies those ideas is Casa Flora, the colorful brutalist Mérida home and studio of the Mexican fashion designer Carla Fernández. As a designer, Fernández seeks to preserve the textile traditions of Indigenous and Mestizo communities through collaborations with artisans, bringing ancient practices to a contemporary audience. Fittingly, the home is full of Mesoamerican art and textiles.

Fernández led the renovation of the house, which dates back more than a century and now hosts workshops for Taller Flora, her design lab, which focuses on traditional Indigenous clothing techniques. (You can also stay in the home; the designer lists it for stays on Airbnb.)

Fernández’s husband, the artist Pedro Reyes, created several elements of the space, including a concrete outdoor shower, as well as some of the tiles throughout the space. His sculptures—including a prototype of Mitla Sillas, chairs inspired by the pyramids of the Mitla archaeological site in Oaxaca—are found throughout the home.

The outdoor shower designed by Pedro Reyes.

Reflecting on Fernández’s home, Wu tells Vogue: “I was very moved when writing the book with the idea of the ‘void’ in Mesoamerican architecture, and how so many architects, going back to the writings of Teodoro González de León, described the patio as a central, defining feature of the Mesoamerican home, a space that is at once enclosed and open to the elements. And in Carla’s home, I think there are many moments that exemplify this idea of the boundary between interior and exterior being not a physical one, but rather a marker of movement across psychological states.”

“A latticed window, known in Mexico as a celosia, is a central part of Mesoamerican architecture, used for cross-ventilation and to create a permeable sense of separation and shadow.”—Su Wu

Prototypes of Reyes’s Mitla Sillas chairs.

For Wu, Casa Flora is also a prime example of an idea that chef Elena Reygadas wrote about in her foreword to the book, “which is that handmade things are meant to be lived with no matter how precious, or especially if precious.”

“An equipale chair made of bent wood by the Wixárika people in Nayarit sits in front of a backstrap-loom tapestry of the Torre Latinoamericana, the iconic skyscraper in Mexico City designed by architect Augusto H. Álvarez. Made by artisans from San Juan Cancuc, Chiapas, the tapestry echoes a collection of dresses from Carla Fernández that featured historic buildings in Mexico City.”—Su Wu

Ever since NASA's James Webb Space Telescope (JWST) started observing the early universe, it has been providing almost as many surprises as it has answers. One such surprise are the so-called Little Red Dots, cosmic objects that seemingly disappear completely before the universe is two billion years old.

New research suggests that Little Red Dots may actually be connected primordial black holes, tiny black holes born not from dying stars but rather from fluctuations in the density of matter just after the Big Bang, in the earliest moments of cosmic history. Should they exist, primordial black holes would predate stars, galactic dust particles, and elements heavier than hydrogen and helium. Now, researchers have used JWST data to test whether one particular Little Red Dot, known as blackAbell 2744-QSO1 (QSO1), could host a primordial black hole.

"A black hole is an object born from the gravitational death of a star. Primordial black holes are a very different story," study team member Valerio De Luca, of the Department of Physics and Astronomy at Johns Hopkins University (JHU) in Maryland, said in a statement.

"They come from the physics of the early universe that predates the first stars, when the universe was expanding exponentially in time and space. So the mechanisms for creating primordial black holes are different," De Luca added.

Though scientists have theorized about the existence of primordial black holes since they were first proposed by Stephen Hawking in the 1970s, no hard evidence has presented itself. One of the issues scientists have puzzled over is how to tell the difference between a primordial black hole and a "traditional" or astrophysical black hole formed from the supernova death and collapse of a massive star.

"Little Red Dots could surround astrophysical black holes, and most probably many of them do, but they could also be primordial black holes, if primordial black holes exist," De Luca said. "So the question becomes, Which ones are primordial and how can we tell?"

De Luca and colleagues turned their attention to QS01, located around 13.1 billion light-years from Earth, which is seen as it was just 700 million years after the Big Bang. What makes this Little Red Dot stand out is the fact that it is almost entirely composed of hydrogen and helium. Because these were the universe's first elements, it could be more unspoiled and primordial than other Little Red Dots.

The team modeled how a Little Red Dot like QS01 could form from a primordial black hole. That was necessary because the circumstances under which this could happen would have to be very specific.

"There are constraints on primordial black holes from cosmological observations," said team member Loris Del Grosso, also of JHU's Department of Physics and Astronomy. "There can only be so many primordial black holes in the universe, for example, and there cannot be too many of them in a given cluster."

Considering three possible scenarios, the team found that the most likely situation is a smaller primordial black hole consuming surrounding gas and energy until it grew to be the size of QSO1.

"The door was open on this theory — that Little Red Dots could be primordial black holes," Del Grosso said. "We've now cracked it open a little farther by showing some scenarios are more likely than others."

Del Grosso and crew now intend to look at the other 100 or so Little Red Dots seen by the JWST to determine how many of them could have started life as primordial black holes.

Then scientists may finally have the evidence to say these long-theorized ancient black holes actually existed.

"We don't have a clear understanding of what happens in the early universe right after the Big Bang," De Luca concluded. "If primordial black holes do exist, then something had to happen to make them in the first place, which would be a groundbreaking discovery in physics."

The team's findings were published in June in the journal Physical Review Letters.
