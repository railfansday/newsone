Digital Asset Treasury Firms Continue to Buy Bitcoin and Ethereum. That's Why I'm Bullish on These Two Cryptocurrencies.

📅 Nov 09, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

These big buyers have slowed their purchases, but they haven't stopped.

Even after many of their smaller peers gave up over the past 12 months, the biggest digital asset treasury (DAT) companies are still buying Bitcoin (BTC -2.20%) and Ethereum (ETH -5.85%). When they do, it means there are fewer coins available for everyone else who might want to buy them. In the week ending Nov. 1, Strategy (MSTR -0.29%) bought 811 BTC, and Bitmine Immersion Technologies (BMNR -1.26%) bought 8,573 ETH.

Their pace of buying is down to a trickle compared to a year ago, but for the coins themselves, that doesn't stop me from being bullish about how they'll perform. Here's why.

DAT companies sell their stock, preferred shares, or debt to the market to raise capital that they then use to buy and hold crypto. When the market caps of these crypto treasury stocks fall beneath the value of the coins on their balance sheets, that plan stops working, and they often need to sell. Per Bitwise Asset Management's October 2026 report, at least a dozen businesses that launched Bitcoin treasuries in 2025 have since sold every coin, mostly to repay debt or return cash to shareholders.

Even Strategy, the pioneer of this entire business model, had to sell some of its Bitcoin to cover its needs.

In its third-quarter earnings report, it said it had sold $431.8 million of Bitcoin this year so far to fund the preferred dividends associated with some of its classes of shares, and also to fund stock buybacks for some classes of its shares. Strategy holds a total of 849,327 BTC as of Nov. 5, or about 4% of Bitcoin's 21 million coin cap. So far, it's still a net buyer despite making some sales.

Because it was still buying, the available supply of newly mined Bitcoin wasn't large enough to make up for the demand. Bitcoin mining currently creates 450 BTC daily. Strategy's hoard grew by 83,901 BTC in the second quarter, which was 205% of miners' output in that period. In Q3, its net additions shrank to 1,666 BTC, or about 4% of new supply, per Cointelegraph's tally of company filings.

Now, think about that classic diagram in every economics textbook which shows how (in theory) the price of an asset changes based on how much supply is produced relative to demand.

Strategy's purchases don't change anything about Bitcoin's fundamental properties, like its supply limit or how much miners can produce. However, it isn't the only buyer, and it controls a meaningful slice of the supply already. That means as Bitcoin's mining reward declines over time as a result of its halving, new buyers will need to compete with each other to secure a slice of their own. That's bullish.

Unlike Bitcoin, Ethereum has no supply cap, which means that DAT companies have a higher bar to clear in terms of exerting supply pressure before their efforts have a chance to translate into higher prices for the coin.

At the Ethereum network's current level of activity, the coin's supply increases by around 0.8% annually, which is approximately 2,764 ETH created on a daily basis. The network pays those new coins to stakers, while part of each transaction fee on the blockchain is destroyed to prevent the increases to the circulating supply from diluting holders much.

As of Nov. 5, Bitmine, the largest Ethereum treasury company, held 6,060,506 ETH, or 4.9% of the supply, and has bought more each week since June 30, 2025. In the last week of October, its purchases were worth 44% of Ethereum's net new weekly supply.

For Ethereum, the reason to be bullish is that Bitmine and other DATs buying the coin is just one avenue for it to gain in value. As its network activity increases, more Ether will be burned, putting upward pressure on prices. With an entire ecosystem of decentralized finance (DeFi) projects, services, and tokens that provide value, the chain has quite a few different avenues for growing network activity and subsequently boosting the price of Ethereum. That variety is one reason it can anchor a well-balanced crypto portfolio alongside Bitcoin.

Some of those projects in the ecosystem will inevitably flare out or become irrelevant, but even that process constrains the available Ethereum for buyers.

So, it makes sense to bet on the coin over the long term.

Bitcoin Slides to $73,510 Over the Weekend: Did Nvidia Scandal Trigger BTC’s $2,000 Flash Crash?

📅 Nov 09, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Bitcoin news: BTC fell to $73,510 on Sunday, 100 minutes after the Nvidia scandal broke. Thin weekend trading let the drop run further.

In Bitcoin news overnight, BTC USD price fell from $75,607 to $73,510 in ten minutes on Sunday evening, its lowest price since August 21. The worst of it came at 21:55 UTC, or 4:55 p.m. in New York. In that single minute, Bitcoin dropped 1.9% on Binance.

Most traders blamed Nvidia. Earlier that afternoon, CNBC reported that Nvidia’s board had opened an independent investigation into CEO Jensen Huang’s relationship with a woman U.S. intelligence agencies believe was passing information to Beijing (it’s just speculation for now).

Huang admitted the affair in an email to staff.

The bounce since then has been small. BTC traded at $74,184 on Binance on Monday morning, 2.2% below where it stood when the Nvidia story broke. The drop also marked a fifth straight losing week: the weekly candle closed at $74,851, below the week’s open of $78,354.

So did the Nvidia scandal really trigger the dump, or did thin weekend trading turn an ordinary sell-off into a sharp one?

The timing does not fit a straight reaction to the news. CNBC published its report at 3:12 p.m. Eastern time on Sunday, which is 20:12 UTC. Bitcoin traded at $75,880 on Binance in that minute. For the next 90 minutes, almost nothing happened. At 21:44 UTC, BTC sat at $75,607, just 0.4% lower than when the story appeared.

Then the selling started, and it started in Ethereum. Between 21:50 and 21:55 UTC, ETH fell 6.9% on Binance, from $2,213 to $2,060. Bitcoin fell 2.4% over the same five minutes.

That pattern points to a liquidation cascade, a chain of forced sales. Traders who borrow to bet on higher prices get their positions force-closed when the price falls past a set point. Each forced sale pushes the price down to the next group of bets.

The news still mattered. Nvidia is the biggest AI stock, and Bitcoin has traded in step with AI shares for weeks: Thursday’s chip-stock sell-off is what pushed BTC below its 200-day average. With stock markets closed, crypto was the only market open to sell into the headline.

A second headline added to the Bitcoin news mood. Secretary of State Marco Rubio called Iran’s demands “outrageous” and said talks had made no progress.

Weekend trading explains why the drop went so far. Over the past year, about $24 million of BTC changed hands on Binance in a typical weekend hour, half the $48 million in a typical weekday hour.

We checked every hour in that year in which Bitcoin fell at least 2.5% below the hour’s opening price. There were 29 of them.

Sunday’s drop came with $180 million of trading in the hour. That is the second-lowest of all 29, and about half the median of $366 million.

In other words, it took less selling than usual to move the price this far. The busiest minute, 21:55 UTC, traded $75 million of BTC on Binance, about three times what changes hands in a whole weekend hour.

Weekends do not cause more crashes, though. Only 6 of the 29 drops came on a Saturday or Sunday, slightly fewer than the 29% of all hours that fall on weekends. What a quiet weekend changes is how far the price moves for each dollar sold.

One note: much of the forced selling happens in futures markets, which this count does not include.

So both played a part. Nvidia and Iran gave traders a reason to cut risk, and thin weekend trading let the selling run further than it would on a weekday.

Bitcoin trades below all three of its daily exponential moving averages (EMAs), trend lines that give recent prices more weight. It lost the 200-day EMA, which tracks the long-term trend, on Thursday.

For traders following Bitcoin news this week, Monday’s stock market open is the next test. It is the first chance for Nvidia shares to trade on the news, eight days before the company reports earnings on November 17.

One of the major social concerns of the 21st century is the environment and the future that awaits it. Against this backdrop, recycling systems deserve closer attention. Cue SMALLrevolution, second-place winner of the designboom special prize at the Seoul Design Award 2026, whose traceable circular systems give objects new life without discarding their material history.

While there is a global consensus on the need to repurpose existing materials and avoid overexploiting fossil resources, Eurostat reports that only 42% of the waste treated in the European Union in 2024 was recycled, while around 30% ended up in landfills.

This is the context in which SMALLrevolution operates. The Danish circular design and manufacturing company proposes treating recycling as an overarching approach considered from the design stage, rather than simply a way to manage a product at the end of its life.

SMALLrevolution designs and manufactures products from waste, documenting the materials’ previous lives. This allows users to understand the journey those materials have taken before becoming the finished product.

As part of this philosophy of traceability and its commitment to sustainability, SMALLrevolution makes preserving the original colors, textures, and characteristics of the plastics it uses an aesthetic principle.

Rather than relying on chemical additives, pigments, or virgin plastic to create uniformity, SMALLrevolution embraces the raw aesthetic of its materials. The resulting furniture, decorative pieces, and architectural installations serve as a constant visual reminder of their previous lives.

One example is its work with Lufthansa Technik’s GuideU emergency floor markings. These strips are transformed into furniture and interior products that retain the original material’s photoluminescent properties, carrying their former function into a new setting.

One of SMALLrevolution’s defining features is its involvement across multiple product life cycles. The approach encompasses waste collection, material processing, design, manufacturing, procurement, and end use, maintaining traceability throughout and supporting individual and corporate accountability.

Today, SMALLrevolution works across sectors including healthcare, aviation, infrastructure, events, and consumer products. Since 2021, it has reintegrated more than 66 metric tons of plastic into commercial value chains, including 16 metric tons in 2025 alone. This has been achieved through collaborations with partners such as Novo Nordisk, Lufthansa Technik, Airbus, Clever, Roskilde Festival, and Danish hospitals. Business partnerships account for 98% of its revenue.

For the creators, working with these partners provides tangible proof that circular systems are not a utopian idea, but can become an operational model capable of functioning under real market conditions.

This approach connects with the values of the designboom Special Prize at the Seoul Design Award 2026, in which SMALLrevolution was awarded second place. Making the history of materials visible brings a creative dimension to recycling, while traceability and commercial results demonstrate the practical impact of its vision.

Keno RTP by Number of Picks: What Changes and What Doesn’t

📅 Nov 09, 2026  |  Category: Other  |  Read Source →

Home » Blitz news » Keno RTP by Number of Picks: What Changes and What Doesn’t

This article explores how keno pick counts affect RTP, hit frequency, and volatility. Using probability calculations and simulated sessions, it shows why more numbers do not necessarily mean better returns, and how paytable structure determines your long-term expected payout.

Ask keno players which pick count “pays best” and you get strong opinions: one or two numbers for steady hits, ten for the big multipliers. Both camps are usually arguing about the wrong thing.

Return to player, or RTP, is the share of every dollar wagered that a game pays back over the long run. In keno, that figure is set by the paytable, and each pick count has its own paytable. So the honest answer to “which pick count has the best RTP” depends entirely on how those tables were built. On a modern crypto board, the answer is often “none of them,” and the more useful question becomes what the pick count does to everything else.

Below, the math runs on a 40-number board that draws 10, using openly published paytables plus a few hypothetical edits. Probabilities are exact; session figures come from 20,000 simulated runs.

Traditional casino keno uses an 80-number board with 20 numbers drawn. Each spot count gets its own paytable, and operators calibrate each one separately. Published estimates put the house edge in land-based keno somewhere between 20% and 40%, and it can shift by several points from one spot count to the next. That is why old guides compare 4-spot and 7-spot tickets so closely.

Online and crypto versions changed the design brief. Many operators now publish a single RTP for the whole game and then build every pick count’s paytable to land on that number. When that is the case, the pick count stops being a lever on expected return. A well-run bitcoin casino keno table that advertises 98% should return close to 98% whether you pick one number or ten. Rounding multipliers to one decimal means no table hits its target exactly.

Before any paytable comes into it, the board itself fixes the odds. With 40 numbers and 10 drawn, each number you pick has a 1 in 4 chance of landing. Your average number of hits is simply your pick count divided by four: 0.25 hits on one pick, 1.25 on five, 2.5 on ten.

The chance of catching at least one number climbs fast as you add picks:

The chance of hitting every number you picked collapses even faster. One pick is 1 in 4. Five picks is 1 in 2,611. Ten picks is 1 in 847,660,528. None of this says anything about RTP yet. RTP is the sum of probability times payout across every paytable row.

Here is what that sum gives for three paytables published on a live bitcoin keno page that states a 98% RTP:

The spread between them is 0.04 percentage points. Over $10,000 of total wagers, that is a difference of about four dollars in expected return. Pick count and risk setting do not move RTP here; the gap to 98% is rounding.

The same arithmetic tells you what a fair one-pick table would look like. One pick hits 25% of the time, so a 98% table has to pay 3.92x on a hit. If a one-pick ticket somewhere pays 3x instead, it returns 75%.

If RTP stays flat, pick count changes two things: how often you get paid and how hard the balance swings.

That last line is the surprise. Five picks on this table is almost nine times as swingy as ten picks on Low, even though both return the same 98 cents per dollar. More picks does not mean more volatility; the paytable’s shape decides it.

Breaking each table’s return down row by row shows where the money comes from.

On ten picks at Low risk, the 2-, 3- and 4-hit rows deliver about 90% of the entire return. The 1,000x prize for ten hits contributes roughly 0.0001 cents per dollar, close enough to zero that removing it would not change the RTP at the second decimal. Medium risk on ten picks moves the weight up a little: the 3- and 4-hit rows carry 74%, and the 5-hit row adds another 17%.

Five picks is different. The 2-hit row carries 42.5% of the return and the 3-hit row 34%, but the 300x prize for five hits carries 11.7% on its own, the same share as the 12x row below it. On a five-pick bitcoin keno game, the top prize is a real part of what you are being paid back. On ten picks, it is decoration.

Knowing which rows carry the return shows where a stingy operator could cut quietly. Two hypothetical edits:

On ten picks at Low risk, cutting the 2-hit payout from 1.1x to 1.0x would lower RTP by 3.1 percentage points, from about 98% to about 95%. Halving the 1,000x top prize to 500x would lower it by less than one ten-thousandth of a point. The jackpot is the row players look at, and the one that barely matters.

On five picks, the picture flips. Halving the 300x prize to 150x would cost 5.7 points of RTP, which is more than the 2.8 points lost by trimming the 2-hit row from 1.5x to 1.4x. So for high counts, check the small rows; for low and middle counts, check the top prize too.

Here is 500 rounds at $1, across 20,000 simulated sessions per table. Expected loss in every case is about $10.

Same RTP, very different evenings. Five picks on Low produces the worst typical session and the best lucky one, because so much of its return sits in a prize that turns up once every 2,611 rounds on average. Ten picks on Low is the slow grind. With crypto keno, where a round settles in seconds, 500 rounds can pass quickly, so choose the session shape you want before starting.

Check that your probabilities sum to 1 before trusting the answer. If totals across pick counts differ by more than a few tenths of a point, some counts are priced worse; avoid those.

On provably fair games, you can also verify each draw against the published seeds.

To see how the published paytables look in practice, you can try keno at Jacks Club and run the spreadsheet check against the tables shown on the game page. The numbers should agree to within rounding.

You don't need to find the Great Fairies in any particular order, and you don't have to accept every gift. The most natural order follows the locations you'll visit throughout the story, in order, but you can visit and accept the gifts in any order once you have access to the areas.

Found at the top of Death Mountain, after completing the Dodongo Cavern dungeon and helping Goron City.

Din's Fire: Create a fiery dome around you when you cast. Uses 2 Magic Power.

After passing through the gate on the path to Zelda, you'll see a short path off to the right with a breakable rock wall. Use a bomb to clear the way to this Great Fairy.

Improved Spin: Strike nearby foes with a mighty wave of magic power. You will be vulnerable while charging it up.

After you give King Zora the letter, you'll gain access to the Zora Fountain. In this area, head to the island at the southern end of the map to find the Great Fairy's entrance.

Farore's Wind: Create a waypoint you can warp to when in a dungeon. Uses 2 Magic Power.

After beating the Fire Temple, you can find this entrance in the Death Mountain Crater, south on the map of the path that leads back to Goron City.

Doubled Magic: Permanently doubles your pool of magic power.

Once you reach the Desert Colossus, look for the breakable wall on the northern part of the map.

Nayru's Love: Grants invulnerability when cast. Uses 4 Magic Power.

After you obtain the Golden Gauntlets inside Ganondorf's Castle, head back out through the entrance and look for the side path with the Great Fairy's entrance that you should now be able to clear.

Halved Damage: Permanently halves the damage taken from foes.

Andri Gudjohnsen is one of the most clinical players in Europe, Blackburn Rovers must brace for interest: Opinion

📅 Nov 09, 2026  |  Category: Other  |  Read Source →

Blackburn Rovers bounced back to winning ways on Saturday as they defeated West Ham 2-1 at Ewood Park. The result sees Tony Mowbray and co enter the international break on a high, having lost back-to-back games against Swansea City and Stoke City beforehand.

And, somewhat unsurprisingly, on the scoresheet for Rovers once again was Icelandic talisman Andri Gudjohnsen. The 24-year-old found space in the penalty area with smart movement and met Jayden Fevrier’s cross with a diving header, powering beyond Mads Hermansen in the Hammers’ goal.

The goal moved Gudjohnsen to 10 goals in 14 league matches, and 11 in 15 across all competitions. It marked a continuation of his stunning form, which has seen him become one of Europe’s most clinical scorers.

Gudjohnsen’s goal record is eye-catching, but the extent of his clinical finishing is highlighted by his goals per shot. The Blackburn Rovers man has taken only 20 shots in the Championship, with 12 on target and 10 of them ending in the back of the net (FotMob).

This equates to a goals per shot rate of 0.50, and this ranks him among the best in Europe. According to FBref, the only player better than Gudjohnsen in this metric is Stade Rennais man Boulaye Dia, who has taken nine shots and scored five Ligue 1 goals, equating to 0.56 goals per shot.

Brighton & Hove Albion midfielder Jack Hinshelwood is tied with Gudjohnsen on 0.50, but it is only him and Dia that better or equal that of the Rovers man in Europe’s top five leagues this season.

For comparison, this is better than goalscoring superstars like Erling Haaland (0.24 goals per shot), Kylian Mbappe (0.20), Lamine Yamal (0.16) and Alexander Isak (0.28).

Blackburn Rovers are in a good position with regard to Gudjohnsen’s contract. He only signed in the summer of 2025, and his deal runs until 2028 with an option to extend.

However, with such clinical finishing and goalscoring form, the Iceland international is bound to draw interest. Then, the Rovers hierarchy will have to decide whether they want to cash in big while he’s in form, or hold out for a bigger fee further down the line while Gudjohnsen plays a key role in Tony Mowbray’s side.

If Gudjohnsen continues at this level, his trajectory will soon be far steeper than that of his club. That will make exit opportunities both tempting and more feasible, with teams willing to pay more to try and pry him away from Ewood Park.

Scott Parker Has a Selection Dilemma and the Numbers Are Damning

📅 Nov 09, 2026  |  Category: Sports  |  Read Source →

Plenty has already been written about Mads Hermansen, with transfer speculation continuing to swirl. Reports suggest Newcastle’s Nick Pope could be a target when the January window opens, but for now, West Ham’s goalkeeping situation remains firmly under the spotlight.

The numbers are difficult to ignore. A goalkeeper who isn’t making enough saves and is shipping goals at an alarming rate, 23 conceded in 15 games, is becoming a problem that cannot be overlooked.

One possible solution could be Alphonse Areola, who previously worked under Scott Parker at Fulham. There is also a potential opportunity for highly-rated youngster Finlay Herrick, who may be worth a closer look.

While Areola’s statistics last season hardly inspire confidence, conceding 37 goals in 20 appearances without recording a clean sheet, there is at least some context. By comparison, Hermansen managed seven clean sheets while conceding 28 goals in 18 matches.

The pair offer very different strengths and weaknesses! Areola is arguably the better shot-stopper but struggles when asked to play out from the back. Hermansen, meanwhile, has been preferred for his distribution and comfort in possession, yet questions remain over his save percentage and ability to keep the ball out of the net when it matters most.

Ex-Goalie Nuno failed to get the best out of Hermansen

Another concern is the absence of an established senior goalkeeping coach. That said, it’s worth remembering that the club’s former manager was a goalkeeper himself, while Rui Barbosa brought years of coaching and playing experience. Neither appeared able to significantly influence Hermansen’s development.

Currently, Billy Lepine, a former Dagenham & Redbridge youth player who has worked within the academy setup since 2008, occupies the role. Lukasz Fabianski is also assisting on a part-time basis with the academy.

And perhaps that’s where the story takes an even stranger twist.

Cast your mind back to 4th December 2024, when West Ham travelled to the King Power Stadium. Leicester won 3-1, ending a six-game winless run. On that night, West Ham peppered the Leicester goal with 31 shots, the highest number they had ever recorded in a Premier League match. Ten were on target and they dominated possession with 61%.

Standing between the posts for Leicester that evening?

None other than Mads Hermansen, who produced a performance worthy of Middle-earth, adopting Gandalf’s famous stance of “You shall not pass!” as he admirably defended his goal.

The previous year in the Championship he featured in the EFL team of the season. One of the most proactive ball playing keepers in the league, and completed 13 clean sheets.

If ever there was evidence of the “West Ham goalkeeper curse”, that might just be it.

The solution feels fairly straightforward. Bring Fabianski into the senior goalkeeping coaching setup, get Areola focused solely on football rather than the DJ decks, and bring in an experienced goalkeeper on loan in January to provide competition.

The ball is now firmly in Scott Parker’s court.
