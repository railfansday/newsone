Nicole Avant to Receive Visionary Producer Award at Bahamas International Film Festival (Exclusive)

📅 Nov 09, 2026  |  Category: Business  |  Read Source →

The producer and former ambassador to the Bahamas will also be bestowed with the title of Honorary Patron of the Bahamas International Film Festival.

Producer and former U.S. Ambassador to the Bahamas Nicole Avant will receive the Visionary Producer Award at this year’s Bahamas International Film Festival.

It’s a full circle moment for Avant, who is also being honored with the title of honorary patron of BIFF, as the award-winning producer, best-selling author and philanthropist attended the 2009 edition of the festival while the ambassador to the Bahamas.

Avant will be honored on the opening night gala of the festival, whose main part runs Dec. 10 to 14 in Nassau, after a three-day affair featuring a screenwriters residency and select film screenings in Harbour Island. The Visionary Producer Award recognizes Avant’s distinctive body of work as a producer who brings powerful and often untold stories to the screen. “Nicole Avant is a powerhouse in her own right: a producer, author and diplomat who has opened doors wherever she has gone, and her contribution to the Bahamas is part of its record,” said Leslie Vanderpool, the festival’s founder and executive director, in a statement announcing the award. “This honour reflects the regard in which she is held, not only by this festival, but by the film industry around the globe and The Bahamas as a whole.”

Avant, who is married to Netflix CEO Ted Sarandos, produced the 2019 Netflix documentary The Black Godfather, chronicling the life and legacy of her father, legendary music executive Clarence Avant. The film was named one of the National Board of Review’s Top Documentaries of the Year and earned Emmy and NAACP Image Award nominations. She went on to executive produce the award-winning 2021 film Trees of Peace, the story of four women who form an unlikely bond while seeking refuge during the Rwandan genocide. And in 2024, she was a producer on Tyler Perry’s The Six Triple Eight, which told the true story of the first all-Black female battalion to serve overseas during World War II. That movie won five NAACP Image Awards and received an Academy Award nomination for best original song.

Storytelling has been at the heart of many of her endeavors. She began her career in in promotions at A&M Records before becoming vp at Interior Music Publishing, overseeing artist relations and music licensing. In 2023, she added author to her credits: her memoir, Think You’ll Be Happy, became an instant national bestseller. A children’s book is now in development.

“One of my earliest and fondest memories as the U.S. Ambassador to The Bahamas was meeting Leslie Vanderpool and everyone from the Bahamas International Film Festival,” said Avant in a statement. “My husband Ted and I were fortunate to be a part of this festival in its early years, and it has been a joy to watch it grow into a platform that gives filmmakers from The Bahamas and around the globe a place to be seen and heard. I have only the fondest memories of serving and living in The Bahamas. To return as this year’s honoree, and to celebrate producers and the power of storytelling, truly feels like a beautiful homecoming,”

With Avant’s presence at the festival acting as inspiration, BIFF will have a focus on producers, the creative forces behind the camera that shepherd stories from idea to screen. As such, BIFF will host a Producers Lab running throughout the Festival, with sessions on producing, pitching and development, giving Bahamian and international filmmakers the chance to sharpen their craft and bring their projects to market.

Hoping to Max Out Your IRA for 2026? Here's How to Get It Done

📅 Nov 09, 2026  |  Category: Business  |  Read Source →

There's still plenty of time left if you're looking to build your retirement savings and save a little on taxes in 2026.

You can still make 2026 IRA contributions until next April, so there's plenty of time left if you'd like to put away some more cash for your future. Doing so could help you reach your retirement goals much faster, and if you're saving in a traditional IRA, it could also help reduce your 2026 tax bill.

Here's how to get started, whether you've already put some money in your IRA this year or you're just beginning to save.

You can save up to $7,500 in an IRA in 2026 if you're under 50, or $8,600 if you'll be 50 or older by the end of the year. This limit applies to all of your IRA contributions, not to each account individually.

High earners may have lower contribution limits if they're contributing to a Roth IRA. Double-check yours before you set aside any money here.

Review your 2026 IRA contributions to date and subtract the total amount from your annual contribution limit, determined in the previous step. The result is the amount you'll need to save to max out your account this year.

How you save that money is up to you. You could aim to set aside a certain amount per week, or put your year-end bonus in your IRA if you get one.

It's also fine if you don't manage to fully max out your IRA. Even if you manage to save a few hundred or a few thousand dollars, that money could still go a long way in the future, especially if it's invested for decades.

You can make 2026 contributions until the tax filing deadline in April, so you can spread this year's contributions out over several months if this is easier for you. But you should know that many IRA providers default to current-year contributions. You may need to contact your plan custodian to ensure your contribution is applied to the correct tax year.

Using January to April to make 2026 IRA contributions also means you'll be getting a late start on your 2027 contributions. If you'd like, you can just start fresh next year and focus on maxing out your IRA in 2027. By spacing them out throughout the entire year, you reduce how much you have to set aside in any given month.

Ever since NASA's James Webb Space Telescope (JWST) started observing the early universe, it has been providing almost as many surprises as it has answers. One such surprise are the so-called Little Red Dots, cosmic objects that seemingly disappear completely before the universe is two billion years old.

New research suggests that Little Red Dots may actually be connected primordial black holes, tiny black holes born not from dying stars but rather from fluctuations in the density of matter just after the Big Bang, in the earliest moments of cosmic history. Should they exist, primordial black holes would predate stars, galactic dust particles, and elements heavier than hydrogen and helium. Now, researchers have used JWST data to test whether one particular Little Red Dot, known as blackAbell 2744-QSO1 (QSO1), could host a primordial black hole.

"A black hole is an object born from the gravitational death of a star. Primordial black holes are a very different story," study team member Valerio De Luca, of the Department of Physics and Astronomy at Johns Hopkins University (JHU) in Maryland, said in a statement.

"They come from the physics of the early universe that predates the first stars, when the universe was expanding exponentially in time and space. So the mechanisms for creating primordial black holes are different," De Luca added.

Though scientists have theorized about the existence of primordial black holes since they were first proposed by Stephen Hawking in the 1970s, no hard evidence has presented itself. One of the issues scientists have puzzled over is how to tell the difference between a primordial black hole and a "traditional" or astrophysical black hole formed from the supernova death and collapse of a massive star.

"Little Red Dots could surround astrophysical black holes, and most probably many of them do, but they could also be primordial black holes, if primordial black holes exist," De Luca said. "So the question becomes, Which ones are primordial and how can we tell?"

De Luca and colleagues turned their attention to QS01, located around 13.1 billion light-years from Earth, which is seen as it was just 700 million years after the Big Bang. What makes this Little Red Dot stand out is the fact that it is almost entirely composed of hydrogen and helium. Because these were the universe's first elements, it could be more unspoiled and primordial than other Little Red Dots.

The team modeled how a Little Red Dot like QS01 could form from a primordial black hole. That was necessary because the circumstances under which this could happen would have to be very specific.

"There are constraints on primordial black holes from cosmological observations," said team member Loris Del Grosso, also of JHU's Department of Physics and Astronomy. "There can only be so many primordial black holes in the universe, for example, and there cannot be too many of them in a given cluster."

Considering three possible scenarios, the team found that the most likely situation is a smaller primordial black hole consuming surrounding gas and energy until it grew to be the size of QSO1.

"The door was open on this theory — that Little Red Dots could be primordial black holes," Del Grosso said. "We've now cracked it open a little farther by showing some scenarios are more likely than others."

Del Grosso and crew now intend to look at the other 100 or so Little Red Dots seen by the JWST to determine how many of them could have started life as primordial black holes.

Then scientists may finally have the evidence to say these long-theorized ancient black holes actually existed.

"We don't have a clear understanding of what happens in the early universe right after the Big Bang," De Luca concluded. "If primordial black holes do exist, then something had to happen to make them in the first place, which would be a groundbreaking discovery in physics."

The team's findings were published in June in the journal Physical Review Letters.

Should you leave Australia to avoid paying 47% tax on crypto gains?

📅 Nov 09, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Some Australian crypto investors are moving overseas to avoid the huge CGT increase from July 2027. But there are also less drastic ways you can pay less tax.

In part one of our special on how to avoid Australia’s massive hike in capital gains taxes on crypto in July next year, we looked at borrowing against your Bitcoin or setting up a self-managed super fund (SMSF), among four ways to mitigate the impact of the taxes.While an SMSF offers Australians the greatest tax advantages, it’s only suitable for older investors, or those who are willing to lock up their crypto for decades until they retire. For younger investors, moving overseas could reduce your future crypto tax bills to 0% — but there are less drastic options to save on tax too.

Australia’s impending tax changes have already inspired crypto king Fred Schebesta, founder of Finder.com, to become a tax exile in Singapore.

From July, the top rate on real, long-term crypto gains will increase to 47%, minus the effects of inflation on the cost basis.

For upwardly mobile, single crypto investors, the “nuclear option” of leaving the country to avoid giving almost half their gains to the government will look increasingly attractive.Many of the world’s top crypto hubs — Singapore, Portugal, Malta, UAE and Hong Kong — offer 0% capital gains taxes on long-term crypto gains in an attempt to attract wealthy investors.

Ben Simpson, founder of crypto advisory service Collective Shift, says some of his members have already pulled the trigger.

“Yeah, we’ve had a number of people that have sort of packed up and gone basically, ‘f—- this. I’m off to the Bahamas or whatnot,’” he says.

Andrea Yuen, CEO of Sywftx, says she is keenly aware of the tax advantages of living outside of Australia. “I lived in Singapore for 30 years. I do miss my no capital gains tax,” she says. But she didn’t move there for tax reasons, and cautions that uprooting your life to avoid a bit of tax is a pretty drastic step.

“I would never say that we should structure our lives only to think about tax outcomes,” she says. “There are many other wonderful reasons why I’ve come back to Australia and continued to live in Australia.”

Simpson has considered the idea, given he already spends half the year in America looking after Collective Shift US, but looking into the rules put him off. “You really have to cut pretty much all ties to Australia. So if you are genuinely happy to leave and don’t want to come back for the majority of the year, and cut your healthcare and cut all your ties back to Australia, then that is a strategy.”

“Bitcoin Jesus” Roger Ver gave up his US residency in 2014, and ten years later he was arrested and charged over failing to correctly report the value of his crypto holdings and paying exit taxes. He was forced to pay almost $50 million to make the case go away. Similarly, Australians leaving the country face a hefty exit tax on departure, which may require selling a decent amount of your stack.

“They tax you on the way out. If you’ve got a lot of gains or profits you’re sitting on, they’ll catch you when you leave,” says Adrian Forza, director of Crypto Tax Australia. “Arguably it’d be bigger in the future, but it’s still difficult to stomach selling off everything to leave.”

Forza explains you also have the option of paying the Australian taxes later on, but on the full value of the gain after you sell the asset. So if you expect Bitcoin to go up, you’d be best off paying the exit tax now.

Simpson is only 27 and the prospect of locking up his crypto portfolio for forty years holds little appeal. Instead, he trades and holds crypto via a company to benefit from the 25% company tax rate.

Running a single-person company is easier than you might think, but not worth the hassle unless you have a reasonable amount of capital and intend to make a lot of trades. It requires registering the company with ASIC and paying an annual fee, paying an accountant to produce financial statements and/or an annual company tax return and mucking about with payroll software and payday superannuation contributions if you plan to pay yourself an income. You can pay yourself a dividend at any time when you need some money.

“The company structure is good if you’re wanting to cap the capital gains tax at the company tax rate,” he says, but notes there’s a big catch: when you take funds out of the company, you need to pay the difference between the 25% company tax rate and your personal tax rate. “You still need to pay your marginal income tax rates. If you’re on the top tax rate, like 47%,” he says.

You may be wondering: if you have to pay your income tax rate anyway, which will equal the CGT rate from July, what is the benefit in paying a few thousand dollars and filling out a bunch of paperwork to run a company?

Simpson explains that the key advantage is that while you are building up your crypto portfolio by trading in and out of different coins, you only pay the 25% company rate for each sale. That means paying up to 22% less than CGT each time you exit a position, which builds into an even more significant saving over time.

“Instead of paying 47% because you’ve just taken the profits… then the CGT amount is only 25% — the company tax rate. So when you put that money back in, you’ve got more capital to play with.”

However, if you don’t trade enough, it might be taxed at 30%.

Forza says the advantages are greater for those on higher incomes.

It’s not a particularly useful structure if you just plan to just hold your Bitcoin, however. There are some potential advantages but arguably not enough of them to be worth the hassle.

The 50% CGT discount has been replaced by an indexation discount, which takes into account how much inflation affects how much you paid originally for an asset.

This means that investors who buy and hold dividend stocks or index funds for a long period will receive a bigger CGT discount. So should you drop crypto and buy a bunch of ETFs so you can pay less tax?

The short answer is no: the new CGT ‘discount’ is largely an illusion, as it simply measures your real gain after inflation.

Simpson says crypto has been hit hard by the CGT changes, but it still offers the best risk-reward opportunity.

History suggests that despite volatility and years-long bear markets, the return from investing in Bitcoin over time would still leave investors ahead of ETF investors even after paying more tax. Investing further down the risk curve with altcoins however, can either pay off handsomely or end in total disaster.

JPMorgan assumes that Bitcoin will compound by 10% on average in the years ahead. (Source JPMorgan/Bitcoin Archive)

Simpson says he convinced his younger sister, who is a basketball player on a low income, to invest in Bitcoin two years ago rather than putting money into a savings account. “She’s up like 40% or 50% in her portfolio and she’s like, ‘wow, this is going to get me so much further towards buying a house one day than sitting in cash and losing 10% [to inflation over time],” he says.

“That’s why I think Bitcoin makes so much sense to accumulate. And the tax considerations are not really there for most people I think.”

Yuen says crypto is one of the few asset classes that offers investors the chance to make double-digit returns in a few months, or to potentially double their money. Even after paying the new taxes, crypto investors with those sorts of returns will still be ahead.

“My dad always said to me, it’s a good thing, if you’ve made profits, you should be happy to pay some tax on it.”

Well maybe not happy about it, but you’ll still be making money.

TL;DR: A tokenized share transaction involving a Nasdaq-listed company has taken place on Canton under a US regulatory exemption, according to an announcement from venue operator TSV. A tokenized share transaction involving a Nasdaq-listed company has taken place on Canton under a US regulatory exemption, according to an announcement from venue operator TSV. The November 4 transaction involved shares of Canton Strategic Holdings and a permissioned pool using Tradecraft's automated market maker technology. The important development is not that a stock price was mirrored on a blockchain, but that the transaction was structured around the issuer, a transfer agent and verified holders.

TSV says DRW Cumberland purchased tokenized Canton Strategic Holdings common stock from existing shareholder Five North. Fairmint, an SEC-registered transfer agent, recorded the shares in token form in a verified wallet, and Flowdesk provided liquidity. The parties describe this as the first trade of its kind under the SEC's innovation exemption. Because this is an operator announcement, the scope of that first-of-kind claim should be attributed to the participants. What can be identified concretely is the named issuer, the parties, the permissioned environment and the transfer-agent role in the share record.

Tokenized equities can describe several very different products. Some are economic exposures issued by a separate intermediary; others aim to represent the issuer's shares with the rights and recordkeeping attached to conventional stock. Here the issuer-sponsored structure and transfer-agent involvement are central. They are intended to connect token holdings with the legally recognized ownership register, rather than just a derivative tracking a market price. Investors still need to know transfer restrictions, voting rights, eligibility rules and how corporate actions are administered. A blockchain token is not proof of all those protections by itself.

The reported transactions occurred in a permissioned pool with verified participants. That is fundamentally different from announcing that any wallet can buy US shares without checks or through a decentralized exchange. Restricted access may be a design choice needed to satisfy securities obligations, investor eligibility and transfer controls. It may also limit liquidity and interoperability compared with the open token markets crypto users are accustomed to. The test of such infrastructure is whether these controls can function at scale without making trading and settlement more cumbersome than conventional systems.

The company referred to an SEC innovation exemption announced in September. The transaction should be read within the specific conditions of the applicable relief, not as proof the SEC has deregulated all tokenized securities. Different venues, issuers or instruments may require their own legal analysis. Reporting on this area is especially sensitive to the distinction between an operational pilot, a live limited transaction and a broadly available retail market. This appears to be the second category: a real transaction, but with explicit participation boundaries.

Future announcements should reveal the number and variety of issuers, whether transactions settle consistently, how ownership and voting are handled, and whether participants can transfer positions between approved venues. Costs, order-book depth and operational incidents will be more revealing than another first-trade headline. If tokenized US equities become a durable market structure, those unglamorous details will matter at least as much as the chain carrying the record.

David Carradine Faced Off Against An Action Legend In A Forgotten 1983 Neo-Western

📅 Nov 09, 2026  |  Category: Entertainment  |  Read Source →

David Carradine came to prominence playing a martial arts master so it was only right that he eventually found himself in a duel with the great Chuck Norris. As Shaolin monk Kwai Chang Caine in "Kung Fu," Carradine traveled the Old West facing down all manner of challenger. But he never reckoned with the might of a solitary Texas Ranger with a deadly aim — that is, until 1983's "Lone Wolf McQuade," which saw Carradine and Norris do battle in what was one of the better projects of the 1980s for both actors.

By 1983, Carradine had come a long way from his TV debut in the short-lived Western series "Shane." Having become a household name thanks to "Kung Fu," the actor tried to subvert audience expectations by appearing in a number of exploitation flicks and B-movies. More well-known 1970s examples include "Cannonball," "Death Race 2000," and "Deathsport." In the 1980s, however, Carradine was basically churning out direct-to-video fare, and in "Lone Wolf McQuade" he came face to face with the king of schlocky DTV actioners, Chuck Norris.

The neo-western was directed by Steve Carver, who'd worked at Roger Corman's New World Pictures at the same time Carradine had fronted several of the studio's features in the 1970s. With "Lone Wolf McQuade," Carver paid homage to the Spaghetti Westerns that had helped move the Western genre into its revisionist era. As such, there was a self-aware aspect to the movie, which knew exactly what it was and wasn't about to apologize for it. The final showdown between its two stars was emblematic of its excess and was, it turns out, a lot more real than you might imagine. In fact, Carradine and Norris seemingly gave everything they had to their climactic battle and ended up injuring themselves.

"Lone Wolf McQuade" isn't the type of '80s actioner that gets mentioned alongside such legendary features as "First Blood" or "Die Hard." But it's also not the kind of movie that should be lumped in with the worst examples of the decades' bombastic nonsense. In fact, it's easily one of the best Chuck Norris movies, and in its unapologetic embrace of the uber macho ethos of the era and its reverence for the great Spaghetti Westerns, manages to turn it into a sort of paean to both the golden age of action cinema the influential films that preceded it.

That's not to say the film is some sort of artistic masterwork. This is, at the end of the day, a film in which Norris and David Carradine beat each other senseless for our entertainment. "Lone Wolf McQuade" stars the former as Texas Ranger Jim "J.J." McQuade, an ex-Marine who is the epitome of the '80s action stereotype: a lone wolf lawman who routinely flouts the rules and lives a solitary existence in the wake of his divorce. Oh, and he also literally has a pet wolf.

The film follows McQuade as he tangles with David Carradine's Rawley Wilkes, an arms dealer who also happens to be a martial arts expert. It seems Carradine's Kung Fu specialist is selling weapons to terrorists, and J.J. isn't about to let that go unpunished. When Wilkes captures his daughter, Sally McQuade (Dana Kimmell), he all but guarantees a confrontation with J.J., which is exactly what we get in the film's climactic moments. That particular battle is a protracted slog in which Norris and Carradine go all out.

In a 1990 issue of Psychotronic Video (via archive.org), David Carradine recalled his showdown with Chuck Norris in "Lone Wolf McQuade." Neither actor opted to use a stunt double, making for what sounds like a brutal on-set experience. "We worked on that fight for four days," he said. "We shot it for four days. And we were little old men by the end of it. All our old injuries came back, we got new injuries, and we were stumbling around like little old men."

According to the actor, he and his co-star needed canes and crutches by the end of shooting their fight, which in Carradine's recollection was the result of Norris wanting to outdo his famous showdown with Bruce Lee in 1972's "The Way of the Dragon." "We were actually trying to go beyond that," continued Carradine. "I think we did. I think photographically we didn't. But as far as the fight was concerned, the combination of the two styles, you know, because I was very flowing and loose, and he was very solid and hard, I think we accomplished what we set out to do."

I'm fairly confident in saying that Jim "J.J." McQuade vs. Rawley Wilkes was not, in fact, quite as memorable as Tang Lung vs. Colt. But it was an impressive ending to one of Norris' best films and reminder of the physical talent that had made Carradine a star a decade prior.

5 Nintendo Switch 2 features everyone should know about

📅 Nov 09, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

The Switch 2 has lots of little tricks to discover: some inherited from the original Switch and some new.

Chances are that you aren't taking full advantage of everything your Nintendo Switch 2 can do. It's easy to jump right into games and ignore the various settings that help your system run at its best.

The Switch 2 is a pretty direct upgrade to the original Switch — its design, OS, controllers and other aspects are refinements rather than something entirely new. That means many of the Switch 1's tips and tricks work on the newer console too, so if you're a Switch veteran you might already know where to look. But there are other easy-to-miss improvements to find, and they're unique to the newer system.

Whether it's making controls more comfortable or repurposing hardware you already have, you should know about the Switch 2's neat little features. For instance, you might have overlooked the option to share games with friends, which can save you all money. Try these features out before you jump into a game on your next session.

The Joy-Con 2 controllers are larger than the original models but still small enough to sink between the couch cushions or lose sight of underneath a blanket. Next time you can't find your controllers, your console can help. Open the Controllers page from the main menu, then hit Search for Controllers.

All compatible controllers you've paired to the system will appear; to find one, highlight it and hold L or R (or press the touchscreen). While you're holding the button, the controller will vibrate and play a sound, helping you track it down. If you can't find one, try walking around with the console to get back in range of it.

There are a few caveats here. While you can pair original Switch controllers with a Switch 2, this feature can't locate them. Third-party Switch controllers may or may not work. You can't ping a dead controller with this, and it won't find a controller that's paired with another console.

Most laptops, phones and similar devices now include a feature to cap their charging at 80 percent (or similar values). This is designed to protect the health of the device's battery over several years, since keeping lithium-ion batteries at 100 percent for extended periods wears them faster than lower percentages.

Your Switch 2 can also do this: open Settings and scroll to System. About halfway down, you'll see a toggle titled Stop Charging Around 80-90%. Turn this on, and your system will limit itself when it's on the dock or otherwise charging. If you're headed on a long trip, feel free to shut this off first to fully juice up. But in general, especially if your Switch 2 sits on the dock most of the time, this is worth having on.

Speaking of charging, the System Battery (%) option next to this is appreciated, too. It will show the exact battery level instead of a general symbol.

While many modern games have expansive control scheme options, sometimes you can't adjust exactly what you'd like. For those cases, you're able to remap buttons at the system level. Open Settings and go to Accessibility. At the top, choose Button Mapping — if you plan to use this often, enable Button Mapping in Quick Settings and a shortcut to this tool will appear when you hold the Home button.

In this menu, you'll see each connected controller on the left side. Select one and choose Edit Controller's Mappings to show its buttons, where you're free to remap most of the inputs to anything else. For example, you could swap the ZR and ZL buttons with R and L, or move an inconvenient input to a button that's in closer reach.

The C button is of particular interest; if you don't use GameChat, you can disable the button to avoid accidental activation. Alternatively, turn it into a secondary input for another button, like a D-pad direction or pressing one of the sticks. Check the Control Stick Settings if you'd like to swap the left and right sticks or use a Joy-Con 2 vertically when a game intends you to hold it horizontally.

While you can't apply a remap to a certain game, you can save up to five profiles per controller and swap them as needed. After hitting Save, choose Save as a Preset and give it a name. When remapping is applied, you'll see a blue wrench icon at the bottom-left of the menu and anywhere else that controller icon appears. Games are not aware of remapping done this way, so if you've swapped ZR and R, for example, you'll need to press the latter when the game prompts the former.

There's one more related option at the bottom of Settings > Controllers & Accessories: Swap A and B. This simple toggle does what it says and is great if you're used to PlayStation or Xbox controllers that have the Confirm button at the bottom and Cancel on the right.

You have options for letting others enjoy your games, thanks to Nintendo's virtual game cards. Any game loaded onto your system is playable by all other users on the console under their own profile. If you have a Nintendo family group set up, other members can borrow your games on their system for two weeks (during which time you're unable to play them).

Open the Virtual Game Cards page on the Home screen to see your collection. Choose one and hit Lend to Family Group Member, then Select a User to Lend To. To do this, you have to be physically near the other person. You can only loan three games at a time, and a borrower can only have a single game "checked out" at once. Either party can return the game early, and you're free to loan it again if desired after 14 days.

There's also the GameShare feature, where you can stream a copy of compatible games so anyone nearby with a Switch or Switch 2 can join you in multiplayer. You can also do this online using GameChat for friends with a Switch 2. Only a few titles (including Donkey Kong Bananza, Clubhouse Games and Super Mario Odyssey) are supported, but it's worth a look for a low-cost multiplayer.

There are a few ways to reduce slowdowns across your Switch 2 so you don't get bogged down in menus all the time. If you're the only user on your system, it's a waste of time to select your profile every time you open a game or the eShop. To avoid this, go to Settings > Users and enable Skip Selection Screen. This option won't appear if your system has multiple users.

You might also be able to minimize loading times in Switch 1 games. These can see smoother performance when playing off the dock if you visit Settings > System > Nintendo Switch Software Handling > Handheld Mode Boost. By enabling this, your Switch 2 runs original Switch titles as if they were in TV mode, which can improve their graphics or performance. As a trade-off, it will likely use more battery, and there are some control quirks (like the touchscreen not working). There's also an option to add this to Quick Settings for easier toggling.

Finally, there's a much better way to share media you create on your Switch 2. To share your screenshots and video clips much faster, open the Gallery icon on the home screen, hit the Settings cog on the left, and pick your profile. Enable Automatic Uploads and your system will upload up to 100 items, held for 30 days. Then on your phone, install the Nintendo Switch app and sign in with your Nintendo account. By tapping the same Album icon in the app, you're able to access your uploads and share them over chat apps, social media or similar.

Hidden Behind the Badge

📅 Nov 09, 2026  |  Category: Health  |  Read Source →

After years or decades, problems and patterns can become deeply entrenched. Be a fly on the wall during a sample couples therapy session and witness the slow, deliberate work of changing those patterns for a more enduring love.

Self Tests are all about you. Are you outgoing or introverted? Are you a narcissist? Does perfectionism hold you back? Find out the answers to these questions and more with Psychology Today.

Posted November 9, 2026 | Reviewed by Monica Vilhauer Ph.D.

Co-authored by Dr. Jessica Muccione and Dr. Michael Pittaro

Every occupation can take a toll on workers’ mental health, but some professions are considered more stressful than others due to the nature of work-related exposures. To date, policing remains one of the most stressful professions. Hidden behind every badge is a law enforcement professional trying their very best to navigate a steady, and at times relentless, stream of psychological and emotional stress that makes resilience a key attribute that is essential for survival where suicide rates exceed those of the average citizen. Most, if not all, law enforcement officers will, at some point in their careers, be faced with stressful, traumatic encounters that quite literally become embedded in their psyche, and if not addressed, will likely worsen.

This topic has been extensively studied, but most studies focus exclusively on male police officers; yet, emerging research suggests that female officers, in particular, often experience these challenges in distinct and deeply personal ways that differ from the experiences of male officers. Exploring these differences can help agencies design wellness initiatives that truly support all who serve by recognizing and adjusting for gender differences. Although the percentage of female officers pursuing a career in law enforcement has been steadily increasing, female officers only comprise 13.8% of all US law enforcement officers; that is likely why their unique circumstances have been largely understudied and, for the most part, overlooked within the research and practitioner literature (Statista, 2024).

Law enforcement officers are repeatedly exposed to traumatic incidents, most of which are attributed to operational stress. Operational stress is commonly tied to the trauma that police officers encounter in the course of their work, such as responding to homicide scenes, domestic and sexual assaults, fatal collisions, suicides, and other acts of violence that can have a profound influence on officers, leading to anxiety, depression, sleep disturbances, PTSD, and in some extreme cases, suicide (Bishopp et al., 2019). To compound matters, today’s law enforcement officers are also faced with staff shortages, mandatory overtime, erratic shifts, insufficient pay, public scrutiny, and at times, unsupportive, ineffective leadership. In fact, Violanti et al. (2017) found that female police officers report a roughly 37% higher prevalence of lack of support from supervisors compared to male officers.

The paucity of research to date suggests that female officers are more likely to internalize the emotional impact of stressors that lead to burnout when compared to male officers (Rabichuk, Williamson, & Frankel, 2025). In law enforcement, female officers often place significant pressure on themselves. There is a constant burden to maintain a professional image and avoid appearing vulnerable or weak. In response, female officers tend to suppress emotions rather than seek help because they are persistently compelled to prove their competence in a historically male-dominated field, contributing to the high likelihood of chronic stress and burnout.

Female officers are challenged with unique workplace stressors that extend beyond the basic demands of police work, some of which male officers cannot relate to, and as a result, can adversely affect performance, well-being, retention, and career advancement. Some of these stressors include, but are not limited to, pregnancy, postpartum recovery, menopause, and the demands of shift work. It should come as no surprise that contemporary research suggests that police leaders who understand these realities are better positioned to implement policies that support female officer wellness while strengthening organizational effectiveness.

Pregnancy presents unique challenges for women in law enforcement, particularly when agency policies regarding modified assignments and accommodations vary widely. Research suggests female officers may experience elevated reproductive health risks, including miscarriage, highlighting the need for evidence-based workplace policies, reproductive health education, flexible assignments, and supportive supervision.

Illias, Demou, and Riach (2025) emphasized that returning to work after childbirth presents another set of challenges unique to female law enforcement officers. Recovery from childbirth, sleep disruptions, breastfeeding, and childcare responsibilities often lead to concerns about job security and future career advancement. Female officers who received little to no agency support upon return to work experienced significantly higher rates of depression and anxiety than those who had the support of their agencies (Illias et al., 2025). It is therefore recommended that police leaders create family-friendly policies, comprehensive return-to-work policies and programs, breastfeeding spaces, and other supportive accommodations.

This is undoubtedly one of the least studied issues affecting female police officers. Hofman and Spiegel (2026) conducted a qualitative study in which the study’s participants (female police officers) reported concerns about stigma, perceptions of reduced competence, and a lack of understanding from leadership. For police leadership, menopause should be viewed as a workforce wellness issue as opposed to a personal matter (Hofman & Spiegel, 2026). Educating and training supervisors and fellow officers normalizes conversations about women’s health that are typically shunned.

Understanding the realities that female officers face throughout their policing careers is not simply a gender-equity issue, but rather an organizational issue because agencies that recognize and respond to these challenges are more likely than not to retain female officers, improve wellness outcomes, and strengthen modern-day law enforcement agencies.

Children’s Place files for bankruptcy protection, seeks buyer

📅 2026-11-09  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Children's clothing retailer The Children's Place has filed for chapter 11 bankruptcy protection, it said Sunday.

The company is looking to sell substantially all of its assets and is seeking offers from potential buyers by Dec. 9, with a goal to close a deal by the end of December.

The retailer, whose brands include The Children's Place and Gymboree, says it operates two digital storefronts and 514 North American stores, and has distribution in 13 countries through franchise and wholesale partners. The company's performance has taken a hit in recent years from tariff hikes and slower consumer spending, among other factors.

Its stores and e-commerce sites remain open for now, and the company is planning to continue its ongoing inventory sales with court approval.

"For nearly 60 years, The Children's Place has supported growing families with apparel that customers have come to know and love. We recognize the significance of this development for our employees, customers, vendors, and other partners, and we are committed to conducting an efficient and transparent sale process," said Rudy Morando, co-chief restructuring officer of The Children's Place. "In the meantime, we remain focused on serving the families who have trusted The Children's Place for generations."

This move comes days after interim Children's Place CEO Muhammad Asif Seemab and Chief Strategy Officer Muhammad Umair stepped down from their roles. Seemab and Umair will both remain on the company's board. CFO John Szczepanski took on the role of acting president and principal executive officer.

Seemab is also managing director of Saudi-based Mithaq Capital, which took a majority stake in The Children's Place in 2024 when the company was facing financial trouble.

Even with support from the investors, the company has not been able to significantly improve its financial outlook. In its bankruptcy filing, the company reported about $742 million in assets, compared to about $881 million in debts.

In the second fiscal quarter of 2026, its most recently reported quarter, the company's net sales dropped nearly 19% from the same period the year prior. At the time, Seemab said in a news release that during the quarter the company was "stabilizing our customer file" and "driving traffic to our stores and websites," and saw "lower conversion, combined with a very clearance-heavy mix."

He also pointed to tough comparisons to the prior-year period, due in part to back-to-school shopping demand that "was uniquely elevated ... ahead of anticipated tariff price increases."

The Children's Place stock has plummeted in recent years and was trading around 18 cents per share on Monday.

Apple TV's Murderbot Is Officially Not Returning In 2026

📅 Nov 09, 2026  |  Category: Entertainment  |  Read Source →

While Murderbot season 1 might have been one of 2025’s most acclaimed new sci-fi shows, the satirical action thriller won’t return in 2026. Even though the early 2000s was home to the iconic space western Firefly, the Battlestar Galactica reboot, multiple Stargate shows, Futurama’s original run, and the underrated James Cameron series Dark Angel, the 2020s still mark a uniquely fecund era for the sci-fi genre on the small screen.

Netflix users can choose between Stranger Things, Black Mirror, the animated anthology series Love, Death & Robots, and the ambitious epic 3 Body Problem. Hulu offers sci-fi fans the Margaret Atwood adaptation The Handmaid’s Tale and its spinoff The Testaments, while audience members who log into Apple TV can choose between Invasion, Severance, the Isaac Asimov adaptation Foundation, the acclaimed dystopian series Silo, and the Monsterverse spinoff Monarch: Legacy of Monsters.

As if this lineup were not enough, Apple TV also introduced the superb satirical sci-fi series Murderbot in 2025. Starring Alexander Skarsgård as the titular sardonic Murderbot, this series focused on a private security robot who is equally fascinated and disgusted by the peculiarities of human society. Based on author Martha Wells’ eight-book series The Murderbot Diaries, Murderbot season 1 adapts the first of the writer’s novels, All Systems Red.

Murderbot was a huge critical hit, with a staggering Rotten Tomatoes critical score of 95% and an almost equally impressive audience score of 82%. Season 1 of the series followed Murderbot on a mission to protect scientists during a dangerous trip off Earth, with the acid-tongued robot offering constant deadpan commentary on the group’s foibles. However, viewers who hoped to see season 2 of the series arrive this year will be disappointed.

Instead, season 2 guest star Steve Buscemi told ScreenRant that Murderbot’s sophomore outing wouldn’t arrive until summer 2027. Buscemi, who is also set to appear in Hulu’s upcoming reboot of The X-Files, let the season 2 release window's timeline slip while walking to ScreenRant's Tatiana Hullender about his role in director Martin McDonagh’s blackly comic crime thriller Wild Horse Nine.

Murderbot fans can expect the show’s sophomore outing to drop sometime in the summer of 2027, which puts the show right in the middle of the standard, typical breaks between seasons of streaming series. At the far end of the scale, some of the biggest streaming shows, such as Netflix’s Stranger Things, have been known to take whopping three-year-plus breaks between seasons.

Although Netflix’s Jenna Ortega vehicle Wednesday took similarly lengthy breaks between its seasons, Murderbot’s two-year gap is a lot more common among streaming shows across the board. Disney’s ambitious fantasy adaptation Percy Jackson & the Olympians had a two-year gap between its first two seasons, although seasons 2 and 3 trimmed this down to a one-year gap by shooting back to back.

This strategy might also be part of the reason that Prime Video’s upcoming historical drama Bloodaxe was renewed for a second season before its first season premiered. Murderbot’s season 1 success could not necessarily have been guaranteed before the sci-fi comedy show debuted in 2025, but the two-year wait for season 2 may be due in part to busy cast schedules.

In contrast, Prime Video’s romance novel series adaptation Off Campus is set to release its second season less than a year after its first since the show shot both outings back to back. After the success of Murderbot season 1, Apple TV may follow this release strategy for seasons 2 and 3, but, in the meantime, the wait for season 2 will sadly extend for another six months or so.
