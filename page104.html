Over the past year, I’ve tested a handful of “affordable” EVs — which are, in a relative sense, priced lower than the national average of around $50,000. Depending on who you talk to, options below $40,000 look genuinely attractive, and anything under that threshold almost feels too good to be true.

Yet, that’s exactly where the Slate Truck lands with its $24,950 starting price. For an all-electric pickup, I have to admit I’m impressed by the genuine real-world capability it delivers on paper: a rear-mounted motor pushing 181 horsepower and 195 lb-ft of torque, a 65 kWh battery pack, 1,500 pounds of payload capacity, NACS DC fast-charging compatibility, and an estimated 205 miles of range.

While those specs might look modest next to six-figure behemoths like the Rivian R1T, Chevrolet Silverado EV, and GMC Sierra EV, you can't ignore just how accessible that price point is. But after getting up close with the Slate Truck in person, a few details caught me completely off guard. Here’s what stood out the most.

Prior to seeing the Slate Truck in person, I already knew it was scaling things back with crank windows and manual seats. Even in 2026, I still come across a handful of cars with manual seats — though that’s usually reserved strictly for the passenger side, and it's rarer still to find both seats manual.

So while manual adjustment wasn’t a shock, I was taken aback to find that the side mirrors are entirely manual, too. I can live with crank windows and mechanical seat levers, but manual side mirrors? That’s a tougher pill to swallow, simply because it’s been ages since I’ve had to adjust a mirror by hand.

The Slate Truck I saw featured a limited-edition wrap created in collaboration with Smarties. Yes, the candy rolls you probably remember from childhood.

Even though the "Blank Slate" base model rolls off the assembly line with a generic gray exterior, Slate sets itself apart by offering bold, fun vinyl wraps right out of the gate. It’s an ingenious way to make the truck feel uniquely yours, especially with options to match your personal style.

I’m just curious to see how well these wraps hold up after enduring a few harsh Northeast winters.

Compact pickups are already at a disadvantage due to their smaller footprint, and in a two-seater like the Slate Truck, cabin space is especially tight. Since the seats sit flush against the barrier separating the cabin from the flatbed, there’s practically no leeway to recline them for a more relaxed driving position.

That might not be an issue if the Slate Truck is strictly kept to quick local errands, but plenty of drivers will want to take it on longer road trips. Fighting driver fatigue starts with comfortable ergonomics, so the lack of recline here makes long stretches behind the wheel tough to manage.

I’ve seen deeper, longer beds on full-size pickups, but the Slate Truck’s usable cargo space rivals compact pickups I’ve tested, like the Hyundai Santa Cruz.

Personally, I think the bed is the perfect size for everyday needs — more than spacious enough for weekly Costco runs, while still handling the occasional large piece of furniture without breaking a sweat.

For many drivers, maneuvering into a tight parking spot is an everyday source of stress. Given the Slate Truck’s aggressive cost-cutting measures, I worried that a backup camera might have been left out — but thankfully, it wasn't.

As federal regulations mandate rear-visibility systems on all new vehicles, Slate included one by mounting an integrated camera right near the tailgate latch, taking the guesswork and anxiety out of parallel parking.

Even knowing the Slate Truck favors a minimalist approach, stepping inside the cabin to find it so stripped-back is an eye-opener. It’s a refreshingly clutter-free dashboard: simple mechanical climate knobs and an integrated phone mount anchor the center dash, while slim air vents span nearly the full width of the interior.

Behind the steering wheel sits a compact digital instrument cluster displaying only essential driving telemetry. It’s an minimalist setup that cuts out distractions and keeps your focus squarely on the road.

The biggest challenge the Slate Truck faces is convincing buyers the company will actually be around for the life of the vehicle. Given its ultra-aggressive pricing, I’m genuinely surprised the EV maker is offering such a generous 10-year, 110,000-mile battery and powertrain warranty.

For a startup automaker, including this coverage at no extra charge gives budget-conscious drivers real peace of mind that a cheap entry price won’t turn into a financial money pit down the road.

Love Is Blind Season 11: Find Out Who Broke Up and Who Got Married

📅 Nov 04, 2026  |  Category: Sports  |  Read Source →

During the Love Is Blind season 11 finale, four couples revealed whether they ready to say yes to forever after falling in love inside of the pods.

Major Warning: This article contains spoilers for the Love Is Blind season 11 finale.

Some couples were determined to hold tight to their vision. But that’s not to say the last few episodes of Love Is Blind didn’t get wicked dramatic.

As a quick recap: During the Netflix series’ 11th season—centered around singles hailing from Boston—though six pairs initially got engaged after finding one another in the pods, ultimately four couples made it all the way until their fateful weddings.

Before their big reveal, Scott “Stew” Stewart and Jacqueline “Jax” Guelli seemed to be off to a promising start—that is until their meeting proved that sometimes love may need some more time.

And though the abrupt end of their engagement may have put their status into blurry territory, all proved to be well between the two by the very end.

But not everyone was able to make amends. After Rob Feeley’s outbursts towards both production and his fellow castmates took center stage twice during episode 10, his bride-to-be, his fiancée Michaella Giorgio came to one clear realization.

"I don’t deserve any of what I’m getting," she expressed while crying to one castmate at a pod squad meet up. "I love him. It’s just a lot. And we’re not getting married. I cannot marry this man under any circumstances."

Giorgio also confirmed to her then-fiancé that while she had “genuine feelings” for him, their relationship was “obviously not gonna work out.”

As for Feeley, he later reflected on his actions, noting the passing of his father and two friends within the past year may have played a role into how he reacted both on and off camera: “I think I have more things going on than some people might have going on.”

So, by the time the big day rolled around, John Shannon and Sarah Williams, Wilson Monteiro and Wilnie Louis, Cam Halloran and Aneha Singh as well as Blake Carver and Erika Ellis were faced with the possibility of forever.

Who really said I do? Keep reading to see who made it down the aisle and into a clearer future…

Though yacht captain Blake’s romance with marketing manager Erika appeared to be smooth sailing after the pods, just one week before their wedding, the couple realized they may be in too deep due to their dramatically different lifestyles.

“I don’t want to hold you back,” Blake told Erika during the season 11 finale. “I don’t want you to be that captain’s wife on the widow walk. I don’t want to marry you and then for you to feel held hostage while I’m sailing around, gone for weeks on end. And then you’ve got a timeline with kids and everything.”

“I just think at this point, there’s too many concerns, too many questions that I can’t say yes at the altar,” she shared. “I just think the best thing to do is to leave the experiment.”

And while Erika cried over their decision, Blake had already concluded their ship had sailed.

“The experiment is done, but it’s the right move,” Blake confirmed. And when a producer asked if he ever had a love like this, he replied: “I’m not gonna answer that one…I’m feeling a little relieved, a little exhausted.”

While both Wilson and Wilnie had the times of their lives at their respective bachelor and bachelorette celebrations, the party was definitely over before they reached the altar.

In fact, they couldn’t see eye-to-eye on life's more mundane tasks, including dividing up cooking duties and household chores.

“When we got out of the pods, you got everything I promised you, plus,” Wilson told Wilnie following an off-camera disagreement, later adding, “You said it countless times, ‘I’ll do the things for you that make you happy.’ And I’ve mentioned what those things might be, but then you’ll be like, ‘But I don’t like doing that…I’m not used to doing that.’”

As for Wilnie, she couldn’t disagree more. “I don’t work in the home,” the mental health counselor explained. “I have long, emotionally draining days and whether you agree with that, when I get home, it is hard for me to always be at your level.”

Ultimately, for Wilson, there were too many issues at hand for them to tie the knot. “It’s not that you’re a bad person,” the senior compliance manager shared, “you’re a phenomenal person but maybe marriage is not for us.”

By the end, though a tearful Wilnie confirmed she still loved Wilson, she doesn’t regret their path.

Cam Halloran & Aneha Singh: To Be Determined

Once Aneha and Cam entered the real world, her hesitation to marry someone with a different background and religious beliefs —especially in the wake of her family’s disapproval—and their communication skills proved to be hurdles. And, yet, they seemed determined to scale them together.

However, once asked whether she was ready to say “I do,” the principal engineer shared that she couldn’t, telling her fiancé: “I still believe you’re my person, but I do not today. I need more time.”

As for Cam, he assured her that he completely understood. “It’s okay,” the human resource coordinator replied. “it’s a big decision. I feel like we both need to be one hundred percent confident.”

But once Aneha declared she didn’t and danced away from the altar while stressing to Cam that he was still her fiancé, he teared up watching her leave. As he later explained to his family, he simply didn’t “know what happens now.”

Shortly after, Aneha and Cam did end up meeting up one final time. And while Cam expressed that he still loved and cared for her, he needed time to process the “I don’t” of it all.

However, despite their perspectives, they both said yes to spending their lives together, becoming the only couple to get married during the show’s 11th season.

“It’s fun looking back on the first few days ‘cause I don’t think we liked each other very much,” she shared. “And then, some people here heard the story about you maybe not being too jazzed to keep talking to me. But I love that that happened, because I think that was a catalyst for us.”

Operational tooling rarely generates the excitement of a new token launch, yet it can determine whether institutions trust a network enough to hold meaningful value on it. Teams want evidence that they can detect unexpected actions before the problem expands. Monitoring is one part of that process, not a substitute for preventive controls.

The Solana Foundation has described Microscope, an open-source monitoring approach for teams managing on-chain programmes and infrastructure. The toolkit is designed to help operators inspect instructions, transactions and alerts while connecting observations to common monitoring workflows. It is an engineering tool, not an announcement that every Solana application now receives automatic protection against attacks. The dated original disclosure is the starting point for the figures and product scope discussed here.

In a complex blockchain application, important activity can be hidden within technical transaction instructions that a general-purpose dashboard does not explain well. Monitoring tools try to decode those events and route relevant signals to people who can respond. This is useful for treasury movements, contract administration and unusual operational behaviour, but good alerts depend on choosing the right thresholds and maintaining the integrations.

The wider lesson is that on-chain transparency is not the same thing as operational comprehension. Transaction data may be public while a business still struggles to identify why an event matters or who should investigate it. A self-hostable tool can give teams more control over their monitoring stack, provided they manage uptime, access and alert delivery responsibly.

There is a meaningful gap between a fast transaction and a complete financial service. Solana infrastructure can settle digital assets, but an actual payments or securities product must also manage eligibility, legal rights, local currencies and exceptions. The best reporting follows the full workflow rather than assuming the chain’s speed is the entire product.

For additional perspective, it is worth reading our existing reporting on Solana’s Samsung Wallet rollout and user test and the latest Solana developer and payments coverage. They explain related market structure and should not be mistaken for evidence that every new proposal is already live.

An alert is not a transaction blocker. Microscope does not automatically fix a vulnerable programme, reverse an executed transfer or eliminate the need for security audits. False alarms can consume attention, while incomplete alert definitions can miss harmful activity. A monitored environment still requires tested incident procedures and clear accountability.

Distribution can be an advantage for blockchain applications, but the user experience needs to justify a change in behaviour. Developers also need monitoring and controls to support systems that operate continuously. A credible launch is one that describes available features, supported participants and how success will be measured after the initial announcement.

Watch for adoption in real applications, public documentation and integration examples showing how teams respond to alerts. The most useful evidence would involve measured detection speed and fewer unresolved incidents, rather than the number of dashboards deployed. For Solana developers, monitoring belongs alongside code review and permission design as a basic operating requirement.
