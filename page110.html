Jensen Huang Steps Down as CEO. Is Nvidia Still a Buy?

📅 Nov 13, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

A slew of extramarital affairs and security risks have shaken Nvidia's investment thesis to the core.

Nvidia (NVDA -2.01%) has been tethered to the spotlight since Nov. 8, when founder and then-CEO Jensen Huang admitted to having an affair with Alice Huang.

Alice Huang (no relation to Jensen) was an executive at a Singapore-based Nvidia customer. And reports indicate that U.S. officials and the FBI are investigating the relationship because Alice Huang is suspected of being a Chinese intelligence operative. On Nov. 10, Nvidia hired a prominent New York law firm to initiate an independent investigation into the affair.

The situation escalated after market close on Nov. 11, when the New York Times reported that Jensen Huang had a romantic relationship with Nvidia's vice president of healthcare, Kimberly Powell. On Nov. 12, reports surfaced of a possible third affair with a second employee. That same day, Nvidia published a press release, announcing that Huang had resigned, with CFO Colette Kress taking over as interim CEO.

As of market close on Nov. 12, Nvidia is down a staggering 27.5% in the last month. Here's how the recent news surrounding Huang impacts Nvidia's investment thesis and whether the growth stock is still a buy.

Jensen Huang was arguably the most important CEO in the world. He co-founded Nvidia with Chris Malachowsky and Curtis Priem 33 years ago and possesses a rare balance of technical, strategic, marketing, and leadership skills.

I recently read The Nvidia Way: Jensen Huang and the Making of a Tech Giant by Tae Kim. It's an excellent book that serves as both a history of Nvidia and a biography of Huang. And the biggest takeaway I had from the book is that Huang is arguably more integral to Nvidia than Steve Jobs was to Apple (AAPL +0.39%), Bill Gates was to Microsoft, or Jeff Bezos was to Amazon.

Tim Cook was able to catapult Apple's market cap up severalfold, even after Jobs's passing, by leveraging Apple's integrated ecosystem of products and services. Nvidia is different. When it comes to artificial intelligence (AI) in the semiconductor industry, competition is fierce, and innovation is paramount. Huang has made several key decisions throughout the company's history. And without them, it's highly unlikely the tech stock would be where it is today.

Huang was a firm believer in parallel computing and the graphics processing unit (GPU) -- a term Nvidia helped coin when it debuted the GeForce 256 in 1999 -- and labeled it the world's first GPU. He was also adamant about CUDA, which is Nvidia's software architecture used by developers with Nvidia GPUs.

By integrating hardware and software, Nvidia paved a runway for repeat business in an industry where a hot product can leapfrog the moats of even the most entrenched industry leaders. After all, Huang saw this in the early days of Nvidia, when the company almost went bankrupt multiple times because it developed products too infrequently and bet the farm on a single launch.

Huang also has an impeccable work ethic and top-to-bottom devotion to Nvidia -- arguably to a fault. Nvidia's organization structure positioned Huang like Jupiter, surrounded by dozens of direct reports that orbit him like moons. This wasn’t just so Huang could keep tabs. It's so that he can contribute through spontaneous bursts of creativity.

To complement this workflow, Nvidia has whiteboards scattered throughout meeting rooms for ideation and real-time problem-solving. The knowledge and willingness to break concepts down, start from scratch, and hold frequent open discussions have become essential parts of Nvidia's corporate culture. And it remains to be seen if that will persist under new leadership.

Huang has also been a firm proponent of AI. And it's a key reason why Nvidia was able to shift its end markets from gaming, professional visualization, and automotive to data centers in just a few years. Gaining a foothold in AI was one thing. But Nvidia has continued to push the bounds of innovation through incredibly impressive product upgrades.

Nvidia could have easily taken its foot off the gas after announcing its Grace Blackwell architecture in March 2024. Blackwell unlocked massive efficiency improvements and cost reductions in AI training. But Nvidia really outdid itself with Vera Rubin, which it announced in January 2026.

Rubin's full-stack platform includes six chips, allowing Nvidia to control a larger share of data center infrastructure. Rubin's cost reductions and efficiency improvements compared to Blackwell are so massive that hyperscalers are lining up at the door to place orders. On the Aug. 26 Q2 fiscal 2027 earnings call, Nvidia said that it began shipping Rubin in August and expects it to account for 20% of its Q3 data center revenue, which it will report on Nov. 17.

Reports of three affairs, an internal investigation, an FBI probe, and now Jensen Huang resigning as CEO directly impact the investment thesis. Until we get answers on the extent of the damage and the next long-term CEO, the stock simply isn't as strong a buy as it used to be -- even with a valuation that's ridiculously cheap for such a high-quality business.

I wouldn't sell Nvidia simply because the company's operating leverage and market position are impossible to ignore. But it's hard to imagine Nvidia without Jensen Huang's leadership. The situation would be different if Huang had a clear successor, but there's nothing of the kind at Nvidia, which poses a major key-person risk.

At the very least, Nvidia must overhaul its organizational structure. Poor character aside, I'd expect any replacement to fall short of Huang's caliber as a CEO. And that's legitimately concerning for investors.

As tempting as the sell-off looks at first glance, investors are better off waiting for the dust to settle. I'd much rather buy the stock at a higher price with greater clarity than bottom-fish in waters this murky.

Norway's Martin Odegaard is out for revenge when his team host Wales in a crunch Nations League fixture.

That is one of three defeats Norway, who reached the World Cup quarter-finals, have suffered across their Nations League Group A4 campaign.

Norway have suffered as many defeats in their last three matches (three) as they suffered in their previous 23 games combined in all competitions (W17 D3 L3). They have not lost four in a row since October 2016.

It means they must beat Wales in Oslo on Saturday to stand a chance of progressing to the last eight.

But Odegaard feels Stale Solbakken's team are well set to get one back on Wales.

"Our level was not where it should be. I think it's probably the only game for a long time where we haven't been playing to our standards," he said.

"Wales is a good team, a competitive team, lots of good players in big leagues and a physical team as well, and we were prepared for that.

"But I think on the day we were just not showing up to our standards, and that's something we want to show [on Saturday], and we can do a lot better. We want to show a different version."

And with Wales in the same boat as Norway when it comes to needing a win to keep their quarter-final hopes alive, Solbakken is anticipating a fierce test.

"It's like a boxing [match], going head-to-head, fist-to-fist," said Solbakken.

Nakamoto Sells More Bitcoin (BTC)

📅 2026-11-13  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

David Bailey's Bitcoin treasury company, Nakamoto Inc. (NASDAQ: NAKA), has reduced its Bitcoin (BTC) holdings by another 184 BTC, according to its latest quarterly financial results.

The company, which has made Bitcoin central to its corporate strategy, held 4,283 BTC as of Sept. 30, down from 4,467 BTC at the end of June.

According to Bitcoin Treasuries, Nakamoto sold 184 BTC during the third quarter of 2026, bringing its total holdings to 4,283 BTC. The company currently ranks 21st on the Bitcoin 100 corporate treasury ranking.

The latest financial filing shows that Nakamoto generated approximately $13.2 million in additional proceeds from digital asset sales during the quarter. However, the filing does not separately identify the 184 BTC reduction as a single sale.

Despite the reduction in its holdings, Nakamoto's remaining Bitcoin was valued at approximately $357.9 million at the end of September.

This is not the first time Bailey's company has reduced its Bitcoin holdings in 2026.

In March, Nakamoto sold approximately 284 BTC for $20 million to support working capital requirements and fund operations following its acquisitions of BTC Inc. and UTXO Management.

The sale took place at an average price of roughly $70,422 per Bitcoin, significantly below the company's original acquisition cost.

During the first quarter, Nakamoto also received approximately 43 BTC in premiums from its derivatives strategies before selling around 40 BTC.

The company made another substantial Bitcoin sale in June.

According to its second-quarter financial results, Nakamoto sold approximately 600 BTC for 35.6 million USDT.

Together with proceeds from certain Bitcoin derivative positions, the transactions generated approximately $48 million.

Nakamoto used $45 million of those proceeds to partially repay its Bitcoin-backed loan from cryptocurrency exchange Kraken, reducing the outstanding principal from 210 million USDT to 165 million USDT.

The latest reduction in Bitcoin holdings comes as Nakamoto continues to manage its substantial debt obligations while expanding its operating businesses.

As of Sept. 30, approximately 3,030 BTC were pledged as collateral against the company's debt, leaving 1,253 BTC unencumbered.

On Nov. 11, Nakamoto extended the maturity of 60 million USDT of its Kraken loan from December 2026 to December 2027. The company now has no scheduled principal repayments remaining in 2026.

Interestingly, Nakamoto reported a net profit of $85.5 million for the third quarter, primarily benefiting from a substantial increase in the market value of its Bitcoin holdings.

However, its adjusted operating loss reached $15.8 million, highlighting the ongoing challenge of generating sustainable earnings beyond Bitcoin price appreciation.

Bailey has maintained that Nakamoto intends to build businesses capable of generating revenue throughout different Bitcoin market cycles.

The company's latest Bitcoin holdings reduction illustrates how corporate treasury strategies can involve selling cryptocurrency to meet financing and operational requirements, rather than simply accumulating BTC indefinitely.

CDT Southern Terminus Closure Lifted, Border Monument To Be Relocated

📅 Nov 13, 2026  |  Category: Other  |  Read Source →

The Continental Divide Trail’s southern terminus and southernmost mile have reopened following a temporary construction closure, according to the Continental Divide Trail Coalition (CDTC). This section of the trail had been closed in late October so that construction crews could widen an access road and construct a second border barrier at the terminus.

The first mile of the CDT falls within the Fort Huachuca New Mexico National Defense Area (NDA), a military zone created in spring 2025 when roughly 110,000 acres along the US-Mexico border were transferred from the Bureau of Land Management into Army control. Hikers seeking to access the trail’s southern terminus must submit to a background check and obtain an Army permit. Noncitizens must also have a US citizen escort through the NDA who also has an access permit.

“These events are unprecedented in the history of our trail system: the transfer of a critically significant element of our national scenic trail into the Department of Defense control, the conversion of the CDT Southern Terminus into a military and construction zone of momentous scale, and trail management partnerships that include land management agencies and state governments, but also the US military,” the CDTC said in a press release.

The CDTC received word today from US Army officials at Fort Huachuca that the trail is once again officially open, although construction activities are ongoing and hikers may still encounter traffic, road closures, and delays in the area. “This closure came at a critical time for southbound hikers,” the organization said. “Completing the 3,100-mile trail is a monumental achievement, and the construction activities at the border created dynamic changes to the permitted NDA access in place during the past year.”

The press release also indicates that the construction of the second border wall means the southern terminus monument will need to be relocated to an area outside of the border walls, and that access to the monument may be temporarily prohibited during construction and relocation. “CDTC does not know the exact timing of the relocation but will provide updates as available,” the organization wrote.

Live Service Fatigue Claims One More Popular Shooter

📅 Nov 13, 2026  |  Category: Business  |  Read Source →

Covering the video games industry since 2017, with experience in news, articles, and reviews. Currently a writer at DualShockers and gaming magazines.

Another ambitious online shooter is biting the dust, and this time, it seems that players won't even get the chance to return for one last update.

GFA Games has officially announced today that PIONER, its post-apocalyptic MMO inspired by titles like STALKER and Fallout, is shutting down. The studio is ending development, canceling future updates, and preparing to take the game's servers offline after failing to secure the funding necessary to keep the project running.

In an announcement shared on social media, the development team explained that maintaining PIONER required additional financial support, but they couldn’t find any. The studio had spent the past few months negotiating with existing and potential partners, hoping to find an investment deal that would allow development to continue and preserve the team.

Unfortunately, those discussions didn't lead anywhere. According to the developers, GFA Games no longer has enough money of its own to continue working on the game or maintain its server infrastructure.

After years of near radio silence, Fairgames has been canceled and its staff redistributed throughout Sony games.

“We were unable to agree on the funding needed to continue the project. The studio no longer has sufficient funds of its own to continue development and keep the servers running.”

Aleksandr Nikitin, CEO of GFA Games, even addressed the community, expressing his disappointment over the decision and apologizing to players who purchased PIONER expecting more updates. He explained that the team had envisioned a long future for the project and had been trying to find a way to keep both the game and its developers together.

The recent announcement effectively puts an end to those plans. Since PIONER is an online-only experience, shutting down its servers also means players will no longer be able to access the game once the infrastructure is taken offline.

It's quite an unfortunate outcome for a game that spent years building up expectations before finally arriving on Steam Early Access in December 2025. The post-apocalyptic MMO promised an enormous world filled with dangerous mutants, exploration, survival mechanics, and multiplayer activities, which initially attracted some attention on Steam, reaching a peak of more than 1,600 concurrent players around launch. However, that interest quickly faded. By September, its 24-hour peak had dropped to just 11 players, according to SteamDB.

It's always disappointing to see an ambitious multiplayer project disappear like this, especially when players have invested money and time into its world. But the studio thanked everyone who supported PIONER, reported bugs, created content, and helped build its community, while acknowledging that it couldn't deliver the future originally promised.

For now, the development team hasn't provided a specific date for when the servers will go offline. Still, with development and updates officially ending, PIONER's days are numbered. And while the game may not have reached the audience its developers hoped for, its shutdown is another reminder of how difficult it can be to keep an online-only game alive when the players and financial support aren't there.

What 12 12 12's Title Really Means

📅 Nov 13, 2026  |  Category: Other  |  Read Source →

Apple TV's newest crime thriller is 12 12 12, so what exactly does that unusual title mean? Anthony Mackie and Jamie Dornan star in 12 12 12, an Apple TV heist series that premiered on November 13, 2026. Created by Jim Keeble and Dudi Appleton, the series currently has a 64% on Rotten Tomatoes.

12 12 12 follows Jack Strickland (Mackie), an FBI agent on the tail of a famed thief and the possible murderer of Jack's former partner, Paul Galvin (Dornan). After a Newark, NJ heist went sideways and Jack's agent friend was killed, his despair hardened into resolve and then into obsession.

Now, he is doing everything he can to catch Paul in the act. For his part, Paul hasn't slowed down, and he now has his eyes on 800,000 Swiss francs that a Russian criminal organization has recently placed into a vault in Zurich. As we watch the cat-and-mouse game, it becomes clear that Paul and Jack may be more alike than it appears.

It's a fairly standard setup for a heist story, with a few curveballs thrown in here and there. The biggest of these curveballs is the structure, which fans of a show like 24 will quickly understand. It's this conceit that sets the show apart from other ones in the genre, and it also helps explain the title.

At the top of each episode of 12 12 12, the audience is reminded of something allegedly in the FBI's Principles of Crime. First, a high-asset robbery, like the one Paul has done in Zurich, takes 12 months to plan. The actual execution takes 12 hours. Then, the police have 12 days to solve it before the thieves can erase their tracks.

The 12 12 12 of the title then comes from those principles. Not only are we reminded of these at the start of the episode, but the show is also structured so that we are jumping through each of these frameworks, sometimes viewing an event in the future before we see its build-up in the past.

The 12-month timeline is titled onscreen as "The Planning", the 12-hour one is "The Heist", and the 12-day one is "The Takedown". As we move through each of these different sections, more details are revealed to us that change the way we think about the future events we may have already seen.

The first two episodes of 12 12 12 premiered on November 13, 2026, and the series will release new episodes weekly until December 25, 2026, for a total of 8 episodes. At the moment, there are no plans for 12 12 12 season 2, so it's unclear what kind of ending viewers should expect.
