Laura Loomer, Elon Musk, George Santos, and More React to Election Night: “I Hope Everyone Is Learning Arabic”

📅 Nov 04, 2026  |  Category: Politics  |  Read Source →

The midterm elections marked a night of historic firsts: Adam Hamilton became the first Democratic senator elected in Kansas in nearly 100 years; Chris Pappas is now the first openly gay man to be voted into the Senate; Keisha Lance Bottoms is the first Black woman to be elected as a US governor; Bentley Hudgins is the first transgender and nonbinary lawmaker to be voted into the Georgia General Assembly—the list goes on.

How better to chronicle a generational evening in US history than on social media? Our founding fathers recounted watershed moments via post riders and printed broadsides; in the mid-19th century, dispatches were sent via the Pony Express. President Lincoln’s shooting was rushed to the public via Morse code tapped over the wires, and during World War I, we utilized undersea telegraph cables. Yet in our long history spanning radio broadcasts, satellite television, and smoke signals, nothing has quite captured the zeitgeist like an X post.

“CNN is now saying the national anthem has been changed to a reggaeton cover of Pink Pony Club,” tweeted one user last night, right as the media joined a chorus of policy analysts and pundits to announce a new dawn as Democrats took control of Congress—a blue wave. Or rather, a blue tsunami.

Social media users across the country then took to X to mourn, deny, or celebrate the victory. Yet amid the cacophony of social media’s tweets, posts, replies, comments, and reels, the most notorious influencer grew shockingly quiet: President Donald Trump himself. Despite posting a half-hearted image of a MAGA-red hat reading “VETO,” Trump has not said much online in the hours following the victory.

In the months leading up to the midterms, social media did more than simply sway victories. In fact, it propelled candidates like Jon Ossoff to the forefront through short-form vertical videos, made contenders such as Abdul El-Sayed go viral through clips set to songs like Eminem’s 2010 hit “Not Afraid,” and directed Gen Z focus to key races through memes (“TEXAS JUST WENT BLUE. PUT PRONOUNS BACK IN YOUR BIO NOW,” New York City Council member Chi Ossé posted in his Instagram Stories last night).

More than three quarters of young adults relied on social media or platforms like YouTube as a source for their midterm election news this year. An astounding 67% of millennials also got the lowdown from content creators. Call it absurd, call it brain rot, or call it sensational: There’s no doubt that social media has become a phenomenon, and the Twitterverse is still alive and well in 2026.

Democrat Roy Cooper wins a North Carolina Senate seat

📅 Nov 04, 2026  |  Category: Politics  |  Read Source →

Former North Carolina Gov. Roy Cooper, the Democratic Senate nominee, speaks at a campaign event on Sunday in Concord, N.C. Allison Joyce/Getty Images hide caption

Midterms coverage: Live results | What to know | Stories around the countryStay connected: NPR Politics Podcast | NPR Politics newsletter | NPR app

Democrat Roy Cooper has won North Carolina's open U.S. Senate seat, according to a race call from the Associated Press, providing the party with a key Senate flip.

The former governor becomes the first Democrat elected to the Senate from North Carolina since 2008.

Cooper defeated Michael Whatley, a former Republican National Committee chair who President Trump picked to replace retiring GOP Sen. Thom Tillis.

Cooper and national Democratic Party groups significantly outspent Whatley and Republicans. A few weeks before the election, national GOP organizations pulled TV ads from North Carolina and redirected their money to other battlegrounds like Kansas.

Cooper, who served as governor from 2017 to 2024, focused his campaign on affordability issues. Each campaign stop he made across the state was promoted as the "Make Stuff Cost Less" tour.

Meanwhile, Whatley's campaign centered around crime and immigration issues. He criticized Cooper over a COVID-era legal settlement that released inmates from state prisons to address overcrowding during the pandemic. But polls indicated that the economy — not crime — was the top concern for North Carolina voters this year.

The race was Whatley's first run for elected office, and Cooper's campaign painted him as a lobbyist for Big Oil who would become a rubber stamp for Trump in the Senate.

Patrick Sebastian, a Republican strategist who did not work on the Whatley campaign, said his party's candidate should have made the economy a bigger focus.

"Really it's the economy is Issue A and then Issue B is probably government corruption, that people are following the most," Sebastian said. "So when we're not talking about those issues, it's going to be hard to connect with people that go to Walmart for their groceries and can only get 80% of what they used to buy because the prices have gone up."

The North Carolina Senate race was initially expected to be one of the most competitive — and most expensive — in the country, but Whatley never led in polling, and Cooper's lead widened over the course of the race.

Even as pro-Whatley TV ads largely disappeared in October, Trump and Vice President Vance came to North Carolina for multiple rallies in the final weeks of the campaign. Sebastian says those visits likely helped improve base turnout for GOP voters, but weren't enough to save Whatley's campaign.

Tillis announced in 2025 he wouldn't seek reelection, after Trump threatened him with a primary challenger for failing to support the president's tax cut and spending bill. Rather than campaign for Whatley, he's been promoting his new book criticizing fellow Republicans.

As 2026 Midterms Results Roll In, Donald Trump Cranks Up the Truth Social Machine

📅 Nov 04, 2026  |  Category: Politics  |  Read Source →

As the results of Tuesday’s midterm elections began to roll in, Donald Trump’s mood seemed to darken, especially when it came to coverage from his network of choice, Fox News. His demeanor was a sharp contrast to how the president has appeared the last few weeks: In the face of war in Iran and a growing affordability crisis at home, the president has attended jam-packed rallies intended to boost support for Republican candidates, often with a smile on his face, even if his presence was reportedly not always desired.

In Georgia, he stumped for gubernatorial candidate Rick Jackson and US Senate nominee Mike Collins, who lost his race against incumbent Democratic senator Jon Ossoff. In Texas, Trump turned out for Attorney General Ken Paxton, whose campaign canceled its final appearances in the last days of the race against Democrat James Talarico.

Trump has long seemed happiest when speaking to a crowd of supporters, but his demeanor seems to grow bleaker in the evening hours, typically the time he takes to his social media platform, Truth Social, where he shares less positive sentiments.

That was the pattern early Tuesday. As polls closed in Indiana and Kentucky at 6 p.m. ET, Trump termed the end to the latter state's election day “A BIG DEAL!” The president was likely aware of the closure as he was “Watching the Pre-Election Results on FoxNews,” he wrote, before complaining that he can't stand the voice of one of the network's few liberal leaning commentators, Jessica Tarlov.

“DUMB AS A ROCK,” Trump complained, his mood apparently turning. “Have to go somewhere else until that LOSER is off the air — I'll be back later!” After less than an hour, he returned, his anger at Fox increasing. “The FoxNews Poll, always the worst, is so insulting with the questions they ask,” he wrote. “Every question is a hit job, and every answer is ‘BULLSHIT!’ This has been going on for years.”

A few minutes later, he returned again, this time with a longer rant about the cable channel. “FoxNews Ratings are way down (27%!) because they’re spending too much time covering the Trump Deranged Dumocrats! All of those people who used to watch Fox, and no longer do, get it. It’s them, not me — My numbers are better than they have ever been.”

“No wonder FoxNews Ratings are collapsing, and they’re going to go down a lot further,” he continued. “They’re way down, not because of me, but because they are AGAINST me! This happened once before, and Fox almost collapsed. It’s happening again!”
