Michael Carrick steps in to escort red-carded Matheus Cunha down the tunnel after Man United match-winner tried to celebrate Roma victory

📅 2026-11-03  |  Category: Other  |  Read Source →

Published: 18:55 EDT, 3 November 2026 | Updated: 20:12 EDT, 3 November 2026

Manchester United's match-winner Matheus Cunha had to be escorted down the tunnel following the stunning win over Roma after head coach Michael Carrick tried to keep the Brazil star out of further trouble.

Cunha was sent off in injury-time after crowning United's 3-2 comeback win with two late goals. Having been booked for taking off his shirt after scoring the winner, he was shown a second yellow card for a foul on Leonardo Balerdi.

Cunha watched the end of the game sat on an advertising board rather than going to the dressing-room, and tried to come on the pitch at the end to celebrate with his team-mates. When United's backroom staff tried to stop the unhappy player, Carrick stepped in to order him down the tunnel.

'I was disappointed with how it finished, but again just shows how much he cares and is willing to do,' said Carrick of Cunha, who also won the penalty that allowed Bruno Fernandes to fire United back into the game early in the second half.

'When he's in that frame of mind, he's in that mood and he plays like that, that's what we want from him. Matheus can do those things, you know. He's got those moments.

'We put him inside and swapped him with Bryan at half-time to try and get him in the middle of the pitch, try and give us a little bit of different shape, and put him in the centre of the pitch, and he found himself in good positions.'

It remains to be seen if Cunha will face any further action from UEFA besides being banned for the next Champions League tie against Sporting Lisbon in November.

Cunha's exit at Old Trafford was reminiscent of an incident in March 2025 when he was playing for Wolves. He was charged by the FA for acting in an improper manner after he was sent off in the FA Cup defeat by Bournemouth after clashing with Milos Kerkez in the final minute of extra-time and then had to be escorted down the tunnel.

Cunha was later handed an additional one-match ban and fined £50,000 for his behaviour.

Defiant Bruno Fernandes claims he wasn't dropped for publicly criticising Michael Carrick - and hits out at 'a lot of lies' in sweary interview after Man United's stunning 3-2 comeback against Roma

📅 2026-11-03  |  Category: Sports  |  Read Source →

By WILL GRIFFEE and CHRIS WHEELER, NORTHERN SPORTS WRITER

Published: 17:42 EDT, 3 November 2026 | Updated: 20:12 EDT, 3 November 2026

Bruno Fernandes launched into an adrenaline-fuelled, X-rated rant after Man United's stunning comeback win over Roma on Tuesday night.

Having been at the centre of the storm surrounding the club's abysmal form and his undermining of manager Michael Carrick, Fernandes offered contrition for publicly criticising tactics but denied it was the reason he was dropped last week.

The club captain was benched by Carrick for United's 3-0 defeat by Bournemouth before being reintegrated against Chelsea and Roma, winning Man of the Match and scoring against the Italians.

Amazon Prime presenter Gabby Logan was forced to apologise for Fernandes' bad language after he let the emotion pour out of him in the post-match interview.

The stirring 3-2 comeback at Old Trafford on Tuesday night was the jumping-off point for Fernandes to unburden himself, saying: 'We don't need to save anyone's job.

'The club needed that win. The players needed that win. It is something we needed as a group of players and staff.

'We have been working so hard to get out of this situation together. It has been tough. There has been a lot of speculation in the media.

"Carrick was right" 🔴👏Bruno Fernandes gave an open, honest assessment after United's 3-2 win over Roma 🗣️ pic.twitter.com/QAWDt6VXQr

Bruno Fernandes gave a passionate interview after starring in Man United's 3-2 win

'A lot of lies around many things that have happened at this club, between me and the manager, like that we have cleared the air. Nothing like that happened.

'The manager took a decision of making myself on the bench because I wasn't performing. And that's fair.

'Everyone felt like that was a punishment for the words that I had [after Como]. And if it was, it was right.

'The manager makes the decisions. I have to accept it. I have to go forward with that. It doesn't change the belief I have in the manager, the staff and every player at this club.

'And it doesn't change my aim to win every f***ing game that I have ahead of me.'

Carrick was given a stay of execution by club bosses at United after losing against Bournemouth when he seemed destined to be sacked.

Defeat at Chelsea appeared to push him closer to the exit door but Fernandes and Co fought back from 2-0 down on Tuesday night to give their boss and a beleaguered fanbase a much-needed lift.

Fernandes, along with Lisandro Martinez, had both publicly criticised different aspects of the club, with the Argentine targeting Jason Wilcox and Fernandes making disparaging comments about Carrick's tactics.

Michael Carrick and Fernandes embraced after the Portuguese came off at Old Trafford

Fernandes was overcome with emotion after United sealed the memorable comeback

His post-Como interview was widely condemned by fans and pundits alike, with Garry Neville lamenting: 'If that's your captain saying that, imagine what they're saying in the dressing room behind closed doors when no one's listening. That's horrific.'

He also told Fernandes to 'read the room' and accused him of being an 'excuse merchant' on his podcast.

'He's gone and basically thrown his manager under the bus the week after [Lisandro] Martinez did the same,' he said.

'So get in your box. You know where you stand. Michael Carrick's won things for the football club on a constant basis, and proven that he can have the humility, the resilience. And Bruno has got that. Bruno has delivered for Manchester United in more ways than any other player in the last 13 years.

'He's massively talented but he let himself down this week. He didn't need to do that because he's basically stuck the knife into his manager.'

Fernandes and the United players were booed by the United fans after the Bournemouth debacle, while Carrick was given a warm reception, clearly indicating where the supporter sentiment fell.

But the mood was altogether different on Tuesday night. Roma raced to a 2-0 lead in what appeared to be another United evening of despair.

Then Fernandes netted a re-taken penalty before two late Matheus Cunha goals snatched a crucial victory.

Afterwards, Carrick reflected: 'This place does it (inspires comebacks). No matter what situation you're in, how tough it can seem, the supporters are desperate to get behind you and we gave them something to go on.

Carrick's job was hanging by a thread but he has given himself some breathing room

'I’m proud of everyone pulling through in a tough situation. I don’t like talking to the lads too much about the history of this place, but it’s magical. There’s something when we have these nights and we do it the hard way, and one moment can flip the whole feeling.

‘It’s one win so we’re not getting too carried away, but it was an important one for us.’

When asked about his future at the club, he added: 'I don't have a crystal ball to know what happens next.

'We move on to the weekend, we've got a really important game here against Aston Villa, which we need to prepare for right.'

Youri Tielemans played a part in the sensational turnaround after coming on as a substitute for Carlos Baleba when United’s £70million summer signing pulled his right hamstring at the start of the second half.

Tielemans said of Carrick: ‘There has been a lot of pressure on him and that’s how we repay him, with a comeback like this. Big character, big fight.’

Asked if the victory might save the head coach’s job, the Belgian replied: ‘That's a different question. The only thing we can do is stick behind him, fight for him and fight for the club.

‘Let's hope this is a good turnaround for us and we can build on this.’

Unai Emery has closed gap between Aston Villa and Europe's elite - but rampant Barcelona proved there is still a way to go as Lamine Yamal dazzles, writes OLIVER HOLT

📅 2026-11-03  |  Category: Other  |  Read Source →

Published: 17:17 EDT, 3 November 2026 | Updated: 02:35 EDT, 4 November 2026

John McGinn heard the question. His eyes darted around Barcelona's packed media auditorium for a second or two on Monday night. What was it like to be about to play in the Nou Camp, the Aston Villa skipper had been asked, as if this were to be some sort of spin off from Bill and Ted's Excellent Adventure.

McGinn was careful with his answer. He did not seek to downplay the nature of the occasion or the thrill that comes with lining up in such a majestic arena. But he was determined not to play the wide-eyed ingenu. 'What our club is trying to do now,' McGinn said, 'is make this normal, make this a yearly experience.'

So when he and his team-mates walked up the flight of steps from the dressing rooms into the vast bowl of the stadium on Tuesday evening, they were here to show that they were not arrivistes. Villa is a historic club. They won this competition in 1982, ten years before Barcelona ever did. They were not here for a treat or to get patted on the back just for turning up.

It is nearly 20 years since Liverpool became the last English club to win here and nobody gave Villa too much chance of ending that drought. They are being told constantly that the odds are stacked against them and that their much-admired manager, Unai Emery, may be tempted by the entreaties of struggling 'giants' elsewhere.

That was the reason why this game early in the league phase had attained so much importance. For Villa, it was a symbol of something they aspire to. They want to get used to belonging in this company, to walking up those steps, to emerging into the stadium in the pouring rain, and staring at players of the calibre of Lamine Yamal, Rodri and Pau Cubarsi staring back at them.

It still feels as if they stand on the cusp of something bigger but they blew their shot at proving that on Tuesday night. They needed to be at their best to have a chance of beating Hansi Flick's side, which sits atop LaLiga, and they were far too naïve in defence to get close to causing a shock. They are rising but this was a step too far at this stage of their evolution.

Lamine Yamal (centre) and Barcelona had plenty to smile about after beating Aston Villa 3-0

Yamal opened the scoring for Barcelona after just seven minutes with this left-footed finish

Raphinha (right) doubled Barca's lead after half-time with this strike at the Spotify Nou Camp

The Nou Camp, which is still in the midst of an extensive renovation and was only about two-thirds full, was ringed by towering yellow cranes, peering down at the floodlit patch of green. From their lofty vantage point, they saw Villa get off to the worst possible start.

Only seven minutes had gone when Emery's team gift-wrapped a present for the home side. Zion Suzuki played the ball out short to Joao Gomes but Gomes panicked under pressure from the Barcelona press and the close attention of Pedri. He played the ball square without looking, straight to Yamal.

Of all the players he could have found, Gomes had to find Yamal. The Spain superstar squared up Pau Torres and then curled the ball around him with a gentle caress of his left boot. Suzuki was helpless. The ball nestled in the corner of the net. Barcelona had scored 56 goals in 14 games before this match. Extending the hand of generosity to them must have felt particularly galling for Emery.

Villa almost hit back immediately. A stinging volley from Matty Cash was deflected wide and Barcelona looked jittery at the back. Nicolas Jackson played a square ball to Johan Manzambi, who should have equalised but scuffed his first-time effort wide of Joan Garcia. Barcelona were thankful for Garcia's intervention when he raced out of his goal to dispossess Jackson as he charged on to a through ball.

Yamal was rampant. His battle with Ian Maatsen was a mismatch and Maatsen was booked after 11 minutes for hacking him down. Villa were still intent on playing themselves into trouble. Suzuki repeated his mistake when he tried to play the ball out to Joao Gomes. This time, Pedri beat him to it and the loose ball fell to Gordon. Gordon hit his shot towards the corner but Suzuki redeemed himself by diving to push it away.

Barcelona had the better of the play. They looked like scoring every time they went forward. Time and again, either Raphinha or Yamal found space to run at the Villa defence, which was barely coping. Even in that context, though, it was an open game. Villa had chances, too. Manzambi forced a fine save out of Garcia. A Jackson breakaway was thwarted by a last-ditch block.

But seven minutes after the break, Barcelona doubled their lead. Fermin Lopez played a clever ball to Raphinha, who had found a yard of space in the box. Raphinha, LaLiga's leading scorer this season, drilled a left-foot shot goalwards and it deflected off the heel of Torres, wrong-footed Suzuki and rolled over the line.

Barcelona put the game out of reach midway through the half when Joao Cancelo forced his way past McGinn on the left flank and hit a sumptuous cross into the box with the outside of his right foot. Raphinha gambled on the path of the ball, darted behind his marker and tucked the ball away.

Raphinha completed the scoreline with this cute finish as Barcelona completed their victory

For Aston Villa, this will be a learning curve as they look to go far in the Champions League

Villa manager Unai Emery will have plenty to think about after another Nou Camp defeat

Substitute Leon Goretzka hit the face of a post for Villa late on but the game was already gone. There was no shame in losing to a team who are one of the favourites to win the competition this season but there was frustration in the way Villa had handed their opponents the advantage.

Emery looked bedraggled as he stood on the touchline in the rain, arms folded, shaking his head. This was his 15th defeat in 16 visits to the Nou Camp. As Villa's coach fights to edge his team closer to the elite of the game, this game will become just one more bad memory.

Man United conjured a miraculous comeback against Roma that was thrilling, visceral and raw... finally Michael Carrick has something to cling to, writes IAN LADYMAN

📅 2026-11-03  |  Category: Other  |  Read Source →

Published: 17:15 EDT, 3 November 2026 | Updated: 20:14 EDT, 3 November 2026

Once again they sang Michael Carrick's name and this time it was not out of sympathy. This time it was different.

This time there had been some guts and this time there had been some glory. This time, most importantly, there had been a victory. So the Stretford End sang Carrick's name out of thanks. Finally some respite from the relentless misery of this tortuous season.

How Carrick's Manchester United team pulled off this comeback is hard to fathom. But for now all that matters to Carrick is that they did. For at last he has something tangible to cling to as he tries to drag his club's season back from the jaws of hell.

How on earth do we describe this game? Maybe just with the facts.

Carrick's team were so bad in the first half it was almost impossible to understand. However awful they have been this season, this was worse. Two down to early goals from Matias Soule and Donyell Malen, United presented both to Roma on a plate.

The game looked over. United's Champions League season looked over. Carrick's reign felt over.

Matheus Cunha's late double rescued Manchester United from the jaws of defeat on a night of madness at Old Trafford

Cunha scored in the 83rd and 86th minute to turn the game on its head against Roma

There was still time for Cunha to be sent off in the dying embers of the game - but that failed to take the shine off a memorable night

In the crowd they waved green and gold balloons in protest at the owners the Glazers. Then they threw tennis balls on to the field. The only surprise was that none of the United players put one in their own net.

But then, somehow and almost impossibly, this game changed. Whether it will be enough to prompt a genuine shift in the course of United's season, only time will tell. The chances are it won't. There is too much wrong.

They were lucky to be given an early second-half penalty and lucky that Bruno Fernandes was given the chance to take it again after he missed the first one. They were then lucky in the 83rd minute when Matheus Cunha was handed an equaliser by a defensive howler.

But luck is no good if you don't ride it. And the winning goal Cunha delivered into the net at the near post with four minutes left was not about luck. It was about a team and a player seizing the opportunity to write a different story.

It was born of desire, desperation and perhaps some anger after the pain of the last few weeks. Within a second, the Brazilian's shirt was off and he was in the crowd.

It was hard to blame Cunha, even if he was to pay a price. Booked for that, he was soon cautioned again for a foul and was forced to leave the scene early. At least it was to an ovation. We have not heard one of those here for a while.

Old Trafford was jumping. Of course it was. Thus far this season has brought United supporters only embarrassment and at times earlier here this had felt like a new low. The way United played in the first half was arguably worse than anything we saw under Ruben Amorim and Erik ten Hag.

Matias Soule had given Roma the lead after only 11 minutes

The game was briefly halted in the first-half when fans threw objects including tennis balls onto the pitch

United boss Michael Carrick suffered a further blow when Carlos Baleba limped off

United were in full self-harm mode and in truth could have been four down by half-time.

There was actually nothing in this game for the first 10 minutes but United hurtled from safety to dire straits in the space of seven minutes. That's the way it tends to go and the first Roma goal was simply extraordinary.

When United midfielder Kobbie Mainoo lost the ball in his own half, his team found themselves a defender short. Diogo Dalot, the left back, was so far up field he may as well have been on the Stretford End.

When Rodrigo Mora switched the ball across into the space where Dalot should have been, Soule had time to cut inside and shoot low in to Senne Lammens' bottom left hand corner.

It was one of those goals that looked stranger the more you studied it. Six minutes later, United presented Roma with another one.

Once again Dalot was out of position as the ball reached goalscorer Soule on the edge of the penalty area. This time only a sliding block from Leny Yoro denied the Argentine his second goal but the ball had struck the defender's raised arm and former Aston Villa forward Malen slid in the penalty.

United were shambolic. Fernandes was booked for being, well, Fernandes. It was a harsh card but sadly the United captain's reputation now precedes him. It's the way it works in sport.

As half-time neared, Malen brought a low save from Lammens before Soule curled one from 18 yards that must have missed the righthand post by no more than six inches.

That was the difference between United being out of the game and still in with the chance of the kind of comeback they eventually produced.

Cunha was desperately fortunate to be awarded the penalty that started it all just before the hour. He seemed to trip himself up. Fernandes was then lucky to be offered a retake after his first kick was saved. The level of encroachment by a Roma defender didn't seem to meet the threshold written in law.

But once Fernandes smashed the retake high into the net Roma suddenly found themselves facing a different home team and a different environment.

Bruno Fernandes' first penalty was saved - but he made amends with his second after the referee ordered a retake for encroachment

Victory eases the pressure on United boss Michael Carrick

Carrick will perhaps argue he deserves some fortune. He certainly didn't get any as his team lost at Chelsea at the weekend. Nevertheless, his team needed some more.

Bryan Mbeumo had struck both posts with one shot after running clear immediately after Fernandes' goal while a header from Harry Maguire at a corner hit an opponent when it appeared to be heading in.

But United's fire seemed to be on the verge of burning out when a harmless 83rd-minute cross from Fernandes was completely missed by Roma defender Leonardo Balerdi to present Cunha with an equaliser from six yards.

Roma were by now a shell of the team that they had been in the first half. They had been knocked from their feet by a red tidal wave, and when Cunha unleashed a hammer shot from an angle three minutes later they were dead.

The mortgage operating model after AI

📅 Nov 03, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

AI could move mortgage origination from role-based handoffs to a persistent, evidence-backed borrower profile. Lenders and agencies could apply different risk models to the same underlying data while AI agents manage conditions and document work with human oversight.   AI Summary

In my previous two articles, I explored why the mortgage industry’s biggest AI challenge is structural and how the traditional brokerage operating model creates complexity as businesses grow. That brings us to a more fundamental question: If we could redesign mortgage origination around what AI now makes possible, what would we build?

Today, the process is organized around a series of narrowly defined roles and handoffs, with loan officers, assistants, processors, underwriters and others each responsible for moving the file to the next stage. Every handoff creates another opportunity for information to get lost and responsibility to be pushed downstream.

I believe the mortgage operating model of the future will be organized around a persistent, provable understanding of the borrower and the lifecycle of the loan, rather than the people, systems and handoffs that define the process today.

At its core, a mortgage involves building an evidence-based profile of an individual and applying a risk model to it. Today, that profile is repeatedly reconstructed or revalidated by originators, lenders, underwriters and other participants because each has limited visibility into, or confidence in, the work that came before.

Imagine instead a persistent, evidence-backed borrower profile that brings together information from pay stubs, tax returns, credit reports and other sources, with each data point retaining a verifiable connection to its original evidence. That includes where the information came from and how it was calculated, interpreted or reconciled with other information.

The result would be a more complete and transparent understanding of the borrower that persists throughout the loan lifecycle. Rather than reconstructing that information each time another party needs to assess the loan, participants could trace the underlying evidence, understand how previous conclusions were reached and build on the work already performed, while still conducting their own necessary evaluations and verifications.

To be sure, understanding the borrower is only part of the equation. Every lender, agency and mortgage product has its own requirements and risk criteria. Today, those requirements are translated into guidelines, matrices and documents that must be interpreted and applied by people across multiple stages of the origination process.

With a common, verifiable borrower profile, different risk models could be applied to the same underlying information. A system could evaluate how Fannie Mae, Freddie Mac or a particular lender would assess the borrower, identify missing evidence and determine what needs to happen next.

Specialized AI agents could then coordinate and execute much of that work, from collecting documents and preparing calculations to addressing conditions and identifying exceptions that require human review.

Consider how often a missing document or unresolved discrepancy surfaces late in the process, forcing a loan officer or processor to revisit work that should have been completed weeks earlier. With a more complete understanding of the borrower and the requirements of the loan, those agents could identify many of those issues earlier, coordinate the necessary follow-up and maintain an auditable record of the work performed.

The implications extend beyond making individual tasks faster to changing how information is maintained and how work gets done, creating an opportunity to rethink the organization itself.

When technology maintains the evidence, context and requirements throughout the loan lifecycle, people no longer need to serve as the connective tissue between every task and system. Specialized AI agents can increasingly handle work traditionally distributed across different roles, while experienced mortgage professionals oversee execution, validate results and exercise judgment.

Consider the processor, whose role could evolve from coordinating documents, conditions and handoffs to overseeing the quality and performance of the loan process. Specialized AI agents could collect documents, prepare calculations and track conditions, while the processor sets standards, validates results, resolves exceptions and continuously improves the workflow.

This shift would also change how mortgage organizations define and reward expertise. Rather than measuring success by the number of people someone manages or the tasks they complete, organizations could focus on accurate files, predictable closings, borrower experience and ultimately, a lower cost to originate. By reducing manual handoffs and repetitive work, experienced professionals could oversee more loans without a corresponding increase in staffing.

The result would be a mortgage organization structured around the lifecycle of the loan rather than inherited job descriptions. Instead of scaling by adding more people to manage each stage of origination, companies could build teams around broader accountability for the loan from application to funding, with AI supporting the work required along the way.

Origination is the logical place to prove this model because it is where the borrower’s evidence is first assembled and evaluated. But once a provable, evidence-based profile exists, its usefulness could extend well beyond funding. Other participants across the mortgage value chain, including investors and capital markets firms, could eventually apply their own risk models to the same underlying information, allowing for more granular risk assessment without repeatedly reconstructing the loan.

My prediction is that AI will reshape not only mortgage organizations, but also how they work together. Even as the technology supporting the industry becomes more sophisticated, the underlying operating model could become fundamentally simpler. With evidence and context following the loan, each participant could build on the work already performed while focusing its expertise on the decisions and responsibilities that matter most.

Low-Calorie Diet and Supervised Exercise May Reverse Early Onset Type 2 Diabetes

📅 Nov 03, 2026  |  Category: Health  |  Read Source →

Follow Healthline on our socials for all things health.

The RESET for Remission trial, presented at the 2026 European Association for the Study of Diabetes (EASD) Annual Meeting and published in The Lancet Regional Health Americas on October 1, found that more than half of adults with early onset type 2 diabetes entered remission after six months on a low calorie diet and supervised exercise program.

Early onset type 2 diabetes is when the condition is diagnosed before age 45.

Those with early onset type 2 diabetes may be at a higher risk of complications like long-term damage to the capillaries (microvascular complications), adverse cardiovascular outcomes, and earlier death.

Typical treatment for early onset type 2 diabetes is often a combination of lifestyle changes, like diet and exercise, and medications.

However, this new study suggests that a supervised low calorie diet and exercise program may help some people with early onset type 2 diabetes enter remission.

The RESET for Remission trial included 96 adults from England and Canada who were between the ages of 18 and 45, had early onset type 2 diabetes, obesity, and were not on insulin treatment.

The participants had an average age of 38 and an average body mass index (BMI) of 35.2 kg/m2. They were randomized into a control group that received usual care alone and the intervention group, which ate a low calorie diet and participated in supervised exercise programs.

During the first 12 weeks, participants in the intervention group followed an 800–900-calorie-per-day diet and completed twice-weekly supervised aerobic and resistance exercise sessions, along with one independent exercise session per week.

For weeks 13–24, they transitioned to a weight-maintenance diet and independent exercise.

Both groups were supervised by physicians, and the intervention group was also closely supervised by licensed dietitians.

The low calorie diet consisted of 30% protein, 50% carbohydrate, and 20% fat. Participants were also encouraged to drink at least 2 liters of calorie-free fluids per day and given access to a fiber-based laxative as needed.

In the first two weeks, all meals were replaced with specially formulated nutritional products (Optifast). Then, in weeks 3–12, the participants maintained the same calorie intake but had more flexibility in their food choices.

This second phase of the diet included one conventional meal per day, with daily intake targets that included protein, fruit, and vegetables. Typically, this meant 60 to 90 grams of protein from all sources combined, one portion of fruit, and two portions of non-starchy vegetables.

At the end of 24 weeks, 54% of the intervention group entered remission of type 2 diabetes, compared with only 4% in the control group. Researchers defined remission as an HbA1c level below 6.5% without glucose-lowering medication for at least 12 weeks.

“This study proves what most physicians have advocated for quite some time. Clearly, diet and exercise can work to improve or even resolve diabetes,” David Cutler, MD, board certified family medicine physician at Providence Saint John’s Health Center in Santa Monica, CA, who wasn’t involved in the study, told Healthline.

Cutler also added that this study provides a clear example of how difficult it can be to enter diabetes remission with diet and exercise alone. He stated that the small number of participants in the study also makes him wonder if these findings can be applied to all populations.

Another limitation of this study, as Cutler pointed out, was that the strict dietary limits imposed on study participants would be very difficult for the average adult to adhere to.

The study also followed participants for only six months, so it’s unclear how many would remain in remission over the longer term.

“It is worth trying diet and exercise first because they can work to achieve meaningful weight loss and remission of diabetes,” he said.
