Circle and Trump-linked crypto firm hit with court freeze order after Binance’s $6.3 billion hack

📅 Nov 20, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

An Abu Dhabi court orders Circle and 17 firms to maintain freezes on crypto linked to Binance's theft ahead of a Dec. 1 hearing.

An Abu Dhabi court has ordered Circle and 17 other companies to keep tokens linked to Binance’s Oct. 29 theft frozen while the exchange tries to recover them.

Binance disclosed the order on Nov. 18 and said some issuers, exchanges and other partners ask for a court order before they will release or transfer assets they have frozen.

The exchange said the order from the Abu Dhabi Global Market Courts helps open that route, describing ongoing efforts to trace the stolen funds and giving no details of any transfer under the order.

The named firms include Circle, which issues USDC, and World Liberty Financial, the company behind USD1.

Reports noted that the firms must maintain the freezes pending further proceedings and cannot lend, swap, or mix the affected tokens with other assets.

The court did not accuse the firms of wrongdoing, and Dec. 1 is scheduled to consider whether to keep the injunction in place and on what terms.

The injunction covers specific assets Binance claimed are tied to the theft, and said it is still tracing the stolen crypto and taking legal steps to reclaim it. A transfer for firms seeking a court order depends on whether they have authority to release the specific balances they have frozen.

Tether falls outside the order covering the 18 firms, and the company said on Nov. 2 it had frozen more than $1.5 billion tied to the theft.

A spokesperson said Tether would work directly with Binance to reissue the tokens, in consultation with the relevant authorities.

Thomas Frank shares his feelings on Crystal Palace appointment

📅 Nov 20, 2026  |  Category: Business  |  Read Source →

Thomas Frank is back, and he sounds exactly like a man who has had enough time away to think clearly. Crystal Palace have appointed the 53-year-old on a two-and-a-half-year deal, and his first message was simple. He is refreshed, reflective and ready for another go in the Premier League.

Palace need that. They are bottom of the table, short on momentum and heading into a survival battle that will demand clarity more than noise. Frank arrives after a bruising end to his previous spell at Tottenham, where he was dismissed in February, less than eight months after taking charge. He could have stayed away longer. He chose not to.

There was no attempt to dress it up. Frank admitted the break had been enjoyable. He travelled to Japan and Australia, spent time in Denmark and relaxed with family and friends. As he put it, he had been "visiting friends and family, eating too well [and] drinking too much wine".

He had every reason to extend that time off too. "I was very aware I didn't want to do anything in the summer. I put a statement out there, I needed more time," he said. Then came the line that tells you where his head is now, "I can actually, honestly say I would have been fine if it was three, four, five, six, seven months more, because it's good to be back in the circus, but it's also unbelievably good not to be in, especially if you enjoy life, which I do."

Still, Palace convinced him. The club's internal pitch clearly mattered. Frank singled out chairman Steve Parish, along with Matt Hobbs and Iain Moody, as key figures in persuading him this was the right job at the right time.

That matters because sentiment gets you nowhere at the bottom of the Premier League. Palace need points, quickly. Frank's reign starts with a trip to Coventry City on Saturday, and there is no gentle easing-in period. The task is obvious, steady the team, sharpen the structure and lift belief.

Frank also made it clear that his time out of football was not spent feeling sorry for himself. "If you don't learn from any of your jobs, I don't think you reflect, I don't think you improve, and I don't think you get better," said Frank. "Of course there's a lot of things I have been reflecting over. The details, that will be for another time, but I can say I've learned, definitely."

This is where experience matters. Frank knows the scrutiny, understands the noise and appears entirely unbothered by it. "I think we all have an ego," he said. "Some are better at controlling it than others. For me, it's not the critics, it's more what I can learn, what I can prove, how I can get better."

He was even more direct when discussing outside opinion. "There's a lot of clever people out there, but the interesting thing is they only know 10 per cent of the truth. That's a tenth of the truth at Brentford, there's a tenth of the truth at Tottenham, and that's just the way it is.

"If you can't handle that as a coach or a player, then you shouldn't be in this business, and I'm very happy to be back in the game."

That is the point. No melodrama, no self-pity, no grand promises. Crystal Palace have hired a manager who sounds realistic about the mess in front of him and calm about the pressure around it. Right now, that is a sensible start.

Isaac Asimov’s Book Series Was Unfilmable — Now It's Apple TV's Greatest Space Opera

📅 Nov 20, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Sci-fi fans would collectively agree that some stories of the genre are better off staying on the page. For long, it was also believed that Isaac Asimov's books could not be translated to the screen, and for good reason. Especially when it comes to the Foundation book series, it was hard not to believe it was largely "unfilmable."

Some of the greatest sci-fi books are written in a way that it is easy to directly adapt them for the screen. Isaac Asimov's Foundation books, in contrast, defy almost every rule of visual drama. It unfolds less as a fictional drama and more as a deep dive into everything from sociological mechanics to human psychology. While at it, it also spans across centuries, shifting its focus from one character to another.

The books' massive time jumps, anti-cinematic action, and prioritization of ideas over character beats made it impossible to adapt for the screen. This explains why many planned big-budget adaptations of the books also struggled to see the light of day in the past. Fortunately, however, Apple TV's Foundation took a massive risk and achieved the impossible by successfully adapting Isaac Asimov's books.

Impossible feats demand extreme risks. Owing to this, Apple TV's take on the Foundation books significantly changed the source material's approach. The biggest adaptation challenge came from the books' revolving characters. Every few chapters, the books experience time jumps, where old characters die of age and new ones emerge.

On the screen, viewers need rootable visual anchors who appear for longer periods.

Apple TV's Foundation carefully navigated this issue by introducing cloning, cyro-sleep, and digital consciousness. These became narrative devices that allowed the series to logically sustain the same characters and cast members across centuries and seasons. Even an immortal humanoid, named Lady Demerzel, emerges in Foundation from Asimov's wider universe.

Unlike the books, Apple TV's Foundation also visualizes key conflicts and collapses from the original story instead of solely focusing on their political implications. Even "psychohistory" from Isaac Asimov's Foundation gets a visual and more tangible representation in the series.

Armed with the Socratic method, Apple TV's Foundation understands that "The beginning of wisdom is the definition of terms." Instead of treating Asimov's story as a plot to be faithfully transcribed, the show first understands the true essence of its terms. It then turns into a more sprawling space-opera by wisely redefining its ideas.

Many readers have complained that Apple TV's Foundation is too distinct compared to its source material. They argue that it is unfair to even call it a worthy adaptation. It is hard not to agree with them. An adaptation cannot be considered great if it is a far cry from its source material.

Similar to Stanley Kubrick’s The Shining and Denis Villeneuve's Arrival, Foundation fails as a book-to-screen adaptation on many levels.

However, if purely judged as a modern television space opera, Foundation is easily one of the best sci-fi shows of our time. While Asimov's books seemingly argue that historical forces matter far more than individual stories of heroism, Apple TV's Foundation carefully bends the original narrative to focus on almost mythic heroes and solo characters.

It is also arguable that a direct page-to-page TV adaptation of Isaac Asimov's Foundation books would likely have failed. Whether Apple TV's adaptation approach is ideal is still debatable, but it at least gives us an epic sci-fi space opera.

Isaac Asimov's novel series is brought to life in Foundation, a television adaptation of the epic sci-fi saga created for Apple TV+. The show follows a group of exiled humans that find themselves as the last chance to save the Galactic Empire, primarily from itself.

Inside the rise of 'humble' Nyoni and why Iraola trusts him

📅 Nov 20, 2026  |  Category: Sports  |  Read Source →

Trey Nyoni scored his first Liverpool goal earlier this month

Trey Nyoni knew this was the season when opportunity beckoned for him at Liverpool.

With Curtis Jones leaving for Inter Milan and the club deciding not to buy another midfielder, Nyoni saw the prospect of regular first-team action if he impressed new head coach Andoni Iraola in pre-season.

First impressions matter. So on the day he returned from a holiday in Morocco in June, Nyoni went from the airport to a personal training session in Leicester - where the 19-year-old grew up and where his family are still based.

Nyoni sought the help of a private football coaching operation as he targeted off-season gains before joining up again with Liverpool.

Coach Steve Stavrou and his son Emillio have also worked with players including Harvey Barnes and Haji Wright. Nyoni asked to work on line-breaking passes, getting away from pressure and improving his finishing.

"We merely supplemented the ability he's already got. He's a quiet and humble lad but knows his ability on the pitch and plays with such a confidence," Emillio Stavrou tells BBC Sport.

Under Iraola, Nyoni has thrived after a stellar pre-season, making 13 appearances already, just one shy of his total appearances last season.

Earlier this month, he scored his first goal for the club in Istanbul.

Liverpool ultimately lost to Fenerbahce in the Champions League that night and Nyoni's belief that "the night has been ruined" showed his inner competitiveness.

His first Premier League start came against last season's champions Arsenal on 1 November.

Nyoni fouled Arsenal's captain early on, leading one source to remark: "He's a nice kid but you saw that steel too with the way he went into Martin Odegaard."

Perhaps the most significant praise came from Liverpool legend Steven Gerrard, who said he couldn't see why Iraola was taking Nyoni off just before the hour mark in that game.

Iraola was managing Nyoni's workload, with the midfielder playing 85 minutes three days later in Turkey.

By now, it is clear that Iraola is unafraid to use him.

"He has to continue in the same place he has been since pre-season: being ready for every game. He is playing all the games lately - so we trust him," said Iraola.
