Players Fear For Riftbound As New Legend Makes Up Huge Portion Of Tournament Meta

The Riftbound European Regional Championship just wrapped up in Stuttgart, Germany, and by all accounts, the event was a success for the game, but two distinct controversies over the weekend left a mark on the event for many players.

One involved a notoriously slow-playing participant who questioned the judges' quality and how the rules were implemented, while another was much larger in scale and related to the share percentage of certain Legends leading decks in the tournament. The latter point led to a final eight where two Legends accounted for seven decks, drawing comparisons to the domination of certain archetypes in Magic: The Gathering in recent years.

Update: As this story went live, game director Dave Guskin confirmed that Riftbound bans are due to be announced this Friday, with an effective date TBC.

The first flashpoint from the Riftbound ERC revolved around a player called Templar, who was accused on day one of deliberately slow-playing to run out the clock on opponents, so they could score points late in the game and leave opponents in a position where they couldn't respond.

Worse still, Templar reached a record 8-1, with time concerns in every match, before he was eventually disqualified from the event. There are some unsubstantiated rumors floating around on Reddit that Templar wasn't actually disqualified for his slow play, and it was instead for marking cards, another huge TCG no-no, but most players commenting on the situation believe it was for the slow play, and they're also demanding a follow-up punishment.

After Koko Lopez's ban last month, players are now calling for an example to be made of Templar, with a swift, long ban. If it's possible to prove that Templar was indeed intentionally slow-playing, and their deck was built for this exact purpose, we can see a reality where a ban is handed out, although it would likely be less than what was given to Koko Lopez, which was a much more flagrant breaking of long-standing rules and dishonesty.

Criticism is also being directed at the judges, who were asked to address pace-of-play issues in previous rounds, with players documenting how poorly some judges handled the situation and repeatedly warning Templar to speed up without taking formal action.

We can forgive Riot's mistakes just this once.

TCG metas are weird, but they come and go with every game. For Riftbound right now, Riven is a problem, and while card bans are expected soon to help address it before it develops further, the numbers from Stuttgart are telling. On day one, 126 decks were Riven decks; that's 43.3% of all decks at the event.

The number of Riven decks on day two fell to 47, but that was still 37% of the remaining field. Finally, in the top eight, four of the players were running Riven decks, meaning the percentage was back up to an even 50, and it just showcases the game in a negative light, because from the outside looking in, it appears as though there is little creativity in the strategy of the game, and players end up playing with cards they feel like they have to play with, rather than ones they want to play with. Ultimately, a Kennen player, Evanshrim, won the event; Kennen was the third most popular Legend at the event, with 44 total decks on day one. Outside of the top three Legends (Kai'sa being the remaining top three Legend) there were very few Legends with a meaningful following, and given how young the game is, that's quite disappointing. Riot will surely know this and will be working to balance the meta as soon as possible, especially with the first full year of tournaments now in the books, but the sooner these changes are implemented, the better.

‘One of the highest-stress occupations’: Why $148,000 median pay can’t fix the air traffic controller shortage

📅 Nov 09, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

The U.S. has about 6% fewer air traffic controllers than it did a decade ago, even as the number of flights relying on the air traffic control system has grown 10%, according to a January analysis from the U.S. Government Accountability Office. The shortage isn’t confined to American airspace.

Countries from Australia to Qatar now compete for the same small pool of certified controllers, recruiting across borders with work visas and pay pegged to experience. Aviation training company CAE projects that 71,000 new air traffic controllers will be needed worldwide by 2034, or roughly 7,000 a year.

The U.S. can’t join that bidding war. Every FAA controller must be an American citizen, leaving the agency dependent on a domestic pipeline that turns away the vast majority of applicants.

For airlines, that pipeline sets the pace of the whole system. Delta CEO Ed Bastian told Fortune in April that the biggest opportunity for AI in aviation is in air traffic control, not inside the plane, because it would “do more in terms of helping our customers have quicker travel, more efficient travel.”

The GAO traces much of the decline to disruptions in training. Government shutdowns in 2013 and in 2018 and 2019 froze hiring and training, and the COVID-19 pandemic halted training for four months, then held it at reduced capacity for nearly two years. High attrition from 2019 through 2024 made it harder to catch up.

The 2025 shutdown made things worse: by November of that year, the FAA said nearly half of major air traffic control facilities were short-staffed as controllers worked without pay.

The deeper problem may be the job itself. “It’s one of the highest stress occupations out there,” says Tiffany Bisbey, an assistant professor of industrial/organizational psychology at George Washington University. When controllers show up in academic research, she says, it’s usually as the subjects of studies on stress.

The federal government says as much. The Bureau of Labor Statistics describes the work environment this way: “Their work can be stressful because maximum concentration is required at all times. Night, weekend, and rotating shifts are common.”

Money is not what keeps people away. The median American air traffic controller earned $148,080 a year, or $71.19 an hour, as of May 2025, according to the BLS, and the top 10% earned more than $215,610.

The bar to apply is also lower than many people assume. The FAA requires a bachelor’s degree, one year of progressively responsible work experience, or a combination of the two, and new hires then spend more than a year training on the job. When the FAA pitched the job to Gen Z video gamers this spring, it advertised pay of at least $155,000 after three years.

Other countries are short too, and some are filling the gap by hiring controllers trained elsewhere. In September, Airservices Australia posted a call for experienced international controllers, offering a work visa with eligibility to transition to permanent residency and promising “the perfect balance between work and leisure.”

Australia needs the help. That same month, unplanned absences left eight of Sydney Airport’s 49 controllers unavailable and delayed roughly a third of inbound flights one morning.

The competition cuts both ways. Qatar has been recruiting Airservices Australia controllers, Canada’s NAV CANADA used an outside firm to recruit eight foreign controllers, and South Africa lost 28 controllers in eight months, Reuters reported this month. The country that trains a controller usually bears the full cost of that training, even if the controller later leaves, a spokesperson for the industry group CANSO told the news service.

The FAA’s qualification rules narrow the pool well before training begins. Applicants must be U.S. citizens under 31 and pass pre-employment tests, a medical exam, and a security investigation. “Less than 10% of all applicants meet these requirements and are accepted into the training program,” the FAA says.

Even fewer finish. About 2% of applicants qualify for and complete the full training process, the GAO found.

The agency is hiring faster than it has in years. The FAA hired 2,235 controller trainees in fiscal 2026, the most in its history, and its 2026-2028 workforce plan puts the certified workforce at about 11,000 as of April, against a target of 12,563.

That target is new. In May, the FAA lowered its staffing goal by about 2,000 controllers, citing forecast demand and changes to scheduling and training. The National Air Traffic Controllers Association said it was not involved in drafting the plan.

Bisbey sees no safe shortcut. Fast-tracking applicants or compressing training, she says, is not a viable fix, and she suspects the people who designed controller training “probably thought they needed to do even more, not less.” As she puts it: “It’s such a high-risk organization, and the training is so arduous for a reason.”

The FAA has experimented at the margins, including a 2024 pilot program that let graduates of two Oklahoma schools skip additional FAA Academy training and go straight to facility training. But certifying a controller still takes years, and until the pipeline catches up, travelers should expect more delays.

Bisbey thinks most will take them in stride, since few would trade a delay for a less safe flight. “As annoying as it is, it’s not going to make people stop traveling.”

As Nvidia probes Jensen Huang affair, here's the board member likely to play a big role

📅 2026-11-09  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang is facing an internal investigation after admitting to an affair with a Chinese businesswoman who U.S. intelligence agencies believe is a Chinese spy.

While the company has not yet named specific investigators, that probe is being helmed by the board with help from outside counsel, according to Nvidia. Lawyer Stephen Neal is the board's lead independent director and head of corporate governance, likely making him a key figure in the investigation.

In a statement, Nvidia's board said it has "no reason to believe that either national security or Nvidia secrets were compromised," though it will examine all evidence. China has dismissed the espionage claims. Huang, one of the most powerful businessmen in the artificial intelligence boom, said he would "cooperate fully" with the process.

Yet the probe has sparked uncertainty around Huang's future at the chipmaker and raised concerns of broader ripple effects to companies in the tech sector that are exposed to the AI build-out.

In a Monday note, Citi analysts wrote that one of the key issues for investors moving forward will be "potential governance implications" and "increased regulatory scrutiny" as the company navigates the investigation.

Nvidia's board has 10 independent members, including a former president of Taco Bell, a former Eli Lilly executive and a former vice chairman from Goldman Sachs. Neal, who joined Nvidia's board of directors in 2019, is a chairman emeritus and senior counsel of the law firm Cooley LLP, where he also served as CEO from 2001 to 2008.

Neal did not immediately respond to a request for comment.

It won't be his first corporate governance probe. According to Cooley, Neal has litigated cases across securities, bankruptcies, antitrust, takeovers and more. And he's conducted several investigations for multiple boards of directors, including at General Motors, where he advised the company in disputes with Ross Perot.

He also guided Walter Hewlett and the Hewlett Trust to stop a merger between Hewlett-Packard Co. and Compaq Computer Corp. and helped PacifiCare Health Systems with transactional matters.

According to the firm, Neal has conducted several investigations for committees of directors and independent directors, though most of those client names are confidential. He's also handled investigations with the Securities and Exchange Commission for both corporations and individuals.

When he joined Nvidia, Neal was also the chairman of the board of directors at Levi Strauss & Co. He stepped down in 2021 after reaching the mandatory board retirement age of 72, the company said at the time. The retailer did not immediately respond to a request for comment from CNBC on Monday.

Cooley has a long history with Nvidia, too. The law firm said it incorporated the company in 1993 and has served as its principal outside corporate and litigation counsel for roughly three decades. Cooley handled Nvidia's initial public offering in 1999.

Investors will be closely watching the investigation, and its emphasis on independence, as the process unfolds, according to Brayden King, a professor of management and organizations at Northwestern University's Kellogg School of Management.

"You definitely want somebody leading it who people can look at and say, 'OK, we believe this is a report that we can fully believe and trust that the outcome is the right outcome,'" King told CNBC. "That's what investors are looking for. They just don't like the instability that comes with all of this."

King said the Nvidia investigation lies at a unique intersection of Wall Street and Washington, D.C., where the company is likely navigating its next steps to try to appease both groups.

Because Nvidia's reputation is highly correlated with Huang's reputation, King said, conducting a transparent investigation will be key to regaining and retaining shareholders' trust. Not having an immediate CEO succession plan in place is only impeding that confidence, he added.

"I think the board realizes that they're doing an independent investigation in part because they really need to understand to what extent there are real problems here, and they also know that if they don't do an independent investigation, then there will be lingering concerns about whether their key stakeholders can trust the company," King said.

If the investigation shows that national security was indeed put at risk, King said, the company will need to take "very strong measures" to restore its reputation.

"That will basically come down to having to put structures in place in corporate governance to assure that this can't happen again," King said. "The independent review is going to have to be truly independent, and it's going to have to be candid enough that people believe that we can restore some normalcy around how the company operates."

It's also likely influencing how other tech companies, particularly those in the AI space, are thinking about their own corporate governance structures and how they might navigate increased scrutiny on their leaders, he added.

Marcel Kahan, a professor of law at New York University, said he's not surprised that Nvidia has been potentially exposed to industrial espionage.

Because the company operates in such a high-visibility and crucial sector, Kahan said companies like Nvidia have to be careful in their operations and whom they trust with their information. The investigation could mean the company may consider adding additional governance committees to ensure this doesn't happen again.

The revelations about its CEO will also likely mean more specialized training from Nvidia and its law firms to ensure trade secrets are kept close.

He also said the company is likely prioritizing an in-depth investigation over transparency to investors, focusing on recruiting "the best of the best" to assist with the probe.

"Nvidia is one of the most valuable companies on Wall Street," Kahan told CNBC. "I think, given all the attention that's being paid to it, this is one of the instances where I would not be that worried about anything being done in an underhanded way."

Still, although Nvidia is a major company with massive influence on the market, the controversy may not end up making too large of a dent in corporate governance structures, according to Kathryn Harrigan, a professor of business leadership at Columbia Business School.

Harrigan said because Wall Street and Silicon Valley have seen similar controversies play out before, this scandal may simply be following the script of its predecessors.

"Is it going to change the basic structure of the AI build-out? Probably not," she told CNBC. "You sometimes wait years to get on their schedule if you have an order for chips. It's not like you can magically switch from one vendor to another."

Scientists Discover a Hidden Weakness in Prostate Cancer That Could Transform Treatment

📅 Nov 09, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

A newly identified vulnerability in a cancer-driving protein could pave the way for a different approach to treating prostate cancer.

Researchers at the University of Michigan have identified a potential way to target a protein that helps drive prostate cancer but has long been considered beyond the reach of drugs. Their experimental compound, PBITE-1, interfered with the protein’s cancer-promoting activity in laboratory models, offering a possible starting point for more targeted treatments.

The protein, known as ERG, becomes abnormally active when two genes, TMPRSS2 and ERG, fuse together. This genetic rearrangement occurs in roughly half of prostate cancer cases among patients of European ancestry and promotes tumor cell growth and migration.

For years, ERG has been considered “undruggable” because it appeared to lack the molecular pockets where medications typically attach. In the new study, published in the Proceedings of the National Academy of Sciences, researchers discovered an accessible pocket in a region called the PNT domain, which helps regulate ERG’s interactions with other proteins.

After screening more than 1,600 compounds, the team identified a promising molecule and refined it into PBITE-1. In experiments involving prostate cancer cell lines and laboratory models of human and mouse prostate tissue, the compound inhibited ERG activity, triggered cancer cell death, and reduced the ability of cancer cells to invade surrounding tissue.

“Although we were the first to identify the gene fusion as a driver of prostate cancer, it was challenging to find a drug target for ERG,” said Arul Chinnaiyan, M.D., Ph.D., S.P. Hicks Professor of Pathology and member of the Rogel Cancer Center.

Most existing prostate cancer therapies focus on androgen receptors, proteins that respond to hormones such as testosterone. These receptors can activate the TMPRSS2-ERG fusion, helping fuel tumor growth. Drugs that block androgen signaling can slow the disease, but tumors often develop resistance, and treatment can cause side effects that affect quality of life.

Targeting ERG directly could provide an alternative approach for patients whose tumors carry the gene fusion, rather than relying exclusively on treatments that interfere with hormone signaling.

“This work shows that instead of treating all prostate cancer as one disease, we have the potential to personalize therapy for subtypes of prostate cancer.”

PBITE-1 remains an experimental research tool, and its effects have not been established in patients. Further research will be needed to determine whether compounds targeting ERG can be developed into safe and effective medicines.

“Although it is unlikely that PBITE-1 is ready to become a drug any time soon, this study shows that ERG is a druggable target,” Chinnaiyan said.

“It is exciting that we now have a path forward to target ERG in prostate cancer.”

9 All-Inclusive Resorts in Cancún That Go Way Beyond an Open Bar

📅 Nov 09, 2026  |  Category: World  |  Read Source →

I first arrived in Cancún, Mexico, more than 20 years ago. I had big hair, pencil-thin eyebrows, and limited expectations for an all-inclusive vacation: A swim-up bar serving frozen margaritas was about all I needed. Since then, my priorities have shifted, and thankfully, Cancún’s all-inclusive resorts have grown up alongside me.

After two decades of return visits made easier by an abundance of nonstop US flights, I’ve become more discerning about what deserves a place in that up-front price. I’ve also discovered that Cancún’s enduring spring break reputation tells an incomplete story about what's on offer. Choose the right resort, and you can have a butler draw your evening bath, a private pool overlooking the Caribbean, and à la carte restaurants you’ll look forward to all afternoon, whether you prefer the Hotel Zone’s long sweep of white sand, the relative quiet of Playa Mujeres, or a boat ride out to Isla Mujeres.

The frozen margaritas are still here. But now, there’s much more worth checking in for. Read on for the best all-inclusive resorts in Cancún worth planning a vacation around.

Mexican art gives this Playa Mujeres resort its personality. Rubén Soto’s concrete mural depicts native wildlife amid geometric patterns, while suites carry the aesthetic through warm woods and marble rather than tropical clichés. At Atelier, which welcomes ages 16+, you might have lunch overlooking the water at El Suspiro, then enjoy a pre-dinner mezcal cocktail at Bar de la Calle. Back in your suite, a rolling minibar holds wine and gourmet snacks, and room service answers around the clock. Most dining and premium spirits are included, though María Dolores by Edgar Núñez warrants a separate reservation—and budget—for its contemporary Mexican menu. Book an Inspira suite, and a butler can arrange your dinner reservations while you spend the afternoon at the rooftop infinity pool.

At Le Blanc, the pampering begins before you’ve unpacked—and your butler can handle that, too. Once you’re settled, the day’s agenda might amount to a poolside cocktail, a soak in the spa’s hydrotherapy pools, and deciding what to wear to dinner. This adults-only Hotel Zone retreat pairs pale interiors and Caribbean frontage with an unusually generous all-inclusive package: À la carte dining, premium drinks, 24-hour room service, and access to the spa’s hydrotherapy circuit are all included. Dinner might mean a French tasting menu at the esteemed Lumière or Italian cooking at Blanc Italia. Massages cost extra, but the daily chance to steam, soak, and unwind in the spa does not.

Two beaches are better than one, especially when they come with a lighthouse and Caribbean views in nearly every direction. Perched on the tip of Punta Cancún, Hyatt Ziva makes the most of its peninsula setting, with room for both family playtime and grown-up downtime. Little ones can race down waterslides and splash beneath fountains, while teenagers claim their own turf over billiards and foosball in the teens' lounge. For those whose idea of vacation involves neither, Turquoize (a tower within the resort) offers an adults-only escape with ocean-facing suites, butlers just a WhatsApp message away, and a rooftop infinity pool blissfully removed from the splash zone.

Behind Paradisus’s five glass-topped pyramids, one of Cancún’s most recognizable silhouettes, vines cascade through a soaring atrium, fountains burble below, and newly redesigned rooms turn beds toward Caribbean sunrises or Nichupté Lagoon sunsets. A 2026 renovation refreshed this Cancún landmark without sacrificing its theatrical streak. Days might involve salsa lessons, pickleball, or children gleefully getting drenched beneath splash-park tipping buckets. Included meals feature freshly pressed tortillas at Taqueria Blue Agave and ceviche at Agua Marina, and bars pour Patrón and Grey Goose without a surcharge. For a quieter stay, The Reserve upgrade tier adds an adults-only pool, restaurant, and concierge service.

Here, the first cocktail arrives before you do, served aboard the open-bar catamaran that carries you from Cancún to this adult-only retreat on Isla Mujeres. Joining Hyatt’s Unbound Collection in November 2026, Impression lives up to its name, with terraces cascading toward a sheltered beach and rooftop infinity pools that give you every reason to admire the water without venturing down to it. Each of the 125 suites comes with dedicated butler service and all-inclusive coverage, including gourmet à la carte dining. At Spezia, decadent Italian cooking offers a reason to dress for dinner, though your butler can arrange a quieter evening in, should you desire.

Not all Cancún resorts are sprawling tropical compounds. At Secrets The Vine, a sleek adults-only tower brings a more cosmopolitan beach experience, with teak furnishings, Italian porcelain floors, and balconies suspended over that improbable Caribbean blue. As the name suggests, afternoons and evenings are devoted to the grape. Sommelier-led tastings gather guests around a handcrafted wooden table at the Vine Bar; evenings bring Mexican and international pours, and between glasses, a white-sand beach and three infinity pools tempt you outside. While many wines and premium spirits are included, special bottles from the cellar carry additional charges but are well worth it.

Dinner without one eye on a restless toddler is a luxury Finest Playa Mujeres has thought to provide. At Imagine Lounge, staff look after potty-trained children ages three and older for up to 90 minutes while parents eat nearby. The practical details are equally reassuring: homemade baby purées, bottle warmers, and family suites with a separate bedroom for the children. Meals, drinks, and 24-hour room service are included, so parents can order dinner after the little ones are asleep. One Spa even offers treatments for younger guests, though those appointments cost extra.

At Almare’s swim-up bar, the rum is blended with coconut or mango; at Botanik, the coffee is Mexican and locally roasted. Those touches give this 109-room, adults-only retreat on Isla Mujeres a sense of place. Get better acquainted through a tikin xic cooking experience devoted to the island’s signature fish dish, or take a kayak out from the beach—all complimentary. There’s a pool by the shore and an infinity pool on the rooftop, where dinner at Seasalt comes with Caribbean views and Mexican-European cooking. The resort also arranges guided visits to Punta Sur’s temple ruins for a fee, where you’ll hear stories of the Maya goddess Ixchel.

Excellence Playa Mujeres is a place to spread out—even the entry-level suites are upwards of 800 square feet, with whirlpool tubs and furnished terraces. Book a rooftop suite, and you can spend the afternoon between your plunge pool and daybed, with lunch delivered whenever you’re ready. Venture downstairs, though, and there's so much more: winding pools lead past swim-up terraces, beach waiters keep the margaritas coming, and dinner could mean Indian curries at Basmati or a lingering French meal at Chez Isabelle. Club suites at this adults-only Playa Mujeres retreat add private pools and beach areas and one Miilé Spa hydrotherapy session per person, per stay.

What is the best all-inclusive resort in Cancún?

For an adult-only stay, Le Blanc Spa Resort Cancún gets my vote. Butler service and French tasting menus make it feel suitably indulgent, and the rate includes access to the spa’s hydrotherapy circuit. With children, I’d choose Finest Playa Mujeres, where separate kids' bedrooms and supervised care during mealtimes give parents a holiday too.

Is it worth getting an all-inclusive package in Cancún?

Yes, especially if you plan to spend most of your vacation at the resort. At the right property, your rate covers dinners you actually look forward to, premium drinks, and activities you’d otherwise pay for separately. If you plan to spend each day exploring and eating around town, a traditional hotel may make more sense. Before booking your all-inclusive, be sure to check the exclusions—you might find you'll have to pay extra for spa treatments, special bottles of wine, and some restaurant experiences.

What’s the cheapest month to go to Cancún?

September is generally one of the cheapest months for Cancún hotels, after summer vacation crowds leave. The trade-off is wetter weather and the possibility of tropical storms during hurricane season. For a better balance of price and weather, consider late October through the week before Thanksgiving.

There’s so much content on Netflix that it has both some of the best movies and TV shows ever made, and some of the worst. But you’ll also find some of the greatest acting ever captured on film: Adam Sandler in Uncut Gems, Vanessa Kirby in Pieces of a Woman, Joel Edgerton and William H. Macy in Train Dreams. So, here are the greatest acting performances in any Netflix original series.

Since her breakout role as Eleven, Millie Bobby Brown has become one of Netflix’s go-to contract stars, and it’s easy to see why. Eleven stole the show in Stranger Things’ first season, and Brown demonstrated an impressive amount of depth and range for such a young performer in such a limiting role.

When we first met Eleven, she was completely alone and she couldn’t speak, so Brown had absolutely nothing to work with to endear us to this character, and yet she pulled it off. Eleven quickly became a beloved sci-fi icon.

Cristin Milioti was so great in Black Mirror, the show ended up giving her episode a sequel to continue her story. Nanette Cole is a young programmer whose consciousness is transferred into her boss’ twisted “It’s a Good Life”-style sociopathic fantasy world.

When she gets there, Nanette is just one of the despicable Robert Daly’s digital playthings. But by the end of her first intergalactic adventure, she’d managed to turn the tables on her oppressor and free herself from his cybernetic shackles. It was an empowering journey, and Milioti played every step of it perfectly.

When Netflix first announced its Ripley series, it didn’t seem like we needed another Ripley adaptation. We’ve seen it all before, and Matt Damon already gave an iconic turn that defined the character on the big screen in The Talented Mr. Ripley.

But Ripley ended up being a worthwhile readaptation. Not only did it have gorgeous, moody, monochromatic cinematography to make it more visually engaging than the movie; Andrew Scott did something completely different from Damon, and used the extra hours of a TV series to really explore the psyche of this curious character.

Everyone in The Crown did a great job of humanizing the royals, including everyone who played Queen Elizabeth II throughout her reign. Olivia Colman was spectacular as an older Queen Elizabeth, but Claire Foy was the one who got everyone hooked on The Crown in season 1.

It was heartbreaking to see Violet McGraw’s young Nell go through all that trauma, but even more devastating to see those traumas sunken into Victoria Pedretti’s anxious eyes as grownup Nell.

The revelation that Nell was being haunted by her own future self all this time makes Pedretti’s performance even more impressive. She’s both the haunter and the hauntee in this chilling haunted-house tale.

Steven Yeun and Ali Wong both gave fantastic performances as the lead characters in Beef, and they both won much-deserved Emmys for them. They both committed so wholeheartedly to the petty rage that you fully believe these two people have nothing better to do than torment each other.

“The Debut” is told from her point of view, as she tries to talk the overbearing local community theater director Jerry (Paul Giamatti) into hiring her, an untrained actress. He does it because he has no one else. But first, he must teach her how to act.

Mona dresses like a schoolgirl, with skirts, blouses, cardigan sweaters. “She’s so contained, and the way she learns is so literal,” said Moore, “the way a baby learns, like, you make a face at a baby, the baby makes a face back, but they don’t have intention behind it. Mona goes from an invisible nonentity in her own home to someone who demands attention. “She’s a kid who has found friends for the first time,” said Eisenberg. “She’s the girl who transferred to the right school at seven years old, and then the first rehearsal happens, and she is told she is terrible, and she has no idea why.
