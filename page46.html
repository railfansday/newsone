Oliver Glasner has finally seen how he can get the best out of Liam Delap at Nottingham Forest.

The summer signing has struggled to adapt to life at the City Ground, with just one goal in seven games before he faced Manchester City.

His performance against Manchester City provided an insight into how he can become a true weapon for Forest this season.

Although Delap scored twice, that is not the important part of his display, but rather the manner in which he had scored them.

His underlying statistics hint as to why his role against Man City should be a permanent change.

With Forest standing to benefit immensely from it.

Delap's physicality makes him an imposing striker, but Forest are not getting the best out of him when they ask him to play with his back to goal.

Delap, despite his stature and physical presence, is not a natural or stereotypical hold-up centre-forward.

The Englishman is much better when he is barreling towards goal than he is with his back to it.

And at times, Nottingham Forest can be found guilty of using him as that back-to-goal striker.

The game against Manchester City was further proof of this, with his two goals coming from him charging at defenders, rather than holding them off.

And it is further exemplified by Delap having one of the higher dribble counts among Premier League strikers.

As per FotMob, Delap's 11 dribbles completed rank in the 88.6th percentile.

Additionally, his 61.2 per cent passing accuracy shows that he is quite poor at link-up play.

So, Oliver Glasner needs to find ways to get him on the ball, running towards goal, rather than him standing up top, holding off defenders and linking up with his fellow forwards.

If Glasner is looking for a hold-up centre-forward, then he already has another option to turn to in Chris Wood, rather than asking Delap to adapt his game.

The New Zealand forward is typically capable of performing the role Delap has often found himself in.

That provides the Austrian with another route to goal, and one that would not affect Delap's transformation at Forest as well.

Delap does not need to become a traditional target man when Wood already offers those qualities.

Bitcoin is struggling to find its footing after another brutal sell-off pushed its price below $73,000. The decline wiped out hundreds of millions of dollars in leveraged positions, leaving us questioning if the market has entered a deeper correction phase.

Bitcoin is now trading around $73,600 after briefly losing the $73,000 level. Ethereum slipped below $2,100, while XRP approached $1.05 and Solana struggled to hold $82. More than $723 million in crypto positions were liquidated over 24 hours, with nearly 79% coming from long bets.

The timing is particularly uncomfortable for Bitcoin bulls. October’s hotter-than-expected US inflation report has revived concerns about interest rates, while recent US election results have introduced another layer of regulatory uncertainty. Now, a banking dispute involving the Federal Reserve is adding fuel to the fire.

The sell-off has several forces working against it. First, October’s US Consumer Price Index (CPI) increased 0.4% month over month, exceeding economists’ expectations of 0.3%. Core inflation also climbed 0.3%, above the 0.2% forecast.

The interest-rate outlook has become even more concerning. According to CME’s latest rate probability data, the chance of the Federal Reserve keeping its target rate unchanged at 3.75%–4% has fallen to zero. This represents a sharp reversal from just one day earlier, when the probability of unchanged rates stood at 16%. It was 17.3% a week ago and 15.9% one month earlier.

Markets now assign an 86.3% probability to a 25-basis-point rate increase, taking the target range to 4%–4.25%. Another 13.7% probability is assigned to a larger 50-basis-point increase.

The liquidation data paints an equally uncomfortable picture. CoinGlass recorded $732 million in forced closures across crypto markets over 24 hours on November 11. Long positions accounted for $578 million, meaning traders betting on higher prices absorbed most of the damage.

Bitcoin also lost its 50-week moving average near $76,100, ending a seven-week streak above the technical level. The breakdown followed a week of heavy institutional selling, with US spot Bitcoin ETFs recording approximately $1.28 billion in net outflows.

The combination of ETF withdrawals, leveraged liquidations and inflation uncertainty has created a difficult environment for buyers. Social chatter has pushed back against describing the move as a full-blown crash, pointing to comparisons with the Nasdaq’s monthly decline. Nevertheless, losing important technical levels has given bears another reason to test market confidence.

Bitcoin price problems are unfolding alongside renewed political anxiety. Recent US midterm election results have strengthened Democratic influence in Congress, raising questions about the future direction of crypto regulation.

The crypto community is increasingly discussing the possibility of a Gensler 2.0 regulatory environment. The concern centers on whether a tougher approach could return for digital asset companies, particularly decentralized autonomous organizations (DAOs), digital asset treasuries (DATs), memecoins, and privacy-focused crypto.

The regulatory uncertainty could complicate efforts to establish clearer rules for the industry. While tokenization and stablecoin initiatives may continue under existing frameworks, Democratic oversight could slow new legislation and challenge the durability of regulatory guidance.

Meanwhile, Old Glory Bank CEO Mike Ring has accused the Federal Reserve of blocking his institution’s crypto-integrated banking launch at the last minute.

What do all these mean for crypto? The immediate challenge for Bitcoin price remains getting back above $75,000 and rebuilding confidence after the latest liquidation wave. A deeper decline would put additional pressure on already nervous traders.

Yet the longer-term Bitcoin thesis has not necessarily disappeared. Concerns about government spending, monetary debasement, and currency purchasing power continue to support the argument for holding scarce digital assets.

📅 Nov 11, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Ethereum's Lighthouse urged all beacon node operators to install a high-priority security patch ahead of a planned vulnerability disclosure.

Ethereum consensus client Lighthouse released a security patch on Nov. 9 and urged operators of all its beacon nodes to upgrade as soon as possible.

The v8.3.3 release makes that work a high priority for users who stake and those who do not, but the release notice rates validator-client upgrades as low priority for staking users.

As a consensus client, Lighthouse connects with other clients in Ethereum's proof-of-stake network. Its beacon node works with an execution engine to validate transactions in blocks, while the validator client connects to the beacon node and manages keys, according to the Lighthouse Book.

Operators can also run a beacon node without a validator client. Lighthouse's documentation includes data analysis and applications among these non-staking uses, and those operators receive the same high-priority beacon-node instruction as staking users in the v8.3.3 update table.

The project's priority definitions put high-priority work on a timescale of hours or days, to be completed as soon as possible. Low-priority work belongs in the next routine update cycle, with two weeks given as an example.

The beacon-node upgrade is high priority with or without staking. Staking users have a separate, lower-priority validator-client update.

Upgrade the beacon node promptly. The validator-client update has low priority and belongs in the routine cycle.

Beacon nodes used for applications or data analysis have the same high-priority instruction. No validator-client priority applies to this group.

Staking and non-staking beacon nodes are high priority; staking validator clients can use the routine update cycle.

The release says technical details of the vulnerabilities will be disclosed later. Links to security advisories are due to be added once network operators have had time to upgrade.

That approach follows Sigma Prime's Nov. 5 explanation of its disclosure process. The Lighthouse developer says it initially holds back vulnerability information to give users time to update their nodes, then adds details to release notes and GitHub Security Advisories.

The v8.3.3 notice does not report active exploitation or identify losses, and attack mechanics and potential impact remain undisclosed. The high-priority label does not disclose the technical severity or consequences of an attack.

The request is specific to Lighthouse's beacon-node software. For operators running both Lighthouse components, completing a validator-client update alone would still leave the beacon-node instruction outstanding.

InfoQ Homepage News Angular's TypeScript 7-Powered Compiler Enters Beta with Rust-Based Preprocessor

Nov 11, 2026 2 min read

The Angular team has shared details of a new version of its compiler, the Angular Preprocessor (ngp), which rewrites the analysis stage of the toolchain in Rust to clear the way for TypeScript 7 adoption. Alex Rickabaugh and Mark Techson announced the work on the Angular team blog in late September, shortly after Microsoft shipped TypeScript 7.0, whose Go-based rewrite removed the ts.Transformer API that Angular's ahead-of-time compiler depends on.

The existing compiler, ngc, wraps tsc and uses the Transformer API to turn decorators such as @Component and @Pipe into runtime instructions. The Go port offers no cross-language equivalent, and crossing the JS/Go boundary at every file would be prohibitive. The Angular team's answer is to decouple compilation from TypeScript's APIs and rebuild it on Oxc, the VoidZero Rust toolchain already powering Vite. The team pointed to the plan on X, framing it as a partial rewrite rather than a fresh start.

ngp runs in three stages. A Rust frontend using Oxc analyses files in parallel to discover Angular declarations and resolve metadata. The existing TypeScript template compiler then emits two artefacts, a runtime file containing the transformed component and a .ngtypecheck.ts file holding the Type Check Block (TCB) for template type-checking. Finally a downstream tool, whether tsgo, tsrs, esbuild or Rolldown, handles type-checking and JavaScript emission independently.

The bulk of ngp landed on 8 October in a single commit of more than 370,000 lines, around 56,000 of them Rust. Cédric Exbrayat of Ninja Squad benchmarked the beta against three projects ranging from 15 to 1,714 components and measured a 2x to 3x speedup on cold builds, with memory use on the largest project falling from 2.8 GB to 1.4 GB. Pairing ngp with tsrs and Rolldown gave the fastest pipeline, with tsgo and esbuild only marginally slower. They concluded there is further gains to be made:

This is already very promising, and there are certainly further gains to be made when the team focuses on performance optimization.

Christopher Holder at push-based.io took a different tack, writing vite-plugin-ngp to compile Angular files on demand. On the Super Productivity codebase (252 components), the dev server reached the first page in 10.5 seconds against 17.4 for ng serve, with template edits applying in 112 milliseconds rather than a 2.6 second full reload. On a generated 5,000-component app the gap widened to 1.6 seconds against 28.4. Holder cautioned that the plugin "is a prototype on a prototype" pinned to 22.3.0-next.2.

On Reddit, a number of developers are excited about the update and write up, with one commenting:

Read the whole article. Impressive. The Angular team surpassed my expectations!

VoidZero's own experimental Oxc Angular compiler, covered by InfoQ in April, reported a roughly 97% improvement on narrower workloads but is now described as "actively seeking maintainers". The Angular team confirmed VoidZero's work influenced the architecture and that the two groups have been collaborating, with ngp becoming the official path.

Teams on earlier Angular versions should first run the standard update guide to reach v20 or later. Adoption of ngp is expected to land in a minor release of Angular 22 once TypeScript 7.1 restores the compiler API; until then production builds should stay on ngc.

Rivian's Vans Got a Serious Upgrade. Could Another Major Fleet Deal Finally Follow?

📅 Nov 11, 2026  |  Category: Business  |  Read Source →

There are more reasons for fleet operators to take a second look.

Rivian (RIVN -2.21%) investors can be forgiven for keeping their eyes glued to the R2. The highly anticipated and more affordable electric SUV is central to the company's growth plans, and it has already become Rivian's best-selling vehicle.

But another growth opportunity that has spent some time on the back burner is quietly getting an upgrade. Last month, Rivian began delivering new configurations of its electric delivery van (EDV) that could help attract another substantial fleet order – years after loosening its exclusive arrangement with retail juggernaut Amazon (AMZN +1.48%). Let's dig into what changed, why it matters, and how Amazon still has a say in certain potential deals.

The upgrades include an all-wheel-drive configuration and a larger battery offering 30% more range. Rivian says these additions are designed to expand coverage into more rural delivery networks. That could help existing customers expand their use while giving prospective buyers another reason to take a look. Nobody orders a fleet because the headlights are cute, or if they do, they probably won't find themselves in business long. These vehicles need to earn their parking spaces through creating some kind of value and/or efficiency for the operator. Here are brief reminders of just some of the advantages Rivian's EDVs offer.

First, the potential for energy savings is real. Electricity can cost less per mile than gasoline or diesel, especially if there's well-managed depot charging, including charging at off-peak times. Fuel prices remain elevated compared to a year ago, giving operators reason to run the numbers again.

Second, electric vans eliminate engine oil changes and have fewer moving drivetrain parts, while regenerative braking reduces brake wear. That doesn't eliminate repairs or downtime, but it generally means less routine maintenance. The upgrades could bring the vans' existing advantages to more routes. For fleet operators, those advantages come down to cost. If pilot testing shows prospective buyers that Rivian's EDVs can lower the cost per delivery, the vehicles become compelling.

Rivian changed its agreement with Amazon in late 2023, opening the door to other commercial customers. There has been progress since then, with HelloFresh adding 70 Rivian EDVs, while AT&T (T +0.64%) announced an agreement to purchase Rivian vehicles through a pilot program.

Those are recognizable names, but the next meaningful milestone would be a substantial fleet commitment demonstrating broader demand beyond Rivian's anchor customer, which really put the company on the map for many investors in the early days.

There's also a bit of a catch for Rivian and its investors. Some of the businesses best suited to use Rivian's EDVs could also compete with Amazon. Rivian's latest filing says sales to certain last-mile delivery and retail customers still require Amazon's consent, potentially discouraging those buyers. Rivian's upgraded EDVs could attract more interest, but Rivian doesn't have complete freedom in turning that interest into orders.

For shareholders, another sizable customer would matter beyond the delivery total. It would emphasize that Rivian can build a broader commercial business around a product developed primarily for Amazon. Furthermore, if potential increased demand for its EDVs increases factory output, it could help spread fixed costs beyond its scaling R2 production. Watch for pilot programs turning into larger purchases and existing customers expanding their fleets. I had hoped to see more of that this year, but the upgrades give prospective buyers another reason to take a look.

Rivian's R2 deserves plenty of the attention it's currently receiving. Just don't overlook the delivery van parked beside it -- it may yet have another big order down the road.
