Taiwan’s Chunghwa Telecom Unveils Investment Slate Headed By ‘Gunshot’ & ‘Call Of Lobster’

📅 Nov 08, 2026  |  Category: Business  |  Read Source →

Taiwanese telco Chunghwa Telecom announced a slate of film and series investments at Taiwan Creative Content Fest (TCCF), including police crime drama Gunshot, starring Kent Tsai, and Call Of Lobster, starring Choi Siwon and Chang Chen.

Chunghwa is one of three Taiwanese telcos that are investing in film and TV content through funds launched in partnership with the Taiwanese government.

The company’s previous content investments include feature films Arrested Memory, a Japan-Taiwan co-production directed by Sabu, and The Photo From 1977, directed by Frank Cheng and Sheng-Jung Tang. The two films have a combined 12 nominations in the upcoming Golden Horse Awards. Gunshot, also starring Chan Tzu-Hsuan, Cheng Chih-Wei, Lu Hsiao-Fen and Mark Lee, follows three police officers with conflicting views of justice trying to navigate a system that rewards visibility over virtue. The eight-part series is produced by PTS Taigi TV with S11 Partners and Third Culture Content. Watch on Deadline

Other drama series investments from Chunghwa include Yesterday’s Daughter, Tomorrow’s Mother, a family fantasy series created by award-winning producer Phoebe Ma and director Hung Tzu-peng; fantasy adventure series Guides To The World Below, adapted from a popular Taiwanese comic; and C’est La Vie, an urban drama blending cuisine and women’s personal growth, with actress Esther Liu serving as executive producer.

On the film front, Chunghwa is investing in Call Of Lobster, a cross-border action comedy loosely based on the true story of a Taiwanese lobster farmer who offered his services to the North Korean government. Chang Chen and Super Junior member Choi Siwon are set to star in the film, directed by Yin Chen-hao and produced by Calendar Studios.

Chunghwa boarded the project after it won the Chunghwa Telecom Award at TCCF in 2024. Taiwan’s Ministry of Culture is also backing the film.

Among other film investments, Chunghwa also announced its participation in Studio76’s Demon Of The East – Black Condo, part of a horror cinematic universe spanning four Asian countries. Outside of films and series, Chunghwa is also backing reality cooking show Next Z Chef and indie band Shallow Levée’s Speak in Silence Asian tour.

At the slate announcement event in TCCF, Taiwan’s Minister of Culture Li Yuan said: “Chunghwa Telecom is like a pyramid. I am grateful that, beyond building a strong foundation through its thriving core business, the company is also willing to invest at the very top of the pyramid, supporting film and television professionals as they continue moving forward and creating even better work.”

Chunghwa Telecom Senior Executive Vice President Lin Wen-chiyh said: “Chunghwa Telecom is more than just a platform operator; we are also a driving force behind the long-term development of the content industry. Moving forward, we will continue to connect funding, creative talent, and domestic and international partners, leveraging our group’s resources and industry partnerships to help Taiwanese content reach international markets.”

Democrats plan to push clean energy to lower bills and challenge Trump’s fossil fuel agenda

📅 Nov 08, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Democrats can begin investigations and hold hearings into climate policy rollbacks and elimination of green energy grants by the Republican administration, potentially trying to undo some changes and restore renewable energy incentives.

Some Democrats are promising to push for more clean energy to help tackle rising energy costs when they take control of Congress in January, though President Donald Trump says he will use his veto power aggressively.

With new majorities in the House and Senate after last week’s midterms, Democrats can set an agenda boosting clean energy and addressing climate change. While those are traditionally core priorities of the party, climate was not a central topic in this election cycle.

Democrats can begin investigations and hold hearings into climate policy rollbacks and elimination of green energy grants by the Republican administration, potentially trying to undo some changes and restore renewable energy incentives.

Sen. Sheldon Whitehouse of Rhode Island, the top Democrat on the Senate Committee on Environment and Public Works, said Trump’s clean energy attacks drove up prices for taxpayers. He said Democrats will “work every angle to lower consumers’ energy costs, and we will demand accountability for the massive corruption wrought by Trump’s fossil-fuel donors.”

American Clean Power Association CEO Jason Grumet said everybody who is trying to build clean energy “feels bolstered.”

Enacting major changes would require Republican support because Democrats will lack the numbers to override presidential vetoes. Even then, lower energy bills are not guaranteed because prices also depend on fuel costs, demand and the expense of maintaining the electric grid.

“The wins give Democrats more leverage over spending and more political power to advance a coherent agenda; the question is whether they’ll use that power effectively,” said Lisa Sachs, director of the Columbia Center on Sustainable Investment. “The vote was for affordability and opportunity. Democrats now need to be clear about which policies deliver them.”

Trump said after Tuesday’s election that Democrats will send over “things that are insane” and he will veto them, speaking generally about legislation. But in the comments to Fox News, he predicted that “after a couple of months of vetoes,” he and Democrats will find ways to work together.

The White House said in a statement Wednesday that Trump is “committed to unleashing reliable, affordable, and secure energy sources to lower prices, strengthen our grid, and protect our national security, and is willing to work with anyone to accomplish this.”

Trump promised to lower energy prices. Instead, his war with Iran and other policies increased the price of gasoline, diesel and electricity. At the same time, the administration canceled many clean energy projects and grants and slowed clean energy permitting.

Trump particularly dislikes offshore wind. Developer Orsted said Thursday that Revolution Wind, a large wind farm in federal waters off Rhode Island, has started operating commercially.

Will the president change his tune on green energy? That’s doubtful, said former Sen. Byron Dorgan, D-N.D., co-chair of the government relations practice at ArentFox Schiff.

“He is likely to keep pushing his energy issues, and I think he will be forced to give some room for a new Congress to recreate a different strategy that will add clean energy,” Dorgan said in an email.

Trump promotes expanding production of oil, natural gas and coal, which cause climate change, because he argues that would give the U.S. the lowest-cost electricity. Mike Sommers, CEO of the American Petroleum Institute, the oil and gas industry’s top lobbying group, said that as voters have changed the balance of political power in Washington, “the affordability message is unmistakable.”

Sommers said in a statement there is a real opportunity for bipartisan cooperation to reduce costs and strengthen the economy, starting with an energy permitting reform proposal announced in September. The legislation is aimed at shortening the timeline to get big energy projects started.

Ife Kilimanjaro, executive director of the U.S. Climate Action Network, said Democrats should reject that proposal and use their stronger position to pass legislation that actually builds out much-needed clean energy.

The administration has reshaped the Environmental Protection Agency, reversing pollution limits as it promotes fossil fuels.

Clear Street energy transition analyst Tim Moore said he expects Democrats to investigate the climate and environmental rollbacks, in an effort to block some from taking effect. Democrats could also try amending the Clean Air Act to cut carbon pollution and push back against EPA budget cuts, he added.

Rep. Sean Casten, D-Ill., said he is cautiously optimistic some Republicans will now seek to differentiate themselves from Trump and back pro-environmental policies. But without Republican support, Democrats’ more ambitious bills will largely lay out their agenda for 2028, he added.

Climate is not necessarily at the top of Democrats’ list of priorities. They are targeting healthcare access, trade tariffs and the Iran war.

Rep. Frank Pallone, the New Jersey Democrat who is expected to become chair of the House Energy and Commerce Committee, said the party should focus on healthcare, affordability, consumer and environmental protections, clean energy and regulating Big Tech.

David Victor, a professor of innovation and public policy at the University of California, San Diego, largely expects a stalemate at the federal level on climate and energy policy because of the divided government. He said agreement could come on some bipartisan measures such as a permitting overhaul.

Victor thinks Democratic-led investigations and hearings will center on corruption and other topics that may only touch on energy and climate. Like Casten, Victor said many of the Democratic bills likely will not become law, but will outline what Democrats could accomplish if they win the White House in 2028.

“The Democrats are going to be drafting things that the administration’s not going to like. They don’t have the votes to override a veto, and on top of it, the administration is going to be in turmoil because of its own electoral failures and because of all the investigations,” Victor said. “This seems to me, at the federal level, largely a recipe for kind of intense chaos over the next two years.”

One of the biggest wild cards is the behavior of the administration, which could use executive power to double down on rollbacks of climate and environmental rules, he said.

Democrats scored victories in races for governor in states long controlled by Republicans, including Texas and Georgia.

Leah Stokes, a political scientist at the UC Santa Barbara, said there could be a lot of state action on climate next year because of those wins. That is where climate leadership has traditionally come from, she noted. At the federal level, Stokes is optimistic about extending clean energy tax credits since some Republicans support that.

Jay Inslee, a former Democratic governor and congressman from Washington, also expects growing momentum for clean energy in states that elected Democratic governors, particularly in states where they also won legislative majorities.

“This is a really powerful instrument for the United States to join the rest of the world and get back in a leadership position, even if we still have a climate denier that might exercise a veto on some things that we want to do federally,” Inslee said. “So I hope people will pay attention to the big progress we’re going to make on a state-level basis.“By JENNIFER McDERMOTT Associated Press

Larson clinched his third Cup Series title in six years early in the 400-mile season finale at Homestead-Miami Speedway on Sunday. He became the first back-to-back champion since Jimmie Johnson in 2010; Johnson won five in a row beginning in 2006.

The 34-year-old Larson also became the 11th driver in NASCAR history with three Cup titles, joining a list that includes seven-time champs Richard Petty, Dale Earnhardt and Johnson. Jeff Gordon has four. Lee Petty, David Pearson, Cale Yarborough.

Darrell Waltrip, Tony Stewart and Joey Logano have three.

Larson was the overwhelming favorite to hoist the Bill France Cup in Homestead long before the green flag dropped.

He entered the weekend with a 67-point lead over Christopher Bell and needed only nine points to wrap up the title. He got one point for starting the race and seven more by finishing fourth in the opening stage. But because Bell didn’t win the stage, he had no path to catch Larson the rest of the day.

Larson responded to crew chief Cliff Daniels dropping the news over the radio by saying, “That was anticlimactic.”

Larson put himself in position for his latest title with a historic postseason, dubbed NASCAR’s Chase for the championship. He ended a 51-race winless skid by winning three of the previous nine playoff events and finished in the top 10 in eight of them.

He enjoyed a similar finish while winning it all in 2021, his first season at Hendrick. He won four of the last five races â including the finale at Phoenix Raceway â while still dealing with questions about how his time ended at Chip Ganassi Racing.

MINNEAPOLIS (AP) — Once Ty Dieffenbach saw the Minnesota secondary align and react the way he expected, it was time for the UCLA quarterback to let it fly.

With two 75-yard touchdown passes from Dieffenbach to Mizell on consecutive possessions in the fourth quarter, the Bruins sped away from the Gophers and showed the Big Ten — and the College Football Playoff selection committee — yet another strength of their unbeaten breakout team in the 34-20 victory on Saturday.

Papua New Guinea’s Most Expensive Film, ‘Mussau: The First Song,’ Wraps Shoot, Unveils First Looks (EXCLUSIVE)

📅 Nov 07, 2026  |  Category: Sports  |  Read Source →

Indian filmmaker Bijukumar Damodaran directs the $4.5 million period drama, shot on remote Mussau Island and in Goroka, which is eyeing a 2027 festival premiere.

Papua New Guinea’s most expensive film to date, the $4.5 million historical drama “Mussau: The First Song,” has wrapped principal photography after a 40-day shoot across two provinces.

Filming took place on Mussau Island in New Ireland Province and in Goroka, in Eastern Highlands Province. PNG-based producer NAFA Productions celebrated the wrap at a Thanksgiving event in Port Moresby on Saturday. Government figures at the event included Belden Norman Namah, Minister for Arts, Culture and Tourism; Chief Secretary Ivan Pomaleu; and Chief Censor Jim Abani. Related Stories Taylor Swift's 'I Knew It, I Knew You' and 'La Bola Negra's 'La Nieve' Among Nominees for Hollywood Music In Media Awards

Indian director Bijukumar Damodaran, a three-time Indian National Film Award winner, is at the helm. Parul Agrawal produces, and Noelene Taula Wunum is executive producer.

Inspired by a true story and set in the 1930s, the film centers on Taula Livovoa, a young islander who walks away from tribal violence and takes up a faith that is new to him. His choice sets off a sweeping social change that the producers rank among the South Pacific’s most remarkable.

The dialogue is in six languages: Mussau, Emirau, Agarabi, Gadsup, Tok Pisin and English. Some of them are indigenous languages with small numbers of speakers that are at risk of dying out. The producers present the film as a way to protect the country’s linguistic and cultural heritage and bring it to a global audience.

Much of the cast was drawn from the local community, and many are acting on screen for the first time. Some had never watched a film before. Several roles are played by great-grandchildren of the real people whose lives inspired the story. Two British actors round out the ensemble.

Mussau Island has little infrastructure and patchy communications, which made the shoot hard to pull off. The 120-person crew, 20 of them technicians from India, spent 30 hours at sea crossing the Pacific to reach the island. Temporary lodging and facilities were set up there. Islanders helped throughout, including on sequences shot in the sea at high tide. The unit then moved on to Goroka to finish filming.

“This has been one of the most challenging and rewarding journeys of my filmmaking career,” Damodaran said. “The people of Mussau welcomed us into their lives, their culture and their history, and their contribution has been invaluable. Having descendants of the original historical figures portray some of these characters, alongside community members who had never seen a film before, made this journey even more profound.”

Hilda Tanimia wrote the story. The screenplay is by Jennifer Litau, who also served as translator, along with Joses Imona and Damodaran. The cinematographer is Yedhu Radhakrishnan and the editor is Davis Palakal Manuel. Arnold Laena handled production design and Gladice John designed the costumes. The music comes from Music Masters of PNG and the Murat Choir. The cast includes Freddy Sutchiff, Candy Ali, Daniel Lee Hird, Kevin Jackson, Jamie Napo, Calvin, Alan Balalua, Frank Eke and Dolvin Aite.

NAFA Productions wants the film to bow at a top-tier international festival in 2027. It also hopes the film will be Papua New Guinea’s official entry for best international feature at the 100th Academy Awards.

Damodaran has made that run once before. His earlier PNG–India co-production “Papa Buka” was the first film Papua New Guinea ever submitted to the Oscars’ international feature race.
