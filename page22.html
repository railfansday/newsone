SoftBank swings to $7bn loss after writing off $3.7bn of AI power gear

📅 Nov 12, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Behind the SoftBank loss: 13GW of gas turbines reserved for AI data centres, the WSJ reports. Its CFO denies Elon Musk’s default claim as borrowing costs double.

SoftBank Group swung to a net loss of ¥1.12tn ($7.1bn) in the July to September quarter. The biggest single hit was a $3.7bn writedown on power equipment for US AI data centres. SoftBank had paid for it in advance, then changed its plans.

The SoftBank loss compares with a ¥2.5tn profit a year earlier. Analysts had expected a profit of ¥129bn this time, Bloomberg reported, and it is SoftBank’s first net loss in almost two years. Revenue came in at ¥2.22tn, above analysts’ estimates, according to CNBC.

SoftBank said it had made advance payments to secure power generation equipment for US data centre projects “in anticipation of shortages”. Those payments reached $9.5bn by the end of September, and plans then changed. SoftBank could not get the fees back when it cancelled orders, chief accounting officer Kazuko Kimiwada said, as Reuters reported. SoftBank can claim rebates of up to $2.1bn on future purchases over the next ten years.

The company gave no detail on the equipment. The Wall Street Journal, citing people familiar with the orders, reported that SoftBank had reserved more than 13 gigawatts of gas turbines, mostly from GE Vernova, between late 2025 and early 2026. That is more than six Hoover Dams’ worth of capacity, the WSJ said. SoftBank then cancelled the reservations it could not use, according to the report.

GE Vernova told the WSJ the moves had “no negative or positive impact” on the company. Its shares still fell after the results, along with those of Bloom Energy, Bloomberg reported.

Elon Musk had claimed on X the week before that SoftBank defaulted on the orders. Chief financial officer Yoshimitsu Goto rejected that.

“This is simply not correct at all. There is no such default,” Goto said, according to the WSJ.

Goto said SoftBank is carrying out “a thorough reassessment” that includes how it buys equipment.

“We need to balance between gas and brake,” he said.

Some analysts see the interest bill as the bigger problem. Interest expenses for the six months to September more than doubled from a year earlier to ¥676bn, Bloomberg reported. SoftBank raised $56.5bn in the quarter, its highest ever, Reuters reported, including an $11.87bn term loan and a $10bn dollar bond.

“The more worrying factor is the increase in financial costs,” Kazuki Sawamura, a strategist at Shinkin Asset Management, told Bloomberg.

SoftBank says its finances are within its own limits. Its loan-to-value ratio was 15.9% at the end of September, below the 25% ceiling it sets for itself, Goto said.

OpenAI remains SoftBank’s largest bet. Its cumulative investment stood at $54.6bn at the end of September, with a fair value of $99.6bn, the company said. OpenAI’s latest funding round, which people familiar with it have valued at $1.25tn, is not yet in the figures. Goto said an announcement was highly likely in the current quarter.

The Vision Fund segment lost ¥81.6bn. A $1.3bn gain on ByteDance did not cover falls in listed holdings such as Coupang and Grab. Lower share prices at Arm and Intel also weighed on results.

SoftBank’s shares rose about 14% on Tuesday, before the results, then fell more than 10% on Wednesday morning in Tokyo. They have lost more than half their value since their record in June. On 9 November we wrote that investors were questioning its AI strategy after a selloff and China’s block on its ABB robotics deal.

SoftBank is still spending. The day before the results, SoftBank and Grab committed $1.85bn in seed capital for AI data centres in Malaysia, building on Son’s plans for the country. On Thursday it led Agile Robots’ $500M round.

Bloomberg Intelligence expects SoftBank to raise more money in 2027. Its annual interest bill is heading above ¥1tn.

Marvel 1943: Rise Of Hydra Isn’t Canceled, But Don’t Get Too Excited

📅 Nov 12, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Amid multiple delays and radio silence, a rumor that Marvel 1943: Rise of Hydra had been canceled began circulating this week. Developer Skydance quickly said the game is on track and nearly done—but it might not be what you expect.

According to Kotaku, executive producer Lu Gigliotti told Skydance employees that he had spoken with the studio's executive leadership team, and that the game had not been canceled. "They asked me to make sure you all hear that directly. There is no truth to these claims," Giglotti reportedly wrote in the Skydance Slack group. Kotaku added that the game is approaching its Alpha milestone and is "close to done," but after reportedly running significantly over budget, the vision for this project has been scaled back. Marvel 1943 was also originally envisioned as a Netflix game before it was announced for PC and consoles, but most of its action is apparently mapped to a single button, and it's "lacking significant interaction" during gameplay.

Essentially, the game might be structurally similar to Quantic Dream's interactive stories like Detroit: Become Human, rather than a meaty brawler like Insomniac's Spider-Man games. "We're continuing development on it, but Amy and the team have big ambitions for the level of quality that they want to hit. They are a relatively small team considering the AAA quality that they're delivering,” senior executive Shawn Kittelsen said earlier this year about the game's lengthy development. "What you've seen in previous footage is what the game looks like, and we're making it with a fraction of the resources that you see on other AAA games of the same type, and really trying to push a new development model that's a lot more responsible and sustainable."

First announced in 2022 and fully revealed in 2024, Marvel 1943: Rise of Hydra drew attention for its good-looking graphics and a storyline featuring multiple characters, including Captain America and Black Panther. The new report comes in the wake of layoffs at Skydance's parent group, Paramount Games Studio, which is also changing following Paramount's acquisition of Warner Bros. While live-service Marvel games like Marvel Rivals and Marvel Snap continue to draw in players, the same can't be said for the big-budget Wolverine game that was released earlier this year. Despite a strong start in September, the game quickly lost momentum and has sold 2.4 million units so far.

Carrick sacked by Man Utd with Pochettino appointment expected

📅 Nov 12, 2026  |  Category: Sports  |  Read Source →

Syndicated reporting provided by Stats Perform and reviewed by the SoccerNews editorial team.

Michael Carrick has been sacked as Manchester United's head coach, with Mauricio Pochettino expected to replace him.

Rumours had been rife that Carrick may be replaced during the international break, after a dreadful start to the season left United 18th in the Premier League table.

Despite fighting back from 2-0 and 4-2 down to draw 4-4 with Aston Villa on Sunday – the first time any team had recovered from two goals behind to equalise on two occasions in a single Premier League match – United are guaranteed to spend at least 15 days in the relegation zone.

It will be their longest run of successive days inside the drop zone since September and October 1986, when they endured a spell of 26 days in the First Division's danger zone.

That was also the last time United were in a worse position in the table at least 10 games into a season, having sat 20th on that occasion.

Back then, Ron Atkinson was dismissed in November, with Alex Ferguson taking the reins and spending 27 glittering years at the club.

Antonio Conte, Julian Nagelsmann and Eddie Howe had all been touted as possible replacements this time around, with the Red Devils seeking their third permanent boss since Jim Ratcliffe and INEOS took control of the club's football operations in February 2024.

However, United States boss Pochettino is widely expected to be announced as Carrick's successor.

A club statement read: "After a disappointing start to the season, the club has come to the extremely difficult conclusion that a change is necessary in order to achieve our ambitions.

"Michael is, and always will be, a Manchester United legend. Just as he did as a player, he has conducted himself with the highest standards of dedication and professionalism throughout his time back with the club. His leadership and achievements last season will always be fondly remembered.

"The club intends to appoint a permanent successor as quickly as possible."

Carrick initially had a hugely positive impact upon replacing Ruben Amorim on an interim basis in January.

He led United to a third-place finish in the Premier League last season, winning 12 of his 17 games at the helm in 2025-26 (70%, D3 L2). The Red Devils also averaged 1.94 goals per game in that span, while conceding 1.05.

Having made Carrick their permanent boss on a two-year contract this summer, United have won three of 15 games in all competitions in 2026-27 (20%, D5 L5), averaging 1.6 goals for and exactly 2.0 goals against per match.

Their 30 goals conceded across all competitions are the joint-second most among all teams in Europe's top five leagues, with only Ligue 1 side Lens shipping more (31, also 30 conceded by Crystal Palace, Sassuolo and Getafe).

United have failed to keep a clean sheet in 10 Premier League games this term, with their only victory coming by a 5-2 scoreline against newly promoted Ipswich Town in August, failing to win any of their subsequent eight matches (D5 L3).

It is the first time they have started a campaign without a shutout in their first 10 matches since that 1986-97 season, while they are the first top-flight team to start a season with such a run, after finishing in the top three the previous campaign, since Liverpool in 1966-67.

Old Trafford was the scene of protests against the way INEOS and the Glazer family have run the club on Sunday, with United's players also attracting fierce criticism from their own supporters throughout the campaign.

Following a 3-0 defeat at home to Bournemouth last month, large sections of the Red Devils' fanbase applauded Carrick down the tunnel, before booing his players off.

Kane ranks 126th cap among his biggest England moments

📅 Nov 12, 2026  |  Category: Sports  |  Read Source →

Syndicated reporting provided by Stats Perform and reviewed by the SoccerNews editorial team.

Harry Kane says winning his 126th cap and becoming England's all-time leading appearance-maker will rank among the greatest moments of his career.

England face Croatia in their penultimate group-stage match in the Nations League on Thursday, with head coach Thomas Tuchel already confirming Kane will start the game.

That will take Kane to 126 senior appearances for the Three Lions, surpassing Peter Shilton (125) outright to become the most-capped player in the team's history.

With 91 goals, Kane is already the most prolific player in England's history by some margin, having passed Wayne Rooney's previous record of 53 goals over three years ago.

Since making his England debut in 2015, Kane has put together a case to be the greatest player to ever represent the Three Lions, helping them reach two European Championship finals and two World Cup semi-finals, one of which led to a bronze medal this year.

Ahead of his landmark outing, Kane will also have the honour of presenting the Ballon d'Or trophy to England's fans, having become his country's first winner of the award since Michael Owen in 2001.

Asked where the match will rank among his best England moments, Kane said: "It's definitely up there as the biggest. I think it showcases consistency at the highest level.

"It's testament to all the dedication, the time people don't see, that keeps me fit and healthy.

"This is one of the biggest milestones in my career. In terms of what's next, I don't like to set limits – 100 goals is a milestone to reach as soon as possible.

"We've done extremely well in tournaments, getting to the latter stages. I'm feeling really good and, hopefully, there will be many more years to see what I can achieve."

“It’s one of the biggest milestones of my career.”  The skipper himself on his record-breaking appearance for the #ThreeLions  https://t.co/FiEwBouest

Asked to name some of his other favourite moments in an England shirt, two matches from this year's World Cup immediately sprung to mind for Kane – a 2-1 comeback win over DR Congo in which he scored both goals, and a nail-biting 3-2 triumph over Mexico at the Estadio Azteca in the following round, when his penalty proved decisive.

He said: "The England debut is always a big one. That dream coming true, scoring at Wembley on my debut… coming on for Wayne Rooney will always stick in my mind.

"From the recent World Cup, the DR Congo game was just an incredible experience, to help the team get through a knockout game like that, to score the two goals. The second goal was one of my best finishes and just the feeling of adrenaline afterwards was as good as it gets.

"I've been lucky enough to have some great moments but the major tournament moments, like the Mexico game, they're the ones that really stick in the memory."

And Kane is very clear on what he still wants to accomplish at international level, as he aims to lead England to their first major trophy since the 1966 World Cup.

"To win a team trophy for England, a major trophy, is my biggest dream," Kane said. "A World Cup or the Euros, or both, hopefully, but I know how difficult that is.

"Individual accolades are incredible, but ultimately they're a consequence of trying to make the team better.

"With Bayern Munich, winning the Champions League [is the aim]. There are many trophies I haven't won and that's what motivates me. I'm surrounded by great teams at my club and country, so, hopefully, it's an exciting time ahead."

European tech buyers add digital sovereignty to cyber shopping list

📅 Nov 12, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Sales from EU HQ'd vendor rocket in two years. The Trump affect? Yes but NIS2 and DORA are factors too

European tech buyers are increasingly choosing cybersecurity vendors from their own continent in a bid to reduce their reliance on American technology.

Digital sovereignty isn't only prompting enterprise customers to question their clouds of choice, it is also reshaping other parts of the industry too, and the shift is marked.

According to sales figures compiled by Context, distributor sales in EMEA of cybersecurity products from EU-headquartered vendors jumped 35 percent in the two years to calendar Q3 2026. Total cybersecurity sales grew up 10 percent over the same period, and sales by US-run companies went up just 8 percent.

That growth is from a relatively small base, though EU vendors gained nearly three percentage points to reach 15 percent of tracked sales by value. There is still a long way to go as US-headquartered vendors accounted for 62 percent.

The trend coincides with a loss of trust in the Trump administration among customers outside of America, after it cosied up to autocratic rulers while criticizing and punishing allies with tariffs and more.

Context stopped short of assigning blame. It said organizations are reviewing their reliance on third-party tech in light of EU compliance  regimes: the NIS2 Directive and Digital Operational Resilience Act (DORA).

NIS2, transposed into national law in October 2024, requires organizations in the bloc to raise their common level of security in the face of digital threats and risks to critical infrastructure. DORA, introduced in January last year, is designed to strengthen cyber security in the financial sector.

"Digital sovereignty is becoming a buying decision. As customers and resellers get clearer about what it means in practice, they're asking who holds the encryption keys, who can access sensitive logs and where that data is stored," said Context veep of research Joe Turner.

"We're seeing a measurable shift in the EMEA channel. EU vendors gained nearly three points of sales share in two years. An organization can keep its existing identity platform and still choose a European supplier to manage its security logs. That's the kind of decision that gives European vendors an opening."

The stats indicate that EU-HQd vendors comprised 92 percent of tracked sales in security awareness training, 82 percent in vulnerability management, and 52 percent for SIEM and log management. US-headquartered companies held into 74 percent of identity and access management sales and 54 percent of cloud security sales.

The Reg has asked Context for UK-specific figures to see whether British customers are also voting with their wallets for European alternatives. That certainly isn’t the case for tech purchases in the British public sector, despite Prime Minister Andy Burnham’s professed desire to buy British.

Data released yesterday by the Institute for Public Policy Research showed just £1 in every £6 spent by government with the biggest technology suppliers goes to Brit businesses.

Still, Burnham only came to power in July so there is time yet for him to put his money where his mouth is.

Sovereign AI is another area of concern for enterprise customers. Analyst Omdia said last month that despite the marketing adjective meaning "local assets," almost a third AI providers disclose no proof of sovereignty, and some "expect enterprise to pay for audits to verify their claims."

Omdia also found that 80 percent of contracts say network resilience is "out of providers control or responsibility," and "US-headquartered AI providers offer better service transparency and contract terms than a majority of local sovereign AI providers, with providers in India a notable exception."

Sovereign services in Omdia's classification include data residency, operational autonomy, jurisdictional sovereignty, and technological autonomy.

"Local hosting is not sovereignty. Serious providers must demonstrate their credibility through transparent operations, fair contracts and proof of competence. That includes the network, because data in transit must be sovereign too," said Camille Mendler, research director for telco B2B at Omdia.

The Argentine has come close to becoming Manchester United manager on three separate prior occasions. Now the stars appear to have finally aligned, but is it too late for either the man or the club?

Want to bookmark your favourite articles and stories to read or reference later? Start your Independent Membership today.

On Sunday evening, Mauricio Pochettino got the call he had been waiting for.

He and long-time assistant Jesus Perez had frequently asked about Manchester United over the last decade. They would often contact friends in the game who were connected to Old Trafford, looking to find out what was happening, whether there could be an opening.

Pochettino came very close to taking one of those openings on at least three occasions: 2018, 2021 and 2024. Now, elements might finally have aligned.

It is a call that others at Old Trafford have long waited for, too. Back when they had a much publicised dinner in 2016, Sir Alex Ferguson essentially told Pochettino he should be the United manager. The Argentine was enthralled at that meeting by stories from one of the game’s great glory eras, and a sense of magic he had felt just standing in Old Trafford had been deepened.

Pochettino had been especially taken by Ferguson’s account of rebuilding United from the 1980s, initially drawing comparisons with what he was doing at Tottenham Hotspur. Now, he’s set to take on that exact same job, if one that has become so much bigger than in 2016. It is arguably even bigger than in 1986.

Pochettino, however, is fired by the idea of being the man to finally fix United. It has become one of the great managerial quests. The United hierarchy have ultimately gone for him because of that personality, and his standing. They don’t want a coach who hasn’t worked at this level. They want someone ready. Both the Glazers and Sir Jim Ratcliffe can sense that, since the two sides of the United ownership have known Pochettino for years.

If this is an appointment that always felt destined, though, that does raise one big question. Is this the right moment for it? Is it like Jose Mourinho, in that it came too late? Is it typical of modern United in that sense? Is it the same old “magical thinking” of hoping a famous name saves them, when the game has changed and the actual problems are now so structural?

Pochettino probably should have gone to United in 2016 or 2018, when he was at a peak in terms of being one of the most exciting coaches in football. The club did consider a move in the summer of 2016, but sources say an exit clause was allowed to expire. How different things could have been.

Old Trafford insiders are now talking about the value of “a name”, but the hierarchy did pass on Pochettino in 2024, when they initially considered sacking Erik ten Hag before ultimately deciding to stick with the Dutchman. His interview wasn’t seen as convincing as that of Thomas Tuchel or Roberto De Zerbi. Those who know him also say he is closer to being “a manager” now, in terms of a wider outlook, rather than the on-the-grass coach that so drove Spurs.

The very fact United are negotiating to pry him from the United States national team, without wishing to be disrespectful, also illustrates how his stock has recently dropped. Some close to Pochettino would nevertheless put that down to necessary choices at certain moments. Taking charge of the hosts of the biggest World Cup ever, that had so much pomp around it, was too enticing. Before that, he probably should have pushed to go to United or Real Madrid in 2018 but he was convinced he could do something special with Spurs. He’d put too much into it.

And that does point to one of the other great twists of fate in modern football, where Pochettino’s profile could be completely different. His Tottenham team were genuinely one of the best in Europe over 2016-18, and one of the great Premier League teams. To achieve 86 points in 2016-17, on what was then only the sixth highest wage bill in English football, was genuine alchemy. It was immense overperformance, fostered by a side that could just overrun you.

We also now know that the wage bill of the eventual champions for that season, Chelsea, had in part come from signings that had been secured by payments against the rules. The punishment from that guilty verdict still looks weak. There is a strong argument that Chelsea should have been stripped of the 2015 and 2017 titles. Pochettino himself said as much in a wide-ranging interview with The Independent and other media in September: “I’ve said that, that we should win one title, one Premier League. We should have been awarded it… for sure, we deserve to be champions for that year.”

Had that justice been done, Pochettino would not just be a “name” who created an exciting Spurs once. He would be an English champion, and one responsible for one of the most historic titles given the much wealthier competition. A great institution would have won just their third title, and first in 56 years.

Both he and Spurs could have had their futures transformed, as well as their place in history. It would have been a modern equivalent of what Ferguson himself did with Aberdeen.

Now, he wants to follow Ferguson in a different way. This is the job he has been waiting for.

A certain apathy towards the US job was displayed in that September interview when he spoke about how his contract didn’t necessarily mean he’d stay the full term.

For all of that, and how their World Cup campaign ended in the chaos of Donald Trump’s disruptive interference in the Falorin Balogun case, there were very impressive spells when the US really looked like a classic Pochettino team. The way they ripped into Paraguay was reminiscent of Spurs 2016-18, right up to some of the flourishes. And that was in a much more limited and restricted environment than a club team.

This is the other thing about just taking the job, too. Regardless of United’s current problems, and perceptions of where Pochettino is in his career, he is energised by this. He has that bite. As one figure who knows him well says, “he has got the IP, he has got the brand, he has got the experience”.

Having already worked for Chelsea and Paris Saint-Germain, he’s been at big clubs with demanding owners. He has the mettle for that. And he now looks set to have the big job he’s always wanted.
