Tidal announces ban on AI-generated music; 27 million tracks will be deleted

📅 Nov 24, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

AI songs account for 6.3 percent of Tidal’s catalog, but it’s about to be zero.

Most streaming music providers have opted to label AI-generated content on their platforms, and until now, Tidal was one of them. However, the company is about to take a harder stance. Tidal announced that in the coming weeks, it will fully ban AI music because of the harm it does to human artists.

Tidal debuted its initial AI policy in July 2026, which included labeling and demonetizing all songs created entirely with AI. That wasn’t enough to stem the tide of AI slop, though. Over the past 90 days, Tidal reports that 43 percent of new uploads were completely AI-generated. On November 6, new uploads to Tidal hit a whopping 60 percent AI-generated.

As things stand, Tidal hosts 27 million tracks labeled as AI, all of which were obviously added in the past couple of years. That accounts for 6.3 percent of Tidal’s total catalog. On December 9, Tidal will delete all those songs. The streamer notes this will have essentially no negative impact because 96 percent of AI-labeled tracks got zero plays in the past 90 days. Throughout its announcement, Tidal is careful to stipulate that it’s only talking about music that has been fully created by AI. Artists can still use AI tools as long as the final product contains human elements.

Credit: Tidal Credit: Tidal

Some people don’t particularly mind AI music, but the team at Tidal has come to believe the harm to artists is too great. AI models can pump out random noises faster than humans can create things of real value, which can mislead listeners and siphon royalty payments away from creators. There’s also an issue with AI uploaders actively attempting to steal royalty payments. Tidal says it has already removed about 66,000 tracks from its service for impersonating artists, which was against its AI policy even before this update.

As of December 9, individual uploaders and distributors will no longer be allowed to add AI music to Tidal. However, they could lie, couldn’t they?

Tidal hasn’t specified how it detects AI music, but it will probably rely on watermarking. Almost every AI song on Tidal (94 percent) comes from Suno, which added watermarking earlier this year. Tidal also cites Google Lyria as a source of AI tunes, which has SynthID labels embedded in the waveform. Deezer claimed it has a licensable AI music detector with a false-positive rate of less than 0.01 percent, but it’s unclear if Tidal is using that or something similar.

That will take care of most AI music, but this approach won’t detect everything. There are open models for music generation that you can run locally on a GPU, and some cloud-based providers aren’t bothering to watermark their outputs. Even if Tidal is using a separate detection system, new models may be able to evade it—it’s always going to be a cat-and-mouse game. This will be a problem with verifying provenance in all types of media in the future. For the time being, at least, Tidal’s policy should clear out most of the AI slop.

The people directing robots to make these songs will have plenty of other options after Tidal blocks AI. Spotify, YouTube Music, Deezer, and Apple Music all allow AI music on their platforms with labeling. Spotify has removed millions of AI tracks from its collection based on user feedback. Other streamers haven’t specified how much slop (if any) they’ve cleared out.

PSG’s Champions League ‘obsession’ cost them league title under Tuchel, says Florenzi

📅 Nov 24, 2026  |  Category: Sports  |  Read Source →

Syndicated reporting provided by Stats Perform and reviewed by the SoccerNews editorial team.

Former Paris Saint-Germain defender Alessandro Florenzi believes their "obsession" with winning the Champions League cost them the Ligue 1 title under Thomas Tuchel.

Florenzi spent the 2020-21 season on loan at PSG, who lost in the Champions League final to Bayern Munich in the previous campaign.

Tuchel won 74.8% of his games in charge of PSG (G127 W95 D13 L19), his best win percentage at any club, and led them to back-to-back titles in 2018-19 and 2019-20, but things unravelled the following season.

PSG sacked Tuchel in December 2020 after they were falling behind in the Ligue 1 title race to Lille, who would go on to win the league by one point.

It was only the second season since 2012-13 in which PSG failed to win the league title, after Monaco won the French top-flight in 2016-17.

However, Tuchel was hired by Chelsea a month after being sacked by PSG and went on to win the Champions League with the Blues in that same season.

Florenzi described Tuchel's Champions League win as "karma", but the Italian was not surprised to see PSG eventually win the competition.

"At the time, PSG hadn't won the Champions League yet, but you could see the president wanted to build something big," Florenzi told L'Equipe.

Palace docked three points for substitution rule breach in Everton victory

📅 Nov 24, 2026  |  Category: Sports  |  Read Source →

Syndicated reporting provided by Stats Perform and reviewed by the SoccerNews editorial team.

Crystal Palace have been deducted three points for breaching substitution rules during their 1-0 win over Everton on the opening day of the Women's Super League.

Lexi Lloyd-Smith scored the only goal at The 360 Engage Stadium in September, but the Eagles have since had those points removed after using too many substitution windows.

The introduction of Kirsty Howat in the 97th minute was Palace's fourth separate change, with other substitutions taking place in the 62nd, 83rd and 90th minutes.

While WSL managers are allowed to make five changes in a game, they must be made across only three stoppages in play to avoid excessive time-wasting.

An independent tribunal determined that the breach was "inadvertent" and that it was "not intended to secure an unfair advantage," but added that a sporting sanction was "necessary."

The deduction means Palace drop to 13th in the WSL and behind Birmingham City on goal difference, after the Eagles were thumped 5-1 by London City Lionesses on Sunday.

The Everton match was one of only two WSL games Palace had won this season, and the Eagles said they were "disappointed" by the outcome.

"Everyone at the club is obviously frustrated that such a minor infraction, given that the player was on the pitch for only 46 seconds while the ball was in play, has led to such a significant penalty – a penalty which we do not agree is reasonable or proportionate," Palace's statement read.

"We are disappointed for the players and staff who worked so hard to secure a deserved victory on our return to the WSL."

Frank Harper, who is a former Millwall hooligan and later made a name for himself as an actor, has named the five toughest fanbases in English football.

He is undoubtedly best known for playing Billy Bright, a member of the 'Chelsea Headhunters' firm in the classic 2004 hooligan film 'The Football Factory' alongside Danny Dyer. But he also played roles in 'The Rise of the Footsoldier' and 'Lock, Stock and Two Smoking Barrels'.

Harper, now 63, didn't need to do much transforming to become any of his film characters as he was a former hooligan himself with Millwall in the 70s, 80s and 90s.

In the book called Terrace Legends, via Daily Star, the Londoner picked out his top five football firms, having included two fanbases within the capital city.

While Liverpool and Manchester United have the biggest fanbases in English football thanks to their dominance in the Premier League - with the latter being crowned the toughest fanbase by another former hooligan - Harper snubbed both on his list.

In fact, there was only one northern club he ended up choosing, having named Leeds United among his five, later recalling a particular time he was involved with the Yorkshire club. He said:

"West Ham, Chelsea, Pompey (Portsmouth), Leeds and Cardiff City."

Now in his forties, Daniel Wright has completely turned his life around since winning the lottery in 2022.

He added: "Leeds at home in the league. I was in a pub and suddenly it emptied. The ones that couldn't get out the door were climbing out the toilet windows to get out on to the street.

"Thousands of Millwall came out of nowhere as bottles and glasses rained down on the Leeds lot. The Old Bill pulled a transit van across the pub doorway to try to stem the flow of Millwall fans. That day, Leeds were dead lucky to reach the ground."

Besides the five fanbases he listed, after having also named Grimsby Town - which ranks among the ugliest stadiums in British football - as the worst away ground he has ever been to, Harper was involved in another scary experience at a Millwall home game, when a pocket of Ipswich fans infiltrated the stands and after plenty of pushing, he was close to smacking his head on a metal barrier.

The number of regions where patients have limited choices for Affordable Care Act plans is on the rise, according to a new analysis.

The Robert Wood Johnson Foundation report found that for the 2027 plan year, there are more than 200 counties in which there is only one insurer available, and close to 1,000 where there are just two plans available for consumers to choose from.

This makes for an increase of 50% from the 2026 plan year, with more than a third of counties having limited options on the ACA's marketplaces for 2027.

Most states (39) will have at least one county where plan choices are limited for enrollees. For about half of these states, this is the norm and aligns with 2026, but for the rest, this marks a significant shift, the RWJF researchers wrote in the report.

"For the individual market as a whole, the decline in participation is sobering but not a fatal blow," per the report. "The presence of federal and some state subsidies provides an important backstop that will likely prevent a complete meltdown."

"Yet the prospect of a growing number of counties with little insurer participation is grim, and likely will not bode well for premium increases, morbidity or enrollment going forward," according to the report.

The foundation cites a recent analysis from Wakely that found the individual market declined by 16% in the first six months of 2026. The end of the enhanced premium tax credits, cost pressures and growing administrative burdens are likely to contribute to worsening trends in this space.

For instance, ACA marketplace exits from Cigna, CareSource and Medica are having a significant impact in Iowa and Indiana for the coming plan year, the researchers found. In the latter case, most Indiana counties have two or fewer plan choices.

However, there are some bright spots, RWJF researchers wrote. There are certain insurers participating in the exchanges who have beefed up their footprints, and there is a smattering of new entrants. Oscar Health, for example, is planning the largest growth, entering a new state and adding new counties in some of the states it already serves.

Some insurers, like Centene and UnitedHealthcare, are making strategic exits in some markets while entering others, for a neutral impact on their footprints overall.

Insurers like Antidote Health and Peak Health are also entering new regions—even if on a limited basis in some cases—which can help staunch the bleeding.

Blue Cross Blue Shield plans also often play a key role in limited markets, as in close to 80% of counties with a single insurer, that payer is a Blues plan. In addition, in 12% of counties, the single insurer is Elevance Health, which operates plans as Anthem Blue Cross Blue Shield.

"This suggests that were it not for the somewhat unique role of Blues plans as a backstop, the threat of a bare county might be higher," per the report. "Single-state Blues, in particular, are under social and political pressure to participate in much if not all of their state, and do not feel as free to exit unprofitable counties as are publicly traded insurers."

U.S. is preparing to raise tariffs on China, the E.U., and 14 other nations

📅 Nov 24, 2026  |  Category: Other  |  Read Source →

The U.S. Trade Representative is expected to unveil results of its industrial subsidies investigation as soon as Tuesday

Allen J. Schaben / Los Angeles Times via Getty Images

The U.S. is getting ready to hike duties on imports from China, the European Union and 14 other economies in response to their industrial subsidies, according to The Wall Street Journal.

U.S. Trade Representative Jamieson Greer could announce the results of his office's investigation as soon as Tuesday, according to Bloomberg. The timing could change, according to The Wall Street Journal. Tariff rates and an effective date are unclear, according to Bloomberg.

The probe falls under Section 301 of the 1974 Trade Act, a provision that lets a president act alone in levying tariffs in response to unfair trade conduct, according to Bloomberg. The Office of the U.S. Trade Representative opened the investigation in March, following the high court's invalidation of President Donald Trump's earlier worldwide tariffs.

Greer has said that state backing, subsidies among it, has left the targeted economies with more industrial production capacity than they need. He has said that this lets companies in those economies undercut competitors abroad on price, which hurts American manufacturers. The U.S. Trade Representative's office declined to comment, according to The Wall Street Journal and Bloomberg. The White House did not respond to requests for comment from The Wall Street Journal or Bloomberg.

The new tariffs are expected to apply to the bulk of goods coming from the targeted nations, among them China, Japan, South Korea and the E.U.'s 27 member states, according to The Wall Street Journal. Steel, aluminum and automobiles are expected to be left out because they are already subject to duties under another law tied to national security.

Administration officials have said they plan to set rates close to the ones Trump put in place during 2025, ahead of the Supreme Court's invalidation of his initial round of tariffs in February 2026, according to The Wall Street Journal. Countries with trade agreements, including Japan, the E.U. and South Korea, are expected to face rates of 15% or lower. Vietnam and India could be hit with steeper rates that reflect the agreements they struck with Trump.

China is expected to be charged a steeper tariff than many of its peers because Trump officials have described its subsidies as especially far-reaching, according to The Wall Street Journal. The administration could add wording that would let it increase tariffs if a country's overcapacity in industry grows worse or if the country fails to change its policies during later talks.

The new duties are expected to stack on top of tariffs announced in July over forced labor, according to The Wall Street Journal. Those tariffs, which run from 10% to 12.5% on about 60 economies, are being contested in a lawsuit before the Court of International Trade, according to Bloomberg.

Anthropic Brings Server-Supplied Skills and Background Tasks to Claude

📅 Nov 24, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Anthropic announced on November 24, 2026 that Skills over MCP is in public beta in Claude and Claude Code, while Tasks and Elicitation are generally available and File Uploads will roll out soon. The additions let connected servers supply workflow guidance, run lengthy jobs, request user input and, once File Uploads arrives, exchange files with Claude.

The features are built around the open Model Context Protocol (MCP) and its extension system, allowing supporting servers to work in compatible clients beyond Claude. Anthropic reports more than 700 million monthly MCP SDK downloads and over 10,000 MCP connectors for Claude, describing the ecosystem it is asking to implement the new server capabilities.

Tool names and descriptions tell Claude what a server can do; skills add guidance on how to handle particular use cases. A server can publish best practices, step-by-step workflows or playbooks tailored to a user’s account or organization alongside its tools. Claude retrieves a skill only when needed, and updates arrive with the server, so users do not install anything separately.

The Skills extension documentation defines a skill as a directory containing a SKILL.md file and optional supporting files, with discovery and retrieval handled through MCP’s existing Resources primitive. Hosts may not fetch files ahead of need. Before use, they must verify each file’s byte size and SHA-256 digest and compare the instruction file’s frontmatter with the approved manifest; changed files require fresh approval. SDK and host support across clients is still being implemented.

The beta is on by default for Pro, Max and Ultra plans, available to Team plans after an administrator enables it, and listed as coming soon to Enterprise. Anthropic’s examples include Shopify for designing and updating online stores, HubSpot for customer onboarding and Atlassian for organization-specific analysis. A forthcoming Carta skill is intended to draft and issue equity from an uploaded document.

Tasks and Elicitation are available to Pro, Max and Ultra plans in Claude and Claude Code and are rolling out to Team and Enterprise organizations. Tasks lets a server run a time-consuming operation or a full agent loop in the background while Claude continues other work and retrieves the result later. ZoomInfo uses it for deeper account and contact research that previously required several prompts.

The Tasks documentation says the server returns a durable task handle instead of blocking the connection. Clients can poll for progress and resume with the same task ID after a disconnect or restart. States cover working, input required, completion, failure and cancellation, and a task can request input before proceeding. Cancellation is cooperative, so the server acknowledges the request without being obligated to stop the underlying work.

Elicitation lets a server pause for user input through a form or link before continuing. Supabase uses it to confirm paid-project costs and destructive SQL, Railway to ask before potentially destructive changes, and GitHub to request a repository name before deletion. The Elicitation specification separates structured form input from URL-based interactions and bars servers from using forms to request passwords, API keys, access tokens or payment credentials. Clients must identify the requesting server and provide decline and cancel options.

Anthropic plans File Uploads for Claude and Claude Code on Pro, Max, Ultra and eligible Team and Enterprise plans. It will allow PDFs, spreadsheets and images to pass to MCP servers without going through the model’s context window.

The File Uploads repository makes its earlier-stage status explicit: the extension is experimental, is not part of the MCP specification and has not been proposed for inclusion, so breaking changes are expected. Its design keeps file bytes out of model context and JSON-RPC messages. A server marks file arguments, the client uploads missing bytes and retries, and a tool can return a download target in result metadata.

Anthropic also reports that on-demand tool search can reduce the context used by tool definitions by more than 85% in multi-server setups. That efficiency focus connects the separate additions: skills load only when needed, tasks avoid holding a connection open, and file transfers are designed to keep bytes out of model context. The result is a broader coordination layer for agent work, whose reach depends on servers and clients implementing the same extensions and on the file-transfer design stabilizing.

At $2,395 for the king size I tested, the Perfectly Snug Smart Topper is absolutely a luxury purchase. After sleeping on it for 2 months during a Florida summer, however, I can say that its temperature-control technology delivers. This 2-inch topper uses fans, airflow, heating elements, and temperature sensors to regulate each side of the bed independently, creating a surprisingly effective way to stay comfortable throughout the night. While some aspects of the design could use a little more refinement, the actual cooling performance left me impressed.

Installation was straightforward. I unpacked the vacuum-sealed topper, laid it over my king mattress, added my usual fitted sheet, and plugged it in. The power supply is fairly big, but I had no trouble tucking it down behind my headboard. The cables emerge from the head of the topper, which is worth keeping in mind if you tend to stretch your arms overhead while sleeping. As for comfort, the topper has an unusual feel at first—slightly firm but with enough give to stay comfortable. Its internal mesh structure allows air to circulate beneath you, and the effect is immediately noticeable, even on lower settings.

The cooling performance is easily my favorite feature. Even at level 3, I could feel a gentle, pleasant stream of cool air underneath me, which was especially welcome during warm Florida nights. The one trade-off is fan noise, which becomes quite noticeable at higher settings. Anything above level 5 was too loud for my sleeping preferences, but I found an easy workaround using the app’s scheduling feature. I’d run the cooling at a higher setting for about an hour before bedtime, then lower it to level 3 when I climbed into bed. This gave me the best of both worlds: a wonderfully cool mattress at bedtime and gentle, nearly silent cooling throughout the night.

There are a few areas where I’d love to see Perfectly Snug make improvements, especially considering the premium price. The power supply and small side-mounted control panel are functional but have a fairly basic appearance. I also occasionally brushed against the fans positioned at the foot of the topper, which could briefly interrupt their operation (making a noticeable “you’ve just stopped a fan from moving” type of buzzing sound). The companion app was another underwhelming aspect of the experience. While it offers useful features like scheduling and temperature adjustments, its interface feels super dated and could benefit from a much more polished, intuitive design. None of these issues prevented me from enjoying the topper, but they’re details I’d expect to see addressed at this price point.

Ultimately, the Perfectly Snug Smart Topper gets the most important thing right: keeping your bed at a comfortable temperature. I enjoyed slipping into a perfectly cooled bed each night, and the subtle airflow on lower settings noticeably improved my sleep comfort. It’s an innovative product with plenty to offer, particularly for hot sleepers or couples with different temperature preferences. Although the fan noise and a few less-refined design elements keep it from feeling like a truly premium experience, the core technology is admirable.
