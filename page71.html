A New Fallout Game Leaks, But Dogmeat Won’t Like It

📅 Nov 19, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Daniel is a lifelong gamer with a love for various genres and gameplay styles who enjoys exploring games and playing from various angles.

Here’s a fun fact: in the lore of the Fallout series, dogs weren’t allowed into the Vault-Tec Vaults, with all the dogs we see throughout the games being those that survived and evolved into wolf-like forms after the Great War. Cats, on the other hand, were permitted into the Vaults within reason thanks to their applications as therapy pets and efficacy as pest-hunters. In spite of that, cats didn’t start appearing in the Fallout games until Fallout 4 and Fallout 76. Apparently, the reason for that is that they all just sorta got lost somewhere.

A newly-discovered document from the Australian Classification Board has leaked the existence of a new Fallout spin-off game titled A Wasteland Full of Cats. This game is apparently being created in cooperation between Bethesda and Devcats, an indie developer specializing in hidden object games about cats like A Park Full of Cats, A Building Full of Cats, and An Arcade Full of Cats.

Is it the Fallout game we’ve all been waiting for in the near-decade since the launch of Fallout 76? No, definitely not, but, well, it’s something, I suppose. Every bit of Fallout-adjacent content we get can continue to tide us over until Fallout 5, whenever that ends up being, or at least the next season of the Fallout television series.

Tim Cain, the creator of Fallout, initially proposed a much happier ending, but it was ultimately scrapped.

According to the description of the game on the Australian Classification Board website, A Wasteland Full of Cats will cast you in the role of one of the various cats living in one of the Vaults that dot the Wasteland. Following some manner of accident, all of the other cats living in the Vault alongside you are let loose out into the Wasteland, and you’ll need to go out there to round them all up.

In the same vein as Devcats’s other games, this will be a jaunt through 14 locations and 33 rooms where you’ll need to point out the errant cats hidden within the scenery, Where’s Waldo-style.

A Wasteland Full of Cats will cast you in the role of one of the various cats living in one of the Vaults.

You’ll be helped along the way by various NPC characters, hopefully including some familiar faces, with many of the locations you visit apparently being classic spots from across the Fallout franchise. Some of these locations will have mild puzzle elements, allowing you to uncover non-critical secret levels. According to the description, the game will culminate in a final battle against a horde of Radroaches, though I’m admittedly not quite sure how finding cats translates into battling giant mutant roaches, but I guess we’ll find out.

It has been an exceptionally hot minute since the last mainline Fallout game, specifically the release of Fallout 4 in 2015. Since then, it’s been pretty much exclusively spin-offs, such as the mobile title Fallout Shelter, its gacha-added version, Fallout Shelter Online, the MMO stylings of Fallout 76, and I guess Zen Pinball’s Fallout table for the Pinball FX games, if you want to count that.

Back in 2022, Todd Howard said in an interview with IGN that development on Fallout 5 would begin in earnest once The Elder Scrolls VI was done and dusted. Of course, considering we only just learned that game’s full title recently, I have a sinking feeling the timeline has shifted on that a bit. For what it’s worth, Bethesda did say in an X, formerly Twitter post this past July that Fallout 5 has officially begun pre-production, but again, that doesn’t give us much to go on.

As of now, we don’t have any particular release dates or even release windows for upcoming Fallout projects, whether it’s mainline games, the TV show, or even this new cat thing. Just gotta keep wading through the Wasteland patiently, I guess…

📅 Nov 19, 2026  |  Category: Business  |  Read Source →

The release of Grand Theft Auto 6 is finally here, bringing with it an avalanche of rave reviews and jokes about creative excuses to miss work. Unfortunately, for many Ubisoft workers, the drive home today will also be their last one.

According to Jon Hicks of GamesIndustry.biz, French gaming giant Ubisoft has dropped the guillotine on 166 roles in Canada, concentrated in the company’s Québec and Montreal studios. Ubisoft attributes the move to matching its resources with “current production priorities and business needs.”

Ubisoft says workers affected “are being provided with severance, extended health care benefits, career transition services and other resources” to help smooth out the ride.

Since the announcing of a joint venture between Ubisoft and Tencent, and a major restructuring plan that comes with it, the company has gone through a turbulent period to try and align its workforce with the newly-set goals. As has become the norm, this has come at the expense of workers.

Ubisoft moves all-in with new bosses, a new operating model, and a billion-dollar investment.

The terminated roles appear to represent approximately 4% of the affected studios’ workforce, primarily in support and production positions, a testament to just how massive Ubisoft’s Canadian operation has gotten over the years. Earlier this year, Ubisoft closed down its Halifax studio in Nova Scotia.

Ubisoft has not commented on specific projects affected, but Jon Hicks claims some of the workers laid off were part of Vantage Studios. Previously known as Creative House 1, Vantage Studios focuses on the company’s big money titles, namely the Assassin’s Creed, Rainbow Six, and Far Cry franchises.

Layoffs have left a dark stain in the gaming industry for a while now, especially as companies have begun overcorrecting for the post-lockdown hiring spree.

According to Ubisoft, the company is finally seeing the light as it closes in on the targeted €200 million in savings (approximately $220 million as of today). Unfortunately, just as the French company is about to leave the meat grinder, its Microsoft peers across the Atlantic are just getting started.

However, the road to a long-term vision for a sustainable, successful XBOX brand also seems to go through the rough parts of town, namely divestments and layoffs that have so far cost us iconic studios like Hellblade’s Ninja Theory.

Elsewhere, California-based Electronic Arts brought the hammer down on BioWare last month, in a move that was equal parts shocking and predictable. The studio was known to be struggling even before its parent company’s acquisition by a Saudi public investment fund became official earlier this year, and it appears EA felt we don’t need a new Mass Effect when there’s Star Wars in the making.

The next release from Ubisoft’s Vantage Studios is set to be a new spin on Rainbow Six, called Tom Clancy’s Rainbow Six Tactics. The game will be a single-player tactics game that brings the franchise back to its more grounded roots as a counter-terrorism simulator, with the 2027 release window set to ride the wave of Star Wars: Zero Company.

Christopher Nolan Explains Why Fans Will Never See an Odyssey Director’s Cut

📅 Nov 19, 2026  |  Category: Entertainment  |  Read Source →

Don’t expect to see any deleted scenes in the epic’s home release

With a runtime of just under three hours, Christopher Nolan's The Odyssey doesn't necessarily need to be any longer, even as an adaptation of Homer's epic poem. Although it isn't Nolan's longest film, fans are always clamoring for more from the prolific director. With The Odyssey's release on home media​​​​​​​, there was some hope that a director's cut or deleted scenes would surface. Nolan has shut that idea down, going so far as to say people will likely never see director's cuts of his films.

Nolan has been pretty excited about the home release of The Odyssey. While the director is known for shooting in IMAX and encouraging audiences to see his movies on the biggest screens possible, he also knows home viewing has a place in movie culture. Speaking to Polygon, Nolan said that at home the viewer is able to control the timeline and look at details, specifically saying that "it's really about the performances." He also supports talking over the movie​​​​​​​ in the comfort of your home.

Still, home releases often come with bonuses such as deleted scenes. Nolan told The Independent, however, that isn't his style. “I don’t really believe in director’s cuts. I want to stand by the film that goes out,”​​​​​​​ the director said. He added that, in the case of The Odyssey, "there are a lot of deleted scenes up in the wastebasket that no one will ever see." Nolan stresses​​​​​​d that there aren't actually too many scenes that didn't make the final cut because shooting on film requires planning. "I tend to try and weed things out on paper because it’s crazy expensive to shoot things that aren’t going to be in the film." He admitted that this has led to a lack of bonuses on home releases, "which always disappoints the DVD crowd."

For those watching the movie for the first time or the tenth time​​​​​​​, Nolan does have some opinions on how to best emulate the movie theater experience. Specifically, he suggests people get a projector. "You can get a relatively low-cost, pretty high-quality projector, darken your living room, maybe introduce some popcorn into the equation or whatever," he told Polygon. "Have a mini movie theater and do it on your own timeline. That's tremendous fun."

Over the course of his career, Nolan said that he's been impressed by how much the home-viewing experience has improved thanks to technology like better projection technology​​​​​​​. Because of that, he told Polygon that a film like The Odyssey carries its cinematic excitement and spectacle to people's living rooms. "Even if people are seeing it for the first time, I think the film sort of carries that with it."

How to switch camera views in GTA 6

📅 Nov 19, 2026  |  Category: Other  |  Read Source →

The game only offers a first-person perspective in very limited circumstances.

Grand Theft Auto 6 gives players lots of ways to customize the experience, from the clothes the protagonists wear to granular user interface controls. Being able to shift your perspective of the action is part of that. Rockstar has long offered GTA players the ability to reposition the camera, and you can do that this time around as well.

In the camera tab of the settings menu, you can adjust the height of the camera while you're on foot, as well as the horizontal and vertical follow speeds. You have similar options for land and water vehicles, along with specific follow speeds based on whether you're the driver or a passenger. When it comes to aircraft, you can adjust the follow speed and determine whether the camera follows your plane when you do a barrel roll.

When you're out there exploring the map in a land or water vehicle, you can change the height of the camera on the fly. To switch between the three options, hold the down button on the D-pad, pick the height you want and confirm. You'll also be able to switch to the cinematic camera mode here. This makes things like high-speed chases look cool, but you might find it harder to steer your vehicle.

In addition, there are some very limited ways in which you can switch to a first-person perspective. While you're on foot, you can hold the left trigger on your controller and click the right thumbstick. This lets you take a closer look at your surroundings.

Going back to the camera section of the settings menu, you can enable a first-person perspective when your character is looking at their phone. If you look at your phone while you're driving (in GTA 6, never in real life), you'll see your device and the road from a first-person view as well. Perhaps Rockstar will add a proper first-person mode to GTA 6 down the line, as it did with the previous game.
