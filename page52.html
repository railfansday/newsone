Legendary Jewelry Designer Paolo Bulgari Dead at 89

📅 Nov 22, 2026  |  Category: Business  |  Read Source →

Paolo Bulgari—whose Bvlgari Group created unforgettable jewelry designs for stars like Elizabeth Taylor and Zendaya—has died at age 89.

Paolo Bulgari—famed jewelry designer and chairman of the Bvlgari Group—has died, the company confirmed on social media Nov. 21. He was 89.

“It is with deep sadness that we learned of the passing of Paolo Bulgari, grandson of the company’s founder Sotirio Bulgari and Chairman of the Bvlgari Group since 1984,” the company said in a statement shared to Instagram. “Our deepest condolences go to his family, friends and all those who had the honor of sharing this journey with him.”

“Thank you, Mr. Paolo,” they added, “your legacy as a brilliant beacon of creative inspiration will continue to inspire generations to come.”

Born in Rome, Italy, in 1937, Bulgari—who shared four kids with ex-wife Alice Valli as well as two kids with wife Maite Caprio—was destined to carry on the family business from a young age.

“Paolo Bulgari began collaborating with his father in 1960,” the statement explained, “specializing in the knowledge of precious stones, of which he was one of the world’s leading experts, and refining the sensibility and aesthetic sense that are at the root of the Bvlgari style.”

And it wasn’t long until he started making his own mark on the brand.

“A true creative soul, he poured his heart into his craft and into nurturing the talents of young designers,” added the statement, “leaving an indelible mark on the world of jewelry.”

In fact, Bulgari, alongside his brothers Gianni Bulgari and Nicola Bulgari, played an integral role in shaping the brand’s distinctive style, becoming known for their use of bold shapes and standout gems.

They also expanded the business from jewelry into watches, perfumes and even hotels.

And over the decades, the brand’s creations have adorned stars from Elizabeth Taylor and Princess Diana to Zendaya and Anne Hathaway.

Just last month, Kim Kardashian said she might just “faint” in a video shared to Instagram Oct. 28 while getting a private tour of Taylor’s jewelry collection at the Bvlgari store in Italy, in which she was able to try on one of the late actress’ beloved pieces—an emerald and diamond necklace gifted to her by Richard Burton in 1962.

As Kardashian put it, “I am freaking out!”

And she of course had to thank her stylist Law Roach for making the dream come true, calling him on the phone to thank him for the “best” birthday present ever.

And while it’s true that Bulgari’s influence on fashion has stood the test of time, that’s because he believed “everything can be a source of inspiration.”

As he used to say, per Bvlgari’s statement, “There are no rules in creativity. Never.”

9 Most Overlooked Thanksgiving TV Episodes, Ranked

📅 Nov 22, 2026  |  Category: Food  |  Read Source →

Come November, it seems like the same Thanksgiving episodes circulate on every streaming list. They're the Friends holiday flashbacks, the parade mishaps of Seinfeld, the tofurkey served by Mrs. Kim on Gilmore Girls. If you've seen one "best Thanksgiving TV" list, you've seen them all. And sure, those episodes earned their spot, but the holiday is a pressure cooker for good TV. One table, too much wine, friends and relatives who know exactly which buttons to push, and a meal no one can escape.

There are plenty of episodes out there celebrating the day spent stuffing faces and cringing at historical re-tellings of Pilgrims and Native Americans getting along. But for every episode that makes the annual rotation, there's one just as good that never gets its due. In fact, the Thanksgiving TV canon runs deeper than most people realize. These are the ones worth adding to your queue.

After nine seasons of burning bridges across Philadelphia, the It's Always Sunny gang decides to use Thanksgiving dinner to make peace with everyone they've wronged. The motivation isn't guilt — it's that they've run out of places to go. Dennis (Glenn Howerton) tallies up the bars, restaurants, and entire neighborhoods they've been banned from, and the only solution anyone can come up with is inviting the McPoyles, the Waitress (Mary Elizabeth Ellis), Liam (Jimmi Simpson), and other recurring casualties of the Gang's sociopathy to Mac (Rob Mac) and Dennis' apartment for a reconciliation feast. The dinner devolves almost immediately.

Every attempt at an apology turns into a new argument. The food fight starts early, and the episode ends with the Gang barricading their guests inside the apartment, drilling the door shut, and leaving while the place catches fire. It's the season finale, and it works as a perfect inversion of what a Thanksgiving episode is supposed to do. Where most shows use the holiday to bring people together, It's Always Sunny uses it to prove that some people are fundamentally incapable of change, and it's funnier for being honest about it.

Bob Belcher (H. Jon Benjamin) takes his Thanksgiving turkey extremely seriously, so when his carefully brined bird keeps ending up in the toilet, he treats it as a crime scene. The kids all have motives (mostly to mess with Bob), though Linda (John Roberts) is too cheerful to be innocent, and Gayle's (Megan Mullally) cats are lurking around with unclear intentions. Every time Bob replaces the turkey, it winds up back in the toilet. He goes through three birds before the episode is over.

The payoff is both absurd and oddly sweet: Bob has been sleepwalking and putting the turkey in the toilet himself because he's dreaming about potty-training a young Tina (Dan Mintz). It's the kind of reveal that could only work on Bob's Burgers, where the emotional logic is always a little sideways but completely sincere. The Belchers' Thanksgiving episodes are a tradition unto themselves at this point, but "Turkey in a Can" is one of the best, a holiday mystery that wraps up with Bob coming to terms with his kids growing up.

By Season 5, Ghosts has gotten really good at finding new ways to strand Sam (Rose McIver) between the living and the dead. "Planes, Shanes and Automobiles" sends her on a book tour road trip with ghost Pete (Richie Moriarty) riding along, and the two of them get on each other's nerves almost immediately. When an airline strike threatens to strand them and wreck their Thanksgiving plans, Pete channels his inner John Candy.

Back at Woodstone, the ghosts are fed up with Jay's (Utkarsh Ambudkar) inability to meet their demands while Sam is gone, so they recruit Kyle (Ben Feldman), the only other living person who can see them. Kyle immediately one-ups Jay at ghost-sitting, and the rivalry between them is a low-key highlight of the whole season. The episode ends with Kyle joining the Arondekars' first Thanksgiving at Woodstone, and the found-family energy of that final scene is the kind of thing Ghosts has always done well, even when the rest of the TV landscape has moved on from network sitcoms.

Five episodes in, Succession was still finding its legs, and then the Roys sat down for Thanksgiving dinner. Logan (Brian Cox) hosts at his penthouse, and the guest list alone is a disaster waiting to happen: his estranged brother Ewan (James Cromwell) shows up from Canada, Kendall (Jeremy Strong) is quietly building a vote of no confidence, Connor (Alan Ruck) brings Willa (Justine Lupe) and raises eyebrows across the table, and Tom (Matthew Macfayden) is starting to realize what he's actually signed up for. Everyone at this dinner has an agenda, and the holiday gives them just enough cover to pretend they don't.

The episode's title comes from a children's memory game the family plays after dinner, and it's during this game that Logan, showing signs of cognitive decline from his stroke, strikes his grandson Iverson (Quentin Morales). It's a moment that reframes everything that came before it. The rest of this list skews funny, messy, heartfelt. This one is none of those things, and it's here anyway, because Thanksgiving isn't always sweet. Some families weaponize the dinner table, and the Roys do it better than anyone on television.

This one aired on Thanksgiving night in 1999, and Sean Hayes absolutely demolished it. Jack's (Hayes) mom Judith (Veronica Cartwright) comes to Will (Eric McCormack) and Grace's (Debra Messing) apartment for the holiday, and Will and Grace are stunned to realize she has no idea her son is gay. Jack has been telling her that he and Grace used to date. Karen (Megan Mullally) jumps in, claiming to be Jack's current girlfriend, and the deception snowballs from there until Jack's friends push him to tell his mother the truth. For a show that wore its queerness proudly from the pilot, the fact that Jack had never come out to his own mother added real weight.

Hayes plays the scene where he finally tells her with the kind of vulnerability he didn't always get to show on the series, and it makes the comedy around it hit differently. The episode won the series a special Thanksgiving timeslot and helped cement Will & Grace's second season as the one that earned its Outstanding Comedy Emmy.

The Pearson family Thanksgiving episodes of This Is Us became a whole thing over six seasons, but the first one is where the template was set. Jack (Milo Ventimiglia) and Rebecca (Mandy Moore) are driving the kids to her parents' house when things go sideways, and they end up at a run-down motel called the Pinewood Lodge. The manager wears a pilgrim hat and calls himself "Pilgrim Rick." Jack, being Jack, puts on the hat, declares himself the new Pilgrim Rick, and turns a busted Thanksgiving into a family tradition that the Pearson kids carry into adulthood.

In the present-day timeline, that tradition is still going on, but the holiday takes a devastating turn when Randall (Sterling K. Brown) discovers that Rebecca knew about his biological father and kept it from him for his entire life. He confronts her during the family's yarn game, and the scene is a gut punch. The genius of "Pilgrim Rick" is how it holds both things at once: the warmth of a family building something out of nothing and the fractures that were always underneath.

When this episode aired in November 2001, Brad Pitt and Jennifer Aniston were married, which turned a solid Friends Thanksgiving into a full-on pop culture event. Pitt plays Will Colbert, a formerly overweight high school classmate of Ross (David Schwimmer) and Monica (Courteney Cox) who shows up to Thanksgiving dinner looking like, well, Brad Pitt. The twist: Will co-founded the "I Hate Rachel Green Club" with Ross back in the day, and he has not moved on.

The episode's best gag is the slow reveal that Ross' grudge against Rachel was organized enough to have its own club, its own membership, and its own rumor campaign. Although Joey (Matt LeBlanc) eating an entire turkey by himself while wearing Phoebe's (Lisa Kudrow) maternity pants is a great subplot. The real-life casting adds a weird meta layer that makes the whole thing funnier in retrospect, and it earned Pitt an Emmy nomination for Outstanding Guest Actor in a Comedy Series.

Modern Family spent eleven seasons proving that the funniest version of family dysfunction is the kind where everyone genuinely means well. Phil (Ty Burrell) dreams too hard, Claire (Julie Bowen) controls too much, Jay (Ed O'Neill) avoids feelings, Gloria (Sofía Vergara) pulls them out of him like teeth, and every week, the confessional interviews let them explain exactly why they were right before the episode proved them wrong. "Three Turkeys" takes all of that and "stuffs it" into one house in one afternoon.

Phil and Luke (Nolan Gould) take over Thanksgiving dinner. Claire lets them, and also secretly cooks a backup turkey, because trusting Phil with a major holiday meal is a bridge too far. Jay and Gloria, meanwhile, fake a trip to Colombia just to get out of going. It's the kind of episode where every character has a totally reasonable plan that falls apart the second it touches anyone else's totally reasonable plan. But the Thanksgiving setting makes everything tighter than a regular episode, because nobody can leave and dinner is on a deadline.

Casting Jamie Lee Curtis and Rob Reiner as Jess' (Zooey Deschanel) divorced parents was one of the best moves New Girl ever made, and this episode is their debut. Jess invites her mom Joan and dad Bob to the loft for Thanksgiving dinner, planning separate meals so they don’t have to see each other. When they both show up at the same time, Jess pivots into full Parent Trap mode, enlisting Nick (Jake Johnson) and Cece (Hannah Simone) to help reunite them. The highlight here is how the rest of the friend group is roped into Jess' childhood scheme.

Curtis and Johnson, basting a turkey together a bit too sexually, have an off-the-rails energy while Reiner and Deschanel share some quiet, grounded father-daughter scenes New Girl didn't always make time for. And then there's Schmidt (Max Greenfield) versus his cousin Big Schmidt (Rob Riggle), which turns Thanksgiving dinner into a physical competition nobody wanted. It's a crowded episode, but New Girl's second season was the show at its most confident, and "Parents" holds all of it together.

Zebroski had been appointed as a player-coach at Southern League side Gosport Borough during the summer.

Chris Zebroski started his career at Swindon Town, but moved to Plymouth Argyle after failing to be offered a senior contract at The County Ground, following a spell with non-league Cirencester City. But he first hit the headlines for all the wrong reasons in August 2007 after he was sacked by the club for gross misconduct following an altercation in the bar during their pre-season tour of Austria the previous month, which left the club captain Paul Wotton with cuts to his head.

Zebroski moved on to Millwall and then to Wycombe Wanderers, but his legal issues continued to pile up. After going on loan to Torquay United, where he was a success in his first season with 19 goals for them in the National League throughout the 2007-08 season, he was given a 12-month community order and a fine for kicking and punching a soldier in a street fight in Torquay in August 2010.

Whether it's a coincidence or not that McGlashan left Gosport on the same day that Zebroski pleaded guilty to the latest set of charges brought against him is unknown.

What is clear about Chris Zebroski's story is that he has been given plenty of chances. Noah Kahan Deserved a Lot More Grammy Love Than Just One Minor Nomination. Here’s Why He Didn’t Get It

📅 Nov 22, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Fans and professional forecasters alike were shocked when the superstar nearly got shut out, after predictions across the board that his 'Great Divide' blockbuster would earn major award recognition. Some of the reasons the Grammys didn't ultimately go for him may be in plain sight.

Senior Music Writer and Chief Music Critic

In previous eras of less pervasive social media, we usually could only guess at just how disappointed music and film personalities were when they didn’t fare well with Grammy or Oscar nominations. But lately, more and more major and minor figures have been up-front and honest in sharing with their fans that, hey, yes, they’re feeling the apparent lack of industry love, too. As long as these reflections come off as more bummed out and philosophical than bitterly entitled or peevish — here’s looking at you, John Summit — they can make for a moment of connection between artists and their fan base. And so it is that, this past week, I followed one of our most likably candid superstars through what I came to refer to as the Five Stages of Noah Kahan Grammy Grief.

Kahan himself skipped over the anger phase, though his fans certainly felt that one, when it turned out that their hero got passed over for everything but one relatively modest nom, in defiance of all expectation. There were other stages they went through together. First came uneasy anticipation: The day before nominations came out, Kahan issued a whatever-will-be-will-be-type tweet, almost as if he’d gotten a hint that things were not going to go well for him. (“Whatever happens tomorrow I love you all and I have done everything I’ve ever needed to do in my career because of YOUR support.”) Then came resignation and premature acceptance: “Life goes on.” Then came gallows humor, as he took a fake phone call on stage from the Grammys and quipped, “Wrong number.” And finally, there was real acceptance, as he admitted in an affecting stage monologue that he was having a teachable moment as, without mentioning the awards, Kahan said he was assessing why he had hoped too much for something and put the value of his worth in the wrong place.

Undoubtedly, some would say he should have just put himself above it all and not even referenced the seeming snub, but my admiration is always going to go more toward people who address elephants in rooms. So maybe now he could move forward with his usual good humor… while, sure, having the fact that he just sold out his next 24 U.S. stadium shows to keep him warm at night.

But maybe us Grammy prognosticators aren’t yet ready to move on, because like pollsters in 2024, we want to figure out how we got Kahan’s chances wrong. And really, he was the only significant thing we forecasted incorrectly, since virtually everything else in the top three categories went as predicted, for once. What Every 65-Year-Old Should Know About Social Security

📅 Nov 22, 2026  |  Category: Business  |  Read Source →

It's below full retirement age, but still a point where key choices need to be made.

By Stefon Walters – Nov 22, 2026 at 2:30PM EST

Age 65 can feel like a gray area for Social Security because it's a few years after you can start receiving benefits, but it's still before you reach your full retirement age. For a while, 65 was the traditional retirement age and seen as the "finish line," but that isn't necessarily the case today.

But age 65 still matters because it affects Social Security benefits and has health insurance implications, so if you're nearing 65 or planning to claim at that age, here are a few things to know.

You can begin claiming Social Security at age 62, but claiming any time before your full retirement age -- which is 67 for anyone born in 1960 or later -- will reduce your monthly benefit. Your full retirement age is when you can receive your full monthly benefit, called your primary insurance amount. This is the starting point Social Security uses to determine your benefit based on when you claim.

Benefits are reduced by 5/9 of 1% monthly for each month you claim early, up to 36 months. So, if your full retirement age is 67 and you claim at 65, your benefit will be reduced by around 13.33%. If your full benefit was $2,000, you'd receive $1,733.33 if you claimed at 65.

If you claim benefits before your full retirement age, you could be subject to the Social Security retirement earnings test if you earn above a certain limit. For 2026, the limit is $24,480 if you won't reach your full retirement age in 2026 and $65,160 if you will. In 2027, these limits increase to $25,560 and $67,920, respectively.

If you earn above the limit in the year you won't reach full retirement age, your monthly benefit will be reduced by $1 for each $2 you're over. If you reach full retirement age that year and earn above the limit, your benefit is reduced by $1 per $3 you're over.

Thankfully, benefits aren't permanently lost -- just withheld. Once you reach your full retirement age, Social Security will gradually return the withheld benefits by increasing your monthly payout.

Social Security and Medicare are two different programs, but they're connected in how premiums are paid. You become eligible for Medicare at age 65, and if you're receiving Social Security at the time, your Medicare Part B premiums are automatically deducted from your monthly Social Security benefit.

This year, the Medicare Part B premium is $202.90, but it will increase to $209.40 in 2027. If you claim both Social Security and Medicare and your monthly benefit is $2,000 next year, you'd receive $1,790.60 after the premium is deducted.

This automatic deduction applies only to Part B premiums. If you're enrolled in another Medicare plan, such as Part C (Medicare Advantage) or Part D (prescriptions), you'll pay those premiums directly.

How long is a new Fitbit actually supposed to last?

📅 Nov 22, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Longevity is a major factor to consider with any fitness wearable. It's meant to be used 24/7, and its subject to conditions that would wreck many other products, from accidental knocks and scrapes to heavy deadlift sessions. As gadgets go, there are few things worse than discovering you're out a few hundred dollars because your tracker wasn't sufficiently sweatproof.

There's more to longevity than durability, though. There's also battery health, not to mention software support, since a tracker is useless if you can't sync and browse its data. At some point, end of support is virtually guaranteed — it's just a question of whether that will be before or after you're ready to move on.

How long can you expect a new product like the Fitbit Edge or Fitbit Air to last? While we obviously can't say for certain, info from Google, third parties and my own personal experience with wearables may be helpful.

Let's return to durability for a moment. Most impacts are only going to do cosmetic damage, as long as you don't destroy the screen or button(s), so the greater concern is water resistance. A product like the Edge has an IP68 rating, meaning it can be submerged and keep running, but that's only for as long as its liquid protection seals hold up.

Over time, the seals on any device are bound to degrade, especially with repeated exposure to moisture. It's just a question of how quickly. While a person who only wears their Fitbit for walking might never run into trouble, someone who swims daily might encounter a sudden breakdown a few years down the road. Sweat killed three generations of my Beats earbuds, despite their supposed IPX4 sweat and water resistance.

As for battery health, all lithium ion batteries have a finite number of 0 to 100 percent charge cycles. In wearables, this is typically about 300, so charging a device roughly once per week could in theory get you over five years of runtime. That's not the whole picture, however. Apart from variable cycle counts, your device's health can be prolonged if you rarely let your battery go dead, but its lifespan can also be harmed by the issue of practical capacity.

Before you hit that 300 mark, various chemical factors will probably reduce capacity to the point you no longer consider a Fitbit usable. Most people don't want to charge a wearable multiple times per day. Also, while the Edge has a replaceable battery, it's the exception to the rule. There's no choice but to dispose of most Fitbits once they cross this threshold.

Google explains that it offers security updates for at least two years after a Fitbit product is no longer on sale. If you buy one of the company's current models, updates are going to last through 2028 and probably beyond, depending on when Google refreshes its hardware. The Inspire 3, Versa 4 and Sense 2 will presumably be first on the chopping block, since they launched in 2022.

Note that security updates aren't the same thing as active feature support. Companies like Google tend to prioritize the newest hardware, both to encourage you to spend money, and because older products can only keep up with feature changes for so long. For example, Google Health Guardian features like irregular rhythm notifications only work with a narrow range of Fitbits, despite some unsupported models having heart rate sensors too. Meanwhile, the original Fitbit app has been replaced by Google Health. There's no telling how long legacy devices will remain compatible as the new app evolves.

You may eventually find features so basic or broken that you end up buying a new device regardless of damage, battery health or security vulnerabilities. Realistically, I wouldn't count on owning a new Fitbit for any more than five years, but I hope I'm proven wrong.
