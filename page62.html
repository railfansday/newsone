Delhi air quality worsens ahead of Diwali as firework restrictions enter force

📅 Nov 04, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Air pollution is an annual problem in Delhi and large parts of northern India

Delhi is once again battling worsening air pollution as the onset of winter brings a familiar deterioration in air quality to India's capital.

The city's air quality index (AQI) - which measures PM2.5, fine particulate matter that can clog lungs - has crossed 300, entering the "very poor" category and raising health concerns for millions of residents.

Breathing such polluted air over prolonged periods can cause respiratory problems and worsen existing illnesses, with children and older people particularly vulnerable.

Authorities have responded by invoking Stage II of the Graded Response Action Plan (GRAP), introducing measures to curb dust, vehicle emissions and other sources of pollution.

GRAP is a set of emergency measures that become progressively stricter as pollution levels rise. The latest restrictions include more intensive road cleaning, higher parking charges to discourage private vehicle use and increased public transport services. Delhi Metro has introduced 20 additional weekday train trips, while authorities have been directed to stagger government office timings to ease traffic congestion.

Officials said the measures were introduced after forecasts suggested air quality would remain very poor over the coming days.

Delhi and neighbouring parts of northern India grapple with hazardous air every winter, particularly around Diwali, one of India's biggest festivals, which is just days away.

The festival is celebrated with lights, lamps and fireworks, which often worsen the city's already poor air quality by releasing fine particles and harmful gases into the atmosphere.

The annual pollution crisis is driven by a combination of factors, including vehicle exhaust, industrial emissions, falling temperatures, low wind speeds and the burning of crop stubble in neighbouring states.

The sharp rise in pollution follows an unusually clean October, when Delhi recorded its lowest average AQI for the month in eight years.

Experts have attributed the sudden deterioration to falling temperatures, slow winds and an increase in crop-residue burning in the neighbouring states of Punjab and Haryana.

A recent storm had brought rain and stronger winds, helping clear the air. But as the weather settled, northwesterly winds returned, carrying smoke from burning fields towards the capital.

India's pollution watchdog classifies AQI readings between 301 and 400 as "very poor", while anything above 400 is considered "severe". On Wednesday morning, Delhi's AQI stood at 377.

The index takes into account eight major pollutants, including PM2.5 - tiny particles about 30 times smaller than the width of a human hair that can penetrate deep into the lungs.

Firecrackers set off during Diwali release particulate matter and gases into the air, adding to the existing pollution

With Diwali just days away, there are concerns that fireworks could push pollution levels even higher.

Last year, Delhi woke up to a toxic haze the morning after the festival, despite a court directive to use less-polluting firecrackers.

Authorities have imposed restrictions on firecrackers over the years, including outright bans on their manufacture, sale and use, but enforcing them has proved difficult.

On Monday, India's Supreme Court allowed the manufacture, sale and use of certified green firecrackers in Delhi and neighbouring areas for a limited period.

It said the crackers could be set off only between 06:00 and 07:00 local time (00:30-01:30 GMT) and between 20:00 and 22:00 (14:30-16:30 GMT) on 7 and 8 November, during the Diwali weekend.

The court also banned fireworks within 300m of hospitals, care homes, schools and other designated silence zones.

Green firecrackers use modified chemical formulations designed to produce fewer emissions than conventional fireworks. They must carry QR codes that allow buyers and authorities to verify details of their manufacturer and certification.

But ensuring that only approved firecrackers are sold remains a major challenge.

In its latest order, the Supreme Court acknowledged that conventional firecrackers had continued to be sold as green alternatives, even when a complete ban was in place.

Even Mumbai, India's financial capital, has recorded very high levels of pollution in recent days

It has directed police to patrol designated sales areas, carry out random QR-code checks and seize illegal firecrackers.

During particularly severe pollution episodes, Delhi authorities have also introduced measures such as hybrid schooling, work-from-home arrangements and restrictions on certain vehicles.

The problem is not confined to the capital. Mumbai, India's financial hub, has also recorded elevated pollution levels over the past month, despite benefiting from coastal winds that typically help disperse pollutants.

On 24 October, Mumbai's AQI had reached 322, placing it in the "very poor" category. The spike was attributed to a combination of low wind speeds, construction dust, garbage burning, firecracker emissions and pollutants carried from neighbouring regions.

The city's air quality has improved in recent days - and it recorded an AQI of 89, classified as "satisfactory", on Wednesday. Neighbouring Navi Mumbai recorded a "moderate" AQI of 118.

The improvement follows nearly two weeks of smoggy conditions, with residents sharing photos and videos of the haze on social media.

The Villans instead paid Parma £30million to sign Zion Suzuki, who has enjoyed a strong start to life in the Premier League.

The 24-year-old's first significant mistake came when he dropped Alex Iwobi's corner into his own net on Saturday, throwing away the lead as Villa drew 1-1 with Fulham.

Yet the Japan international made several other key saves in the game and has been backed to bounce back from the error.

Suzuki has drawn some early comparisons to Andre Onana, who joined Manchester United in 2023 and is now at Turkish side Trabzonspor.

The Cameroon international caught eyes for his superb ability on the ball when he first arrived, but mistakes quickly creeped in.

📅 Nov 04, 2026  |  Category: World  |  Read Source →

Alexis Mac Allister has spoken out about his ‘painful, unpleasant situation’ at Liverpool in terms of not being offered a new contract.

The Argentine caused a stir in September when he said that he was ‘very sad‘ not to have been offered a new deal by the club, having seen fellow 2023 signings Ryan Gravenberch and Dominik Szoboszlai both pen contract extensions earlier this year.

The Telegraph’s Dominic King reported last month that Anfield chiefs aren’t in ‘any rush’ to begin talks over a new deal for the Reds’ number 10 (who’s contracted to June 2028), despite his resurgence in form since the start of this season.

Appearing on the Los Edul show in his homeland, Mac Allister spoke candidly about Liverpool’s decision not to offer him new terms over the past few months.

The 27-year-old said: “We’re in a situation where I have a year and a half left on my contract. Last season I was told that the club’s idea was not to renew it, at least for now, and obviously that was a painful, unpleasant situation.”

When asked if the situation was making him despondent, he replied: “Not that much, because at the end of the day, I still had – and still have – time left at this club, which is what I want most – to stay here.

“The reality is that the manager has now changed, the sporting director has changed, and new investors are coming into the club, so I think I’m now in a position where anything can happen. We’ll see what happens in the future. I’ve always said that I’m very happy here, that I really enjoy it, and we’ll see.”

Mac Allister added: “Look at Szoboszlai and Gravenberch. They’re two players who were in the same contractual situation as me, and when I was told that, they renewed their contracts.

“It was more a position taken by the club, and generally that’s how things are done. At the end of the day, as a club you don’t want to lose a lot of money, and you don’t want players leaving on free transfers either.”

Could Buying This 1 Cryptocurrency Make You Rich?

📅 Nov 04, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Venice Token has skyrocketed in value this year. But can it continue its momentum over the long haul?

For the year, Venice Token (VVV +14.57%) is up a heart-pounding 1,250%. That makes it the top-performing cryptocurrency with a market cap of $1 billion or higher.

For good reason, then, some investors have embraced Venice Token as a potential millionaire-maker and a pathway to life-changing wealth. But is that really the case?

Artificial intelligence continues to be one of the few bright spots in the crypto market this year. Traditional market leaders Bitcoin and Ethereum are down 12% and 22%, respectively, for the year. Meanwhile, AI cryptos continue to soar much higher.

Of all the top AI crypto stars, Venice Token has burned the brightest, with its 1,250% return. But don't forget about Bittensor or NEAR Protocol, the latter of which is up 133% for the year. Both also represent ways to get exposure to AI via blockchain technology.

But investors have latched on to Venice Token specifically because it powers a privacy-focused version of ChatGPT. In other words, you can type in a quick prompt on the Venice AI platform and get a response in the form of text, images, or video. Best of all, your usage of the platform remains anonymous and private, so you never have to worry about large language models training on your personal data in the future.

But here's the thing: You can't simultaneously believe in companies such as Anthropic or OpenAI and Venice at the same time. Their vision of AI is very different. Anthropic and OpenAI are pushing a vision of centralized AI, in which the majority of power and control lies within the corporation. Venice is pushing a vision of decentralized, privacy-focused AI built with blockchain technology.

And that's where things get tricky for investors. The thinking until recently has been that the upcoming Anthropic and OpenAI IPOs will lift all AI boats. And that includes AI cryptocurrencies such as Venice Token.

According to this way of thinking, even more money will flood into the AI crypto space after the IPOs, helping to push up the price of Venice Token. That means crypto investors might be able to squeeze another year of triple- or even quadruple-digit returns out of Venice.

But I don't think so. The AI crypto space is littered with big names that soar in value for one year before ultimately collapsing. Several prominent early AI crypto names have already fallen by the wayside. Even AI cryptos specifically pegged to the AI agent investment thesis have already imploded.

So what makes investors think Venice Token will be any different? It's still too early to pick winners and losers in the AI space. That makes it problematic to pick cryptocurrencies you can buy and hold for the long haul. Venice makes for an interesting short-term momentum play, but is simply too risky and volatile as a long-term wealth creation tool.
