Cardiff 3-3 West Ham: The Hammers came back from three goals down to secure a point in Parker’s first match in charge

Want to bookmark your favourite articles and stories to read or reference later? Start your Independent Membership today.

Scott Parker saw his West Ham side recover from three goals down to draw 3-3 at Cardiff in a thrilling first game in charge.

Jack Moylan’s first hat-trick in English football had Cardiff in cruise control at half-time and Hammers fans venting their anger.

But the Hammers – inspired by captain Jarrod Bowen – fought back in incredible fashion as Divine Mukasa, Arne Engels and Max Kilman netted to secure a point.

Parker was confirmed as Hammers boss on Wednesday morning – just 24 hours after Nuno Espirito Santo’s departure – but it was a tough watch for the former England midfielder initially as Moylan had Cardiff 3-0 ahead inside 39 minutes.

Moylan scored a hat-trick on his Republic of Ireland debut against Grenada in May, but this was his first club treble since scoring three for Shelbourne in May 2023.

The draw extends Cardiff’s unbeaten home league run to 10 games over the last two seasons, while West Ham occupy the final play-off place in eighth.

Nuno’s 13-month tenure had ended after the Hammers had won just twice in six matches and lost successive home games to Southampton and West Brom.

Taty Castellanos had hinted at better times ahead, forcing Nathan Trott in to early action after Bowen’s effort had deflected to him.

But the Hammers were behind after Alex Robertson found Moylan in the 11th minute. Moylan advanced to find the top corner of Mads Hermansen’s net with a ferocious strike from 20 yards.

Hermansen tipped aside Calum Scanlon’s cross and Will Fish headed over as Cardiff probed. There was no reprieve for West Ham when Perry Ng curled a ball over a static defence after 36 minutes.

Moylan cleverly slowed down for Morato to bundle him over, a clear penalty. Cardiff had missed their last two spot-kicks, but this time Moylan was entrusted with the responsibility and sent Hermansen the wrong way.

Angry Hammers fans responded with cries of ‘you’re not fit to wear the shirt’, and many of them appeared to be making a swift departure three minutes later.

Joel Bagan’s corner cleared Fish at the near post, but Moylan came in behind him to head home his sixth goal in five games, and his 10th of the season.

Parker sent on Mohamadou Kante and Joel Piroe for Soungoutou Magassa and Castellanos at half-time and there was instantly more conviction from the Hammers.

Bowen floated a delicious pass through after 58 minutes, and Mukasa lifted the ball over Trott and in off a post.

The deficit was reduced further seven minutes later when Piroe flicked on Bowen’s pass and Engels slid home his first Hammers goal.

Kante nearly toe poked the ball past Trott before Kilman touched home Bowen’s brilliant cross, and West Ham almost won it through substitutes Ezra Mayers and Tomas Soucek.

Durbin Is Retiring, Marshall Just Lost—Why The Bill Threatening Your Credit Card Rewards Isn’t Dead

📅 Nov 04, 2026  |  Category: Business  |  Read Source →

Dick Durbin is retiring and Roger Marshall just lost his Senate seat. The two most prominent champions of the Credit Card Competition Act, the law intended to regulate credit card interchange which will mean far fewer rewards for consumers, will be gone in January. The bill threatening the economics of card rewards, and the airlines that depend on them, has support from President Trump and Vice President JD Vance was an original co-sponsor so it may come back with his presidential campaign. But the thing about this legislation is that it’s more useful as a way to raise campaign money from both retailers and banks than as something Congress actually passes. Departures of Durbin and Marshall haven’t made the Durbin-Marshall bill go away. Durbin identified Vermont Senator Peter Welch as his successor on the issue. The Airline Lobby Is Right To Worry In this week’s Airlines Confidential, Airlines for America CEO Chris Sununu warns that state initiatives interfering with card fees would eliminate points-funded travel. Banks buy miles because rewards help attract customers and their spending. By reducing margins on that spending, it’s less valuable to spend to encourage consumers to use the product. In markets where government has gotten involved in interchange rates we’ve seen: smaller initial bonuses higher annual fees annual caps on awards award chart devaluations (if each mile is worth less, an airline can still award one mile per dollar on spend despite receiving less money for it from the bank) It also means less revenue for the airline. In the third quarter, American Express represented all of Delta’s profit. American Airlines is barely breaking even even with several billion net from Citibank. We’d have much shakier airlines and fewer flights, and aspirational leisure destinations like Hawaii in particular would see less air service. What The Credit Card Competition Act Actually Does The current Senate bill, S. 3623, would require covered issuers with more than $100 billion in assets to enable at least two unaffiliated payment networks. At least one would have to be outside of Visa and Mastercard. Merchants get to choose the routing. That’s meant to put downward pressure on merchant payment costs by changing who controls which network handles the transaction. Cards issued in a three-party system, such as American Express issuing its own cards, are exempt from the routing requirement. Merchants benefit from accepting cards. They get more sales, and higher average sales. It’s often less expensive to accept credit cards than to take cash (due to employee theft, incorrect change, higher insurance costs). Retailers want the service cards offer, but want the government to force Visa and Mastercard to provide it for less. Trump’s promise of $1,200 in annual family savings was completely made up and inconsistent with the bill. Currently a majority of interchange gets rebated to the customer. Regulation of interchange has never lowered prices to consumers. And card payments, funded in large part by interchange, provide services to consumers beyond just payments (and borrowings). They often fund consumer protections – not just stronger dispute resolution than debit cards, but also extended warranty, return protection, and travel protections. The Bill’s Strongest Value Is As A Threat Back in July 2023, I argued that this was an especially useful issue for opening contribution wallets on both sides. Retailers have a large, concentrated interest in lowering their acceptance costs. Banks, payment networks and airlines have a large, concentrated interest in preserving the existing economics. An unresolved legislative threat gives both sides a reason to pour dollars into campaigns and political committees. I’d still bet on another round of proposals, threatened amendments and lobbying. Durbin and Marshall are leaving but the retailers and banks with billions at stake aren’t. Politicians still want the money. Year-end giving campaign launches for the AAN Research Program

📅 Nov 04, 2026  |  Category: Business  |  Read Source →

Support the urgent need for brain research funding

MINNEAPOLIS, Nov. 4, 2026 /PRNewswire/ -- The need for brain research funding has never been more urgent. That is why the American Academy of Neurology (AAN) today announced its year-end giving campaign for the AAN Research Program, inviting people to support the researchers working to find new treatments and cures for brain diseases and disorders.

One hundred percent of every tax-deductible donation will go directly to brain research.

"Scientific breakthroughs begin with researchers who have the ideas and expertise to pursue important questions," said American Academy of Neurology President Natalia S. Rost, MD, MPH, FAAN, FAHA. "The AAN Research Program gives all of us the opportunity to help advance high-quality brain research, bringing hope to people affected by brain disease."

Brain research is what makes it possible to prevent, diagnose, treat, and eventually even cure conditions of the brain and nervous system. It also advances our understanding of memory, learning, sleep, mental health, and healthy aging, helping people live healthier lives.

The AAN Research Program has funded brain research for more than 30 years, owing to its success the exceptional pool of talented researchers in the AAN community, the world's largest association of neurologists and neuroscience professionals.

Demand for funding, however, continues to outpace available resources. Hundreds of researchers apply for AAN grants. The AAN invested nearly $1.8 million in 2026, but millions in viable neuroscience research went unfunded.

"We know that excellent research proposals go unfunded," said American Academy of Neurology Chief Executive Officer Mary E. Post, MBA, CAE. "We want to make sure that we have the funding to support the research that may eventually lead to a cure for MS or a way to prevent Alzheimer's. Every contribution can help researchers pursue discoveries as they work toward improving brain health for all."

With neurologists leading the AAN Board of Directors, Science Committee, and Research Program Subcommittee, the AAN is guided by trusted experts who understand both the science and the real-world application of research.

Help advance research by making a tax-deductible gift on the BrainHealth.com "Donate to Research" page. Organizations interested in supporting the AAN Research Program may contact donations@aan.com.

The AAN Research Program is administered through the American Academy of Neurology Institute, the Academy's 501(c)(3) charitable organization.

Discover more about brain health at BrainHealth.com, a free public resource from the American Academy of Neurology. It offers a website, podcast, and books, connecting you with trusted information from the world's leading experts in brain health. Follow BrainHealth.com on Facebook, X, and Instagram.

The American Academy of Neurology is the leading voice in brain health. As the world's largest association of neurologists and neuroscience professionals with more than 44,000 members, the AAN provides access to the latest news, science and research affecting neurology for patients, caregivers, physicians and professionals alike. The AAN's mission is to enhance member career fulfillment and promote brain health for all. A neurologist is a doctor who specializes in the diagnosis, care and treatment of brain, spinal cord and nervous system diseases such as Alzheimer's disease, stroke, concussion, epilepsy, Parkinson's disease, multiple sclerosis, headache and migraine.

Explore the latest in neurological disease and brain health, from the minds at the AAN at AAN.com or find us on Facebook, X, Instagram, LinkedIn, and YouTube.

Is It Over for DAPT After Left Atrial Appendage Closure?

📅 Nov 04, 2026  |  Category: Other  |  Read Source →

— Antithrombotic monotherapy worked at least as well at 6 months

Antithrombotic monotherapy was similar to dual antiplatelet therapy (DAPT) for 6-month outcomes after left atrial appendage (LAA) closure, the SIMPLAAFY trial showed.

The incidence rate of combined all-cause death, stroke, systemic embolism, or major bleeding at 6 months was 5.1% with reduced-dose non-vitamin K oral anticoagulants (NOACs), 3.3% with aspirin alone, and 6.9% with DAPT, reported Saibal Kar, MD, of Los Robles Hospital and Medical Center in Thousand Oaks, California, at the Transcatheter Cardiovascular Therapeutics annual conference hosted by the Cardiovascular Research Foundation.

Statistically, the strategy of reduced-dose NOACs was at least as good as DAPT (risk ratio [RR] 0.75, 95% CI 0.48-1.18), as was aspirin alone (RR 0.49, 95% CI 0.29-0.82).

"These findings support the simplification of antithrombotic management after uncomplicated LAA [closure] and may be individualized according to competing bleeding and device thrombosis risks and patient preferences," Kar said.

The study was simultaneously published in JAMA.

Among the secondary analyses in the trial, there was no excess stroke at 6 months with the aspirin or NOAC monotherapy regimens. However, bleeds alone gave both an edge over DAPT, while low-dose NOAC therapy stood out for a relatively low incidence of device-related thrombus (DRT) at 45 days (0.9% vs 3.4% with DAPT and 2.7% with aspirin).

As it stands, standard practice varies widely for antithrombotic therapy after LAA closure on account of a "lack of legitimate evidence" for guidance, said session discussant Mohamad Alkhouli, MD, of the Mayo Clinic in Rochester, Minnesota.

"Aspirin seems to emerge a winner," Alkhouli suggested, based on SIMPLAAFY, while he noted that "the jury is still out" for NOACs.

Complicating the picture is the ANDES trial, which found that after LAA closure, short-term NOACs were no better than DAPT at preventing DRT at 60 days, and were associated with even more DRTs at 1 year (5.2% vs 2.3%, P=0.154).

Kar said these data gave him pause on making a blanket recommendation for NOACs after LAA closure.

Meanwhile, 12-month results from SIMPLAAFY are still pending.

"The fact is that we really didn't know what to do with these patients," said Kar. "What we will do beyond 1 year, I don't know."

SIMPLAAFY included patients with nonvalvular atrial fibrillation who had the Watchman FLX Pro LAA occluder successfully implanted. The investigators randomized 1,857 patients 1:1:1 to aspirin 81 to 100 mg alone, initial DAPT for 6 months (followed by aspirin thereafter), or initial reduced-dose NOACs -- 2.5-mg apixaban (Eliquis) twice daily or 10-mg rivaroxaban (Xarelto) daily -- for 3 months (followed by aspirin thereafter).

Across the three study groups, mean age was 76 years, 43% were women, mean CHA2DS2-VASc score was 4.2, and mean HAS-BLED bleeding risk score was 2.6.

Acute events through 7 days were rare in general. Rates of clinically relevant nonmajor bleeding were 1.0% with aspirin alone, 1.6% with DAPT, and 1.5% with low-dose NOACs; major bleeding rates were 0.2%, 0.5%, and 0.2%, respectively.

The proportion of participants adhering to their assigned medications (for 80% or more days) was 87.9% with aspirin alone, 76.8% with DAPT, and 85.3% with reduced-dose NOACs. "Both monotherapy treatments were better tolerated than DAPT," Kar noted.

A subgroup analysis indicated that people ages 75 and older had disproportionately better outcomes on aspirin (RR 0.34, 95% CI 0.18-0.64) or reduced-dose NOACs (RR 0.53, 95% CI 0.31-0.93).

LAA imaging at 45 days suggested no differences in the Watchman device seal across groups.

Kar stressed that only uncomplicated LAA occluder implants were included in the trial, limiting the generalizability of the findings. Some high-risk populations were also excluded (e.g., those with severe kidney disease, recent stroke, myocardial infarction, or major bleeding).

Of note, there was no prespecified comparison of the two monotherapy arms.

Despite its enduring popularity throughout its run, Stranger Things became a victim of its own success. Though some newer additions became breakout fan favorites, like the adorkable Robin Buckley (Maya Hawke) or the rebellious Eddie Munson (Joseph Quinn), by the time Stranger Things ended, the cast was so bloated that several OG characters got completely sidelined in the series finale.

How Much Premium Economy Costs On London-New York Flights In 2026

📅 Nov 04, 2026  |  Category: Business  |  Read Source →

Premium economy on a London-New York flight can cost more than twice as much as an economy ticket, but the difference between the two cabins and business class can sometimes be surprisingly small. For passengers facing an approximately eight-hour transatlantic journey, a wider seat, extra legroom, better meals, and extra baggage can be appealing, but does the experience justify the added cost?

In 2026, passengers traveling between London Heathrow Airport (LHR) and New York JFK Airport have several airlines to choose from, including British Airways, Virgin Atlantic, American Airlines, Delta Air Lines, and JetBlue. Those flying to Newark Liberty International Airport (EWR) have fewer nonstop options, primarily British Airways and United Airlines. With different premium economy products, competing fares, and varying baggage allowances, how much should passengers actually expect to pay for a more comfortable transatlantic journey?

Premium economy on the London-New York route typically costs approximately $1,000 to $1,300 for a return journey, compared with around $500 to $600 for a standard economy ticket. This means passengers can expect to pay roughly twice as much for the upgraded cabin, although the difference depends on the airline, travel dates, departure airport, and availability.

According to our analysis of premium economy fares in 2026, the transatlantic market demonstrates how airlines position their intermediate cabin between standard economy and business class. Premium economy has become a major revenue opportunity for carriers, letting them charge more for added comfort without offering the considerably more expensive lie-flat seating associated with business class.

However, the difference between cabins isn't always consistent. A NerdWallet comparison of British Airways fares found that one evening departure from New York JFK to London offered premium economy for $1,162, more than twice the $532 economy fare. Business class was available for just $1,222, meaning passengers would have saved only $60 by selecting premium economy instead. Another comparison showed a return premium economy fare of $1,459 against $737 in economy. These examples raise an important question: how much competition exists between airlines on the route, and where can passengers find the best selection?

The greatest selection of nonstop services is available between London Heathrow and New York JFK. According to FlightConnections, five airlines serve the route: British Airways, Virgin Atlantic, American Airlines, Delta Air Lines, and JetBlue. The route covers approximately 3,440 miles (5,536 kilometers), with flights generally taking around eight hours.

However, passengers should distinguish between the airlines operating the route and those offering a dedicated premium economy cabin. British Airways calls its intermediate product World Traveller Plus, Virgin Atlantic markets its cabin as Premium, and American Airlines offers Premium Economy on eligible long-haul aircraft. Delta also operates Premium Select on eligible international services. JetBlue, meanwhile, offers its Mint business class and Even More Space economy seating rather than a conventional dedicated premium economy cabin.

The situation is different for passengers traveling to Newark. According to FlightConnections' LHR-EWR route information, British Airways and United Airlines operate nonstop flights between the two airports: United's intermediate product corresponding to British Airways’ World Traveller Plus is called Premium Plus. The route covers approximately 3,454 miles (5,559 km), with an average flight time of seven hours and 55 minutes.

Such a variety of airlines at JFK makes it attractive to travelers seeking schedule flexibility and competitive fares. Nevertheless, more airline options do not automatically mean cheaper premium economy tickets. British Airways and Virgin Atlantic offer useful examples of what passengers can expect to pay.

A closer look at the price bump that is required to fly in BA's Club cabins.

British Airways and Virgin Atlantic are two of the most prominent competitors on the London-New York market, and both offer dedicated premium economy cabins on their long-haul services. Their current fares demonstrate that passengers departing London can sometimes find considerably more attractive prices than those booking an equivalent journey from the United States.

Virgin Atlantic's official website provides a particularly useful snapshot of its pricing. For a return journey from London Heathrow to New York JFK between November 17 and November 22, 2026, the airline advertised Premium fares starting at approximately $1,529. Its published offers for selected dates in May, July, and August 2027 also showed starting fares of approximately $1,542 to $1,549. These are advertised starting prices, not guaranteed fares for every departure.

The airline's official Premium flight deals page also provides information about its cabin and included services. British Airways, meanwhile, allows passengers to compare World Traveller Plus fares through its official website.

Furthermore, the KAYAK sample for February 5–12, 2027 provides another useful comparison point during low season, with prices slightly lower than those for immediate departures within two weeks, although those dates fall outside the 2026 calendar year. With the fares set, the next question is what passengers get for spending more than £1,000 on a return ticket.

One of the main advantages of premium economy is extra personal space, which can make a big difference on an overnight transatlantic journey or for passengers with health issues. British Airways' World Traveller Plus cabin features wider seats, additional legroom, greater recline, lumbar support, and adjustable headrests and footrests. Passengers also receive an enhanced meal service, complimentary bar service, personal entertainment systems, and priority boarding.

Virgin Atlantic's Premium cabin offers a similarly upgraded experience, although the precise seat specifications depend on the aircraft operating the flight. According to its official cabin information, the airline offers up to 38 inches of seat pitch, additional room to stretch out, and a footrest. Its onboard experience also includes restaurant-inspired meals served on porcelain dishware, a welcome glass of bubbly, and additional snacks on selected aircraft.

Baggage is another important consideration when calculating the actual cost difference between economy and premium economy. Virgin Atlantic Premium includes two checked bags weighing up to 50 pounds (23 kg) each, plus a 22-pound (10-kg) hand luggage allowance. British Airways World Traveller Plus also provides two checked bags on most routes, while some economy fares are hand-baggage-only tickets. According to NerdWallet, adding two checked bags to certain British Airways economy bookings can cost up to $175 each way.

These additional services make premium economy more than simply an upgraded seat. However, the cabin doesn't offer the same experience as business class, especially for sleeping arrangements and airport lounge access. This distinction becomes important when the price difference between the two cabins starts to narrow.

The price difference between premium economy and business class fares is not always huge. Airlines use dynamic pricing systems that adjust ticket prices according to demand, remaining inventory, booking patterns, and the number of seats available in each fare category. As a result, a premium economy ticket can occasionally approach the price of a discounted business class seat, especially when demand for the intermediate cabin is high.

NerdWallet’s British Airways example illustrates this well. One New York JFK departure offered premium economy for $1,162, compared with $532 for economy and $1,222 for business class. In this comparison, choosing premium economy instead of business class saved only $60. For passengers seeking the additional comfort of a fully flat bed on an overnight flight, that relatively small difference could make business class a more attractive option.

Passengers also have another option when considering British Airways: using Avios. According to Award Travel Finder's London-New York redemption analysis, a one-way off-peak World Traveller Plus redemption requires 46,750 Avios, increasing to 66,000 during peak periods. Economy requires 27,500 Avios off-peak and 33,000 during peak periods. Taxes and charges are additional, meaning passengers must compare the monetary cost of an award ticket with the cash fare before deciding whether redemption represents good value.

Available loyalty points and occasional business-class promotions make comparing all three cabins especially important. Nevertheless, for passengers without enough points or who can't find attractive business class fares, premium economy remains an interesting middle ground. The question is whether the added comfort justifies the higher cost on this transatlantic route.

Premium economy occupies an interesting niche in the London-New York market because it provides a noticeable improvement over standard economy, often without the substantial expense of business class. On an approximately eight-hour journey, additional legroom, wider seats, improved dining, and priority airport services can make a meaningful difference for some passengers.

However, the financial case weakens when the fare approaches business-class pricing. A passenger paying $1,162 for premium economy when business class costs $1,222 is arguably better off considering the higher cabin, provided the business class fare is genuinely available for the same itinerary and booking conditions. Conversely, Virgin Atlantic's advertised return fare of $1,529 for November 2026 demonstrates that premium economy can be considerably more accessible than some of the higher-priced examples found in the US market.

Competition also gives passengers some flexibility. Travelers to JFK can compare British Airways, Virgin Atlantic, American Airlines, Delta, and JetBlue, while Newark offers British Airways and United as nonstop options. Checking both New York airports, comparing departure dates, and examining the differences between economy, premium economy, and business class can reveal opportunities that aren't immediately apparent when searching for a single airline.
