AI models regularly misidentify civilians as military targets in tests, report found

📅 Nov 17, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Free daily briefing on global business news.

Anthropic's Claude Opus 5.5 targeted a school in Iran more than any other model tested, according to evaluator Vals

Vals, an independent AI evaluator, published a report on Tuesday showing that frontier AI models flagged civilians, ambulances and surrendering soldiers as military targets in simulated scenarios.

Vals said no request was blocked by a classifier and that refusals from the models themselves were uncommon. Models that did refuse complied after being told they were playing a game. In a battlefield survey task, most models kept going after seeing casualties from earlier strikes.

The researchers ran three tests: drone target identification and a battlefield survey, both based on the war in Ukraine, and a satellite imagery test of Minab, Iran, where a school sits next to a military complex. The United States bombed the school on Feb. 28, killing over 150 civilians, mostly children, Vals said.

In the satellite test, Anthropic's Claude Opus 5.5 targeted the school more than any other model, Vals said. The model picked the school in 22 of 32 tests, whereas no OpenAI model chose it even once, according to Bloomberg. When the prompt framed the images as coming from a game, the Anthropic model chose the school on 23 occasions, and three OpenAI models chose it on 9, 11 and 13 occasions.

Vals said Claude Opus 4.5, released in November 2025, gave at least one target in 6% of satellite trials, compared with 92% for Opus 5.5. OpenAI models' targeting rate stayed low.

Vals said it did not evaluate targeting accuracy, compliance with the law of armed conflict or performance in real U.S. military workflows. It also said it did not establish how effective the models would be in a deployed weapons system. The firm recommended that AI labs add further protective measures and train their models on alignment.

An Anthropic spokesperson said the report's examples run "very clearly" counter to company policy, and that further safeguards are due to take effect shortly, according to Bloomberg. OpenAI said in a statement shared by Vals that it is working on alignment updates covering drones and their software systems, including assistance framed as simulation, gaming or academic or defensive use.

A Google $GOOGL spokesperson said the company's models were not built with such uses in mind, according to Bloomberg. A Pentagon official said the department declines to weigh in on reports produced by outside parties.

Vals said it disclosed its findings on Nov. 1 to OpenAI, Anthropic, Google, Meta $META and SpaceXAI and held publication for two weeks.

The Pentagon designated Anthropic a national security supply chain risk in March, after contract talks collapsed over the company's request for guarantees that Claude would not be used in autonomous lethal weapons systems or for domestic mass surveillance.

In a September report on misuse of its models, Anthropic flagged attempts to enlist Claude in developing software for missiles, armed drones and bombs. The company said it banned the accounts tied to the activity it identified.

After 3 Marathons Without a Plan, I Finally Followed One. Here’s What Happened in NYC.

📅 Nov 17, 2026  |  Category: Sports  |  Read Source →

My 3:45 goal didn’t go to plan, but the NYC Marathon still delivered a win.

The first time I entered the New York City Marathon drawing, I got in. I’m not sure what possessed me that day to apply, having never run another race in my life. I figured I’d never get in, so why not? I played soccer growing up, and when the world shut down during COVID-19, running became the easiest way to get out of the house and see my friends. That’s where my running journey began.

My NYC Marathon training also happened during my senior year of college, which brought its own challenges. While I was excited to run, I also didn’t want to miss out on any of the socializing that happened that year. I did my best to make it to every party, every club soccer game, and anything in between while running as much as possible. I was scared that running was going to infringe on my everyday life and I wouldn’t have time to keep having fun. Despite all of that, I had caught the marathon bug and after finishing the New York City Marathon in 2022, I went on to run the Paris and Chicago Marathons in 2024.

While training for those first few marathons, I didn’t have any real plan, except to not let it interfere with my social calendar. My strategy consisted of building up mileage in order to reach 20 miles about four weeks out from race day. In between, I pieced together training information from influencers on Instagram and Google searches (this was pre-ChatGPT!). Now that I’m a Runner’s World+ member, I have all those resources right at my fingertips.

Although I caught the marathon bug almost instantly, I also felt defeated after New York. I knew I could have done better. Between New York City in 2022 and Paris in 2024, I never ran more than 10 miles at a time, but I was running enough that I knew I could do it again if I really wanted. I followed a similar approach for Chicago in 2024: Build up to one long run about a month out, then fill the rest of the weeks with shorter runs and one medium-length run. It wasn’t much of a plan, but it worked for me. My times kept improving, and I broke four hours for the first time in Chicago.

Looking back, I realize that my no-plan approach was partly a way to protect myself. If I never said my goal out loud, I couldn’t feel disappointed if I missed it. Breaking four hours in Chicago changed that. For the first time, I wanted to see what I could do with a real plan.

I was now living in the city I had expected to call home ever since I left college, so I really wanted this race to feel meaningful. For the first time in my marathon career, I downloaded a Runner’s World training plan, the 16-week Break 3:45 plan to be exact, printed it out, and started tracking all of my runs. I wanted to beat my time in Chicago and get as close to 3 hours and 45 minutes as possible.

In the beginning, this felt super daunting. I had never done an interval run in my life nor had I ever intentionally sought out hills to make my runs feel harder than they already did. Nonetheless, I found my old Garmin Forerunner 55 and learned how to upload intervals to run up and down the West Side Highway. When the plan called for hills, I made my way back to the Queensboro Bridge for the first time since 2022, ready to face it with excitement instead of fear. Even while pretending to be excited, the incline is no joke!

For a while, I really felt like I had gotten the hang of things. I actually started to recognize what different paces felt like during the intervals, instead of sprinting and pulling back and looking at my watch while I ran. And, I could now confidently run over many of the bridges in New York City, notably the Williamsburg and Manhattan, even though they aren’t even part of the marathon course. I wasn’t following the plan exactly, but I was able to reorganize the days of the week to hit all the workouts.

Near the end of August (roughly week eight of the plan), it became hot all the time, I was constantly traveling for weekend trips, and I had started to lose the excitement of running. So while I was making sure to hit all of the long runs, the interval training fell off completely. I was a little disappointed in myself because the interval training runs were workouts that made me feel like I was getting stronger each week. I had never done this type of workout before, so I could tell I was getting better each time.

To find the joy of running again, I really started to lean on my friends. Every week, I’d send out a few texts to see who was available and wanted to go for a run with me. I ran the loop in Central Park almost every week and met up with my friends in Brooklyn to run every Friday morning. I started to explore parts of the city I had never been to before, which helped ground me in my training and remember that running is supposed to be fun and is an exciting way to explore the city.

From my previous experience, I knew that running the long runs was the most important thing for me to feel confident on race day. So much of marathon running is mental, so knowing that I could run 20 miles (and have run 26.2 before), was enough for me. So, while I started with a plan, I started reverting back to my tried-and-true method—the just make sure you run the long runs plan.

Outside of the training plan itself, I leaned on Runner’s World a lot. I didn’t have access to this in the past so I didn’t feel confident about planning for prerun meals or even how to take gels during the race. I also found that being able to read runner stories online made me feel more connected to the community, which helped me mentally with my training.

Plan or no plan, I’m proud of myself! My official time was 3:57:39, so while I did not achieve my goal of breaking 3:45, I did run a sub-4 race, and following the Runner’s World plan helped me get there. The last time I ran this race, I was a senior in college and thought I had it all figured out. I was going to move to New York City, get a real adult job, and start the life I had been imagining. Similar to my experience following the Runner’s World plan, this is not how everything went. Instead, I moved to a different city after graduating, eventually found my way back to New York, and my life is so much more full than I ever expected because of it. Maybe things aren’t always supposed to go to plan, and that’s a good thing!

EU, ASEAN pursue deeper trade ties, but hurdles remain as businesses struggle with informal barriers

📅 Nov 17, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

2027 could be a landmark year for EU–Southeast Asia trade, with several deals nearing completion. But the harder task will be to ensure that businesses actually use them.

The European Union and the Association of Southeast Asian Nations (ASEAN) could be heading for a breakthrough year in 2027, when the two sides mark 50 years of diplomatic ties.

A long-awaited trade agreement with Indonesia is expected to take effect, while a deal with the Philippines is nearing completion and negotiations with Thailand and Malaysia are making progress. By the end of next year, the EU could have trade deals in place with six Southeast Asian states, up from just two today.

The anniversary could also revive the long-standing ambition of a broader trade agreement between the EU and ASEAN.

On November 17, the European Commission hosted a dialogue in Brussels to discuss trade relations with Southeast Asia, including progress on existing and pending free trade agreements (FTAs).

But attention is turning to what comes next. "Finalizing FTAs is only the beginning, not the end," Chris Humphrey, executive director of the EU-ASEAN Business Council, told DW.

FTAs can reduce tariffs and remove trade restrictions, but that is of little use if businesses don't know how to use them, he added.

"Unfortunately, in many cases, this is where there is a failure. Communication and training are key," Humphrey said.

For Brussels, the priority should now be to tackle the less conspicuous obstacles to trade, he stressed.

Cutting customs delays, simplifying regulations, and helping smaller European businesses enter unfamiliar Southeast Asian markets will determine whether an FTA succeeds.

"European companies with little or no experience in Southeast Asia would benefit from greater on-the-ground support, particularly practical, firsthand guidance on informal business practices and the unwritten local rules," Alfred Gerstl, an associate professor at Palacky University Olomouc in the Czech Republic, told DW.

One option is to expand the assistance offered by European chambers of commerce, trade delegations, and EU offices in Southeast Asia. This could range from helping firms find distributors to explaining local licensing requirements and how to qualify for preferential tariffs.

In Vietnam, for instance, sustained pressure from Brussels has helped improve market access for European pharmaceutical firms. In Indonesia, the EU has worked on trade facilitation while pressing Jakarta over import restrictions and halal certification requirements, said Camille Nessel, a researcher at the Universite Libre de Bruxelles.

Regular consultations with businesses on both sides could also help Brussels spot problems before they become trade disputes.

Trade policy has also acquired a geopolitical purpose in Brussels. "If trade policy is expected to reduce strategic dependencies, we are asking it to deliver on objectives that remain insufficiently defined, let alone demonstrating how to achieve them," Nessel told DW.

The EU wants to reduce its dependence on a handful of suppliers for the minerals needed for batteries and other clean technologies, while Southeast Asian producers, particularly Indonesia, want foreign investors to help develop domestic processing industries rather than merely buy raw materials.

The EU and Indonesia have already clashed over Jakarta's restrictions on nickel exports.

European investment in processing facilities, agreements on environmental standards, and greater regulatory certainty would give both sides concrete gains, said Nessel. "The real value of an FTA is not just opening a market but giving the EU the leverage to keep it open," she added.

Pasha Hsieh, Lee Kong Chian Professor of Law and Jean Monnet Chair at Singapore Management University, said Brussels should prioritize ratifying its investment protection agreements with Singapore and Vietnam.

Signed in 2018 and 2019, respectively, the agreements still require ratification by all EU member states before entering into force. "These delays underline the need to follow through on negotiated commitments," Hsieh said.

The issue is particularly relevant as the EU looks to develop investment protocols in other Southeast Asian states where investors have sometimes faced uncertainty over legal protections. Indonesia, for instance, has terminated numerous bilateral investment treaties, including agreements with the Netherlands and Spain.

A larger question is whether the EU should continue pursuing bilateral deals with Southeast Asian countries or revive its original ambition of a region-to-region agreement with ASEAN.

The EU and ASEAN launched negotiations for a region-wide FTA in 2007 but suspended them two years later amid disagreements over wide economic disparities among ASEAN members and political concerns. Technical discussions resumed in 2017, but little has come of them.

ASEAN already has regional agreements with China and several other economies. Beijing upgraded its free trade agreement with ASEAN in October 2025.

Does It Matter That We’ve Taken the “Mormon” Out of the Mormon Wives?

There is something about hearing “come ye saints, come ye sinners” that sets off a Pavlovian response in my brain; a physical trigger to sling the (dirty) Diet Coke back and turn the volume up. It means The Secret Lives of Mormon Wives is back, this time with its OC-set spinoff: The Secret Lives of Mormon Wives: Orange County.

In the show’s marketing, Hulu leans into the California setting as the clear divide between SLOMW: OC and its Utah predecessor. SLOMW: OC isn’t as ensconced in the shadow of the Mormon church as the Utah show is. Instead, it offers a more laid-back version of Mormonism (they drink! they get tattoos! they’re in throuples!). But in taking the “Mormon” out of the “Mormon Wives,” are we missing out on what makes the show so interesting?

It’s a bold choice to greenlight a spinoff about Mormons without actually casting any Mormons. In fact, of the eight new cast members (Jen Affleck is the only familiar face), only four identify as practicing Mormons at all: Mayci Neely’s identical-looking sister McCall DaPron, McCall’s bestie Jordan McClanahan, YouTuber Madison Bontempo, and Ashleigh Pease.

Meanwhile, the other half of the cast have either left the church altogether, as is the case with influencers Aspyn Ovard and Salome Andea, or were never religious to begin with like podcasters Avery Woods and Bobbi Althoff. It’s the “Molly Mormons,” or traditional Mormons, vs. the “Black Cats,” the rebels of the group. What connects these women is not so much a shared religion as a shared career path: influencing and podcasting.

SLOMW Utah’s strength was its portrayal of the contradictory rules of Mormonism. For example, what do you mean it goes against the church to drink caffeine but it’s totally fine to get high off laughing gas at a Botox appointment?? The rules only got more interesting as the show progressed and the women went from housewives to reality TV megastars overnight. Suddenly they were grappling with bigger religious conundrums: how to be both subordinate in the church and a breadwinner at home.

SLOMW: OC takes a different approach by focusing more on influencer mom drama, shifting away from the central tension of the show: women striving for independence and equality while navigating deeply patriarchal relationships. Take Mayci in season 5 when she fends off a fourth pregnancy request from her mother, sister, and husband (keep in mind, she just popped out baby #3!). Or Jessi, whose husband Jordan felt emasculated by the number of zeros in her paycheck.

In the Orange County-set spinoff, the men are curiously absent. Madison, Salome, and Aspyn are the only women to feature their romantic partners on screen—and even then it’s only a few moments of the show’s eight-episode run. The absence of the men feels like a strategic response to the DadTok hate that plagued the Utah show. But Hulu may have mistaken our TFP relationship fatigue for not wanting to see messy relationships at all.

Instead the show has channeled all that messy relationship tension into the women’s dynamics with each other. Their fights originate from the different ways they interpret feminism, religion, money, and power. Take McCall and Bobbi, for instance. McCall, who sees the group’s religious divide as her “McCalling” to convert, is the clear villain of season 1 because she tries to center Mormonism in the show. It’s Bobbi, hoodie pulled up and strings pulled tight, who brings McCall back down to earth and challenges her to see her faith through different perspectives.

“Show me the [bible] verse that says to be a bitch and talk shit on people, please,” Bobbi throws out in episode 4 after learning McCall made a dig about her comedy show and threatened to have another cast member replaced for not piling on Bobbi too. Later in the season, Bobbi confronts McCall over her drama with Aspyn Ovard and urges her to rethink her mean girl internet campaign. She points out that McCall might not agree with all of Aspyn’s life decisions, but Aspyn has a terminally ill child; McCall should be the bigger, more religious woman and show her some grace.

If the show wants to lean away from messy marriages, then it needs to lean into the messiness of religion in friendships. What are the boundaries that divide? What are the boundaries that unite? Without the religious tension, the show becomes hot, rich women fighting in public, and there are stronger contenders in that genre (hello, Real Housewives).

Maybe taking the “Mormon” out of “Mormon Wives” will rejuvenate a flailing brand, but the religious tension has to stay a priority regardless of the women’s religious affiliations. Taking the “Mormon” out of “Mormon Wives” is bad for the show—and bad for the mess!

Cursor Traces Malicious Claude Code Extension to Third-Party Breach

📅 Nov 17, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

A compromised key issued to AI infrastructure provider Fireworks was used by threat actors to access Cursor data and credentials.

Cursor has traced the origins of a security incident involving a malicious version of Anthropic's Claude Code extension in its marketplace to a breach at AI infrastructure provider Fireworks.

As many as 5,000 users may have downloaded the tampered extension during the roughly 11 hours it was live on Oct. 29. It was a reminder of the risk organizations face when they rely on third-party marketplaces for software applications and extensions. It also illustrates how one security lapse can lead to a cascade of subsequent breaches and supply chain attacks.

When Cursor first disclosed the incident, the company said attackers had placed malicious versions of Anthropic's Claude Code extension in a cache serving its Visual Studio (VS) Code extension marketplace. Users who requested either of two specific cached versions of the extension (2.1.312 and 2.1.313) during the exposure window may have downloaded the malicious builds, the company said. The malicious extensions attempted to collect information about infected systems and establish persistent remote access, including by installing an attacker-controlled SSH key.

Related:Grok Bots Attempt to Access Corporate Data Without User Approval

Cursor quickly removed the malicious extensions, temporarily shut down its marketplace, and audited the store for signs of further tampering. Cursor also rotated its keys, rebuilt the marketplace, and worked with the cloud provider hosting the attacker's server to take it offline. The company's initial advisory, however, did not explain how the attacker might have gained access to its environment and planted the malicious extension in its extensions marketplace.

In an update on Nov. 13, Cursor filled in that blank, with a detailed description of what its investigation had unearthed in the days following the Oct. 29 incident.

The intrusion, according to Cursor, began when an attacker obtained an access key the company had issued to Fireworks, an AI startup based in San Mateo, Calif. Cursor said "the key was exposed by Fireworks," and the attacker used it to copy data from a read-only storage bucket containing historical coding-agent session records belonging to Cursor users. Most of the records were from 2025 and involved sessions that were not in Privacy Mode and that Cursor was permitted to use for model training purposes.

Some of the stored session records contained credentials that had not been properly redacted. The attacker used those credentials to access what Cursor described as select SaaS tools for accessing non-production source code repositories, customer support data, emails, usage metrics, and other data.

On Oct. 29, the attacker subsequently used one of the compromised credentials to tamper with the cache that Cursor uses to deliver extensions through its VS Code marketplace. The attacker inserted unauthorized copies of Anthropic's Claude Code extension in the marketplace where they remained available to users for about 11 hours.

"Anthropic did not publish this copy, and this was not a vulnerability in Anthropic's product," Cursor stressed in its update. "The marketplace source, Open VSX, never had the malware. At most 5,000 users who requested specific cached versions of the extension on October 29 potentially received the malicious build."

Cursor estimated that API credentials belonging to fewer than 0.01% of its customers were exposed in the incident. Historical coding-agent session records, or traces, belonging to fewer than 0.05% of users were accessed, the company said.

However, the supply chain attack goes back even further than Fireworks, which itself was a victim of a third-party breach. In a Nov. 9 update, Fireworks said a cloud provider hosting the AI company's R&D systems "experienced a security incident on Oct. 23." The attackers used the access to the R&D systems to obtain sensitive data, including API keys and access tokens.

According to Fireworks, the available evidence linked the breach of its environment to a financially motivated cybercriminal group seeking to monetize unauthorized access to large language models. It's a practice known as LLMjacking, which Google and others have described as a rapidly emerging threat for organizations across multiple sectors in North America, Asia-Pacific, and other regions.

Following the incident, Cursor said it has implemented a broad security overhaul to reduce the risk of similar compromises. Immediate steps the company has taken include cutting off Firework's access to all its shared data sets, revoking and replacing potentially exposed credentials, and rotating internal credentials. The company has also tightened restrictions on how its systems and third-party service providers can communicate with one another and expanded automated scanning for secrets across its code repositories and internal data.

The Cursor incident is another indication of how the rapid adoption of AI-powered development tools and increasingly interconnected software ecosystems has exacerbated software supply chain risks. Recently, for instance, researchers uncovered more than 550 exposed secrets in Microsoft VS Code and Open VSX extension packages, including over 100 publishing tokens that could have allowed attackers to distribute malicious updates to legitimate extensions. Similarly, earlier this year researchers identified dozens of malicious GlassWorm VS Code extensions seeded in the Open VSX marketplace to steal developer credentials that attackers could use to spread poisoned software packages.
