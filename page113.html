3 Super Growth Stocks to Buy Hand Over Fist If the Stock Market Crashes

📅 Nov 09, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

One of these stocks is up 265% over the past year -- and its shares don't seem wildly overvalued, either.

Is a market crash imminent? No one knows for sure, but on the day that I'm writing this, the S&P 500 has pulled back by more than 4% -- a big move. Will that continue? We can't know. A pullback of some sort, potentially triggering a recession for a while, is likely at some point, though, because crashes and corrections simply happen every few years or so -- and several indicators are pointing to a crash.

You shouldn't keep any short-term money (funds you expect to need within the coming five, if not 10, years) in stocks, and if you're following that guideline, you should plan to just ride out any downturns. Better still, downturns present great buying opportunities. Here are some growth stocks to consider buying the next time the market corrects.

Eli Lilly (LLY -0.79%), home to popular GLP-1 weight-loss drugs such as Mounjaro and Zepbound, has been a blockbuster performer, averaging annual stock price gains of nearly 40% over the past five years. (It's up some 57% over the past year, too, as of early November.) It's no surprise, then, that the stock seems somewhat overvalued, with a recent price-to-sales (P/S) ratio of 14.7, above the five-year average of 13.3.

You could invest in it now if you plan to hold for many years, but buying after a market pullback will get you in at a lower price and boost your eventual gains.

The company is performing quite well lately, posting a 39% year-over-year increase in revenue for its third quarter while management upped its projections for the full year. It's true that its pricing has gone down a bit, but that was more than made up for by increased volume. Lilly also faces competition, but it remains a major player in the GLP-1 world and is expanding there, too, such as via its oral drug Foundayo.

Of course, Lilly is not solely a GLP-1 specialist. It's also home to Kisunla for Alzheimer's disease, Ebglyss for eczema, and cancer drug Jaypirca, among other medications.

Micron Technology (MU +5.12%) is a major semiconductor company specializing in memory and storage chips. It's been posting boffo gains lately, as it supplies memory chips used in artificial intelligence (AI) processing, and AI has been growing briskly. Micron's shares have averaged annual gains of 47% over the past decade, much of that due to outsize gains in recent years. Over the past year, for example, shares are up 265%!

Should you invest in Micron? It's certainly tempting, especially for long-term investors. The shares are even down some 26% over the past month (as of early November). That's due in large part to competition from Chinese company CXMT, as well as worries that the AI and data center build-out might take a breather. But such worries may be overblown, with rival chipmaker SK Hynix seeing a memory shortage persisting through 2030.

Meanwhile, Micron shares seem reasonably valued or a bit undervalued at recent levels, with a forward price-to-earnings (P/E) ratio of 5, below the five-year average of 11. If you hold out for a market pullback (which may happen this year but could happen later), you'll be getting in at an even more attractive point.

Then there's Nvidia (NVDA +2.77%), the semiconductor titan with a recent market value of $4.4 trillion. It actually seems attractively valued right now, with a recent forward P/E ratio of 13, well below the five-year average of 33. So you might buy into it now if you like what you see after further research.

Shares have averaged annual gains of 52% over the past 15 years, and 65% over the past three years. Growth is likely to continue, though possibly at somewhat slower rates. Nvidia is the biggest supplier for AI model training and inference, and it's expanding its scope to include software, networking, and services.

The company will be reporting its third-quarter results on Nov. 17, and Nvidia has guided for revenue to more than double year over year. If it outperforms, shares could get a nice boost, and if it doesn't, well, arguably, its shares are already down in expectation of that.

Take a closer look at any of these stocks that interest you, and as long as you plan to hold for years, you might buy some shares sooner rather than later. Otherwise, add them to your watch list.

TL;DR: Core Scientific reported $215.4 million in third-quarter colocation revenue, nearly 60% above the prior quarter, while billing 485 MW of capacity. Its results show how an operator once known for Bitcoin mining is building a much larger business around contracted AI infrastructure.

Some Bitcoin mining companies have talked about their artificial-intelligence ambitions for years. Core Scientific’s latest financials show what that transition looks like once facilities begin producing substantial revenue from customers.

According to its November 5 earnings release, the company generated $215.4 million of colocation revenue in the third quarter of 2026, up from $136.7 million in the second quarter and $15 million a year earlier. Total quarterly revenue reached $232.9 million.

Core Scientific said it was billing for 485 megawatts as of September 30, representing more than $700 million in average annualised colocation GAAP revenue. That is a different unit of operating performance from hashrate or Bitcoin produced. It describes contracted power and computing infrastructure delivered to tenants.

The move is important because mining revenue depends on several volatile factors at once: Bitcoin price, network difficulty, block rewards and electricity costs. Colocation can offer a different mix of long-term contracts, infrastructure obligations and construction spending. The income stream may be easier to forecast after a facility is delivered, but the capital commitment can be enormous.

Core Scientific says it is approaching full delivery under its first colocation agreement and is constructing capacity under a second agreement across five campuses. Those claims are meaningful milestones, yet remaining construction work and billing ramps still matter to the final financial outcome.

The company reported approximately $1.18 billion in quarterly net income, but said the result benefited from a non-cash change in the fair value of warrants. That makes the headline profit figure a poor stand-in for recurring cash generated by running data centres.

Adjusted EBITDA was $133.6 million, more than three times the previous quarter’s $41.1 million, while capital expenditure reached $985.1 million. Both figures deserve attention. A rapidly growing operating business can still consume substantial cash as it adds the facilities needed to earn future contracted revenue.

This is a useful counterpoint to the more familiar NewsBTC discussion of Bitcoin miners’ AI pivot. The opportunity is becoming measurable, but comparing revenue without spending, financing and delivery schedules would make the transformation look easier than it is.

Investors should follow the number of megawatts actually billing, customer diversification, operating margins, remaining capital needs and any modifications to lease terms. A nominal pipeline is less revealing than a site whose systems have been accepted by a paying tenant.

The results also offer a warning against treating every company in this sector as an identical bet on Bitcoin’s price. Core Scientific now has a material infrastructure business whose fortunes may depend more on demand for AI compute and construction execution than on daily mining profitability. Its history remains part of the story, but the metrics are changing underneath it.

Lululemon Athletica vs. On: Which Consumer Stock Is a Better Buy for 2027?

📅 Nov 09, 2026  |  Category: Business  |  Read Source →

Lululemon's steady profits stand in contrast to On's blistering revenue growth, but its declining sales raise a key question.

Investors face a choice between established market leadership and high-growth momentum when comparing Lululemon Athletica (LULU -0.88%) and On (ONON -0.48%). Both companies are vying for dominance in the competitive athletic category.

Lululemon is known for its high-end yoga and technical apparel, whereas On has built its reputation on innovative running footwear. This comparison explores their financial health and market positioning to determine the better long-term pick.

Lululemon Athletica designs and sells technical athletic apparel, footwear, and accessories through an omni-channel model. It reaches customers via more than 800 company-operated stores and a robust e-commerce platform across multiple countries.

The company also leverages wholesale channels by partnering with fitness studios and university retailers. This diversified approach helps it maintain high brand visibility and reach a loyal customer base in the apparel sector.

In FY 2025, revenue reached nearly $11.1 billion, representing revenue growth of approximately 4.9% compared to the prior year. Net income for the period was roughly $1.6 billion.

On develops and distributes premium performance sports products, primarily focusing on its innovative footwear technology. As of the end of 2025, the company operated nearly 70 retail locations worldwide and maintains a strong presence through wholesale partners and its direct-to-consumer e-commerce platform.

Its growth strategy emphasizes global expansion, with a significant retail footprint already established in China. The company relies on its high-performance brand image to attract runners and lifestyle consumers willing to pay a premium price.

In FY 2025, revenue reached roughly $3.7 billion, which reflects impressive revenue growth. Net income for the fiscal period was approximately $246 million.

Lululemon Athletica faces several legal and operational hurdles, including multiple class action lawsuits regarding its financial guidance and brand health. The company is also navigating a management transition at the CEO position. External factors like changes in U.S. tariff policy and supply chain concentration in Asia add further uncertainty. Increased competition from rivals that can quickly imitate technical designs may also pressure its historical brand premium.

On must navigate the challenges of scaling a global logistics network while competing against much larger footwear giants like Nike. Maintaining its premium pricing and brand exclusivity becomes more difficult as the company moves into broader wholesale distribution. Inventory management is a critical risk, as any miscalculation in demand could lead to heavy discounting that hurts net margin. The company also remains sensitive to shifts in consumer discretionary spending across its primary markets in Europe and North America.

Lululemon currently offers a more conservative valuation profile, while On carries a premium multiple that reflects its significantly faster revenue growth trajectory.

Each company provides slightly different offerings, but both are generally in the fitness and activewear segment. Both are considered premium brands and can struggle when consumers tighten their budgets. They may like Lululemon pants or shoes from On, but in tighter times, a consumer may just trade down to a different brand with similar items at lower prices. That said, there's still opportunity to find the right stocks in that sector of the market. According to Fortune Business Insights, the global sportswear market is expected to grow from $235 billion in 2026 to $402.5 billion by 2034.

For this battle, I would choose On for one main reason. The reason is that On is more in the opening stretch of its journey, with less than 100 stores. In comparison, Lululemon ended 2025 with 811 stores, and it's transitioning into a more mature company, looking for what its next stage will look like. Also, you can see it in the sales growth. For the second quarter of 2026, On reported that revenue increased 13.5%, while Lululemon reported net revenue decreased by 4%. Ultimately, anyone interested in On should dig into its financials a bit more before considering it as an investment. Still, based solely on a comparison with Lululemon, I'd rather own On at this moment in time.

Austin Reaves steps up in Luka Doncic's absence to lead Lakers over Pistons

📅 Nov 09, 2026  |  Category: Sports  |  Read Source →

Austin Reaves raised three fingers to the rafters. Then, after hitting a clutch three-pointer with 46 seconds remaining against the Detroit Pistons on Sunday, he turned his palm to fans sitting courtside at Little Caesers Arena and waved goodbye.

The Lakers guard scored a season-high 41 points as L.A. Lakers survived a 121-116 win over the Pistons without star point guard Luka Doncic. The Lakers (9-2) led by 26 early in the second quarter, but nearly collapsed under an avalanche of second-half turnovers and 53 combined points from Detroit bench guards Daniss Jenkins and Kevin Huerter.

Lakers center Walker Kessler had 12 points and 16 rebounds for his fifth double-double in six games. He crossed into double-digit scoring with a clutch put-back three-point play with 2:15 remaining to put the Lakers ahead by seven.

On the front side of a back-to-back, Doncic missed his second game of the season with low back tightness, although Lakers coach JJ Redick said he didn't anticipate it to affect Doncic significantly throughout the season.

The Slovenian superstar is averaging a league-high 40.1 points per game, but Doncic was doing more than just scoring at rates not seen since Wilt Chamberlain. His estimated on/off of 37.7 — the difference between the Lakers' net rating when he was on the court compared to off — was the second-highest in the league, following only Victor Wembanyama's 41.5. In addition to having the highest scoring average through nine games since Chamberlain, Doncic is the team's second-leading rebounder at 7.8 per game and is second with 1.7 steals.

But the Lakers already showed they could compete without Doncic this season. They dominated the Clippers on Oct. 28 when he was sidelined, also with back tightness.

Just as as did against the Clippers, the Lakers needed contributions from everyone. Ziaire Williams hit four three-pointers and finished with 17 points. Collin Sexton, serving as the Lakers' second ball-handler, had 17 points on five-of-10 shooting.

Jarred Vanderbilt's energetic six rebounds, two assists and one block in less than six minutes during the first quarter helped lift the Lakers to a 26-point lead early in the second.

The Pistons, playing without All-NBA center Jalen Duren (hamstring), didn't get their first points from star point guard Cade Cunningham until early in the third quarter. Instead, Jenkins nearly mounted the comeback on his own.

Jenkins, who averages 8.6 points across his career, should be a familiar Lakers foe. Last season, on L.A.'s lone trip to Detroit, the guard who went undrafted in 2024 scored a then-career-high 30 points against the Lakers to end their season-long nine-game winning streak.

Jenkins scored 22 points of his new career-high 31 in the first half, cutting the Lakers' 26-point lead to 13 by halftime. Reaves had 13 points in the third quarter, including five straight on difficult fadeway jumpers that pushed the lead to 17 with 6:41 to go in the third, and watched the near collapse from the sideline.
