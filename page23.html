Russia Opens Legal Bitcoin, Ethereum and USDT Trading to Retail Investors Under New Rules

📅 Nov 19, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Russia finally allowed retail access to Bitcoin, Ethereum, and USDT on regulated exchange operators under its new rules that took effect today, Nov. 17.

Russia has finally opened its crypto market for retail customers as its central bank today enabled trading in Bitcoin, Ethereum, and USDT for the general public. With the law signed by President Vladimir Putin, the Bank of Russia officially rolled out BTC, ETH, and USDT for public trading on domestic exchanges, beginning Nov. 17, 2026.

The new regulation, announced by the Bank of Russia last week, took effect today, Nov. 17. The move allows individual customers to purchase these assets through intermediaries, including brokers, exchanges, and authorized crypto marketplaces. This means ordinary investors can now buy up to 300,000 rubles ($3,700) in a year through a single broker, exchange, or asset manager, after passing a mandatory test.

The announcement expands access to regulated crypto trading in the country and connects the assets with TradFi. This allows non-qualified investors to buy and trade BTC, ETH, and USDT directly through traditional exchanges and clearing systems.

However, qualified investors are not subject to such asset limits and trading restrictions.

The development points out that Russia has taken decisive action to deepen the integration of the crypto market with traditional finance. The debut indicates surging retail interest in cryptocurrency amid regulatory evolution.

It signals a strategic shift in how traditional exchanges handle digital assets. Accommodating retail clients signals growing confidence in the crypto market’s maturity. This highlights rising demand for coordinated exposure to crypto assets within Russia’s capital markets.

The announcement introduces new entry gateways that give retail investors organized access to the crypto market, redefining how these assets are traded locally and internationally.

In the past, crypto in Russia operated in a highly restrictive, complex regulatory environment.  Now, the regulator is moving toward a balance between investor freedom and financial stability. In July, the central bank opened the door to crypto for companies to facilitate global trade. This month, it introduced new legislation that allows retail investors to trade crypto within specified limits.

Roberto De Zerbi Ready to Unleash Mykhailo Mudryk for Tottenham vs Ipswich

📅 Nov 19, 2026  |  Category: Other  |  Read Source →

Tottenham Hotspur have had a very mixed start to their Premier League season so far, but they will be desperate to get back on the winning trail against Ipswich Town this weekend.

The north London side saw a run of three consecutive victories brought to an end prior to the international break as they fell to a 1-0 defeat to Leeds at Elland Road, which left them 11th in the table.

But Roberto De Zerbi's side will now have a long stretch of games to put together some impressive performances and pick up some much-needed points to climb the table, and also convince the fans that the Italian is definitely the right man to be in charge of this project after consecutive 17th-placed finishes.

One of the biggest problems that Spurs have had so far in this campaign is scoring goals. The team have managed to score just 10 goals in their 10 Premier League games so far, with summer signing Jan Paul van Hecke the only player to have scored more than once.

That includes central striker Dominic Solanke, who has played in all 10 games but so far managed just one goal, an injury-time equaliser against Man Utd at Old Trafford.

The England international has really struggled to find a place in De Zerbi's system that suits him, with his overall play not good enough to link, and his finishing far from consistent enough to make him being a guaranteed starter make sense.

Considering Tottenham splashed big money on Savio and Omar Marmoush in the summer to bolster the attack too, there is considerable competition for places now. Mohammed Kudus has three assists so far, more than anyone else in the squad, while James Maddison has been a key player, Mathys Tel has impressed, and Dejan Kulusevski has returned from a serious knee injury.

Youngster Luca Williams-Barnett has also impressed in his brief cameos off the bench, and it feels like a matter of time before the manager decides to cut his losses on Solanke as a starter in the number nine role.

Both Tel and Marmoush are capable of playing there, and with summer signing Mykhailo Mudryk expected to be fit to finally be available, De Zerbi is surely now ready to unleash him on the Premier League and get him into the side.

De Zerbi and Mudryk worked together at Shakhtar Donetsk, prior to his move to Chelsea, and the Italian admitted that he felt the Ukraine international has the potential to win the Ballon d'Or in the future. Having that talent available isn't an option from the bench when you need to score more goals, and a start is surely not far away.

The talented winger has featured for just 16 minutes so far this season before he picked up a muscle injury, but he is now believed to be closing in on a return.

Speaking this week, the 25-year-old admitted that he was hungry to get back to his best, having been told by Blues boss Xabi Alonso that he needed to impress out on loan.

"He [Alonso] came up and told me straight to my face, 'You’d better go, play, get some game time, and then come back to Chelsea. I’m counting on you."

Having a player who knows the system, is eager to impress, and has pace to burn sounds like it could be the perfect remedy for Spurs' attacking issues. It also gets Solanke out of the firing line, and the starting lineup, which would be a boost too.

Spurs have got more than enough talent to start scoring goals, and this is a chance after the international break to return to winning ways, and for Mudryk to hit the ground running for his new team, and help his manager get out of a hole.

Africa's Digital Progress Accelerates, but 64% Usage Gap Shows Next Challenge for Policymakers

📅 Nov 19, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

GSMA Digital Africa Index 2026 finds seven more countries joining the continent's leading group for digital development and enabling policy

CAPE TOWN, South Africa, Nov. 19, 2026 /PRNewswire/ -- Africa is making measurable progress on digital development and enabling policy, but turning widespread mobile broadband coverage into meaningful digital participation remains a critical challenge, according to the GSMA's flagship Digital Africa Index 2026, launched today at the GSMA Digital Africa Summit Cape Town.

Mobile broadband networks now cover 92% of Africa's population, yet only 28% use mobile internet on their own devices, leaving a 64% usage gap. The cost of internet-enabled devices limited digital skills, and a lack of relevant local content and services remain significant barriers. While the usage gap is a global challenge, it is largest in Africa, making accelerating adoption particularly urgent.

The Index, drawing on data from 54 countries, also points to encouraging policy momentum. Ten African countries now combine stronger digital development with more enabling policy and regulation, up from three in 2024. Egypt, Ghana, Nigeria, Rwanda, Senegal, Seychelles and Tunisia have joined Kenya, Mauritius and South Africa in the Index's leading group.

Angela Wamola, Head of Africa, GSMA, said: "Africa has made real progress in expanding mobile broadband coverage. The challenge now is ensuring that more people can actually use it. With 64% of the population living within coverage but not using mobile internet, closing the usage gap requires us to tackle the barriers that continue to keep people offline.

"South Africa shows what targeted reform can achieve. Making smartphones more affordable, building digital skills and ensuring people have access to relevant, trusted digital services can help turn connectivity into meaningful economic and social participation."

Device affordability remains a key barrier. South Africa demonstrates how targeted policy reform can support adoption: after removing the 9% excise duty on smartphones priced below R2,500 in April 2025, monthly sales of entry-level smartphones increased by 80%, from around 250,000 in March 2025 to around 460,000 in February 2026. The GSMA is urging governments where duties or sales taxes still apply to entry-level smartphones to remove those charges.

The Index calls for coordinated action across five areas: affordable and reliable connectivity; public value from digital services; trust and accountability; inclusive digital service design; and secure, connected government systems.

First, Slow Horses was renewed for Seasons 9 and 10, meaning we’ll be getting at least four more seasons of the hit show, available with an Apple TV subscription. Then, just hours later, Amazon MGM finally dropped the bomb we've all been waiting for and announced that the next actor to play James Bond would be Robert Pattinson. Many of us had been waiting for that for months. Years, even, yet it still came as a surprise, given the latest rumors. In hindsight, I probably should have been clued in that the announcement was imminent after the Slow Horses news dropped.

Over the last few weeks and months, there have been a ton of rumors regarding the next Bond ahead of the long-awaited Bond 26 finally beginning production (we’re still not sure when that will actually happen). Names like Jacob Elordi, Henry Cavill, Callum Turner, and more have been rumored. Slow Horses star Jack Lowden was also heavily rumored in recent weeks. Lowden, who plays River Cartwright on the show about disgraced MI5 agents, was looking to make the jump to MI6 and become the ninth man to play Bond.

It’s certainly possible for an actor to appear in multiple projects, but given Slow Horses' aggressive filming schedule (they have already wrapped Season 7, due next year, and are filming Season 8), it might have been a tough schedule to work out for Lowden had he been named Bond. When the next two seasons were announced, it should have clued me in that Lowden likely wasn’t going to be James Bond going forward. It’s quite possible that Apple may have actually been waiting on the Bond decision to announce it was moving forward with more seasons of Slow Horses, too. So, when the news dropped about the show's renewal, I should have known Bond’s announcement was on deck.

Interestingly, despite the announcements coming almost at the same time, there are also rumors that Lowden might not be part of Seasons 9 and 10. A report in Variety calls Lowden’s return “uncertain.” I’m not sure what to make of that. The book-to-screen adaptation has been adapting one book per season, generally. Season 6 was an exception, as it combined two books, the 6th and 7th in Mick Herron’s series Slough House. That's not all, though.

How to Recover From High School

📅 Nov 19, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

After years or decades, problems and patterns can become deeply entrenched. Be a fly on the wall during a sample couples therapy session and witness the slow, deliberate work of changing those patterns for a more enduring love.

Self Tests are all about you. Are you outgoing or introverted? Are you a narcissist? Does perfectionism hold you back? Find out the answers to these questions and more with Psychology Today.

Posted November 19, 2026 | Reviewed by Michelle Quirk

High school may have been a high for many. But if it was a low for you, you ain't alone. A 2025 YouGov survey found that 12 percent of respondents disliked high school while 8 percent hated it. Consider me among the 8 percent.

Having a lot more "grrrrs" than "rah-rahs" in high school could leave you holding onto Rs—namely, resentment and regret—which could keep you trapped in your high school days—and daze—way longer than you'd want. Here's how to instead truly graduate from all that:

A friend once described high school as "The last time that you had no choice of who is going to be around you most of the time," because your high school totally depended on where your family happened to live and what resources and connections they happened to have. Therefore, chances were quite high that you were around high schoolers who didn't fit very well with you.

My high school, for example, had very little diversity and was filled with people not used to dealing with those of different racial, ethnic, cultural, and socioeconomic backgrounds and ways of thinking. For example, my high school soccer team had never seen someone like me play soccer. So, it was pretty common to hear racial stereotypes and epithets hurled around throughout my teenage years, making me feel quite alone then.

This brings up an age-old problem—or perhaps an age-young one. Your high school classmates were in their mid-teens at the time. Their brains and senses of self were still developing like fruitcake batter that had just entered the oven. That, along with their lack of life experience, left them quite limited in their ability to process things around them, know right from wrong, and think for themselves. There are reasons why rental car companies still charge extra until you've gotten past your 25th birthday.

Does that guarantee that all of your high school classmates will eventually grow out of their high school selves? Of course not. Nevertheless, surely you wouldn't base your worth on what any teenager would say about you now? Heck, I wouldn't even listen to my own high school self.

That's because I was still full of "Never Have I Evers" back then—way before I had actually traveled around the world, met a much greater diversity of people, developed real friendships, and engaged in real romantic relationships. For a while, it was hard to look back at my naive high school self without a ton of cringe. But I have since learned to forgive my teenage self—recognizing that I didn't know better at the time.

Part of healing is forgiving your high school classmates as well. For example, the female classmate who once told me, "You are smart and funny, so I like hanging out with you, but you are so incredibly ugly," probably wasn't used to seeing a diverse range of looks and people. Also, her frontal lobe may not yet have been developed enough to tell her, "OK, maybe don't say something like that if you want that person to help you with your homework or teach you how to play sports."

Of course, the fact that to save money my dad—rather than, you know, a real barber—would regularly cut my hair—leaving my head looking like that of a LEGO person—probably didn't help. But eventually you realize that you can recover from not only a bad haircut but also high school. After all, high school is only four years and not forever—lasting about as long as a pair of heavily distressed jeans.

It is important, though, to treat any distressing high school experience like you would a pair of out-of-fashion distressed jeans. Throw them away so that they don't keep taking up space—space that you could otherwise use for new memories, experiences, people, and growth. In fact, get rid of anything that may trigger low high school memories, such as severing ties with anyone who doesn't recognize that you have outgrown your high school years. If you do find it difficult to move on from those years, get help from others, such as a professional therapist.

You'll need to free up that mental and emotional space because you may have a lot of catch-up growing to do. I certainly did, which probably delayed a lot of things and led to a lot of late blooming. But don't despair. Think of going through life as what the character Kandi said on an episode of "Two and a Half Men" about being with Alan: "[It's like being] in the back seat of a car driven by a really smart kangaroo. He may go up on the curb a couple of times, but he'll get you there." The key then is to learn from your faulty high school days—understand what went wrong, what you did wrong, and learn from it. Adapt accordingly.

Now, throwing stuff out doesn't mean simply suppressing your bad high school memories and feelings. That can be like hiding a ferret in your pants. It could end up eating at your rather sensitive spots over time and emerging at the most unexpected, inopportune times. Instead, consider directly confronting your past and processing it.

In the process, you may even find that your memories don't match reality. The supposedly cool things you missed out on may not have been so cool. Those who made you feel bad may have actually felt very bad about themselves.

In lieu of going to my high school reunion, I had dinner with someone I actually liked from high school. We had never really talked about our high school experiences until then. But it soon became apparent that her high school experience was similarly negative. When you are open about such experiences, you may end up realizing that you weren't quite as alone as you thought you were.

Data for the third quarter for 2026 continues to showcase the effects of high rates and elevated housing-related costs, as the latest data reveals that the quarterly slowdown was driven by declines in both home purchase and refinance lending.

ATTOM—the California-based property data and real estate analytics company—found that all mortgages and lending in housing fell in Q3 2026, in the latest U.S. Residential Property Mortgage Origination Report.

According to the report, a total of 1.29 million purchase and refinance mortgages secured by residential property (one in four units) originated in the third quarter, which is down 12% from Q2 2026 and down 15% compared to the same period last year.

“The increase in refinance activity we saw late last year and into early this year cooled in the third quarter, with refinancing declining more than purchase lending,” ATTOM CEO Rob Barber said. “Purchase loans also slipped to their lowest third-quarter level since 2012, as higher mortgage rates and elevated average purchase loan amounts continued to strain affordability for many buyers.”

Barber—alongside other economists—attribute the third quarter’s cooling housing activity to the sharp jump in mortgage rates. After the Federal Reserve’s rate hike in September, the average 30-year fixed-rate mortgage jumped to 7.40% in mid-October, and has slowly decreased but remains over 7%.

Purchase loans fell by 9% quarter-to-quarter, for a total of nearly 730,000. The report notes that this drop is the lowest third quarter since 2012. Annually, purchase loans were down 12%, although the average purchase loan increased by 3% to nearly $412,000.

Purchase lending was also down in Q3 2026, totaling $300.6 billion or down 10% from last quarter, and down 10% on a yearly basis.

While activity declined, purchase loans accounted for 56.5% of all purchase and refinance originations, which is up from the 54.5% in Q2 2026 and Q3 2025.

Alongside purchase loans, refinance activity fell 16% quarter-to-quarter, totaling 561,290 loans. This marks the lowest quarterly total since Q2 2024, and is down 19% from the same period last year.

The share of refinance originations decreased slightly, from 45.5% in Q2 to 43.5% in Q3 2026. Although not a significant drop in shares, refinance lending declined more drastically than purchase activity by city, falling 89.4% on a quarterly basis.

Breaking it down by government-backed activity, Federal Housing Administration (FHA) loans accounted for 15.7% of all purchase and refinance originations in the third quarter, down from the previous quarter’s 16.1% and last year’s 17.9%. VA-backed loans were also down, representing 6.9% of originations in Q3 after the previous quarter’s 8.2% and 7.2% the same period last year, or the lowest share since Q2 2024.

Again, the 2026 housing market is shaping up to have a muted end, as pending home sales and mortgage applications have been on a downward trend for the past few months.

Love the interview scene with Rachel. That was such a fun scene to shoot. It was the first scene that we shot. It was two days and it was like shooting like a 1950s Cary Grant movie.
