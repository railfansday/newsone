The Netherlands Has €231 Million for Cleaner Inland Ships. It Still Needs Electric Freight Corridors.

📅 Nov 11, 2026  |  Category: Business  |  Read Source →

The Netherlands is preparing to spend €231.2 million over four years helping inland ships replace fossil-fuel propulsion. The first €36.93 million tender opens on November 17, 2026, and battery-electric vessels are among the eligible investments. This is a serious step toward decarbonizing freight on the waterways that link Dutch ports and Europe's industrial heartland. But the programme's most important scoring calculation rewards emissions avoided per euro of propulsion subsidy while excluding separate subsidies for the shore infrastructure on which the ship may depend. That distinction matters when the commercial product is a complete freight service, not an electric vessel sitting beside a quay.

The new Dutch tender is considerably more sophisticated than a technology demonstration fund, yet it leaves an important accounting boundary. My TFIE Strategy Briefing assessment follows that boundary from subsidized vessels to working routes, drawing on my open, 98-page Inland Freight Electrification report.

The RVO programme can support battery-electric systems, some hybrid arrangements and other propulsion technologies, together with eligible charging and refueling facilities. Its published explanation prioritizes electrification where practical. That is sensible: electric inland vessels are already operating in the Netherlands, and a subsidy should encourage the pathway that provides the required service at low emissions and acceptable cost.

The scheme also recognizes that a vessel does not operate in isolation. Applications receive points for commercial cooperation, cargo-owner agreements and the quality of supporting infrastructure arrangements. These are material criteria, not decorative promises. They are a welcome correction to programmes that fund novel equipment and assume the market, terminal and utility will somehow arrange everything else.

Yet most of the available points are tied to projected emissions reductions per euro of propulsion support. That is a consistent measure for comparing applications for individual ships. It is not an accounting of the entire investment required to turn a diesel freight route into an electric one. One barge may need access to battery exchange at both ends of its journey, reserve battery inventory, grid capacity, a crane or handling system and a dependable service agreement. Some of those facilities can serve many vessels, making it hard to allocate their cost and value to any single propulsion application.

Zero Emission Services, or ZES, has already shown what putting the pieces together looks like. Its swappable battery containers allow inland vessels to obtain charged energy without each owner purchasing and maintaining every battery required by the route. At Moerdijk, a terminal arrangement combines battery exchange, handling equipment and access to an existing electricity connection. The ZES network is extending beyond isolated demonstrations, although operating sites, planned stations and funded vessel conversions must not be counted as the same thing.

The difference between a capable ship and reliable freight is familiar to ports. Charged batteries must be available at the right time, terminals must handle exchanges without disrupting cargo work, and the energy-service provider needs enough committed traffic to finance its containers and chargers.

The Netherlands deserves credit for recognizing these dependencies. It need not discard vessel-level grants or wait until every future route is planned before making early awards. But a second programme-level measure is needed alongside emissions per propulsion subsidy: what complete, repeatable electric freight service is each funded project helping create, and which shared assets make that service possible?

This matters all the more because the freight being carried will change. Fossil-linked cargo is likely to decline as coal and petroleum use falls, and ports should not size batteries, terminals and connections around the assumption that today's bulk flows continue indefinitely. A route with strong contracted demand may justify shared charging equipment while a low-utilization route requires a different architecture or timetable. The answer depends on the freight task, not merely on the specifications of an available vessel.

The Dutch programme is a promising way to accelerate the first investments. Its next achievement should be to show that those investments connect: cargo moving on named routes, vessels using dependable shared energy infrastructure, and operating data strong enough to finance the following corridor without perpetually reinventing the subsidy. The ship is the visible part of the transition. The corridor is what makes it commercially useful.

My full TFIE Strategy Briefing examines the subsidy rules, the Moerdijk battery-exchange arrangement and the difference between vessel awards and operational corridors. The open, 98-page Inland Freight Electrification report contains the modelled cargo, fleet, battery-pool, terminal and investment evidence behind that analysis.

Russia-Ukraine talks turn to post-war security but end without breakthrough

📅 Nov 11, 2026  |  Category: Entertainment  |  Read Source →

On Thursday Steve Witkoff and Jared Kushner will travel to Moscow and Dublin to hold talks with the Russian and Ukrainian presidents

Trilateral talks in Abu Dhabi between Russia, Ukraine and the US aimed at ending the war in Ukraine have concluded after a second day.

Although the American side said they saw "some openings", there was no hint of any breakthroughs to end the nearly five year-long war sparked by Russia's invasion.

Davyd Arakhamia from Ukraine's negotiating team said that this round of talks - the first since February - "was one of the hardest."

"I believe the Russians came with very strict directives, so it was extremely difficult to soften the discussion," he said. But he added that he felt "good momentum" and urged for a second round of talks to be organised as soon as possible.

Both Moscow and Kyiv confirmed that a large-scale prisoner of war exchange was in the works and could be carried out soon.

After the talks it was announced that on Thursday US negotiators Steve Witkoff and Jared Kushner - President Donald Trump's son-in-law - would travel to Moscow to meet Russian President Vladimir Putin, and then to Dublin to speak to Ukraine's Volodymyr Zelensky.

This latest round of talks had been framed as centred around the "security architecture" of Europe.

For Kyiv, this may include reassurances that allies will come to its aid if Ukraine comes under attack again, despite the country not being a Nato member.

For Moscow, it is likely to include a limit to the influence of Nato in eastern Europe - a central concern for the Kremlin since the alliance's eastward expansion.

Earlier this week Kushner said the US hoped to "create a new framework" of security between Ukraine, Russia and Europe that could "secure lasting peace", while Kremlin negotiator Vladimir Medinsky said the world needed a "new" and "fair" security system.

Ukraine and Europe have long been concerned about the current US administration's commitment to European security and its softening towards the Kremlin.

Representatives of Britain, Nato and various EU countries were also in Abu Dhabi and met with US and Ukrainian negotiators, but the Kremlin - with whom the EU does not officially communicate - said its delegation had not had any contact with them.

Moscow has been dismissive of European involvement, although Europeans have long argued that they are central to any post-war dynamics on their continent.

Many - particularly in eastern Europe - feel their security is linked to that of Ukraine. Agreements cannot be implemented "if no consensus can be reached among Nato members," a German government spokesperson said on Wednesday.

Kushner said the US was keen to bring the Europeans on board and said that getting them "added to the format" would be on the agenda for his talks with Putin.

There is also disagreement on whether discussions on a future security framework should be preceded by a ceasefire, as the Europeans and Kyiv would want, or whether the fighting only stops after an agreement has been reached, as the Kremlin has insisted.

Any agreement on a peacetime security framework, however, is contingent on the war ending, Kushner noted. But that cannot occur "until there's an agreement on territory and on all other bilateral issues," he added.

Since talks began last year, a core obstacle to any settlement has been Ukraine's eastern region of Donetsk, which is mostly occupied by Russia. Moscow has demanded that Ukraine relinquish the parts of Donetsk it still holds, while Kyiv wants to freeze the contact line to maintain control over those areas.

This matter was the "hardest", Kushner acknowledged, while Zelensky's chief of staff and negotiator Kyrylo Budanov refused to be drawn on whether Russia was still demanding a Ukrainian withdrawal from the region.

"These deals are very hard and complex, and this one is very emotional, very complex," Kushner said. "The reality is there is no trust right now between the sides and that is to be expected after a conflict like this."

The talent management firm's acquisition of the Max Stein-run boutique adds a client roster of Substackers and podcast players.

Entertainment 360 has accelerated its drive into the creator economy by acquiring Brigade, the creative management firm focused on digital talent.

Terms of the deal were not disclosed, but the acquisition brings elite digital tastemakers, including Substack writers, podcasters and influencers, to the Los Angeles-based talent management company run by a collective of partners as it moves beyond traditional film and TV representation.

Brigade founder Max Stein will become a partner at Entertainment 360 and bring his team and clients with him to help the combined company tap a growing influencer universe and manage and monetize celebrity creators coveted by brand marketers.

Entertainment 360 shares that philosophy and brings an extraordinary depth of experience, relationships, and resources. Joining forces gives our clients access to many more opportunities, while allowing us to preserve the personal approach that has always defined Brigade.”

The Brigade acquisition comes as YouTube stars, podcasters and other digital creators increasingly rise to the fore in Hollywood and a fast-shifting entertainment ecosystem. Connecticut solar projects directly support Veterans Affairs campus

📅 Nov 11, 2026  |  Category: Business  |  Read Source →

The Connecticut Department of Veterans Affairs (DVA), Connecticut Green Bank and their partners recently marked the completion of solar installations serving DVA’s main campus and skilled nursing facility in Rocky Hill, Connecticut.

The projects will deliver renewable electricity and reduce energy costs at the campus. The Green Bank developed the projects through its Solar Marketplace Assistance Program Plus (Solar MkAP+). Kearsarge Energy has partnered with Connecticut Green Bank as the long-term owners of these projects as well as nine others and will also oversee operations and maintenance. Greenskies Clean Energy was the engineering, procurement and construction contractor for the two DVA installations.

“To Gov. Lamont and your administration, thank you for having the vision for the GreenerGov CT initiative, providing reliable, environmentally friendly and cost-saving energy for residents and businesses in Connecticut, which is one of your top priorities, along with countless others, enabling Connecticut’s 3.6 million residents to thrive,” said Ron Welch, a retired brigadier general with the U.S. Army, and commissioner of the DVA. “Our team at the Connecticut Department of Veterans Affairs thanks you for your full support of this solar field project and for all you do through your significant partnerships to sustain and improve the environment and the quality of life for Connecticut residents and beyond.”

The two systems have a combined capacity of 1.404 MWDC and are projected to generate approximately 1.84 million kWh of electricity annually. The systems are projected to offset 85% of electricity use for the skilled nursing facility and 35% of the electricity use at the main campus building.

“This project brings together two important commitments: advancing sustainability in state government and supporting the people who have served our nation,” said Michelle Gilman, commissioner of the Department of Administrative Services. “DAS is proud to support this project and the broader Greener Gov CT partnership.”

The two arrays built at the Rocky Hill campus are 604.8 kWDC and 799.2 kWDC.

American Airlines Plans To Bring Back First Class Mini-Meals On Shorter Flights—Starting With New York–Chicago

This is a very good change. A flight can take up much of your afternoon without crossing American’s 900-mile meal threshold, especially when you include getting to the airport, boarding, and a connection. A small meal makes first class more worthwhile.

JonNYC reports that American Airlines is bringing back light bites and mini-meals on 700–899-mile domestic first class flights, starting on NYC/ORD.

American used to have a service between a basket of packaged snacks and a full meal. Before the pandemic, shorter flights could get something plated that would tide you over.

In 2016 I reported plans for plated morning items, including yogurt and breakfast cake. And my 2018 menu coverage included Lite Bites cheeses with cracked black pepper cheddar, cave-aged cheddar, Appalachian Tomme, and smoked Gouda.

American’s mainline standard is a snack basket on 500–899-mile flights during meal times, with meals starting in the 900-mile range.

United actually raised its meal threshold from 800 to 900 miles in January 2024. American would be moving in the other direction, offering something more substantial on flights where Delta and United generally hand out snacks.

Alaska is still the standard to beat. They already feed passengers on shorter flights, and I’ve found their domestic first class food better than competitors’.

American also used to have meal “exception markets”: shorter routes that got meals for competitive reasons. My January 2020 service guide listed Chicago–Dallas, Chicago–Boston, Chicago–New York, Los Angeles–Denver, and LaGuardia–Atlanta, among others.

I like a broad 700–899-mile rule better than making customers depend on whether their particular city pair merits an exception, though the exception markets got real meals and some were even shorter like Chicago–Washington National at just 612 miles which still wouldn’ qualify for a lite bite under this distance band.

This does mean more work for flight attendants. Individual food service means preparation, distribution, and clearing, with less time to do it on a shorter flight. American is also adding first class seats to some of its aircraft.

The airline’s product turnaround has been surprising, and surprisingly fast. They’ve introduced new business class suites, improved regional cabins and internet, upgraded coffee, and put money into lounges. They’re also reversing years of bad product decisions, including plans to bring back seatback screens and add more extra legroom coach seats.

Their returning coach food reservation program, announced with an initial Dallas–Fort Worth–Los Angeles test, should help passengers get the item they actually want. Mostly, though, I like that American seems to be loading enough food that there’s still something left when the cart reaches my aisle.

Mortgage Demand Jumps 6.2% as Rates Fall For Fourth Straight Week

📅 Nov 11, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Mortgage applications rose last week, as interest rates decreased for the fourth week in a row, hitting their lowest levels since mid-September, as a positive jobs report and the results of the midterm election bolster the outlook.

For the week ending Nov. 6 the Mortgage Bankers Association's Market Composite Index—a measure of total mortgage loan application volume—increased 6.2% on a seasonally adjusted basis from one week earlier.

The Purchase Index, which measures the volume of mortgage applications to purchase a single-family home, increased 8% from one week earlier after seasonal adjustment. Purchase applications were down 18% from a year ago.

According to MBA calculations, the average contract interest rate for 30-year fixed mortgages with conforming loan balances is 7.21%, a slight decrease from last week's rate of 7.3%. Rates averaged 5.99% a year ago.

Mortgage rates dropped to 7.18% last week from 7.21% the week before, according to a separate survey from Freddie Mac.

“Mortgage rates declined for the fourth straight week,” said Joel Kan, MBA’s vice president and deputy chief economist. “The declining trend in rates helped to spur stronger application activity, although the pace of purchase and refinance applications remain considerably lower than a year ago."

In recent weeks, high interest rates had pushed more buyers toward riskier Adjustable Rate Mortgages. However, this past week, ARM applications decreased to 10.9% of the total. applications, down from 11.4% the previous week.

FHA and VA loans saw "particularly strong growth" this past week, said Kan.

The share of FHA loan applications decreased to 15.5% from 16% the prior week. The VA share of total applications increased to 12% from 11.2% the prior week. And the USDA share of total applications decreased to 0.5% from 0.6% the week prior.

The refinance share of mortgage activity remained unchanged at 39.8%, but was 57% lower than the same week one year ago, as homeowners remain locked into their lower interest rates.

October's positive jobs report, which showed that the U.S. economy added 165,000 nonfarm jobs in October, has increased the odds that the Federal Reserve will raise interest rates when it next meets in December.

CME Group's FedWatch has the likelihood of an additional rate hike at around 77%.

The positive jobs report news was compounded by broad Democrat wins in the midterm elections. Democrats, many of whom ran on a message of affordability, won the majority in both the House and the Senate. Voters aged 18 to 34 and men aged 18 to 49 had ranked housing costs as a top political issue in a rcent CNBC survey.

Trump's former Federal Housing Commissioner Frank Cassidy called the midterms a "wake-up call."

"Everyone needs to get serious about tackling and addressing housing affordability. It can't be ignored anymore," he told Realtor.com®.

SpaceX/xAI is developing a custom version of Nvidia's Vera Rubin NVL72 rack-scale AI system that replaces conventional NVLink cable assemblies with a massive 84-layer printed circuit board (PCB), in part for use in orbital data centers, reports SemiAnalysis. The system is codenamed Kessel and retains 36 Vera CPUs, 72 Rubin GPUs, and nine NVLink switch trays, but introduces a different solid-state interconnect architecture. While SpaceX/xAI will continue to buy Nvidia hardware directly from the company, Elon Musk's company will source other components independently, which could give it greater control over its hardware supply chain.

The Kessel system retains the fundamental configuration of Nvidia's Vera Rubin NVL72 platform, which comprises 72 GPU packages distributed across 18 compute trays and nine NVLink switch trays. However, instead of Nvidia's conventional arrangement with internal NVLink cable cartridges, SpaceX plans to position compute and switch trays back-to-back around a passive PCB midplane. The architecture resembles a smaller version of Nvidia's Kyber design with 576 AI GPUs (albeit in 144 packages), which also relies on a PCB-based interconnect rather than conventional cable assemblies, SemiAnalysis notes. Interestingly, the design is aimed at both terrestrial and orbital deployments, according to the analysts.

Arguably the most distinctive feature of Kessel is its 84-layer PCB midplane, which uses ultra-low-loss M10 copper-clad laminates (CCLs). By contrast, Nvidia's Kyber architecture relied on a backplane based on a considerably more complex 156-layer PCB featuring M9 CCLs. The reduction in layer count by nearly half could substantially improve manufacturability and production yields, but the more advanced copper-clad laminate will almost certainly increase material costs. In any case, an 84-layer PCB is a device that is exceptionally hard to build.

The move to a PCB midplane could simplify system assembly by eliminating numerous high-speed cable connections, but it would not necessarily reduce manufacturing costs. An 84-layer PCB that relies on ultra-low-loss M10 laminate is an exceptionally expensive component that requires sophisticated manufacturing processes and precise quality control. To put an 84-layer PCB number into context, modern high-end server motherboards use 15 – 20 layers, whereas server development boards use more than 20 layers to virtually eliminate any electrical issues with the board itself. Furthermore, manufacturing yields of an 84-layer PCB could become a major obstacle, as defects in internal layers may render an entire board unusable. Consequently, the economic viability of Kessel will depend on whether savings from cable elimination and independent component procurement can offset the cost of its complex midplane.

Perhaps more importantly, Kessel represents a departure from Nvidia's conventional system-level procurement model. Instead of purchasing Nvidia's assembled L6 compute boards or even L10 trays that Nvidia plans to sell with its Vera Rubin generation, SpaceX reportedly plans to acquire AI accelerators, CPUs, switches, and networking components directly from Nvidia and procure other components from third-party suppliers (one can wonder how easy it will be for SpaceX/xAI to procure memory amid today's shortages). This would enable SpaceX to select its own PCB manufacturers, component vendors, and manufacturing partners, optimize its hardware for its infrastructure (which will be important for orbital deployments), and perhaps reduce intermediary costs. Meanwhile, the company would assume greater responsibility for system design, validation, manufacturing quality, and supply-chain management, which is something that most hyperscalers have been doing in recent years.

For Nvidia, the implications are less straightforward. Although direct sales of CPU and AI GPU packages could reduce the company's revenue from complete compute-board assemblies, the arrangement could also encourage other large customers to develop their own Nvidia-based systems and eventually deploy more Nvidia-based machines, something that may be important in a world where custom silicon developed by hyperscalers is rapidly gaining traction. However, to make this happen, Kessel must be a smashing success that will offer clear advantages over systems designed by Nvidia.

Siri co-founder backs Helix for its voice ID tech

📅 Nov 11, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

For years, websites have relied on technology like CAPTCHA to prove that the user on the other end is human. More recently, social media platforms have turned to photos or videos to verify users’ identities. Now, a startup called Helix is betting that voice technology can do the same job, without requiring users to hand over sensitive personal information.

The voice technology, which can run directly on a user’s device, has attracted backing from Siri co-founder Dag Kittlaus, who will also act as an advisor.

The company raised $6 million in funding in a round led by Chicago Ventures, with participation from BAM Ventures, Aglaé Ventures, Corazon Capital, Tusk Ventures, Yonder Ventures, Rule30, and Brown Alumni Group.

The startup was founded by Alex Oberg, Anna Dorofiyenko, and Mike Hall in 2025. Oberg previously worked at Ticketmaster, which acquired his startup; Dorofiyenko led data science teams at Live Nation and Zillow; and Hall has worked at Nike and Tinder, where he helped develop the initial matching algorithm.

At Ticketmaster, Oberg played a role in creating Verified Fan, which is designed to keep bots out of the platform. As AI advanced, he started thinking along the same lines for a new solution.

“As AI started to come up in the last two years, I was realizing that it’s going to make it increasingly difficult to determine who’s human and who’s not on the Internet. Identity on the Internet’s always been about proxies for identity, right? It’s your email, it’s your phone, it’s your IP address, it’s your device fingerprint, it’s your device ID,” Oberg said.

He noted that with the rise of AI agents like Instinct and Muse, the industry needs to rethink human identity on the internet and rely on biometric signals like voice, which is where Helix comes in.

Its core technology can run directly on a device to determine whether someone is human and estimate their age. The company is also running pilots to explore whether voice can be used a biomarker to detect conditions such as adrunkenness or distress.

The company can deploy its technology to replace or supplement CAPTCHA and help block bot attacks. Helix said that because its model can run locally even on an iPhone 13, the cost of deploying the technology is minimal. The startup said one of its customers is Zeel, an in-home healthcare provider that uses Helix’s tech to verify the identities of providers visiting customers’ homes.

Oberg said verifying workers, such as cab drivers, is another use case the startup is exploring. He also said that age-verification regulations for social media platforms around the world are forcing users to trade privacy for access. Helix’s voice technology, he said, could offer an alternative.

“There’s like an avalanche of regulation coming down because essentially society has decided children should not be able to use the Internet as adults. That has created a bit of a strawman argument that everyone needs to trade their privacy for the sake of security and child safety. We are that company that’s saying, nope, don’t agree. It’s a four-second voice challenge,” he said.

The CEO also emphasized the limitations of Helix’s technology, noting that it cannot reliably detect whether someone is lying. He added that its voice technology doesn’t identify individual speakers and claimed that even if the company stores a voiceprint, it cannot be used to reconstruct a person’s voice.

AI-powered fraud is also a growing problem in phone calls. But Helix believes its technology isn’t yet capable of reliably detecting AI agents during live calls.

Siri co-founder Kittlaus said that voice identification technology has finally reached a point where it works reliably, positioning Helix to scale as AI-powered fraud increases. He also said that Helix’s work to compress its models gives the startup a cost advantage.

“What folks at Helix are doing is, instead of teaching computers how to understand speech, these guys are understanding, what the speech of the speaker actually means, what’s going on with them, who they are they lying, are they impaired? That side of it is fascinating to me within the larger picture that I just described,” he told TechCrunch.
