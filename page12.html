Ex-NFL cornerback Alphonso Smith Jr. was crying and praying in the moments before he died by suicide ... and a newly released police report reveals he fatally shot himself in front of two women, TMZ has learned.

According to a Palm Beach County Sheriff's Office report obtained by TMZ ... Smith was at a home in Pahokee, Florida, on September 2 when a woman inside heard him crying, praying aloud and speaking to several people on a conference call.

The woman told investigators she tried comforting Smith and getting him to open up ... but he asked her to go upstairs. Moments later, the mother of Smith's children arrived at the home, and both women witnessed him shoot himself while sitting on a living room couch.

Deputies responding to the scene found Smith suffering from a gunshot wound. He was rushed to a nearby hospital before being airlifted to St. Mary's Medical Center, where he was pronounced dead at 3:19 PM.

Toxicology results also revealed THC, the psychoactive compound found in marijuana, was present in his system.

As TMZ previously reported ... sources with direct knowledge told us Smith died by suicide at a Florida housing community.

A troubling Facebook post published around the time of his death also raised concerns about his emotional state, which seemed to show he was struggling before his passing.

Smith was a standout cornerback and a member of the Hall of Fame at Wake Forest, where he earned All-American honors and set an ACC record with 21 career interceptions.

The Denver Broncos selected him in the second round of the 2009 NFL Draft, and he later played for the Detroit Lions. Smith recorded 8 interceptions, returning a pair for touchdowns over 4 NFL seasons.

I Finally Bought Nvidia Stock During the Sell-Off. Here's the Price I'd Pay to Buy More.

📅 Nov 24, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

I finally invested in Nvidia after its sell-off. Here's why I bought at $155 and the price that would tempt me to add.

By Matt DiLallo – Nov 24, 2026 at 1:15PM EST

I've wanted to own shares of Nvidia (NVDA -7.46%) for a while. However, while the company's artificial intelligence (AI) business continued delivering extraordinary growth, its premium valuation kept me on the sidelines.

The recent sell-off finally changed that equation. I capitalized on the recent downdraft in Nvidia stock, buying five shares for $155 apiece, investing $775 in the chipmaker. Even though I'm excited about Nvidia's long-term potential, I'm not ready to increase my position just yet. Here's why I finally bought Nvidia stock and what I'd need to see before buying more.

Nvidia is growing remarkably fast. The company's revenue grew a blistering 103% in the third quarter to nearly $116 billion, driven by a 110% surge in data center revenue to $108 billion. Its earnings also more than doubled to over $65 billion. The semiconductor company expects its revenue to rise to around $130 billion in the fourth quarter, a roughly 12% sequential increase. Meanwhile, analysts expect Nvidia to earn about $9.67 per share this year, more than triple last year's earnings.

Despite its exponential growth, shares of Nvidia have tumbled more than 30% from their recent peak due to concerns about an AI-spending slowdown and Nvidia's leadership. With its business booming but its share price under pressure, Nvidia's valuation has come down considerably. It currently trades at less than 17x earnings, down from over 45x earlier this year. That much less demanding valuation led me to finally buy a few shares of Nvidia.

Before I bought shares, I ran bear, base, and bull scenarios for what Nvidia could be worth in five years, based on the following assumptions about earnings growth and valuation multiples:

My base case is reasonably conservative. It assumes Nvidia grows its earnings by 75% over the next five years, with a slight improvement in its valuation multiple. That would deliver a double-digit annualized return from my purchase price. That's my sweet spot; I want companies that can deliver double-digit returns from very reasonable assumptions.

Meanwhile, the bull case would produce an even stronger return if it delivers faster growth and multiple expansion. On the other hand, the bear case shows the potential risk. I think the risk/reward has shifted to the point where buying Nvidia looked too compelling to ignore.

While I thought Nvidia was compelling at $155 a share, I didn't buy a full position, given all that's going on with the company. At the time of my purchase, there was significant uncertainty due to the departure of CEO Jensen Huang, who has since returned to lead the company. Additionally, there remain concerns about a potential slowdown in AI spending and Nvidia's role in funding the AI infrastructure build-out.

That's why I want an even better price before adding. My next potential buying zone is in the $140–$145 range. Still, I'd only add if the problems driving the decline were temporary rather than evidence of accounting irregularities or a deteriorating AI investment cycle. I want to be very disciplined because my portfolio already has significant exposure to the AI megatrend. So, adding more of even a high-quality company like Nvidia can still be a risky investment if it represents excessive exposure to the same economic theme.

Nvidia's remarkable growth and more attractive valuation finally convinced me to buy shares. At my $155 purchase price, my base-case projections suggest that this investment could nearly double in value over the next five years.

However, I'm not rushing to buy more. Nvidia still faces important questions about AI infrastructure spending, profitability, and regulatory investigations. I'd be interested in adding to my position around $140 to $145, provided the investment thesis remains intact.

For now, I'm comfortable owning a small piece of this AI powerhouse while waiting for a better opportunity to increase my investment.

Though David Fincher’s fans had been eagerly anticipating The Further Mis-Adventures of Cliff Booth, early screenings of the Once Upon a Time in Hollywood sequel starring Brad Pitt have raised questions about whether generative AI was used in the film. People have claimed that the movie is chock-full of uncanny, inconsistent visuals that make it feel more like slop than the sort of polished film you’d expect from the director of sleek productions like Fight Club and The Social Network.

Following a number of Cliff Booth critics screenings that took place last week ahead of the film’s premiere on November 25th, moviegoers rushed to social media to lament what they saw. One of the most vocal reactions came from YouTuber / director Patrick Tomasso, who said on X that he walked out of his screening feeling “completely gutted” and that the film featured “some of the most blatant and egregious AI use” he has ever seen from a major studio. Tomasso also insisted you can see AI-generated footage in Cliff Booth’s trailers that have been posted online, and he was confident that Fincher’s latest film is filled with “slop as you know and loathe it. The shit you can pump out of Higgsfield.”

Others echoed Tomasso’s sentiments, writing that “the amount of AI in the CLIFF BOOTH movie is astounding to an absurd degree” and vowing to “never watch another Fincher, Pitt or Tarintino [sic] movie again.” Yet some critics enjoyed the film and suggested that the bad AI people thought they were seeing might have actually just been bad CGI. Netflix has neither confirmed nor denied whether AI was used in any capacity to make the film, but Netflix’s heavy involvement in the film’s production and the company’s recent investments in generative AI technology left many suspicious.

It did not help that David Fincher, in a recent interview with the Los Angeles Times, responded to questions about his thoughts on AI with “I’m not a Luddite, so I don’t fear the unknown.” Fincher also reasoned that the anti-AI crowd is advocating for artists to stop iterating on their creative processes: “Is everyone satisfied that we have figured this art form out enough that what we really need to do is go back to celluloid? How long have people painted? 40,000 years? We’re talking about a thing that’s a hundred years old and everyone’s going, ‘Stop. Pencils down. No more iterating. It’s not necessary to have at your fingertips the ability to change the color of that person’s eyes.’ Well, it exists. Should we not use it?” The spiel made it sound like he might be jumping on the AI bandwagon.

Set about a decade after Once Upon a Time in Hollywood, Cliff Booth picks up in 1977 at a time when Booth (Pitt) has left his life as a stuntman behind to become a fixer who quietly deals with scandalous problems for Hollywood elites. Cliff still works a number of side gigs as a projectionist and bartender. But the movie is primarily focused on his budding romance with critic-turned-talent agent Karen Crosby (Elizabeth Debicki) and his working relationship with Charlie Peters (Scott Caan), a TV actor who needs Booth’s services to keep his wife (Carla Gugino) from being murdered. At least one trailer for the film features some baffling shots of what looks like people sitting backward in a horse-drawn carriage that make it seem like there might be some generative AI in the mix. It’s hard to tell from the trailers whether that’s actually the case, but in a moment when many creators have been trying to pass off AI generations as human-made art, it’s not surprising that people are suspicious.

Netflix has been incorporating AI into its movies and series for some time now, and it seemed like the company was doubling down earlier this year when it acquired Ben Affleck’s startup, InterPositive, for around $600 million. Rather than turning text into visual outputs like many commercially available AI tools, InterPositive’s AI models are designed to generate assets based on footage captured during a film’s production. According to Netflix, InterPositive’s AI was developed with the intention of making it easier to tweak details like lighting or to remove unwanted background elements. The company also made a big deal of emphasizing that InterPositive’s models were highly customizable and well suited to replicating a human director’s signature style.

Netflix buying InterPositive and absorbing the company’s development team is part of why there has been so much speculation about AI being used in Cliff Booth. If Cliff Booth actually uses AI and the visuals really are as bad as some have said, it would be a major knock against both Netflix and Fincher — a director known for his meticulous (obsessive, some might argue) attention to detail.

Jensen Huang addresses Nvidia employees a day after returning as CEO

📅 Nov 24, 2026  |  Category: Business  |  Read Source →

Nvidia stock is down more than 7% in Tuesday trading after jumping more than 30% on Monday's reinstatement

Nvidia $NVDA CEO Jensen Huang spoke to staff on Tuesday at a company-wide meeting held at the Santa Clara, California, headquarters, one day after the board reinstated him, according to Bloomberg. The session was slated for a 9:30 a.m. Pacific Time kickoff.

On Monday, Huang came back to headquarters as employees clapped and cheered for him. Speaking briefly in the lobby, he said to employees that he "will do a better job," according to Bloomberg. Huang has said he intends to improve during his second stint as CEO.

Nvidia stock surged more than 30% on Monday, then gave back over 7% during trading on Tuesday. The company's market value has fallen by nearly $2 trillion since it peaked in October.

Senior leaders held a similar employee gathering last week without Huang and used it to show confidence that the company could thrive without him, according to Bloomberg. The board's decision on Monday surprised most of those leaders.

Nvidia said its board voted unanimously to reinstate Huang after receiving input from senior levels of the U.S. government. The company said it is confident his return poses no national security risk. President Donald Trump said he spoke to board members and "made the deal."

Huang left the job on Nov. 12 after reports of multiple affairs. U.S. officials suspect one of the women of relaying information to China's intelligence services, according to Bloomberg, and Beijing has dismissed the allegation. No charges have been brought against Huang.

Several Republican lawmakers criticized the reinstatement. "No national security risk? Based on what?" wrote Sen. John Cornyn, a Texas Republican.

The chairs of three House committees said Huang and Nvidia General Counsel Tim Teter confirmed they will appear for transcribed interviews on Dec. 1 and Dec. 2. The Senate Banking Committee announced a public hearing with Huang on Dec. 3.

Focker-In-Law Review: A Few Good Laughs In A Film That Falls Flat

📅 Nov 24, 2026  |  Category: Entertainment  |  Read Source →

Ben Stiller's 26-year-old franchise is back with a new sequel, and while it has the heart of the original at times, it ultimately misses the mark.

Ben Stiller's Meet the Parents franchise began in 2000, and fueled by the on-screen chemistry of Stiller and Robert De Niro, along with a host of brutally awkward scenes, it quickly found popularity. Ultimately, Meet the Parents was seen as a cult classic, and it was followed by two sequels, Meet the Fockers in 2004 and Little Fockers in 2010.

Unfortunately, neither Meet the Fockers nor Little Fockers found the success of their predecessor. It is against that backdrop that Stiller, somewhat surprisingly, decided to take another stab at it 16 years later with the brand-new fourth installment, Focker-in-Law.

Focker-in-Law sees the return of the core cast members, with Stiller's Greg and De Niro's Jack still taking center stage. However, the franchise has added some star power to the next generation, with the DCU's Skyler Gisondo playing Greg's son Henry and singer and actress Ariana Grande playing his fiancé, Olivia. Perhaps, with that beefed-up cast, Universal was hoping for a repeat of the original that side-stepped the two follow-up flops. Sadly, Focker-in-Law offers much of the same.

In truth, Focker-in-Law got several genuine laughs out of me. It's impossible to deny how naturally funny Stiller is, and his dynamic with De Niro clearly works, given the legacy of the franchise. Yet, for as much as Greg's antics occasionally find their original charm, they also raise questions about whether this concept is well and truly played out. In fact, in a moment that is played for laughs, the movie points out that Greg is now older than Jack was when they first met.

Rather than reading as funny, this revelation underscores the fact that Greg has evidently never matured, nor has he really grown as a person. If anything, he is all the worse in this movie. Whereas in Meet the Parents in particular, he is a well-meaning, nervous future son-in-law who accidentally creates mayhem wherever he goes, some of Greg's behavior in Focker-in-Law is outright mean, and borders on cruel.

Unfortunately, the addition of Grande's Olivia fails to help. Ahead of the movie's release, there were grumbles about Grande joining the cast; however, she had plenty of potential to shine in the role. Her appearance in projects like Wicked have proven that she can, indeed, act (and she actually started out acting), and many of her Saturday Night Live skits have been incredibly funny, suggesting that she was up to the task of a comedy.

Yet, in Focker-in-Law, Grande's charm and wit are largely absent. Hampered by a script that doesn't seem to know what it wants to do with this new character, most of Olivia's laugh lines fall flat, and while the Meet the Parents franchise has always stretched the limits of realism, Olivia's skills, talents, and apparent ability to succeed in every single area of her life border on the absurd. In fairness, this has less to do with Grande and more to do with Focker-in-Law's refusal to give her nuance – or, frankly, any realistic, humanizing flaws.

Gisondo, meanwhile, is a standout among the cast. Arguably, he out-acts his co-stars more often than not, and he consistently seems to be the only person on screen committed to conveying a mood other than silly or goofy. That may very well be because most characters were not granted the room to explore other emotions. Teri Polo's Pam and Blythe Danner's Dina are all but sidelined, with the latter in particular being almost non-existent.

Still, Focker-in-Law isn't a complete flop. Those looking for a few good laughs and a nostalgia-packed return of the franchise will no doubt enjoy the movie, and even those who are just hoping for a feel-good film for the holiday season will have plenty of fun. It's far from the most inventive movie of 2026, to be sure, but it shouldn't be counted out altogether.

Focker-in-Law releases in theaters nationwide on Wednesday, November 25.

You can expect a similar leap between The Witcher 3 and The Witcher 4 as there is between GTA 5 and GTA 6, CD Projekt Red says

📅 Nov 24, 2026  |  Category: Business  |  Read Source →

CD Projekt Red has said people can expect a similar qualitative and technological leap from The Witcher 3 to The Witcher 4 as has been witnessed between Grand Theft Auto 5 and Grand Theft Auto 6.

CDPR joint-CEO Michał Nowakowski made the remark during a Q3 earnings call this afternoon, during which he and CDPR faced a handful of questions related to GTA 6.

"First of all, obviously this is a call about us, not about Rockstar," Nowakowski began by saying, "but I do understand the connection here. Having said that, I'm not going to make a deep-dive and a full-blown review of GTA 5 versus GTA 6. But of course we did look at the game, we did see the differences.

"We believe there's going to be a comparable leap between The Witcher 3 and The Witcher 4 - I think so, yes. It's been 11 years, we are working on The Witcher 4 to make it significantly better looking and improved in terms of pretty much every possible facet of the game, so yes you should expect us to make a much bigger jump between Witcher 3 and Witcher 4."

Another question was about whether GTA 6 had affected development plans for The Witcher 4 and Cyberpunk 2 - a question well-timed after Cyberpunk 2 creative director Igor Sarzynski joked at the weekend that "Cyberpunk 2 is cancelled" because of how impressive GTA 6 is.

"Is seeing GTA 6 launch making us make the games more expensive?" said Nowakowski. "We have certain ambitions when it comes to Witcher [4] and Cyberpunk 2; they have not changed when it comes to GTA 6. If anything happened as a result of GTA 6, it's [that it's] always great to see games like that because they show you how much cool stuff can be done, and that's for sure an inspiration in many ways for our developers."

Specifically in terms of budget, Nowakowski said nothing had changed, and also echoed a point made earlier this year when he said Witcher 4 and Cyberpunk 2 are "not budget-driven developments".

"I'm not saying we don't care about the budgets: we do, and I'm pretty sure [Rockstar] did as well. But you cannot make games like that having in mind 'this is the fixed number of money I want to spend and that's it, otherwise I'm gone'," he said.

"They're ambition-driven developments and money's important - the budgets are not indefinite, that's not what we're saying. But the philosophy is like I said, and honestly that philosophy has not changed for us because of GTA 6's release; it has just always been like that."

When asked whether the arrival of GTA 6 had impacted sales of The Witcher 3 and Cyberpunk 2077, Nowakowski answered "yes and no". "You do see a certain dip in engagement on the PlayStation and Xbox for sure," he said.

Statistics apparently show a drop of "at least" 25 percent in CDPR's players as they "engaged themselves with GTA 6". "But you do have to remember that a lot of our players are PC-based," Nowakowski pointed out. "GTA has not launched on PC; we have not seen any impact whatsoever on the PC sales or engagement. So it's a bit of a mixed answer.

"And also on the PS5 and Xbox, it's not like our sales came to zero," he added. "Some short impact, yes, on the consoles. Long-term, we do not really see that affecting ourselves in any material way in Q4."

Finally, the questioning turned to marketing, which Rockstar has shown can reach far and wide if you have the brand-power of GTA and some imagination. We've seen prestigious department stores like Selfridges taken over by Rockstar for GTA 6, trailers premiering on Netflix, Miami launch festivals - Rockstar's marketing department has gone all out.

CDPR is no stranger to grabbing headlines with glitzy actor hirings such as Keanu Reeves, but will Rockstar's approach to marketing GTA 6 push it a step further?

"The most important takeaway - I'm not sure if I'd call it a lesson because this is our playbook as well - is that if you want to reach a certain critical mass, you do have to get out of just the core gaming bubble, something we did in the past as well," Nowakowski said. "For sure, GTA 6 went there.

"If the underlying question was if we're going to repeat or copy-paste their playbook: no, for sure not. Our games are different and they require a certain different approach - we just do things differently than they do. But in terms of the ultimate effect, in terms of breaking out to the mass market awareness: this is for sure something we consider pivotal to whatever we're going to do with our marketing campaigns in the future."

CDPR's Q3 financial results were positive. The company made less money than this time last year, but the "fresh momentum" - as Nowakowski put it - provided to The Witcher 3 and Cyberpunk 2077 by the release of the Remastered edition of Witcher 3 and the Edgerunners anime series on Netflix, and associated game update, went a long way.

The Witcher 3 reached its highest concurrent player count on Steam as a result, and Cyberpunk 2077 reached its second highest concurrent player count, which represents serious staying power for both games so long after they were first released. And with Witcher 3 expansion Songs of the Past on track for 2027, and The Witcher 4 for 2028, the future is loaded.

On Wednesday (Nov. 25), the distant ice giant Uranus will be at its best and brightest. Here's when, where and how to look for the seventh planet.

When you purchase through links on our site, we may earn an affiliate commission. Here’s how it works.

One of the hardest planets to see in the solar system — the distant ice giant Uranus — reaches its annual opposition on Wednesday (Nov. 25), just a day after the Beaver Supermoon. It's the best time to see the seventh planet from the sun, but you'll need to use stargazing binoculars or a good backyard telescope to find it.

Uranus is faint, slow-moving and rarely a target for casual stargazers. However, on Nov. 25, and, for a few weeks on either side, it will be at its closest point to Earth this year and will appear at its brightest, making it possible to spot.

Opposition occurs when Earth passes between Uranus and the sun; Uranus will be "opposite" the sun, hence, "opposition." The moment of opposition will happen at 6 p.m. ET on Nov. 25, according to EarthSky. Consequently, Uranus will rise in the east around sunset, reach its highest point in the sky around midnight and set in the west around sunrise.

You can find it in the constellation Taurus, close to the Pleiades open star cluster, also known as the Seven Sisters and M45. Uranus will be a few degrees southeast of the cluster, appearing as a faint point of light, about two-fifths of the distance between the Pleiades and Aldebaran, the brightest star in Taurus.

At magnitude +5.6, Uranus is technically visible to the naked eye under very dark skies. However, binoculars or a telescope will make it much easier to find.

Uranus as seen by NASA's Hubble Space Telescope.

At opposition, Uranus will be 18.4 astronomical units (AU) from Earth — about 1.7 billion miles (2.7 billion kilometers) away. Through a telescope, it will appear as a tiny blue-green disk. Its color comes from methane in its atmosphere, which absorbs red wavelengths of sunlight.

Named after Ouranos, the ancient Greek god of the sky and the heavens, according to the Planetary Society , Uranus is one of the least understood planets in the solar system. A day on Uranus lasts for about 17 hours, and the planet rotates sideways, according to NASA. It takes 84 Earth years to orbit the sun.

Uranus also has 13 faint rings and at least 29 known moons, the most recent of which was found in 2025 by the James Webb Space Telescope. It was named Uranus XXVIII and orbits between the moons Ophelia and Bianca. Many of Uranus' moons are named after characters from the works of William Shakespeare and Alexander Pope.

The distant planet has been visited only once, by NASA's Voyager 2 spacecraft on Jan. 24, 1986. However, contrary to what the probe found, scientists now think the planet emits its own internal heat. That could increase pressure on NASA to prepare a Uranus Orbiter and Probe (UOP), a mission concept the National Academy of Sciences recommended in 2022 as a high-priority planetary science mission for the next decade.

Uranus will next come to opposition on Nov. 30, 2027.

Broader macro factors also weighed on sentiment. Markets continue to price in a potential Federal Reserve rate hike in December, with key inflation data (PCE) due shortly.
