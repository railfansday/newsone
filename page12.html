Pixel Tag, Google’s $30 Android-ready answer to Apple’s AirTag, is now available

📅 Nov 10, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Google’s answer to Apple’s AirTag, the Google Pixel Tag, goes on sale starting Tuesday, allowing Android users to track items like their keys, wallet, or bag using their smartphone’s built-in Find Hub.

First announced in August, the $29.99 device takes cues from the AirTag and other lost-item finders by offering connectivity via Bluetooth and ultra-wideband for precision finding. This lets Android device owners, including Pixel Watch wearers, locate their missing items using a combination of visual distance and directional guidance.

Plus, the tag has a built-in speaker to play a chime that helps you locate your item if nearby. (Or, if you’ve lost your phone, you can press the button on the Pixel Tag twice to ring it, too.) You can choose between three different levels of loudness in the settings within the Find Hub, with the maximum volume around 80 decibels.

Though the Pixel Tag serves the same purpose as an AirTag, Google has opted for a different design. Instead of a small, round device, like Apple’s, the Pixel Tag is slimmer and more elongated.

Spec-by-spec, the Pixel Tag is 1.1-inches wide, compared to the AirTag’s 1.26 inches. As the Pixel Tag is oval-shaped, it’s 1.8-inches high from top to bottom. Thickness-wise, the Pixel Tag clocks in at a depth of 0.2 inches, compared with the AirTag’s 0.31 inches (which Apple calls its height). The slimness of the device might make it more appealing for some, particularly if they’re adding the tag to a small item, like a keychain.

The device weighs just 0.4 oz, with the battery included.

Unlike Apple, Google opted for a physical button you can press on the tag, which makes it more obvious how it’s meant to work.

However, the Pixel Tag doesn’t include NFC, which Apple’s AirTag uses to aid the people who find lost items, as they can tap the side of the tag to launch a webpage that displays the owner’s contact information.

Instead, for lost items that are not nearby, the Pixel Tag relies on Google’s crowdsourced network of over a billion Android devices globally. The network, similar to competitive offerings from Apple and Tile, taps into the connectivity provided by other Android devices, which can identify and share the location of the lost item back to its owner. This location information is displayed on a map inside the Find Hub app.

While the person who found the item can’t tap it, as with NFC-enabled devices, Pixel Tags that are put into “Lost” mode will be able to share the owner’s email, number, and a short message so the person who finds the tag can contact you. When the tag comes back into range of the user’s phone, the item is automatically marked as found.

The Pixel Tag’s location can also be shared with up to 10 people in the Find Hub app, which could be useful for shared items like the family car keys, which multiple people might rely on.

Like AirTag, the Pixel Tag is also powered by a user-replaceable coin cell battery (the CR2032), which makes the device reusable for the long term. (Google claims the device has up to one year of battery life to start.)

Meanwhile, the device itself is made from stainless steel and features IP67 water and dust resistance (maximum depth of 1 meter up to 30 minutes). It pairs with devices running Android 9 or newer via Fast Pair technology.

The Pixel Tag comes in two shades: silver and a pale green, and can be bought individually or in a four-pack for $99.99.

Alongside the launch, the company Bellroy is debuting its own keychain cases in a variety of colors and styles, which will also be sold on the Google Store.

New U.S. vehicle prices hit a record high of $51,109, Kelley Blue Book said

📅 Nov 10, 2026  |  Category: Business  |  Read Source →

Free daily briefing on global business news.

The October average rose 1.3% from September and 2.1% from a year earlier, below the 3.6% typical annual gain for the month

Kelley Blue Book, a Cox Automotive brand, reported a record average transaction price of $51,109 for a new U.S. vehicle in October. The figure rose $649, or 1.3%, from a revised September average of $50,460 and was 2.1% above October 2025.

The year-over-year gain fell below the 3.6% longer-run average for October, according to Kelley Blue Book. The average manufacturer's suggested retail price rose to $52,933, up 1.4% from September and 2.4% from a year earlier.

Kelley Blue Book said price inflation and strong sales of midsize and full-size SUVs and full-size pickup trucks helped push the average higher. Incentives averaged $3,490, or 6.8% of the average transaction price, up from 6.7% in September and 6.5% a year earlier. Kelley Blue Book said that level remains below long-term averages.

Midsize SUVs became the top-selling segment in October, moving ahead of compact SUVs. The midsize SUV average transaction price was $50,570, up 1.9% from a year earlier. The full-size pickup average was $67,912, up 1.4%. The five highest-volume segments made up 64.4% of sales.

"The average is telling us as much about product mix as it is about affordability," Erin Keating, an executive analyst at Cox Automotive, said. She said three of the five highest-volume segments sold well below the $51,109 average, and vehicles priced from about $28,000 to $38,000 gained 9.6% in volume from a year earlier.

Electric vehicle prices also rose. The average transaction price for a new EV was $56,220, up 1% from September and down 2.7% from a year earlier. EV incentives averaged 11.3% of the price, up from 11.2% in September and 10.3% a year earlier.

The previous record was set in December 2025, according to NPR. Tariffs and buyer demand for larger, higher-priced vehicles have contributed to rising prices.

Microsoft awards $20K grants to 25 Seattle-area nonprofits amid state budget crunch and tax debate

📅 Nov 10, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

by Todd Bishop on Nov 10, 2026 at 10:48 amNovember 10, 2026 at 11:20 am

The Lighthouse for the Blind is adding braille displays to its computer lab. Friends of the Issaquah Salmon Hatchery is taking its lessons to low-income schools. And Lake Washington High School’s band boosters are replacing marching capes that date to the MS-DOS era.

Those are among 25 King County nonprofits that received $20,000 apiece from Microsoft in grants announced this week. It’s the first round of the company’s Community-First Grants, a $1 million program launched in September.

Microsoft said it received 813 applications.

“Boy, I think we hit a nerve,” said Jane Broom, Microsoft’s senior director of Community Affairs, during an event Monday recognizing the nonprofits on the company’s Redmond campus, featuring Mariners catcher Cal Raleigh and the team’s mascots.

The grants are meant for immediate needs, such as replacing a broken-down van or a failed freezer, Broom explained. Nonprofits that already received or were invited to receive Microsoft corporate grants in 2026 weren’t eligible.

Funding and the tax debate: Nonprofits are dealing with rising costs and tighter budgets. As a group, their largest funding source is the state government, which is in the middle of an ongoing debate with members of the business community over taxes and economic competitiveness amid a budget crunch.

Microsoft is among the companies asserting that current and proposed taxes are hurting the business climate. It’s also the second-largest funder of the state’s nonprofits, said Brad Smith, the company’s president and vice chair.

“In my view, a healthy nonprofit community actually has aspects of support from more than one source,” Smith said in an interview after the event, noting that state grants are often at risk when a recession forces budget cuts.

Where the money goes: Demand is climbing for many of the grant recipients. At Community Lunch on Capitol Hill, which serves free hot meals, it’s up 40% this year, and the diaper bank at Byrd Barr Place has grown from 720 families in 2021 to 1,600, Microsoft said at the event.

Friends of the Issaquah Salmon Hatchery gives more than 700 tours during the salmon season. It will use its grant to bring lessons to classrooms, mainly at Title I schools, which serve large numbers of low-income students and often can’t afford a field trip to the hatchery, said Mark Clemens, the group’s executive director.

“If they can’t bring the school to the hatchery, we will go out to the school,” said Clemens, a former Scientific American art director, who was creative director of the Modernist Cuisine cookbook project before taking over the nonprofit in 2023.

One bonus for the group, which goes by the clever acronym of FISH, was the appearance of the Mariners’ salmon mascots, which joined the group onstage for a photo op when Clemens and his colleagues accepted its grant.

The Lighthouse for the Blind, a Seattle-based nonprofit that employs blind people in manufacturing, retail and other jobs, will use its grant to upgrade its computer training lab with Windows 11 PCs, braille displays and wireless keyboards, said Antonio Rozier, the nonprofit’s IT director.

The goal is to keep employees current on screen readers and other tools so they can work alongside sighted colleagues, Rozier said. The Lighthouse also has computer labs at its three manufacturing plants, and employees can use them for personal tasks, since some don’t have that equipment at home.

Rozier called the upgraded lab “a game changer for the future.”

The Lake Washington High School Band and Jazz Boosters will buy 150 uniforms for the school’s marching band, which returned in 2025 after a 40-year absence and has been performing in capes from 1981.

Microsoft’s company culture: Most of the company’s local giving is directed by its employees. Microsoft matches their donations and adds $25 for every hour they volunteer, up to a combined $15,000 a year per person, Smith said.

That program accounts for most of the $1.9 billion that Microsoft and its employees have given to about 13,000 Washington nonprofits over the past 30 years, he said.

“We let our people decide where the money goes,” Smith said.

Microsoft’s employee base is going through a period of major change. After layoffs in July and September, the company offered voluntary buyouts last month to about 60,000 people, roughly a quarter of its global workforce. Employees have described mounting pressure to use AI, stagnant pay and a growing sense of job insecurity, The New York Times reported.

Asked about the buyouts, Smith said it’s a good time for Microsoft to keep investing in the employee experience, and he described the giving program as part of that.

Silicon Valley companies may offer more free food as a perk, he said, but Microsoft matches employee donations, giving workers who often move to the region from elsewhere a way to get involved in the community.

This year’s Microsoft Give campaign in October, the company’s biggest month for employee donations, was again a success, Smith said, showing that “employee participation and employee giving is alive and well.”

Microsoft plans to open a second round of applications for the Community-First Grants in the spring of 2027.

We’re in a James Bond Fever Dream Now

📅 Nov 10, 2026  |  Category: Entertainment  |  Read Source →

“Next James Bond ‘REVEALED,’” reads a headline published by The Sun last week. “After a loose-lipped co-star blabs secret after ‘a few lagers.’” The article suggests that Josh O’Connor, who has recently been named in the wider press as a last-minute hopeful in the race to find the next Bond actor, is the shoe-in to be 007—all based on what one of O’Connor’s cast mates in the theater production Golden Boy might have maybe told a stranger while drunk at The French House in London.

“They said that he had confided in them about getting the role when the cast of Golden Boy had celebratory drinks after the play finished its run on Halloween,” the article continues. So, there it is: we have our next Bond! Source: someone told someone else down the pub. No, really, honest!

This is nothing against O’Connor, mind. As Ben Allen argued in the latest edition of GQ’s Intermission newsletter, he would make for a killer, surprising pick, suggestive of an “actually new direction” delineating from the hyper-machismo that defined Bond through to the end of the Craig era. His characters tend to be quieter and more introspective; his performance style is similarly subtle. (The hipster choice? Maybe, but obviously a great one.) But the article is a symptom of how utterly insane the maelstrom of reports around the next Bond have become as we seemingly enter the casting endgame, with screen tests reportedly taking place in October. Further, producer Amy Pascal has said that we can probably expect an announcement to come before the end of the year.

Over the past weekend, Robert Pattinson and Nicholas Hoult emerged out of nowhere as having taken part in those aforementioned screen tests. Both would’ve come as whiplash to anyone who has had a passing interest in the endless Bond casting conversation: sure, they’ve been in and around the pack over the years, but given neither are called “Callum Turner” or “Jack Lowden,” the only speculated names taken seriously for much of 2026, they’re big—but both welcome!— shocks. I’m as much of an RPatz supremacist as anyone else who likes to think they know ball about films, the actor routinely being the best thing about his projects, from Nolan’s Tenet and all of his zany arthouse movies to both The Odyssey and Primetime this year. Much love to our man Hoult too, an underrated cornerstone of franchise cinema writ large, from X-Men to the new Superman. But still: what the hell?

In their own article about Pattinson and Hoult joining the race, Variety might’ve actually buried the most baffling lede: according to them, Harry Styles has “had discussions about the part,” while they also note that The Gentlemen’s Theo James—long thought out of the running—is actually yet to be ruled out. No shade on Styles, but he hasn’t been in a film since he briefly dipped a toe in acting in 2022’s Don’t Worry Darling and My Policeman, neither of which exactly kickstarted his movie career. And, like, he’s Harry Styles. Bond song? Yeah, we can see the vision. But the idea of him actually being 007 feels more like the product of an ayahuasca fever dream.

Regardless, Styles’s suitability for the role isn’t really the point. What’s more insane is that the Bond casting conversation only seems to have hit a level of psychosis as it nears the finish line: every other hour, the reported frontrunner switches from Lowden to Turner to O’Connor or Pattinson, a fever pitch of speculation and rumor-mongering that was tired before it was tired. Gary Oldman reckons it should be Lowden! Pierce Brosnan backs Turner! As one person astutely put it on Threads: “OH MY GOD JUST PICK A JAMES BOND. DO ANY JAMES BOND, JUST JAMES BOND A JAMES BOND.”

But it’s not just Bond himself, it seems. As I sat down to write this piece, an email hit my inbox from the publicist for a betting company. Subject line: “Maya Jama odds slashed to star in the next Bond film.” Damn it. Just bring back Pierce.

Plaza Premium Opening Hong Kong Arrivals Lounge… With Live Music?!

📅 Nov 10, 2026  |  Category: Business  |  Read Source →

Plaza Premium will be opening an arrivals lounge soon at Hong Kong International Airport (HKG). While these facilities are always useful for a shower and breakfast after an overnight flight, it sounds like there might be a bit more planned for this arrivals lounge? I’m certainly intrigued (thanks to Sel_D for flagging this)…

Plaza Premium is expected to open an arrivals lounge at Hong Kong Airport in late November 2026. The lounge will be located on level three of Terminal 2, in the arrivals portion of the terminal, below the Airport Express platform. That means it’ll be landside.

This actually technically represents the reopening of a lounge, as the global lounge operator had such a facility until 2019. At that point it closed due to an expansion project at the airport. Once open, it will be the only arrivals lounge at the airport. Cathay Pacific used to have an arrivals lounge at its hub, but it closed in 2018, and the airline has no plans to reopen such a facility.

The exact entry requirements of the Plaza Premium arrivals lounge in Hong Kong remain to be seen, but like most Plaza Premium lounges, the expectation is that entry will be possible through select credit cards and Priority Pass, and access can also be purchased directly.

Generally we think of an arrivals lounge in terms of its functional purpose, which is to shower, have breakfast, and get some work done, before heading into the city. Many arrivals lounges are only open in the morning, since that’s when there’s the most value. That’s especially true for people who either plan to head to the office, or who are staying at a hotel, where it’s well before the published check-in time.

While it remains to be seen what exactly Plaza Premium’s facility will be like, it sounds like this will be a rather unconventional arrivals lounge, in a good way. The social media post about the new lounge describes it as “Plaza Premium Lounge – Rhythms.” It will offer a “seamless blend of dining, music, wellness, and refreshment.”

In terms of the range of amenities, we’re being promised live music, wellness experiences, refined dining, and private resting spaces. I’m sorry… live music? Like… someone performing music live? In an arrivals lounge? Fascinatingly, the pictures make it look more like an evening-oriented lounge serving cocktails and wine, for lounging around, rather than your typical arrivals lounge.

A post shared by Plaza Premium Lounge (@plazapremiumlounge)

Suffice it to say that I am very interested in what they come up with here. From a monetization standpoint, this seems kind of smart, since it could drum up more business in the afternoons and evenings, when people might not otherwise consider visiting such a facility. Then again, are people really thinking “I just got off a 15 hour flight, what I could really use is a cocktail before heading to my hotel?” Maybe they are, I suppose!

In the coming weeks, Hong Kong Airport will once again have an arrivals lounge, thanks to the opening of a facility by Plaza Premium. Arrivals lounges are usually pretty basic, and are about showers and breakfast. While we’ll see how much of this is fluff rather than actual substance, Plaza Premium is promising what sounds more like a bar vibe, with live music and cocktails. Bring it on, Plaza Premium, I can’t wait to learn more!

AI Coding Agents Leak Thousands of Secrets to GitHub

📅 Nov 10, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

A command in Claude Code inadvertently saves files with credentials and other sensitive data to public Git repositories, creating security gaps for organizations.

AI coding assistants may inadvertently be leaking thousands of secrets to GitHub public repositories, making what seem like routine interactions between developers and these tools a gaping enterprise security risk.

Research from security firm Apiiro found 2,539 credentials across nearly 2,000 public GitHub repositories, with secrets such as database passwords, cloud keys, API tokens, and SSH root logins among the exposed data, according to a recent report published online. Apiiro tracked some of the leaked data to Claude Code, which was, unbeknownst to users, copying secrets to a hidden file and committing them with the rest of the developer's code.

Among specific secrets leaked were "a database vendor's own admin-level cloud key, a production Azure Postgres password, a Square production token, and the owner password of a medical-records database," Apiiro researchers Shmulik Cohen and Matan Giladi wrote in the post.

Related:AI Gave Your Incident Archive a Second Job

Most of the secrets belong to individual developers across 63 organizations, "from student groups to startups, software agencies, and established vendors," they wrote.

The issue stems from how coding assistants — Claude Code, in a specific case that the researchers examined — carry out certain commands. "Ask Claude Code to check a database or call an API, and the command it proposes carries the secret from your config file," the researchers explained.

The tool will ask the user before running the command and, if the choice is "Yes, and don't ask again," then "it saves that exact command, secret included, to .claude/settings.local.json inside the project folder," according to the report.

"Nothing in the repo marks that file as private, so it gets committed with the rest of the code," the researchers explained.

The fact that a routine development task creates a security exposure is dangerous for organizations, especially because these AI coding tools are supposed to be helpful, not harmful, notes Ryan McCurdy, vice president of marketing for database security firm Liquibase.

“A developer approves a command to save time, the AI agent retains credentials as part of that approval, and those credentials can eventually end up in a public repository," he observes via email. "Nobody intended to expose them, but the workflow made it possible."

In the case of Claude Code, Apiiro examined 104,185 publicly accessible copies of .claude/settings.local.json on GitHub, finding that 1,122 files contained real secrets. The researchers identified 1,379 distinct secrets in those files alone, including 636 provider-specific keys and 743 passwords or tokens associated with public hosts.

Related:What We Missed: Knicks Phishing Test Goes Out of Bounds

Claude Code is not being overtly careless; it does try to protect files when it creates its .claude/settings.local.json file inside a Git repository, the researchers noted. The tool does this by adding the file to the developer's global Git ignore list, which tells Git to ignore the file on that particular developer's machine. This prevents it from being included in routine commits.

However, the protection is not built into the development project itself, and the "ignore" rule does not travel with the repository when it is cloned onto another machine, the researchers noted. "The rule never goes into the repo's own .gitignore, so nothing travels with the project to protect it," they wrote.

Moreover, Claude Code itself was sometimes responsible for committing the exposed files. In 539 of the 1,105 repositories containing real secrets that the researchers investigated, a commit co-authored by Claude Code had modified the affected settings file.

The action that exposes these secrets while using Claude Code appears to be a "dead zone" of sorts for developers using AI coding assistants, so they likely have no idea that they've just exposed a secret, observes Seemant Sehgal, founder and CEO of AI penetration testing firm BreachLock.

"A 'don't ask again' prompt is a decision to write a credential into a file, and many developers clicking it likely don't know that file exists," he says. "Once it lands in a repository, it sits in a place many secret scanners were never built to check, which appears to be why some of these leaks slipped through."

Apiiro contacted Anthropic about the issue before publishing the findings. After their publication, Boris Cherny, Anthropic's head of Claude Code, said in public comments that although Claude Code historically has not done secrets scrubbing, the company is looking into what it can do to prevent the secrets exposure described by Apiiro, according to Apiiro's report.

Those approaches may be to make sure auto mode catches the exposure, or to build in basic secrets scanning, among other ideas that Anthropic will prototype "to understand the tradeoffs" before shipping something with its next version of Claude Code, Cherny said.

Indeed, the leakage exposes how Claude Code's protection relies heavily on a developer-specific Git setting rather than a safeguard built into the project or the AI coding tool itself, exposing a gap that can turn an ordinary command approval into a persistent credential leak.

To avoid this, Apiiro recommends that developers rotate every credential a file has ever held, and open all fields and delete every rule containing a secret, even if a file was never committed to a project. Meanwhile, security teams should treat all AI agent folders as secret-bearing on laptops as well as in repos, and add them to pre-commit and secret scanning, including history.

"As AI agents become more involved in software development, organizations need to recognize that source code isn't the only thing they need to secure," McCurdy observes. "Agent configuration files, transcripts, and execution histories can contain sensitive information, and traditional security checks may not catch everything."

‘Long Long Night’ Director Youm Kyu-bock on Creating Animation That Takes Kids Seriously

📅 Nov 10, 2026  |  Category: Business  |  Read Source →

The Korean filmmaker discusses the existential themes, environmental concerns and visual ambitions behind his acclaimed and unconventional animated feature debut.

Long Long Night is the rare children’s animation that respects its audience enough to believe they are equipped to handle some of the hardest realities of adult life — the inevitability of loneliness and the loss of our dearest loved ones, not to mention the cold, uncaring nature of the cosmos. The film is also beautifully poignant in its depiction of how we can create meaning and forge loving connections despite the fundamental brutality of our existential plight. Not bad for a colorful road movie for kids featuring a gentle rhinoceros and a baby penguin.The film marks the feature debut of Korean filmmaker Youm Kyu-bock, who spent two decades directing commercials, including campaigns for Samsung, BMW and Canon. A visual design graduate of Kookmin University, Youm worked as a producer at AlphaVille 44 and a director at Elvis Production before becoming CEO of GoogGoog Studio, an advertising and branded-content company now venturing into original features.Independent animated features remain relatively uncommon in South Korea, and the production tested how far Youm could stretch a budget of about 5 billion won ($3.7 million). He estimates that the film took four-and-a-half years to make, including an 18-month pause when funding ran short. The animation team typically numbered about 60, rising to roughly 120 artists at the height of production.Youm’s film is adapted from an award-winning children’s book of the same name that has sold more than 800,000 copies in South Korea. The story’s hero, Norden, is a White Boulder rhino who experiences the uniquely tragic plight of becoming the last living example of his species (the book’s author, Ru Ri, took inspiration from Sudan, the real-life last male northern white rhinoceros who died in captivity in 2018). Raised by caring elephants in an orphanage, Norden eventually ventures out on his own and finds a mate. They have a daughter, and for a brief while life is good. Then poachers kill his partner and child, leaving Norden badly wounded. He is rescued by conservationists but soon confined to a zoo. Later, a civil war breaks out, and rocket fire punctures the zoo’s fences; Norden escapes and falls in with a plucky penguin who is guarding an abandoned egg. The egg eventually passes into Norden’s care, and when it hatches, the rhino and the chick make their way across desert and savanna toward the sea. The orphan once taken in by elephants now finds himself raising a creature whose home is a place he will never be able to venture.Made in 3D with Unreal Engine, the film gives Norden’s weathered hide and Africa’s endless grasslands and deserts an almost tactile detail. But Youm uses light and color to temper that realism, lending the animals’ experiences in the African landscape the warmth and wonder of a storybook illustration.Long Long Night screened in competition at the Busan International Film Festival in October, drawing praise for the nuance of its storytelling and the painterly detail of its animation. It had its world premiere at the Toronto International Film Festival in September, making it the first Korean animated feature to debut at TIFF since Yeon Sang-ho’s The Fake in 2013.GoogGoog Studio and Studio Eon produced the film, with The Contents On presenting. Seoul-based Finecut handles worldwide sales and will give the English-dubbed version its first market screening at AFM on Nov. 11.The Hollywood Reporter recently connected with Youm in Seoul to discuss the creation of Long Long Night.How did you first encounter Ru Ri’s book, and what made you want to turn it into a film?I had originally been developing a horror thriller series, but it stalled when COVID-19 hit. That was when I remembered Long Long Night, a book I had read with my daughter some time before and been deeply moved by. We later got permission from the publisher, Munhakdongne, and from Ru Ri herself, and the film project began.What drew me most to the original was the emotional bond between Norden the rhino and the baby penguin. I also loved its road-movie structure — two lonely souls who have each suffered great loss, leaning on one another as they make their way to the sea. The scene of Norden and the penguin crossing the harsh desert in particular made me feel a strong urge to bring it to the screen.As Norden raises the baby penguin, love slowly heals the pain of his losses, and the penguin, nurtured by that love, grows into an independent being. That process was so tender, and it left my heart full. When Norden finally lets the penguin go into the sea, I couldn’t hide my tears in front of my daughter. It felt like watching our own lives play out in miniature. And with that story unfolding in a setting as captivating as Africa, I knew I had to make it into a film. For a children’s story, both the book and the film are unusually frank about loss, human cruelty and death. How did you decide how far to go with those elements, particularly for younger viewers?Ironically, part of why the original book is so popular is because it confronts those themes head-on. But young audiences are very important to us, so we put a lot of effort into keeping the tragic beauty and emotional mood of the original to a certain degree, while making sure it never came across as gruesome or shocking.For the brutal poaching scenes, we tried to imply them through sound and the mood of the lighting, and for the deaths of the supporting characters, we avoided showing them directly as much as possible, pulling back the angle and creating some distance so that viewers could understand what happened through the context of the story. Still, even after all of this, the world of Long Long Night is indeed not a film coated in the fairy-tale beauty of conventional children’s films, and that was never what we were aiming for.After everything Norden has lost, he finds an unexpected modicum of solace in taking responsibility for another vulnerable life. What interested you about that response to grief?I believe that those who have been deeply wounded recognize the wounds of others more easily, and that is why they cannot turn away.There is a Korean saying that goes, “The bond of raising a child is more fearsome than the bond of giving birth.” “Fearsome” here doesn’t mean scary or frightening; it means that the love built up through living together is that much deeper and stronger. Norden and the baby penguin are not related by blood, nor are they even the same species, but through countless long long nights together they gradually become each other’s family. It’s also the kind of salvation story I personally love. But what makes it all the more meaningful is that it isn’t a typical Hollywood hero story; it’s a story about “ordinary heroes,” fragile beings who stand together, heal one another and, in the end, come to realize the true meaning of love.Was communicating an environmentalist message — or more care for animals and the natural world — something that was important to you with this project?Personally, the scene that hurts me the most is when the humans at the zoo cut off Norden’s horn after he has already lost his friend Angabu to horn poachers. Having lost both his friend and his horn, Norden is in shock and sunk in grief, while the human visitors walk right past him, laughing and chattering away. That scene was meant to show how easily an animal’s life can be damaged or cast aside by human indifference.Behind the deaths of Norden’s family and the supporting characters, there are almost always traces of human violence — poaching and war. These are the villains. They are shown in shadow and silhouette. But there are also humans who do their best to protect the lives of the animals. These I showed in bright light and in close-up. I directed it so as to show the audience — and especially younger viewers — that there are broadly two kinds of people with regard to animals and to ask them where they would want to stand. Although, to be honest, it’s framed as a question, I’m quite clearly gently nudging them towards the answer: “The kind humans are better, aren’t they?” (Laughs.)I found the film’s animation style to be quite beautiful and emotionally affecting. How would you describe your style and what were some of your inspirations?I guess I would describe my broader intentions as something like, “Telling an existential story in the language of a fairy tale.” I’m drawn to such stories because this world is not so simple that it can be told as a fairy tale. And when the characters are delivering their lines, I think my camera tends to focus on the mood of the space around them. I think it comes from my background as a commercial director, and I also seem to favor scene transitions, close-ups and building a mood without dialogue. A phrase I tend to use often with my team is “the color temperature of the air.” I’m obsessive about varying the color of the lighting depending on the situation the characters are in. I also tend to shoot tragic scenes boldly, in a documentary-like way, while giving emotional scenes a fairy-tale palette and plenty of time.As for inspirations, Steven Spielberg is a director I deeply admire, and I picked up tips from E.T. on how to portray humans according to a storytelling binary of good and evil. In composing what functions as a war scene in Long Long Night, I even quoted a specific shot from Saving Private Ryan.For me, what matters more than the mechanics of how events unfold is the time emotion is allowed to linger. My obsession with this idea is probably what produced the style of Long Long Night more than anything else.How would you describe the film’s existential worldview — and what do you hope the film leaves young viewers thinking about?The world is cruel and indifferent. Our existence is finite and fragile. There are many people living lives without purpose. But I believe that even if life comes with no given meaning, as long as you have the heart to love and protect someone, you can create meaning in your life. And if there were one message I’d want to leave with the audience, it would be something like. … Even if you feel worn down and insignificant right now, please don’t forget that just by being born, you were already a radiant joy to someone.

Trump's first Iran war escalation post-midterm losses seeks to choke off Tehran’s trade

📅 Nov 10, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Farzin Nadimi explains how the U.S. could target Iran’s remaining land routes — and why sealing them off will be a "mid- to long-term project."

President Donald Trump isn't backing off his pressure campaign against Iran despite claims the conflict contributed to sweeping Republican losses in last week's midterm elections.

In fact, Trump is doubling down by expanding U.S. efforts to choke off the trade routes Tehran increasingly relies on as it tries to rebuild its military in the midst of war.

The new effort, dubbed Task Force Iron Gate, takes aim at a vulnerability that has persisted despite the U.S. naval blockade: the land and Caspian Sea routes Iran can use to bring in goods, dual-use technology and military supplies beyond the reach of American warships.

That approach, one expert claims, fits into a broader administration strategy of tightening the squeeze on Tehran while stopping short of restarting full-scale military operations. Meanwhile, others caution that it could produce escalation rather than an immediate diplomatic breakthrough.

IRAN VOWS ‘ASTONISHING’ STRIKES AND LIFTS MISSILE-RANGE LIMIT AS US TARGETS LAND ROUTES

An Iranian cargo truck crosses into the Pakistan-Iran border as an Iranian national flag (L) flies at half-mast at Taftan, Balochistan province on March 1, 2026 after the death of Iran's supreme leader Ayatollah Ali Khamenei amid US-Israel strikes. (Banaras KHAN / AFP via Getty Images)

"We have Operation Iron Gate that just went into effect. That means nothing comes in by land," Trump said Sunday.

But the roughly 20-person task force established at U.S. Central Command headquarters in Tampa is taking a quieter approach than Trump’s sweeping description might suggest.

"This is not a deployable special operation task force," Farzin Nadimi, a senior fellow at the Washington Institute for Near East Policy, told Fox News Digital, describing Iron Gate instead as a "fusion cell" coordinating military, civilian and intelligence efforts.

Nadimi said its immediate role is likely to center on gathering intelligence about activity along Iran’s borders and building cooperation in the region that could support sanctions and other efforts to disrupt the flow of goods.

Secretary of State Marco Rubio, who heads the department responsible for spearheading the administration's diplomatic foreign agenda, supports taking action that could decrease military escalation.

"We don't need to conduct military strikes if we're achieving the same objective through economic means and economic pressure," Rubio told reporters on Tuesday.

He rejected the suggestion that the U.S. and Iran were at a stalemate, arguing that sanctions, the naval blockade and other measures were steadily weakening Tehran.

"They’re bleeding. They’re bleeding economically," Rubio said. "It is an increased pressure on Iran that continues to grow every day."

The push comes a week after an election in which the war proved a political liability for Republicans.

The Iran war was a crucial factor in the House vote for 34% of voters, according to the Fox News Voter Poll, while about six in 10 said the U.S. had made the wrong decision to take military action against Iran.

An Iranian oil tanker is seen floating on the Caspian Sea, as Iranian President Mohammad Khatami attends the opening ceremony of the Neka oil terminal on the Caspian Sea, 340 kms northwest of Tehran in Mazandaran province. (HENGHAMEH FAHIMI/AFP via Getty Images)

A separate Fox News/NORC survey conducted around Election Day found 68% said avoiding a prolonged military conflict in Iran was very important. When forced to choose, 61% said ending U.S. military involvement to lower gas prices was more important than restarting military action to stop Iran from developing nuclear weapons, while 35% chose restarting military action.

Yet four days after the election, CENTCOM announced Iron Gate, extending the administration’s pressure campaign to the land routes Tehran could use to blunt the effects of the naval blockade.

CENTCOM said the task force would coordinate efforts to disrupt advanced weapons, dual-use technologies, sanctioned materials and illicit revenue moving across Iran’s land borders and the Caspian Sea.

"We will not allow the Iranian regime to rebuild its weapons factories or replenish its arsenal with materials smuggled across its borders," CENTCOM commander Adm. Brad Cooper said in announcing the task force.

CENTCOM declined to provide additional details about how Iron Gate would operate beyond its announcement.

The challenge is the sheer scale of the territory involved.

Iran shares land borders with Afghanistan, Armenia, Azerbaijan, Iraq, Pakistan, Turkey and Turkmenistan, spanning roughly 6,000 kilometers, according to Nadimi. While it has dozens of official border crossings, goods can also be smuggled through mountainous and desert terrain.

"It will be extremely difficult to know where and how things get in or out of Iran," Nadimi said.

Much of Iron Gate’s effectiveness will also depend on governments outside Iran.

Nadimi said the task force could build relationships with military and intelligence services in neighboring countries to identify and disrupt suspicious shipments. Turkey and Pakistan have already placed some limits on goods moving across their borders with Iran, he said, contributing to long truck lines and higher shipping costs.

But even if neighboring governments cooperate, Iran and the Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps are likely to look for ways around the restrictions.

Behnam Ben Taleblu, senior director of the Iran program at the Foundation for Defense of Democracies, said the effort should not be judged by whether Washington can completely eliminate Iran’s overland trade.

"That is not going to mean zeroing out this trade overnight," Taleblu told Fox News Digital. Instead, he said the goal is to increase the cost of moving goods, pressure the foreign companies and individuals facilitating Iranian trade and give Washington more leverage over Tehran.

"This is literally akin to boxing in the Islamic Republic," he said.

The routes have taken on added importance as Iran’s other avenues to the outside world have narrowed.

Taleblu said Iran’s missile program remains dependent on foreign inputs including fuel precursors and high-strength steel. China and Russia are important to those flows, he said, but Iraq, Turkey and Pakistan also represent important regional routes.

The U.S. military has been enforcing a naval blockade in the Strait of Hormuz, even during the ceasefire. (U.S. Central Command)

Nadimi described effectively restricting Iran’s land trade as a "mid- to long-term project" and predicted Tehran would initially "become more aggressive" as the pressure mounts.

"It is entirely plausible that you squeeze them hard enough that they feel like the only way to throw off the strangulation is to throw a punch," he said.

That leaves the administration trying to increase its leverage over Iran without reigniting the full-scale fighting voters strongly signaled they wanted to avoid — while Trump has publicly described the objective in much starker terms.
