Why Serie A players are wearing red paint on their faces in Week 12 fixtures

📅 Nov 21, 2026  |  Category: Sports  |  Read Source →

Serie A players and coaches will be donning a splash red paint across their cheeks during the 12th round of league fixtures coming up this weekend to raise awareness for the ‘Un Rosso alla Violenza (‘a red card to violence’) campaign.

This will be the 10th time that Serie A has participated in the Rosso alla Violenza campaign, which is aimed to raise awareness around domestic violence towards women.

A number of gestures to raise awareness for the campaign are planned for the 12th round of Serie A fixtures: Players, coaches and officials will don the red face paint, and for the first time, supporters in the stands have been encouraged to participate as well.

Serie A captains will also wear a symbolic armband during Week 12. The hashtag #unrossoallaviolenza and several adverts and graphics will be shown in the stadiums and will be broadcast on TV ahead of kick-off.

“Serie A has been at the forefront of the fight against violence towards women for 10 years, and coincide with the international day for the elimination of violence against women during the 12th round of league fixtures, we will renew our determination and commitment to combating all forms of abuse and especially towards women,” said Lega Serie A President Ezio Simonelli.

Marco Chiesara, President of WeWorld, who organise the campaign, added: “Violence against women is not a private matter and does not only concern those who suffer from it. It is a collective responsibility. With this campaign, we want to reach a wide audience, particularly men, and invite them to question their own role in combating it.”

Bayern Munich are considering selling Jamal Musiala over concerns that his ongoing health problems will prevent him from returning to his best form.

Musiala, 23, was diagnosed with a “neurological dysfunction” after suffering an on-pitch collapse twice in a matter of days during pre-season friendlies against Heidenheim and RB Leipzig.

The Germany international said he was “very optimistic... there is no additional health risk beyond this” and indicated the condition would not hamper his highly promising career.

However, Bayern chief Karl-Heinz Rummenigge has now admitted that the club are assessing his future amid a downturn in performances, publicly questioning whether the club can now justify his lofty salary.

Rummenigge told Sky Germany: “He has a health-related issue. It's also well known that he has a contract involving very substantial sums of money. As a club, we have to consider how to deal with that situation. I think that's responsible and perfectly normal.

“What we know unfortunately suggests it will be difficult for him to get back to the level he has often shown here in this stadium.”

Musiala was reportedly Bayern’s best-paid player until Ballon d’Or winner Harry Kane penned a contract extension earlier this month.

He has been limited to just four appearances in the Bundesliga this term due to a muscle injury, scoring one goal and notching one assist, with fitness problems continuing to plague him after spending over half of last season out with a broken leg.

Rummenigge says “a decision will have to be made” over his Musiala if he fails to show signs of improvement, but admitted that the former Chelsea academy product’s health issues would make a sale difficult.

Rummenigge added: “We need to talk to the family, the player, and the agency, because then these sums of money, sorry, with all due respect, are no longer feasible.

“I cannot imagine that Jamal wants to leave the club or that any other club would buy him under these conditions.

“We are all interested in his return, ideally in top form. But what happens if that isn’t the case? Then we still have to think about the finances.

“The discussion hasn’t taken place yet. These are just ideas at this stage.”

Fortnite Officially Disables Geysers Due To Flying Exploit

📅 Nov 21, 2026  |  Category: Other  |  Read Source →

Fortnite players are coming up with some incredibly inventive ways to make the Pirate Shop Access grind a little easier. Endlessly opening chests gets tedious, fast, and the community wants to scratch off as many lottery tickets as it can.

One pretty busted way of doing that was ramming an SUV into a geyser and jumping out to infinitely glide, essentially giving players flight. That might not sound useful, but you could fly into and destroy entire buildings, opening hundreds of chests in minutes.

Epic Games quickly caught wind of what players were doing and, earlier this morning, disabled geysers. Considering the next Treasure Hours is today, with boosted token odds, it's no wonder the team worked so quickly to shut this one down.

This comes just days after the Respawn Rumble exploit. For whatever reason, rewards — including the Pirate Shop Access tokens — were enabled, and it was clear this was unintentional because they popped up with greyed-out chest icons and the name, 'PH' (placeholder).

Since you would respawn infinitely in Respawn Rumble, players took advantage of this open window to rush the storm and open however many chests they could. Later that day, Epic rolled out a fix.

Don't get the RNG twisted, though. Opening 137,500 chests doesn't guarantee you a token. There's a 0.00073% chance of finding the Pirate Shop Access, and the odds reset on every single chest. The reason players are finding inventive ways to open as many chests as possible is simply to roll the dice more, but your odds are in the toilet no matter how many you open.

Still, as far as Pirate Shop Access grinds go, flying like Superman into every building, boat, and pirate cove is one of the more creative.

Fortnite players are already getting fed up hunting for Pirate Shop tokens, and are calling on Epic Games to disclose the odds.

If you haven't given up and moved onto GTA 6 yet, the next Treasure Hours window is later today at 11am PT (7pm GMT), with 1.5x boosted odds (meaning you have a 1 in 91,667 chance of finding a token, or a 0.00109% chance). You won't be able to open 500 chests in minutes with the SUV-geyser trick, but if you come back at 6pm PT (2am GMT), the odds will be doubled.

Mamdani’s plan to dismantle NYPD’s riot unit ripped as ‘smoke and mirrors’ by former force leaders

📅 Nov 21, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Former NYPD Chief John Chell and former NYPD inspector Paul Mauro are accusing New York City Mayor Zohran Mamdani's new disbanding of the Strategic Response Group as a move to please his base, with little real change to protest response being made.

New York City Mayor Zohran Mamdani's plan to gut the city’s tactical police unit is not what is seems, two former high-ranking NYPD officials tell Fox News Digital.

They argue the stunt is a political sleight of hand in which specialized officers responsible for handling violent protests will be rebranded under a different name despite the socialist mayor's promise to disband it completely.

"The problem is…day-to-day New Yorkers don't understand all this gas lighting," former NYPD Chief John Chell told Fox News Digital. "And they feel the angst of it."

"But at the end of all that, this is nothing more than smoke and mirrors," he added.

EXCLUSIVE: RETIRED POLICE SERGEANT ISSUES WARNING OVER MAMDANI'S NYPD SHAKE-UP AHEAD OF THANKSGIVING PARADE

New York City Mayor Zohran Mamdani attends a news conference at NYPD headquarters in New York City on Jan. 6, 2026. (Spencer Platt/Getty Images)

The move to dissolve the Strategic Response Group (SRG) and replace it with a "first-of-its-kind" civilian dialogue team fulfills one of Mamdani's campaign promises — noted in the first sentence of his news release on the move.

But Chell and retired NYPD inspector Paul Mauro are questioning whether it's all a facade to please the mayor's left-wing base and is a restructuring rather than a disbandment. They say the unit will likely be rebuilt under a new name to continue dealing with mass protests, major crimes and counterterrorism.

"The con here is that the SRG is simply being rebranded into an unknown name right now," Chell said.

"First of all, Mamdani did add…that there will be uniformed police standing by to take more forceful enforcement if necessary," Mauro said. "So what really changed? Well, he changed the name of that unit and he inserted this civilian unit between those uniformed cops and the protesters."

The newly proposed civilian-led Community Dialogue and De-escalation Division (CDDD) is meant to work as a buffer between protesters and the on-duty police that will remain present at large demonstrations.

"Socialism and public safety do not go hand in hand," Chell insisted.

Mamdani’s office did not respond to Fox News Digital’s request for comment on the criticism of his plan.

NYPD officers arrest a demonstrator during an anti-ICE protest outside 26 Federal Plaza Building in New York City, New York, U.S., June 10, 2025. (Mostafa Bassim/Anadolu via Getty Images)

Both former NYPD officials warned that relying on civilians to mediate rowdy crowds could delay police intervention if a peaceful protest turns into a violent riot. This, he said, could leave officers responding to an escalating situation rather than preventing it in the first place.

"Once a cop, wherever they're assigned, becomes reactive versus proactive — public safety suffers," Chell said. "And [when cops] become reactive, they tend to hold back and that can get them hurt, and it can get civilians hurt."

"That is one of the risks inherent in this thing," Mauro added. "That you deploy the civilians, they're trying to talk…and by the time the civilians turn around and say, ‘Police, we need help,’ it may be too late."

SRG receives information from NYPD’s Intelligence and Counterterrorism Bureau to be kept aware of known or potentially dangerous demonstrations that will require specialized training to deescalate.

The SRG unit is trained to handle crowds that have turned violent, beginning with verbal de-escalation. The physical training teaches officers how to get a noncomplying individual and into police vans. A common tactic used by the SRG is bicycle crowd management where officers can line up their bikes to build a barricade in crowds, using those formations for crowd control.

"The purpose of the SRG was to make sure that you had highly trained officers who could diffuse, spin down, but then control if need be, protests that were getting out of hand," Mauro said. "That's why they were only deployed in about 6% of the instances."

In a budget hearing earlier this year it was noted the SRG did not often deal with peaceful protests, and were mostly only deployed for protests that turned violent. In 2025, NYPD reportedly facilitated 4,255 protests and the SRG was available for roughly 6% of them.

Critics of the SRG, including the ACLU's New York affiliate, say the unit is inherently violent due to its counterterrorism specialization that they claim "suppress protesters" and disregards "focus on First Amendment protections."

The training manual, reviewed by Fox News Digital, distinguishes between "peaceful/compliant" crowds and those deemed "violent/non-compliant." It also identifies three categories of protesters: "everyday citizen," "professional" and "anarchist."

The first identifier under the "everyday citizen" protester immediately acknowledges that they are honoring their First Amendment right to organize.

Mamdani has not yet said when the civilian-led team will be established or take the streets. He also announced the creation of the First Amendment Support Team (FAST), a 30-officer NYPD unit, that will help deescalate crowds while the CDDD is being developed.

Chell, who has announced he will be running for mayor of New York City in 2029 when Mamdani would likely be seeking a second term, said he doubts the new team will take form until at least 2028 – if ever.

A new positioning technology could make autonomous vehicles and robotaxis more reliable at recharging themselves.

When you purchase through links on our site, we may earn an affiliate commission. Here’s how it works.

Researchers in Japan have developed a new technology that could enable electric vehicles (EVs) to charge "simply by parking".

Wireless charging is still relatively nascent in the EV market, but scientists hope that integrating more wireless charging devices into roads and parking spaces will move the EV industry away from bulky plug-in infrastructure. It uses the same wireless power transfer technology found in modern smartphones and consumer electronics: a transmitter coil inside in a ground pad generates an oscillating magnetic field, which induces an electric current in a receiver coil underneath the vehicle. This current recharges the EV's battery cells.

However, small misalignments between the two coils can affect how quickly and efficiently the battery recharges. This new system uses short-range radio signals to determine the position of the vehicle's magnetic charging coil relative to the charging device in the ground, including its distance and angle. This helps the EV and its driver get into the optimal position for efficient charging.

The technology could be particularly useful for driverless "robotaxis" and other autonomous vehicles that need to be able to recharge quickly and efficiently without human assistance, the researchers said. They detailed their findings in a study published Aug. 26 in the IEEE Open Journal of Vehicular Technology.

"We hope this research will lead to a future where EVs charge simply by parking, much like smartphones placed on wireless chargers," Ryotaro Hada, first author of the study and a master's student at the University of Osaka's Graduate School of Information Science and Technology, said in a statement.

Existing methods for aligning an EV's charging coil with a wireless charging pad use magnetic sensors or cameras. Magnetic systems work best when the coil is close to the pad, while cameras can struggle in rain, poor lighting or when dirt obscures the pad, the researchers explained.

This new system uses four ultra-wideband radio devices mounted around the vehicle's charging coil to communicate with four antennas around the charging pad.

Get the world’s most fascinating discoveries delivered straight to your inbox.

By measuring how long radio waves take to travel between the ultra-wideband devices and the antennas, as well as detecting tiny differences in how the signals arrive at each of the vehicle's sensors, the system can pinpoint the position and angle of the EV's charging coil relative to the ground pad.

The same technology is used in some digital car keys. They use short radio pulses to detect when the driver is close by, allowing the driver to unlock their vehicle without requiring a physical key.

In laboratory experiments using a scaled-down vehicle model, the system worked out where the vehicle's charging coil was to within 0.09 inches (2.32 millimeters) of its actual position and which direction it was facing to within 0.25 degrees. Correcting for distortions in the radio signals reduced the positioning error by around fivefold, the team said.

The researchers noted that the system still needs to be tested outdoors, on full-size EVs and under real charging conditions.

When I use the Aura, I get what it's supposed to be. It's the taste of a Vision Pro or Quest headset in a lighter, more comfortable package. Xreal has set the bar; it'll be up to everyone to see if they can raise it.

Mercury is about to receive a robotic visitor from Earth.

The BepiColombo mission — a joint effort of the European Space Agency (ESA) and the Japanese Aerospace Exploration Agency (JAXA) — will arrive in orbit around Mercury today (Nov. 21), more than eight years after leaving its home planet.

The big moment will come after an orbit-insertion burn, which is scheduled to begin today at 1:24 p.m. EST (1824 GMT) and last for 37 minutes.

BepiColombo consists of two main spacecraft: ESA's Mercury Planetary Orbiter (MPO) and JAXA's Mercury Magnetospheric Orbiter (MMO), also known as Mio.

The duo launched — along with a power and propulsion element called the Mercury Transfer Module (MTM) — aboard an Ariane 5 rocket from French Guiana in October 2018, beginning a long and looping trip through the inner solar system.

Mercury is a difficult planet to reach because it's so close to the sun. Spacecraft approaching the solar system's innermost planet are accelerated to high velocities by the immense gravitational pull of our star and must shed a great deal of kinetic energy to be captured by Mercury's much weaker tug.

So the BepiColombo team designed a trajectory that featured one flyby of Earth, two of Venus and six of Mercury over the course of about seven years. That flight time was increased by a year, however, after the mission suffered an issue with its solar panels, which reduced the power available for the MTM's electric thrusters.

Today's orbital insertion is the latest milestone in BepiColombo's Mercury arrival phase, which is expected to take about six months in total.

Arrival began on Sept. 3, when the MTM released MPO and Mio. The two orbiters remain connected today; they won't separate until Dec. 9 or thereabouts.

Shortly thereafter, Mio will settle into its final orbit, a highly elliptical path that will take it over Mercury's poles. MPO is also destined for a polar orbit, though a much less elliptical one than Mio's; the ESA probe will conduct a series of engine burns before finally getting there on March 10, 2027.

BepiColombo won't start doing science work until April 6, 2027. The two probes will gather complementary data from their two different vantage points, shedding a great deal of light on a mysterious world.

"The mission will study all aspects of Mercury, from the structure and dynamics of its magnetosphere and how it interacts with the solar wind, to its internal structure with its large iron core, and the origin of the planet’s magnetic field," ESA officials wrote in a BepiColombo mission description.

"The data will enable scientists to understand more about the origin and evolution of a planet located close to its parent star, and a better understanding of the overall evolution of our solar system," they added.

The Trump administration’s approach to cutting red tape is starting to pay off.

A rendering of the proposed Xe-100 nuclear reactor facility at Dow’s Seadrift plant in Texas.

Federal regulators have approved construction of four advanced nuclear reactors at a Dow chemical plant in Texas, the first approval of its type in 60 years and the next step in America’s red tape–free nuclear renaissance.

The U.S. Nuclear Regulatory Commission (NRC) issued a construction permit for the proposed plant featuring four Xe-100 small modular reactors (SMRs) at Dow’s Seadrift site in Texas — the first construction permit for a high-temperature gas-cooled reactor issued in the U.S. in more than half a century. It’s the most recent sign that President Trump’s cutting of nuclear red tape last year is paying dividends.

A Swing Through Montana’s Art Treasures

📅 Nov 21, 2026  |  Category: Other  |  Read Source →

Life on the range, Native spirits, Vienna, and Little Big Horn are pretty good raw material.

A “Greetings from Montana” postcard published by the E. C. Kropp Company, c. 1940.

Steven R. Shook Collection, via Wikimedia Commons, CC BY 2.0

Today I’ll write about Big Sky Country — Montana. I’d never been. The Mountain States aren’t the place to go for Raphaels, Rembrandts, and Renoirs, but there are treasures to be found. What ought to be there? I very easily found my answer: Montana’s own Charles M. Russell, the perceptive, precise artist famous for his cowboy pictures but proudest of his scenes of Montana’s Chippewa, Blood Indian, and Lakota Natives. At 16, Russell (1864–1926) left school in Missouri to work on a sheep ranch in Montana. He lived under those spacious skies for the rest of his life, making thousands of works of art. A contemporary of Frederic Remington, he’s less known but just as good if not better.

Russell has a museum of his own in Great Falls, Mont., but he’s also got a suite of revelatory galleries at the Montana Heritage Center in Helena, my starting point as I explored the state from west to east. The Heritage Center, whose galleries are filled with art and artifacts from Montana’s Historical Society, opened late last year after a $160 million expansion and renovation. It’s wonderful. The Russell section is more or less chronological and a mix of art, hagiography, and atmosphere. I loved it. The entire new complex is great. It’s got a Russell section as well as multiple galleries on Montana’s history.

Brian T. Allen is National Review’s art critic.
