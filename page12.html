Galaxy Research Withdraws Call That Bitcoin's Bear Market Is Over

📅 Nov 26, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Galaxy Research Withdraws Call That Bitcoin's Bear Market Is Over

Galaxy Research has withdrawn its call that Bitcoin's bear market ended in June, saying the signal that underpinned it has failed.

"We withdraw our call that the bear market ended in June," the firm wrote in a report sent to clients on Tuesday. It said a daily close below Bitcoin's June 30 closing low of $58,525 would mark "the start of a move to a materially lower low."

Bitcoin is currently changing hands at around $60,600, up 3.4% over the past 24 hours, according to Decrypt data. It fell as low as $57,308 during that period, as inflation data and proposed U.S. tariffs weighed on markets.

In an October 26 report, Galaxy's head of research, Alex Thorn, argued that Bitcoin had bottomed in June, in part because it had climbed back above its 50-week moving average in September after 44 weeks below it. In past cycles, such a sustained recovery above that average had only come after a bear market's low.

That reclaim lasted seven weeks. Bitcoin closed below the 50-week average in the week ended November 8, and the following week fell below its 200-week average, which Galaxy describes as historically the floor. The October report had set a tripwire: a weekly close back below the 50-week average that was not regained within a month. To regain it by around December 8, Bitcoin would need to rise almost 22% from its November 22 close. "We are not waiting for it," Galaxy wrote.

On an intraday basis, Bitcoin has already set a new bear market low, falling to $55,100 on November 21, below its July 1 intraday low. On closing prices, the June 30 low of $58,525 is still intact.

In seven previous bear markets in which Bitcoin closed below a low that had held for at least 60 days, the eventual bottom came at least 17% lower, with a median of 52%, according to Galaxy. Applied to the June low, that would put Bitcoin at about $48,500 at the shallowest and $28,200 at the median.

The firm said the evidence points two ways. By the calendar, this bear market is already older than any before it, 412 days after Bitcoin's October 2025 record. By depth, it looks unfinished: Bitcoin's 53% decline compares with 77% to 93% in previous bear markets, and it has not yet closed below its realized price of $53,205, the average price at which all coins last moved, something every prior bear did for at least 140 days. Only two of the 13 bottom signals Galaxy tracks have been met.

Galaxy said a weekly close back above the 200-week average, now $68,159, and then the 50-week average, now $74,401, would move it back toward its October view.

Tim Sun, senior researcher at HashKey, told Decrypt he agreed the market remained in a bear phase, but said a longer bear market need not mean a deeper one. As Bitcoin is pulled into global asset allocation, its price is driven increasingly by "macro liquidity, interest-rate expectations, and institutional risk appetite, rather than solely following the crypto market's traditional four-year cycle," he said.

U.S. spot Bitcoin ETFs recorded $453.1 million in net outflows on Wednesday, their seventh straight day of withdrawals, according to SoSoValue data. That brings November's net outflows to $5.39 billion, extending the funds' worst month on record.

Options traders are also positioning for further declines. Bitcoin's put/call open interest ratio on Deribit rose to 0.82 last week, its highest in more than a year, according to a weekly report from digital asset trading platform Talos.

"BTC's muted rebound shows the relief in equities has not translated into demand for crypto," Tanay Ved, senior research associate at Talos, said in the report. "Until short covering turns into net buy pressure and sustained spot demand, the move remains fragile heading into thin holiday liquidity."

Mikel Arteta’s side are still in fine shape to progress straight through to the last 16 as one of the top-eight sides in the Champions League. Only the Gunners, holders Paris Saint-Germain and Real Madrid remain unbeaten in this season’s competition.

However, it could have been so much better, after an extraordinarily wasteful display in the draw with Borussia Dortmund on Tuesday night. Arsenal registered 30 shots, but only four on target and they scored just once.

It should have been so much more for Martin Odegaard, too. He has registered only one assist in Europe this season, ranking him 45th in the competition for that metric.

The discourse around Odegaard is often hypercritical. That, to an extent, is understandable. He has been the captain and creative heartbeat of the team in a period in which, for all it has been successful, there have been ongoing questions surrounding the attack.

It is perhaps also a hangover from the last two seasons, when injuries hampered Odegaard and he was well off his best. Frustration towards him built and some minds appear to have been made up.

However, the criticism that he does not deliver consistently enough just does not hold up to scrutiny this season.

Oliver Glasner has criticised Nottingham Forest players that cannot speak English after being in the country for several years, as he called for higher standards at the club.

Forest are currently outside the Premier League relegation zone on goal difference after 11 matches, having won just twice in the top flight since Glasner's arrival in the summer.

Speaking ahead of their Friday night clash against Chelsea, Glasner highlighted the failure of some of his squad to learn English to illustrate how standards needed to rise at the club.

'Stability in a company is having standards,' Glasner said. 'Everybody who joins commits to the standards. I think, very honestly, the standards were not high enough.

'We have players who are here for four years, can't speak English. If you go to Italy, after three months, every player gives his interview in the Italian language.

'For me, if I want to manage in England, I have to speak English. If I want to manage in Italy, I have to speak Italian.

Football clubs regularly enlist the services of language experts in an effort to help foreign stars to settle and communicate with their team-mates.

West Brom and Wolves have worked with Language Partners for around 20 years, with the company providing tailored classes to players and coaches, offering specific skills required for their role at the clubs.

Another company, Communicate Well, has worked with Premier League clubs since 1999, with lessons taking place at training grounds, players’ homes and ‘out and about’ to provide real-life tasks in English.

Chelsea goalkeeper Emiliano Martinez previously revealed Arsenal had included a £20,000 bonus in his first contract with the club, which would be paid when he passed the English proficiency test.

Martinez, who signed for Arsenal in the summer of 2010 from Argentine side Independiente, had reportedly passed the test before Christmas.

U.S. moves Strategic Bitcoin Reserve bill forward with a 20-year lockup plan

📅 Nov 26, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

The U.S. House Financial Services Committee has advanced H.R. 8957, known as the American Reserve Modernization Act of 2026, bringing the proposed legislation one step closer to a full House vote.

Introduced on the 21st of May 2026 by Republican Representative Nick Begich of Alaska, alongside Democratic Representative Jared Golden, the bill seeks to give the U.S. government’s Strategic Bitcoin [BTC] Reserve a formal legal foundation.

It proposes that the U.S. Treasury Department establish two separate structures within 180 days of the legislation becoming law. This includes a Strategic Bitcoin Reserve exclusively for Bitcoin and a Digital Asset Stockpile for other cryptocurrencies held by the federal government.

Under the proposal, Bitcoin owned by the federal government through criminal or civil asset forfeitures and other qualifying sources would be transferred to the Strategic Bitcoin Reserve.

Here, the most significant provision is a 20-year minimum holding requirement, which would prevent the government from selling, swapping, auctioning, or using reserve Bitcoin as collateral during that period. At the same time, the bill also allows the Treasury Department to explore budget-neutral ways of acquiring additional Bitcoin without increasing the national debt.

Separately, the legislation proposes a Digital Asset Stockpile to manage other cryptocurrencies held by the federal government. Lastly, it also introduces requirements for transparency, auditing, and congressional reporting to ensure proper oversight of these assets.

The bill is particularly significant because it seeks to give the U.S. Strategic Bitcoin Reserve a statutory foundation. For its part, President Donald Trump had already established the reserve through an executive order in March 2025.

However, H.R. 8957 aims to formalize its structure and introduce additional safeguards, including the proposed 20-year holding period.

As of now, twenty months have already passed, and the U.S. government reportedly holds around 328,000 BTC, largely from seizures and forfeitures, while governments globally hold about 435,000 BTC.

Henceforth, if H.R. 8957 becomes law, the reserve would receive statutory backing, making it more difficult for a future administration to reverse the policy unilaterally.

The bill received approval from the House Financial Services Committee on 16th September 2026 in a 28-21 vote, with all Republican members voting in favor and all Democratic members opposing it.

Then, on the 24th of November, the committee formally reported the revised legislation to the full House, placing it on the Union Calendar and making it eligible for a floor vote. However, its chances of becoming law remain uncertain.

This is because the 119th Congress ends on the 3rd of January 2027, leaving limited time for the bill to pass the House, clear the Senate, and receive presidential approval.

Adding to this, market expectations also remain pessimistic, with Polymarket traders assigning only a 4% probability to the U.S. establishing a national Bitcoin reserve through legislation before 2027.

All of this coincided with Bitcoin trading at $60,355.92 at press time after a hike of 2.1% in the past 24 hours.

GigaChat 4 Debuts With 1M-Token Context and Two Model Sizes

📅 Nov 26, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

The GigaChat Team announced GigaChat 4 on November 26, 2026, releasing 708B- and 37B-parameter mixture-of-experts models with native one-million-token context windows. Both activate only a subset of their parameters per token, support tool calling and multi-step agent tasks, and allow Instant or Reasoning responses to be selected per request within one checkpoint.

The flagship 708B-A41B uses approximately 41B active parameters per token, compared with roughly 3.8B for the compact 37B-A4B. Both were trained on sequences extending to the full context length for work with long documents, collections of sources and large codebases. This arrangement replaces GigaChat 3.5’s separate instant and reasoning checkpoints.

Both models use a 3:1 mix of Gated DeltaNet layers and multi-head latent attention layers enhanced with DeepSeek Sparse Attention (DSA). Gated DeltaNet maintains a fixed-size recurrent state, while DSA selects up to 2,048 earlier positions for each query rather than computing attention over the entire sequence. The companion technical report says indexer and selection overhead can outweigh the attention savings at short contexts or small decode batches.

The flagship has 64 decoder layers and 256 routed experts, selecting eight per token together with one shared expert. The compact model has 32 layers and 192 routed experts, using the same combination of eight routed experts and one shared expert.

For the 708B model, context extension progressed from 32K to 128K tokens before indexer training and joint optimization at the native length. After supervised fine-tuning, specialist models underwent reinforcement learning in mathematics, coding, tool use and multi-turn agent workflows. Multi-teacher on-policy distillation then consolidated their capabilities into one model offering both response modes.

Under the team’s evaluation protocol, the 708B model scored 71.26% on Terminal-Bench 2.0 in Reasoning mode and 60.47% in Instant mode, versus 30.30% and 12.94% respectively for GigaChat 3.5. Its reported Reasoning results also include 80.89% on SWE-bench Verified and 79.55% on BFCL v4, although GLM-5.3 is listed higher at 86.67% on SWE-bench Verified.

The compact model’s reported Reasoning results include 85.00% on LiveCodeBench v6, 70.80% on SWE-bench Verified and 72.00% on BFCL. For the flagship, evaluation combined public runners and scoring code with the team’s own implementations, including seven-hour timeouts for specified agentic benchmarks.

The team’s demonstrations emphasize tasks that chain artifacts and tools: the flagship modified a Mario cartridge’s palette in its binary file and ran the game on a Mac M4, produced an STL vase for 3D printing, and used MCP access to the Cadenza music workstation to compose a track before building an accompanying visualization site.

The flagship model card lists FP8, BF16 and GGUF instruction-tuned variants, along with base and intermediate checkpoints, and releases its instruction-tuned checkpoint under the MIT License. The compact model card lists the same variant categories but uses the Noncommercial License for Individuals 1.0. The detailed technical report currently covers the 708B model; a dedicated report for the 37B model is planned for later.

‘Root-to-Tip’ Eating: Are You Missing Out on Fiber by Discarding Scraps?

📅 Nov 25, 2026  |  Category: Health  |  Read Source →

Follow Healthline on our socials for all things health.

Eating fruit and veggie scraps may boost fiber intake and cut food waste.

This study from King’s College London found that peels, skins, seeds, and stalks of fruits and vegetables—parts often discarded—contained significantly more dietary fiber than the commonly eaten portions in 10 of 12 produce types analyzed.

In some cases, the fiber content was up to five times higher in these “root-to-tip” components.

These findings, recently published in the International Journal of Food Sciences and Nutrition, are noteworthy, since around 95% of U.S. adults aren’t getting enough fiber. What’s more, around one-fifth of all food produced globally is wasted every year.

The researchers identified 12 fresh produce items that were deemed suitable for “root-to-tip” inclusion:

In the laboratory analysis, they measured the dietary fiber content of both the “root-to-tip” components (skins, peels, seeds, stalks) and the parts that people typically eat.

The key findings of the study showed that in 10 of the 12 produce types, the “root-to-tip” components contained more fiber than the parts typically consumed.

However, broccoli stalks contained 28.4% less fiber than the florets that people usually eat, meaning individuals are already consuming the most fiber-rich part.

Most participants in focus groups saw the nutritional value of the “root-to-tip” approach as more of a motivator than its sustainability.

However, these findings show that a “root-to-tip” approach to many fruits and vegetables could not only support higher dietary fiber intake but also reduce food waste.

Kristin Kirkpatrick, president of KAK, Consulting, LLC, and a dietitian at the Cleveland Clinic Department of Wellness & Preventive Medicine, said that the takeaway isn’t to eat every peel or seed, but to look for safe, simple ways to get more fiber from the foods you’re already buying. Kirkpatrick wasn’t involved in the study.

This applies to some of the plants analyzed in this study, but not all produce. For instance, you can only eat a kiwi that’s peeled. This is still a better choice than no kiwi (or a processed-food snack in its place) at all.

Fiber is important for promoting health and preventing chronic conditions such as cardiovascular disease, type 2 diabetes, colorectal cancer, and obesity.

“Fiber does much more than support regular bowel movements. It helps regulate blood sugar, supports beneficial gut bacteria, and certain types of soluble fiber can help lower LDL cholesterol by increasing bile acid elimination,” noted Michelle Routhenstein, a preventive cardiology dietitian at Entirely Nourished, who wasn’t involved in the study.

On average, adults are recommended to get 25–30 grams (or more, depending on age) of fiber daily.

Both nutrition experts told Healthline that people generally aren’t getting enough fiber because of their food options or choices.

“Most likely because individuals are not consuming enough plants, and overconsuming processed and ultra-processed foods which are typically very low in fiber,” said Kirkpatrick. “Simply not eating enough fruits and vegetables or choosing white refined grains over whole grains can be why a person’s diet is lacking fiber.”

Routhenstein added, “I think it is important that when we talk about fiber we point out that it doesn’t have to mean completely changing how you eat. I encourage people to incorporate fiber-rich ingredients into familiar, culturally meaningful foods they already enjoy.”

“Start with small increases with foods you enjoy and build gradually to allow your digestive system to adjust.”

It is important to always research any new plant parts before eating them, wash thoroughly, and consult a healthcare professional if you have digestive conditions or concerns.

And remember that not all produce scraps are edible or recommended.

“Any dietary change will be dependent on personal preference. Some individuals may not enjoy or may not know how to add components of a plant they are not used to,” said Kirkpatrick.

She noted that, based on the study’s examples, an easier option might be to add more kiwi peels and seeds. “In fact, kiwi skin is probably the most practical fruit peel to highlight for consumers,” she said.

Routhenstein added, “I think there is a knowledge gap that the skins, peels, from the root to the tip are edible and nutritious, so we need to start there.”

She suggested that one way to start is by eating the skins of apples, pears, and well-scrubbed carrots. Trim and thinly slice broccoli stalks for stir-fries or soups. Sautée beet greens and stems just as you would spinach. Freeze appropriate vegetable trimmings, such as carrot ends and celery leaves, for homemade broth.

“Root-to-tip eating helps reduce food waste by encouraging us to use more of the edible portions of fruits and vegetables rather than discarding them. It also maximizes the nutritional and economic value of the food we purchase while making better use of the water, land, and energy required to produce it,” said Routhenstein.

“The key is finding simple, enjoyable ways to incorporate these parts into meals we already eat.”

From Files to Cited Answers in One API Call: Managed Ingestion and SearchDocuments in Agent Retrieval

📅 Nov 25, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

A guide to the new managed ingestion and SearchDocuments features in Agent Retrieval, for searching PDFs, Office files, and videos without writing an ingestion pipeline.

Before an agent can answer a question about your PDFs, someone has to build the pipeline: parse every file, split it into chunks, embed each chunk, write the chunks to a vector database, and then learn that database's schema to query it. For video it gets harder: transcode the files, cut them into clips, embed the frames, and keep track of which second of which video each clip came from.

Most of that code has nothing to do with your application. Two features recently added to Agent Retrieval (formerly Vector Search 2.0), now in Public Preview, take that work off your hands. Managed ingestion builds the pipeline for you: point a data store at files in Cloud Storage, and the service parses, segments, and embeds them with Gemini Embedding 2. SearchDocuments, the second new feature, then searches the data store with one API call and returns the matching passages with their page numbers or timestamps. In our tests, 18 Alphabet investor PDFs went from a public Cloud Storage bucket to searchable, page cited results in about 9 minutes, and 19 minutes of video became searchable by both visuals and spoken audio in 0.3 to 0.5 seconds per query, with no parsing, chunking, transcription, or embedding code.

This guide covers how to create a data store, import documents and videos, and search them with filters, reranking, and audio-aware video search.

Pairing managed ingestion with SearchDocuments gives you several immediate benefits:

Agent Retrieval Collections already give you semantic, text, and hybrid search over your own data objects. But you have to produce those data objects yourself, and to query them you need to know how they are stored. Here is what changes when you let a data store do the work:

The backing Collection is still a regular Agent Retrieval Collection, so you can query it directly whenever you need full control.

A data store holds your files and the configuration that controls how they are parsed and segmented. The data store and SearchDocuments APIs are currently served over REST at v1beta, so the examples use an authorized session from google-auth:

Two segmentation modes are available. Chunk segmentation (the default) splits text into chunks of 100 to 500 tokens and works with layout parsing, which also detects tables and headings. Page segmentation produces one segment per page. For paginated documents such as PDFs, both modes record page numbers (startPage and endPage) so you can cite pages or filter by page range: page segmentation returns a single page per segment, while chunk segmentation returns the page range each chunk spans.

Point an import at your files with URI patterns such as gs://my-bucket/reports/*.pdf, or with a JSONL manifest when you want to set your own document IDs and attach metadata:

The import runs as a long-running operation. Re-importing the same files updates existing documents instead of duplicating them, and files whose content didn't change are skipped.

Each result is a segment of a document, with the text, the page, and the source file:

Pass filter to search only the segments that match, using the same paths you read back in results:

You can also pass document_ids to search only inside specific files, and output_fields.metadata to return each document's metadata with its results. In our test, searching "What did the CEO say about AI?" with the page-1 filter returned the CEO quotes from the headline page of five different earnings releases.

For "How many employees does Alphabet have?", plain hybrid search returned two balance sheet pages among the top five, which don't mention employees at all. With reranking on, every top result reported the headcount: the annual report's "Alphabet had 190,234 employees" and the "Number of employees" row of the quarterly supplemental tables.

Managed ingestion doesn't change what good retrieval needs: well-segmented content, good embeddings, and the right filters. What changes is who builds it. Instead of maintaining parsers, chunkers, embedding jobs, and a schema, you point a data store at a bucket and get back segments that are ready to cite.

For agents, that means a retrieval tool that returns where each answer came from, a page in a PDF or a moment in a video, with one API call.
