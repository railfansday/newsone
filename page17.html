Magnite vs. Reddit: Which Media Stock Is a Better Buy Going Into 2027?

📅 Nov 07, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Reddit's revenue surged far faster than Magnite's last year, yet only one of these ad stocks trades at a meaningfully lower forward earnings multiple.

As digital advertising shifts away from traditional formats, investors must decide which platforms can best navigate the changing landscape. Choosing between Magnite (MGNI -0.47%) and Reddit (RDDT +1.38%) depends on your preferred growth strategy.

Magnite serves as a vital middleman for publishers looking to sell digital ad space, while Reddit offers a massive community-driven platform for direct engagement. Both companies represent different segments of the modern advertising ecosystem.

Magnite operates as the world's largest independent sell-side advertising company. It provides the technology that helps publishers monetize their content across connected TV, online video, and display formats. The company serves major partners like LG Electronics-owned LG Ad Solutions, and recently partnered with OpenAI to boost ad placements within ChatGPT.

In its latest annual report, filed for its 2025 fiscal year (FY), revenue reached nearly $714.0 million. This represents revenue growth of 6.9% compared to the previous year. Magnite reported net income of $144.6 million for the period, which resulted in a net margin of 20.3%.

As of its December 2025 balance sheet, the debt-to-equity ratio was 0.7x. This metric compares total debt to shareholder equity to show how much a company relies on borrowing. The current ratio, which measures the ability to cover short-term debts with short-term assets, stands at 1.0x. Free cash flow was $165.6 million. Note that stock-based compensation (SBC) represented 32.5% of operating cash flow, which inflates reported cash generation since SBC is a non-cash expense added back in the cash flow statement.

Reddit is a prominent name for those investing in social media stocks. It generates most of its revenue from third-party advertising, but it has also expanded into content licensing for training generative artificial intelligence models. The platform reported 130.3 million daily active unique (DAUq) users as of the third quarter of 2026, showing its massive reach.

In its latest annual report, filed for FY 2025, revenue reached $2.2 billion. This reflects significant revenue growth of 69.4% over the prior year. Reddit reported net income of $529.7 million, delivering a net margin of 24.1% as it transitioned into profitability.

As of its December 2025 balance sheet, the company carried a debt-to-equity ratio of zero. Its current ratio was 11.6x, indicating a strong ability to meet short-term obligations. Free cash flow reached $684.2 million for the year. Note that stock-based compensation represented 49.7% of operating cash flow.

Magnite faces significant revenue concentration, with two buyers indirectly contributing about 44% of its total revenue. Its success depends heavily on the growth of the connected TV market and continued access to that inventory. The company also deals with intense competition from Alphabet (GOOGL +0.78%), Amazon (AMZN -0.35%), and Comcast (CMCSA -2.24%). Furthermore, ongoing litigation with Alphabet's Google regarding anticompetitive practices remains a potential financial risk.

Reddit relies on its ability to maintain user engagement and contributions, which are the primary drivers of its advertising value. The company faces concentration risks, as its top ten advertisers account for roughly 21% of total revenue. Its community-led moderation model can lead to disruptions that may alienate advertisers or users. Additionally, Reddit depends on infrastructure provided by third-party cloud services from Amazon and Alphabet to remain operational.

Reddit stock trades at a lower multiple of future earnings estimates, while Magnite appears more attractively priced based on its sales over the past twelve months.

Between Magnite and Reddit, I would prioritize investing in Reddit. Both rely on digital advertising to fund their businesses, but Reddit's consumer-facing community platform gives it the edge.

The company's 130.3 million DAUq users in Q3 represented strong 12% year-over-year growth, indicating Reddit continues to attract an engaged user base. This makes the social media giant a compelling place for advertisers to spend their budgets. Consequently, the company grew Q3 revenue by a whopping 53% year over year to $895 million.

Reddit stock is also much cheaper based on its forward earnings multiple, and while its sales multiple is a bit higher than Magnite's, the company's much faster revenue growth signals that it's business is still rapidly expanding. Magnite's Q3 sales of $204.2 million represented an excellent 14% year-over-year increase, but is still much lower than Reddit's 53%.

The one important downside to note is that Reddit's reliance on search engines to deliver traffic to its site is at risk with the artificial intelligence boom. Users are increasingly looking to AI when performing searches, bringing a drop to traditional search visits. Reddit is responding by testing a requirement for users to log in when visiting the site, which prevents AI from taking its content.

Novo Nordisk Just Delivered Great News for Investors. Is a Comeback Finally Underway?

📅 Nov 07, 2026  |  Category: Business  |  Read Source →

Novo got a much-needed boost from its latest earnings report.

Are things finally starting to look up for Novo (NVO +0.03%)? The Denmark-based pharmaceutical leader has had a rough past couple of years, but its third-quarter financial results were strong enough to send the stock up 6% post-earnings. That's great news for shareholders, but can Novo maintain this momentum?

At first glance, Novo's third-quarter results didn’t seem particularly impressive, as some of the company’s longtime growth drivers underperformed. For instance, sales of Ozempic, the company's famous diabetes medicine, were 29.7 billion Danish kroner (about $4.5 billion), down 3% compared to the year-ago period. The company's weight-loss medicine, Wegovy, recorded sales of 20.4 billion DKK (about $3.1 billion), roughly flat compared with the prior-year quarter.

However, several other medicines helped pick up the slack, and arguably none was more important than Novo's oral Wegovy. The company launched this weight loss pill -- the first of its kind to earn approval -- in January. The Wegovy pill has helped attract many patients who were hesitant to start treatment with injectables. Novo reported that, as of June, oral Wegovy had surpassed three million prescriptions, and more than 80% were for patients who had never taken GLP-1s before. In the third quarter, oral Wegovy generated 5.4 billion DKK (about $810 million) in revenue.

Novo's total net sales were 79.2 billion DKK (about $11.9 billion), up 6% year over year. The company's net income dropped by 16% year over year to 16.7 billion DKK (about $2.5 billion), largely due to non-cash impairment charges. Still, Novo’s results beat analyst estimates, and the pharmaceutical company also raised its guidance for the fiscal year 2026.

It now expects revenue to be flat (at the midpoint) year over year, while the previous guidance implied a 3% year-over-year drop at the midpoint. It's no wonder Novo's shares jumped following this beat-and-raise quarter. But what's next for the drugmaker?

There are several catalysts ahead that could help Novo improve its financial performance. Let's consider three of them. First, the company could make meaningful progress with oral Wegovy. The medicine has now been approved in several other countries, including Germany and the U.K.

These markets might not be as lucrative as the U.S., but they should move the needle for the company. Novo will also likely attract brand-new GLP-1 patients in these regions. The company is also looking to earn label expansions for oral Wegovy. Novo has submitted an application to the U.S. Food and Drug Administration for oral Wegovy as a potential treatment for metabolic dysfunction-associated steatohepatitis.

The original injectable version of Wegovy is approved for this indication, but the pill offers a more convenient option. It could be successful in this niche and continue driving Novo's sales higher. Second, Novo will launch new products to strengthen its position in the diabetes and weight-loss markets.

It is inching closer to approval for CagriSema, a medicine that beat Wegovy in a head-to-head Phase 3 clinical trial for weight loss. CagriSema also posted strong clinical trial results in diabetes. This medicine could help Novo steal some market share from its biggest competitor, Eli Lilly (LLY -0.79%).

Third, Novo has several pipeline candidates that could improve its approved lineup over time. The company's Phase 3 pipeline features zenagamtide, a promising diabetes and weight-loss medicine that is being developed in subcutaneous and oral formulations.

The weight loss market is growing rapidly, and this product, along with others, may help Novo ride that tailwind for a long time, even as competition intensifies. Novo may still experience some clinical setbacks, but after the beating it took over the past two years, the company is slowly staging a comeback. In my view, the stock is attractive following its third-quarter results.

Over Pinchot Pass — John Muir Trail Day 10

📅 Nov 07, 2026  |  Category: Business  |  Read Source →

The night before, at Rae Lake, I ate some kind of buffalo stew, which was supposed to provide about 900 calories.  It is funny how flavor (usually) takes a backseat to caloric value on the trail.  I am just so conscious that I am exerting myself — a lot — and need to maximize my fuel intake.

After dinner I had my headlamp on in the tent, probably dictating my notes, and was not aware that Evelyn (or Serena?) was taking pictures of my tent in the darkness under a starry sky.

Night camping at Rae Lakes (photo courtesy of Evelyn or Serena)

My sleep started deep and sound.  Then I did wake up, coughing a bit, and I thought, “Oh, no!”  But it was still much less than before, and I was very tired, so I slept again.  I vaguely remember dreaming about how some people were organizing something-or-other and I was confused how it was going to work.  Maybe it meant I was wondering how to manage this journey?

Around 4:40 in the morning, when I got up to pee, I checked the Garmin and saw the time.  I thought, “Well, I think I’m not going to go back to sleep.”  So I didn’t; instead I fetched my food from the bear box, and ate my breakfast.  But I felt sluggish, like I was swimming through molasses.

How sluggish?  Well, I rose at 4:40 but I did not get on the trail until 7:00 a.m.  Despite everything, it was only thirty minutes later than the previous day, but it was far from efficient.  I said goodbye to Evelyn and Serena; they were heading southbound, and I was heading northbound.  I left the crowded campsite and started on my way.

One consequence of the Rae Lake crowds is that it was hard to find a private place to dig a cathole.  I decided to wait for a better place.  About a half hour down the trail, I pulled off and found a spot that was quite lovely and dug my hole and did my business.  In the middle of it, a deer appeared in the meadow next to me and gazed at me curiously.

Cat hole in the foreground, amazing view in the background!

Less than a half mile further, I discovered a beautiful camping site with a bear box and no crowds at all.  I thought, “Oh my gosh, if I’d just known and been willing to take the risk to go a little further yesterday, it would have been a much nicer situation.”  It was too late for me, but I resolved to tell any southbound hikers I encountered that they would be more comfortable stopping at the last camp site before the main Rae Lake location.

More beautiful and fewer crowds — the place to be!

As I looked at the long day ahead of me, I realized it would be 6-7 miles, all downhill, followed by 8-9 miles all uphill, followed by a mile or two of downhill to my next camp site at Lake Marjorie.  In elevation I started at 10,500 feet, would drop to 8,500 feet, and then would cross Pinchot Pass at 12,200 feet.  I planned for a long day.

I did about four miles on that nice downhill to start the day when I encountered a small stream I had to cross.  In general, these stream crossings had not been too challenging, frankly.  I brought my water shoes explicitly because I thought I would wade through river crossings, perhaps as much a thigh-deep, and there hadn’t been any like that – at least so far.

This one was no different, a two-stone hop across with a couple of different options as to route.  That, it turned out, was the problem.  As I prepared to take my second step I decided to go via a different sequence of stones, and I turned slightly to change direction.  It was a slight turn, nothing notable about it at all.  Except… Suddenly I felt a sharp pain in my left knee.

I let out a yowl and finished crossing.  But I was worried.  Clearly, I had injured something. The only question was, what was it and how badly was it damaged?

I was still able to move it, so that was a good first sign.  Working it gently, it barked at me in certain angles; but just going straight ahead, it seemed not to bother me too much.

I decided to take an Ibuprofen at the bottom of the hill to prevent swelling from whatever it was that had happened.  Five minutes later I changed my mind, thinking, “Why am I waiting  to take the medication?  I just sustained the injury.  I should take the ibuprofen immediately, right NOW.”  And I did.

I continued down gingerly, favoring my knee a bit, worried that this might be something serious, and not knowing whether it would be or not.

My main distraction was lizards.  Yes!  I had observed no reptiles at all above 10,000 feet, but coming lower they started to appear.  I think they were most likely Western Fence Lizards, but they could also have been Sagebrush Lizards.  Because of my focus on my knee, I did not photograph them, but I resolved to do so the next time they appeared.

Finally, I reached the bottom of the downhill.  There was a suspension bridge to cross there, and it was one of the bouncier ones I had ever been on.  With a GoPro I would gladly have filmed myself crossing it, but with my hand-held phone it was too easy to imagine it flying out of my hand into the rushing waters below.

From solid ground I took a couple of pictures of the bridge anyway.

Then I crossed to the other side and began the big uphill.  I was facing around 3,600 feet of elevation gain, while worrying about my stupid knee.  I also worried that the trail might feel kind of endless, and it did feel kind of endless.

Along the way I started leapfrogging another hiker named Amy.  Our hiking styles almost made it inevitable.  I would go fast but then stop and rest; she kept a steady pace.  So, I would go by her moving faster, and she would move by me when I stopped.  Then I would resume and pass her again, and the cycle would continue.  At one point, when I was ahead, I saw a grouse.  I wondered if it would still be there when she came by.

The good news in all of this was that either the Ibuprofen was working, or my knee was not badly injured, or both.

Amy and I had both set Lake Marjorie, on the other side of Pinchot Pass, as our destination for the night.  This was the first camping space about a mile and a half below the summit.

We kept ascending.  The one advantage was that the ascent took place over many miles, so it was rarely going up very steeply.  But over the course of the afternoon, it was feeling quite relentless.  Only the series of waterfalls along the way kept me engaged and appreciative.

…another waterfall falling in perpetuity!

Approaching Pinchot Pass I ran into a guy coming downhill toward me.  We chatted and I learned that, like me, he was 69 years old.  He seemed very demoralized.  He said he felt like he was shuffling, like he could barely even do it.  He said he was planning to get off at Kearsarge Pass and probably would not return.  I said, “Well, what you should do is get off at Kearsarge and go to Independence and then spend a day there doing nothing.  After a day off trail, your mind will be clearer, and then you can decide if that’s the end for you, or if you do actually want to come back.”  That was my recommendation; I don’t know if he took it.

Nearing the top, there were many rocks patterned with white striations.  I wanted to turn to Sam and ask the geologist to explain what had caused them.  But of course, he was back in Boulder by then.

You can see the white stripes in the foreground rocks, but also in the background cliff face. Quartz maybe? Sam could have told me…

The end of the trail up to the pass was a series of switchbacks.  You couldn’t really see where they were leading until, suddenly, ta-da!, you had reached the saddle of the pass.

It was a really, really hard day.  I started at seven a.m. and I got to the pass at exactly five p.m.  If you do the math, that’s ten hours.  And I still had the mile and a half to go down to the lake.

The views over the lakes as the sun was setting were beautiful.  Amy finally caught up to me again, at a stream where I stopped to top off my water supply before making camp.  That late in the afternoon, we were both worried that the camping spots might already be filled up.  But when I saw a truly beautiful location, on nice flat sandy ground, I said, “That’s the one for me!”  Amy kept going to the next spot further down the trail, but I called it a day.

Arriving at Rae Lake the previous day, I was so tired it was difficult to function.  On this occasion, at Lake Marjorie, I set up calmly and reasonably efficiently, and it just felt much more natural than it had up to that point.  I pitched my tent, soaked my food, and set up my sleeping system, of course.  But I also went down to the lake shore where I washed my socks and washed my feet.  While I had my feet in the lake’s icy cold water, I thought, “Oh, I need to ice my knee too.”  So, I went a little deeper into the lake and got my knee in the icy cold water as well.

Unfortunately, my dinner was disgusting.  Some of these freeze-dried meals work great for a cold-soaker, but some really need boiling water to be edible.  I just wish I had kept track of which was which for future hikes!  Dinner was the low point of the day (other than my knee tweak), but I ate it for the calories.

Early Evening by Lake Marjorie. Notice my tent to the right of the boulder and — at that point — nothing to the left of the boulder.

Sitting in my tent pitched next to a large boulder it was getting colder-and-colder at our high elevation.  It was definitely a night for my sleeping bag liner!  In deep twilight I heard a large animal moving around on the other side of the big boulder.  Initially I was quite spooked, but when I checked I saw that a late-arriving hiker was setting up camp there.

For a moment I was annoyed that my delicious privacy had been disturbed.  Then I overcame that feeling and went over to welcome the newcomer and see if they needed anything.  It was a solo hiker named Sally, born in China but now an Australian citizen, also NOBO on the John Muir Trail.  She was friendly and we chatted for a few moments before I returned to my tent to call it a night.

It had been a 17-mile day, my longest yet on the trail.  Sleep came easily.

MLB free agency: Six international players to know for the 2026-27 offseason

📅 Nov 07, 2026  |  Category: Sports  |  Read Source →

Major League Baseball's free agency period opened earlier this week and teams are now clear to pursue any roster upgrades they deem necessary. That includes players coming over from Japan's Nippon Professional Baseball league, South Korea's Korea Baseball Organization, and Taiwan's Chinese Professional Baseball League. Those are high-level leagues that have sent many impact players MLB's way over the years.

Look no farther than the Dodgers to see how important international talent can be to a club. The Dodgers employed four different former NPB and KBO players this past season, including two-way star Shohei Ohtani, ace Yoshinobu Yamamoto, and starter Roki Sasaki. Ohtani was signed as an MLB free agent, but the others went to the Dodgers through the posting system.

Per the posting agreement with MLB, posted NPB and CPBL players get a 45-day window to negotiate and sign with an MLB team. KBO players get a 30-day window. Players who do not sign during that posting window return to their previous team in NPB, CPBL, or KBO. The MLB team that signs a posted player pays a fee to the player's former NPB, CPBL, or KBO team.

$5M plus 17.5% of contract value over $25M

Modern life is changing the DNA of bacteria in our guts

📅 Nov 07, 2026  |  Category: Business  |  Read Source →

Gut bacteria can acquire genes from neighboring microbes, adding to the genetic differences linked to how people live.

Diet, antibiotics, and indoor plumbing help shape which bacteria settle into a person’s gut. That much has been clear for years.

What researchers couldn’t say is whether the bacteria themselves have been evolving in response.

A study of nearly 6,000 gut bacterial genomes finds that they have.

Across the species examined, strains from industrialized communities carried more genes than those from non-industrialized communities.

Most of the extra genes arrived recently. The bacteria didn’t inherit them from a parent cell. They took them from other microbes living alongside them.

So modern life hasn’t only changed which bacteria live in the gut. The same species now carries a different set of genes depending on how its host lives.

Researchers at Kiel University’s Institute of Clinical Molecular Biology and MIT’s Department of Biological Engineering ran the work, with the evolutionary modeling done in Japan.

Everything here was measured in bacterial genomes, so none of it shows that a bigger bacterial genome makes a person healthier or sicker.

All of the genomes come from the Global Microbiome Conservancy, a non-profit effort to bank living strains from communities that microbiome research has mostly passed over.

For this study, 20 volunteers in Cameroon, Ghana, Malaysia, Nigeria and Rwanda gave fresh stool samples. None had taken antibiotics recently.

Scientists processed each sample on site within three hours. The samples were then frozen in liquid nitrogen at minus 321°F (minus 196°C).

In the lab, the material went onto plates inside an oxygen-free chamber. Colonies grew at 99°F (37°C) for one to two weeks. Single colonies were then picked and sequenced.

The cultures yielded 1,841 new bacterial genomes. Added to 4,140 strains already banked, the collection reached 5,981 genomes. They came from 56 donors in nine countries.

Researchers classified donors as industrialized or non-industrialized using a development score for where they lived, compared with their country’s median.

From that collection, the team picked ten species sampled well on both sides of the divide, with at least eight strains from each side. Most are Bacteroides and close relatives, the workhorses of the lower gut.

E. coli was left out on purpose. Its genetic diversity has already been mapped in detail.

In all ten species, strains from industrialized guts carried more protein-coding genes. The difference was statistically significant in eight of the ten species.

Core genes, the ones every strain of a species has, showed no difference. The extra material was all optional genes, the kind one strain carries and the next one doesn’t.

The optional sets also varied more from strain to strain in industrialized hosts, again in all ten species. The team then widened the comparison to 34 species and saw the same pattern.

A bigger genome could mean two different things. Either these strains picked up genes lately, or strains that already had big genomes happened to do well in modern guts.

Separating the two fell to a group in Okinawa, Japan. They compared the family tree of a single gene with the family tree of the species carrying it.

A mismatch between the two marks the spot where a gene was gained, lost or handed over.

Gene gains clustered near the tips of the evolutionary trees, indicating that they occurred relatively recently.

They also piled up on the branches belonging to industrialized hosts.

Between 80% and 90% of those gains were transfers from other microbes. Only a small share came down from a parent cell.

Many arrived packaged inside plasmids and viruses that infect bacteria.

Both deliver several genes at once. Swapping of this kind goes on well beyond the gut, in soil, seawater and thawing permafrost.

Gergely Szöllősi heads the Model-Based Evolutionary Genomics Unit at the Okinawa Institute of Science and Technology.

His group built these tools for a much older problem, and Lénárd Szánthó, a senior bioinformatician in the unit, adapted them to a far shorter stretch of time.

“These methods were built to look back hundreds of millions and billions of years, so it was not obvious that they would tell us anything about something this recent,” said Szöllősi.

“Lénárd made them work for this problem, and the picture that comes out is that bacteria in industrialized hosts have been acquiring genes from their neighbors recently, and this is what makes their genomes larger.”

About 5% of the gene families in a typical species collection tracked with the host’s way of life.

The team checked that against bacterial DNA read straight from the stool of 1,015 people, and 83% of the signals pointed the same way.

A few gene families turned up in more than one species, which is harder to put down to chance.

Two of them help build the outer coat of the cell, the part that other microbes and the immune system meet.

Another repeat hit was an enzyme that breaks down penicillin-type drugs. Genes like that ride the same mobile DNA now spreading antibiotic resistance through cities and farms.

The team also counted genes where protein-changing mutations had built up faster than mutations that did not change the protein. That pattern is the usual sign of a change being favored.

About 1.4% of the genes examined qualified. One of them helps a cell hold its outer envelope together under stress, and it carried the signature in three separate species.

In the five species with the most genomes, the researchers tracked single-letter DNA differences in core genes.

The variants tied to industrialized hosts kept landing on the same few jobs: building the cell wall, handling stress and making vitamin B12.

A companion study from the same group found more vitamin B12 production in the gut communities of people more exposed to industrialized environments.

Another cluster of changes hit the genes that build a molecule on the bacterial surface. The human immune system reads that molecule and reacts to it.

Researchers have not tested whether any of this changes how a person’s immune system behaves. The authors wrote that it is a possibility worth following up.

“I hope our results will inspire further studies to use this knowledge in supporting us to live a healthier life in our changing environment,” Szánthó said.

The analysis can’t say which part of industrialized living is responsible. Diet, medicine, sanitation and exposure to outdoor microbes all travel together, and this data can’t pull them apart.

Bigger genomes may not be an adaptation at all.

Extra genes build up on their own when the pressure to discard useless DNA eases, and the authors couldn’t rule that out.

Ten species is a thin slice of a gut that holds hundreds, and most of the ten belong to one bacterial group.

Species that have never been grown in a lab are missing here, which matters because industrialized guts hold fewer bacterial species to begin with.

The study itself doesn’t test what those extra genes actually do inside a person. Getting that answer will take experiments rather than more genomes.

The authors call for controlled experiments with bacteria, laboratory studies of evolution, and more detailed measurements of people’s living conditions.

The full study was published in the journal Cell.

Both options from these AI giants are meant to provide users with a better understanding of their finances and are not designed to offer expert financial advice, but there are slight variations in how they do it that might make you lean towards one or the other. To help you decide which AI chatbot to go with for more clarity on your finances, we've laid out the major similarities and differences of Claude's Personal Finance and ChatGPT's Finances features.

Covering a broad spectrum of personal finance topics, there's a lot of crossover in what both OpenAI and Anthropic's offerings can do. Both of their AI chatbots can address general questions about financial goals, like buying a house, planning an expensive family vacation or creating a savings plan. Claude and ChatGPT will also generate dashboards to offer users better visuals at things like monthly expenses, investments and spending habits.

To activate either Claude's Personal Finance tool or ChatGPT's Finances feature, you have to link your bank account. Both AI companies partnered with Plaid to make this happen, allowing connections with the most popular financial institutions, like Bank of America, Capital One or Chase. Once set up, both Claude and ChatGPT only have "read-only" access to your financial accounts, meaning they can only view the info provided and would not be able to make any changes.

Anthropic specified that Claude's Personal Finance feature can only see your bank name, account name, account type, last four account digits, balances, credit limits and up to two years of transactions. Similarly, OpenAI said that its Finances tool can see balances, transactions, investments and liabilities.

While the core function of Anthropic's Personal Finance and OpenAI's Finances are very similar, the major difference between the two has to do with privacy settings. By default, Anthropic said, "when you chat with Claude in the Personal Finance hub, those conversations aren't used to train our models." Anthropic added that Claude will no longer see any data that you previously provided to its Personal Finance feature if you unlink your bank account.

On the other hand, OpenAI said that "your conversations with connected financial accounts follow the same model training settings you choose across ChatGPT." In this case, you have to opt out of your data being used by navigating to Data controls in ChatGPT's settings and turning off the option called Improve the model for everyone, which is on by default. When you disconnect your bank accounts from OpenAI, the company said that "your synced account data will be deleted from OpenAI's systems within 30 days."

To differentiate itself from OpenAI's alternative, Anthropic said that its Personal Finance feature "has no ads or sponsored recommendations, and it doesn't offer credit cards, loans or savings accounts of its own." While OpenAI doesn't explicitly mention ads with ChatGPT Finances, the company has been testing out ads for its cheaper tiers as of January.

OpenAI said it's looking to bring more features to ChatGPT's Finances by partnering with Intuit to cover more bases like credit card applications or tax questions. As for Anthropic's next steps with Personal Finance, the company wants to hear from banks, wealth managers and financial services companies to help improve the AI tool.
