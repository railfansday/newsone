Deep ocean oxygen kept rising and falling long after animals appeared

📅 Nov 04, 2026  |  Category: Science  |  Read Source →

Geologists found deep ocean oxygen built up and vanished again for over 100 million years after the Cambrian, complicating how animals evolved.

Researchers often explain the Cambrian Explosion, the burst of animal life half a billion years ago, with a lasting rise in ocean oxygen. In the deep ocean, though, the oxygen didn’t last..

Geologists studied mud that settled on the seafloor between about 485 million and 380 million years ago and later hardened into rock.

They found oxygen near the bottom building up for short stretches and then disappearing again. So animals may have evolved under less deep-sea oxygen than researchers once assumed.

Chadlin Ostrander, an assistant professor in the Geology & Geophysics department at the University of Utah, led the study. His team found no evidence of sustained oxygen in the deep ocean during that span.

The team concluded that oxygen didn’t settle into the deepest water for good until sometime after roughly 380 million years ago.

“Just a decade ago, it was thought that a deep ocean oxygenation switch was flipped around 540 million years ago. Our new dataset pushes that forward in time by at least ~160 million years,” he said.

The team didn’t measure oxygen directly. Instead, it read a chemical record in the rock that follows oxygen indirectly.

Researchers have invoked oxygen in the deep ocean to explain nearly every major step in early animal history. That includes the first animals and the Cambrian Explosion. It also includes a second burst of marine diversity from about 485 to 444 million years ago.

Other scientists have raised serious counterarguments to each. A common objection is that oxygen may have risen during these events, but not high enough, or for long enough, to fill the deep ocean.

Visitors to the Grand Canyon can see some of that early history. Its walls include about 1,600 feet (500 meters) of rock that settled in a Cambrian sea.

Ostrander’s samples start right after the Cambrian ended, about 485 million years ago, so the team didn’t sample that sea.

Geologists find tiny amounts of thallium, a heavy metal, in ordinary rock. It comes in two forms that differ by a hair in weight.

When a manganese mineral settles into seafloor mud, it pulls more of the heavier form out of seawater. That mineral survives in mud only where oxygen is steadily present in the water above it, and a few days without oxygen dissolve it again.

Mud formed in oxygen-poor places keeps a sample of the surrounding seawater, including its mix of the two forms. The team read a lower value, meaning more of the lighter form, as a sign that more seafloor lay under oxygen-rich water.

The team measured 82 shale samples from the Peel River in Canada’s Yukon. The river has cut into the rock, exposing fresh layers that stayed largely unweathered in the cold.

Erik Sperling of Stanford University and Justin Strauss of Dartmouth College led the expeditions that collected the Yukon mud.

Another 44 samples came from sites including Latvia, Libya, Wales and Montana.

For most of the record, the team found the same thallium mix as in ordinary continental rock. A seafloor with little oxygen above it should leave exactly that. In Yukon mud that formed between about 405 million and 386 million years ago, the readings dropped.

The lowest value came from a Marcellus Shale sample from the Appalachian Basin that formed about 388 million years ago. By the team’s calculations, that value means oxygen-rich water covered much more than 30% of the seafloor, well past the continental shelves and into the deep ocean.

Readings from the peak of the last ice age look similar, with surface oxygen reaching the deep ocean only slightly less effectively than it does now.

That high-oxygen stretch didn’t last. By about 378 million years ago, the readings returned to ordinary values and stayed there to the top of the rock. That could mean more of the deep ocean had lost its oxygen again.

Local conditions could have faked the dip, so the team checked. The 11 Yukon samples from the dip had the same chemistry as the other Yukon samples, and the Marcellus Shale, from a separate basin, dipped the same way.

By the time of those samples, Ostrander said, marine life was already large and doing energy-expensive work.

“You have some phenomenal changes in evolutionary biology happening during this time. And none of it seems to require substantial and sustained deep ocean oxygenation,” he said.

Animal life was changing fast before that window opened.

Paleontologists working at a fossil site in southwest China have found that many animal groups already existed before the Cambrian began. Researchers who analyzed fossil trails from about 545 million years ago reached a similar conclusion.

In rock about 520 million years old, researchers have found colonies of tiny filter-feeding animals and an early relative of octopuses and squid.

The deep ocean has had steady oxygen since then. The team can’t say when that began, only that it came after about 380 million years ago. Ostrander called that the study’s one disappointment.

Gaps remain. The team has only one rock sample for roughly every 450,000 years, so the real swings may have been even larger than the mud shows.

The team also couldn’t fully rule out that thallium cycled through seawater too fast for the mud to reflect the whole ocean. Its models and the match between basins make that unlikely.

The researchers wrote that long, closely spaced oxygen records from the late Paleozoic and the Mesozoic are still needed to learn when the deep ocean filled with oxygen for good.

The full study was published in the journal Science Advances.

What Trump losing power over Congress means for Iran, Ukraine and foreign policy

📅 2026-11-04  |  Category: Business  |  Read Source →

With Democrats taking control of Congress, U.S. President Donald Trump could intensify his focus on foreign policy in his final two years in office, while facing greater scrutiny over the wars in Iran and Ukraine, experts told CNBC.

The Associated Press is projecting Democrats will have a Senate majority, flipping seats from Republicans in Iowa, Kansas, North Carolina, Ohio, South Carolina, and Texas.

Democrats also won control of the House, giving the party greater scope to scrutinize the administration, shape spending and challenge Trump's foreign-policy agenda.

"We are going to see a president that is somewhat constrained on matters of oversight and investigation, but on foreign policy, he does still retain a lot of latitude," Laurel Rapp, director of Chatham House's U.S. and North America Programme, told CNBC's "Squawk Box Europe."

Rapp said the Iran war was a "major issue" in this election cycle amid soaring fuel prices, food costs, and inflation. The war began when Trump launched joint strikes with Israel on Feb. 28.

"Republicans don't really have an off-ramp here at this stage. We know the Democrats will seek to take votes in the House and the Senate to constrain the Republicans, to restrain the administration on Iran war powers. But whether that will bind the administration is another matter," Rapp added.

She said that Democrats could exercise the "power of the purse" to limit spending on U.S. military and other Iran-related costs.

With control of both chambers, Democrats could pass legislation seeking to direct Trump to end U.S. involvement in the conflict. However, making such a measure binding over a presidential veto would require enough votes in Congress to override it.

J.C. Lintzenich, visiting senior fellow at the German Marshall Fund, said that the president is not a "lame duck."

"Remember that this election changes the Congress; it does not change the president," Lintzenich told CNBC's "Squawk Box Europe" on Wednesday.

"The President of the United States still has lots of power, lots of authority. We still have pending action in Iran. We still have an unresolved situation with Russia and Ukraine."

He added that while Congress has changed, foreign policy is still executed by the president and his appointed officials.

"What we'll now see is a little bit more congressional oversight, the application of funding, for example, Ukraine, that's required by statute and law," Lintzenich said.

Lintzenich said the outgoing Congress was "more willing to let the White House slide," but Democratic control of both chambers would mean a more assertive Congress, using its Article I powers over legislation, spending, and oversight to hold the White House more closely to existing law and congressional requirements.

Trump posted through the U.S. midterm elections, and he wasnt happy

📅 Nov 04, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

President Donald Trump has been posting through the U.S. midterm elections, and he doesn't appear happy with the results.

Millions of Americans went to the polls on Tuesday, casting their vote to choose their members of Congress. Democrats decisively took control of both the Senate and House of Representatives in a sweeping midterm victory, one which featured several upsets. South Carolina elected Annie Andrews as its first Democratic senator in 28 years. Texas voted in James Talarico as its first Democratic senator in over 30 years. Even Kansas swung blue, with Adam Hamilton becoming the first Democrat elected to its Senate seat in 94 years.

Still, it's possible that nobody was more upset than Trump. The U.S. president characteristically took to his social media platform Truth Social on election night, expressing his displeasure with the midterm's results as they came in.

Trump's day started off well, the president posting at 12:07 p.m. ET / 9:07 a.m. PT that Guam had been called as a Republican victory. Guam has elected Republican Tony Ada as governor, taking the position from the Democrats for the first time since 2014.

"Guam, which has always been considered strongly Democrat, just had an early call for a REPUBLICAN Victory in the top spot, Governor — Congratulations to Tony Ada!" he wrote. "Also, the Republicans have the Majority in the State Senate. Wow, hopefully that’s an indication for things to come!"

However, Guam's election was separate from the U.S. midterm elections, and had no impact on the makeup of the Senate or House. Once the midterm results began rolling in, the Republicans' situation began looking less rosy.

At 4:11 p.m. ET / 1:11 p.m. PT, Trump shared a screenshot from right-wing YouTuber Benny Johnson in which he claimed that "Republicans are currently showing signs of PRESIDENTIAL-LEVEL midterms turnout already on Election Day." The president also posted that had been watching the pre-election results on Fox News, sharing a clip of commentators praising him. Republican pollster Matt Towery refuted the New York Times' prediction of a "blue surge" in the video, stating that his own polling placed both parties much more evenly matched.

Despite this, Trump began attacking Fox News by 6:36 p.m. ET / 3:36 p.m. PT, shortly after the first polls started to close.

"The FoxNews Poll, always the worst, is so insulting with the questions they ask. Every question is a hit job, and every answer is 'BULLSHIT!'" wrote the U.S. president.

Trump continued to criticise Fox News less than half an hour later at 7:00 p.m. ET / 4:00 p.m. PT, claiming that its ratings are down "because they’re spending too much time covering the Trump Deranged Dumocrats." Fox News' viewership is down 27 percent compared to the same time last year, with Fox Corp. CEO Lachlan Murdoch blaming "viewer fatigue" after "some incredible news cycle[s] the last [few] years."

"All of those people who used to watch Fox, and no longer do, get it," Trump wrote. "It’s them, not me — My numbers are better than they have ever been."

Trump's approval rating fell to a record low just prior to the midterm elections, a Reuters/Ipsos poll finding that just 31 percent of Americans approve of his performance in office.

"The new CNN Poll stated that 'TRUMP' is at 99% among Republicans," Trump continued, misinterpreting a 2024 poll in which Trump received 99 percent of the "MAGA GOP" vote specifically when put up against Democrat Kamala Harris.

"Among Republican Presidents, 'TRUMP' is at 53%, compared to Ronald Reagan at 18%, and Abraham Lincoln at 8%!," he wrote, citing a CNN poll asking Republicans which president throughout history they most admire. The same poll found that 64 percent of Democrats most admired former president Barack Obama. Without accounting for party affiliation, 30 percent chose Obama, while 19 percent named Trump.

"No wonder FoxNews Ratings are collapsing, and they’re going to go down a lot further," Trump posted, further blasting Fox Corp. for donating to Democratic candidates. Fox Corp. donates to both Republicans and Democrats. "They’re way down, not because of me, but because they are AGAINST me!"

By the time of Trump's next posts at around 10:37 p.m. ET / 7:37 p.m. PT, the midterm election results were clearly indicating a Democratic sweep.

"I wasn’t on the Ballot and if I was, we would have won IN A LANDSLIDE! Thank you for your attention to this matter," Trump wrote on Truth Social. "I'm going to become a Political Pundit, both good and bad, on the reasons these Candidates won and lost!"

The president then posted an image of a red cap with the word "veto" embroidered upon it, reminiscent of his "Make America Great Again" hats. Calling in to Fox News, Trump said he was "a little bit surprised" by the midterm election results, and stated that he intended to deal with the Democratic majority Senate and House by blocking its attempts to pass legislation.

"It's the power of veto, it's one of the stronger powers that a president has. And they'll send all the things that are insane and I'll veto them and after a couple of months of vetos they'll come over and they'll say, 'how do we get this stuff done?' and we'll work together," said Trump. "I have the power of veto. The power of veto is impervious."

The U.S. Constitution does allow presidents to refuse to sign legislation, however it is not impassable. Congress can override a president's veto if it has a two-thirds majority in both the House and Senate.

While Trump called in to speak to Fox News, he also blamed the program for the Republicans candidates' poor results. The U.S. president ended midterm election night by claiming on Truth Social that Fox News "bears responsibility for these losses by constantly and consistently putting on the Dumocrat opposition." Many former Republican voters have reported growing disillusionment with the party since Trump took office for his second term, disapproving of how he has handled issues such as the economy and the war in Iran.

Denver Broncos quarterback Bo Nix rallied in the fourth quarter with a 15-point comeback to beat the Kansas City Chiefs on Sunday. Despite leading the Broncos to a 28-24 victory, Nix's inability to drive the ball into enemy territory in the first three quarters has become the center of criticism.

On Monday, former NFL scout John Middlekauff refused to back down from his criticism of Nix. The former scout explained the issue with Nix in the first three quarters, where he has struggled to help the Broncos this season.

"I think we have to acknowledge that this goes back to college; he's not a very good pocket quarterback," Middlekauff said via 3 & OUT with John Middlekauff's X. "When he has to make a throw inside the pocket, I think you would all agree that he doesn't excel. I'm not acting like the guy's a scrub, but we have to all come to an agreement that he's the most clutch quarterback in the NFL. Well, he's literally in this situation every week because of his own undoing."

"If you could just have him for the fourth quarter, he'd be your pick," Middlekauff added. "You do not want him in the first, second, or third quarter."

In Sunday's comeback win against the Chiefs, Nix completed 15-of-26 passes for 153 yards in the first three quarters. Only seven of his 26 attempts resulted in first downs, and he was also just 1-of-8 on third-down attempts. However, in the fourth quarter, Nix went 5-of-6 for 84 yards, helping Denver erase an 11-point deficit and take the lead.

Adding to Bo Nix's reputation as the most clutch quarterback, the Broncos signal-caller has 12 fourth-quarter comebacks since entering the NFL in 2024, tied for the most by a quarterback in his first three seasons.

Sunday's win, in which Nix engineered two touchdown drives and a two-point conversion in the fourth quarter of a 28-24 victory, marked his 15th career game-winning drive in the fourth quarter or overtime. According to ProFootballTalk's Michael Smith, no other quarterback in NFL history has that many game-winning drives in his first three seasons.

Nix surpassed Dallas Cowboys quarterback Dak Prescott, who had 14 game-winning drives in his first three seasons in the NFL.

Syfy Cancelled Its Ambitious Sci-Fi Series — Now It’s Prime Video’s Greatest Space Opera

📅 Nov 04, 2026  |  Category: World  |  Read Source →

After just three seasons, Syfy decided to pull the plug on an ambitious sci-fi series. Adapted from a bestselling book series, the show in question consistently landed in critics' books and even acquired a cult following. Yet, its run was cut short after three seasons. Fortunately, this is when Prime Video stepped in and renewed the series before it lasted for three more seasons.

Adapted from James S.A. Corey's acclaimed book series, The Expanse was first developed by Mark Fergus and Hawk Ostby for the Syfy Network. However, in 2018, Syfy prematurely ended the show's run in 2018. Sci-fi fans were devastated as The Expanse was easily one of the greatest additions to the genre in the 21st century.

Even some of the best sci-fi series, no matter how much potential they initially show, do not return after being canceled. Perhaps, in a parallel world, The Expanse, too, saw the same fate. Fate, however, had different plans for the series. After extensive campaigns from viewers, Amazon picked up the show and continued it for three more seasons, which cemented its "masterpiece" status.

Even in its first three seasons, The Expanse was undeniably an incredible sci-fi series. This explains why many relentlessly campaigned to bring it back. However, it was still short of becoming the masterpiece it eventually turned into. Everything from its portrayal of Newtonian physics to geopolitical conflicts between Earth, Mars, and the Belt were compelling in the first three seasons.

However, the show initially struggled with pacing issues. Especially in season 1, The Expanse felt inconsistent when it balanced Joe Miller's detective noir arc on Ceres with overarching political tension surrounding the Rocinante crew's escape. Syfy's network budget also limited the visual scope of the series, although its VFX team still did an incredible job.

Even main characters in The Expanse, like Holden, Amos, Naomi, and Alex, were yet to break free from their early archetypes in the show's initial chapters. The Expanse season 3 also had to race through Book 2 (Caliban's War) and significantly compress Book 3 (Abaddon's Gate) before ending its run. All in all, the sci-fi series was from its true potential after three installments.

The Expanse started putting its brilliance on full display after it returned for three more seasons on Prime Video. If it wasn't for its revival, it seems possible that The Expanse would have become another forgotten sci-fi series that was canceled a little too soon.

Landing on Prime Video after its revival not only allowed The Expanse to slightly extend its episode runtimes but also become more realistic. With no broadcast censors holding the show back from exploring the grit and violence of its world, The Expanse gradually became a more accurate and relatable representation of how space colonization works.

Going into season 5, The Expanse also finally got to split the Rocinante crew across different locations. This, in turn, helped the series explore and test the individual strengths and journey of its main roster. Even story beats surrounding the Ring Gate and the Marco Inaros and the Free Navy appeared much later, allowing the series to dive deeper into its portrayal of revolutionary stress and economic extinction.

All in all, if Prime Video had not given The Expanse a second chance, its legacy would have been far less long-lasting than it is today. While it is unfortunate that even Prime Video canceled the show before it could cover the complete length of its source material, it is hard not to be grateful that Syfy's cancellation did not mark the end of its run.

The Expanse is a sci-fi series set in the distant future where humanity has spread out across the solar system, but the alliances between the three most potent governing bodies have reached a state of cold war. A mixed-up crew finds themselves at the center of a dangerous intergalactic conspiracy that threatens to bring war to the colonized worlds.
