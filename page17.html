‘Shaun the Sheep: The Beast of Mossy Bottom’ Directors on ‘Game of Thrones’ Easter Egg and the ‘Challenge’ of Filming Michael Jackson’s ‘Thriller’ Dance in Stop-Motion

📅 Nov 24, 2026  |  Category: Entertainment  |  Read Source →

Michael Jackson’s “Thriller” sets the tone for “Shaun the Sheep: The Beast of Mossy Bottom,” the third feature film in Aardman Animations’ popular stop-motion franchise that follows Shaun as a mad scientist after the farmer ruins the flock’s pumpkin patch the night before Halloween.

While the film opens with an upbeat homage to the King of Pop, bringing the sequence to life required elaborate staging. During Variety’s Screening Series presented by Laika’s Wildwood, “Shaun the Sheep” filmmakers Steve Cox and Matthew Walker broke down the elaborate stop-motion animation process and how they brought the beloved character into the monster-filled world of Halloween. The filmmakers initially considered opening with a time jump to a cargo ship at sea, where the monster breaks out of its hold. They ultimately abandoned the idea, however, as it was “a bit too intense, and also too expensive,” Walker told Variety senior entertainment writer Angelique Jackson.

“It was scary in two different ways,” Cox joked.

Their backup plan — the “Thriller” homage — began with the filmmakers positioning the puppets on set and staging run-throughs, which could take up to several days. Animator Chris Sadler then posed the characters in sections, creating a “blocky” version of the animation to match the soundtrack. From there, the team could make adjustments before moving into the more detailed parts of the stop-motion process.

Typically, the puppets’ feet are nailed into the floor to keep them steady during filming, but because the characters needed to dance, the team had to specially rig them with rods that could later be removed for the remaining scenes. The staging also proved too cramped to accommodate the entire flock in front of the barn, requiring the sequence to be filmed in three separate passes.

But “Thriller” wasn’t the only pop-culture reference featured in the film. “Shaun the Sheep” famously features minimal dialogue, but there was one word that caught the audience’s attention: “Hodor,” the fan-favorite stableboy from “Game of Thrones.”

“I just laughed out loud when I read it [in the script], because I was a big ‘Game of Thrones’ fan,” Cox recalled, laughing.

“Shaun the Sheep: The Beast of Mossy Bottom” initially began as a half-hour Valentine’s Day special, centered on The Farmer meeting The Vet (a new character in the “Shauniverse”) and their budding relationship. As the creators explored the story further, however, they began gravitating toward Halloween, experimenting with different storylines and color palettes that would give the franchise a new look.

“We really wanted to go bold with the colors and play around with the horror genre as well,” Walker said. “We’re really conscious of not making it too scary, because obviously the audience for ‘Shaun the Sheep’ starts very young. Right from the beginning we were talking about it as a Halloween film, not a horror film, and just using the tropes of horror for comedy rather than scares.”

Even as the story evolved, the filmmakers wanted to preserve The Farmer and The Vet’s growing romance, plus introducing The Vet created room to explore a more emotional side of the franchise’s characters.

“We really wanted The Vet to feel like a character who could hopefully — I’d love to see her again in future Shaun the Sheep projects,” Walker says. “So we spent a lot of time getting her character right, because we really loved the idea of her character.”

The Vet also serves as something of an unwitting antagonist, with the flock initially frightened by her due to her occupation. While she shares a strong connection with The Farmer, the two have contrasting personalities: The Farmer is “lazy and slow,” while The Vet is “energetic and proactive.” But they find common ground in that they’re shy around other humans.

One thing that also sets The Farmer apart is his transformation into a hairy beast. Developing the monster was an extensive design process, first drawing various sketches, then building clay sculptures created by the puppet department. The team experimented with different shapes, explored computer-generated versions with varying hairstyles and even created a prototype using layers of teddy bear fur.

“It took a long time to find the right look,” Walker said. “When we started, we knew it had to be mistaken for Bigfoot. That kind of influenced the design, and it’s still The Farmer underneath, so it’s meant to just be hair. ”

The filmmakers wanted the beast to have a significant amount of bulk and a distinct silhouette that audiences could immediately recognize. Its horn-shaped head was ultimately inspired by The Farmer’s own receding hairline.

By the time the creative process was complete, the puppet of the beast featured a detailed Marquette inside and a 3D-printed core with control points that allowed animators to manipulate its movements. Its head was also mounted on a winder so it could move up and down.

In total, the puppet department created 11 different puppets of the character for the film.

“With the beast, he’s got such distinctive fronds of hair that come out of his head that each one of these things have to look identical,” Cox said. “So you have to give a big shout out to the puppet guys, that they’ve managed to make these puppets and make them look identical so that the continuity flows through the film.”

The beast first appears as he climbs over the top of the mountain. The sequence was initially much scarier, the filmmakers revealed, with heavy footsteps that shook the camera and more intense music. But they ultimately decided to break up the tension with a switch in music and the comedic shot of the flock running into the house.

Shaun also gets a reimagined look in the film, complete with a lab coat, goggles, mad scientist hair and even a human glove with five fingers despite his distinctly shaped hooves. The costume was initially intended to be worn throughout the film, but the team ultimately reserved it for the moments when Shaun is plotting in his windmill laboratory, which was inspired by “Frankenstein,” Walker revealed.

Alongside the windmill, the filmmakers also built a large-scale ship that pushed the limits of both the production’s budget and ambitions. The ship, the filmmakers said, was the largest single prop ever built for a “Shaun the Sheep” project.

“We knew we had a limited budget at the start of the film, but Matt and I wanted to be really ambitious. We were pushing things as much as we could,” Cox said. “We took inspiration from James Cameron. He only built half the Titanic, so I was like, ‘Well, we’ll do the same as James Cameron. Let’s do that!’”

But in the shot of the ship leaving the dock, the unfinished side of the vessel began to show, which posed a challenge for Cox and Walker. In just three days, the creative team constructed the other half the ship to use for the shot.

The giant jack-o’-lantern was also another ambitious moment in the film. Created through 3D printing, the pumpkin was so large that it exceeded the capabilities of the printers at Aardman. The team ultimately had to outsource the job to another company to bring the massive prop to life.

GTA 6 launch knocked Call of Duty's daily players down 42 percent, Alinea estimates

📅 Nov 24, 2026  |  Category: Entertainment  |  Read Source →

Grand Theft Auto 6's launch took a big bite out of Call of Duty’s player base, according to new estimates from Alinea Analytics, and four days on, it still hasn't recovered.

Writing in Alinea's newsletter, Rhys Elliott said Call of Duty HQ's daily active users (DAUs) across PlayStation, Xbox, and Steam fell 42 percent when GTA 6 launched on 19th November, dropping from an average of 7.5 million to 4.4 million. On PlayStation, which Elliott describes as "CoD's main platform", the drop was 44 percent.

"Sadly, CoD has barely bounced back after four days," Elliott wrote. "It's still down 41% from its baseline."

Fortnite and FC 27 took a hit too, but both are already recovering. Fortnite's DAUs on PlayStation and Xbox fell 28 percent, from 17.8 million to 12.8 million, but by the end of the weekend it had clawed its way back to 19 percent below its baseline.

Epic is also holding a live event to close out the current season on 5th December, which Elliott says the studio "hopes will pull players back after they’ve polished off GTA 6’s story".

FC 27's DAUs dropped by around 27 percent, from 5.9 million to 4.3 million, and it's already back within 20 percent of its baseline.

According to Alinea, of those who'd played GTA 6 by the end of its launch weekend, 29 percent had also played Fortnite this month, 16 percent had played Call of Duty HQ, and 11 percent had played FC 27.

Elliott said the pattern is largely what Alinea had predicted: the big live service games took an instant hit from GTA, but bounced back "because they're ingrained habits". Call of Duty is "the outlier here", he added, "and Xbox should - candidly - be worried about one of its premier franchises".

Elliott also points out that all of this is the impact of GTA 6's single-player mode. He expects its online component and PC port to arrive "some time next year", and when they do, he says, "the bounce back will not be so obtainable".

Alinea also estimates that GTA 6 has sold 44.9 million copies and made more than $4 billion since launch.

GTA 6 launched on Thursday, 19th November, for PS5 and Xbox Series X/S. While it's the biggest entertainment launch of all time, selling 32.5 million pre-release copies and making $3.79 billion by the end of its first day, you wouldn't know it from its Metacritic user score, as players review bomb it over its limited FPS and purported "woke" agenda.

Miley Cyrus’ Wedding Venue Was Frank Sinatra’s Former Palm Springs Home—and It’s Available as a Vacation Rental

📅 Nov 24, 2026  |  Category: Entertainment  |  Read Source →

After a stunning ceremony in Palm Springs, CA, Miley Cyrus and Maxx Morando are officially husband and wife—and part of the history of an iconic midcentury modern estate that formerly belonged to Frank Sinatra.

The musicians said “I do” on Nov. 21, surrounded by family, friends, and a bounty of stunning flowers. The picturesque celebration took place in the backyard of Twin Palms, right on the edge of the piano-shaped pool the “Fly Me to the Moon” singer once lounged by, according to Elle Decor.

Commissioned by Sinatra in 1947, the 4,500-square-foot home is located in the Movie Colony, a legendary Palm Springs neighborhood that Hollywood entertainers flocked to in the 1930s and 1940s.

The official Twin Palms website explains that Sinatra came to the desert town in hopes of finding a weekend retreat for his family after signing a movie contract with MGM Studios. He was initially looking for a Georgian-style estate with a lavish brick facade, but architect E. Stewart Williams persuaded him to go for something more “desert appropriate.”

The website also claims that the $150,000 commission was a hassle, with Sinatra giving the team just a few months to design and build the property so it would be ready in time for a Christmas party. The abbreviated timeline required contractors to work around the clock and made the process much more expensive.

The six years the famed crooner resided in the home were action-packed. He initially moved in with his first wife, Nancy Barbato, and when their relationship ended in 1948, it would become the backdrop for his highly publicized relationship with second wife, Ava Gardner.

Today, Twin Palms is available as a short-term rental for a variety of needs. It can be used as a vacation home or private event venue, and can also accommodate commercial uses such as photo shoots and filming. While exact pricing varies for vacation rentals, it’s typically about $4,400 a night.

Cyrus and Morando are far from the first couple to tie the knot at Twin Palms, with the website including a gallery of photos from previous weddings. The property can host up to 150 guests and includes a variety of spaces that can be used throughout the evening.

While the “Black Mirror” star opted to seat guests around the pool, the impressive great room, circular motor court, and additional lawn space opposite the pool are also available.

With four bedrooms and seven bathrooms, the estate comfortably sleeps eight guests. Each of the bedrooms boasts an en suite bathroom.

The primary suite has its own wing—a feature that was particularly appealing to Sinatra, who valued privacy.

In true midcentury modern fashion, the home features massive sliding glass doors that integrate indoor and outdoor living, connecting the great room to the sprawling patio and uniquely shaped swimming pool.

A detached pool house includes a kitchenette, bar, and cabana-style bathrooms.

In recent years, the home has been totally restored, using vintage designer furnishings to maintain the original retro character. Twin Palms still has the original sound and recording system, as well as Sinatra’s original recording studio—although, per the website, it is no longer functional.

The kitchen has been updated with modern Viking appliances and St. Charles modern cabinetry to call back to the original design.

In addition to memorabilia celebrating the singer around the home, there’s a chip in a bathroom sink which Sinatra had apparently damaged with a Champagne bottle.

Sinatra reportedly sold the home in 1953, after his split from Gardner. The home was designated a Palm Springs Class 1 Historic Site in 2011, by the Palm Springs Preservation Foundation.

BYD in talks to buy Ineos’ French factory as Grenadier moves to US

📅 Nov 24, 2026  |  Category: Business  |  Read Source →

The French factory Mercedes built for the tiny Smart city car, and that Jim Ratcliffe turned into the home of his Land Rover Defender tribute, could soon be building BYDs.

BYD confirmed today that it has entered exclusive negotiations to acquire Ineos Automotive’s plant in Hambach, France. If it closes, it would be the Chinese giant’s second passenger car factory in the EU after Hungary, and the first time a Chinese automaker buys an existing car plant in Europe.

The other half of the deal is Ineos packing up. Alongside the announcement, Ineos Automotive said it plans to move Grenadier and Quartermaster production to the US, which it says accounts for about 70% of its sales. It has shortlisted US sites, hasn’t named any, and is aiming to start production there in 2028.

The math is simple. Every Grenadier shipped from France to the US gets hit with a 15% tariff, and the Quartermaster pickup falls under the 25% tariff on imported pickups. When 7 out of 10 of your customers are American, building your trucks in Lorraine stops making sense.

Ineos says it will keep building both models at Hambach “for the medium term” if the sale goes through, so the two companies would share the site for a while.

Neither side disclosed a price or a closing date, and the sale still has to go through consultation with employee representatives. BYD will soon find out that France is nothing like China when it comes to employee representation. There’s also no word yet on which BYD models would come out of Hambach or when. Same for the plant’s roughly 1,000 employees: BYD made no commitment on how many it would keep.

BYD executive vice president Stella Li said the company wants to give European consumers “vehicles built in France” that use BYD’s technology.

Hambach opened in 1997 as “Smartville,” Mercedes’ factory for the Smart Fortwo, and it later built the all-electric Smart EQ Fortwo. Ineos bought it from Mercedes at the end of 2020 after dropping its plan to build a new factory in Wales, and Grenadier production started in 2022. Ineos says it has put nearly €540 million into the site since 2019.

Capacity is about 30,000 Grenadiers a year. That’s not much by BYD’s standards. BYD sold 3,561,615 passenger vehicles globally in the first 10 months of the year, which means 30,000 units is less than three days of its output.

There’s also a bit of irony in who’s buying. Ineos unveiled its first EV, the Fusilier, in early 2024 while romanticizing gas cars at the same event. A few months later, it shelved the electric model indefinitely, blaming “reluctant consumer uptake” of EVs. Now it’s selling its only factory to the world’s largest EV maker.

This isn’t a surprise. Back in May, Stella Li said BYD was in talks with Stellantis and “other companies too” to take over underused plants in Europe, and that BYD would rather own and run a plant than form a joint venture. Hambach fits that description exactly.

BYD’s first European car plant, in Szeged, Hungary, started trial production earlier this year and is expected to reach mass production in early 2027, with the Dolphin Surf and Atto 2 first in line. BYD eventually wants more than 150,000 vehicles a year out of it. Its separate factory project in Turkey has been put on pause.

The motivation is regulatory. China-built BYD EVs face a 17% countervailing duty in the EU on top of the standard 10% car tariff, imported Chinese hybrids are now subject to quotas, and the EU’s proposed “Made in Europe” rules would tie EV subsidies and public contracts to how much of a vehicle is built locally. A French plant solves a lot of those problems at once.

And BYD isn’t alone. Leapmotor started building the B10 at Stellantis’ Zaragoza plant in Spain in October, Dongfeng plans to build cars at a Stellantis factory in France, Geely is reportedly lining up a Ford plant in Spain, and Chery already assembles vehicles in Barcelona.

Chery is the one BYD should watch. Through October, BYD registered 320,445 vehicles in Europe, up 133%, according to Automotive News. Chery Group was just ahead with 320,631, up 235%. A gap of 186 cars.

Europe matters more than ever for BYD because home isn’t going well. BYD brand sales in China fell 40% to 1,544,699 vehicles over the first 10 months, and its global passenger vehicle sales are down 2.7%.

This is a smart, cheap move for BYD. Building Szeged took years, including land disputes with private owners. Hambach comes with a trained workforce, a paint shop, a supplier base on the German border, and a seller that’s motivated to leave. You don’t get a much easier entry into France, but the easiest in France is still probably more difficult than anything BYD faced in China when it comes to deploying new production capacity.

The 30,000-unit capacity is small, but BYD isn’t buying it for what it builds today. It’s buying a “Made in France” label ahead of local content rules.

We also can’t ignore the symmetry here. Ineos is leaving Europe to build in the US because of tariffs. BYD is buying into Europe because of tariffs. That’s protectionism moving factories. Europe keeps the jobs, and the workers who used to build Smart EVs might get to build EVs again.

And yes, it’s funny that BYD is moving into the home of the Grenadier, a vehicle that exists because Ratcliffe loved the old Defender, days after a court in The Hague barred BYD’s Ti7 from the EU for infringing Land Rover’s Defender design.

The real question is what BYD puts on the line in Hambach. If it’s battery-electric cars, great. If it’s plug-in hybrids built to get around quotas, Europe is trading one gas-burning 4×4 factory for another.

Electric boat maker Zin Boats raises $5M and launches propulsion business beyond the water

📅 Nov 24, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Chronicling the Seattle and Pacific Northwest startup scene. SEE MORE

by Kurt Schlosser on Nov 24, 2026 at 9:58 amNovember 24, 2026 at 9:58 am

Zin Boats made a splash with fast, pricey electric boats. But most of its business is now coming from what’s under the floor.

The Seattle startup has launched Zin Power, a new division that will supply its electric propulsion systems to other boat builders and to industries that rely on diesel engines, such as construction and mining.

The company also raised $5 million to scale up production and expanded its headquarters in the Interbay neighborhood to 11,000 square feet.

About 90% of the company’s revenue comes from selling motors and batteries to customers in the marine and drone industries, founder and CEO Piotr Zin told GeekWire. A typical setup includes two motors and about 500 kilowatt-hours of battery storage.

“We noticed a steady demand for pleasure craft year over year, but at the same time a massive increase in propulsion sales,” Zin said.

Customers include StanCraft Wooden Boats, which teamed with Zin on the 340 Hammerhead E, an all-electric wooden runabout. StanCraft delivered the first one to a customer this summer.

GeekWire first checked out the company in early 2024, when it was relaunching after a pandemic shutdown with its Z2R, a 20-foot carbon fiber runabout. Later that year Zin showed off the Z11, a 37-foot electric tender designed to shuttle passengers to and from megayachts at more than 60 mph. The company said boats on the platform would cost $1.75 million to $3.75 million.

The company also offers the Z7 Convertible, a 22-foot runabout with a removable roof that it pitches for fishing, surfing and cruising. The boat has a top speed of 40 mph and a range of more than 80 miles

Zin Power grew out of the drivetrains engineered for those boats. Zin sees the marine track record as a selling point for other industries that need high torque, including construction, mining and remotely operated vehicles (ROVs).

“The ocean is one of the harshest environments there is, so the technology has already proven itself under about the toughest conditions it could face,” Zin said.

Many of those machines still run on diesel engines, a technology more than a century old. Zin said operators are eager to switch to electric.

“What’s interesting is that economics is driving the shift, not regulation or image,” he said.

Electric systems cost up to 70% less to operate, according to Zin. He estimated that replacing a single 250-horsepower industrial diesel engine that runs eight hours a day could save as much as $1.5 million over 10 years.

The new funding will pay for more manufacturing capacity, a dedicated sales and engineering team to work with boat builders, and support for customers converting from diesel. Zin calls it a seed round, with a larger raise planned down the road. The company has raised $6.5 million to date, including a $1.275 million seed round in early 2024.

Zin Boats has nine full-time employees and brings on about a dozen contractors as needed.

For now, each Zin Power system is built to order. The company’s motors range from 100 to 600 kilowatts and deliver up to 2,700 pound-feet of torque, and its systems can run fully electric or as hybrids. A proprietary control unit that Zin Power calls the “Magic Box” manages everything from charging and cooling to software and safety.

“Our goal is to keep delivering what we’ve built while developing simpler, more advanced plug-and-play systems,” Zin said. “Eventually, we want a customer to be able to pull out a diesel engine and drop in a Zin Power system as a direct one-for-one replacement.”

New York Just Banned Subscriptions For Features Your Car Already Has

📅 Nov 24, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

In-vehicle subscriptions are pretty universally maligned among car owners. Most folks don't like the feeling of having to pay every month for features their cars were supposed to come with, and now New York State is doing something about it. Governor Kathy Hochul just signed a bill that bars automakers, dealers and their agents from selling subscriptions for features that are already installed.

The new law, which takes effect on February 27, 2027, is part of a new section of the state's General Business Law, according to Spectrum News. You can read the law for yourself here. It lays out fairly simple ground rules when it comes to subscriptions dealers or manufacturers can offer you: The feature must use hardware already in the car when it was bought or leased, and it must keep working without continual costs or support from the automaker or a third party. Those two things pretty much bring an end to crap like subscription-based heated seats and alike. It also is supposed to cover hands-off driving systems and enhanced navigation. Basically, once the hardware is physically in the car and the manufacturer isn't paying anything for you to use it, they can't then charge you. OEMs will also not be able to charge for software updates that fix safety defects.

Hochul called the practice a "deceptive business model," according to CBS 6 Albany. She also told automakers to keep their hands out of New Yorkers' wallets. It passed through the New York State Senate 57-3.

As with all good things, there are some exceptions. First of all, your lease or payment plan doesn't count as a subscription, so don't even try it, slick.

The law also doesn't apply to navigation updates, infotainment features, satellite radio, in-vehicle WiFi, telematics, roadside assistance or "vehicle-connected services" that rely on cellular data or other networks, according to Motor1. It also exempts software-dependent driver assistance features, like Tesla's Full Self-Driving or GM's Super Cruise.

I know what you're thinking. Didn't I just mention that hands-free driving systems are covered? Well, yes, but subscriptions tied to software and data are likely to survive, while hardware-only features are the real targets. Automakers will almost certainly make the argument that things like FSD and Super Cruise are "connected" or "software-dependent."

One prime example of what this bill aims to get rid of is BMW keeping things like a 360-degree camera behind a paywall. That hardware is already built into the car, and, as we reported, it doesn't require a data connection to operate. It's the same thing with features like high-beam assist and remote start, so I imagine it'll be hard for BMW and other companies with similar schemes to argue that these are "connected." I mean, these are the same people who argue that buyers actually want subscriptions, which is certainly not true.

In any case, violators will face civil penalties of up to $250 per point-of-sale for each violation, though it's not clear exactly how that'll be counted. It's not clear if other states are going to follow suit, but here's hoping.

TinyBuild CEO Downplays Threats of AI Game-Cloning

📅 Nov 24, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Daniel is a lifelong gamer with a love for various genres and gameplay styles who enjoys exploring games and playing from various angles.

As part of the ever-growing ingress of AI slop into the gaming industry, a new threat has recently arisen in the form of AI game-cloning. In a nutshell, a user feeds footage and descriptions of a game into their toaster of choice, as well as more specific data like coding cribbed from released demos, and instructs it to make effectively the same game. The infamous recent example of this was Needle in a Haystack Simulator, a small indie game that, following its announcement, received a deluge of AI-made knock-offs that flooded the Steam store.

Developers are taking this threat very seriously. During the most recent Steam Next Fest, which was already plagued by AI slop, several indie devs decided against releasing demos out of concern that bad actors would churn out knock-offs before they could release their actual games. Some developers have admitted to receiving anonymous extortion threats, saying that if a ransom isn’t paid, the crooks will release an AI knock-off of their game before they can release the real one.

There’s even been rumors that the PC release of Grand Theft Auto 6 could be expedited just to get ahead of the proverbial slop factory that’s already churning out identical open-world crime sandbox games.

Obviously, this is a big problem, and it’s only going to get worse as AI gets smarter, but at least according to game publisher tinyBuild’s CEO Alex Nichiporchik, developers should not be afraid of these malicious duplicates. As he explained in an interview with Gamesindustry.biz, AI may be able to imitate a game’s basic aesthetics, but game feel is another matter.

If you're looking for interesting new games to keep an eye on, check out the Coolest Demos from Steam Next Fest October 2026.

As Nichiporchik points out, while AI-made knock-offs of existing or announced games may have some superficial similarities, such as setting, basic game concepts, or story beats, those elements are only part of what makes a particular game a unique experience. In fact, it’s the “experience” itself that serves as the primary differentiator, and despite its best efforts, AI can’t do much in that department.

“There's a reason that every kind of AI slop game feels the same," he explained. "It's because when you try to make something from zero, AI will multiply your skills, and zero times a thousand is still zero.”

There's a reason that every kind of AI slop game feels the same.

Nichiporchik went on to say that, rather than being afraid of having their ideas stolen, developers should consider what they can do to make their games more difficult to duplicate, primarily in how the game handles and in its core gameplay loop.

As Nichiporchik himself puts it, you could have an entire army of designers and researchers trying to completely duplicate something popular, but it just won’t have that same distinctive zing as the real thing, authentically brought to life by its original creators. It’s not unlike the number of bodies games like Fortnite or Grand Theft Auto have left in their wake as other studios try unsuccessfully to ape their formulas.

“As long as you have great game feel and a great game loop, I think you're going to be OK,” Nichiporchik assured.

Despite his assurances, Nichiporchik admitted that these AI-made games, not unlike fast food, are ubiquitous and rather obnoxious. Part of the problem is that AI knock-offs are basically trying to capitalize on the hype generated by a game’s announcement, as well as promotion like trailers or demos, stealing attention away from the genuine article or even fooling players not in the know into buying the wrong product.

Nichiporchik’s suggestion for this is to narrow the gap between announcement and release. While the standard strategy is to give the hype thrusters time to warm up and let the game accumulate wishlists and word-of-mouth, playing things close to the vest with a faster turnaround seems to work just as well.

As Nichiporchik explained, the most recent game published by tinyBuild, Probably Stolen - Cyberpunk Shopkeeper Sim, was announcement-ready as early as May, but tinyBuild and Questing Goose Studio decided to wait until August to announce, then release in October instead. That game’s done gangbusters despite somewhat repetitive gameplay, so it seems like the strategy worked out, and without giving bad actors nearly as much time to try to copy their notes.

"We have certain ambitions when it comes to [The Witcher 4 and] Cyberpunk 2"

CD Projekt Red were subjected to such questioning in their latest earnings call, part of which was related to sales figures of The Witcher 3 and Cyberpunk 2077, that boring kind of thing. But CEO Michal Nowakowski did also respond to a comment about how GTA 6 may affect the development of The Witcher 4 and Cyberpunk 2.

"We have certain ambitions when it comes to [The Witcher 4 and] Cyberpunk 2, they have not changed when it comes to GTA 6," Nowakowski quite plainly explains in response to a question about how the biggest game of the year may influence budget or design, already sounding just that little bit exasperated over the questions related to Rockstar's latest. Nick Carter Loses Bid to Dismiss Sexual Battery Claim From Melissa Schuman

📅 Nov 24, 2026  |  Category: Business  |  Read Source →

A Los Angeles County judge has rejected Backstreet Boys singer Nick Carter’s bid to dismiss a sexual battery claim brought by singer and actress Melissa Schuman.

In a new ruling obtained by Rolling Stone, the judge said the claim could proceed under a California law that took effect Jan. 1, opening a two-year revival window for otherwise time-barred claims of adult sexual abuse against individual perpetrators. Unlike the law in effect when Schuman first sued Carter, the new statute allows lawsuits against individuals without requiring plaintiffs to establish a cover-up or name a business as a defendant.

“The individual claim against defendant Carter for sexual battery is proper irrespective of the existence of a cover-up. Defendants cannot prevail on summary adjudication merely by attacking the cover-up allegations,” Los Angeles County Superior Court Judge Lisa K. Sepe-Wiesenfeld wrote in her ruling issued Monday. “There is a conflict in the evidence presented as to whether a sexual assault of plaintiff occurred. Accordingly, a triable issue of material fact is present as to the first cause of action [for sexual battery].”

Rolling Stone reached out to lawyers on both sides for comment but did not immediately hear back on Tuesday.

Carter had sought to dismiss Schuman’s sexual battery claim by challenging the purported cover-up alleged in her lawsuit. He argued in filings that her “entire complaint would be time-barred except for her cover-up claim” and that there was “no viable sexual battery claim against Carter individually” without the claim of an “underlying cover-up.”

In a declaration, Carter said, “At no point in time have I ever offered, solicited and/or secured a non-disclosure agreement, a confidentiality agreement, a payoff, a settlement and/or otherwise participated in silencing an alleged victim of sexual assault.”

At a Nov. 6 hearing, Carter’s lawyer, Dale Hayes Jr., argued that Schuman’s “only evidence” of a cover-up came from Shannon Ruth, who alleges Carter sexually assaulted her on tour bus outside the Tacoma Dome in Washington after a show on February 25, 2001. Ruth claims Carter told her the incident was their “special secret” and that she would go to jail if she reported it to police. Hayes argued to the judge that Ruth’s testimony was “heavily contradicted” by 13 other witnesses who testified that Carter had no opportunity to encounter Ruth the night of the alleged assault. Silver Dawn Emerges from Drydock with New Pool and Dining Concept

📅 Nov 24, 2026  |  Category: Sports  |  Read Source →

Silversea has unveiled a series of enhancements onboard the Silver Dawn following its recent drydock in Genoa.

They include the addition of Riviera, a new poolside dining, bar and lounge concept first introduced on the Silver Spirit earlier this year, as well as new outdoor experiences.

The company describes Riviera as an open-air concept that is part restaurant, part bar where guests can enjoy drinks, fresh seafood and a mix of daily activities, from morning yoga to evening soirées.

“Silver Dawn’s enhancements reflect our commitment to bringing more options to guests across our fleet,” said Bert Hernandez, president, Silversea.

“With the expansion of Riviera and the addition new outdoor sports, guests have greater variety in how they spend their time onboard. As Silver Dawn sails the Mediterranean and the Caribbean in the coming seasons, guests will find that choice alongside the personalized service that defines Silversea.”

The Riviera concept brings a significant upgrade to the ship’s outdoor guest spaces, bringing new cabanas, loungers, umbrellas, ambient lighting and refreshed furniture layouts.

Guests will also be able to enjoy outdoor sports, with the ship getting three outdoor recreation spaces – a pickleball court, a golf putting area and a golf hitting bay.

The Silver Dawn will welcome its first passengers after drydock on November 25, 2026, a 14-day sailing from Civitavecchia (Rome) to Lisbon through the Mediterranean.

The voyage calls at 10 ports across Italy, Malta, Tunisia, Spain, and Portugal, including Naples, Valletta, Trapani in Sicily, Tunis, Cagliari in Sardinia, Cartagena, Malaga and Cádiz, with overnights in Valletta and Malaga.
