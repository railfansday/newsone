GitHub Pilot Turns AI Security Findings Into 55 Open Source Fixes

📅 Nov 17, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

GitHub reported on November 17, 2026, that a security pilot with Microsoft produced 55 fixes across six open source projects as of November 15, 2026. The Codename MDASH trial filtered 9,791 possible issues into 99 private reports, each checked by researchers and accompanied by a proof of concept and suggested fix.

Fourteen of the fixes had already been included in releases. The projects were AutoGPT, Caddy, Express, FastMCP, ImageMagick and Netty. Maintainers decided which findings to fix, how to fix them and when to ship, with reports and patches staying on GitHub.

The design addresses a maintenance problem created by cheaper AI scanning: duplicates and reports that are not actual vulnerabilities already consume maintainer time, while remediation still requires human judgment. GitHub wants researchers to consult maintainers before pointing another model at their projects and to submit patches alongside any findings.

Microsoft’s Codename MDASH system scanned each project with its MAI Cyber model and suggested fixes. A GitHub Security Lab AI triage agent, built on tailored Copilot skills, removed duplicates, checked validity and focused on high-impact bugs. Seven Microsoft FORGE Lab researchers then manually checked every remaining finding before reports went to maintainers through GitHub private vulnerability reporting.

The first fixes shipped about a week after the initial scan, according to GitHub. Caddy’s maintainers fixed all 22 reports they received in a week or less. ImageMagick, maintained by two people, also received 22 reports; in one case, its maintainers pushed a fix in 15 minutes after a summary identified the exact lines of code and showed that an apparently familiar issue was different.

At AutoGPT, a report arrived three days after maintainers shipped a broken check and pointed to the right line. They took the fix further than the suggested patch, wrote it within a day and had it in production less than a week later. Across the pilot, maintainers used some suggested fixes as written and adapted others.

A Netty maintainer described the remaining constraint: “Finding vulnerabilities is no longer our bottleneck. Fixing them is the hard part, and that depends on maintainers.”

GitHub described the project sample as small, the conclusions as early lessons and the manual review as not yet scalable; researchers spent about two days per project checking findings by hand. Low-quality reports still reach maintainers every day.

GitHub also identified private-fork CI/CD testing and maintainer access to cyber-capable models as remaining needs. The tests would verify fixes on private forks, and model access would help maintainers verify a proof of concept before and after remediation. Its recommended standard for AI-generated reports is to check each finding against the project’s existing knowledge, threat model and security policy, report privately, include a suggested fix and let maintainers set the pace.

GitHub plans to bring these lessons into private vulnerability reporting and security advisories. It will offer AI security scans with findings checked before private reporting, plus GitHub Copilot Max, to all alumni projects of the GitHub Secure Open Source Fund and extend the support to future Fund sessions. GitHub and Microsoft are also working to expand researcher review while preserving those checks.

For AI-assisted vulnerability disclosure, the pilot’s trade-off is concrete: automation can reduce a flood of raw findings to a manageable private queue, while expert review and maintainer control determine whether those reports lead to patches. Expansion hinges on preserving that standard without returning the triage burden to maintainers.

How reverse mortgage LOs can help borrowers to self-service

📅 Nov 17, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Reverse mortgage servicers are investing in borrower portals, mobile apps and smarter phone systems to reduce call volumes. Celink is launching ReverseLink soon, while Onity is expanding digital draw requests and emphasizing closing checklist steps for loan officers.  AI Summary

Reverse mortgage servicers are investing in portals, mobile applications and smarter phone systems so borrowers can manage their loans without contacting a human customer service representative. Servicing executives say that loan officers can play a key role in whether these tools actually work.

Gail Balettie, senior vice president of client satisfaction at Celink, and Ryan McIntire, director of reverse mortgage servicing controls at Onity Mortgage, outlined their companies’ self-service offerings during a panel discussion moderated by Chris Downey of CrossCountry Mortgage at last week’s National Reverse Mortgage Lenders Association (NRMLA) Annual Meeting & Expo.

“In today’s world, where we are conditioned to some extent to be able to go online, get immediate satisfaction, to do things without talking to somebody, the more we can do that, the better we can reach our customers,” McIntire said.

Balettie announced at NRMLA Annual that her company’s mobile app, ReverseLink, will be launching “in the next few weeks.” Its design is similar to Celink’s website and borrowers will receive an invitation to download it. The company is the largest independent subservicer of reverse mortgages and has an estimated market share of about 75% across federally insured Home Equity Conversion Mortgages (HECMs) and proprietary loans.

McIntire told the audience that Onity‘s servicing app is already available on iPhone and Android devices and mirrors its borrower portal. He stressed the importance of providing familiar technology to senior homeowners so “they don’t feel like they’re having to learn something all over again.”

Celink’s soon-to-be-released app will allow clients to accomplish multiple tasks, Balettie said. They’ll be able to request payoff quotes, make voluntary payments, upload forms and download up to two years of statements. The app automatically generates confirmation that an uploaded form has been received, cutting down the number of calls needed for this purpose.

“They can request a line-of-credit draw, and if they do so, they’re going to get their funds a day or two faster than the typical five-day turn time, because it comes in an automated fashion,” she added.

ReverseLink also has a number of FAQs and educational videos, covering subjects like how to manage the account online, what happens to the reverse mortgage when the borrower dies, how to shop for insurance coverage and how to file a claim, and how to find help with small home repairs.

Onity’s portal ties into the company’s system of record, McIntire said. It shows three months of transactions and includes an introduction package that’s tailored to the structure of the loan — for example, any funds set aside for required repairs. Onity is also in the process of building fully digital draw request forms. Today, borrowers have to download and complete these forms before returning it via email or fax, but the goal is to process e-signed draw requests within a few business days, McIntire said.

“Obviously, information that’s available to the borrower via this site is very sensitive. We obviously, as servicers, have to be very cognizant and mindful of fraud. So it does have multifactor authentication that just helps provide that additional sense of security,” he added.

Borrowers commonly contact their loan officer, rather than their servicer, when they have questions about their loan. Balettie said that LOs can take an “ABCD” role to approach to providing assistance in this area.

Making sure a servicer has the correct contact information for a borrower may seem obvious, but it doesn’t happen with perfect uniformity. “We get about 70% of our loans that board with an email address. I want to see that be 100%,” Balettie said. “I don’t know very many people who don’t have an email address in this day and age.”

Balettie sought to distinguish between the alternate contact on the loan application and an authorized third party. The latter may discuss the account but cannot change contact information or request a draw or payoff. Conversely, servicers use the alternate contact only when they cannot reach the borrower, she said.

In response to an audience question, Balettie said that loan officers are welcome to call a servicer on their client’s behalf, but to receive a relevant response, they must be listed as the authorized third party. This is especially important for brokers, who lose access to information once a loan is sold. “We have privacy laws we have to follow,” she added.

A power of attorney can act for a living borrower, but legal authority ends at death, the panelists noted. Downey urged originators to execute the authorized party form at closing and warned that without it, payoffs for a borrower “can be a nightmare.” Having this information on file removes the need to hire an attorney to search for heirs after the borrower dies.

Balettie said originators should collect a canceled check and the direct deposit form at closing. Borrowers without a check can provide a redacted bank statement or bank letter with their account and routing numbers. Due to security concerns, servicers don’t allow bank details to be added online, but they’ll accept faxed or emailed documents, and closing is the best time to gather them.

AWS Brings OpenTSLM Time-Series Reasoning to SageMaker JumpStart

📅 Nov 17, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Amazon Web Services announced on November 17, 2026 that OpenTSLM-Flamingo Gemma 3 4B is available in Amazon SageMaker JumpStart, AWS’s machine-learning model hub. Customers can deploy the 5.24-billion-parameter model to a real-time SageMaker AI inference endpoint within their own AWS environments.

OpenTSLM interprets numerical measurements alongside text such as maintenance records, market news and engineers’ observations, then returns natural-language explanations and answers. The JumpStart package pairs Gemma 3 4B with a time-series encoder and cross-attention layers, which let the language model attend to encoded signals. Its weights occupy about 10.5 GB of GPU memory when stored in bfloat16.

The OpenTSLM research paper distinguishes this Flamingo architecture from SoftPrompt. Both treat time series as a native input modality, but SoftPrompt adds learned time-series tokens to the language model’s sequence, while Flamingo uses a Perceiver Resampler to produce a fixed-size latent representation and gated cross-attention to integrate it. The original evaluation covered ECG question answering, sleep staging and human activity recognition, giving the managed release a research foundation rooted in medical and wearable data.

The separate OpenTSLM repository is MIT-licensed and supplies fine-tuning code, with pretrained checkpoints available through the project’s Hugging Face organization. It identifies the project as a collaboration between Stanford University’s Schmiedmayer Lab and ETH Zurich’s Agentic Systems Lab.

AWS describes Nexthink and Aionic Labs as building toward proactive assistance through Spark, Nexthink’s personal IT agent, so employees can receive help before asking for it. The workflow combines recent CPU, memory, disk, application-stability and connectivity measurements with timestamped events, device configuration and software versions. The model supplies a state summary with supporting signals, a recommended check and action, and an expected outcome that Spark can verify.

AWS’s low-disk-space example is illustrative. The model connects falling free space, a rising disk queue and more frequent application hangs to symptoms such as slow performance or difficulty saving files. Spark checks whether an IT-approved cleanup is appropriate, shows the employee what would be removed and seeks confirmation before acting. It then checks the result and offers escalation to IT support if the problem persists.

The endpoint standardizes each series to a mean of zero and a standard deviation of one, while supplying the original mean and standard deviation as text so the model receives both shape and scale. Real-time calls have a 60-second limit and a 6 MB request-body cap. AWS reports roughly 20 generated tokens per second on the default instance; instances are processed sequentially, and asynchronous inference is recommended for longer-running work.

Flamingo’s fixed-size representation bounds what reaches the language model, but it does not give the complete system unlimited memory or guarantee that a brief event remains identifiable in a very long recording. AWS advises testing representative normal and abnormal windows, retaining sampling resolution for short events, aligning related channels and including units, sampling intervals and observation periods in the context. The payload cap and the model’s useful analysis window are separate constraints.

AWS’s adaptation workflow uses TimeNet, which provides a common dataset format and Python API with connectors and immutable versions. TimeNet’s documentation explicitly limits its scope to data; training, inference, model definitions and evaluation metrics are outside it. The example loads a pretrained checkpoint from Hugging Face and ARFBench data through TimeNet, runs a SageMaker training job, stores the adapted model in Amazon S3 and deploys it to a separate real-time endpoint with the same request format.

Datadog’s ARFBench contains 750 multiple-choice question-answer pairs derived from 142 time series across 63 real internal incidents, using production telemetry and additional context. The training job updates the time-series encoder, Perceiver Resampler, cross-attention layers and language-model token embeddings while keeping the rest of Gemma 3 frozen.

In AWS’s own run on one ml.g7e.2xlarge instance, accuracy on 135 held-out ARFBench questions rose from 5.0% before fine-tuning to 45.9% afterward. The pretrained model left most answers empty, while the adapted model answered every question with a supplied option. The run demonstrates adaptation to a new task and answer format; it is not a comparison with other models or an official ARFBench leaderboard result.

The JumpStart release makes a native time-series architecture easier to deploy and adapt, while its evidence remains tied to particular windows, tasks and answer formats. For operational teams, the central question is whether reviewed, representative examples show that the model’s explanations and recommended actions are reliable enough for the workflows they are meant to support.

Microsoft Launches AI Transparency Initiative for Enterprise Agents

📅 Nov 17, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Microsoft announced the AI Transparency Initiative on November 17, 2026, at Microsoft Ignite, bringing together more than 40 organizations to develop open standards for securing and governing AI models and agents across providers.

Chairman and CEO Satya Nadella introduced the effort with customers and partners including Accenture, Adobe, KPMG, Mastercard and Volvo Cars. The collaboration aims to make AI systems accountable, independently verifiable and safe by design, regardless of the model or provider. Its governing principle is that greater AI autonomy requires greater transparency.

Enterprises are connecting models to data, tools, credentials and infrastructure, allowing agents to take actions without human approval at every step. Microsoft says existing controls can observe activity around models but cannot see what happens within them or independently verify models’ accounts of their own behavior.

The company’s Security blog makes a similar distinction for agent containment: discovering, governing and containing an agent still does not reveal how its underlying model behaves. That leaves three questions after consequential activity: what the AI was asked and authorized to do, what happened between request and outcome, and whether the activity can be independently verified.

Microsoft compares agents with access to data, tools and credentials to privileged insiders operating at machine speed and scale. The resulting evidence gap creates security, compliance, audit and accountability risks, while accountability remains with the deploying organization when leaders must answer to customers, employees, boards, auditors and regulators.

The initiative’s framework organizes those requirements into three parts. See covers the request and authorization, the model and runtime, the data and tools accessed, and the actions taken. It also calls for continuous monitoring of agent activity and risk signals against policies and thresholds.

Verify addresses the source of evidence. Records of agent identity, delegated intent and actions would show whether information came from the model, its runtime or independent identity and policy systems. Model-reported signals could then be checked against independently observed records through an organization’s own security and compliance systems, rather than relying solely on the provider’s account.

Control uses evidence of each workflow’s purpose, owner, data and delegated authority to enforce policies before consequential actions. The same records would support reconstruction during investigations, containment, recovery and audits. They are intended to remain usable when organizations change models or providers, including across closed and open-weight models, so development teams can update agents without compromising security governance or compliance audits.

Microsoft will expand its participation in NIST’s SI Agent Standards Initiative and draw on its own research to develop open, industry-wide standards for AI transparency, security and governance. The NIST program is designed to foster industry-led technical standards and open protocols so autonomous agents can operate securely and interoperate across digital systems.

Its strategic pillars combine voluntary guidelines and gap analyses for industry-led standards, work to reduce barriers to interoperable community-led protocols, and research into agent authentication, identity infrastructure and security evaluations.

Microsoft also plans to incorporate the initiative’s work into Microsoft AI, Microsoft Foundry and Microsoft Copilot. Customers would be able to use the resulting evidence with security and compliance tools from Microsoft or other providers.

The initiative’s practical test is whether participants can turn that framework into common standards and portable evidence: records that let an enterprise govern agents before they act and reconstruct their behavior afterward, even when the underlying model or provider changes.

Microsoft and Salesforce announced Copilotforce on November 17, 2026, unveiling the partnership at Microsoft Ignite with a Salesforce plugin for Microsoft Copilot in public preview. The plugin includes more than 38 prebuilt sales skills and connects customer records and supported actions with workplace context from meetings, email and documents.

Microsoft Work IQ, the intelligence layer powering Copilot, supplies context from Teams meetings, Outlook emails and documents held in SharePoint and OneDrive. Salesforce in Copilot combines that activity with information held in Salesforce, giving sellers a view of recorded account details and customer interactions across Microsoft 365. Authenticated Salesforce MCP access, the connection used for these interactions, lets sellers retrieve customer information and take supported actions within their existing Salesforce permissions.

The announced workflows address opportunity planning, deal risk and follow-up. Pipeline Review and Close Planning are intended to help sellers decide which opportunities need attention and develop plans whose customer and deal context can inform proposals and business cases in Word and PowerPoint. Deal Review, Customer Health and Renewal Radar combine Salesforce opportunity data with concerns raised in Outlook, escalations discussed in Teams and unresolved commitments, helping identify where a seller may need to intervene.

Call Follow-Up turns customer conversations into follow-up drafts, internal recaps and proposed activities for Salesforce. Sellers review and confirm the next steps rather than reconstructing conversations and updating several systems manually.

Sales Cloud in Teams extends the integration into ongoing conversations with pipeline and quota progress, deal notifications and briefing materials. It is in pilot with enterprise customers, and an open beta is planned for later in 2026.

The governance model divides responsibility between the platforms. Salesforce controls customer-data access and governs supported business actions, while Microsoft applies its existing enterprise compliance controls to communications and documents. Supported writebacks to Salesforce require explicit user confirmation and must respect Salesforce validation rules, keeping customer records governed and auditable. User approval and existing permissions therefore form the boundary between Copilot’s assistance and an actual change to a customer record.

The partnership also addresses enterprises that keep customer data in Salesforce and operational or financial data in Microsoft Fabric. Its two-way Zero Copy integrations between Salesforce Data 360 and Fabric are intended to support queries and analysis across both without building pipelines or maintaining duplicate datasets.

Data Federation is generally available, allowing joint customers to query supported Microsoft Fabric OneLake data directly within Data 360 while the source data remains in Fabric. The reverse connection, Salesforce Data 360 shortcut, is in public preview and shares curated customer intelligence into OneLake for analytics, reporting and AI workloads without duplicating the data. Regional availability and customer access depend on applicable customer agreements.

Accenture is the design and implementation partner and is also deploying and validating the integrations in its global sales organization. The consulting firm says it brings experience across 13 industries and will share lessons from its internal deployment with clients.

The scale of that internal test is substantial: Accenture reports that it has rolled out Copilot to more than 740,000 people and has 55,000 Salesforce users. Chair and CEO Julie Sweet described the company as “customer zero for Copilotforce” and said the deployment is intended to give sellers more time with customers while trusted intelligence and agents work alongside them.

Copilotforce’s immediate significance lies in how those pieces fit together: Microsoft supplies the Copilot interface and workplace context, Salesforce retains control over customer data and actions, and the Fabric connections limit the need for duplicate datasets. Accenture’s pilot is an early test of that arrangement inside a large sales organization. Of the capabilities with stated availability, only Data Federation is generally available, so the near-term enterprise question is whether the preview and pilot tools can reduce manual sales work while preserving those controls.

The U.S. Senate plans to vote this week on a bill aimed at easing America’s affordability crisis and fighting climate change that hunting and fishing groups worry will also erode many of the nation’s bedrock conservation laws.

The complex and winding 400-page Bipartisan American Affordability and Jobs Act focuses on lowering energy costs, something both Republicans and Democrats have championed and and that helped drive Democratic gains in the midterm elections. BAAJA also, however, ties jobs and household bills with curtailing public-lands protections. And in a twist that’s perplexing many conservation groups, the bill is co-sponsored by Sens. Martin Heinrich (D-N.M.) and Mike Lee (R-Utah), two lawmakers known for being on nearly opposite ends of the public-lands spectrum.

BAAJA is largely focused on permitting reform, something the country hasn’t tackled in decades, according to the Bipartisan Policy Center. In practice, the legislation would raise the standard for whether projects approved by one administration can be revoked by a future administration.

That means an industrial solar project permitted by one president, for example, could not be stymied by a future president intent on preventing renewables. It also speeds up permitting processes and cuts red tape for everything from energy projects to landscape-scale changes like prescribed fire.

But sportsmen and conservationists say the bill would not just expedite climate or environmentally friendly projects. BAAJA would also make it much harder — or potentially impossible — for an administration to overturn leases or approvals for projects as controversial as hyperscale data centers on public lands or mines in the same watersheds as sensitive wilderness areas. It’s a bill that prioritizes development, many observers say, at the cost of the very “red tape” that has been responsible for conserving many of the country’s most priceless fish, wildlife, and wild places.

“I understand this is a big, bipartisan bill — it has some things in it I would rather it not have. But I negotiated the deal based on what was actually possible to get signed into law,” Heinrich wrote in an email to Outdoor Life. “Because when I say climate change is an existential threat, I mean it. And I will not let an opportunity to do something about it pass by.”

Conservationists like Ingrid Lyons, executive director of Save the Boundary Waters, understand the need for stability around permitting development. The whiplash between issuing and revoking permits for public land development projects every time an administration changes has likely hurt the U.S.’s ability to move forward with important energy projects, including in renewables like wind and solar.

But she fears this bill could instead cement the creation of controversial projects like a copper-sulfide mine at the headwaters of the Boundary Waters Canoe Area Wilderness or a copper-molybdenum-gold mine in the salmon-rich waters of Alaska’s Bristol Bay. Permits for projects like these have been denied, among other issues, because of environmental risk. If permits are reissued in similar scenarios, legislation like BAAJA could prevent future administrations from stopping development in places that truly need protection.

“What about landscape certainty?” Lyons says. “We’re overlooking in some ways the impact to communities that are relying on protection and not just projects.”

The conservation community has become increasingly vocal in its opposition to the bill since Heinrich and Lee partnered with Sens. Sheldon Whitehouse (D-R.I.), and Shelley Moore Capito (R-W.Va.), to introduce the legislation in late September. Lee has a clear track record of supporting public land sales and undermining existing public-lands conservation, including 2025’s attempt to include federal land sales in the One Big Beautiful Bill.

A lengthy version of BAAJA is before the Senate as of Tuesday. The current bill includes a variety of red flags for hunters, anglers, tribes, endangered-species advocates, and other conservationists.

The Theodore Roosevelt Conservation Partnership acknowledged the need for permit reform but said in a statement that “progress should not come at the expense of the conservation safeguards and public review that give hunters and anglers a voice in how our lands and waters are managed.”

For Lyons, it comes down to the potential unintended consequences of sweeping legislation like BAAJA.

“We need to be exceptionally aware of the implications of this bill, which could be that a lot of these really beloved places could have projects many communities have worked really hard to oppose cemented into place.”

Many in the conservation community were surprised by the bill’s sponsors, given Heinrich’s history as a sportsman and conservationist who regularly defends public lands and sensitive places, and Lee’s general position as a proponent of development and public-lands sell-off.

But in Heinrich’s statement to Outdoor Life, he emphasizes the impact of climate change on our planet, including the places where people hunt, hike, bike and camp. Independent modeling, he said, predicts the bill will lead to a 20 percent reduction in power sector emissions, or the “equivalent to taking 39 coal plants offline for 10 years.”

“You cannot protect a trout stream without paying attention to the warming temperatures and declining flows that threaten it,” he wrote. “You cannot protect wildlife habitat while ignoring the impacts of increasingly severe drought and wildfire. We need to reduce emissions, and that means getting more clean energy onto the grid.”

In an Oct. 30 op-ed in the Albuquerque Journal, Heinrich explained how New Mexico’s SunZia Wind and Transmission project took 17 years to permit and build.

He also pushed back on what he called misinformation about the bill, stating he doesn’t believe data centers should be on public land. The bill also requires large scale data center operators to pay the full cost of electricity consumed by the facility.

The Izaak Walton League’s conservation director, Kate Miller, thinks each of the bill’s sponsors are acting in good faith and trying to do what is right for their constituents. Many projects, including those aimed at restoring forests and wildlife habitat, have been held up by litigation. Something like BAAJA could move those forward.

But Miller would have liked to see these issues tackled individually instead of in a massive bill. And while she hopes that, if the bill passes, the administration will respect existing laws, the past two years has brought an onslaught of changes to most of the nation’s bedrock environmental laws including the Clean Water Act, the Endangered Species Act, and the National Environmental Policy Act.

“We want to make sure what comes out is not something that sacrifices those core protections that allow us to have healthy waters and habitats,” Miller says. “That we don’t sacrifice one for the other.”

Meanwhile, both industry and conservation groups say they’re working to lobby lawmakers for changes and amendments. In a change set to roll out in the coming weeks, you’ll be able to put the brakes on Gemini model training without losing access to your previous conversations with the AI. Here’s how this works now — and how it will work down the road.

Currently, Gemini offers a setting called Keep Activity that controls not only whether and for how long your chats are saved but also whether they’re used to train the AI. You can’t separate the two.

To correct this problem, Google will replace Keep Activity with two new settings. With the first setting, you’ll be able to keep or delete your past chats and choose how long your history will be saved. That means you’ll need to head to Gemini’s Settings if you wish to opt out of model training but still be able to view and access your previous chats.

Almost all AIs include a setting for model training that you typically have to manually disable if you’re concerned about your privacy. But most let you opt out without turning off your chat history.

Chester Cabalza, president of International Development and Security Cooperation, a Philippine think tank, said: “Strategically, Manila opens its doors for conciliation with Beijing to stabilize normal ties. But on the ground, the real battle has yet to subside.”

Domestically, Mr. Marcos has to walk a tightrope, too.

“For Filipino fishermen and coastal communities, the South China Sea is not simply about a geopolitical dispute. It concerns livelihoods, access to maritime resources and national dignity,” said Arnaud Leveau, president of the Asia Centre, a Paris-based think tank. Any diplomatic accommodation perceived as sacrificing Filipino interests, he said, would therefore be politically risky.

The wider region has been battered by the consequences of another Trump policy: the war in Iran. Southeast Asia depends heavily on oil and gas imports from the Middle East and was roiled by soaring prices of fuel. In the Philippines, crops rotted in fields because farmers could not afford to transport them to markets. In Thailand, public servants were asked to use stairs instead of elevators to reduce energy expenses.

ASEAN comprises 11 nations — Brunei, Cambodia, Indonesia, Laos, Malaysia, Myanmar, the Philippines, Singapore, Thailand, Vietnam and, since last year, East Timor — with vastly different degrees of freedom and wealth. The bloc represents almost 700 million people, or almost 10 percent of the global population.

Making decisions on a consensus-basis and pledging not to interfere in one other’s internal affairs, the bloc has often been criticized as slow and ineffective, with the situation in Myanmar often cited as an example. A coup in that country in 2021 unleashed a civil war that ranks among the world’s most violent conflicts.

Myanmar’s leaders remain barred from ASEAN summits, under an agreement that was extended on Monday. Still, almost half of the bloc’s member states have had bilateral engagements with Myanmar since the coup leader, Min Aung Hlaing, used stage-managed elections to take the post of president this year. Washington has also recently re-engaged in direct talks with Myanmar.

Mr. Trump’s attendance at the summit, which concludes with an ASEAN-U.S. meeting, is part of a state visit to the Philippines. He is expected to hold a bilateral meeting with Mr. Marcos on Wednesday before heading to China for an Asia-Pacific Economic Cooperation summit. Mr. Marcos has said little publicly about meeting Mr. Trump; but earlier this year, he described Washington as a “steadfast partner.” His administration has hinted that the two sides are working on a military aid agreement.

In recent days, hundreds of demonstrators have gathered in Manila to protest Mr. Trump’s visit. They oppose his “Pax Silica” initiative, a move to build a supply chain for the artificial intelligence industry that bypasses China, saying it would exploit Philippine natural resources.

Renato Reyes, who led one of the protest groups on Sunday afternoon, said, “Marcos is so desperate to welcome Donald Trump that he will roll out the red carpet, even if Trump arrives late for the summit.”

President Trump sets off this week for meetings in Asia that will culminate in a summit with two strongmen he has often praised: President Xi Jinping of China and President Vladimir V. Putin of Russia.

It will be the first time the three men, who command the world’s largest nuclear arsenals, will have sat down together. Mr. Trump plans to discuss the reduction of nuclear weapons in the world and the war against Iran, according to a White House spokeswoman. He is not expected to bring up Russia’s war with Ukraine.

Mr. Trump will travel first to Manila, then to southern China, and will meet with regional leaders confronting an array of uncertainties. Those include the course of the war between the United States and Iran, high energy prices and mounting concern over the risks of artificial intelligence.

For Mr. Trump, the trip will be a pivot to foreign policy and relationships after American voters delivered a swift rebuke to his party in the midterm elections. His trip also comes as his administration faces scrutiny over a scandal involving the former chief executive of Nvidia, the tech giant, which has raised questions about Chinese espionage and American tech exports.

The highlight of Mr. Trump’s trip is expected to be his meeting with Mr. Xi and Mr. Putin on Thursday in China.

The prospect of their summit has drawn comparisons to the 1945 conference in the Crimean city of Yalta, where the leaders of the United States, the Soviet Union and Britain met to decide the shape of postwar Europe.

The White House has said that Mr. Trump wants to discuss the threat of nuclear proliferation. U.S. officials have expressed concerns about China’s accelerating nuclear buildup, expected to reach 1,000 nuclear warheads by 2030.

The last nuclear arms control treaty between the United States and Russia expired in February. Trump administration officials have said that any new agreement on nuclear arms control must include China, but Beijing has shown no interest in the topic. China has an estimated 620 nuclear warheads, compared with the more than 5,000 that Russia and the United States have each, according to the Stockholm International Peace Research Institute.

U.S. administration officials said they did not expect a deal out of the meeting, which they characterized as a starting point for discussions.

The gathering has prompted concern in Ukraine that Mr. Putin might push for a deal, deciding Ukraine’s fate without Kyiv’s involvement. A U.S. administration official said Ukraine was not expected to be part of the discussion between the three leaders.

Mr. Trump will start his trip in the Philippines, the oldest American treaty ally in the Asia-Pacific region, as the United States faces questions about its support for its partners in a region where China’s military and economic might continues to grow.

In Manila, President Ferdinand R. Marcos Jr. plans to discuss with Mr. Trump the South China Sea, where the Philippines and China have competing claims. Philippine officials have said that they hope Mr. Trump’s visit might result in more American military assistance.

Mr. Trump is also expected to meet with leaders of ASEAN, a Southeast Asian grouping of 11 nations, as well as a gathering of the so-called Quad grouping of the United States, India, Australia and Japan.

Prime Minister Anthony Albanese of Australia said on Sunday that he expected “lots of engagement” with Mr. Trump during the meetings.

“Obviously, that’s our most important ally,” he said. “But we want to see something constructive come out of that, including we want to see a de-escalation in the conflicts that are around the world.”

Chinese analysts described the meeting of the Quad group, formed in part to counter China, as an effort by Mr. Trump to project power after his party’s defeat in the midterms. “Its symbolic value outweighs its practical value,” Xu Liping, a senior scholar at Guangdong University of Foreign Studies, told Shanghai Observer, a state-run outlet.

After Manila, Mr. Trump will travel to the Chinese tech hub of Shenzhen, where he will meet Mr. Xi on the sidelines of the Asia-Pacific Economic Cooperation.

Jensen Huang, a key adviser who accompanied Mr. Trump on his last trip to China, will not be by his side. Mr. Huang helped shape the president’s views about technological competition with China and persuaded him to loosen restrictions on selling advanced chips to China.

Mr. Huang stepped down from his role at Nvidia after revelations that he had an affair with a Chinese businesswoman, Alice Huang, who U.S. intelligence officials believe was collecting intelligence on Mr. Huang for Beijing. China has rejected the allegations, calling them “smears.” Ms. Huang has not responded to requests for comment.

So far, the president has not raised the issue with Mr. Xi and is not expected to during their meeting on Thursday. But the saga is likely to cause unease in China about whether Washington will impose tighter controls on the export of technology to the country.

As a result, U.S.-based analysts speculated that Mr. Xi might try to use his personal relationship with Mr. Trump to forestall such restrictions.

Yun Sun, director of the China program at the Stimson Center in Washington, said Mr. Huang’s downfall would likely mean “there is going to be more anxiety on the Chinese side about their access to American chips.” She expects Mr. Xi to emphasize the importance of easing U.S. controls on chip exports to China during their meeting.

Cooperation between China and the United States on artificial intelligence may be more difficult after the Nvidia scandal.

Still, there may be progress on the creation of a notification system that would allow the countries to alert each other about national security issues related to artificial intelligence, which the two sides agreed to in September.

“There’s some shared concern on both sides that U.S. or Chinese models might be misused by bad actors and cause damage to the U.S., China, or other countries,” said Kyle Chan, a fellow at the Brookings Institution focusing on China’s technology development.

When Mr. Xi sits down with Mr. Trump in Shenzhen, he will be dealing with a president who will soon face a new constraint at home: a Democrat-controlled Congress.

Lawmakers may pressure Mr. Trump to tighten export controls against China or accelerate weapons sales to Taiwan, according to Ali Wyne, a senior research and advocacy adviser focusing on U.S.-China relations at the International Crisis Group. That means that “Xi will have to work much harder to keep Trump on side,” Mr. Wyne said.

Mr. Trump will be the first American president to visit China twice in one calendar year.

Analysts say the leaders’ summits, which have included state visits in Beijing and then in Washington, have been the only thing holding together a fragile détente between the two countries as they clash over A.I., export controls, Taiwan and other issues.

The two sides declared a trade truce last year when they met in Busan, South Korea. That deal, which was to expire this month, has been extended until January.

China’s restrictions on the export of crucial rare-earth metals and rare-earth magnets, which are needed to manufacture most modern technology, remain one of the most difficult unresolved issues.

Mr. Trump is likely to raise this with Mr. Xi, according to a senior U.S. administration official who said that China’s continued restrictions is one reason Washington only agreed to a two-month extension of the trade truce.

Humana is continuing its push to reform prior authorization with the launch of a national provider gold card program.

The program will go into effect on Dec. 1, the company announced Tuesday. It will focus initially on MRI, CT and MRA imaging as well as physical and occupational therapy. Through the program, eligible providers will no longer need to secure a prior authorization approval and can instead use a streamlined process.

Humana estimates that about 50,000 providers will qualify for the program. Eligible providers must be able to demonstrate a proven track record of high-quality care and patient outcomes.

Traci Granston, M.D., vice president of clinical innovation and trend at Humana, told Fierce Healthcare in an exclusive interview that prior authorization and other utilization management approaches are key in ensuring that patients receive the appropriate care.

However, lengthy decision times and other barriers can create a painful friction. So for providers that consistently demonstrate that they are aligned with clinical guidelines, the gold cards can remove that guardrail.

"If they're following those guidelines and providing appropriate care, prior auths don't really serve a purpose for them," Granston said.

The insurer conducted a pilot earlier this year with about 400 provider organizations and approximately 8,000 clinicians. Through that process, the team conducted interviews with clinicians and office staff who shared their desire for fewer administrative burdens and a simpler experience.

Those insights shaped the broader launch, Humana said, and the insurer intends to continue to iterate and evolve the gold card program over time, including by adding new service lines. It's not uncommon for a program to roll out that aims to support providers, but their voice is less often at the table in shaping what the experience looks like to ensure that their pain points are being addressed.

Granston said that the team is examining more time-sensitive services that could fit within the program, for example.

"For our top-performing providers, we would like to essentially get out of the way and let them continue to care for our members," she said.

The gold card program builds on Humana's existing work to reform prior authorization, including a streamlined approval process for electronic submissions that took effect in January of this year. Under this initiative, Humana responds within one business day in 95% of electronic prior authorization cases.

The insurer also announced earlier this month that it would join forces with athenahealth to accelerate the use of electronic prior authorization. Through the partnership, more of the process is embedded directly in the provider's workflow, streamlining the experience for them at the point of care.

Granston said that the goal is to make it easier to surface the data necessary to make an approval and enable that to occur in real time. This also means that fewer people have to be dedicated to the work of submissions and approval.

"When the information exists, you don't really need all these people to be touching it," she said.

Cost and access to GLP-1s remains a challenge, but the benefits of these drugs aren't in doubt.

A new study from CVS Health aims to quantify at what price a GLP-1 drug has generated enough savings elsewhere through improved clinical outcomes. The healthcare giant used anonymized claims data from Aetna members in fully insured plans and found savings from reduced hospital and medical spending, as well as improved health outcomes.

But those benefits weren't enough to offset the increased pharmaceutical spending at the current price point, the study found.

The study tracked a mix of members who took GLP-1s from 2023 to 2025, so some patients had three years of follow-up care and management.

Amy Compton-Phillips, M.D., chief medical officer at CVS, told Fierce Healthcare that part of the challenge in this equation is that these drugs gained steam as a treatment for obesity at a high price. And while those costs have come down, the reductions haven't yet been enough to offset the expenditures.

Another challenge for employers in particular — where GLP-1 coverage has been most in flux — is turnover, she said. While GLP-1s do drive significant improvements, an employer may not reap the benefit of the reduced cost if an employee moves to a different company before those are realized, even though they paid for the medication initially.

The study sought to identify a price point where those savings would be felt and landed at $350 per month over a three-year period. Achieving that, Compton-Phillips said, would likely lure employers into embracing broader GLP-1 coverage.

"For them, it might be really worthwhile to say, 'Hey, at this price point, I can afford to cover this for my employees, knowing that in the near term the savings will pay for my investment,'" she said.

The study found obesity rates declining after a 2022 peak, as well as a reduction of between 34% and 42% in hospitalizations over three years. Patients also had 14% lower odds of a serious cardiac event.

For patients who remained on the drugs for three years, their total medical spending decreased by $9,400, but associated pharmacy spending increased in tandem by $13,300, the study found.

Clinical improvements also fell off when patients were not able to remain adherent to the medication over several years, highlighting the importance of a wraparound approach that supports broader care management rather than simply prescribing a patient a GLP-1.

Compton-Phillips said CVS' own weight management program brings nutrition, peer coaching and other supports into the fold, in addition to the prescription. This is especially critical, she said, as evidence suggests weight gain is common after a patient stops taking their GLP-1, making behavior change key.

"At the moment, there's not great evidence that if you come off the drugs, you will maintain the weight loss," she said.

The CVS researchers said that the ultimate goal of the study was not to demand that all GLP-1 prescriptions repay their own costs, but to ensure that drug costs align with patient benefits and the broader cost of access. By lowering the net price of these products, more people can access them, the study concludes.

"These drugs make people's lives better, and I want to give them out," Compton-Phillips said. Doing so becomes a challenge, though, "at the price they are today," she said.
