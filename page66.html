Opinion: Liverpool handed major transfer boost in chase for £45m sensation

📅 Nov 08, 2026  |  Category: Other  |  Read Source →

Liverpool were probably expecting their squad to be completed by now.

They've spent over £600 million on new players across the last couple of seasons and, if we're being honest, things still aren't great.

Multiple areas of of Andoni Iraola's squad have failed to meet the standards expected of them and have forced fans to question whether some of the players that their team have signed were even worth buying in the first place.

This thought process has led to different questions being asked about their first-team squad and has caused many players to be linked with a move.

And now, the Anfield outfit seem to have just been handed a major boost in their pursuit for one of their main targets.

Barcelona want to sign Levi Colwill which could open the door for a Ronald Araujo transfer

Ronald Araujo has arguably been one of Liverpool's best signings this season.

The Uruguayan was brought in on a season-long loan with the Reds having an option to buy worth €55 million (£45.9 million).

This may not seem like all that much money with how good the centre-back has been but, since he'll be 28 years old by the time Liverpool can activate their option to buy, it's unclear whether he'll be worth them signing or not.

Obviously, things such as his injury history also need to be taken into consideration.

But, luckily for Liverpool, a fresh update from Caught Offside has shared that Barcelona now want to sign Levi Colwill from Chelsea.

"Barcelona view Colwill as a top target after studying his performances and stats in great detail, there have been internal discussions about putting a bid together, possibly as early as this January," they wrote.

Levi Colwill could be Barcelona's replacement for Ronald Araujo

With how happy Araujo seems to be at Liverpool and how happy the Reds' fanbase seems to be with the Uruguayan, a permanent deal for him makes sense.

His ability to play as a right-back or a centre-back is massive with both of those areas major problems for Liverpool at the moment.

In fact, their whole defence could do with a re-jig.

Barca are likely to stick to their price of €55 million for Araujo but, if they manage to get a deal over the line for Colwill before then, they could become a little more lenient.

Araujo's experience is unmatched compared to some of the other players that Liverpool have been linked with.

But, only time will tell whether the Reds feel like a move for the 27-year-old is worth it.

The one Starlink perk that gives it an edge over traditional internet

📅 Nov 08, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Why satellite internet has a unique advantage over terrestrial providers.

It's debatable as to whether Starlink markets its services properly. Anyone who's seen some of the company's ads probably understands the basics, namely that, regardless of which Starlink setup you think best suits you, you're getting the internet via a satellite dish instead of a cable or phone line. Also, if you choose the right plan, like one of the wildly different $55 plans, you can get internet access virtually anywhere in the world. However, until you dig a little deeper, many details remain mysterious, such as important specs like expected speed.

One of those gaps is why you might want satellite internet when you're not roaming or otherwise isolated. In a country like the US, UK or Canada, it's rare to find a community that doesn't have at least some basic landline option. Given that Starlink can get quite expensive with its equipment costs, there has to be serious incentive to sign up.

For some consumers, there is, and it's an adaptability that traditional internet lacks. It's baked into the nature of Starlink, and it could save your bacon if you value resiliency over raw speed. There are some important exceptions, which we'll get into.

Starlink's network is based around clusters of small satellites inserted into low Earth orbit (LEO). As of October 2026, there were over 11,000 working satellites, flying at altitudes ranging from about 205 to 311 miles (330 to nearly 500 kilometers). SpaceX continues to launch more, whether to expand the network or replace malfunctioning units. Essentially, there should always be a cluster overhead at any given time.

The most obvious advantage of this is that whereas traditional internet connections can be knocked offline for hours by a single cable or server going down, it's rare for Starlink to suffer a similar fate. If one satellite fails, another in the same cluster should be able to pick up the slack. Even sightline obstacles aren't necessarily an issue, since the cluster approach means there's almost always a satellite with a viable angle as long as your dish is positioned properly.

As mentioned, this coverage is only slated to get better. SpaceX recently filed an application with the US Federal Communications Commission to operate up to 100,000 of its Gen3 satellites, following up on a recent approval to expand Gen2 numbers as high as 15,000. If you can afford to install and maintain a dish, your internet access could hypothetically become bulletproof, short of power outages or other rare disruptions.

On paper, Starlink dishes should be extremely resilient. Current-generation models can melt snow and endure temperatures from -40 to 110 degrees Fahrenheit (-40 to 43 degrees Celsius). Properly mounted, they can supposedly withstand heavy rain and winds in excess of 99 miles per hour (about 160 kilometers per hour).

In practice, things don't always work perfectly. Snow takes time to melt, and there are regular, brief disruptions while your dish switches from one satellite to another. The possibility of prolonged outages remains, whether because of extreme weather, some systemic problem on Starlink's end or issues with the rest of the internet. It hardly matters how many satellites are in the sky if a particular trunk of the internet is having trouble with its local ground-based infrastructure.

Some prospective users may want to actively avoid Starlink altogether. Apart from the many controversies involving founder Elon Musk, those momentary signal blips can be terrible for real-time activities like online gaming. In a shooter like Fortnite or Counter-Strike, a split second can mean the difference between landing a headshot or taking one.

You open your phone to check the time or a quick message. The next thing you know, an hour has passed and you’ve scrolled through endless celebrity drama, cat videos, awful news stories, influencer rants, and whatever else the algorithm decided to throw at you.

Even though you probably don’t want to keep wasting your time and energy on this mind-numbing content, you do it again the next day.

Doomscrolling, the habit of spending excessive amounts of time consuming content on social media, has become incredibly widespread. A survey from last year found that 64% of Americans say they doomscroll.

Researchers have warned that doomscrolling can negatively affect several aspects of your well-being, including your mental health and attention span. Spending long periods scrolling can lead to brain fatigue, difficulty focusing, and disrupted sleep. And if a lot of the content you’re consuming is negative or stressful, it can leave you feeling disheartened, anxious, and emotionally drained.

It’s hard to break the cycle of doomscrolling, but there are plenty of apps that provide content that’s engaging and productive.

Of course, you could always read a book or go for a walk (we have a guide on how to stop doomscrolling), but this list is for when you have a few spare minutes and want something to do on your phone that isn’t endless scrolling.

Finch is a self-care pet app that’s designed to help you build healthy habits. You care for a cute virtual pet by completing small daily goals, such as drinking enough water, going on a walk, and practicing mindfulness. You can write about your day, complete a breathing exercise, or send good vibes to a friend by adding them to the app.

The more self-care you do, the more your pet thrives. The app offers a fun way to trade mindless scrolling for small moments of self-care.

Finch is available for free on iOS and Android.

Libby is a free reading app that connects you to your local public library’s digital collection of ebooks, audiobooks, and magazines. All you need is a library card to borrow thousands of titles, which makes it easy to turn screen time into something productive.

You can explore a new book during a commute or while waiting at a doctor’s office, turning moments when your fingers might otherwise itch to scroll into opportunities to read.

Libby is available for free on iOS and Android.

If you want to take a break from doomscrolling and try something creative, Dudel Draw might be a good app for you. Each day, the app gives you a random shape that you turn into a drawing. These daily shapes vary from basic geometrical forms to more complex and abstract designs.

You can also get your friends in on the creativity with some fun competition by comparing your different creations, which could provide a nice break from sending each other endless TikToks or Instagram Reels.

If you still want to feel connected to the world without scrolling through social media, you can check out Radio Garden. The app lets you listen to over 25,000 live radio stations from across the globe.

Once you open the app, you’ll see green dots marking cities and towns. Tap any dot to listen to radio stations broadcasting from that location. You can add favorites or search for radio stations, countries, and places.

Radio Garden is free, but it also offers an ad-free premium plan for $2.99 per month. The app is available on both iOS and Android.

Elevate is an app designed to help improve focus, memory, reading, math skills, and other cognitive abilities. It includes over 40 games to train different abilities you use in everyday life, from reading faster to comparing prices more efficiently.

You can track training streaks and compare your mind’s performance over time.

The app offers a free version with access to three games per day, or a yearly subscription of $39.99 for unlimited access. It’s available on both iOS and Android.

Vocabulary is an app that helps you learn new words every day. You can pick your difficulty level and choose categories that interest you, like emotions, the human body, business, and more. Each word comes with a definition, example sentences, and a guide on how to pronounce it.

The app also includes mini-games to help you review the words you’ve learned. You can set a goal of how many words you want to learn each week and create a regular learning routine.

Vocabulary offers a free trial, and then costs $4.99 per month or $59.99 per year. It’s available on iOS and Android.

If you’re a geography nerd looking to test your knowledge or just want to improve your geography skills, Seterra is perfect for you. The app features over 300 different games to test your map skills. You can test your knowledge of world flags; discover oceans, seas, and rivers; and explore mountain ranges and volcanoes across the globe.

Seterra lets you track your progress across categories and see leaderboards for top scorers for each game.

The app is free and available on both iOS and Android.

The NYT Games app offers several word, logic, and number games that change every day to exercise your mind. You can play the crossword, try the word-guessing game Wordle, group words with a common theme in Connections, see how many words you can make from seven letters in Spelling Bee, and more.

The app costs $5.99 per month for unlimited access and archives, but some games like Wordle, Strands, and the Mini Crossword are available to play for free.

NYT Games is available on both iOS and Android.

If you want to learn a new language but want to try something other than Duolingo, Drops is a good option. The app uses visually engaging mini‑games to help you build vocabulary and common phrases in more than 45 languages, with bite‑sized lessons designed to be completed in about five minutes a day.

Drops is designed for both beginners and experts who want to grow their foreign language vocabulary.

The free version of the app offers five-minute lessons per day. You can get unlimited access and premium features for $11.99 per month or $79.99 per year. The app is available on both iOS and Android.

Lewis Hamilton says a lack of information led to him overriding Ferrari's call for him to come into the pits as rain fell during the closing stages of the Sao Paulo Grand Prix.

Hamilton was running in eighth at Interlagos when heavy rain began to fall with 10 laps to go. While the leaders had already passed the pit entry by the time the rain intensified, Mercedes brought in George Russell from fifth.

Ferrari instructed Hamilton to come in, but he decided to do the opposite to the car just in front of him at the time, Racing Bulls' Liam Lawson. Hamilton's team-mate Charles Leclerc, running just behind him, came into the pits.

The seven-time world champion spun on the next lap in the treacherous conditions, before pitting at the end of it to follow his rivals onto intermediates.

While there was little at stake, as Hamilton ultimately finished ninth rather than claiming what could have been seventh if he had got past Lawson, it was the latest example of imperfect execution from the British driver and his Italia team.

Speaking to Sky Italy after the race, Hamilton said: "The last bit of information I got was that the rain was going to stay the same, and in those conditions my grip was feeling pretty good.

"And I was like, 'ah, I'm catching.' And I was coming out the last corner, just before the pit lane they asked me to box, and I was right behind Lawson.

"He went in, and I ended up staying (out). They didn't say it's raining more in the first half of the lap. If I'd have known that I would have come in.

"As soon as I got to Turn 1, I realised it was wet. So, it just lacked a little bit of information there.

"And ultimately, that's on me. Sorry for the team, for that. I gave it everything. We just didn't have the pace today unfortunately."

Hamilton had come into the weekend off the back of a major controversy with his team-mate in Mexico last weekend, as he was angered by a first-lap clash between the pair that he felt cost him victory and the team a one-two.

While Hamilton revealed on Saturday that the pair had "squashed" their issues, the precious nature of their winning opportunity in Mexico City was highlighted by their struggles in Brazil.

Hamilton told Sky Sports F1: "Really tough day and weekend for the whole team. They have done a really great job.

"All the mechanics did such a great job switching out the engine and making sure the car was as good as it could be."

Hamilton had a great start as he climbed to ninth on the first lap, and successfully pulled off being the first driver to switch from intermediates to slicks as the track dried early in the race, but didn't have the pace to take advantage of either moment.

“It is a process, it’s a good example when you concede, it’s (about) adaptation to the Premier League for Zion,” he added.

“They are pushing, they are disturbing the goalkeeper. In some countries, in other leagues, they are not permitting it.

“Here yes, and we have to continue analysing, having experiences and trying to avoid these situations.”
