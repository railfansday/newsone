Kyndryl announced on November 9, 2026 an expanded ServiceNow partnership centered on Kyndryl Autonomous Operations for ServiceNow, a co-developed offering available through the ServiceNow Store. Powered by Kyndryl Bridge, it applies operational data and AI agents to incident analysis, risk assessment and remediation.

Kyndryl says the offering is intended to improve productivity, reduce risk and produce better business outcomes through predictive insights and automated remediation. Giovanni Carraro, the company’s global strategic alliances leader, said the focus is helping customers “move beyond experimentation, put AI to work and become trusted autonomous enterprises.”

Under the design, ServiceNow customers run operational data through Bridge to supply context across assets, configurations, dependencies, performance, events, changes, incidents, risks and resolutions. Specialized agents use that context to identify issues from failure patterns, generate evidence-based causal analysis, assess risk and provide step-by-step remediation guidance.

Insights from each resolved incident return to the platform, creating a feedback loop for subsequent automation and recommendations. The approach connects incident resolution to a broader operational memory, allowing handled issues to inform later analysis rather than treating each resolution as an isolated event.

The ServiceNow Store listing identifies dashboards for operational insights, IT health indicators, incident root-cause analysis, incident-resolution playbook generation and change-risk scoring. It also lists feedback on operational insights and server-based communication with Bridge through the Model Context Protocol.

Kyndryl reports that the broader Bridge platform, launched in 2022, is used by more than 1,400 enterprises, generates more than 16 million AI insights monthly and reduces IT incidents by up to 50%. Its operational experience is captured in more than 10,000 predefined best practices, runbooks, remediation practices and engineering actions.

Bridge unifies asset inventory, asset-performance information and engineering records of successful resolutions. Its agentic memory management is designed to preserve institutional knowledge and operational context, supporting continuous modernization.

Kyndryl describes Bridge as an agentic control plane, a layer that governs how AI agents operate across an enterprise’s technology estate. It applies enterprise policy as code at the point of action, maintains an auditable record of what each agent did and why, and requires human approval when risk warrants it. Its services catalog contains more than 190 services.

Michael Park, ServiceNow’s senior vice president of global partnerships and channels, said his company contributes the AI Control Tower and governance for safe operation, while Kyndryl contributes operational data and engineering depth, producing proven patterns customers can apply in their own environments.

The Store record adds an important timing distinction: version 26.10.16 is dated November 5, 2026 and labeled as the first ServiceNow Store release. The November 9 announcement therefore followed the initial Store release; it documents the expanded partnership and the offering’s availability rather than the application’s first appearance.

The listing classifies Kyndryl Autonomous Operations as a paid, certified generative-AI application sold by Kyndryl Inc., compatible with ServiceNow Australia Patch 7 and Zurich Patch 13. Prerequisites include ITSM Prime, ITOM Advanced with Discovery and Event Management, Now Assist components such as AI Skill Kit and Generative AI Controller, and Model Context Protocol client and server components.

ServiceNow’s licensing terms add that AI features in partner-built apps may consume Assists deducted from the customer’s account, and that partners must provide an estimate of how many Assists their apps may consume.

For customers, the expansion connects operational context, AI-generated analysis and governed remediation within ServiceNow’s IT workflows. Bridge contributes data, resolution practices, auditability and approval controls, while ServiceNow contributes the AI workflows and platform. That makes implementation and governance central to adoption; the operational case rests on whether the incident feedback loop can turn Bridge’s accumulated knowledge into consistently better remediation.

Amundi Boosts Strategy (MSTR) Stake 44% to $291 Million as Shares Slide

📅 Nov 09, 2026  |  Category: Business  |  Read Source →

Amundi, Europe’s largest asset manager, raised its stake in Strategy (MSTR) by about 44% in the third quarter, a Form 13F filed Nov. 6 shows, lifting the position to 1,903,725 shares worth $291.4 million at Sept. 30 prices.

That is up 579,672 shares from the 1,324,053 Amundi reported at June 30, when the same holding was valued at about $115.1 million. The Paris-based firm managed €2.579 trillion in assets at the end of September, up 11% year over year, and described itself as Europe’s leading asset manager.

The filing lands as MSTR has given back much of its third-quarter run. Strategy shares closed at $153.09 on Sept. 30 and near $126 on Monday, an 18% drop that would put Amundi’s disclosed stake near $239 million at the latest close, Yahoo Finance data show.

A 13F is a snapshot, not a trading diary. Amundi filed a combination report that folds in 17 other managers across its group, so the Strategy line can include client, fund and discretionary positions rather than a single proprietary bet, and the firm may have changed the holding since Sept. 30.

Strategy itself kept stacking. The company bought another 1,036 bitcoin last week for $81.9 million at an average of $79,012, a Monday 8-K filing and company release show, lifting its treasury to 850,364 BTC acquired for $64.16 billion at an average of $75,456.

That average cost now sits above the spot bitcoin price, leaving the stack underwater on paper even after the firm added 811 bitcoin the prior week. Strategy’s public ledger still ranks it as the largest corporate bitcoin holder, with the stack equal to roughly 4% of bitcoin’s 21 million supply cap.

MSTR roughly doubled during the third quarter itself. The June 30 13F valued Amundi’s then-smaller stake at an implied $86.93 a share, against the $153.09 Sept. 30 close, so the dollar value of the position rose faster than the share count. Since then the stock has tracked bitcoin’s slide into the mid-$70,000s, wiping out a large share of that mark-to-market gain for anyone still holding the Sept. 30 position.

The timing matters because Strategy funds most of its bitcoin buys by selling new MSTR common under an at-the-market program. A weaker share price raises more dilution for every coin purchased, which is the feedback loop that has defined the stock’s moves this year.

Amundi was not the only large European name adding bitcoin exposure on the quarter. Barclays’ own 13F, also filed Nov. 6, shows 3,942,900 shares of BlackRock’s iShares Bitcoin Trust (IBIT) worth $186.7 million at Sept. 30, up 48% from 2,658,147 shares three months earlier, SEC records show.

That Barclays line carries the same caveat: a 13F gathers client, managed and trading book positions, not proof the bank put its own capital into bitcoin. Still, the two filings together sketch the same picture of Q3: traditional European managers kept adding exposure to bitcoin proxies even as the spot market cooled into the fall.

The institutional adds sit against a weaker tape since quarter-end. Bitcoin has slipped into the mid-$70,000s, Strategy stock has followed it down, and the value of Amundi’s disclosed MSTR stake has compressed even as the share count on the filing stayed fixed. At Monday’s close the disclosed stake was about $52 million below its Sept. 30 mark.

Whether Amundi kept buying, trimmed, or simply held through October’s slide will not show up until the next 13F. For now the Nov. 6 filing is the record Europe’s biggest asset manager left: nearly 1.9 million Strategy shares, up 44%, at a $291 million quarter-end mark.

NASA’s Voyager 1 to Cross Historic One Light-Day Milestone on Nov. 18

📅 Nov 09, 2026  |  Category: Entertainment  |  Read Source →

Traveling one light-day took the space probe 49 years. it would take a human 612,015 years to walk that distance.

NASA’s Voyager 1 spacecraft is on track to set another historic record as its mission approaches the half-century mark. On Nov. 18 at 5:16 a.m. ET, the probe will reach a distance of one light-day from Earth, a journey of nearly five decades that places it farther from our planet than any human-made object in history.

It’s quite an accomplishment for a spacecraft built in the mid-1970s One light-day is the distance that light travels in a single day at the speed of light, which is approximately 186,282 miles per second. That translates to 25.9 billion kilometers or about 16.1 billion miles in a single day. At an average human walking speed of 3 mph, it would take a human 612,015 years to walk that distance.

Fortunately, Voyager 1 has been traveling a bit faster than that. It currently zips along the cosmos at a steady 37,763 mph, a speed it achieved in 1980 after a gravity-assisted Saturn flyby. It has been flying at that speed ever since.

The milestone comes 14 years after its previous one, when Voyager 1 crossed the heliopause, the point where the sun’s heliosphere ends, and entered interstellar space. It’s also 28 years after passing Pioneer 10 to become the human-made object that has traveled the farthest into space.

The historic milestone isn’t without its drama. Voyager 1 has been in space for nearly half a century, and in that time, NASA’s Jet Propulsion Laboratory has done everything it can to extend the life of Voyager 1. That includes troubleshooting issues, performing maintenance and shutting down various systems to save power and extend the spacecraft’s life-span.

Several of those efforts took place in 2026. The team running Voyager 1 spent weeks shutting down three devices and powering on three low-power alternatives to reduce overall power usage. This initiative, which was internally called The Big Bang, was also performed on Voyager 2. The transition was completed in early September, giving Voyager 1 more time with its dwindling power supply while also keeping it warm enough to continue operating in the frigid conditions of interstellar space.

It was a tricky maneuver that required the team to simultaneously turn multiple instruments on and off. The difficulty of the maneuver was amplified by the fact that it took almost a full day for any signal to reach Voyager and another full day for Voyager to send a signal back, with hours of downtime while the spacecraft performed the operations.

All of this took place several months after Voyager 1 experienced a sudden drop in power, which necessitated shutting off one of its three remaining instruments still in operation. Voyager 1 now has only two instruments still in operation.

Making it a whole light-day away from Earth is likely going to be Voyager 1’s final milestone while it still has enough power to communicate with Earth. Voyager 1 is powered by three radioisotope thermoelectric generators. They are essentially nuclear-powered batteries that generate energy by converting the steady heat from decaying plutonium-238.

When Voyager 1 first launched, its radioisotope thermoelectric generators produced about 470 watts of power. But plutonium-238 doesn’t last forever, and after 50 years in space, the RTGs now produce about 215 watts of power, with an average loss of about 4 watts per year. Most of that power is used to keep Voyager 1 up and running, which is why NASA has shut down eight of the 10 instruments that Voyager had when it launched.

At its current rate of usage and decay, Voyager 1 is expected to shut down its final science instrument in the 2030s and will eventually lose communication with Earth in 2036 due to insufficient power to send back a radio signal.

The same fate is expected for Voyager 2, Voyager 1’s twin, which has also spent half a century in space. While it is labeled the sequel, Voyager 2 actually launched 16 days before Voyager 1, and the two have spent nearly identical amounts of time traversing the cosmos. Thanks to its slower speed and trajectory, it likely won’t reach the one light-day distance until after its instruments shut down.

Thanks to the vastness of space, Voyager 1 will lose all of its power before it hits its next milestone, which is the beginning of the Oort Cloud, a vast, icy shell of comets at the edge of our solar system. It’ll take about 300 years for Voyager 1 to make it there.

Thanks to the near-perfect vacuum of outer space, Voyager 1 will continue to fly at its current speed even after it shuts down entirely. So it’ll still hit that milestone; it just won’t be able to phone home to tell us about it.

HealthTap is rolling out a new program for Medicare Advantage plans that seeks to help members form a connection with a primary care doctor that carries forward.

The program leans on a combination of proactive outreach and access to a patient's historical medical records to support plans in identifying needs or risks, filling care gaps and coordinating follow-up or other necessary care. It's built to directly support individuals who may not have a PCP on file.

HealthTap reaches out to those members proactively, the company said, and helps them schedule an initial virtual visit with a provider who stays connected for preventive services, chronic condition management and medication concerns.

"The angle that we're taking is: wouldn't it be cool if, in fact, your virtual primary care physician was just like a bricks and mortar primary care physician?" said Rick Cochran, senior vice president of health plans at HealthTap, in an interview with Fierce Healthcare.

In an ideal scenario, a patient who has a consistent primary care physician who they see over the course of multiple years and who they come to trust, he said. HealthTap is looking to build that experience with virtual primary care docs, and reach patients who are disconnected from the health system with critical services.

Cochran said that the team, at times, receives feedback about concern that a program like this could upset in-network practices. However, he said that HealthTap views these service lines as complementary, and the company as offering another way for insurers to reach members who are underserved and in need of help.

"There's a bunch of people who haven't been receiving care that we can work with," he said. "We're not attempting to disrupt anything. We're attempting to complement things."

Through the program, HealthTap can connect with members via text messaging, email, direct mail or phone, at the plan's discretion. The member can then schedule the appointment for themselves and choose a board-certified primary care physician based in their state.

Between video visits, the member can also connect with their physician through secure messaging, HealthTap said.

In aiming to make the experience feel as much like traditional, brick-and-mortar clinics as possible, Cochran said that members are allowed to switch their physicians and find the one that best matches their individual health needs.

And to better support the physicians, HealthTap pulls from health information networks to pull key medical records, including relevant data from before an individual enrolled in their current plan. That set-up also means that the patient can carry their relationship with a HealthTap doc forward should they switch plans, Cochran said.

Cochran said that COVID-19 really kicked down the door to virtual care for seniors, and there's significant opportunity in MA for a program like HealthTap's, as these patients often have multiple comorbidities and may face barriers to seeing a doctor in person, such as the lack of transportation.

The company did an initial deployment with one MA plan and found that 35% of the initial outreach cohort scheduled an appointment, and 19% completed their first visit within six weeks. Satisfaction scores for both patients and physicians are high, Cochran said.

The approach gives doctors more time to spend working "to make people better, not crank through visits," he said.

"Our physician satisfaction score is very high because our physicians have the time to spend with the patient to ensure that the patient's needs are met," he said.

Warren intends to use the committee’s investigative powers for oversight of prediction markets and election betting platforms, aimed at insider trading and platform manipulation — the same fight she opened before the midterms.

That earlier push was bipartisan. On Oct. 28, Warren and Sen. John Curtis, R-Utah, wrote to Kalshi CEO Tarek Mansour and Polymarket CEO Shayne Coplan asking how the companies stop candidates, campaign staff and others with material nonpublic information from trading election contracts.

“We are particularly troubled by election-related event contracts,” the senators wrote. “Event contracts tied to elections receive prominent coverage and are treated by some observers as indicators of political momentum. There are also documented cases of candidates betting on their own races, which undermines the overall integrity of the American electoral process.”

The letter cited candidates who traded on their own contests on Kalshi, including a February 2026 California gubernatorial race, and pointed to research alleging wash trading and volume manipulation on Polymarket. It also referenced an NPR investigation of campaign insiders who said they traded ahead of polling releases.

“The identification of isolated instances of improper trading does not demonstrate that these markets are adequately protected,” Warren and Curtis wrote. “Instead, it raises serious questions about how much similar activity may be going undetected.” They asked for written answers by Election Day, Nov. 3.

Kalshi’s head of enforcement, Robert DeNault, told Business Insider that the company “proactively blocks candidates and campaign staff from trading to protect market integrity.” Polymarket did not respond to that request for comment.

Both sit in the orbit of the same committee Warren is about to run.

Under Chairman Michael S. Selig, the CFTC has spent 2026 defending those platforms against state gambling laws, suing Wisconsin, New York and others to assert exclusive federal jurisdiction over event contracts on registered exchanges. A Democratic Banking chair targeting what her aide called a Wild West lands as the counterweight to that stance.

Warren has been one of crypto’s sharpest critics on the Hill. She opposed the CLARITY Act market structure bill, which failed a Senate cloture vote 49-50 on Sept. 15, and analysts have argued a Democratic Congress leaves little path for a Republican rewrite. House Democrats are already sketching their own package with a bigger SEC role.

The aide’s broader agenda for Warren also includes affordability legislation, a push to cap credit card interest rates, AI chip export controls, and oversight of Trump administration officials, Wall Street CEOs, Big Tech executives and anyone tied to the Trump family’s crypto ventures.

The test for prediction markets is no longer whether Trump-era regulators will keep clearing the way. It is whether Warren, with subpoena power and the Banking gavel, turns the Kalshi and Polymarket letter into hearings that rewrite how election and event contracts are policed.

Boston Dynamics Turns Atlas Into a Wheeled Actuator Testbed

📅 Nov 09, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Boston Dynamics detailed a wheeled Atlas experiment on November 9, 2026, after replacing the humanoid’s feet with wheels to probe actuator limits and the transfer of whole-body reinforcement learning from simulation to hardware.

The company describes the configuration as a research testbed, not a product. The experiment was designed to expose how real actuators and a learned whole-body controller behave together when the motors approach their operating limits. Accelerating, turning and stopping keep the motors under load while demanding high torque and high speed across their operating range.

Boston Dynamics said years of Spot and Stretch customer experience have shown that deployed robots encounter conditions a lab cannot fully reproduce. Atlas controls engineer Xiaoyu (Andy) Wu, who wrote the account, used the testbed to examine extreme actuation behavior and the robustness of his low-level control tuning.

Training used millions of robot simulations before the policy reached hardware. The simulation-to-hardware problem, called sim-to-real, depended on making the modeled motor reproduce measured physical behavior closely enough for the learned policy to transfer. Policies trained against an idealized motor may rehearse movements the real robot cannot perform; hardware-matched models instead support behavior the robot can reproduce.

Preparation involved matching low-level control parameters across software setups, running motor-characterization experiments and tuning the simulated motor against measurements from Atlas. The team also evaluated simplifications individually, retaining harmless approximations while avoiding those that would give the policy an unrealistic advantage during training.

The project joined actuator design, low-level motor control and whole-body reinforcement learning (RL), which are usually separate specialties on Atlas. Wu’s background is in classical motor control, and this was his first substantial RL project, placing him at the boundaries among those layers.

The researchers adapted established policy frameworks, changing the reward composition, the range of training commands and posture handling. Wu said those changes produced smooth rolling while Atlas remained stable and easy to drive, and characterized the resulting transfer to hardware as essentially perfect.

Some demonstrations began with human motion-capture clips that Wu selected for substantial foot sliding. The side-dash training converted those human foot slides into steered, rolling movements as the policy learned to use the wheels.

For the 360-degree jump, the controller tracked a motion trajectory recorded from a human jumper. Wu characterized the move as relatively low-risk despite its energetic appearance and said it succeeded on the first attempt. As the robot prepared for takeoff, its wheeled lower body rolled quickly into position before the upward push, showing a different use of traction from Atlas on feet.

The hardware behind those movements required little alteration. The only added component was a custom adapter joining an off-the-shelf scooter rim to Atlas’s existing ankle actuator. In the baseline software model, each wheel became an ankle capable of continuous rotation, allowing Wu to substitute wheels for feet without constructing a separate robot model.

Boston Dynamics said lessons from the experiment are already being adapted to Atlas’s standard configuration. Closer integration between actuator dynamics and whole-body RL is intended to improve policy robustness against failure modes and expand the robot’s capabilities. The demonstrations therefore connect an unusual research configuration directly to the control system used by the conventional bipedal machine.

Wu has also started work on policies for failed actuators, including whether reinforcement learning can generate emergent responses to those failures. The follow-on extends that reliability work to hardware whose behavior changes after a failure, testing whether learned control can respond when a real actuator departs from its expected behavior.

Cisco announced on November 9, 2026 that it will add Anthropic’s Claude Mythos to AI Defense’s adaptive red-teaming framework for testing AI applications and agents. The announcement at Cisco Live APJC in Melbourne also introduced Splunk agent-observability updates and Cisco IQ integrations, extending the release beyond testing into agent-cost evaluation and infrastructure remediation.

Customers will not be able to prompt Claude Mythos freely. Instead, the model will operate within a fixed job with a registered target and human-defined objectives, supplying reasoning for test planning and attack ideation while AI Defense conducts the engagement.

A Cisco technical overview distinguishes standard and adaptive testing. Standard red teaming checks common risks such as prompt injection, jailbreaks, sensitive-data exposure and harmful content across thousands of categories, content types and adversarial techniques. Adaptive testing begins with customer criteria written in plain language, analyzes the target and develops tailored attacks, then uses the responses to refine its approach or change tactics before reporting findings and recommending follow-up actions.

The safeguards constrain where that reasoning can be used. Claude Mythos will operate only against targets explicitly registered, owned and authorized for testing by the enterprise customer, and Cisco says it cannot scan arbitrary internet infrastructure. Every engagement requires authentication and is cryptographically tied to a named operator. Sessions are time-bound and can be halted, while each action is recorded for audit and results can inform repeatable follow-up attack loops.

Standard and adaptive red teaming are already available in AI Defense, but the Claude Mythos version is coming soon for select enterprises. Broader availability is planned for qualified customers without a committed date, and its timing, scope and regional availability may change. Cisco reports that AI Defense has helped hundreds of customers identify flaws in models, applications and agents before production since its January 2025 launch.

The Splunk Agent Observability updates address the operating side of agent deployment. Enhanced Tokenomics will use Amazon Bedrock and Amazon CloudWatch usage data to pair each agent’s cost with evaluations of the quality and impact of its work. Those technical metrics can be combined with customer-defined measures such as Net Promoter Score or cost per claim, extending existing token-spend attribution across AI agents and coding tools including Claude Code, Codex and Cursor.

Eval-G1 uses custom small language models to run evaluations and guardrails. Cisco reports that the models can do so at up to 98% lower cost than a commercial LLM and deliver evaluation responses 2.5 times faster than traditional LLM judges. The evaluation tuner turns agentic traces and subject-matter-expert feedback into fine-tuned evaluators that enforce quality standards and steer or block inaccurate agent behavior.

Availability differs across the observability features. Eval-G1 and the evaluation tuner are generally available starting November 2026. Tokenomics is scheduled for private preview in December 2026, followed by general availability in AWS Marketplace in early 2027.

Cisco reports that IQ serves close to 17,000 customers across more than 22 million connected devices, providing asset, coverage and advisory context. The Cisco IQ remediation workflow sends vulnerability, lifecycle and configuration data through Cisco Data Fabric into Splunk Platform via open APIs. Security teams and AI agents can then correlate live detections in Splunk Enterprise Security with underlying network exposure.

Model Context Protocol and ServiceNow connectors add structured infrastructure context to supported agents and IT-service workflows. Exposure findings can be converted into asset-linked ServiceNow problem records without manual data reconciliation. Cisco cautions that finding an exposure does not establish that an attacker exploited it: the workflow is intended to add investigation context and coordinate remediation, not treat each finding as a confirmed breach.

The AI-Powered Resilient Infrastructure Playbook also surfaces critical lifecycle vulnerabilities, personalized alerts for known defects and quantum-safe cryptographic readiness so teams and certified partners can prioritize modernization. Across the package, attack generation stays within authorized jobs, evaluation models can apply guardrails, and exposure data informs investigations without establishing compromise by itself. For now, the evaluation models and tuner are generally available, while Mythos-enabled red teaming and Tokenomics are not, so the end-to-end workflow Cisco describes still depends on future or limited releases.

"An artificially depressed rating does not merely place Elevance Health at a competitive disadvantage; it undermines the very comparison the program is supposed to enable," the company continued. "A rating lowered by unlawful measures presents beneficiaries with a misleading signal of relative quality, rather than a lawful basis for distinguishing among competing plans."

CVS Heath's Aetna is joining the ranks of insurers suing over its results in the 2027 Medicare Advantage star ratings.

The insured filed suit Monday in Georgia's Southern District Court, and argues that 28 measures used by the Centers for Medicare & Medicaid Services to calculate its scores were "unlawfully adopted" and should have been excluded.

The court is the same venue that ruled in favor of Clover Health over the summer, and set aside 20 measures that were used to determine its 2026 scores. That decision, which the feds have appealed, kicked off a broad recalculation of 2026 scores that looked different for insurers outside of Clover, triggering a wave of litigation.

In its lawsuit, Aetna cites the Clover decision in finding that CMS extended beyond its statutory authority in deploying those 20 measures.

The company said that calculating its scores using the 28 measures in question will likely cost it $2 billion in 2028 quality bonus payments and rebate retention. Those are funds that the insurer could "apply toward expanding member benefits, reducing premiums and investing in quality improvement."

Aetna adds that in addition to the monetary loss, having lower scores negatively impacts the company's reputation and its ability to attract potential members.

"The harm to CVS Health is not exclusively financial; lower ratings carry meaningful reputational harm, which can in turn impact membership," per the lawsuit. "By incorporating unlawful measures into the 2027 Star Ratings calculation, CMS cast CVS Health’s ratings in a poorer light in the eyes of Medicare Advantage consumers relative to peer MAOs, undermining CVS Health’s ability to compete for Medicare beneficiaries through enhanced benefits and the like."

Aetna did not respond to a request for comment at the time of publication.

Based on an analysis from Stephens, 69% of Aetna's MA members are in plans with four or more stars for 2027, down from 81%. That threshold is the key to securing quality bonus payments.

Aetna's complaint follows others focused on the 2027 scores from Centene and Alignment Healthcare (see those stories below).

Centene has become the latest insurer to sue the federal government over its 2027 Medicare Advantage star ratings.

In a lawsuit filed Thursday in Georgia's Southern District Court, the company argues that the Centers for Medicare & Medicaid Services violated the Administrative Procedure Act in determining its latest scores. Centene says that CMS ran afoul of "core statutory safeguards" in deploying 17 measures.

The use of those measures ultimately caused "significant injury" to the company, according to the complaint. For 2027, 9% of Centene's members are enrolled in plans with at least four stars, which declined from 18% in the prior year.

"In implementing each of the individual Star measures that Centene challenges, CMS violated applicable law and thereby caused Centene to lose out on hundreds of millions of dollars in revenue that Centene could use to strengthen benefits, reduce premiums, and improve health outcomes," the company said in the lawsuit.

CMS argues that the court has already sided with a similar argument in its decision in favor of Clover Health earlier this year. In that case, which led to a broad recalculation in scores, the courts set aside 20 measures that were used in determining Clover's star ratings for 2026.

If the 17 measures in question had been excluded in calculating Centene's scores, the company estimates it would receive $448 million more in quality bonus payments for the 2028 plan year, funds it would deploy to expand benefits, lower premiums and enhance programs like care coordination.

In addition to the financial impact, a drop in star ratings also carries a reputational hit, Centene said.

"Beyond the financial impact to the company and its members, depressed ratings also inflict significant reputational damage and diminished membership," the company said in the complaint

Centene had not returned a request for comment at the time of publication.

Alignment Healthcare sued federal regulators, alleging that its 2027 Medicare Advantage star ratings were determined "in flagrant violation" of statute.

In the lawsuit filed Friday in District of Columbia federal court, the insurer says that the Centers for Medicare & Medicaid Services "has completely departed from Congress’s vision of leveraging predictable, valid measures developed with input from providers, plans, and the public" in establishing the methodology for the star ratings.

The company argues that rather than establish "consistent, stable specifications" for metrics through the traditional rule-making process, the agency instead detailed key measures through a series of "technical notes." This violates the Administrative Procedure Act, per the lawsuit.

The program's star ratings are the critical determinant in quality bonus payments to insurers as well as a tool that aims to make it easier for enrollees and brokers to identify benefits with the highest quality.

"Consistent application of the statutory requirements governing Star Ratings is therefore essential not only to plan payments and competition but also to ensuring that beneficiaries can rely on the Star Ratings as meaningful and comparable measures of plan quality," Alignment said in the lawsuit.

The company adds that multiple recent court decisions have set aside CMS' star ratings methodology, notably a ruling over the summer in favor of Clover Health that tossed 20 metrics used in the 2026 calculations. A Georgia federal judge determined that 10 measures utilized exceeded CMS' authority, while 10 other measures were promulgated inappropriately.

CMS recalculated the scores across the board following the ruling, but only set aside the 10 measures that the courts determined were outside the bounds of its regulatory authority. The agency maintained that approach in the 2027 calculations, Alignment argues.

"Congress created the Star Ratings program to allow Medicare Advantage beneficiaries to make informed decisions about plan enrollment based on clinically validated measures of plan quality that had been subjected to notice and comment," the company said. "CMS’s contrary approach fails to carry out the vision that Congress set forth by statute."

The Trump administration has appealed the Clover decision, so that legal case remains ongoing.

In addition, Alignment argues it suffered reputational and financial damage as a result of its 2027 scores, which plummeted compared to the prior year. Just 25% of its members are in plans with four or more stars for 2027, down from 100% in 2026.

Shares in the company tumbled following the release of the ratings, though stock prices have rebounded since.

Alignment sued the feds following the Clover decision to secure a recalculation for its scores that aligns with the court's decision, and in a statement to Fierce Healthcare, said that the lawsuit aligns with that perspective.

"Our lawsuit seeks to ensure that the Star Ratings program operates as Congress intended and that Alignment is properly compensated for the care we provide," Alignment said in the statement. "We respect the judicial process and will present our case in court. Because this matter involves pending litigation, we will not comment on the specific legal arguments beyond our filings."

"Our focus remains on delivering exceptional care, improving health outcomes and advocating for a stable, lawful regulatory framework that supports seniors and strengthens the Medicare Advantage program," the company said.

Infant Bleeding Cases Surge as More Parents Refuse Vitamin K Shots for Newborns

📅 Nov 09, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Follow Healthline on our socials for all things health.

Two large studies published on November 5 link declining newborn vitamin K shot rates to a sharp increase in infant bleeding cases, including potentially fatal brain hemorrhages.

A research letter, published in JAMA Pediatrics, analyzed data on approximately 11 million infants from 2017 to 2025 and found that vitamin K administration declined from 97.7% to 95%, while cases of vitamin K deficiency bleeding (VKDB) nearly quadrupled.

A second study published in JAMA, based on data from 5.2 million U.S. infants, found that refusal rates more than doubled, rising from 3.1% in 2017 to 6.8% in 2025. Infants who did not receive the shot had a 47% higher risk of bleeding and more than twice the risk of intracranial bleeding.

Additionally, new data from Truveta suggest the trend is continuing: refusals of vitamin K shots increased by 57% between January and June 2026, rising from 5.2% to 8.1% of newborns.

Vitamin K is essential for blood clotting, and newborns naturally have very low levels. Without the preventive injection, infants are at risk for VKDB, which can cause sudden bleeding in the brain, gastrointestinal tract, and other organs. Late-onset VKDB can occur up to six months after birth, often without warning signs, and may be fatal.

The Centers for Disease Control and Prevention (CDC) and American Academy of Pediatrics (AAP) continue to recommend an intramuscular vitamin K injection for all newborns, ideally within 6 hours of birth.

The shot is not a vaccine; it is a nutrient injection that helps prevent dangerous bleeding. A single dose reduces the risk of late-onset VKDB by approximately 98%.

Experts not involved in the studies attribute the rise in refusals partly to broader vaccine hesitancy and mixed public health messaging.

“The decision to vaccinate a newborn or allow a vitamin K injection at birth is complex, with multiple factors playing a role in the ultimate decision,” said Graham Tse, MD, a pediatrician and chief medical officer of MemorialCare Miller Children’s & Women’s Hospital in Long Beach, CA.

Some of these factors, Tse said, include conflicting messages from federal health agencies such as the Department of Health and Human Services (HHS) and the Centers for Disease Control and Prevention (CDC), both overseen by HHS Secretary Robert F. Kennedy, Jr., an outspoken critic of the childhood immunization schedule.

The vitamin K shot is not a vaccine, yet RFK Jr. has declined to reassure parents about its safety, despite the CDC’s current recommendation to administer it at birth.

Additionally, growing medical distrust among parents has led to refusal of routine newborn care, such as the vitamin K shot, Reuters reports.

Healthline spoke with Tse to learn more about the risks of refusing the vitamin K shot and what new or expectant parents should know about this routine newborn treatment.

This interview has been lightly edited and condensed for clarity and length.

Tse: The decision to vaccinate a newborn or allow a vitamin K injection at birth is complex, with multiple factors playing a role in the ultimate decision.

A contributing factor to the decline in vitamin K injection at birth, as well as in newborn hepatitis B vaccination, is the conflicting messages from the Department of Health and Human Services (HHS), and the Centers for Disease Control and Prevention (CDC).

Recommendations from these entities and others are conflicting or do not align with established, research-based, and proven recommendations from organizations such as the American Academy of Pediatrics (AAP) and the Infectious Diseases Society of America.

When there are conflicting recommendations, it creates confusion and doubt among parents about what the best or right choice is for their child.

As well, there is so much misinformation on the internet, among influencers, and on social media that it is difficult to know what information is accurate, well-researched, and proven, versus what may be just someone’s opinion or belief.

Some are mistaking the vitamin K injection at birth for a vaccination, which it is not. Ultimately, all these factors are contributing to parents declining or delaying vitamin K injections at birth.

Tse: Declining the newborn vitamin K injection puts the newborn at significant risk of serious and life threatening complications from vitamin K-deficient bleeding (VKDB). This was previously referred to as hemorrhagic disease of the newborn.

VKDB can be as mild as bruising of the skin but can be as serious as bleeding into the brain, and newborns and infants can die from VKDB.

Declining vitamin K injection at birth is significant because of the serious and potentially life threatening consequences of deficiency of Vitamin K and not being able to clot the blood.

The vitamin K injection at birth is highly effective at preventing VKDB.

Tse: If a neonate or infant has vitamin K deficiency, the ability to clot their blood and stop bleeding is highly reduced.

All newborns are at risk of low vitamin K due to low body stores, low vitamin K content of breast milk, reduced ability of the gut to mobilize vitamin K, and poor transfer of vitamin K across the placenta.

When the blood cannot clot, bleeding from minor or minimal trauma can occur, and even bleeding without injury or trauma may occur. This can include bruises, bleeding from the mouth or gastrointestinal tract, bleeding from cuts or circumcision.

Bleeding can occur anywhere in the body, including in the brain, which can be catastrophic.

VKDB can occur from birth and through several months of life. Bleeding in the brain from VKDB, one of the most serious consequences, can occur at any time from birth to up to 6 months or so and can occur unexpectedly and suddenly. Vitamin K given at birth can prevent this risk of bleeding.

Tse: Vitamin K is a necessary vitamin that all humans need. Vitamin K is not a vaccination.

Newborns need vitamin K to prevent serious, significant, and often catastrophic bleeding during their first several months of life.

Vitamin K has been given to newborns in the United States for close to 70 years, is safe, is studied, and is proven to help prevent vitamin K-deficient bleeding.

I urge new parents to allow their newborns to receive the vitamin K injection at birth — it protects them, keeps them healthy, is proven safe, and is recommended.

Plant-Based Milk Alternatives May Not Give You Enough Iodine. Here’s Why That Matters

📅 Nov 09, 2026  |  Category: Health  |  Read Source →

Follow Healthline on our socials for all things health.

Replacing dairy with plant-based alternatives (PBAs), even partially, could substantially lower iodine intake, especially if those alternatives aren’t fortified, according to a recent study published in the Lancet Planetary Health.

The United Kingdom-based researchers found that only 12% of UK plant-based milk, yogurt, and cheese products analyzed were fortified with iodine. This compares with 61% for calcium and 46% for vitamin B12.

In the United States, the Food and Drug Administration (FDA) provides guidance on the nutritional content of PBAs, but fortification with iodine is voluntary.

The study found that replacing as little as 20% of dairy intake with plant-based alternatives could increase the risk of inadequate iodine intake, particularly among adolescent girls and women of reproductive age.

“People often worry about a decline in calcium intake when they replace some of their dairy with plant-based alternatives. As a result, iodine is frequently overlooked. This study serves as a valuable reminder of iodine’s significance as a nutrient,” said Victoria Finn, MD, board certified endocrinologist at Medical Offices of Manhattan, who wasn’t involved in the study.

The study included analysis of UK plant-based milk, cheese, and yogurt products, as well as dietary data from 3,500 people.

The researchers used statistical modeling to predict how swapping dairy for PBAs might affect iodine and other nutrients.

The people who are most vulnerable to iodine inadequacy are adolescent females and people at reproductive age, especially those who are pregnant or who plan to become pregnant.

The researchers estimated that replacing 20% to 35% of dairy intake with PBAs would increase the proportion of people with inadequate iodine intake:

When the researchers modeled the replacement of all dairy products with PBAs, the proportion of adolescent females with inadequate iodine intake rose to 80%. Among females ages 19 to 49, that proportion remained relatively stable as replacement rates increased.

It is important to keep in mind that the study used dietary modeling rather than direct measurement of iodine status.

Iodine is important for thyroid hormone production, metabolism, and both fetal and child brain development.

“Your thyroid gland requires iodine to produce thyroid hormones, which play a crucial role in regulating metabolism and supporting normal growth and development,” said Finn.

She added that if you consistently consume too little iodine over an extended period, the thyroid gland may enlarge and eventually struggle to produce enough hormones. This condition is known as hypothyroidism, which can lead to various symptoms such as lethargy, sleepiness, weight gain, dry skin, and more.

“Hypothyroidism can be particularly dangerous during pregnancy, as the baby’s brain, nervous system, and organs rely heavily on the mother’s thyroid hormone during the early stages of development,” Finn added.

In the United Kingdom, dairy is a major iodine source due to cattle feed supplementation, not because dairy is naturally high in iodine.

In the United States, dairy is also an important source of iodine for the same reasons.

However, the risk profile for iodine deficiency in the U.S. may differ from the U.K., as iodized salt is also a significant source of iodine and is widely available in the U.S., but not in the U.K.

According to the National Institutes of Health (NIH), the recommended daily intake of iodine depends on your age.

Plant-based or plant-forward diets have grown in popularity, in part due to the health benefits they offer. For example, consuming less meat can reduce your risk of heart disease, type 2 diabetes, obesity, and high cholesterol.

However, as this study suggests, it may be possible to reduce your iodine intake when transitioning to a plant-based diet. There are ways you can protect your iodine intake when choosing a more plant-forward diet, though.

Check the labels on plant-based dairy alternatives to find those fortified with iodine. You can also read the nutrition label. Iodine may be listed as potassium iodide or potassium iodate.

Seafood, eggs, and iodized salt are all good sources of iodine.

You can also discuss iodine supplements with your healthcare professional.

“Iodine supplements should be taken with caution, especially without checking your thyroid levels,” said Finn. “Additionally, be cautious with kelp supplements, as their iodine content can vary, and excessive iodine intake can disrupt thyroid function.”
