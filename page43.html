Of course, at present, they can turn to the likes of Jarrod Bowen as a reliable and prolific creator coming in off the right, and elsewhere in recent times, they found joy turning Michail Antonio into their star striker for a decent spell.

Speaking with ZDF’s 'Aktuelles Sportstudio', Fullkrug was quick to pull up the reporter, simply responding: "A little respect. We just lost a game. That's not in my head right now."

6 world leaders show what age reveals about who holds power

📅 Nov 09, 2026  |  Category: Business  |  Read Source →

Free daily briefing on global business news.

Pew's age data on 186 leaders frames 6 cases, from Trump and Biya to Shah, Frostadóttir and Traoré, and what each age reveals about power.

People tend to treat a world leader's age as trivia, a number to marvel at on a birthday and forget by the next news cycle. At the extremes, though, a leader's age is evidence of how that person won power and how he or she holds it. When a leader is very old, citizens and allies face a practical question: whether the person in office is still the one running things, and how anyone outside the government would know. When a leader is very young, the question changes. Voters and observers want to know what happened to the usual order of promotion that let someone in their 30s reach the top. Both questions matter now. Several of today's oldest and youngest leaders govern countries where age is tied to live political stakes rather than ceremony.

A ruler can reach great age in office by holding power for decades or by receiving a crown passed down within a family. As they age, the public usually learns little about their fitness, and they rely more and more on aides, heirs, and officials to do the daily work of governing. The youngest leaders share a different origin. They reached power through disruption, such as street protest, a military takeover, or voters' rejection of an older political class, rather than through a slow climb up party or military ranks. Youth at the top is therefore a weak promise. A young leader may hold office on the strength of public anger at predecessors, or on a seizure of power, rather than on a program or a record of results. Each age is best read as a question about legitimacy and control, not as a verdict on a leader's vigor or wisdom.

Pew Research Center set out, as President Donald Trump reached 80, to identify which leaders now in office worldwide are the oldest and which are the youngest, and those leaders govern countries as different as Cameroon, Saudi Arabia, Nepal, Iceland, and Burkina Faso. The distance between the two groups is the real story. One of these leaders has held office longer than some of his counterparts have been alive, while others reached the top only within the last few years. What follows looks past each age to what it shows about that leader's hold on power, and about what the public in that country can and cannot see.

Donald Trump turned 80 on June 14, and Pew Research Center reports that he is older than all but 16 of 186 leaders now in office worldwide. Even so, the public has no guaranteed view of his health. CBC reports that no law requires an American president to disclose his medical condition. Americans therefore know only as much about his fitness as the White House chooses to release, and they have no independent way to check it. A president in good health has every reason to share the details, while one in poorer health has little reason to. "I think we can expect very little disclosure about the true health status of any president unless they're in perfect health," said Sara Rosenthal, a bioethicist at the University of Kentucky.

The official account of Trump's health is as favorable as it could be, and nobody outside the White House can confirm it. The White House has reported a perfect 30/30 for Trump on the Montreal Cognitive Assessment, according to CBC. Favorable accounts of his health have been shaped before. In 2020, Harold Bornstein, a doctor who once treated Trump, told CNN that the 2015 letter on Trump's health had come from Trump's own dictation. His health remains a live dispute, and without a disclosure rule, no one outside the White House can settle it.

At the old end of world leadership, Trump's case shows that the stakes of age lie less in vigor than in whether citizens can see who is fit to govern, a test of transparency his White House has not yet clearly passed.

Paul Biya's age cannot be separated from how long he has held power in Cameroon. No current world leader is older than Biya, who is 93 and has been Cameroon's president for nearly 44 years, according to Pew Research Center. He did not win the office from voters. Cameroon has had only two presidents since independence in 1960, The Associated Press reports, and Biya took over in 1982 after Ahmadou Ahidjo, the first, resigned. His power began as a succession inside the ruling order, and the presidency has not changed hands in the decades since.

Biya has kept that power through elections, but the length of his rule is no clear sign that voters chose him. Official results have named him the victor seven times since he took power, The Associated Press reports, and under Cameroon's rules the presidency goes to whoever finishes ahead after one round of voting. Under that rule, no candidate needs a majority, and a divided opposition gets no runoff in which to unite behind one challenger. "The system makes it such that the elections cannot be free and fair, that we know," said Joshua Osih, a member of parliament who ran for president as the candidate of the opposition Social Democratic Front. That charge comes from a man who had to compete under the rules he condemns. On his view, Biya's long rule is less a verdict of voters than a product of how Cameroon's elections are built.

At his age, the more pressing question about Biya is who runs Cameroon day to day. Biya is away in Europe for much of the year, according to The Associated Press, so Cameroonians outside his circle have little way to judge how much of the work of government he still handles. The doubts have reached his own camp, and two men who served in his government, former employment minister Issa Tchiroma Bakary and former tourism minister Bello Bouba Maigari, ran against him in October 2025. Benjamin Akih, an activist and professor at Syracuse University, argues that the ruling party could have fielded no one more vulnerable than Biya, given his age and how badly Cameroon has fared under him. The term Biya sought in that vote would last until he is 99.

For Cameroonians, the age of their president is less a record than an open question about who holds power and how long it can go unanswered.

In Saudi Arabia, the oldest man in the government is not the one who runs it day to day. At 90, King Salman is older than every national leader in office but one, Pew Research Center reports, and he has held the throne since 2015. By 2024 his son, Crown Prince Mohammed bin Salman, was the de facto Saudi leader, according to CNBC. The useful question about King Salman is therefore not whether a man his age can govern but how much his health can still interrupt.

The king's age becomes a practical matter when his health changes the plans of the man who runs the government. King Salman was treated for lung inflammation in May 2024, according to CNBC, and the Saudi government put off a trip abroad by the crown prince because of it. "Saudi Arabia informed the Japanese government that due to the health condition of King Salman of Saudi Arabia, Crown Prince Mohammed's visit to Japan, which had been scheduled to begin on the 20th, had to be postponed," Japan's Chief Cabinet Secretary Yoshimasa Hayashi said. A king who has handed off day-to-day power can still hold back the son who exercises it, and the king has grown older since that illness.

People outside Saudi Arabia can see who governs the kingdom from day to day. They cannot see how often the crown prince will have to change his plans because of his father's health, as he did in 2024. For now, the king holds the title while his son holds the power.

No leader in office anywhere is younger than Nepal's prime minister. Pew Research Center reports that Balen Shah, who took the post in March 2026, is 36, younger than every other one of the 186 leaders now governing worldwide. He is also a rap artist, which makes him a performer and an outsider rather than a figure produced by party machinery. Before the national vote he served as mayor of Kathmandu, ABC News reports, and he led the Rastriya Swatantra Party as its candidate for prime minister. In early March, with counting unfinished, his party led on early trends. His standing rests on one election rather than a long national career, and his age reflects a political system that voters went around rather than one that handed power down.

Young protesters broke Nepal's political order in the streets months before anyone voted. Youth-led protests began in September 2025 and brought down the government of KP Sharma Oli, according to ABC News, and at least 77 people died in the unrest. At the polls, voters finished what the protesters had started, and they did it against Oli himself. Shah won 68,348 votes in the constituency, while Oli won 18,734. The street had already pushed Oli out, and his defeat at the ballot box was so lopsided that it amounts to a judgment on him personally. In Nepal, a leader's youth shows how hard the public turned against the people who governed before him.

The size of that rebuke is also Shah's greatest weakness. He now runs Nepal on a mandate defined by what voters rejected rather than by anything he promised. "The people understand that the new do not really have strong agendas, but it is a punishment to the parties for their decades-long poor governance," political analyst Chandra Dev Bhatta told ABC News. Shah's age signals a clean break from the old parties, but it guarantees no results.

The open test is whether he can build a record of governing before the public's anger turns on him too.

Pew Research Center reports that Kristrún Frostadóttir, Iceland's prime minister, is 38 and younger than every other national leader in office except one. Her age reflects how quickly she rose in politics rather than how early she started. She entered politics in 2021 after a career in banking and economics, Bostonia reports, and her party made her its leader within a year. She became prime minister about three years after she started. Her youth points to a brief political career, not to a lack of working experience.

Frostadóttir has made her age part of her political case. She presents it as a break from the politics that grew out of Iceland's 2008 financial crisis, according to Bostonia. "I definitely am part of a new generation that’s not tied to [financial] crisis–related politics," she said. In her telling, her youth is evidence that she carries none of the blame from that period. That claim is her own, and it rests on her generation rather than on anything she has done in office.

Her age clears one bar, which is distance from the crisis years and the politics they produced. Whether she governs well is a separate question, and her youth cannot answer it yet.

Ibrahim Traoré reached the top of the government of Burkina Faso through a military coup, not an election. Pew Research Center reports that he is 38 and was the world's youngest national leader in May 2024, though two leaders younger than he is now hold office. Traoré was an army captain of 34 when he took control of the country in a coup in September 2022, according to Small Wars & Insurgencies. As a captain, Traoré skipped the usual climb by seniority in the army as well as in politics. Under Traoré the constitution is suspended, so no constitutional calendar sets a date for voters to judge him.

Traoré's military rule did not make Burkina Faso safer in its first years. The country held one of the top spots on the Global Terrorism Index from 2022 to 2024, according to Small Wars & Insurgencies. That ranking places Burkina Faso among the countries worst hit by terrorism in the years that began with his coup. Rule by an army officer did not bring that violence under control during those years.

Traoré has also broken with the old order in security, diplomacy, and the economy. He has made the Africa Corps, a Russian force formerly known as Wagner, a security partner, according to Small Wars & Insurgencies, and Burkina Faso is a member of the Alliance of Sahel States with Mali and Niger. At home, his government nationalized the country's gold mines. Together, these steps show a leader trying to remake the state rather than hold it until the next election.

At 38, Traoré represents a military upheaval more than a new generation taking its turn, and no election is set to judge the result.

Hegseth impeachment articles introduced by Rep. Massie

📅 2026-11-09  |  Category: Business  |  Read Source →

Rep. Thomas Massie, R-Ky., on Monday introduced articles of impeachment against Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth, triggering a vote in the House of Representatives as early as this week.

The impeachment push against Hegseth comes after Democrats swept to majorities in both the House and Senate during last week's midterm elections. Many Republicans, including Vice President JD Vance, have cited the Iran war Hegseth has led as a reason for the electoral bludgeoning.

The Kentucky Republican, who lost his reelection bid in a Republican primary earlier this year, introduced eight articles of impeachment against Hegseth. They include waging war in contravention of war powers resolutions passed by Congress, extrajudicial killings tied to boat strikes in the Caribbean that purported to target drug smugglers and suppressing free speech by targeting Sen. Mark Kelly, D-Ariz., a veteran.

"Wherefore, Secretary of Defense Peter Hegseth, by such conduct, has demonstrated that he will remain a threat to the Constitution if allowed to remain in office, and has acted in a manner grossly incompatible with his duties and the rule of law," the articles, which Massie read from the House floor, read. "Peter Brian Hegseth thus warrants impeachment and trial, removal from office, and disqualification to hold and enjoy any office of honor, trust, or profit under the United States."

Massie's resolution will force House Republican leaders to put the measure up for a vote by the end of this week. Impeachment resolutions are privileged, meaning the House must act on them, table them or vote to refer them to committee within two legislative days.

Congress has the authority to impeach Cabinet secretaries, though it is a highly unusual move. Republican leadership in the House has not yet laid out how they plan to deal with the Hegseth impeachment, and it is unclear whether they have the votes to dispose of Massie's measure following the election.

The last Cabinet Secretary to be impeached was Homeland Security Secretary Alejandro Mayorkas in 2024 over his handling of the U.S.-Mexico border. Before Mayorkas, a Cabinet secretary had not been impeached since Secretary of War William Belknap in 1876. Neither was convicted by the Senate.

If the House votes to impeach Hegseth, the Senate would be tasked with holding a trial and voting on removing the secretary from office. If convicted by a two-thirds majority of the Senate, Hegseth would be removed from office.

Republicans hold a razor-thin 218-214 majority in the House, meaning only a handful of Republican defections could result in Hegseth's impeachment.

House Minority Leader Hakeem Jeffries, D-N.Y., reiterated his support for impeaching Hegseth on Monday at his first press conference at the Capitol since last Tuesday's elections. "Pete Hegseth has abused his power, undermined America's national security, and he's the architect of the reckless and costly failed war of choice in Iran," Jeffries said.

Hegseth has appeared to be preparing for an impeachment fight. The secretary put out a video last week defending his actions atop the Pentagon, and the department has led a media campaign to "Stand. Fight. Defend the Republic." President Donald Trump has also stood by Hegseth.

Jensen Huang affair puts Nvidia's future at risk, with no clear successor to the renowned CEO

📅 2026-11-09  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang's admission of an affair with a purported Chinese spy has raised questions about his future at the company, and what happens if the 63-year-old co-founder, who built the chipmaker into one of the most valuable businesses on the planet, is forced out.

Shares of Nvidia sank more than 4% on Monday, even as the broader market rallied sharply, after Huang acknowledged the affair in a message to staff on Sunday, and said that the board was launching an investigation into whether he was targeted by a foreign intelligence agency. The board said it has "confidence in Jensen," and that it has "no reason to believe that either national security or Nvidia secrets were compromised."

The New York Times first reported that the Chinese businesswoman was a spy for China's Ministry of State Security, according to people briefed on the intelligence circulated in a memo among the Trump administration in the spring. China has called the allegation a smear.

While Nvidia says Huang will continue to lead the company during the probe, pressure is rapidly mounting on the chipmaker, whose technology has powered the first four years of the generative artificial intelligence boom. Leaders from both parties are calling for briefings, and Democrats poised to take control of both houses of Congress are calling for investigations and new legislation "to stop advanced AI chips from fueling China's military," Sen. Elizabeth Warren of Massachusetts wrote in a post on X on Monday.

Huang has outsized influence over Nvidia, and is the sole face of the company with no clear second-in-command. He's also among the loudest champions of AI in the country, is critical to the financing of the broader infrastructure buildout and has a close relationship with President Donald Trump, giving Nvidia a front-row seat when it comes to discussions on AI policy, safety and regulation.

"The issue at Nvidia is that there is no clear successor to Jensen, and we had expected him to stay in this role for at least 10 more years," analysts at Melius Research said in a Monday research note.

Stacy Rasgon, an analyst at Bernstein, told CNBC's "Squawk on the Street" on Monday that, "Jensen is Nvidia. Nvidia is Jensen." He added that Huang "runs the thing pretty much top down," and "sort of pulls all the strings."

Nvidia didn't immediately respond to a request for comment.

Nvidia has long maintained a flat, non-hierarchical organizational structure. Huang has deep involvement in the company's day-to-day operations, with around 60 direct reports, and frequently serves as its spokesperson in front of world leaders and at industry conferences. In addition to being co-founder and CEO, Huang is also Nvidia's president.

However, relative to some of his founder peers in tech like Meta's Mark Zuckerberg and Elon Musk at SpaceX, Huang has minimal actual control. He owns just over 3% of the outstanding shares and is one of 11 members on a board that has no official chair.

And in the risk factors of Nvidia's financial filings, Huang isn't specifically identified as a key person. Rather, the company says, "we are highly dependent on the services of our longstanding executive team."

"Failure to ensure effective succession planning, transfer of knowledge, and smooth transitions involving executives and key employees could hinder our strategic planning, execution, and long-term success," Nvidia says.

Still, Huang's influence is undeniable, so much so that the question of succession is one that's hardly broached among analysts and investors.

Timm Schulze-Melander, an analyst at Rothschild & Co. Redburn, wrote in a Monday note that Huang could be forced to step back from the CEO role if the board's investigation reveals "something materially more serious" than an undisclosed relationship.

But Schulze-Melander said Huang's potential resignation isn't a foregone conclusion of the investigation, pointing to the importance of his leadership at the company.

Nvidia's board "needs to demonstrate independence and protect shareholders without destabilizing a company whose success is unusually dependent on its founder," Schulze-Melander wrote. "Its initial statement suggests it is attempting to achieve both."

Several analysts said the investigation could lead to governance changes at Nvidia, including putting a transition contingency plan in place.

The Melius analysts wrote that Nvidia has a "strong bench" of executive talent across its networking, hyperscale and high-performance computing businesses, along with "technical expertise" stemming from Chief Technology Officer Michael Kagan and Chief Scientist Bill Dally.

"While these folks can keep the engine running, Jensen is one of a kind, and his energy will be needed to maintain confidence in the AI trade," the analysts wrote.

Tips for Hunting Mature Bucks Over Bait (Plus the Ethics of If You Should)

📅 Nov 09, 2026  |  Category: Travel  |  Read Source →

Published Nov 9, 2026 4:16 PM EST

Get everything from whitetail news and opinion pieces, to advanced hunting tactics and cutting-edge gear reviews.

During the 2023 deer season, I shot my biggest buck ever: a Kentucky main-frame 12-pointer with a couple extra points that scored 168 6/8 inches. And I shot that big sum-buck over a yellow pile of ear and persimmon-flavored shelled corn. I even dropped a protein block in the middle and seasoned it with scent for good measure.

And as I write this, I can hear blood boiling and I can see the angry comments coming. Hunting whitetails over bait is still a controversial topic across much of deer country. It is, of course, illegal in many states in the Midwest, Northeast, and West. And even in places where baiting is legal, some argue that hunters should avoid using it.

The National Deer Association published an article explaining how baiting deer can spread disease: “By design, baiting and supplemental feeding concentrate deer and other wildlife species for the purposes of hunting, trapping, viewing, or simply increasing nutrition available to the animals. Some argue baiting and feeding have positive attributes, but no one can disagree they have numerous negative implications to a deer herd, including altering animal behavior, degrading the habitat near bait and feed sites, increasing the susceptibility to predation, and greatly enhancing the opportunity for disease transmission.”

The NDA’s formal position on baiting deer is this: “The NDA opposes the expansion of baiting where not currently legal. The NDA will not work to repeal baiting where currently legal, except where CWD (or other known diseases) is present. The NDA supports the use of baiting by wildlife professionals conducting scientific research. The NDA supports continued research on the effects on baiting in deer management programs.”

Baiting is legal and very popular in many Southern states. All hell broke loose when Kansas regulators began discussing the potential of banning deer baiting. Hunting deer in Texas is synonymous with hunting over a feeder. But still, many folks don’t understand how big bucks relate to bait piles.

Two comments I always hear when the bait debate pops up go something like: “Oh, you bait, bro? Must be easy killing those big bucks.” Or, “Bait won’t kill big, mature bucks, Jack.”

I scoff at both of those statements. Neither are true, and it’s far easier to screw up on a mature buck with a poor baiting strategy than it is to fill a tag with one. Like other tactics, baiting is just another tool in the arsenal. It isn’t a cheat code.

I know because I’ve been baiting Kentucky whitetails most of my hunting career, and it took a long time before I started experiencing success on mature deer. Here is how to bait deer and, more specifically, how to bait a monster buck—plus some considerations if you’re on the fence about legal baiting.

The early season is my favorite time to bait a monster buck. Hunting pressure is low. There’s plenty of cover for concealment. Deer are on predictable bed-to-feed lines of movement. There’s no better time to catch a buck off-guard over a bait pile.

Target warm-season bedding areas, such as north-facing slopes or low-lying areas near water that offer a high stem count. Then, determine what destination food source deer are targeting, such as green soybeans, fresh-cut corn, or dropping white oak acorns. Place the bait between the bedroom and dinner table.

Don’t overlook the pre-rut. This is a great time to bait a monster buck. Bucks are moving more but aren’t running wild just yet. So, capitalize on that buck’s rising testosterone levels. Often that leads to increased daylight travel.

As with the early season, it’s still all about bed-to-feed patterns. But now, deer are likely settling into their fall bedding areas and food sources. So, find these locations (which vary greatly by region and habitat type) and plant bait stations along lines of travel.

Once the rut hits, you might get a buck to hit a bait pile. But most of the time, a rutting buck is more interested in scent-checking does that have been through the buffet line. Because of this, bait stations are still effective during the rut, but you must hunt them differently.

Usually, I prefer to place bait piles on the downwind sides of doe bedding areas. Or locate the bait between two doe bedding areas. Bucks like to travel these locations anyway, so it’s a no-brainer game plan.

Once the rut is over, and the late season arrives, it’s all about the food again. But still, focus on late-season bedding areas. Think solar bedding (south-facing slopes) and thermal cover (dense stands of coniferous trees). Also, consider what deer are eating now, including remaining waste grain, green plots, remaining acorns, but most importantly, woody browse.

Once again, the best bet is placing bait piles between bedding areas and destination food sources. Use these as staging areas to intercept bucks during daylight. At this point, they aren’t likely to reach destination food sources before dark. But if your bait site is located close enough to bedding, they might hit that corn pile during legal shooting hours.

No matter the time of year, there are certain tactics that apply to virtually every baiting scenario. Of course, you have to locate a big buck before bait will help you. No volume of bait will bring in a monster buck if he doesn’t already live there. But once you’ve got a big buck located, follow these tips.

Some people say deer hunting over bait isn’t ethical. But here’s the thing: Some hunting ethics are situational. I’m not talking about taking poor shots or breaking hunting laws. Those ethics are clearly black and white.

For me, baiting deer is more of a gray area. First off, if it’s legal, it’s legal. When analyzing the differences of state-to-state guidelines on baiting, you’ll see regional discrepancies just like you will with crossbow regulations, the use of deer urine lures, and season structures. So if your area allows baiting, then it’s likely that agency wildlife managers for that area have decided baiting is not detrimental to the deer and deer hunting.

Second, it’s important to realize that it’s not like you can just throw out bait at any random spot in the woods and start shooting trophy bucks. It’s far from a canned hunt. If you live in an area that permits baiting, understand that bait has several different applications. Where legal, it can be used to help you attract deer, hold deer, feed supplemental nutrition, take inventory of deer, and more.

As for effectiveness, people commonly say bait stations are either fool-proof or that they turn bucks nocturnal. In my experience, neither are true.

Whitetails are smart, especially around bait piles. They learn to approach bait piles downwind and they pick up on hunters’ scent in the area. Yes, they will often hit bait at night. But no buck is 100 percent nocturnal—that’s a myth.

If you suddenly start seeing a target buck avoiding bait during daylight, it’s likely because you screwed up and disrupted that deer. Or, he started bedding elsewhere. Or, he started targeting a different destination food source. Or, maybe he’s just being a wild, unpredictable deer.

The point is, hunting over bait is still deer hunting. Anything can happen, and most of the time the big buck wins. But if you understand how to bait deer and you execute a well-crafted game plan, then baiting just might lead to the biggest whitetail of your life.

That translates to 25.9 billion kilometers or about 16.1 billion miles in a single day. At an average human walking speed of 3 mph, it would take a human 612,015 years to walk that distance.

Fortunately, Voyager 1 has been traveling a bit faster than that. It currently zips along the cosmos at a steady 37,763 mph, a speed it achieved in 1980 after a gravity-assisted Saturn flyby. It has been flying at that speed ever since.

The milestone comes 14 years after its previous one, when Voyager 1 crossed the heliopause, the point where the sun’s heliosphere ends, and entered interstellar space. It’s also 28 years after passing Pioneer 10 to become the human-made object that has traveled the farthest into space.

The historic milestone isn’t without its drama. Voyager 1 has been in space for nearly half a century, and in that time, NASA’s Jet Propulsion Laboratory has done everything it can to extend the life of Voyager 1. That includes troubleshooting issues, performing maintenance and shutting down various systems to save power and extend the spacecraft’s life-span.

Several of those efforts took place in 2026. The team running Voyager 1 spent weeks shutting down three devices and powering on three low-power alternatives to reduce overall power usage. This initiative, which was internally called The Big Bang, was also performed on Voyager 2. The transition was completed in early September, giving Voyager 1 more time with its dwindling power supply while also keeping it warm enough to continue operating in the frigid conditions of interstellar space.

It was a tricky maneuver that required the team to simultaneously turn multiple instruments on and off. The difficulty of the maneuver was amplified by the fact that it took almost a full day for any signal to reach Voyager and another full day for Voyager to send a signal back, with hours of downtime while the spacecraft performed the operations.

All of this took place several months after Voyager 1 experienced a sudden drop in power, which necessitated shutting off one of its three remaining instruments still in operation. Voyager 1 now has only two instruments still in operation.

Making it a whole light-day away from Earth is likely going to be Voyager 1’s final milestone while it still has enough power to communicate with Earth. Voyager 1 is powered by three radioisotope thermoelectric generators. They are essentially nuclear-powered batteries that generate energy by converting the steady heat from decaying plutonium-238.

When Voyager 1 first launched, its radioisotope thermoelectric generators produced about 470 watts of power. But plutonium-238 doesn’t last forever, and after 50 years in space, the RTGs now produce about 215 watts of power, with an average loss of about 4 watts per year. Most of that power is used to keep Voyager 1 up and running, which is why NASA has shut down eight of the 10 instruments that Voyager had when it launched.

Boston Dynamics Turns Atlas Into a Wheeled Actuator Testbed

Boston Dynamics detailed a wheeled Atlas experiment on November 9, 2026, after replacing the humanoid’s feet with wheels to probe actuator limits and the transfer of whole-body reinforcement learning from simulation to hardware.

The company describes the configuration as a research testbed, not a product. The experiment was designed to expose how real actuators and a learned whole-body controller behave together when the motors approach their operating limits. Accelerating, turning and stopping keep the motors under load while demanding high torque and high speed across their operating range.

Boston Dynamics said years of Spot and Stretch customer experience have shown that deployed robots encounter conditions a lab cannot fully reproduce. Atlas controls engineer Xiaoyu (Andy) Wu, who wrote the account, used the testbed to examine extreme actuation behavior and the robustness of his low-level control tuning.

Training used millions of robot simulations before the policy reached hardware. The simulation-to-hardware problem, called sim-to-real, depended on making the modeled motor reproduce measured physical behavior closely enough for the learned policy to transfer. Policies trained against an idealized motor may rehearse movements the real robot cannot perform; hardware-matched models instead support behavior the robot can reproduce.

Preparation involved matching low-level control parameters across software setups, running motor-characterization experiments and tuning the simulated motor against measurements from Atlas. The team also evaluated simplifications individually, retaining harmless approximations while avoiding those that would give the policy an unrealistic advantage during training.

The project joined actuator design, low-level motor control and whole-body reinforcement learning (RL), which are usually separate specialties on Atlas. Wu’s background is in classical motor control, and this was his first substantial RL project, placing him at the boundaries among those layers.

The researchers adapted established policy frameworks, changing the reward composition, the range of training commands and posture handling. Wu said those changes produced smooth rolling while Atlas remained stable and easy to drive, and characterized the resulting transfer to hardware as essentially perfect.

Some demonstrations began with human motion-capture clips that Wu selected for substantial foot sliding. The side-dash training converted those human foot slides into steered, rolling movements as the policy learned to use the wheels.

For the 360-degree jump, the controller tracked a motion trajectory recorded from a human jumper. Wu characterized the move as relatively low-risk despite its energetic appearance and said it succeeded on the first attempt. As the robot prepared for takeoff, its wheeled lower body rolled quickly into position before the upward push, showing a different use of traction from Atlas on feet.

The hardware behind those movements required little alteration. The only added component was a custom adapter joining an off-the-shelf scooter rim to Atlas’s existing ankle actuator. In the baseline software model, each wheel became an ankle capable of continuous rotation, allowing Wu to substitute wheels for feet without constructing a separate robot model.

Boston Dynamics said lessons from the experiment are already being adapted to Atlas’s standard configuration. Closer integration between actuator dynamics and whole-body RL is intended to improve policy robustness against failure modes and expand the robot’s capabilities. The demonstrations therefore connect an unusual research configuration directly to the control system used by the conventional bipedal machine.

Wu has also started work on policies for failed actuators, including whether reinforcement learning can generate emergent responses to those failures. The follow-on extends that reliability work to hardware whose behavior changes after a failure, testing whether learned control can respond when a real actuator departs from its expected behavior.
