Free video-streaming service Tubi lets users share curated watchlists now

📅 Nov 10, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

User-based recommendations could make it easier to find stuff to watch on Tubi.

Tubi users can now create and share playlists of movies and shows available on the streaming service.

Tubi is a free ad-supported streaming television (FAST) service that has both live channels and on-demand content.

Today, the Fox-owned company announced “Mixtapes” as a way for users to make watchlists from Tubi’s “nearly 400,000 movies and TV episodes,” Tubi’s announcement said, adding:

They can give each mixtape a title and description and arrange the entertainment inside in whatever order feels right to them. Viewers can build one entirely on their own, or describe what they’re looking for and let Tubi offer a starting place that they can edit, rearrange, or rebuild entirely. When their mixtape is finished, they can publish and share it with other people or keep it for themselves.

Tubi’s Mixtapes website already features numerous playlists, including: “Millennial Memory Lane”—with movies like Cruel Intentions (1999) and The Fifth Element (1997) and shows such as Everybody Hates Chris (2005–2009) and Degrassi: The Next Generation (2001–2015); and “Bald Baddies”—which includes Fast & Furious Presents: Hobbs & Shaw (2019) and Central Intelligence (2016).

Watching a Mixtape doesn’t require a Tubi account, but making one does.

Tubi’s Mixtapes comes at a time when streaming service providers are seeking a way to address the content fragmentation that’s frequently cited as a frustration among streaming users, as well as users struggling to find something to watch. Gracenote’s “2025 State of Play” report, for instance, surveyed 3,000 consumers in the US, Brazil, France, Germany, Mexico, and the UK and found that “nearly 33 percent of streaming users feel content and service fragmentation negatively impacts their TV experience.”

Users are also likely to stop using a streaming service if they struggle to find something they want to watch, per the survey. Forty-nine percent of users said they “are willing to cancel a service based on difficulty finding what to watch.” Additionally, 19 percent of respondents said they would “abandon a viewing session if their content search is not successful.”

Although Tubi users don’t have paid subscriptions to cancel, making it easier to find relevant content could help Tubi grow its user base and increase the time people spend watching, which supports the advertising business Tubi relies on.

Sharable watchlists are rare among streaming services. Mubi has a similar feature where users can make lists of movies and shows that people can follow, but the lists can feature content that’s not available on Mubi.

Plex and JustWatch also let people create and share curated watchlists with other users.

Still, it’s nice to see a streaming service, especially a free one, add new capabilities at a time when many other streaming services are consistently increasing subscription prices. And while some streaming service companies are turning to AI to help with content recommendations, a human connection may appeal more to certain users, while keeping costs lower for Tubi.

Phishing Threats Totaled 7.3 Billion in Q3 2026

📅 Nov 10, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

E-mail phishing threats reached 7.3 billion in Q3 2026, with September activity increasing 41% from August, according to Microsoft Threat Intelligence.

According to a report from Microsoft Threat Intelligence and the Microsoft Defender Security Research Team, phishing detections reached nearly 3 billion in September alone.

The findings also highlighted changes in attacker techniques, including business-service impersonation, credential theft, and social engineering through Microsoft Teams.

The Redmond-based firm recognized approximately 129 million malicious messages spoofing the sender domains of tracked business services during Q3.

DocuSign was the most frequently spoofed brand in September, accounting for 29% of the month's tracked spoofing activity. Adobe, SurveyMonkey, and Barracuda also experienced increases toward the end of the quarter.

The research found that some malicious messages were delivered despite failing e-mail authentication checks and being identified as threats.

In September, tenant- or user-level security overrides applied to 63% of these messages with a recorded security outcome — most involved tenant-level exceptions.

Microsoft warned that overly broad exceptions intended to support legitimate business communications can weaken protection against impersonation attacks.

The company emphasized that these findings do not establish whether recipients opened the messages or whether accounts were compromised.

Microsoft also recorded approximately 141,000 malicious Teams call attempts during Q3.

Attackers increasingly used ordinary personal names instead of explicitly impersonating IT support personnel. Such display names accounted for 46% of an observed sample in July, rising to 88% in September.

Meanwhile, QR code phishing declined 67% compared to Q2, totaling roughly 12.1 million attacks.

CAPTCHA-gated phishing activity increased toward the end of the quarter, reaching 3.1 million detected attacks in September. SVG attachments played a significant role in the increase.

Credential theft remained the primary objective of malicious attachment campaigns, with approximately 94% to 96% of payload-based attacks directing users to phishing sites or fraudulent sign-in pages.

Microsoft recommended reviewing Exchange Online Protection and Defender for Office 365 configurations, enabling Safe Links and Safe Attachments, and strengthening authentication protections.

The company also advised organizations to review security exceptions and verify the identities of external Teams contacts before responding to suspicious requests.

For more information, go to the Microsoft blog post.

James Gunn’s DC Universe Was Weird, Ambitious, and Ultimately Unfinished

📅 Nov 10, 2026  |  Category: Business  |  Read Source →

The writer-director is a casualty of Skydance’s acquisition of Warner Bros. He was building to a future that fans will now likely never see, for better or worse.

The hierarchy of power at DC Studios is changing again. On November 9, news broke that writer-director James Gunn and his producing partner Peter Safran left—later reported as ousted from—their jobs as co-CEOs and co-chairmen of DC Studios. Jeremy Latcham, a former Marvel Studios producer whose past credits include The Avengers and Gunn’s own Guardians of the Galaxy, will replace them on December 1.

Suddenly, DC fans woke up on a Monday to a new regime. But how will we look back on the four years that a prolific genre writer and director, who came up under Lloyd Kaufman’s B-movie studio Troma, oversaw an entire superhero universe?

It was a time of a lot of promise, not all of it fulfilled.

Before we start the eulogies for the DC Universe, it’s important to note that things aren’t over. Yet. Latcham will take over Gunn and Safran’s posts, so DC Studios is still up and running with more movies in the pipeline, many of which bear Gunn’s fingerprints. Man of Tomorrow, for example. The 2027 movie is a direct sequel to Superman, also written and directed by Gunn. Among its biggest moves is introducing a new Wonder Woman (rumored, but not confirmed, to be Adria Arjona). The Batman: Part II, the long-awaited sequel to Matt Reeves’s The Batman, will also open in 2028 with Gunn and Safran involved as producers. Gunn also said on Instagram they have been “engaged on another DC project which I’m writing.”

Both Gunn and Safran are expected to stay at DC, with Deadline confirming they will “continue to produce on various DC projects.” That said, Matthew Belloni at Puck published a story citing sources who say the DCU will reboot, again, after Man of Tomorrow. And at The Hollywood Reporter, Borys Kit and Aaron Crouch detailed the friction Gunn faced while at the studio, and how Gunn and Safran’s exits were forced by new Skydance leaders David Ellison and Ynon Kreiz. THR reports the DC heads felt secure after the big Warner-Paramount merger; Clayface had grossed $100 million, and Gunn was preparing a draft for the third Superman movie when they were told they were out. You can say the DCU is in a crisis right now.

For now, we can say that a critical chapter of DC is over. Gunn will no longer apply his vision and filter across all DC projects, which he must feel a little relieved about. By his own admission, which he wrote on Instagram on November 9, Gunn expressed feeling stretched thin, having to exert energy on movies and shows he wasn’t emotionally invested in to begin with. Said Gunn: “[T]he thing my ADHD hyperfocus has had the most difficulty with has been dividing my time between projects I’m incredibly passionate about and the ones I’m not so much and all the things I have to do as a studio head.”

Gunn and Safran got their jobs in late 2022, but it wasn’t until 2024 that people saw the first glimpse of their reign. Though Gunn had already worked for DC on 2021’s The Suicide Squad and created the spin-off Peacemaker, those projects were separate from other things at DC, then run by Walter Hamada. At the time, its biggest bet was Black Adam, Dwayne Johnson’s long-in-development antihero project that fell flat and led to nowhere. When Gunn took the reins of DC soon after, the former Marvel director had carte blanche to tailor the sandbox to his vision.

In January 2023, Gunn revealed his plans for DC Studios in something he called “Chapter 1: Gods and Monsters.” In a six-minute YouTube video, Gunn ran down his plans for the movies and shows that made up his master plan for the next decade of DC Studios. But for every mention of this movie or that HBO Max show, the first project carried significant weight. It was the first step, the real first impression, as a showcase of what Gunn would do with a DCU bent to his will. What icons would make it to the screen? What legend would get their spotlight? What iconic storylines might be retold on the big screen? What, really, will James Gunn’s DC become?

His first order of business was... Creature Commandos.

In fairness, Creature Commandos was a great show (if you saw it). It was an adult animated series where horror and pulp-inspired superheroes teamed up as a black ops strike force. While a show like that wouldn’t be anyone’s idea of a great introduction, Gunn’s background as the writer of movies like Tromeo & Juliet and his success with Guardians of the Galaxy for Marvel at least made sense. It basically said without ambiguity that this new DCU is being molded by no one else but Gunn.

Then, Superman premiered in summer 2025. Initially written by Gunn, the DC Studios head eventually named himself the director of DC’s first theatrical release under his stewardship. Despite my own misgivings about the movie, it was the reorientation a refreshed superhero universe needed. You simply can’t have a DC Universe without the Man of Steel, whose first appearance in Action Comics #1 in 1938 kicked off the modern superhero as we know it.

Superman made a solid first impression for DC at the cineplex. So there was plenty of optimism for the 2026 spin-off Supergirl starring Milly Alcock. Largely based on the acclaimed Tom King and Bilquis Evely graphic novel Supergirl: Woman of Tomorrow, the pseudo-sequel was poised to keep up DC’s newfound momentum. It didn’t. Supergirl hit a wall, much like Black Adam. No superhero universe is immune to duds, but given it was just DC’s second time at bat, a blemish on what still felt like a new, fresh-out-of-the-box DCU felt grave.

But one dud wasn’t enough to kneecap the DCU. The same summer saw the premiere of Lanterns, which fostered a faithful audience on HBO Sunday nights during its buzzy eight-episode first season. Despite mixed reviews, Clayface has grossed a profitable $100 million against a slim budget. Between the next Superman and Batman sequels, there was still plenty to look forward to. Until now.

James Gunn’s reign wasn’t impervious to bad news either. Since his January 2023 reveal, many of the projects have wound up on the back burner: The Authority, which had been a “passion project” for Gunn (with plenty of setup for it in Superman); Waller, a TV show with Viola Davis reprising her role from the Suicide Squad films; Paradise Lost, a political epic set in Wonder Woman’s home; and Booster Gold, a superhero comedy about a goofball from the future. And throughout his reign, Gunn put up with questions about his Batman (not Matt Reeves’s), which he would have introduced in The Brave and the Bold. Gunn sweated over the character so much that a cameo of Batman in Creature Commandos was shrouded in shadow, because he said he was still deciding on what the costume was going to look like.

Since the start of Gunn’s DC, it was clear his architecture came from a place of affection for the brand, and for very specific corners of it. His body of work reflected his scraggly upbringing as a punk creative, with empathy for characters who were too weird for their own Saturday morning cartoon. The Suicide Squad elevated such figures, like Weasel and Polka-Dot Man, whose climax involved a fight with Starro, a colossal evil starfish. Though his Superman was a sunny throwback to the Christopher Reeve classics, it was also jam-packed with the likes of Green Lantern, Mister Terrific, Metamorpho, and a villain Gunn was clearly setting up to be Bizarro.

Multiple times on social media, Gunn shared artwork from one DC book in particular: Kingdom Come, written by Mark Waid and illustrated by Alex Ross. Published in 1996, the story was a treatise of sorts by Waid, who lamented the state of superhero comics at the time. The story sees an older Superman who comes out of retirement to reform the Justice League, only to meet physical and philosophical resistance—predominantly from Batman.

Gunn’s allusions to Kingdom Come, including the exact shape of Superman’s “S” in his film, suggested he was laying the groundwork for a Kingdom Come-style event movie. It would have been an ambitious endeavor, oddly fitting to Gunn’s sensibilities. In the few years we saw Gunn at work for DC, he was building a universe where tried-and-true icons of superhero comics brushed up against the prickly and the bizarre. Where weirdness leaked from the orifices of a summer tentpole with an action figure set.

It often feels like Gunn was just getting started, but four years with two (soon three) movies written and directed by him, several TV shows—and Supergirl and Clayface, whatever you think of them—leave a big enough impression to suggest that Gunn’s DC had no shortage of imagination, even if his signature flourishes had grown familiar. Not every superhero film needs to have shock F-bombs and action scenes set to ironic licensed music, but Gunn had a lane. He excelled at staying in it.

It’s doubtful Gunn and Safran (who took on the business end of things) will stay on at DC under Skydance leadership for long. They will probably see Man of Tomorrow and The Batman: Part II through. If those movies do well, they might stick around to keep making movies, because for the most part, they have a magic that works. If the new movies fail to take off, then Gunn might finally find new stories to tell. It’s hard to say which path is the better one.

Gunn’s Kingdom Come, whatever it might have been, pointed to a future that never became a promise. In his farewell message, Gunn wrote that “the characters we set up will live on.” Whether the next era will let the DCU stay weird is another matter.

NYC Property Transfers Show Growing Warning Signs of Deed Theft

📅 Nov 10, 2026  |  Category: Business  |  Read Source →

More than one-third of New York City transfers of one- to three-family homes from individual owners to companies last year had gaps in their ownership records that researchers say could signal deed theft or other predatory schemes.

The findings come just days after Victor Quimis, 40, became the first person convicted under New York's criminal deed theft law, after admitting to stealing the home of an elderly Queens widow who was receiving hospice care.

Quimis and an accomplice forged Renuka Bherwani's signature to transfer ownership of the Kew Gardens Hills home she had owned for decades, according to prosecutors—first to themselves and then to a corporation controlled by Quimis.

He subsequently obtained a $552,500 mortgage against the property, pocketing more than $300,000 after paying off existing debts. Bherwani's family discovered the theft only after receiving a city notice congratulating Quimis on becoming the home's new owner.

“My mother lived in that house for nearly 40 years, and she was in hospice care in it when her home was stolen out from under her," said Kamal Bherwani, Renuka’s son and the executor of her estate. “Deed theft targets the elderly and the vulnerable, and I urge any family that sees something wrong with a deed to call the Attorney General's Office immediately."

Researchers at New York University's Furman Center are now seeking to better detect potential fraud through patterns in public ownership records, too.

Their latest analysis found that 34.5% of transfers from individual homeowners to companies in 2025 involved a mismatch between the seller on the new deed and the buyer on the previous deed—the highest share in at least a decade.

The share of transactions with mismatched ownership records has climbed from 26.8% in 2021, while an additional 1.4% of transfers in 2025 involved properties with no previous deed on record.

While these discrepancies don’t necessarily indicate fraud—they can also arise through legitimate transactions like foreclosures—researchers say they can help identify properties warranting closer scrutiny.

In New York City, that scrutiny is particularly important. Decades of rising property values have left longtime homeowners and their heirs with a huge amount of wealth that could be vulnerable to exploitation.

Earlier research from the center identified approximately 45,000 homes worth a combined $34.7 billion with at least one indicator of vulnerability to title-related problems or predatory tactics.

The latest report narrows its focus to 2,300 properties at particularly high risk because at least one listed owner is deceased and the property has no recorded deed or one dating back more than 30 years.

That can create difficulties for heirs from establishing their legal rights to the house—an issue that can compound by cutting off access to home equity or financial assistance, or even create difficulties paying necessary bills.

Indeed, nearly 13% of these high-risk properties owed more than $500 in property taxes, compared with fewer than 2% of all individually owned one- to three-family homes. And they were nearly seven times as likely to have appeared on the city's 2025 tax lien sale list.

The findings come as New York City expands efforts to combat deed theft. In April, Mayor Zohran Mamdani established the Mayor's Office of Deed Theft Prevention to coordinate investigations and protections for vulnerable homeowners.

“This is exactly the kind of crime the state's new anti-deed theft law is intended to deter," Queens Borough President Donovan Richards Jr. said in a statement following Quimis' plea. "Queens homeowners can sleep better knowing our Attorney General will be aggressive in using this anti-deed theft law to protect the homes they have worked so hard for.”

The borough accounted for more than half of the city's high-risk properties, according to NYU's latest research. Meanwhile, the Bronx had the highest prevalence relative to its housing stock.

Jamaica and Hollis in Queens also recorded the greatest number of transfers with mismatched ownership records between 2015 and 2025, followed by Queens Village.

Neighborhoods with the greatest numbers of high-risk properties and flagged transfers tended to have larger shares of Black, Asian, and foreign-born residents than the city overall.

The Furman Center has also developed a restricted mapping tool to help government agencies, law enforcement, and legal services providers identify properties that may need intervention.

Researchers say more detailed property records, targeted legal assistance, and estate planning could help protect homeowners before ownership disputes or fraudulent transactions put their homes at risk.

Garmin and Waze just teamed up on a clever new way to keep cyclists safer

📅 Nov 10, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Garmin and Waze are teaming up on an interesting new safety feature that could give cyclists a little extra protection when sharing the road with cars – by making cyclists themselves show up in Waze.

The new feature allows cyclists using a compatible Garmin Edge bike computer or Garmin smartwatch to share their location through Garmin’s LiveTrack feature. That location can then appear in real time to nearby drivers using Waze, with cyclists displayed as moving icons on the road.

As someone who recently joined the Garmin family myself with a smartwatch for tracking my cycling, running, and other activities, this is exactly the type of feature I love seeing added to the platform.

I’ve long maintained that safety – or more accurately, the perception that cycling isn’t safe – is one of the biggest impediments to getting more people onto bikes.

That’s especially true in the US, where cyclists are often forced to share roads with cars instead of enjoying the kind of widespread separated cycling infrastructure found in many more bike-friendly countries. And when a cyclist and a multi-ton vehicle have to occupy the same space, making sure the driver knows that cyclist is there can make a big difference.

Of course, an icon on Waze isn’t going to magically make cycling safe. It won’t replace protected bike lanes, better road design, slower vehicle speeds, or attentive drivers.

But I can certainly imagine situations where it could help. Riding at night or in poor visibility, approaching a blind curve, or simply traveling on a narrow road where a driver might otherwise come upon a cyclist unexpectedly are all situations where an advance warning could be useful.

The feature is opt-in through Garmin Connect and activates when the cyclist starts an outdoor cycling activity. Garmin says location sharing is temporary and session-based, and that cyclists’ personal information isn’t displayed in Waze. Of course, anyone who uses a personal GPS device has already accepted that their location is shared with Big Tech in exchange for features they value, but at least we know that any personalized info stays within Garmin instead of proliferating even further.

Garmin LiveTrack itself is free for users with compatible Garmin devices and previously allowed cyclists to share their live location, speed, route, and other information with friends and family. I’ve used it myself as a safety feature when venturing off-road and on longer solo journeys. The Waze integration essentially expands that safety net to nearby motorists.

There’s obviously one big limitation: You need to be riding with a compatible Garmin device, and the driver needs to be using Waze.

But with Waze claiming around 200 million users globally and Garmin being one of the biggest names in cycling computers and fitness watches, that’s not exactly a tiny Venn diagram.

Anything that gives drivers another chance to see cyclists before actually seeing them sounds like a win to me.

Goldman, Citi and Global Banks Commit $270 Million to Challenge Crypto’s Stablecoin Giants

📅 Nov 10, 2026  |  Category: Business  |  Read Source →

Goldman, Citi and Global Banks Commit $270 Million to Challenge Crypto’s Stablecoin Giants

The group of global banks building a joint stablecoin has put real money on the table, committing around $270 million to fund the venture as it moves from blueprint to buildout.

Goldman Sachs, Citigroup, Banco Santander, and Deutsche Bank are among the institutions backing the effort, Bloomberg reported Tuesday, citing people familiar with the matter.

The capital is meant to cover five years of operations for a planned U.S.-incorporated company that will issue a dollar-denominated stablecoin. Known among participants as Project Faro, the venture is still searching for a chief executive, and its final name remains under discussion.

A spokesperson for the project declined to comment, as did Santander and Deutsche Bank, while Goldman and Citi referred questions to the project.

The funding is the concrete next step for an initiative that first went public late last year. As Decrypt previously reported, 21 of the world’s largest financial institutions committed in September to form a company to issue a U.S. dollar stablecoin, targeting a launch in the first half of 2027, with a euro version and other currencies to follow.

That consortium had more than doubled from an initial group of 10 banks that began exploring the idea in October 2025, and now spans North America, Europe, East Asia, the Middle East and Africa.

Beyond the four named by Bloomberg, its participants include Bank of America, Wells Fargo, Fidelity Investments, PNC, Capital One, TD Bank, UBS, BBVA, Deutsche Bank, Lloyds Banking Group and MUFG, among others. JPMorgan, notably, is not on the list.

The venture is one of several collaborative pushes by established finance into a business long dominated by crypto-native players. Visa, Mastercard and Stripe are among more than 100 companies backing the issuer Open Standard, while European banks have formed Qivalis to issue a euro-denominated coin.

At the moment, Tether’s USDT and Circle’s USDC together account for about 85% of a market worth roughly $290 billion, nearly all of it dollar-pegged. When the bank consortium first went public in September, Circle’s stock fell about 6% as investors priced in the prospect of bank-backed competition for USDC.

The banks are betting that institutional trust, regulatory alignment and existing client networks can win them a share of a settlement business that promises round-the-clock, cross-border payments at lower cost than traditional rails.

The token is being designed to comply with the U.S. GENIUS Act, which created the first federal framework for dollar-pegged tokens in 2025, and, where applicable, the EU’s MiCA regime. The group has said it wants the stablecoin used across wholesale, institutional and retail markets.

For now, Project Faro remains pre-launch, with no issued token, no named CEO, not even a final name, and a 2027 target that leaves ample room for delay.

But the $270 million commitment marks a shift, and a concrete signal that this bank consortium intends to compete for the onchain dollar rather than leave it to crypto.
