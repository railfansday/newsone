Hemab Therapeutics Holdings, Inc. (COAG -9.62%) is transitioning into a late-stage clinical biotechnology organization focused on blood coagulation disorders. Management reported the initiation of its first pivotal trial, SOLARA, which evaluates sutacimig for Glanzmann thrombasthenia following Phase II data that showed significant bleed reductions. The company stated that it is also advancing subcutaneous prophylactic candidates for Von Willebrand Disease and heavy menstrual bleeding, with clinical data updates expected through mid-2027. CFO Behrndt indicated that current cash and marketable securities provide a multiyear runway to support these clinical milestones into 2029.

Google is expanding ads in AI Mode to 10+ countries in English and launching multiturn ads in the U.S. Here is what PPC advertisers need to know about reporting.

Google is expanding ads in AI Mode to more than 10 new countries in English while launching multiturn ads for follow-up queries in the U.S.

Google shared the update from its official Google Ads X account on November 9, following an earlier LinkedIn post from Dan Taylor noting plans to bring ads in AI Mode to additional countries in Q4.

Eligible campaigns can already participate, though advertisers still can’t see AI Mode performance separately in Google Ads.

Here’s what’s changing, how conversational placements work, and what you need to know when clients ask about AI Mode reporting.

Google’s October announcement named 10 additional countries where ads in AI Mode are expanding:

The expansion currently supports English. Google also announced plans to bring ads in AI Mode to Brazil, Mexico, and the United Kingdom in Q4 2026. Those markets were listed as upcoming rather than part of the initial rollout.

Google’s availability documentation notes that Pakistan supports text ads only.

Multiturn ads are launching in the U.S., allowing ads to appear on follow-up questions within an AI Mode conversation. Google hasn’t announced when those placements will expand outside the U.S.

Google describes AI Mode as a search experience for questions involving exploration, reasoning, or complex comparisons. Users can ask a detailed question an dcontinue with follow-ups as they work through their options.

AI Mode may use “query fan-out,” issuing related searches across subtopics and data sources to develop a response.

With multiturn ads, Google can show an ad during those follow-up exchanges when the conversation has commercial relevance.

According to Google’s advertiser documentation, ads can match directly to a search query or draw on context from both the question and AI response. An exploratory question can then create an advertising opportunity even when the user hasn’t searched for a specific product.

In his LinkedIn update, Taylor noted that people are asking longer, more detailed, and exploratory questions in AI Mode, and described many of those interactions as upper-funnel queries that carry commercial intent.

For advertisers, that means ads may reach people still comparing approaches or deciding what they need. A longer question or another follow-up does not, on its own, indicate that someone is ready to buy.

As ads in AI Mode expand, clients will likely ask whether their campaigns are appearing there and how those ads perform.

In my view, the most important thing for agency and in-house teams to communicate right now is that reporting is limited, and you can’t separate out AI Mode performance granularly.

Google’s reporting documentation confirms that AI Mode impressions, clicks, conversions, and costs are included in standard campaign reporting. Search terms are also combined with other traffic, without a separate AI Mode breakdown. That prevents advertisers from calculating an AI Mode-specific CPA or ROAS.

Google’s keyword prioritization documentation also says reported search terms for experiences including AI Mode may approximate the user’s intent. Advertisers should not assume they are seeing the exact question someone entered.

Teams can still review those terms for relevance, compare conversion performance, and check lead quality. If results change after the rollout, though, the timing alone can’t directly establish that AI Mode caused it.

When clients ask how AI Mode ads are performing, advertisers can explain that those results are included in campaign totals. Google Ads doesn’t report them separately, so teams can’t show how much spend or how many conversions came from AI Mode.

Google is adding countries and follow-up placements without giving advertisers a separate view of their results.

That leaves teams answering questions about AI Mode with campaign-level data. A campaign could improve overall while AI Mode underperforms, or the reverse, and the combined totals wouldn’t show that granularity.

Separate reporting would help advertisers understand which searches and placements are producing sales or qualified leads. Until Google provides it, teams can explain how their campaigns qualify for AI Mode, but can’t necessarily tell clients what those placements contributed to.

OpenAI launched Businesses in ChatGPT in beta, letting businesses claim verified profiles, update their details and see aggregated data on how often ChatGPT mentions them. US ecommerce and online-service firms can also launch Business Agents, which OpenAI also sells as ads.

OpenAI is letting businesses claim their profiles in ChatGPT and launch AI agents that talk to customers on their behalf, the company said on Tuesday. The beta, called Businesses in ChatGPT, is aimed at the 1.2 billion people OpenAI says use ChatGPT each week.

Through a tool called Business Hub, verified companies get a blue check and can update their details, prices and stock. They can also see how often ChatGPT mentions them, how many people click through to their website and common topics in those conversations. OpenAI says it does not share users’ chats with businesses.

Eligible US ecommerce and online-service firms can also launch a Business Agent. When a business shows up in a ChatGPT answer, users can open its profile and chat with its agent, which answers using the company’s product details. The chat is labelled and kept apart from ChatGPT’s own answers.

The same agent can be sold as an ad. Businesses can sponsor it as the landing spot for ChatGPT Ads, building on Sponsored Agents, which OpenAI began testing in September. OpenAI says paying does not change how often ChatGPT recommends a business, Digiday reported.

Agents must say they are AI, keep prices accurate and avoid pressure tactics. Purchases and sharing personal data need the user’s explicit approval. For now, buyers are sent to the brand’s own website, though OpenAI is building agent payments with Visa.

The move deepens OpenAI’s push into advertising, which passed a $1B annual run rate in August and now has 100,000 advertisers. It could also squeeze startups such as Peec AI, which sell brands estimates of how visible they are in ChatGPT.
