PepsiCo to cut about 1,000 jobs as North American business struggles

📅 2026-11-09  |  Category: Business  |  Read Source →

PepsiCo is cutting about 1,000 jobs across North America as the company tries to turn around lagging sales in its home market.

"We have a clear ambition for PepsiCo North America: to build a stronger, more future-fit business that delivers industry-leading growth," Ram Krishnan, CEO of PepsiCo North America, wrote in a Monday letter to employees obtained by CNBC. "To do that, we need to be faster, more connected, and more capable, while continuing to invest in growth – behind our people, capabilities and brands."

Over the next week, Pepsi plans to cut jobs as it says it aims to streamline its operations and better connect teams across the company's North American business. PepsiCo employs about 306,000 people worldwide, including roughly 125,000 in the United States, as of Dec. 27, according to a company filing.

The Wall Street Journal first reported the coming layoffs.

In October, PepsiCo cut its full-year earnings outlook, citing the struggles in its home market. During the third quarter, its North American beverage unit saw volume shrink 2%, while its North American food division reported flat volume.

While the company has tried to boost sales by cutting prices and investing in marketing, the turnaround is taking longer than expected, CFO Steve Schmitt said on the company's earnings conference call.

Shares of Pepsi rose slightly in afternoon trading. The stock is down nearly 1% this year, lagging behind the S&P 500's gains of nearly 11% over the same period.

📅 Nov 09, 2026  |  Category: World  |  Read Source →

An exceptionally well-preserved, approximately 47-million-year-old owl fossil from the UNESCO World Heritage Site Messel provides new clues as to when owls became nocturnal. The newly described species Masillastrix blattophaga was likely still diurnal—as indicated by an iridescent cockroach in its stomach, as well as the structure of its feathers and wing bones. In their new study, published in the journal Current Biology, the Senckenberg researchers speculate that nocturnal behavior in owls only developed between the middle and late Eocene, some 47 to 34 million years ago.

On the eve of Tuesday's trade deadline, dominoes are starting to fall across the league with Week 9 largely in the books. And now, the Jaguars have acquired All-Pro safety Jessie Bates III in a trade with the Atlanta Falcons, according to ESPN. As Bates heads to Duval County, the Falcons receive a 2027 second-round pick and a 2028 fifth-round pick in return.

Bates is in the final year of his contract and is set to be a free agent at the end of this season. While this is looked at as a rental, the 29-year-old should prove to be a key addition to Jacksonville's secondary as it looks to make a serious postseason push following a 6-2 start to the regular season.

In nine games played for the Falcons this season, Bates has recorded one interception, three forced fumbles, and 60 total tackles. The veteran has also allowed a 78.3% completion percentage to go with a 115.2 passer rating in coverage, per TruMedia.

Bates entered the NFL as a second-round draft choice of the Bengals in 2018 out of Wake Forest. After spending his first five years of his career in Cincinnati, he inked a four-year, $64.02 million contract with the Falcons in the spring of 2023 and has been lurking in their secondary ever since.

Bates is tied for the second-most interceptions (28) in the NFL since coming into the league and has been named a second-team All-Pro three times, including in 2025.

Atlanta spent last offseason inking several veterans to extensions, including Bijan Robinson and Drake London. Bates was not among them, and after dropping to 3-6 on the season with a loss to the Bengals in Madrid on Sunday, they're electing to move off of the talented safety for future draft capital.

Jacksonville gave up quite a bit to land Bates. Two picks -- particularly a second-rounder in 2027 -- is a hefty price to pay, especially considering that Bates is set to be a free agent at the end of this season. I do wonder, however, whether that price indicates that Jacksonville views Bates as more than just a rental. After all, the organization does have a history of extending players that they've acquired in midseason trades. For instance, after trading for wideout Jakobi Meyers around last year's deadline, they later signed him to a three-year, $60 million extension. I'll be curious to see if Bates follows a similar path.

Regardless of whether he's with the Jags for the next few months or years, it's a great move for the 2026 squad. Bates is one of the best safeties in the NFL. In fact, he's the player in the past 40 years with at least 800 tackles and 25 interceptions before age 30. He comes in as a premier piece for the secondary, while also deepening a safety room that has been hit by injuries, most recently losing Eric Murray for the rest of the season to an elbow injury.

Give Atlanta the slight edge here. While they are losing a blue-chip player in their secondary, they were probably going to lose him anyway in free agency later this offseason.

The Falcons spent the bulk of last offseason extending key pieces to their core, and that did not include Bates. While the team is technically still in the division race despite a 3-6 record, it doesn't feel like this season will see them making a sizable push if/when they reach the postseason. With that in mind, it's the more prudent move to move off of Bates, get worse in the secondary in the immediate, but collect legit draft capital to position yourself better down the road.

Getting a second-rounder as part of this deal is nothing to turn your nose up at and is better than any sort of compensatory pick they'd receive if Bates walked in free agency.

Online Gaming, Crypto Payments and On-Chain Games Are Different Categories

📅 Nov 09, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Home » Uncategorized » Online Gaming, Crypto Payments and On-Chain Games Are Different Categories

Online gaming, crypto payments, and blockchain gaming are not the same thing. Learn how to distinguish payment methods, game mechanics, and on-chain execution when evaluating crypto-enabled gaming platforms.

A wallet button beside a game does not explain where its rules run. A token ticker does not reveal who maintains the game state. Even a transaction recorded on a blockchain may describe a payment rather than a move inside the game. For readers assessing gaming products, these details matter because three separate ideas are often compressed into one label: access through the internet, payment with cryptocurrency, and execution on a blockchain.

An online game uses a network connection for some part of its operation. That could involve downloading content, joining a match, checking an account, or sending actions to a server. The browser or installed app supplies the interface, while processing can be divided between the player’s device and remote systems.

None of those arrangements requires a blockchain. A server can maintain account balances and game records in an ordinary database. A browser can display animation produced locally while showing information returned by that server. The visual experience alone reveals little about the underlying architecture.

This is also why an app download and browser access do not define opposing financial models. Either interface can connect to a conventional payment system, a crypto payment service, or a mixture of both. Distribution tells you how the product reaches the player. It does not identify the system that records ownership or enforces its rules.

DailyCoin’s analysis of DeFi front ends and smart contracts examines the separation between a website and the programs it accesses. The same distinction helps when assessing a game: its interface and the system executing its rules need to be identified separately.

Accepting cryptocurrency adds a payment method. A service might receive an asset directly or work through a payment processor that monitors transfers and updates the service’s account records. The asset received and the balance displayed to the customer need not be represented in the same way.

For example, a hypothetical platform could accept a token transfer and then record a corresponding account credit in its own database. Subsequent game actions could take place entirely within that platform’s existing infrastructure. The blockchain transfer and the account credit would be connected operationally, but they would remain separate records.

A transaction hash can help identify the transfer. It does not, by itself, reveal the logic used to calculate an unrelated game result. To understand the product, the next question is what happened after payment: was only a balance updated, or did a transaction call code that changed the game’s state?

Casino catalogs illustrate why these labels need to stay precise. Slots are identified by their reel or grid layouts, symbols, paylines, and feature rules. Classic slots, video slots, and progressive jackpot formats describe aspects of the game or prize structure. Those descriptions do not specify a blockchain network.

Consider how little a slot’s format tells us about its payment infrastructure. A payline defines which symbol positions form a combination, and a bonus rule defines what triggers an additional feature. Neither tells us how a deposit reaches the account or where the outcome calculation runs. Spin Casino’s description of online slots in Canada focuses on those game formats and mechanics. It belongs to the product-description layer of the analysis, separate from evidence about a wallet integration or a blockchain contract.

That separation prevents a familiar classification error: treating a digital casino game as an on-chain game simply because both are discussed alongside cryptocurrency. To establish a blockchain connection, identify the actual payment flow, asset record or executed game action. The slot label alone supplies none of those details.

The same discipline applies to a product described as a blockchain game. That label could refer to tokenized items while the match itself runs on a server. An ownership record and a record of the rules being executed are different kinds of evidence.

To understand on-chain gameplay, look for the programs that govern actions and store their consequences. Ethereum’s introduction to smart contracts describes contracts as code and state at an address on the blockchain. Transactions can call their functions, and execution follows the programmed rules.

In a hypothetical turn-based game, a contract could check whose turn it is, determine whether a move is permitted, and update the board. Recording only a collectible associated with that game would be a narrower use of the technology. The board and its rules might still be managed elsewhere.

Products can also combine these approaches. Some assets or actions may be recorded on-chain while other systems remain on servers. The interface can stay off-chain even when the underlying game logic runs in contracts. A claim of fully on-chain gameplay therefore needs a clear account of which rules and state are included, plus any outside data on which they depend.

When reading a product description, follow one action from start to finish. Identify what the player submits, which system processes it, and where the resulting change is recorded. That gives “online,” “crypto-enabled,” and “on-chain” concrete meanings that a logo, wallet connection, or token name cannot supply.

Scholar Dishes About Finding a Long-Lost Caravaggio Painting in Paris

📅 Nov 09, 2026  |  Category: World  |  Read Source →

On a gray winter day in Paris three years ago, art historian Maria Cristina Terzaghi walked into the back room of a Parisian gallery and came face-to-face with a painting that had been missing for nearly two centuries. The picture was covered in grime, but she recognized it right away: Young Man with a Carafe of Roses, a work believed to have been painted by Caravaggio in the 1590s.

Terzaghi, a professor at Roma Tre University and one of the world’s leading Caravaggio scholars, has now offered her most detailed account yet of the discovery. Writing in La Repubblica on Sunday, she explained why she believes the painting is an original by the Italian Baroque master, rather than one of the copies that have intrigued art historians for decades. Related Articles Former High Museum COO Sentenced to One Year in Prison for Siphoning $600,000 to Buy Guitars Artist Duo Brognon Rollin Awarded 2026 Prix Marcel Duchamp, France's Top Art Prize

The painting shows a curly-haired young man in a white shirt leaning against a table covered with fruit, an artichoke, and a glass carafe filled with roses. His mouth hangs just slightly open. One eyebrow is raised, like he’s about to say something. Look closely at the carafe, and there’s another surprise: reflected in the glass are a window and the figure of another young man, possibly Caravaggio himself.

The composition has fascinated scholars for nearly a century. Four old copies are known to exist, including one at the High Museum of Art in Atlanta, but the original had apparently vanished. According to Terzaghi, the newly identified canvas is that missing painting, made around 1596 or 1597, when Caravaggio was in his twenties and beginning to establish his reputation in Rome.

Her research found that the painting appeared in a 1607 inventory of works belonging to Giuseppe Cesari, known as Cavalier d’Arpino, whose workshop employed the young Caravaggio. Cesari eventually ran into trouble with papal authorities after being accused of illegally possessing weapons and orchestrating a street brawl that “disfigured a painter” named Pomarancio. He surrendered his art collection to escape prosecution, and the works eventually passed to Cardinal Scipione Borghese, nephew of Pope Paul V.

The painting was recorded in the Borghese collection in 1693, and Terzaghi believes it remained with the family until at least 1831, when restrictions that had previously prevented its sale had passed.

By the early twentieth century, the painting had made its way into the holdings a Paris bookseller. Following his death, his widow put the work up for auction in 1909―cataloged simply as a work by an anonymous Italian painter. An ancestor of the current owners bought it, and it has remained in the family ever since.

Terzaghi believes the painting is closely connected to Caravaggio’s Boy Bitten by a Lizard, another early work showing a young man alongside an elaborate still life. Scholars have previously suggested that the two compositions might belong to a series representing the five senses, with the lizard representing touch and the roses representing smell.

For Terzaghi, the painting’s quality is the best evidence of its authenticity. She points to the young man’s expressive face, the shimmering white fabric of his shirt, and the remarkable handling of light passing through the water-filled carafe. The floral arrangement also bears a striking resemblance to a description of Caravaggio’s work by his contemporary and rival Giovanni Baglione, who recalled the artist’s extraordinary treatment of water, glass, and flowers covered in dew.

The discovery isn’t entirely new to the art historical world. In January 2025, Spain’s Ars Magazine reported that a French family believed it owned the missing Caravaggio. Terzaghi also alluded to the painting in the catalog for the Caravaggio 2025 exhibition in Rome. Her account in La Repubblica, however, offers a much fuller explanation of the attribution and the provenance research behind it.

There are still questions, particularly about how the painting traveled from Italy to France. And as with almost any newly attributed Caravaggio, other specialists will need to assess the evidence before the work can gain wider acceptance.

The news comes during an unusually busy few days for Caravaggio scholars. On Saturday, another Italian art historian, Gianni Papi, attributed a Portrait of a Young Man to the artist during a conference in Montepulciano.That painting had previously been sold at Dorotheum as a work from the 16th-century Tuscan school. Papi has yet to publish the evidence supporting his claim.

Supreme Court allows Trump administration to continue border wall construction

📅 Nov 09, 2026  |  Category: Other  |  Read Source →

Type to search articles, cases, and authors.

The Supreme Court on Monday cleared the way for the Trump administration to resume construction of a border wall in the Big Bend region of southwestern Texas. In a four-page, unsigned opinion, the justices granted the government’s request to pause an order by a federal district judge that had temporarily halted the project. Only Justice Ketanji Brown Jackson indicated, without explanation, that she would have denied the government’s request.

The dispute began earlier this year, when then-Homeland Security Secretary Kristi Noem and her successor, Markwayne Mullin, issued three waivers of various environmental and conservation laws to authorize construction of the wall in the Big Bend region, which includes parts of Big Bend National Park. Noem and Mullin relied on a provision of the Illegal Immigration Reform and Immigrant Responsibility Act of 1996 that gives the DHS secretary the power to “waive all legal requirements” necessary “to ensure expeditious construction” of border walls and roads. When they announced the waivers, Noem and Mullin indicated that the Big Bend area “is an area of high illegal entry” requiring additional walls and barriers.

A group of plaintiffs opposed to the construction – an environmental group, a local landowner who works as a river guide, and a group formed to preserve a church – went to federal court to challenge the waivers’ legality. On Oct. 2, U.S. District Judge Kathleen Cardone temporarily barred the Department of Homeland Security from continuing construction on the border wall in the Big Bend region “until and unless” the government complies with environmental and other applicable laws.

In a written opinion that she released on Oct. 14, Cardone reasoned that the challengers were likely to prevail on their claim that the DHS secretary did not have the authority to issue the waivers. She relied on the major questions doctrine – the idea that if Congress intends to give a federal agency the power to make decisions of vast economic or political significance, it must say so very clearly. In particular, she emphasized, the Big Bend region is not an area with a high number of illegal entries. To the contrary, she stated, although it “comprises over 25% of the U.S.-Mexico border,” it makes up only a tiny percentage (“between 0.3% and 2.2%”) of the migrants apprehended while crossing the border. Therefore, she wrote, “if the Big Bend Sector is an area of high illegal entry, so is every other sector, and the waiver authority would apply to the entire U.S.-Mexico border. If that were the case, Congress could have simply authorized construction ‘to deter illegal crossings.’” The major questions doctrine instructs courts to avoid “interpretations that would render limiting language meaningless,” Cardone concluded.

After Cardone declined to pause her ruling to give the government time to seek Supreme Court review, the Trump administration came to the Supreme Court, where it asked the justices not only to temporarily freeze Cardone’s order but also to grant review and summarily (that is, without additional briefing or oral argument) reverse it. U.S. Solicitor General D. John Sauer argued that Cardone lacked the power to consider the challengers’ claim based on the major questions doctrine because their complaint specifically alleged that the DHS secretary “lacked ‘clear congressional authorization’ … to issue the waivers” under the IIRIRA – which, he said, was a statutory claim that “Congress denied district courts” the authority “to entertain.” But in any event, Sauer continued, even if the DHS secretary’s power to issue waivers under IIRIRA “implicates a question of ‘vast economic and political significance,’ Congress did clearly authorize the Secretary both to build border barriers and to waive legal requirements in order to facilitate their expeditious construction.”

The challengers urged the justices to leave Cardone’s order in place. Eric Glitzenstein, a lawyer representing the plaintiffs, told the justices in a 40-page filing that if her order were paused or lifted, it would permit the Trump administration to “immediately resume blasting, bulldozing, road construction, and other border barrier-related construction across one of the most remote and ecologically and culturally significant landscapes in the United States.”

The challengers argued that the major questions doctrine applied to their claim. They emphasized that the DHS secretary’s “decision to waive myriad federal, state, local, and tribal laws across vast geography for the purpose of building border infrastructure, even through national and state parks where border crossings are miniscule, is a truly ‘extraordinary’ assertion of power.” Because Congress “did not clearly authorize the sweeping exercise of power asserted here,” they wrote, the government’s “actions therefore amount to a constitutional violation of the major questions doctrine.”

In Monday’s opinion, the justices denied the government’s request to summarily reverse Cardone’s ruling but granted its request to put it on hold. “The Government,” the court explained, “is exceedingly likely to prevail on its argument” that Cardone did not have the power to consider the challengers’ “so-called major-questions-doctrine claim.” That claim, the court emphasized, contends only “that the Secretary exceeded his statutory authority,” which is not a constitutional claim. This is true, the court continued, even if the challengers rely on the major-questions doctrine, because that doctrine is a rule used to interpret laws. “There is no such thing as a ‘constitutional’ major-questions claim.”

The other factors that courts normally consider in deciding whether to pause a lower court’s decision also point in the government’s favor, the justices said. For example, Cardone’s order “delays the Government’s construction of border barriers and roads in the Big Bend Sector” and, as a result, “impedes the Government from advancing its ‘compelling interests’ in border security and integrity.” Moreover, the government is suffering permanent harm from the costs – “nearly half a million dollars … per day” – that it is incurring while the construction is on hold, but which cannot be recovered. Finally, the court wrote, “[t]he Government’s interest … outweighs any aesthetic, recreational, and environmental harm alleged by” the challengers.

IBM plans to bring Anthropic’s Claude Mythos to enterprise customers

📅 Nov 09, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Mythos would hunt for unknown flaws in customers' code, and developers would approve every fix.

IBM’s office in Dubai Internet City, Dubai, 3 November 2026

IBM plans to add Anthropic’s Claude Mythos to Concert Protect so customers can scan their own code for unknown flaws, test which are exploitable and get fixes for developers to approve. It is aiming for early 2027. Mythos itself remains limited to vetted organisations.

IBM plans to build Anthropic’s Claude Mythos into its Concert Protect security software, the company said on Monday. Mythos is so good at finding security flaws that only vetted groups can use it. IBM wants to offer the feature in early 2027.

Mythos would scan code that customers pick for flaws no one knew about, including ones that normal scanners miss. Concert Protect would then check whether hackers could really use each flaw in that customer’s systems.

IBM and Red Hat tools would draft fixes, which developers must review and approve before they go in. IBM cautioned that its plans could change. It is not betting on one lab: in June it joined OpenAI’s cyber programme for a similar service.

“The bottleneck is no longer finding vulnerabilities. It is determining which can actually be exploited and fixing them before attackers do,” IBM wrote.

That gap is real. Project Glasswing, Anthropic’s restricted Mythos programme, flagged more than 10,000 possible serious flaws in its first month, but only 97 had been patched by then.

IBM says its past Mythos work, through Glasswing, was internal research. This is its first step toward selling it to customers. That fits Anthropic’s plan to put Mythos inside partners’ products, where users get findings and fixes rather than the model itself.

Access to Mythos is tightly controlled. Anthropic briefly switched it off in June to comply with US export controls, and the White House now reportedly decides who joins its partner list. Anthropic released Mythos 5.2 on Monday, still only for vetted organisations.
