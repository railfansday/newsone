Trump FCC declares California isn't allowed to enforce phone service rules

📅 Nov 20, 2026  |  Category: Business  |  Read Source →

FCC preempts state rules that require AT&T to keep offering home phone service.

The Federal Communications Commission ordered California to stop enforcing its Carrier of Last Resort (COLR) rules against AT&T, setting up another battle between the Trump administration and California over the state’s right to enforce its own telecom laws and regulations.

AT&T’s status as a Carrier of Last Resort in California requires it to offer telephone service to any potential customer in its territory. AT&T wants to stop offering basic phone service to new customers and discontinue service for 184,000 residential customers and 15,000 business customers on June 1, 2027.

The order released today granted AT&T’s request to preempt state rules, following up on the FCC’s previous approval of AT&T’s petition to discontinue service. AT&T had tried to achieve the same result in court, but a federal judge rejected its request to override California’s rules in July.

The FCC order probably isn’t the last word, as California could sue the commission. California has stood alone against AT&T, which has received relief from COLR obligations in 20 of the 21 states in its wireline service territory.

“California’s Carrier of Last Resort framework may have made sense when it was established 30 years ago,” FCC Chairman Brendan Carr said in a statement released with today’s order. “But those rules were designed for a bygone era and no longer make sense in today’s competitive, advanced marketplace.”

If AT&T ends service on its old copper lines, many homes and businesses would not be offered modern fiber networks. AT&T claims a wireless home phone service that connects to the cellular network will be good enough, and the FCC agreed.

“The FCC has granted AT&T’s applications to replace legacy voice service with a modern, IP-based alternative in certain areas of the state. California’s state requirements are blocking AT&T from proceeding with a federally authorized transition,” Carr said.

California says its rules allow wireless replacements if they meet the state’s standards for basic phone service, but that AT&T must demonstrate that its wireless phone service would meet the COLR obligations. California’s requirements include flat-rate options for unlimited voice calls, discounted Lifeline rates for eligible low-income customers, and telephone relay service for people who are deaf and hard of hearing.

FCC Democrat Anna Gomez voted against the order, saying that state telecom commissions “are often the last line of defense for consumers. They know their communities, they take their complaints, and they answer to the people who rely on these services in a way that a federal agency in Washington cannot always match.”

Gomez said the California Public Utilities Commission (CPUC) has an open proceeding on how to modernize its COLR rules, and that the FCC should let that process play out. Large technology transitions work best “when the state regulator is a partner with a real role,” but the FCC is treating the CPUC “as an obstacle to be cleared,” she said.

The CPUC “proceeding is the right place for California to weigh what a modern basic service requirement should look like, with input from the people who will actually be making that switch,” Gomez said. “AT&T has said it does not plan to discontinue service before June 1, 2027, which leaves time for the CPUC to finish its work. Rather than wait, this item tells the CPUC to write its new consumer protections around a federal ruling it opposed.”

In March, the FCC issued an order that made it easier for carriers to discontinue copper networks. The order asserted that state rules are subject to preemption if they conflict with the FCC’s discontinuance authorizations and authority. AT&T cited the order in its lawsuit against California.

Trump officials and telecom companies have been trying to block California’s rules for years. The first Trump administration lost an attempt to preempt state net neutrality laws throughout the country in 2019, and California later defeated ISPs in a lawsuit that challenged its net neutrality law in court.

California’s net neutrality law and other telecom regulations are again being threatened by rules the Trump administration applied to federal grants, but California rejected US demands to stop enforcing its laws this month. In that case, California may have to choose between enforcing its telecom laws and receiving $1.42 billion to deploy broadband to 270,571 locations.

E.J. Carroll Dies: ‘The Deuce,’ ‘Boardwalk Empire,’ ‘Law & Order’ Actor Was 66

📅 Nov 20, 2026  |  Category: Entertainment  |  Read Source →

E.J. Carroll, a veteran character actor whose credits include The Deuce, Boardwalk Empire, Blue Bloods three Law & Order series and Grand Theft Auto V, died November 19. He was 66. His rep Jon Mason of Mason-Lewis Entertainment said Carroll died suddenly while en route to a voice-over booking, but he did not provide a cause of death.

Born on May 21, 1960, Carroll appeared in features, television, short films and video games during a nearly three-decade screen career. His first screen credit was playing a cop in a 1998 episode of NBC’s Law & Order, and he would appear as other characters in three other episodes through 2004. He also had roles in two early-2000s episodes of its juggernaut spinoff Law & Order: SVU and two more in Law & Order: Criminal Intent that decade.

He would appear in a number of movies during the later 2000s and into the 2010s including Darren Aronfsky’s acclaimed 2009 drama The Wrestler, along with The Cake Eaters, The Capers, The Winning Season, The Hungry Ghosts, A Fish Story and the Robert De Niro-Kate Beckinsale pic Everything’s Fine, among others.

Carroll played Sheriff Victor Sickles in two 2012 episodes of HBO’s lauded period crime drama Boardwalk Empire, from Tenrence Winter and MArtin Scorsese and starring Steve Buscemi, appeared in episode of FX’s Glenn Close-Rose Byrne legal thriller Damages that same year. He went on to guest on NBC’s The Blacklist, CBS’ Blue Bloods, HBO’s High Maintenance, FX’s Pose and Netflix’s Marvel series The Punisher.

He landed perhaps his most recognizable role in 2017, recurring on the HBO’ ‘s The Deuce as Fat Mooney, who ran a New York City sex shop owned by Frankie Martino (James Franco). He appeared in a half-dozen episodes of the series about the rise of the porn industry in the 1970s and ’80s.

Most recently, Carroll filmed a role in A Delicate Arrangement, the upcoming film written and produced by Jayce Bartok and directed by Nick Sandow. It stars Carla Gugino as Vivien Leigh in the true story of two-time Oscar winning actress’ relationship with a Philadelphia florist (Matthew Modine). David Hyde Pierce also stars in the film, which is in post-production.

“E.J. Carroll was a consummate actor known and loved for his intense commitment to the characters he played,” said who noted that Carroll was passionate about A Delicate Arrangement and helped ensure the project would be completed. “There isn’t an actor in New York that hadn’t worked with or crossed paths with E.J., known for stealing scenes alongside Robert De Niro, Mickey Rourke, Bruce Dern and most recently Jalen Brunson and Quinta Brunson. He will be greatly missed by the acting community which he lived for.”

An annual federal report cited interagency agreements and a handful of executive orders as key factors that will challenge the agency in 2027.

Since early 2025, the second Trump administration has outsourced many of the Education Department’s core functions such as grant management, monitoring and technical assistance to other agencies through interagency agreements. The department has also reorganized many of its offices and laid off a bulk of its staff.

"Change of this magnitude can result in challenges associated with employee resistance, fatigue, and uncertainty — such as decreased productivity, operational disruption, and lost efficiency — and have an impact on the organization’s ability to respond to stakeholder questions and concerns," the Nov. 13 report from OIG stated.

The Education Department has not yet issued an annual performance plan for fiscal year 2026 and has yet to issue its annual performance report for FY 2025, strategic plan for FY 2026-2030, or annual performance plan for FY 2027, according to the report.

Other reports through various federal offices have flagged similar concerns with the Education Department's output amid McMahon's efforts to close the agency, which ultimately requires Congressional approval.

In June, another OIG report found that layoffs at the Education Department appear to have impacted units that were performing legal duties.

The staffing changes were made to 16 of the department's 17 offices, resulting in an overall reduction of at least 40% of the more than 4,000 employees who were working for the Education Department when Trump reentered office in January 2025.

Many lawmakers, civil rights groups and lawyers have raised concerns with output from at least one of those offices, the Office for Civil Rights, which saw a slowdown in case resolutions that included no sexual harassment or assault cases being settled in 2025. A later report from the office of Sen. Bernie Sanders, I-Vt., showed that OCR resolved only 1% of its cases overall in 2025, the lowest percentage from any OCR in over the last decade.

Responding to a draft of the most recent OIG report, the Education Department said it had made progress in many of the areas of concern, including change management. According to OIG, the department said it has "continued efforts to improve operational processes and maintain program delivery during a period of substantial organizational change." Additionally, the agency said it has "engaged in contractor support to assist with resolution for external audits," among other adjustments.

The department has previously maintained that its staffing and organizational changes will not affect the statutory functions it is legally obliged to perform.

The department also stressed in a comment to K-12 Dive that the OIG report is an annual update. In a January OIG report, the office flagged similar oversight challenges stemming from executive orders and resulting organizational changes.

"It is critical that the Department strategically plan for and effectively manage these and other major operational changes that it may decide to undertake," it warned at the time.

Past reports have flagged challenges under previous administrations, such as monitoring grantees' use of COVID-19 pandemic relief funding.

The party clinched the state’s House and governorship in the midterm elections, though that still may not be enough to get the legislation passed.

In the state’s House, Reps. Nicole Collier and John Bucy III have put forward bills that would repeal the 2023 law. Texas state Sen. José Menéndez introduced a companion bill in the state’s Senate.

At any other time in the past three decades, the new bills might have faced little chance of passage simply by virtue of being championed by Democrats. But the party now controls two-thirds of a trifecta for the first time since 1996 — a makeup that may still not be enough to pass the legislation.

Senate Bill 17 — the target of the new bills — bans several DEI activities at public colleges, including:

Texas was one of the first states to ban DEI offices and initiatives in higher education. Following SB 17 and a similar law in Florida, universities from Ohio to Iowa to Alaska shuttered their DEI offices, often under legislative mandates.

Institutions quickly responded to SB 17. For instance, the University of North Texas said before the law took effect that it would restructure its Multicultural Center and Pride Alliance into the student affairs office and launch a new Center for Belonging and Engagement.

After passage, the Texas A&M University System issued guidance stating that DEI terminology couldn’t be used on the institution’s websites. Offices dedicated to student affinity communities would have to close, though it said some programs and libraries could be absorbed into broader offices and initiatives.

Texas Tech University also closed its DEI office.

Texas Tech University System’s latest chancellor — Brandon Creighton,  who helped author SB 17 — has gone even further than the bill’s requirements, which don’t cover academic content. The system has moved to eliminate academic programs focused on sexual orientation and gender identity and put restrictions on teaching about race, sex and gender in courses.

State audits so far have determined that the University of Texas System, nearly all of the institutions within theTexas A&M University System and 19 of the state’s community colleges are compliant with SB 17.

Audits last year deemed two institutions noncompliant — a community college because it made a new employee participate in a DEI training and Texas A&M-Central Texas because it struck a contract that didn’t conform to the law’s ban.

Kane set to miss three months with ankle injury

📅 Nov 20, 2026  |  Category: Other  |  Read Source →

Harry Kane was withdrawn after 20 minutes of Bayern's Bundesliga game against Cologne; the injury comes days after the England captain became his country's most-capped player; Kane won the Ballon d'Or earlier this month; Michael Olise also taken off injured in 1-1 draw

Harry Kane is expected to be sidelined for three months after suffering an ankle injury during Bayern Munich's Bundesliga game with Cologne on Friday.

Sky Germany report Bayern sporting director Max Eberl confirmed shortly after the 1-1 draw that Kane had suffered a "syndesmosis ligament tear" - also known as a high ankle sprain.

The Ballon d'Or winner - who became England's most-capped player last week - was seeking a first goal in five matches for the Bundesliga leaders.

However, as he looked to latch on to Michael Olise's deflected low cross at the back post, Cologne full-back Joel Schmied got to the ball first and clattered into Kane's left leg in his follow through.

Kane immediately clutched his lower leg and writhed in pain for a few moments before gingerly getting back to his feet, but he was a virtual bystander for the next couple of minutes.

The 33-year-old gestured to the dugout, sunk to the turf and then received treatment on his ankle before making way for Tom Bischof, limping off the field alongside two physios.

Kane had a quick exchange with Vincent Kompany, who was back in the Bayern hot-seat for the first time in six matches after being diagnosed with meningeal irritation last month, before heading down the tunnel.

To make matters worse for Bayern, Olise was also taken off injured at half-time.

A three-month period on the sidelines would rule Kane out for 11 Bundesliga games, the DFB-Pokal round of 16 tie against Hertha Berlin, as well as all four of Bayern's remaining Champions League league phase matches.

Although Downey Jr.'s iteration of the villain has already appeared in promo material for the highly anticipated crossover, one of the original designs offers a completely different take on the character.

Although the collectible is officially associated with Avengers: Doomsday, its appearance should not be seen as Doctor Doom's final costume in the movie. The action figure is specifically based on concept art instead of what the character will actually look like in the upcoming film.

This is not the first time concept art of Lord Doom has appeared in merchandise for Avengers: Doomsday. Hasbro previously released a Marvel Legends action figure featuring a similar design, as well as Funko! Pop's version of the character. Both toys were also described as being inspired by concept art from the film.

Where the 49ers need to improve defensively vs. Vikings

📅 Nov 20, 2026  |  Category: Sports  |  Read Source →

Last weekend was not a good day for the San Francisco 49ers defense, as they gave up 40 points on the Dallas Cowboys, yet still managed to come away with the 46-40 victory.

San Francisco's defense struggled on all fronts. The secondary especially had its issues, as Dallas's top three receivers all had quality games. George Pickens had 11 catches for 145 yards and three touchdowns, and that was with him dropping three passes. CeeDee Lamb had seven catches for 72 yards and a touchdown, while Ryan Flournoy had six catches for 71 yards and a touchdown.

Cornerbacks Deommodore Lenoir, Renardo Green, and Upton Stout entered the game banged up, and it showed, as both had their fair share of struggles against Dallas's elite offense. Of course, the matchup wasn't easy facing a high-caliber quarterback with two Pro Bowl receivers, but it was a rough day for a defense that had been quite good over the first half of the season.

That wasn't the only issue. The 49ers pass rush was also ineffective, failing to sack Dak Prescott, and Nick Bosa came out this week and took accountability for his play this season. Bosa has yet to record a sack, although his other numbers still indicate a productive season, especially with the double teams and chips he's facing.

Now, this week, the health is looking better. Stout remained limited in practice with his ongoing shoulder injury and is questionable, but Lenoir wasn't on the injury report and Renardo Green practiced in full on Thursday and is off the injury report for Sunday's game.

But the 49ers still have a tough challenge against the Minnesota Vikings, who put up 30 points on the Green Bay Packers in Kirk Cousins's return. Minnesota's offense suddenly looks formidable, and this battle should be a tough one on both sides.

After their struggles against Dallas, where do the 49ers need to improve defensively against Minnesota? Ahead of Sunday night, defensive coordinator Raheem Morris broke down the main issues he saw from the Cowboys game and what to take from it.

"Extending drives early with some of the penalties to get those guys to go down there, particularly on the first drive [was a problem]. We gave up three, I believe, on that first one," Morris said. "We got the big penalty to get those guys down there. They got a big-time field goal kicker. A couple of big plays in the first half. The two big plays were the ones that killed us there early. Got a pretty good grip on it after that and kind of held it down. It was at some point it was like 38-19. Then that finishing moments, we didn't do a very good job of getting the finishes off. Usually, the finishes happen when you're able to generate pressure with sacks or you're able to get interceptions.

"The Cowboys did a nice job, to give them credit, of getting the ball out of their hand. Did a nice job of getting the ball into their good players' hand, keeping drives going, extending drives. They had about three or four drives there where it was almost like overtime-like play for them, where it was going to be fourth down territory regardless and they were able to capitalize and go down and score on us. You would like to be able to stop some of those plays and be able to get those guys going and get off the grass at those moments and then that score wouldn't have got to where it was, but those things didn't happen.

"We didn't execute that way. And it's a collective group. I know [Nick] Bosa stood up in front of you guys and took all the credit and took all the accountability, but I really think it's just all of us. Some of it could be better play calls for them. Some of it could be better for us, what we can do for those guys. Some of it can be better plays made by us. Some of it, we just ran out a little bit of gas, got a little bit tired. Let's not kid ourselves. Human nature as well. So, fortunate to get out of there with a win. Definitely want to learn those lessons when you get a chance to do those things in those moments, when you get wins. Something that we can learn from and grow from and something that we definitely will."

One thing Morris did see from his team was accountability after the game, with the leaders on the team stepping up.

"They're such an accountable group," Morris added. "Most guys, you come in and you're just upset after a game. These guys, they hold themselves to such a high standard. They come in, they're able to speak very clearly right off the bat. So it's not going to be more vocal the next week because they were vocal the first week. Not necessarily Bosa. Bosa's kind of a to-himself guy, but for a guy like Fred [Warner], he's always going to speak up and do the things that happened. It wasn't like there was mistakes. They did some better job executing. There was a few mistakes. There's always going to be mistakes and there's always correcting mistakes. I'm not saying there's not mistakes, but there's always going to be correcting mistakes, but you correct those mistakes, you move on.

"There's some other things that, just you talking to as an individual player. The best thing about our guys, they always point at themselves first. Whether it's Fred, whether it's Bosa, whether it's DMo (Deommodore Lenoir), Renardo [Green], anybody on our football team. That's the cool part about being here with these guys is they point at themselves first. Then a lot of the times I'm able to say, 'Hey, I can help you here' or 'we can do these things better. We can make these calls. We can help you out, do a little bit better in these situations.' They are all so upfront and forthgoing with those things. That makes it really cool to talk to those guys that way."

Health seemed to be a factor last weekend, but the 49ers do need to be more disciplined and avoid the killer penalties (although some looked to be questionable calls).

But there is a lot at stake this weekend for the 49ers, as their No. 1 seed odds shift dramatically with a win or a loss. San Francisco's offense has always been up to the task, but they'll get their toughest matchup of the year with Brian Flores's defense. So, the 49ers defense will need to show up as well against this new-look Vikings offense with Kirk Cousins and execute on all three levels.

SpaceX's Starshield troubles in Ukraine could threaten its broader military business

📅 Nov 20, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

“Explain the changes that were made to the Starshield service in October 2026.”

The representative in the US House expected to chair the House Intelligence Committee in January, Jim Himes (D-Conn.), has threatened to withhold support for funding government-owned systems from SpaceX amid the ongoing inquiry into the use of Starshield in Ukraine.

“SpaceX’s potentially unilateral modification of a U.S. Government contract, combined with its lack of responsiveness to congressional oversight, calls into question every contract the U.S. Government has with SpaceX,” Himes wrote in a letter to SpaceX founder Elon Musk on Friday.

CNN first reported the letter’s existence. The network also reported that Himes sent a classified communication to Director of National Intelligence Jay Clayton with a similar message that it would be difficult for Congress to authorize funding for systems with SpaceX as the primary vendor.

SpaceX has developed the consumer Starlink service over the last decade, which provides high-speed Internet from low-Earth orbit to user terminals on Earth. For government customers, SpaceX has also developed Starshield, a suite of satellite services that includes a militarized version of Starlink communications. The US government has purchased Starshield services and extended access to Ukraine, although the terms governing that access are largely classified.

During the last several weeks, a controversy has erupted over interruptions to Starshield service in parts of Ukraine, cutting off connectivity to drones used to defend against Russian attacks.

On October 19, for example, Ukraine President Volodymyr Zelenskyy said of Starshield on social media, “Unfortunately, we have established that the equipment stops functioning at speeds of 130–161 kilometers per hour in various sectors of the front. This is slowing down our frontline logistics and limiting the use of various types of drones, particularly mid-strike drones and interceptors of jet-powered ‘shaheds.’” Additional disruptions were reported a week ago.

The central question is who decided to curtail the performance of Starshield assets in Ukraine. Accounts have variously implicated Musk, SpaceX, the Pentagon, and the White House. Musk has said that US officials control Starshield access, while defending SpaceX’s right to restrict the use of commercial Starlink service in war.

“My ask to you is straightforward,” Himes wrote to Musk. “Explain the changes that were made to the Starshield service in October 2026, why those changes were made, and whether those changes were made unilaterally by SpaceX or were directed by a U.S. Government official.”

The communications from Himes suggest that Congressional Democrats, who will control the House and Senate in January following decisive victories in the Midterm elections, are prepared to not just investigate the disruption of these SpaceX services in Ukraine, but to take action that could threaten SpaceX’s broader national security business.

“It is difficult for me to continue supporting the authorization of funding for these SpaceX-developed, government-owned systems, until I receive satisfactory answers to my questions about the Starshield service in Ukraine,” Himes wrote in his letter to Musk.

Various options are open to Congressional Democrats, as well as some Republicans who have raised similar concerns, including demanding classified briefings and documents and restricting funding through appropriations to the US Department of Defense and other federal departments. Notably, the US Space Force awarded SpaceX a $2.29 billion contract earlier this year to develop the Space Data Network Backbone intended to support the Golden Dome missile defense initiative. That program, along with SpaceX’s work building intelligence satellites for the National Reconnaissance Office, could become a focus of congressional scrutiny.

It seems unlikely that the US government would sever ties with SpaceX for space-based data services or launches, as the company is currently providing capabilities that no other US company can match. But Democrats are clearly serious about putting restrictions on Musk’s ability to influence geopolitical conflicts that have traditionally been the purview of governments rather than corporations.

New York’s Luxury Rental Market Sees Influx of Cash from AI Boom

📅 Nov 20, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

The artificial intelligence boom has expanded from the rolling hills of San Francisco and Silicon Valley to the busy streets of New York City, where an already tight luxury market is feeling the impact.

The AI wealth boom was in the spotlight this week, following Anthropic’s highly anticipated IPO and jaw dropping $1.494 trillion valuation. With the tech constantly growing and producing newly minted millionaires, the impacts of deep-pocketed AI professionals entering the New York real estate market are just beginning to be felt, agents there say.

There are more than 2,000 AI companies operating in New York City, according to the city’s Economic Development Corporation, and AI companies signed leases for about 1.5 million square feet in just the first six months of 2026, the Brooklyn Daily Eagle reported.

Major AI frontrunners like Anthropic, Palantir, OpenAI, and EliseAI have opened offices in New York, contributing to the thriving commercial real estate market.

“It’s been pretty unbelievable, what’s happening in the public sphere of the AI business. We’re seeing a ton of AI leasing activity on the ground in New York,” Evan Kleinberg, co-founder of Cornelia Street Partners, a boutique commercial real estate firm, told Realtor.com. “And it’s been happening so fast, it’s almost representative of the technology itself. The companies hire immediately and they need space yesterday.”

While Kleinberg has been keeping eyes on the AI industry for the past couple of years, his brokerage worked with its first AI specific company about 12 months ago, at the end of 2025. Since then, the brokerage has secured leases for five companies developing proprietary AI models.

There are a number of reasons Kleinberg sees AI companies expanding east and planting roots in New York. The city has fully rebounded from the pandemic and has an attractive lifestyle that draws many young professionals. Plus, companies see the benefit of being close to their portfolio businesses.

“Ultimately, if your goal is to raise money from Goldman Sachs or JP Morgan or one of the major private equity groups like Apollo, they’re all here,” Kleinberg explained. “So wanting to get close to them is an underlying reason to move.”

With these new offices comes a hoard of young, newly wealthy professionals who are looking to build a home in New York, many of whom are stepping into an already fiercely competitive luxury real estate market.

“Wall Street has historically been a major driver of Manhattan luxury real estate, and now we’re really starting to see the effects of AI. It’s becoming an important engine for wealth creation and housing demand,” Michelle Griffith, a residential real estate agent in New York City, told Realtor.com. “Right now in Manhattan, we are already in a very tight rental market. Even for the last year, rents are up anywhere from 10% to 20% in Manhattan, especially for these luxury properties.”

As of November, the median sold price of a home in Manhattan is $1,150,000, a 15% year over year increase. Some Manhattan neighborhoods have had more extreme changes.

The Lower East Side, for example, saw the median sale price jump 28% year over year to $1,435,000. Rent in the Lower East Side also increased 11% year over year, for a median of $5,947 per month.

The New York City luxury residential landscape is no stranger to competition, with deep pocketed masses vying for a severely limited inventory of spaces. With the flood of AI professionals, another layer of people has been added to the market.

Griffith recalled a recent listing at 20 Gramercy Park North, which had historically rented for $30,000. While a rental like this, which is in the higher end of the market, would previously take 30 to 60 days to identify a tenant, the listing had eight rental bids after 48 hours on the market, with half above asking.

Most workers are determined to live close to work, making it especially challenging to secure an apartment in these highly sought after neighborhoods.

OpenAI’s 90,000-square-foot New York office is located in the Puck Building, which sits on the boarder of SoHo and Nolita in Manhattan.

Anthropic’s New York hub takes up the entirety of a 16-story building at 330 Hudson Street in the Hudson Square neighborhood of Lower Manhattan.

“The offices are in fantastic locations, so it’s easy to find great housing within walking distance,” said Griffith. “The challenge is finding that apartment because there is very, very limited inventory because those neighborhoods were already popular and in demand.”

For luxury agents, the challenge is no longer finding tenants with the money to rent high priced rentals, its finding the rentals for deep pocketed tenants.

When Griffith began in real estate 15 years ago, an apartment for $100,000 per month might be the biggest rental of the year. Now, rentals for $80,000 to even $150,000 are common and moving quickly, she shared.
