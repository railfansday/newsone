Prime Video's 'The Rings of Power' Is Making a Truly Unprecedented Move With Season 3

📅 Nov 21, 2026  |  Category: Entertainment  |  Read Source →

Editor's note: The below contains spoilers for The Rings of Power Season 3.

The Lord of the Rings: The Rings of Power is one of Prime Video's biggest shows, thanks to its massive budget and sprawling world, but the fantasy series is only getting bigger. The show has already featured several cinematic moments, like the eruption of Mount Doom or the Sack of Eregion, and Season 3 promises to take things to a new level. Prime Video is releasing Episode 7 in theaters, allowing fans to see the story in a new way. It's a bold step for the streamer, but it could be a major moment for The Rings of Power.

Season 3's penultimate episode will depict an important event in Middle-earth's history, adding to the significance of The Rings of Power's first theatrical release. In addition to being the first episode on the big screen, Episode 7 will come to theaters early, on November 23, raising expectations. However, fans who can't make it to theaters will still get to watch the conclusion sooner than expected, as Prime Video will make both Episode 7 and Episode 8, the finale, available for streaming on Tuesday the 24th rather than following the season's original Wednesday schedule.

The Rings of Power Season 3 has a lot going on: Gandalf (Daniel Weyman) is in Rhûn trying to save the Dark Wizard (Ciaran Hinds), Elrond (Robert Aramayo) is helping to resolve the civil war in Númenor, the Elves are in open war against Sauron (Charlie Vickers), and Durin (Owain Arthur) is fighting his own brother. Yet Episode 6 tees up a major discovery for Sauron that promises to change Middle-earth entirely. Episode 7's theatrical screenings have left little doubt about what the plot will feature, and the creation of the infamous One Ring deserves a bigger viewing experience. Fans have been waiting for this, as Season 2 left only one ring yet to be made, and Sauron has been slowly working toward achieving that goal.

While Sauron's success is inevitable, informing Frodo's story hundreds of years later, accomplishing the creation of the One Ring hasn't been an easy process. In Episode 6, Sauron tricks Celeborn (Jamie Campbell Bower) into revealing the secret of the Elven Rings' craftsmanship by impersonating Galadriel (Morfydd Clark), setting up the forging of the One Ring. Despite its significance, the moment it was made has never been depicted onscreen before, making Episode 7 the perfect choice for a theatrical release.

Many episodes of The Rings of Power would be a spectacle on the big screen, but Season 3's penultimate hour deserves this opportunity. Prime Video gave The Boys' series finale a similar treatment earlier this year, and other popular series, like Stranger Things, have done the same. However, Prime Video is taking a slightly different approach with The Rings of Power by choosing not to release the Season 3 finale in theaters. The Rings of Power is also set to run for two more seasons, giving it even more time to grow beyond this crucial Middle-earth moment.

Prime Video's choice capitalizes on the franchise's name recognition, especially with the story now reaching this landmark scene. Between J. R. R. Tolkien's books and Peter Jackson's beloved film franchise, virtually every fantasy fan knows about the One Ring, but, more importantly, The Rings of Power is making a deliberate choice to prioritize Season 3's penultimate episode based on its story importance. The best episodes of many epic shows aren't their finales, and the most action-packed episodes are the ones that deserve a theatrical experience. As TV series grow, there is something to be said for releasing them in theaters, and now is the perfect moment for The Rings of Power to make its way to the big screen.

Retired FBI agent warns NCIC breach could threaten police safety during traffic stops

📅 Nov 21, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Retired FBI Supervisory Special Agent Jason Pack breaks down what sits inside America’s law enforcement computers, how hackers get in and how to protect your data.

A retired FBI supervisory special agent warned Saturday that a cyber intrusion into the bureau's National Crime Information Center (NCIC) could endanger police officers during traffic stops if hackers altered records inside the database.

Jason Pack, a former FBI supervisory special agent who worked in cybercrime and public information, told Fox News Digital that the bureau's incident response team must quickly determine whether data was manipulated inside the system, which feeds criminal records to patrol cars nationwide.

"What they worry about is if any of this data has changed, then it might affect officers' safety on the side of the road," Pack said, noting there is no indication yet that any records were altered or that the system is experiencing an outage.

FBI PROBES SECOND CYBERATTACK IN MONTHS AS HACKERS BREACH KEY CRIME DATABASE, SOURCES SAY

Hackers have targeted the FBI for the second time in recent months. (Kevin Lamarque/Reuters, File)

Three sources familiar with the matter told Fox News that hackers breached the NCIC, marking the second cyberattack against the bureau in months. The FBI is investigating how the intruders gained entry and whether any information was compromised.

In an interview with Fox News' Kayleigh McEnany on Saturday morning, President Donald Trump said he had "just heard" about the intrusion, adding, "I will say that it’s not good. I don’t like that."

Pack described the NCIC as "America's computer for law enforcement," explaining that roughly 56 state terminals feed data out to hundreds of thousands of patrol car computers, so officers can check warrants, criminal histories, missing children, gang members and suspected terrorists.

FBI SAYS SOURCE OF ITS JOBS PORTAL BREACH STILL UNKNOWN AS HACKERS ALLEGE EMPLOYEE DATA COMPROMISED

While the database does not hold classified intelligence or sensitive FBI investigative case files, Pack said it stores personally identifiable information, including names, dates of birth and Social Security numbers.

Investigators will examine IP logs and metadata to determine whether attackers exploited a compromised password among the system's more than 100,000 users, breached a state terminal or infiltrated through a third-party vendor, Pack said, adding that cyberattacks have accelerated to "machine speed" as criminals increasingly use artificial intelligence to probe for vulnerabilities.

"You have this cat and mouse game where cybersecurity experts are trying to patch the vulnerabilities that they find. They're looking for their own vulnerabilities at the same time, they have to play defense against the criminals who are out there trying to steal information or do harm to our systems. I think we're just seeing now this speed of these intrusions ramp up a lot," Pack said.

TECH OUTAGE CASCADE EXPOSES US VULNERABILITIES TO FOREIGN CYBER THREATS

A cyber intrusion into the FBI's National Crime Information Center (NCIC) could endanger police officers during traffic stops, a retired agent says. (Getty Images, File)

The intrusion follows a September cyberattack in which the extortion group ShinyHunters breached an FBI job portal website and claimed to steal personal records belonging to bureau employees.

The latest incident also comes as a Government Accountability Office (GAO) report found 17 of 22 government agencies were not in compliance with federal cybersecurity regulations.

The FBI did not immediately respond to a request for comment on the NCIC breach.

Fox News Digital's James Cirrone and Greg Norman-Diamond, and Fox News' David Spunt contributed to this report.

5 Actors Who Should Play M In Robert Pattinson's James Bond Movies

📅 Nov 21, 2026  |  Category: Entertainment  |  Read Source →

The next era of the "James Bond" film franchise is finally taking shape. After years of wondering who will be suiting up as our new 007, Robert Pattinson has officially been cast as the next James Bond, taking over for Daniel Craig following his final entry with "No Time to Die," which came out way back in 2021. A lot has happened since then, including Amazon buying the rights to the property. Now, with a new lead actor in place, it's time to get the rest of the pieces of the puzzle locked in. That includes finding Bond's boss.

Historically, this has been the one they call M. Originally portrayed by Bernard Lee in the earliest Bond films, the codename given to the person behind the big desk at MI6 is an important one, as they head up the 00 program. Thus, M is the one who gives Bond his assignments and, at times, even helps him out in the field. It's an important role, one that has been played by the likes of Dame Judi Dench and Oscar-nominee Ralph Fiennes.

There's much to be done. Director Denis Villeneuve also has to find an actor to play the villain in Pattinson's "James Bond" movie, but M is a longer commitment, a multi-picture deal. It's a big responsibility. So, who should play the role alongside Pattinson? Who should be the one to help guide 007 through his next several missions for her majesty's secret service?

We're going to go over a handful of options, ranging from very obvious to unlikely yet intriguing. Let's get into it.

"The amount of time I have wanted to be M. I'm not sure who I need to call..."

Those are the words of Olivia Colman speaking to British Vogue in 2023. She wants the role. She's very well qualified for the role. If everyone wants to make this easy, why not just offer her the role? Sometimes, the easiest answer is also a very good answer. That not every actor who campaigns openly for a role should get it, but we're not just talking about any old actor here.

Colman is responsible for one of the most shocking Oscars upsets of all time when she took home Best Actress for "The Favourite." But it was more shocking because the Academy recognized Colman's impressive work over the rest of the pack. Ever since, she's been on a tear, turning in outstanding performances in everything from "The Lost Daughter" to "Paddington in Peru." Heck, she even ate up some screen time in Marvel's otherwise very disappointing series "Secret Invasion."

But Colman has been doing it at a very high level for years, long before she won an Oscar. "Hot Fuzz," "The Iron Lady," "Broadchurch." You name it, Colman is one of the best parts of everything she's in. Beyond that, she could bring both a seriousness to the role of M, which is required, while at the same time allowing for a playful dynamic with Bond. Colman feels like a slam dunk. It just so happens that the person campaigning for the role is very much right for the role. That's not always the case.

If Denis Villeneuve or Amazon want to go with a distinguished, male British actor who could be older and wiser than Robert Pattinson's Bond, one capable of being both convincingly stern and charming, Colin Firth is the man. An Oscar-winning actor who is almost too easy to picture sitting at a big desk behind a padded burgundy door, this is another "the obvious choice can also be the right choice" pick.

We have an actor here capable of delivering exposition, as M often has to, in a way that would be entertaining, as Firth could read the phone book and it would be engaging. But let's not forget that Firth politely destroyed fools in "Kingsman: The Secret Service," too, and that movie owes a lot to the "James Bond" franchise. What's more, it proved that Firth looks absolutely fantastic in an expensive suit, an important quality of a good M.

His resume speaks for itself. "A Single Man," "The King's Speech," and even "Mamma Mia!" prove that Firth is one of the finest actors of the last 30 years. There's zero question as to whether or not he's qualified to be M. So, it begs the question: Is he right for M? If we're looking to address that question in the traditional sense, then the answer is a resounding yes. The shoe fits and it fits well. Simple as that.

If it weren't for Judi Dench playing M so damn well for so damn long, we might have to have the utterly irritating "let's gender-swap M" conversation. Fortunately, that's not the world we live in. Bearing that in mind, it's going to be damn hard for anyone to argue against Tilda Swinton bossing around Robert Pattinson while dressed to the nines. What's not to like about that?

Swinton won a Best Supporting Actress Oscar for "Michael Clayton" so, much like many of the other performers on that list, she's Oscar-caliber. That almost feels like a must for M. Setting that aside, Swinton brings a domineering, powerful quality to so many of her best roles. Even so, she's a bit of a chameleon, which is partially what would make her an interesting M. It's not hard to imagine her playing an MI6 agent who used to work in the field before taking a desk job, yet she still has that darkness behind her eyes from yesteryear.

Oddly, it's Swinton's transformation into the Ancient One" in "Doctor Strange" that might make the best case here. She can do powerful yet quiet without having to be at the center of the action, and she can absolutely handle being a part of a giant, globally popular franchise. Really, there's nothing this role requires that she couldn't do, seemingly with ease.

Some might argue Swinton would be better as a "Bond" villain. That certainly wouldn't upset me, but it might be subverting expectations a bit to have her as Bond's buttoned-up boss. She could also help bring something fresh to the role for this new era.

And now we arrive at the most obvious answer. Does that make it the wrong answer? Using history as our guide, absolutely not. Ralph Fiennes made his entry into the "James Bond" franchise in 2012's "Skyfall" before fully taking over as M in "Spectre" following the death of Judi Dench's M. (May she rest in peace.) So, why replace a perfectly great actor in the role if we don't have to?

Let's not forget that the "James Bond" franchise is notorious for bringing actors back in roles even after reboots. Dench as M for both Pierce Brosnan and Daniel Craig's Bonds. Desmond Llewelyn was Q for every Bond except Craig. That's the nature of the beast as it exists. If Amazon doesn't totally buck tradition, it could easily bring back Fiennes for another go-around. And why not? He's already proven he can do it, he's a stellar actor, and he's arguably as popular as ever.

He's also an actor who seems to really understand the role. "Spectre" was going to make M the villain until Finnes refused. That, to this longtime fan, demonstrates a real understanding of who M is. On top of that, Fiennes is very familiar with shouldering the weight of a major movie franchise, having previously played Voldemort in the "Harry Potter" movies. The only reason to not do this is if Amazon and Denis Villeneuve truly and completely want to hit the big red reset button, which frankly seems likely.

But if not? The debate could be settled before it even gets started.

Last, but certainly not least, we arrive at the choice that's both decidedly out-of-the-box yet technically in the very box we're talking about. Pierce Brosnan previously played James Bond in four movies stretching from 1995 to 2002. His "GoldenEye" remains the most important box office hit in "James Bond" history, in addition to being a beloved entry in the franchise. But Brosnan's run was uneven and ended on a sour note with "Die Another Day." Perhaps playing M would give him a shot at redemption?

Let's get some things out of the way up top. Yes, it might be a little weird to have a former 007 play M, but it's not that much stranger than fully ignoring continuity in order to have the same actor play M for multiple Bonds. More importantly, Brosnan absolutely looks the part. The man, well into his 70s, still looks better in a suit than most of us could ever hope to. He's got an authoritative on-screen presence, but he's also got that charm that could work wonders for M.

Brosnan feels he was never good enough as James Bond. I would humbly argue that the scripts weren't always good enough for his James Bond, not the other way around. Why not use the past to help build a future for the franchise? Let Brosnan bring his experience to the role and mentor Robert Pattinson's 007. The meta nature of it all might add something to their dynamic.

The question of whether or not Brosnan would want to take on the role is another one entirely, but it's a wild card idea that's just inspired enough to be a whole lot of fun.

The new "James Bond" movie doesn't have a release date, but stay tuned.

Customers had authorized the prompts for routine software testing, and Together AI stored the samples in a private Hugging Face repository. They involved requests to the MiniMax M3, GLM-5.2 and Kimi K2.6 models, primarily on June 24, 2026, July 4, 2026, July 8, 2026 and July 14, 2026.

The coding agent committed a credential that granted access to the private Hugging Face repository during xorl’s development. Together AI’s automated scanning detected the exposure, and the company reverted the commit. During that exposure window, an unauthorized party obtained the credential and used it to download the stored samples.

The sequence makes clear that detecting and reverting the commit did not prevent the credential from being used during the exposure window. The model names establish which requests were sampled, while the repository credential explains how the stored prompts became accessible.

After the download, the party searched the samples for credentials to other services and tested whether they were valid. Five of the tested credentials were canary credentials associated with a Dragos security experiment. The searches and validity tests show that the activity extended to possible reuse of secrets contained in prompts, creating a risk beyond disclosure of the prompt text itself.

Together AI worked with an external forensic investigation firm and law enforcement. The company said its review has not identified evidence of additional prompt leakage beyond this incident, that it has no reason to believe the data was leaked further, and that it understands the data has now been deleted.

Customer impact still depends on the content of the samples. The review is ongoing to determine whether sensitive personal information or other customer data was exposed, and Together AI will contact affected customers directly as needed with findings relevant to them.

Zero data retention, abbreviated ZDR, is now the default setting for new accounts. That’s what Elon Musk said about the idea to merge Tesla and SpaceX, back in October 2026.

Since then, however, it would seem that this somewhat fanciful idea has only accelerated. Over the last few weeks alone, both Tesla and SpaceX have constituted special exploratory committees consisting of independent directors.

Will it go through? Probably, yes. After all, despite the CEO of SpaceX and Tesla claiming that he is a “conflicted party,” his companies’ shareholders have often acceded to his wishes. That’s an opinion Walter Isaacson, author of “Elon Musk,” shares too.

Especially after the entrepreneur made his case by stating,

…there’s a lot of merit to combining the companies as we are pretty much in the Super Intelligence singularity.

Needless to say, such a merger will have some legal, regulatory, and financial implications. But what about the crypto sector?

Tesla, Inc., the automotive and clean energy company, holds 11,509 BTC valued at over $747 million right now. Similarly, SpaceX, the aerospace manufacturer, holds 18,712 BTC worth over $1.22 billion.

In simple terms, the merger will simply be a transaction that pools in digital assets owned by both these entities into a single balance sheet. This consolidation would allow the merged entity to hold 30,221 BTC in reserves.

Those are significant numbers. However, here’s where it’s also worth looking at the context and scale and the bigger picture.

Neither Tesla nor SpaceX are crypto companies. They are primarily tech manufacturing companies with valuations of $1.54 trillion and $1.89 trillion, respectively.

Yes, they have crypto holdings, but they aren’t significant enough for each individual entity to suggest that these are more than a passing interest and a diversification opportunity.

To put it in financial terms, for both Tesla and SpaceX, Bitcoin is a non-core balance sheet asset. For them, their BTC holdings are financially immaterial to their core CapEx. As Dr. Andrei Belonogov, Managing Partner at TechAccountingPro pointed out,

The size of treasuries is relatively small compared to the company’s debt maturities and non-cancellable commitments.

This will remain the case if the merger does go through. If and when it happens, the merged companies’ valuation would dwarf the value of its crypto holdings.

What about after, though? This is an important question because more often than not, non-core balance sheet assets go through a period of evaluation and restructuring and sometimes, disposal too to streamline operations and facilitate profitability.

Could this happen to the aforementioned Bitcoin holdings then? Well, that’s always a possibility. However, recent history suggests the odds are low.

Consider the purchase and sale history of both these companies, for instance. The last time Tesla sold any of its Bitcoin holdings was back in 2022. Since then, it has bought additional BTC while also holding on to the stash it already had.

Similarly, SpaceX last sold any of its holdings back in 2024. In the two years since, the aerospace manufacturer has only bought Bitcoin. Specifically, over 10K BTC in May 2026.

Now, it’s up for debate how much input the “conflicted party,” Musk, had in any of these sales or purchases. However, it is safe to assume that, given the consistencies between the two boardrooms so far, that will remain the case after the merger too.

The merger doesn’t necessarily mean a sell-off or any spike in price. What it will do, however, is put a roughly $2bn position into a single company’s balance sheet. Bitcoin can be especially volatile, so that may make some investors seasick

Will the merged entity enjoy the same degree of flexibility Tesla and SpaceX enjoyed individually when it comes to crypto sales and purchases though? According to Kevin Constantine, Senior Associate at Caldwell Law,

Protections could become relevant to cryptocurrency holdings, particularly if management’s ability to purchase, liquidate, or transfer Bitcoin raised concerns about the companies’ valuations or the treatment of shareholders.

The fact that both Tesla and SpaceX refused to sell their Bitcoin in 2026, a year that has been particularly bearish for the crypto-sector, is testament to the confidence they have in this asset. It is also a sign of institutional conviction.

After the merger, the merged entity will have the potential to be the sixth-largest corporate Bitcoin holder in the world. Only behind crypto-native firms or treasury-focused players like MicroStrategy and Metaplanet. All while Bitcoin remains its non-core balance sheet asset.

That, more than anything, will be confirmation of Bitcoin’s value as a corporate treasury asset. That might not sound like much on paper, but that is still VERY bullish news.

Avery Fisher of Delphi, Indiana, and his best friend since high school, Nathan Van Dokkenburg, put in for an Indiana parks draw hunt on the recommendation of a buddy. Both drew the two-day Nov. 16-17 firearms season.

Indiana has special limited-draw, short-term deer hunts on about 25 public parks around the state. The hunts help manage the burgeoning deer herds, and most allow sportsmen to take three deer per hunt, with a limit of one buck. The number of hunters drawn for each park varies, but about 150 hunters are typically allowed on a park for each two-day hunt.

Neither Fisher nor Van Dokkenburg had ever walked the park they drew to hunt, which they declined to name. But they spent a lot of time figuring out where they should be on the property well before the hunt.

“We studied a lot of Google Maps and onX to get an idea where we should hunt far back in the park well away from other hunters,” Fisher tells Outdoor Life. “It’s rolling hills with big creek drains and draws … I’ve hunted that kind of land previously, and I had a good idea where to go early that Nov. 16 opening morning.”

The hunters checked in when the park opened at 5 a.m. and were prepared for an all day sit with packs, gear, and food.

“We got far back into the park, about one and a half miles from the road,” says Fisher, 25, who works as a financial advisor. “We stayed on ridgetops, and about one mile in, Nathan and I split up.

Fisher went about another half mile, hiking by the light of his headlamp.

“In the dark I spotted a tree line that had a lot of buck rubs. Then I saw a doe, and I figured it was a pretty good place.”

He climbed about 30 feet off the ground and settled into his saddle with his coffee and snacks his favorite .44-cal Ruger, a gift from his father years ago.

It was cold early that morning, and he heard something in a nearby ravine around 7:45 a.m.

“I thought I saw a buck and put my binoculars on it, and it was one with a good spread and nice tines about 100 yards away.”

He raised his rifle and rested it against the tree for support while looking for the buck in his scope.

“But my breath kept fogging the scope,” he says. “I kept cleaning it with my gloves, and the deer kept getting closer … I tried not looking at his rack as he turned uphill and then I saw his sticker points as he was rubbing trees.”

Fisher found the shoulder in his scope and squeezed the trigger. The buck ran uphill past Fisher, wobbling as it went. When the buck stopped at 60 yards, Fisher shot again. The buck dropped.

Fisher texted Van Dokkenburg and told him he’d shot a nice buck, but to keep hunting because they were allowed to shoot up to three deer apiece.

“A group of does came in at about 9 a.m., and I shot one and it fell,” Fisher says. “I decided not to shoot any more deer that morning.

“We had to bone and pack out two deer like we were on a Western hunt. I figured that was enough for us to lug out of there that day.”

Van Dokkenburg saw nothing to shoot and met up with Fisher about 11 a.m., filming Fisher as he first walked up to his buck and realized how huge it was.

“I was speechless at the size of his rack,” says Fisher. “I knew he was big, but not a rack like that. I’m grateful and thankful to the Lord.”

Using a wheeled cart and backpacks, the two hunters hauled both deer back down to their vehicle.

“I think my pack with deer and gear weighed over 100 pounds, and I had to stop and offload more from my pack to the wheeled cart,” Fisher says.

They then drove to check in their deer with park personnel.

Fisher’s 13-point buck has been measured with an unofficial green score of 199 5/8 inches. It turned out Fisher’s second shot had been unnecessary, since his first had struck the heart.

Il futuro di Alessandro Bastoni continua a far discutere, soprattutto in Spagna. Il difensore dell'Inter rimane uno dei principali obiettivi del Barcellona, ma il club blaugrana dovrà fare i conti con una concorrente particolarmente agguerrita: il Chelsea di Xabi Alonso, pronto a inserirsi nella corsa al centrale della Nazionale italiana.

Secondo quanto riportato dal quotidiano catalano Sport, nelle ultime ore sarebbero emerse nuove indiscrezioni anche sul fronte del rinnovo contrattuale. Un recente incontro tra l'entourage del giocatore e l'Inter non avrebbe infatti prodotto i risultati sperati.

Bastoni, 27 anni, è legato ai nerazzurri fino al 30 giugno 2028. La società vorrebbe prolungare l'accordo prima che il difensore entri nell'ultimo anno di contratto, ma la situazione rimane aperta e il mercato internazionale continua a osservare gli sviluppi.

Xabi Alonso vorrebbe rinforzare la propria difesa con un centrale mancino di altissimo livello e Bastoni risponderebbe perfettamente alle caratteristiche ricercate dal tecnico spagnolo.

Il difensore dell'Inter garantisce qualità nell'impostazione dal basso, forza fisica, esperienza internazionale e una particolare abilità nel giocare sul centrosinistra di una difesa a tre.

Caratteristiche che lo rendono uno dei giocatori più apprezzati sul mercato europeo.

La disponibilità economica del Chelsea rappresenterebbe un ostacolo importante per il Barcellona, che rischierebbe di trovarsi coinvolto in un'asta particolarmente costosa.

Transfermarkt attribuisce a Bastoni una valutazione di circa 65 milioni di euro, ma la cifra rappresenta una stima di mercato, non il prezzo fissato dall'Inter. L'eventuale concorrenza tra più società potrebbe far aumentare ulteriormente il costo dell'operazione.

Secondo la ricostruzione del quotidiano spagnolo, infatti, il Chelsea avrebbe maggiori possibilità di competere sul piano economico, mentre il Barça dovrebbe valorizzare soprattutto le prospettive sportive e il ruolo che il giocatore potrebbe ricoprire all'interno della squadra.

Il club catalano considera particolarmente preziosa la capacità di Bastoni di costruire l'azione partendo dalla difesa, una qualità fondamentale per il proprio sistema di gioco.

La possibilità di trasferirsi a Barcellona sarebbe quindi legata anche alla convinzione del giocatore di poter continuare il proprio percorso di crescita ai massimi livelli.

Secondo Sport, il recente incontro tra i rappresentanti di Bastoni e la società nerazzurra non avrebbe portato a un avvicinamento significativo.

Il quotidiano spagnolo prospetta quindi uno scenario nel quale, in assenza di rinnovo, l'Inter potrebbe valutare eventuali offerte durante il prossimo mercato estivo.
