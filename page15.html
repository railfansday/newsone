What View Homes’ rebrand says about Gandhi’s operating overhaul

📅 Nov 09, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

View Homes is consolidating its regional brands into a single identity as CEO Natasha Gandhi works to standardize operating practices and data systems. The company is expanding in Colorado and assessing how Hunt’s new Strata platform could support land strategy.  AI Summary

Seven months into her effort to transform View Homes from a loose collection of regional builders into a cohesive enterprise, CEO Natasha Gandhi has drawn a line between the company’s past and its ambitions.

A newly unified brand makes that promise visible. Now Gandhi and her team must overdeliver on it.

View Homes announced Monday a new corporate brand identity, unifying its formerly distinct Aspen View, Desert View, Horizon View and View Homes operations under one name. The change spans five divisions across Colorado, New Mexico and Texas and marks a milestone in the operational transformation Gandhi began after becoming CEO in March 2026.

The announcement also comes one week after View’s parent, Hunt Companies, joined with global investment manager Wafra to launch Strata Land Partners, a new land-financing platform led by former Landsea Homes president Michael Forsum. Together, these developments offer a clearer view of Hunt’s expanding ambitions in U.S. residential construction and development.

In an exclusive interview with HousingWire TBD, Gandhi said the branding initiative is part of a broader effort to prepare View for growth, strengthen operating standards, and compete across more markets and homebuyer segments.

“We want to be a premium builder, and by premium, it’s not that we’re unaffordable,” Gandhi said. “It just means that we want to be the best in every price point that we’re out there, we want to be the best in every market that we build in.”

Hunt acquired full ownership of View Homes in 2025, inheriting an established 35-year-old homebuilding business whose regional operations had developed distinct identities, systems and operating practices.

When Gandhi arrived in March, she found an organization with strong local relationships and experienced teams, but lacking the consistent management processes, reporting systems, and enterprise infrastructure needed to support the growth Hunt envisioned.

As The Builder’s Daily reported in October, Gandhi’s initial work focused on leadership changes, clearer accountability, more disciplined sales concessions, selective land investment, and a comprehensive review of enterprise technology.

The new branding establishes four standards intended to apply across the organization: exceptional customer experience, people-first leadership, design-forward homes, and premium quality across price points.

“I felt like previously, we had different standards in different markets, but now with this new brand, it really sets the stage,” Gandhi said. “These four pillars are something that is now going to be operationally executed in all our markets.”

The challenge is to translate those commitments into consistent performance without compromising the local knowledge and customer relationships that have made View’s regional businesses successful.

For a multiregional homebuilder, Gandhi notes, that means more than consistent signage and marketing. It requires shared expectations for product quality, customer service, sales execution, and management accountability, supported by reliable information and operating systems.

Gandhi is candid that the transformation remains unfinished. “This is just the start of our growth journey,” she said. “I will tell you, we’re not there yet, but we’re on the path of getting there.”

The branding initiative has prompted further scrutiny of the operational capabilities needed to support the company’s ambitions to up its game. Gandhi acknowledged that View’s enterprise resource planning systems and website are not yet up to par.

A builder seeking to expand across markets needs timely, consistent information on sales, margins, construction costs, inventory, and land commitments. Without it, management cannot easily compare performance across divisions or make informed decisions about capital allocation.

Gandhi previously outlined a 12-to-18-month enterprise technology implementation, along with efforts to standardize sales concessions and improve the quality of operating data. Those initiatives remain important to the company’s ability to scale.

View has also raised the bar in its home merchandising across markets and is developing products designed to reach beyond its traditional entry-level customer base. The objective is to preserve affordability where that remains the market opportunity while broadening View’s appeal to move-up buyers and customers seeking greater design and product differentiation.

The transformation is unfolding amid difficult housing economics. Slower sales, persistent affordability pressures, and costly incentives continue to strain builder margins, while land acquisition and development require substantial capital commitments long before homes generate revenue.

Hunt’s ownership gives View access to a larger real estate investment organization and a longer-term investment perspective. Its recent partnership with Wafra to establish Strata Land Partners adds another potentially valuable capability.

Strata launched with projects comprising approximately 1,300 single-family lots and a mandate to provide flexible land-financing solutions for public and private homebuilders and developers nationwide. Forsum has described the platform as a capital partner “built by builders, for builders,” emphasizing the need to understand the operational risks and financial requirements of homebuilding businesses.

Gandhi confirmed that View and Strata are already discussing opportunities to work together.

“We are actually already exploring opportunities to where Strata and [View] can partner together,” she said. “There’s definitely opportunity and conversations already in place.”

She did not identify specific transactions or financing structures under review.

Still, the potential relationship illustrates how Hunt’s residential investments could create complementary advantages. View brings homebuilding operations, local market relationships, and demand for future lots. Strata brings a platform designed to finance land acquisition and development while managing builders’ capital exposure.

Whether those capabilities translate into direct financial or operational advantages for View will depend on the transactions and terms the companies ultimately pursue. Strata’s mandate extends well beyond Hunt-owned businesses.

Gandhi said she has assembled the leadership team she believes can execute View’s next phase, including experienced executives recruited during the restructuring process.

“It’s your people that get you the results,” she said. “It’s your people that help you navigate a tough market.”

That leadership investment comes as View prepares to expand into Sterling Ranch, a master-planned community in Douglas County, Colorado, near Denver.

View currently operates in Northern Colorado, Colorado Springs, and Las Cruces, El Paso, and San Antonio. Sterling Ranch gives the company an opportunity to establish a stronger presence in the Denver metropolitan market, where Gandhi sees additional growth opportunities.

She said View is close to completing other land transactions in the Denver area and is pursuing opportunities in additional master-planned communities.

The expansion also offers an early test of the unified brand’s positioning. View intends to compete as a premium regional builder while maintaining a product range that serves buyers across price points.

Gandhi’s transformation is underway as many homebuilders protect cash, reduce land commitments, and prepare for the possibility that difficult conditions could persist through 2027 and 2028. View must balance those immediate pressures with Hunt’s longer-term objective of building a substantially larger homebuilding enterprise.

The company is not simply adding communities to its existing business. Gandhi is working to establish the leadership, systems, product standards, and capital discipline needed to support growth without replicating the fragmented operating structure she inherited.

“My goal, Hunt’s goal, is for View to grow into a premium regional builder,” Gandhi said. “I would like to see View be among the top 50, top 25 home builders in the next five years.”

Achieving that scale will require more than additional land and investment capital. View must demonstrate that its five divisions can operate consistently, protect margins amid changing market conditions, and deliver – or exceed – the customer experience its new brand promises.

On Sunday, President Donald Trump indicated that he is open to negotiating an immigration deal with the incoming Democratic Congress. However, despite softening post-election rhetoric, the Trump administration appears to be sending mixed signals on immigration, clouding the outlook for any potential bipartisan agreement.

The stakes of the nation’s highly contentious immigration debate remain high for homebuilders. After a surge in job site raids by U.S. Immigration and Customs Enforcement (ICE) this summer and fall, many homebuilders say their workers, including those in the country legally, were swept up in enforcement actions.

Whether a bipartisan immigration deal materializes remains an open and pressing question for the numerous builders facing ongoing job site disruptions.

Speaking to CBS News’ 60 Minutes on Sunday, President Trump suggested his administration’s immigration policies “might be too tough” and may have contributed to last week’s election outcome.

“When you go into a hotel…and you have a woman that’s worked there as a maid, as a cleaner, as a this, as a that, as a waitress – a lot of good things, great jobs – and all of a sudden, they’re being taken out of the country. It’s tough. It’s a tough thing,” Trump said.

Trump added that it may be “doable” to reach an agreement allowing undocumented workers who have lived in the U.S. for many years, construction workers among them, to legally remain in the country.

“Get the killers out. Get the rapists out. Get all the rough ones out. Get the drug dealers out, but let the people that are here, and in some cases here for 10 and 15 years, work a deal for them,” Trump suggested.

It’s unclear whether Trump’s softer rhetoric will translate into a bipartisan immigration deal. However, his administration’s decision to promote Stephen Miller, a longtime immigration hardliner, to White House Chief of Staff suggests an agreement may be less likely than Trump implied in his CBS News interview.

And in a post on Sunday, the Official White House Rapid Response account, in a post on X, denied that Trump will push for an amnesty deal with Democrats.

However, Trump has at times shown a willingness to soften his stance on immigration, at least briefly. In June 2025, the Trump administration momentarily directed ICE to pause job site raids in sectors like agriculture, hospitality and restaurants after facing blowback from business leaders worried about staffing shortages.

Within days, however, the administration reversed course. It later set a target of 3,000 ICE arrests per day, leading to a record number of immigration arrests in recent months.

Trump has also helped kill a bipartisan deal before. In early 2024, ahead of the presidential election, he urged Republicans to vote against a Senate border package introduced by Sen. James Lankford (R-OK) that would have tightened asylum requirements, ended releases of migrants into U.S. communities while they await hearings, and let the president close the border when asylum claims surged. The bill ultimately failed.

Some homebuilding organizations, like the National Association of Home Builders, now have their eyes on a different bipartisan measure, the Dignity Act, which would allow certain undocumented workers to remain in the U.S. without citizenship. Trump’s words signaled openness to such a proposal, but the administration’s actions since the election suggest otherwise.

Vice President JD Vance, meanwhile, laid out in a post-midterms election post on X that the administration may be open to changing its approach to immigration, with less room for negotiation.

“Expect to hear any number of establishment politicians say the solution to our woes is amnesty. I’m open to the idea that we should change enforcement – by focusing more on businesses that hire illegal labor, for instance. But anyone pushing amnesty is lying to you,” Vance wrote.

For homebuilders, immigration policy and its impact on the construction labor pool remains among the biggest policy wild cards heading into 2027. However, history suggests that a bipartisan deal may be a heavy lift, as Washington hasn’t passed comprehensive immigration reform since 1986, under President Reagan.

Whether Washington breaks its four-decade drought on immigration reform, and whether enforcement eases on job sites, remains a key policy wild card to watch in 2027.

AWS Adds Single-Shot RAG and Retrieval-Only Mode to Bedrock

📅 Nov 09, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Amazon Web Services announced on November 9, 2026 that Amazon Bedrock Managed Knowledge Base now supports single-shot retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) and passage-only requests through AgenticRetrieveStream. An expanded 0–5 range for maxAgentIteration lets applications select source retrieval, a one-search answer, or the service’s existing multi-step agentic retrieval.

The update brings the basic RAG pattern, one retrieval followed by a generated answer, into the same streaming API used for complex searches. Single-shot RAG uses one foundation-model call for generation, while retrieval-only requests use none. AWS says single-shot operation can reduce latency and model usage relative to agentic retrieval, but the announcement supplies no measured latency or cost comparison.

A value of 0 returns matching passages without generating an answer; 1 retrieves once and generates an answer with generateResponse=true; and 2 through 5 preserve existing agentic behavior. Every mode supports multiple knowledge bases. Single-shot therefore refers to one retrieval, not a restriction to one knowledge base.

One iteration represents one foundation-model call. When generation is enabled, a limit of n reserves the final call for answer generation and allows as many as n minus 1 planning rounds. Each planning round pairs a query rewrite with a retrieval. The initial speculative retrieval runs before planning and never counts against the limit.

At 0, response generation must be off. Enabling it produces a 4xx error because an answer requires a model call. At 1, the stream carries one retrieval and one grounded answer with no planning trace events. Existing requests using 2 through 5 do not change.

The AgenticRetrieveStream API reference identifies messages, retrievers, and agenticRetrieveConfiguration as required request inputs, while generateResponse is optional. Generated answer chunks are emitted only when generation is enabled. The final result can contain retrieved items, a synthesized answer, and citations that connect spans of the answer to supporting results.

Retrieval-only operation is equivalent to calling Retrieve directly, but its response format differs: passages appear in the stream’s result event without score or location fields. Applications already using AgenticRetrieveStream can consequently obtain Retrieve-style behavior by changing a parameter instead of integrating a separate API.

For complex questions, the Amazon Bedrock User Guide describes a workflow that decomposes queries into sub-queries, checks whether the retrieved evidence is sufficient, conducts additional searches, and can expand to a full document. Agentic retrieval supports only fully managed Bedrock knowledge bases and permits up to five retrievers per request. Lowering the iteration ceiling can stop the agent sooner and potentially reduce accuracy on complex queries.

AWS illustrates the settings with a fictional bank’s internal policy assistant, not a customer deployment. A sole-proprietor documentation question uses maxAgentIteration=1, while a comparison of sole-proprietor and LLC requirements that also checks rules for nonresident owners uses 4, permitting up to three planning rounds. A request to inspect policy language uses 0 and returns source passages without a summary.

The application must select the mode; maxAgentIteration does not automatically route requests. The example assistant handles policy lookup and comparison, but it neither approves accounts nor replaces bank review and authorization.

For adoption, AWS recommends comparing single-shot RAG with the existing agentic configuration on the same knowledge base, using representative questions and expected answers. The evaluation should measure latency and request cost, verify correctness and support in retrieved passages, and include multi-document and unanswerable questions. Those results can guide selection of the lowest iteration limit that satisfies each workflow’s quality requirements.

The change makes retrieval depth an explicit per-request infrastructure decision. Direct lookups can avoid planning calls, while complex queries retain iterative evidence gathering. For enterprises, the central trade-off is whether reduced model usage preserves complete, supported answers on their own documents, rather than assuming a higher iteration limit is always better.

Google Research Showcases Language Explorer’s 7,000-Language Map

📅 Nov 09, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Google Research presented Language Explorer on November 9, 2026, an interactive platform for examining language geography, scripts and speaker populations. Its world overview spans about 7,000 languages and 159 scripts, with records intended to support linguistic research and language-technology development.

Developed under Google’s 1,000 Languages Initiative, the platform turns the open-source LinguaMeta dataset into a public visual interface. The announcement by Daan van Esch and Uche Okonkwo connects that interface to a broader effort to make language technologies useful across more of the world’s linguistic diversity.

The Language Explorer site supports searches by language name, country or region, and continent or subcontinent. Users can browse by speaker numbers, endangerment status or scripts, then select a map pin to open a detailed language page. Those pages bring together names, locations and identifiers including BCP-47 tags, Glottocodes and Wikidata IDs, providing consistent reference points across language resources.

Every value displayed on the site comes from LinguaMeta, an open-source repository that harmonizes language information from 19 cited sources. The 2024 LinguaMeta paper documented the underlying dataset at 7,511 languages with BCP-47 and ISO 639-3 codes, with an origin tag on every data point for traceability and correction. When sources disagreed, the authors either preferred the more authoritative source or conducted further research using academic publications, census material and constitutions.

The paper reported that machine translation, speech recognition and text-to-speech were widely available in only about 100 languages, with low-quality or nonexistent coverage for most others. That gap gives the metadata a practical role: speaker counts can help prioritize work, while language-script-locale combinations identify the exact combinations a localized system must handle. The repository’s structure preserves those combinations in JSON records; a simpler TSV summary leaves out source details, extended names, script-use tags, official-status research and coordinates.

Language Explorer also appears as an interactive installation in Real Talk! Language. Power. World. at the Museum of Communication Berlin. The museum records the exhibition opening on November 5, 2026.

Six themed rooms address language worlds, diversity, identities, contact, power and the future. The final room asks how AI and chatbots are changing communication and how researchers and activists are documenting endangered languages. An online companion began November 6, 2026, adding background material, videos and quizzes across the same six perspectives, including the relationship between AI and multilingualism. Both the Berlin exhibition and its companion are listed through February 27, 2028.

The paper’s coverage table shows substantial differences between fields. Speaker-population figures were available for 6,253 languages, or 83%, but endonyms, the names communities use for their own languages, were documented for only 1,136, or 15%. The map therefore offers a broad entry point, while the depth of information varies considerably by language and category.

Population values carry additional qualifications. The underlying methods omit second-language totals because reliable statistics were difficult to obtain, round larger speaker estimates by order of magnitude to avoid false precision, and warn that aggregating locale populations can magnify errors in the sources. The resource focuses on living spoken languages: signed, ancient, historical and constructed languages are excluded. Macrolanguages and languages classified as extinct are included, the latter because some have retained small speaker communities. Esperanto is a stated exception to the constructed-language exclusion because several language technologies already support it.

Each map pin marks a notional language centre derived from Glottolog coordinates and manual revisions. The site cautions that languages are spoken by communities that may move or cross political boundaries, so the pins should not be read as fixed territories.

There is also a maintenance discrepancy. Explorer’s FAQ describes LinguaMeta as an active project and invites researchers to contribute corrections and resources.

The containing url-nlp repository carries a GitHub archive notice dated October 6, 2026 and is marked read-only. The full, version-controlled dataset remains downloadable, but that status leaves the route for future contributions unclear as Google presents the interface to a wider audience.

Powered by Kyndryl Bridge, it applies operational data and AI agents to incident analysis, risk assessment and remediation.

Kyndryl says the offering is intended to improve productivity, reduce risk and produce better business outcomes through predictive insights and automated remediation. Giovanni Carraro, the company’s global strategic alliances leader, said the focus is helping customers “move beyond experimentation, put AI to work and become trusted autonomous enterprises.”

Under the design, ServiceNow customers run operational data through Bridge to supply context across assets, configurations, dependencies, performance, events, changes, incidents, risks and resolutions. Specialized agents use that context to identify issues from failure patterns, generate evidence-based causal analysis, assess risk and provide step-by-step remediation guidance.

Insights from each resolved incident return to the platform, creating a feedback loop for subsequent automation and recommendations. The approach connects incident resolution to a broader operational memory, allowing handled issues to inform later analysis rather than treating each resolution as an isolated event.

The ServiceNow Store listing identifies dashboards for operational insights, IT health indicators, incident root-cause analysis, incident-resolution playbook generation and change-risk scoring. It also lists feedback on operational insights and server-based communication with Bridge through the Model Context Protocol.

Kyndryl reports that the broader Bridge platform, launched in 2022, is used by more than 1,400 enterprises, generates more than 16 million AI insights monthly and reduces IT incidents by up to 50%. Its operational experience is captured in more than 10,000 predefined best practices, runbooks, remediation practices and engineering actions.

Bridge unifies asset inventory, asset-performance information and engineering records of successful resolutions. Its agentic memory management is designed to preserve institutional knowledge and operational context, supporting continuous modernization.

Kyndryl describes Bridge as an agentic control plane, a layer that governs how AI agents operate across an enterprise’s technology estate. It applies enterprise policy as code at the point of action, maintains an auditable record of what each agent did and why, and requires human approval when risk warrants it. Its services catalog contains more than 190 services.

Michael Park, ServiceNow’s senior vice president of global partnerships and channels, said his company contributes the AI Control Tower and governance for safe operation, while Kyndryl contributes operational data and engineering depth, producing proven patterns customers can apply in their own environments.

The Store record adds an important timing distinction: version 26.10.16 is dated November 5, 2026 and labeled as the first ServiceNow Store release. The November 9 announcement therefore followed the initial Store release; it documents the expanded partnership and the offering’s availability rather than the application’s first appearance.

The listing classifies Kyndryl Autonomous Operations as a paid, certified generative-AI application sold by Kyndryl Inc., compatible with ServiceNow Australia Patch 7 and Zurich Patch 13. ServiceNow’s licensing terms add that AI features in partner-built apps may consume Assists deducted from the customer’s account, and that partners must provide an estimate of how many Assists their apps may consume.

For customers, the expansion connects operational context, AI-generated analysis and governed remediation within ServiceNow’s IT workflows. Bridge contributes data, resolution practices, auditability and approval controls, while ServiceNow contributes the AI workflows and platform. That makes implementation and governance central to adoption; the operational case rests on whether the incident feedback loop can turn Bridge’s accumulated knowledge into consistently better remediation.

Amundi Boosts Strategy (MSTR) Stake 44% to $291 Million as Shares Slide

📅 Nov 09, 2026  |  Category: Business  |  Read Source →

Amundi, Europe’s largest asset manager, raised its stake in Strategy (MSTR) by about 44% in the third quarter, a Form 13F filed Nov. 6 shows, lifting the position to 1,903,725 shares worth $291.4 million at Sept. 30 prices.

That is up 579,672 shares from the 1,324,053 Amundi reported at June 30, when the same holding was valued at about $115.1 million. The Paris-based firm managed €2.579 trillion in assets at the end of September, up 11% year over year, and described itself as Europe’s leading asset manager.

The filing lands as MSTR has given back much of its third-quarter run. Strategy shares closed at $153.09 on Sept. 30 and near $126 on Monday, an 18% drop that would put Amundi’s disclosed stake near $239 million at the latest close, Yahoo Finance data show.

A 13F is a snapshot, not a trading diary. Amundi filed a combination report that folds in 17 other managers across its group, so the Strategy line can include client, fund and discretionary positions rather than a single proprietary bet, and the firm may have changed the holding since Sept. 30.

Strategy itself kept stacking. The company bought another 1,036 bitcoin last week for $81.9 million at an average of $79,012, a Monday 8-K filing and company release show, lifting its treasury to 850,364 BTC acquired for $64.16 billion at an average of $75,456.

That average cost now sits above the spot bitcoin price, leaving the stack underwater on paper even after the firm added 811 bitcoin the prior week. Strategy’s public ledger still ranks it as the largest corporate bitcoin holder, with the stack equal to roughly 4% of bitcoin’s 21 million supply cap.

MSTR roughly doubled during the third quarter itself. The June 30 13F valued Amundi’s then-smaller stake at an implied $86.93 a share, against the $153.09 Sept. 30 close, so the dollar value of the position rose faster than the share count. Since then the stock has tracked bitcoin’s slide into the mid-$70,000s, wiping out a large share of that mark-to-market gain for anyone still holding the Sept. 30 position.

The timing matters because Strategy funds most of its bitcoin buys by selling new MSTR common under an at-the-market program. A weaker share price raises more dilution for every coin purchased, which is the feedback loop that has defined the stock’s moves this year.

Amundi was not the only large European name adding bitcoin exposure on the quarter. Barclays’ own 13F, also filed Nov. 6, shows 3,942,900 shares of BlackRock’s iShares Bitcoin Trust (IBIT) worth $186.7 million at Sept. 30, up 48% from 2,658,147 shares three months earlier, SEC records show.

That Barclays line carries the same caveat: a 13F gathers client, managed and trading book positions, not proof the bank put its own capital into bitcoin. Still, the two filings together sketch the same picture of Q3: traditional European managers kept adding exposure to bitcoin proxies even as the spot market cooled into the fall.

The institutional adds sit against a weaker tape since quarter-end. Bitcoin has slipped into the mid-$70,000s, Strategy stock has followed it down, and the value of Amundi’s disclosed MSTR stake has compressed even as the share count on the filing stayed fixed. At Monday’s close the disclosed stake was about $52 million below its Sept. 30 mark.

Whether Amundi kept buying, trimmed, or simply held through October’s slide will not show up until the next 13F. For now the Nov. 6 filing is the record Europe’s biggest asset manager left: nearly 1.9 million Strategy shares, up 44%, at a $291 million quarter-end mark.

Plant-Based Milk Alternatives May Not Give You Enough Iodine. Here’s Why That Matters

📅 Nov 09, 2026  |  Category: Health  |  Read Source →

Follow Healthline on our socials for all things health.

Replacing dairy with plant-based alternatives (PBAs), even partially, could substantially lower iodine intake, especially if those alternatives aren’t fortified, according to a recent study published in the Lancet Planetary Health.

The United Kingdom-based researchers found that only 12% of UK plant-based milk, yogurt, and cheese products analyzed were fortified with iodine. This compares with 61% for calcium and 46% for vitamin B12.

In the United States, the Food and Drug Administration (FDA) provides guidance on the nutritional content of PBAs, but fortification with iodine is voluntary.

The study found that replacing as little as 20% of dairy intake with plant-based alternatives could increase the risk of inadequate iodine intake, particularly among adolescent girls and women of reproductive age.

“People often worry about a decline in calcium intake when they replace some of their dairy with plant-based alternatives. As a result, iodine is frequently overlooked. This study serves as a valuable reminder of iodine’s significance as a nutrient,” said Victoria Finn, MD, board certified endocrinologist at Medical Offices of Manhattan, who wasn’t involved in the study.

The study included analysis of UK plant-based milk, cheese, and yogurt products, as well as dietary data from 3,500 people.

The researchers used statistical modeling to predict how swapping dairy for PBAs might affect iodine and other nutrients.

The people who are most vulnerable to iodine inadequacy are adolescent females and people at reproductive age, especially those who are pregnant or who plan to become pregnant.

The researchers estimated that replacing 20% to 35% of dairy intake with PBAs would increase the proportion of people with inadequate iodine intake:

When the researchers modeled the replacement of all dairy products with PBAs, the proportion of adolescent females with inadequate iodine intake rose to 80%. Among females ages 19 to 49, that proportion remained relatively stable as replacement rates increased.

It is important to keep in mind that the study used dietary modeling rather than direct measurement of iodine status.

Iodine is important for thyroid hormone production, metabolism, and both fetal and child brain development.

“Your thyroid gland requires iodine to produce thyroid hormones, which play a crucial role in regulating metabolism and supporting normal growth and development,” said Finn.

She added that if you consistently consume too little iodine over an extended period, the thyroid gland may enlarge and eventually struggle to produce enough hormones. This condition is known as hypothyroidism, which can lead to various symptoms such as lethargy, sleepiness, weight gain, dry skin, and more.

“Hypothyroidism can be particularly dangerous during pregnancy, as the baby’s brain, nervous system, and organs rely heavily on the mother’s thyroid hormone during the early stages of development,” Finn added.

In the United Kingdom, dairy is a major iodine source due to cattle feed supplementation, not because dairy is naturally high in iodine.

In the United States, dairy is also an important source of iodine for the same reasons.

However, the risk profile for iodine deficiency in the U.S. may differ from the U.K., as iodized salt is also a significant source of iodine and is widely available in the U.S., but not in the U.K.

According to the National Institutes of Health (NIH), the recommended daily intake of iodine depends on your age.

Plant-based or plant-forward diets have grown in popularity, in part due to the health benefits they offer. For example, consuming less meat can reduce your risk of heart disease, type 2 diabetes, obesity, and high cholesterol.

However, as this study suggests, it may be possible to reduce your iodine intake when transitioning to a plant-based diet. There are ways you can protect your iodine intake when choosing a more plant-forward diet, though.

Check the labels on plant-based dairy alternatives to find those fortified with iodine. You can also read the nutrition label. Iodine may be listed as potassium iodide or potassium iodate.

Seafood, eggs, and iodized salt are all good sources of iodine.

You can also discuss iodine supplements with your healthcare professional.

“Iodine supplements should be taken with caution, especially without checking your thyroid levels,” said Finn. “Additionally, be cautious with kelp supplements, as their iodine content can vary, and excessive iodine intake can disrupt thyroid function.”
