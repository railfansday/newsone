Mike Flanagan’s 10-Episode Netflix Thriller Masterpiece Is Everything ‘Carrie’ Should Have Been

📅 Nov 09, 2026  |  Category: Other  |  Read Source →

Carrie is missing something this show has in spades

Mike Flanagan knows how to make horror. He also knows how to make serialized TV. His most recent show is a mix of most of the things we’ve come to love about him: his bond with Stephen King, his skills as an adapter, his great eye for casting, and his ability to deliver pathos. But one key thing is missing: fear.

Carrie came out in October of this year, sparking much conversation, with many praising how it brought King’s ‘70s novel to 2026. One of its major criticisms, though, is that it’s not scary, especially for Flanagan, a guy who’s known for making some truly spooky stuff. Case in point: The Haunting of Hill House.

Released on Netflix in 2018, The Haunting of Hill House was Flanagan’s first TV show. Based on Shirley Jackson’s landmark novel of the same name, Flanagan takes her characters and reimagines them, at times scrapping their identities completely. While Jackson collected a group of strangers in a haunted house to explore their deepest traumas, Flanagan turned them into siblings, transforming Hill House into a story about their childhood home.

It’s an adaptational choice that many hardcore Jackson fans don’t forgive. Still, if Flanagan does one thing, it’s commit fully to the haunted house concept, taking the psychological fear that permeates the novel and literalizing it. Hill House is a true house of horrors.

Rather than leaning on jump scares, Hill House runs on dread, the type that has your eyes scanning through every inch of the screen so you’re not caught off guard by something that looks out of place. “If you go back and watch the series again, in every episode there’s probably somewhere between eight and 10 ghosts that are just hidden in plain sight that we called no attention to but they’re there,” Flanagan told Bloody Disgusting.

Flanagan also revealed that the ghosts had personal origins. The tall man with the bowler hat was added because one of the writers is afraid of tall people. “People on stilts freak her out,” he said. “It’s one of the most specific and weird phobias I’ve heard. We all thought it was so funny that we decided to make the tall man.”

And while Hill House delivers plenty of scary ghosts for texture and disquiet, some pull double duty, driving the story too. Flanagan splits Jackson’s Eleanor in two characters, giving her instability to the Crain siblings’ mother, Olivia (Carla Gugino), and her frailty and imagination to Nell (Victoria Pedretti). He turns the latter into the perfect example of a house cannibalizing its weakest. The series’ standout episode, “The Bent-Neck Lady,” relies on the twist that (spoiler alert) the ghost that has haunted Nell her entire life is herself, driven mad and killed by the house. Hill House’s biggest twist makes the horror intimate. Carrie’s twist does the opposite, removing the horror from her completely.

Warning: Spoilers follow for Carrie’s final episodes.

While Flanagan’s Carrie sticks closer to the book, keeping its mythology, sequence of events, and its characters' identities, it veers off in the one place it shouldn’t: the prom. We spend an entire season building up to one of the most famous moments in horror history, only for the series to unnecessarily complicate it. Instead of making the prom the place where Carrie breaks and enacts her revenge, it’s Emaline (Thalia Dudek), Carrie’s friend, who bears the majority of the blame, taking over Carrie’s body for most of the massacre.

In an interview with Polygon, Flanagan discussed the prom scene at length, clarifying what had happened. He shared that he and the writers wanted Carrie to be "100% solely responsible for" the deaths of key characters, innocent and guilty alike, including Tommy Ross (Joel Oulette), Billy Nolan (Arthur Conti) and Chris Hargensen (Alison Thornton).

They drew the line at the prom massacre. "None of us believed the Carrie White we created would consciously choose to kill Principal Grayle (Matthew Lillard), would choose to kill Ms. D (Amber Midthunder), and would choose to ignite innocent people around her,” he said.

Carrie has always been more than a story about a girl setting her prom on fire. It’s about the cruelty of high schoolers who use their weaker peers to feel better about themselves. That comes through in Flanagan’s show; as with Hill House, he has the ability to make us care deeply about his characters. But the prom is the moment where all that cruelty infects Carrie, where she transcends the page and becomes a horror icon. It’s there the show flinches. There’s no Bent-Neck Lady moment, just a massacre with no one clearly to blame. Is the show called Emaline?

The Haunting of Hill House is streaming on Netflix.

The Elder Scrolls: Return To Skyrim Arrives On November 9

📅 Nov 09, 2026  |  Category: World  |  Read Source →

One might argue that Skyrim is a tad overplayed in the world of The Elder Scrolls, if only because the fifth game in the series has been re-released ad nauseam, and the setting, as beautiful as it may be, has been so dutifully ingrained into our respective consciousness that one would be forgiven for mistaking it for one's second home. Still, Skyrim is as crucial to Tamriel as TES V is to Bethesda's bottom line, so it's understandable why it continues to crop back up every few years.

Additionally, now that we know the setting of The Elder Scrolls 6, it makes sense for Bethesda to make a return to Skyrim one more time, if only to help differentiate the series a little from what TES VI aims to offer. Fortunately, those who've missed Skyrim or simply wish to return there can do so in the brand-new The Elder Scrolls: Return to Skyrim event. This offers fans the opportunity to see the region like never before, unlock a whole host of goodies, and, one hopes, have a good time in the process.

The Elder Scrolls Online has received a major update centered around the region of Skyrim. Titled Echoes of Skyrim, it aims to bring the vast majority of content in the region to all ESO players. This already includes the Greymoor Chapter, Wolfhunter DLC, and Moon Hunter Keep and March of Sacrifices solo dungeons, but will also include the Markarth DLC, several Golden Pursuits campaigns, gameplay improvements, and a new PvP season at a later date. It's a huge amount of content that was previously locked behind a paywall and an obvious win for long-term fans and newcomers alike.

One of the aforementioned Golden Pursuits campaigns is Return to Skyrim. This event is running from November 9 to December 2, and, as the name suggests, will see players need to head to the region and complete a series of objectives. As with previous Golden Pursuits, these objectives will likely be tasks and activities you would have completed anyway in Skyrim, such as running dungeons and crafting items. Once you've completed the Golden Pursuit, you'll then be given a series of rewards.

The rewards for completing the Return to Skyrim Golden Pursuit in The Elder Scrolls Online will be a new pet, mount, and Antiquities leads. You can track the Golden Pursuit campaign in the Activity Finder, and it shouldn't take you too long to complete, especially if you're a seasoned player already. In addition to the Return to Skyrim Golden Pursuit, the Markarth DLC will also be given to all ESO players for free on November 9, making it a day you'll absolutely want to log in for.

It's great to see so much content being given to Elder Scrolls Online fans for free, especially surrounding Skyrim. With numerous events running alongside all the DLC and Chapters being handed out for free, players should be kept extremely busy while awaiting the upcoming Elder Scrolls 6. However, should you burn through all of this ESO content quickly, fear not, as even more expansions and updates are on their way, ensuring there will be absolutely no shortage of Elder Scrolls content to enjoy.

Warning: SPOILERS for the Cupertino episode “Harper” are ahead!Cupertino’s Ella Stiller opened up about her character’s most emotionally-vulnerable episode yet in the new CBS legal drama series.

When Cupertino premiered last month, Stiller’s Christy Palladino was introduced as the assistant of Mike Colter’s Michael Price, an attorney and equity co-founder of S-Talk. After Michael was fired from the rising technology company, Christy met the same fate shortly thereafter for maintaining communication with him, forcing her to go back to work at TGI Fridays. However, after Michael and Rachel Keller’s Olivia Siffre, another freshly-fired S-Talk attorney, started their own law firm, Christy came aboard as its office manager.

Since then, Christy has held down the fort at Price and Siffre and become the firm’s informal investigator, as well as made first contact with their AI client, Bo. Cupertino’s newest episode, “Harper,” delivered its deepest look into Christy’s personal life yet. Her roommate Harper was initially denied a liver transplant due to what initially looked like an AI algorithm switching her place in the queue with an older, wealthy man. It was later revealed that it was a doctor who had actually gone against the judge’s order for the liver to go to Harper.

In an interview with ScreenRant’s Adam Holmes ahead of the airing of “Harper,” Stiller talked about getting into the mindset of playing Christy’s emotional scenes in this episode, which ended with Harper getting the liver just in the nick of time thanks to Christy, Michael, and Olivia’s coordinated efforts from different locations. The actor explained that it was important for her not to just draw from her professional training, but also envision what she would do if she was placed in the same kind of position with one of her friends:

It's interesting because you're right. It was the most emotional and the most I had gotten to do in terms of the high stakes for me thus far on the show. You just have to put yourself in the position. And for me, I draw from a lot of different methods of acting. In drama school, they say you build your little toolbox. It's living truthfully under imaginary circumstances, that's what I was taught.

Also, imagine it's your best friend in that position, and then you picture it, and that's helpful for me, always connecting things to my real life and connecting to people I love. I also feel like by that point, I was so invested in Christy. I was from the beginning, but by Episode 6, I believe I think this girl is really special.

Stiller also highlighted the guest stars in “Harper” as another reason why she enjoyed working on this Cupertino episode, as she admired several of them before the cameras started rolling. In her words:

I think that Christy has a real way with people and a real sense of being in touch with her feelings and knowing when to put that aside and when to play into it. It was also really easy with these incredible actors that I got to work with on that episode. Aaron Serotsky, who played the doctor, I was a huge fan of his before like I saw him from Marcel on the Train. When I saw his name on the call sheet, I was like, ‘Oh, this is huge for me!’

Cupertino comes from Robert and Michelle King, the same duo behind The Good Wife, The Good Fight, Evil, and Elsbeth, among other TV shows. ScreenRant: What was it that drew you to working on Cupertino when you first read the pilot script?

ScreenRant: How does your personality in real life compare to Christy’s?

I think we're very similar. She's a little bit more fearless than I am, almost like physically. Even just her willingness to go on any adventure and just be in danger. She craves it, really. I think that was something early on that I really used to think about, and then it became normal as part of her character. But early on, it was like, ‘Oh, that's an interesting thing.’ It's like she actually lives for this; the danger and the thrill and the adrenaline. She's a little bit of an adrenaline junkie. I'm not so much that, but I am the level of fiery she is, the feistiness. Feisty is kind of a stupid word, but she's just wise beyond her years …

But people maybe think because she is a party girl, and she does love to have fun, and she lives for that too, but the second that she starts getting involved in something deeper with this law firm, and with Michael and Olivia, who she really adores. Yeah, I think her wit, her sense of humor, her intelligence, that's all stuff I relate to. I would like to think that we're similar and are in our sensibility, and I just get to say a little bit more of what I'm thinking all the time through her.

ScreenRant: 2026 has been a big year for you, because along with Cupertino, you also starred in The Comeback season 3. How do those two experiences compare?

I mean, two very different experiences. First of all, we shot The Comeback in LA on the Warner Bros. lot, which was incredible and a once in a lifetime kind of experience at this point. But it was very different because the stakes for me were lower, in that I was able to just show up and get the experience and soak it all in and learn so much.
