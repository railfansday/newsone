Musk says many Cybertruck buyers will get deposits back as production ends, arguing ‘opportunity cost’ is vastly outweighed by new robotaxi

📅 Nov 09, 2026  |  Category: Business  |  Read Source →

After sidelining the Cybertruck as he bets Tesla’s future on the autonomous Cybercab, Elon Musk now says customers still waiting for their electric truck may not get one after all.

In an early Monday post on X, he said the number of Cybertrucks ordered “far exceeds our production rate,” which means many buyers will be out of luck following the CEO’s announcement Friday that Tesla would be “winding down” the electric truck’s production by the middle of next year.

“We will try to make as many as possible before shutting down the line in about 8 months, but will have to return deposits for a lot of people,” Musk said Monday.

The news comes as some fans of the boxy and futuristic-looking Cybertruck said on social media that they were putting in new orders or upgrading their existing Cybertruck orders before the vehicle ends production. As of Monday, Tesla’s website seemed to still be accepting new orders.

Musk said in his latest post that Tesla was shutting down Cybertruck production not because the vehicle was unprofitable but because, “the opportunity cost of the complexity of Cybertruck is vastly outweighed by the value of incremental Cybercab production.”

The Cybertruck, unlike other Tesla vehicles like Model Y, has struggled to reach and maintain high sales since it was released in 2023. When the vehicle was first announced in 2019, Musk hailed it as Tesla’s best product “ever.” He later predicted Tesla would make between 250,000 and half-a-million units of the vehicle per year. Yet, between 2024 and 2025, Cybertruck sales plummeted 48% to 20,237 from 38,965.

Through the third quarter of this year, Tesla had sold an estimated 11,586, according to Kelley Blue Book. Meanwhile, Tesla sold 262,491 Model Ys and 106,962 Model 3s over that same time.

In recent years, Tesla has discontinued other models like the Model S luxury sedan and Model X SUV, narrowing its consumer vehicle lineup as Musk increasingly shifts the company’s focus to autonomous vehicles, but also humanoid robots. Tesla’s focus could drift further away from cars as it explores a merger with Musk’s other public company, SpaceXAI.

To be sure, the Cybertruck has still amassed a cult following for its unique design despite, or sometimes because of, the association with Musk, whose rightward shift once made him a close adviser of President Trump and the leader of the Department of Government Efficiency (DOGE).

After a brief falling out with Trump, he returned to the fold and reportedly spent as much as $474 million in the midterm election cycle to boost Republican candidates. But many of his preferred candidates lost, following a surprisingly strong showing for the Democratic Party in the midterms last week.

The end of the Cybertruck’s production run also triggered criticism from Tesla fans who accused the company of neglecting existing customers as Musk chases a future where consumers don’t own their own vehicles.

“Watching the decisions Tesla is making recently has just left me puzzled as a consumer. We went from having lots of choices in vehicles. To those being whittled down,” wrote one X user.

When Tesla unveiled the Cybercab concept in 2024, Musk portrayed the vehicle as “a little lounge” rather than a car in the traditional sense. He pitched a future of “individualized mass transit,” where people wouldn’t necessarily need to own a car, but could buy one if they wanted.

He noted that ownership can be expensive, between the price of the car, insurance, and storage. But the price per ride in a Cybercab, he predicted, would be about 30 to 40 cents per mile.

“Your average passenger car is only used about 10 hours a week out of a 168 hours. So the vast majority of the time, cars are just doing nothing. But if they’re autonomous, they could be used, I don’t know, 5 x more?” Musk said at the time.

In reply to Musk’s post Monday, X users pushed back on this vision.

“The problem is no one is going to want to give up their personal cars for a ride share service. Are you going to annihilate uber? Yup. Are you going to be every Americans daily transport via Robotaxi anywhere in the near future? Nope,” the user wrote.

Other X users complained that the company was cutting out customers whose needs aren’t met by other vehicles in Tesla’s lineup, while others said Tesla should be expanding vehicle production not cutting it.

“This is still not making any sense. Why even make the Cybertruck in the first place?,” wrote one user.

Role: Reporter Marco Quiroz-Gutierrez is a reporter for Fortune covering general business news.

JetBlue Launches 3 New Nonstop Routes In Only 1 Day: See Full List Now

📅 Nov 09, 2026  |  Category: Business  |  Read Source →

New routes are usually intriguing, and can be especially notable when more than one launch on the same day. That is what happened with JetBlue Airways last week, as the carrier introduced two links from Fort Lauderdale-Hollywood International Airport (FLL) and one from Baltimore/Washington International Thurgood Marshall Airport (BWI).

JetBlue's growth at FLL has been significant recently, as the airline benefits from the end of the now-defunct Spirit Airlines. In the present week, Cirium Diio data indicates that FLL is currently the airline's most-served airport. 856 weekly departures are available, exceeding those from New York JFK (816) and Boston (811).

On November 2, the carrier took off from BWI to San Juan's Luis Muñoz Marín International Airport (SJU). As Puerto Rico is a US territory, flights are considered domestic. As the service only covers 1,360 nautical miles (2,519 km) on a great circle basis, it is only JetBlue's ninth-longest route from the Lower 48 states to SJU through next summer.

Flown daily on a year-round basis, it is the first time that JetBlue has served the airport pair. The airline will be one of three carriers running nonstop, joining Frontier Airlines (up to one daily service) and Southwest Airlines (up to three daily flights). JetBlue's entry follows the exit of Spirit earlier this year.

Given the schedule, the operating Airbus A320 will fly from somewhere to SJU to BWI to SJU and then elsewhere. For example, on November 8, Flightradar24 indicates that the airframe, the 20.5-year-old N638JB, flew from Hartford to Puerto Rico. It then flew to Maryland and back, remained overnight in SJU, and flew to FLL the next morning.

Also on Monday, November 2, the airline began flying from FLL to John Glenn Columbus International Airport (CMH). Flights run daily throughout the year. While the 162-seat A320 is nearly always scheduled, the 140-seat A220-300 is due to appear from September 8, 2027. As that is so far away, it is quite possible that it'll change. The operating airframe remains overnight in Ohio.

JetBlue was interested in this market because Spirit pulled out early this year and had up to two daily services. It joins Frontier, which entered the market in March this year (up to a daily departure), and Southwest (usually daily, but up to four daily services for spring break). JetBlue's entry means that the airport pair has up to six daily flights, just like it did when Spirit operated, although that frequency is very time-limited.

In the 12 months to July 2026, the US Department of Transportation shows that 235,200 round-trip passengers flew between FLL and CMH. Excluding seasonality, around 640 people traveled daily. About three-quarters of the traffic flew nonstop, meaning approximately 57,000 passengers (156 daily) flew via a hub or other airport en route. Spirit had 38% of the total traffic (89,500).

Launched on (you guessed it) November 2, FLL to Indianapolis International Airport (IND) follows the same pattern as the other two routes. But it is even more significant because it is the first time that the carrier has flown to IND and Indiana.

Spirit operated FLL-IND earlier this year, which motivated JetBlue's entry. Like the other routes mentioned earlier, it is also primarily served daily on the A320. However, the A220 (shown above) is scheduled from December 17, 2026, until January 3, 2027, then from February 11 to 22, and again from September 8 next year.

JetBlue is one of four operators running nonstop between FLL and IND, coexisting with Allegiant Air (up to 12 weekly), Frontier (up to one daily), and Southwest (up to three daily departures). When added to CMH and the upcoming transcontinental route from FLL to San Diego, JetBlue has 62 routes from FLL this winter.

20 Best Netflix Series Performances Of All Time, Ranked

📅 Nov 09, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

There’s so much content on Netflix that it has both some of the best movies and TV shows ever made, and some of the worst. Some of the acting you’ll find in Netflix’s library is Hallmark-level tripe: Joey King in The Kissing Booth, David Spade in Father of the Year, Emma Roberts and Luke Bracey in Holidate.

But you’ll also find some of the greatest acting ever captured on film: Adam Sandler in Uncut Gems, Vanessa Kirby in Pieces of a Woman, Joel Edgerton and William H. Macy in Train Dreams. So, here are the greatest acting performances in any Netflix original series.

Since her breakout role as Eleven, Millie Bobby Brown has become one of Netflix’s go-to contract stars, and it’s easy to see why. Eleven stole the show in Stranger Things’ first season, and Brown demonstrated an impressive amount of depth and range for such a young performer in such a limiting role.

When we first met Eleven, she was completely alone and she couldn’t speak, so Brown had absolutely nothing to work with to endear us to this character, and yet she pulled it off. Eleven quickly became a beloved sci-fi icon.

Cristin Milioti was so great in Black Mirror, the show ended up giving her episode a sequel to continue her story. Nanette Cole is a young programmer whose consciousness is transferred into her boss’ twisted “It’s a Good Life”-style sociopathic fantasy world.

When she gets there, Nanette is just one of the despicable Robert Daly’s digital playthings. But by the end of her first intergalactic adventure, she’d managed to turn the tables on her oppressor and free herself from his cybernetic shackles. It was an empowering journey, and Milioti played every step of it perfectly.

When Netflix first announced its Ripley series, it didn’t seem like we needed another Ripley adaptation. We’ve seen it all before, and Matt Damon already gave an iconic turn that defined the character on the big screen in The Talented Mr. Ripley.

But Ripley ended up being a worthwhile readaptation. Not only did it have gorgeous, moody, monochromatic cinematography to make it more visually engaging than the movie; Andrew Scott did something completely different from Damon, and used the extra hours of a TV series to really explore the psyche of this curious character.

Everyone in The Crown did a great job of humanizing the royals, including everyone who played Queen Elizabeth II throughout her reign. Olivia Colman was spectacular as an older Queen Elizabeth, but Claire Foy was the one who got everyone hooked on The Crown in season 1.

Foy set the gold standard for The Crown’s ever-changing cast going forward. She embodied this regal historical figure in all her glory, but also interrogated the human being just under the surface.

Nell is the most tragic of all the Crains. She never did anything wrong, she just wanted her family to get along, and she’s the one who suffered the most from their dysfunctions. It was heartbreaking to see Violet McGraw’s young Nell go through all that trauma, but even more devastating to see those traumas sunken into Victoria Pedretti’s anxious eyes as grownup Nell.

The revelation that Nell was being haunted by her own future self all this time makes Pedretti’s performance even more impressive. She’s both the haunter and the hauntee in this chilling haunted-house tale.

Steven Yeun and Ali Wong both gave fantastic performances as the lead characters in Beef, and they both won much-deserved Emmys for them. They both committed so wholeheartedly to the petty rage that you fully believe these two people have nothing better to do than torment each other.

Wong has a slight edge over Yeun, because she had a more complicated character to play, and a tougher character to make us like. Danny is just an angry guy who’s fed up with the system, but Amy’s misery is a lot more nuanced.

Omar Sy is essentially giving a performance within a performance in Lupin. Assane Diop is a lot like Jean-Paul Belmondo’s antihero Michel from the French New Wave classic Breathless.

In Breathless, Michel emulates the hard-boiled characters of Humphrey Bogart. In Lupin, Assane emulates fictional master thief Arsène Lupin, who was created by Maurice Leblanc in the early 20th century. Sy nails that two-sided performance, capturing both the mask Assane wears and the real Assane underneath it.

A mysterious priest arrives in a remote town at the beginning of Mike Flanagan’s deeply personal horror opus Midnight Mass. Hamish Linklater’s subtly frightening, yet undeniably charismatic portrayal of this God-fearing eccentric keeps you hooked as Midnight Mass unfurls its vampiric mystery.

In the early episodes, Linklater maintains a mesmerizing air of mystery around Father Paul Hill, so we’re not quite sure what to make of him. But, the more overtly villainous he gets, the more fun Linklater has with the character.

One of the toughest challenges an actor can take on is playing a legendary real-life figure. When an actor is cast to play John Lennon or Cleopatra or Martin Luther King, Jr. or some other historical icon, they have to look beyond the myth to the man (or woman). Pablo Escobar is both a revered folk hero who gave back to the community, and a notorious crime kingpin who ran one of the most ruthless drug cartels ever assembled.

Wagner Moura managed to find the humanity in Escobar when he played him in Narcos. He deconstructs the mythos to show us that Escobar was just a human being like anyone else.

A little over a decade after Ben Affleck’s disastrous Daredevil movie, Charlie Cox finally did the Man Without Fear justice on-screen. Netflix’s Daredevil series is both a brutal, bloody comic book actioner and a gritty, engaging prestige crime drama.

Charlie Cox ripped the Matt Murdock written by Frank Miller right off the page. The Daredevil from Miller’s comics — the brooding antihero, wracked with Catholic guilt — is the Daredevil that Cox brought to the screen. Cox was perfectly matched with a huge, intimidating Vincent D’Onofrio as his arch-nemesis, Wilson Fisk; their intense cat-and-mouse feud is comparable to Al Pacino and Robert De Niro in Heat.

How USMNT's Neil Pierre turned overseas loan into breakout season with Philadelphia Union

📅 Nov 09, 2026  |  Category: Sports  |  Read Source →

If not listed, please contact your TV provider.

CHESTER, Pa. - Walking into Philadelphia Union training, there's one player who immediately sticks out due to towering over the rest of the team at center back: Neil Pierre. While a soft-spoken person in life, when you're 6-foot-7 on a soccer field, it's hard to miss him—even down to Chris Richards not believing it until he saw the Union defender in the United States Men's National Team camp. But height may also be the least interesting thing about Pierre's journey as he, like the Union, has taken MLS by storm since he returned from a loan spell in Denmark with Lyngby, where he helped the club gain promotion back to the Danish Superliga while getting a taste of what first-team soccer is like.

For an 19-year-old, just going to a new country is enough of an adjustment, and doing so while needing to break into a squad with ambition is quite a task, and Pierre did it while also learning a new position. Because of the club setup, Pierre actually played right back, and explaining the setup to CBS Sports, one of his co-managers, Andreas Bjelland, wanted to find out how he could get the talented young defender into the squad.

"He had the power, he had the drive, but he still needed the game smartness and the calmness of playing more senior minutes," Bjelland said. "And then he could get some extra minutes playing right back because we also had some really good center backs at that point playing really well. So it was a little bit of a mix of also getting him into play more and more."

This is a credit not only to Pierre's talent but also to his drive and versatility, as the Union man turned this into an excellent breakout where he scored four goals in 10 appearances while winning promotion and building confidence that he could do it as a soccer player along the way. Both the Union Academy and Pierre's own family helped set him up for success in a year that has become an absolute whirlwind. Following his success at Lyngby, alongside the Union falling to the bottom of the league, it was easy to question why Pierre wasn't with the team to start the season, but his time in Denmark was also formative. While it's easy to say that he should've been in Philadelphia from the start in hindsight, it was an important step for the Pierre that we see before us today.

"In the beginning, it was definitely a little nerve-wracking and definitely nervous," Pierre said, reflecting during Philadelphia Union training. "I think I can give all credit to them; they've kind of taken me in as like a part of the family immediately, so I felt like there was no stress there, and then that translated to on the pitch and they gave me the confidence to play how I was supposed to play. And then also off the field, I think, because we would go to team outings all the time and get dinner together, so it was like a family on and off the field."

Fast forward, and not only has Pierre been part of pulling the Union out of the bottom of the Eastern Conference, but he's also earned his first USMNT call-up and cap, and that was quickly followed by a return call for Concacaf Nations League play, where the USMNT will face Haiti twice during this international window, but if not for his family, soccer may not have happened. When Pierre was younger, basketball was the first sport that he connected with, but he credits his brother Nelson Pierre (who is currently on loan from the Vancouver Whitecaps to the HFX Wanderers of the Canadian Premier League) and his father for why he is where he is now.

"If it wasn't for them, I would be playing basketball right now. 100%," Pierre said. "But I think through them it was like I saw the love they had for the sport, and like how much passion they had, so I was like obviously I wanted to follow in my dad and brother's footsteps and be able to have that passion for something. So then I followed them into soccer and it's been since ever since it's been like this."

Considering his play for the Union this season, plenty are happy that Pierre made the decision to stick with soccer, but while his frame and closing speed do come with advantages, they also comes with things that he has to watch out for on the field, especially when defending against smaller players. Not only are they at times more agile than Pierre, but challenges that he makes can look worse too, and these are things that gaining more in-game experience has helped him prepare for, dealing with them and excelling.

He also has the benefit of playing not only for a coach that he knows from the Union youth ranks, but also for one who was a defender during his own professional career. In fact, Pierre has always had a defensive-minded head coach, even with the USMNT in Mauricio Pochettino, and that's something else that has been a benefit while he's getting his feet in the game.

"Obviously, being young, playing with older players [my coaches have] pulled me aside, either showing me film or what they feel like they've seen, and it's obviously different hearing it from a coach that's played in your position and done it at the highest level," Pierre said. "Same with the national team with Poch, I think hearing stuff like that, it's like these guys have done it at a pro level for a long time, so they know what they're talking about."

Add that to his natural skills, and you get a young defender that the Union can anchor the squad around. Before even getting into his goals, his closing speed and positioning are able to anchor a defense that has allowed the third-fewest goals in MLS since the World Cup, with only 21. They may not have risen to first in the Eastern Conference, but when the squad only had seven points prior to the World Cup and has picked up 40 after it so far, the impact that Pierre and the rest of the squad have had to push their way into the playoffs has been quite remarkable. You can also see why Lyngby wanted to keep him for their season in the Danish top flight, but when the Union had a chance at a starting spot, it was hard to allow Pierre to go back on loan when he could help the first team.

"We can be super aggressive because Neil is comfortable in one-on-one situations. If it's a direct ball, he wins; he eats all of these. If it's open space that he has to defend, physically, he can deal with that, and he's also a good defender," Philly's interim coach, Ryan Richter, said about how Pierre's play impacts his squad. "The way he puts out so many fires allows us to be aggressive higher up the field, because we know when they play the ball long that we have someone who cleans this up almost every time."

Defenders usually peak later than attackers in soccer, so it's impressive that Pierre is catching on so quickly, but given his path of getting into first-team soccer and also being with the coach who he came up through the academy with, it's a good spot for Pierre to succeed and grow before his next step likely takes him back to Europe.

"I think as a person, he's much more mature and much more focused than I would say at the point he was at a year ago, when I was coaching him with the second team. I'm really proud of him, the way that he's been able to put focus on the day-to-day and the game-to-game," Richter said. "Because I think of a lot of things, positive and negative, that happened to him this year. Having a great experience in Denmark, but it didn't start out perfect, and he earned his way in. When he came back here, he earned the starting spot. A couple months later called for the national team for his first caps to the national team. Comes back and is able to right away again put his full attention back in with the team and play a couple of good games after that. Um, so overall, his maturity in dealing with the things that are going on around him, I think, has been really impressive."

Pierre credits his family and the team for helping him stay grounded through what has been an absolute whirlwind year. Next up is a trip to USMNT camp before helping anchor the defense for the club as they face the New England Revolution in the first round of the MLS Cup playoffs. Plenty for a young player to juggle, but after the year Pierre has had, this will feel more normal than what he has already accomplished.

Best Actress Frontrunner Julianne Moore Nailed ‘The Debut’ by Singing, Styling Her Hair Like Liza Minnelli, and Avoiding Playing ‘Some Suffering Mom’

📅 Nov 09, 2026  |  Category: Entertainment  |  Read Source →

“Hello, I’m Julie,” said a radiant Julianne Moore, holding out her hand as she popped out of the elevator at Middleburg, Virginia’s Salamander Resort. Her hair was red, her skin was white, her dress as yellow as gold. A few hours later after a rapturous Middleburg Film Festival reaction to “The Debut” (December 11), we talked in a hotel meeting room. The A24 crowdpleaser has been wowing audiences and critics since Telluride.

Jesse Eisenberg has done it again. His follow-up to “A Real Pain,” which won Kieran Culkin the Supporting Actor Oscar, could score Best Actress for Moore. The musical comedy has earned Moore raves on the festival circuit for her role as a sad suburban homemaker turned anxious aspiring actress in a New Jersey community theater. A sixth Oscar nomination is inevitable, and possibly a second Oscar win after “Still Alice” in 2015. It’s hard to believe she launched her career in 1988 as a regular on soap opera “As the World Turns.” Related Stories Rejected Twice by Sundance, ‘Mouse’ Is One of the Year’s Best Movies. Why Aren’t More People Talking About It? From Los Javis to Lord and Miller, Director Duos Are a High-Risk, High-Reward Prospect in the Oscar Race

Moore is the current Best Actress frontrunner, even though she has won before. Sometimes nabbing an Oscar is a disadvantage in the Oscar race. But not if the performance is undeniable: Katharine Hepburn holds the record with four, while nipping at her heels are three-time winners Meryl Streep, Frances McDormand, Jack Nicholson, and Sean Penn.

Eisenberg wrote a rich character for Moore, knowing what the veteran actress could do with the childlike housewife Mona who, in order to learn how to act, must find herself. The director and actress had worked together on “When You Finish Saving the World” (2022). “[‘The Debut‘] is about this woman who has been chewed up but not spit out,” said Eisenberg, quoting Mona’s signature song. “It’s my point of view through somebody I consider a muse. I was writing something about myself as a seven-year-old in 1990, but I couldn’t picture anybody else except her doing it.”

It helped that Moore had told Eisenberg exactly what she was looking for. “I said I had just done ‘Dear Evan Hansen,’ and I loved singing, and I would love to have another opportunity,” she said. “I told him that I would love to do more comedy, and I didn’t want to play some suffering mom.”

Eisenberg put all of this in the screenplay, “but in the most surprising and inventive way,” she said. “He’s such a specific writer. He’s so incredibly empathetic. He has a compassionate worldview. He understands creativity. All of that stuff went into this wonderful and delightful story. It’s joyful and life-affirming too.”

Moore didn’t know she was playing a seven-year-old version of Eisenberg until he started telling that to the press. “Then he was like, ‘This is my experience of being a kid and going to community theater.’ But I could sense that there was a personal point of view in it. I very much felt with Mona that she was on a subjective journey, and that he was inside of that. With Mona, I always knew he was looking through her.”

“The Debut” is told from her point of view, as she tries to talk the overbearing local community theater director Jerry (Paul Giamatti) into hiring her, an untrained actress. He does it because he has no one else. But first, he must teach her how to act.

Mona dresses like a schoolgirl, with skirts, blouses, cardigan sweaters. “She’s so contained, and the way she learns is so literal,” said Moore, “the way a baby learns, like, you make a face at a baby, the baby makes a face back, but they don’t have intention behind it. But they’re learning things in an exterior way, and then they match it with feeling later on. So everything she’s doing is a childlike way of learning.”

Mona goes from an invisible nonentity in her own home to someone who demands attention. “She’s a kid who has found friends for the first time,” said Eisenberg. “She’s the girl who transferred to the right school at seven years old, and then the first rehearsal happens, and she is told she is terrible, and she has no idea why. And it’s the girl who transferred to the right school and gets an F on the very first test, and then the rest of the semester is panicking, and all she’s seeing is everybody else get A’s, and she still has the F, and she doesn’t know what to do. And then the teacher pulls her aside and says, ‘You either show up, or you are out on your ass.’ And something switches in her, and she makes the decision: ‘I am going to show up.’ And she begins to turn into a monster.”

The theater troupe embraces Mona as she unfolds like a flower. “We’ve been like those theater kids cracking corny jokes and finding community,” said Moore. “It’s all done with so much affection. Jesse looks at that world with not a hint of malice.”

Giamatti’s director relentlessly pushes the newbie through her paces until they have a powerful confrontation on the empty stage where he forces her to break down. Eisenberg reduced the actors’ anxiety by placing the eight-page showstopper in the middle of the shoot: not the beginning, and not the end. On the first day he went over to Moore’s apartment to talk about the movie. He said, “I really don’t want you to worry about that scene. I don’t want you to worry about memorizing that because I know that as an actor, I would I lose my mind. I would just be drilling it all the time, and I wouldn’t be able to concentrate. We’re going to have a teleprompter on that day. So don’t worry about it.”

So Moore worked on it normally, she said, “like I worked on everything else in the movie. So when we got to it, I knew it. I didn’t need a teleprompter. He knew instinctively as a director and an actor that what scares us sometimes is just a volume of language. He didn’t want to freeze me up. And having a partner like Paul who was so awake and aware and generous made it a pleasure. We were enjoying it when we did shoot it.”

Moore and Giamatti had never worked together before. She once introduced herself to him in a bookstore in Montauk. “He was very shy,” she said. “He is the greatest scene partner. I didn’t expect him to be so generous. He’s so funny, and he’s so dear. He’s present, incredibly modest, and as a partner, so available. Paul and I would work on it occasionally. When we were doing that scene, the rhythm was so important in it. You throw something, he hits it right back. It was our sense together of keeping it aloft, keeping it moving, keeping the river flowing. It felt electric with the two of us.”

The big blowout happens early in the story. “In a lot of movies, that kind of set piece would happen later,” said Moore. “Instead, it’s like a discovery place. It’s where she starts to begin to unlock herself emotionally, but she doesn’t learn from it. She comes in the next day, and starts to rehearse. And her belief is that acting is size, that it’s just huge, right? That’s when she starts yelling. She thinks it’s about being loud, which then allows her to go on the next journey with Jerry, where he says, ‘Let’s talk about feeling and reality and intention.’ So she’s slowly putting these pieces together. But that’s the first moment where she attaches something outside of herself to her own emotion.”

The whole shoot was fast, as Eisenberg didn’t have time to muck about and often shot just one take of a scene. “She and Paul Giamatti are so good on take one,” said Eisenberg. “It’s embarrassing. This is a small budget movie. The first two days of our schedule, we filmed in one day. The first two days, which were big ensemble musical numbers, we filmed in one day because we were prepared as a crew, but the actors were perfect on take one.”

Moore didn’t feel rushed. “It doesn’t feel, working with Jesse, like you’re moving too fast,” she said. “Working in independent film, you’re always moving quickly because you need to. You don’t have the time. You don’t have the money. So you want to move through things quickly. But I never felt rushed with him. I never I never felt like I was leaving something, that I didn’t get to complete something.”

The challenge for Moore, as it is for every actor shooting out of order, was making her character changes seamless. Eisenberg started with the theater scenes, then shot her family, and then jumped into Mona’s increasing weirdness. “The hardest thing always is making sure that you’ve pitched things correctly,” Moore said. “You want to make sure that you’ve managed to match it to another scene. So it was always hard to figure out. That’s our job.”

Eisenberg wanted Mona to move from looking quite childlike in the beginning, and swiftly move through her evolving transformations. Moore had fun with the costumes, figuring out the crazy wigs, makeup, and outfits in the second half. “Jesse had written that Mona electrocutes herself and destroys her hair, and that she was going to get her hair cut,” said Moore, “but he hadn’t written in a color change. We were messing around with it and trying to figure out what to do with hair and makeup and wardrobe and we had this idea that by having her dye her hair dark and chop it off, I wanted to homage the great ladies of musical theater, Liza Minnelli and Chita Rivera and Patti LuPone. It’s a wonderful Easter egg for that character.”

Making everything easier for Moore was actor-turned-director Eisenberg, who’d tell her when to ‘raise the stakes’ or ‘go hotter.’ “He’s maybe the most collaborative person I’ve ever worked with my entire life,” said Moore. “He listens to everyone. He asks people for opinions. He’ll ask people for notes, in a way that I would be like, ‘you don’t have to do that. This is your movie. It’s your creation.’ But he does collate opinion in a way. He’s fascinating in his generosity.”

Needless to say, singing her centerpiece song (“This City May Have Chewed Me Up (But It Hasn’t Spit Me Out”) was nervous-making, given that Moore was surrounded by Broadway super-belters like Tony winners Danny Burstein and Bonnie Tillman. “These people have the voices of angels,” said Moore. “When I did sing that song for the first time, and they’re all sitting there watching me, I think they did feel worried for me because they have so much ease. The song that she sings is so beautiful. In the singing of the song, she’s attaching her own emotional experience suddenly to the song. She understands the song in the moment she’s singing that. Somehow she wants more, and she knows that this is an avenue for her to find that. And so it’s a journey back to herself: finding that creativity and connection and community brings her to an essential sense of self.”

If degree of difficulty counts with Oscar voters, Moore may have that advantage.
