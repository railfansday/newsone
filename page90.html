Robert Pattinson Is The New James Bond

📅 Nov 16, 2026  |  Category: Entertainment  |  Read Source →

It looks like Robert Pattinson is trading in his bat mobile for an Aston Martin. After a highly scrutinized and lengthy audition process involving some of Hollywood's most sought after leading men, it was the Batman star who emerged at the eleventh hour to nab the highly sought after role of M16 agent James Bond.

“James Bond is one of the most legendary characters in cinema, and to be entrusted with 007 is the honor of a lifetime,” said Pattinson in a statement. “I’m deeply grateful to Denis, Amy, David, Tanya and everyone at Amazon MGM Studios for their belief in me. To say this role is a dream come true is an understatement. I couldn’t be more excited about this partnership and the work ahead with this fantastic team.”

Created by Ian Fleming for his 1953 novel Casino Royale, James Bond, the British intelligence officer who lends his immense talents to M16, is one of the most recognizable characters in the modern world. Figuring prominently in twelve of Fleming’s novels and two short stories, Bond is both tall, dark, and handsome and calm, cool, and collected—the platonic ideal of a world class spy. Bond leapt from page to screen in 1962 with the Eon-produced Dr. No, spawning one of the biggest media franchises in history. Since its inception, the twenty-five Bond films have grossed over $7 billion dollars world wide.

Pattinson follows in the suave footsteps of Daniel Craig, who played James Bond for five films, from 2006's Casino Royale until 2022's No Time To Die where (spoiler alert) Craig's 007 dies while protecting the world from a global terrorist organization. Around No Time To Die, there was another shake up in Bond ownership when Amazon acquired Bond's parent studio, MGM, in an $8.5 billion. Barbara Broccoli, daughter of Eon founder Albert R. “Cubby” Broccoli, producer of the first Bond film and her half brother, Michael G. Broccoli, Barbara held the rights for the franchise for almost 30 years, and pledged to work with Amazon after founder Jeff Bezos. However, in February 2025, the Broccolis announced that they’d be stepping back from the franchise and ceding complete creative control to Amazon MGM. At the Oscars a month later, the Broccoli family was honored with a bizarre musical tribute to James Bond, starring Margaret Qualley, Doja Cat, and Lisa, unofficially ushering in a new era in the Bond Cinematic Universe.

Although a new era of Bond loomed, Amazon MGM had one major problem: they needed a new Bond. Ahead of No Time To Die, Craig had announced that he was stepping away from the franchise, leaving the producers with a 007 sized hole to fill. Before she stepped away, Broccoli insinuated that this new era of Bond was looking for a younger actor, in their 30s, to take over the reins from Craig, who was 51 when he retired from M16.

Technically, they landed on someone slightly outside their preferred age range; Pattinson is 40 years old. Just don't tell him that. During Vanity Fair's lie detector test with Jennifer Lawrence while promoting their film Die My Love, Pattinson insisted he was from a different generation. I am Gen Z," he said to Lawrence. "I am." Hey, you're only as old as you feel. In the same video, Pattinson said he “loves taking jobs of younger actors," which he managed to do with his Bond casting. Good for Pattinson.

Almost every leading man in Hollywood's name came up as a potential predecessor to Craig—except for Pattinson, who the masses assumed was out of contention due to the fact that he locked into playing another beloved fictional character with massive IP: Batman. Pattinson's take on the caped crusader in Matt Reeve's The Batman (2022) was a major success, but several production delays on The Batman Part II, and a shakeup at Batman's studio DC—last week, it was announced that co-CEO's of DC James Gunn and Peter Safran were ousted from the studio—meant that there might be time in Pattinson's schedule for some sexy espionage.

Still, it was far from a done deal that Pattinson would land 007. In recent years, some fans have advocated for a twist on the classic Bond formula. Lashawna Lynch made history as Nomi, the first Black person or woman to ever to hold the 007 moniker in No Time To Die. But Nomi is not James Bond. There was a seemingly endless period when the world was convinced that Idris Elba—arguably the most tall, dark, and handsome man to ever exist—would take on the mantle at some point, but while Elba auditioned, it wasn’t meant to be. Now, at 53, Elba has seemingly aged out of the role. (For the record, Elba thought the next Bond should be a woman.) Elba wasn’t the only name bandied about in recent years. It was reported that Aaron Taylor Johnson was close to signing a contract to take over the role in 2023, but those reports turned out to be false.

Although a favorite at the beginning, Jacob Elordi reportedly didn't screen test for the role. Riz Ahmed made an Emmy-nominated television show, Bait, inspired by the idea of a British Pakastani actor getting cast as Bond. Other actors rumored to be in the mix over the course of the process include, but are not limited to Tom Francis, Harris Dickinson and apparently Harry Styles. At the end of the day five actors screen tested for the role: Slow Horse's Jack Lowden, One Night Only's Callum Turner, Disclosure Day's Josh O'Connor, Superman's Nicholas Hoult, and Pattison. Hoult and Pattinson were the last two to screen test for the role, implying that the powers that be at Amazon MGM may not have been happy with the three options presented and sent a hail mary. When Puck reported that Pattinson was able to negotiate a 3 picture deal rather than a 4 picture deal despite the fact that he commanded a much higher salary then the other options, it looked like the writing was on the wall that Pattinson would be the next Bond.

It also helps that Pattinson is having a career high at the moment, having starred in a handful of the most critically acclaimed and commercially successful films of 2026. He began the year by turning his controversial A24 comedy The Drama with Zendaya into a box office success, generating $134 million worldwide on a budget of $28 million. He then followed that up with a deliciously sniveling turn in Christopher Nolan's epic The Odyssey, an Oscar front-runner which earned $1.8 billion at the box office. Then there's Primetime, Lance Oppenheim's To Catch A Predator drama which stars Pattinson as host Chris Hansen. (Yes, in a way Chris Hansen is now one degree from being James Bond. Let that sink in). Primetime hit big with $61 million globally on an $18 million budget, and is earning Pattinson leading actor buzz. On top of it all, he's still got Dune: Part 3 with Villeneuve coming down the pipeline in December.

Ultimately, the Twilight star was handpicked by the powers that be at Amazon MGM, producers Amy Pascal, David Heyman, executive producer Tanya Lapointe, and director Denis Villeneuve, who is helming the next iteration of the series based on a script from screenwriter Steven Knight. Given the fact that Pattinson is starring in Villeneuve's highly-anticipated Dune: Part 3 it might not be that much of a surprise that the director chose Pattinson as his next Bond. “Before anything else, I want to express my deep gratitude to the remarkable actors who joined us on this journey," said Villeneuve in a statement. "We were surrounded by extraordinary talent, but there can only be one 007. I am overjoyed to welcome Robert Pattinson as our new James Bond. He has the timeless soul of 007 and brings a spirit that is completely new. I cannot wait to begin.”

Pattinson enters a long lineage of spectacularly suave spies—some who lasted longer than others in Bond’s impeccably tailored suits. The first ever James Bond was, of course, Sean Connery, who went on to star in seven Bond films, including From Russia with Love (1963), Goldfinger (1964), and Diamonds Are Forever (1971). Connery was followed by George Lazenby, who starred in a single Bond film, On Her Majesty’s Secret Service (1969), before passing the reins back to Connery. The third Bond was Roger Moore, who took over for 1973’s Live and Let Die, and appeared in a total of seven Bond films, most notably The Spy Who Loved Me (1977) and Octopussy (1983).

After Moore came Timothy Dalton, who became 007 with 1987’s The Living Daylights, then threw in the towel after 1989’s License to Kill. Millennials will fondly remember the next Bond: Pierce Brosnan, who starred as the sexy spy in four films from 1995’s Golden Eye to 2002’s Die Another Day, until Craig took over in 2006.

Will Pattison's Bond be more debonair like Brosnan, who told VF he was “eagerly” awaiting the Bond casting news, or brooding like Craig? Will he be remembered as a rightful heir to Connery's throne, or will his Bond feel closer to Austin Powers in The Spy who Shagged Me? In any case, lets hope Pattinson prefers his martinis shaken not stirred. He's going to be drinking a lot of them for the foreseeable future.

My murderous wine merchant whines for a chance at republican rule in Crusader Kings 3’s Silk and Silver expansion, which isn't only about the money

📅 Nov 16, 2026  |  Category: Business  |  Read Source →

Longing for the Venetian Dogehouse, stuck in the Venetian doghouse

The tentacles of my trade empire snake up towards France in the west, down towards Africa in the south, and off into Hungary in the east. Yet, I still can’t reach out and grasp the throne that sits right in front of me. I’m Marco Sanudo, and the throne of the serene republic of Venice eludes me.

I’ve shed blood in its name, the blood of someone else named Marco no less. I’ve managed to avoid punishment for that crime, despite the fact it earned me a fiefdom to rule while I’ve tried to up my patrician score enough to be the lucky party the next time a ruling Doge dies in office (or runs out their 10 year term). Sadly, Doge Leonardo, the mysterious masked lord who’s had an iron grip on power since the first couple of old blokes to hold the position in this playthrough popped their clogs, is proving incredibly tough to oust.

It should, in theory, help that he’s my best mate, thanks to a well-timed feast. It certainly did in being granted a pardon for offing that other Marco, but not even that and a plague which infected nearly everyone in the realm aside from me have paved the way to Dogedom. In the meantime, I’ve striven to gain wealth and influence. I’ve worked up to having four trade routes running at once, with goods like wine, iron, and textiles flowing from market to market across southern Europe and bringing a steady supply of coin back to my Venetian estate. I’ve built trade posts at the extremities of my routes, giving me the option of starting from further afield and reaching new lands if the cash flow dries up closer to home.

This is where Crusader Kings 3’s Silk and Silver expansion has blended a bit with the game’s traditional feudal moneymaking to supercharge my coffers. The land holding I killed a dude for, the Proveditorate of Zara, means I’ve also had tax money from modern day Croatia to add to both my trade profits and the income my domicile generates on its own. I am, as the medieval kids say, minted.

Getting to the point of serious pocket clinkage is, as you might imagine given Silk and Silver’s focus on filling the game’s world with travelling merchant companies and wheeler-dealing republican patrician families, the beating heart of the expansion. A near-constant stream of notifications about prices of different goods I’m trying to hawk in different locations encourage me to regularly prune and re-work my routes so that max profits are being earned and no carrying capacity’s being wasted. The bubbling up of wars and plagues impacts prices in affected cities, making it a never-ending battle if you’re desperate to optimise everything within an inch of its life. I’ve found that degree of micromanagement unnecessary if the goal is just to keep a decent profit margin ticking over. Just remember to replace the trader assigned to a route if they die, recall them every few years so they don’t stress themselves to death, and check in every so often to re-evaluate whether you’re running goods that are suitably in demand.

If you stick at it for a decent amount of time, with a certain type of good - alcohol or jewellery, for example - your expertise in dealing in them increases, making it easier to sell for better prices. Trade routes left running between the same markets also gain boons from being in place for a long time, offering a counterpoint to rejigging them every five seconds to wring out a few more coins. Most of my routes have run along a path that includes stops at four markets, with the occasional location boasting a monopoly I’ve bargained for with the local ruler, so I don’t have to worry about competition at every stop.

When you’re playing as a merchant who isn’t locked into the ruling inner circle of a republic, making your routes as lengthy as possible is one of the three moneymaking areas your company can specialise in. That first one’s caravaneering, and is the one I leant into in a non-Marco Sanudo playthrough. Beyond that, you can become an expert loan shark, lending cash to rulers or paying their ransoms in order to recoup interest and collect hooks you can use for favours. Manufacturing expensive artifacts that can be sold individually to collectors for a profit is the third option. The latter two I’ve only dabbled in a little bit as of writing, having earned enough gold mostly from caravanneering to buy the land containing the city with my home market and be anointed a Sheikh. So, beginning as a landless merchant is a nice alternative to Crusader Kings 3’s long-established wandering adventurer starting point, with both allowing you to work your way up from lowly serf to landed lordly dynasty in your location of choice.

If you’re after a more specific historical scenario, Silk and Silver has Guelphs and Ghibellines. This is where we find Marco Sanudo, as well as the other patrician family head I’ve played as thus far, Speronella Dalesmanni. The setup is that the Pope and the Holy Roman Emperor have had a bit of a tiff, essentially rooted in who should have certain powers, with different cities and powerful families in northern Italy taking either the Guelph (Pope-likers) or Ghibelline (Holy Roman Empire-enjoyers) side depending on what suits their goals or principles. I’ll be honest, aside from being another way to easily earn friends or enemies in the ruthless crucible of merchant republican politics, where votes and reputation-bolstering aid from other nobles are key, the situation’s not had a hugely obvious bearing on my playthroughs thus far.

I think the closest it’s come is during my Dalesmanni run, if largely due to the existence of the By God Alone expansion allowing a new branch of Catholicism, dubbed Italian Christianity, to be created. The initial conversion push from the bishop responsible led a number of my fellow northern Italian nobles away from the Roman Rite headed by his Popeliness, making the Guelph/Ghibelline split more clearly defined.

In the early hours of both my Sanudo and Dalesmanni playthroughs, I’ve been far more focused on simply trying to push the respective patrician families up the hill from posh toffs with naught but a vote and a manor to elected rulers of their great cities, with land holdings to match. Speronella’s situation in Padua is made incredibly tough by the fact she’s a woman in a world where every succession rule is designed to put blokes first, so I’ve had more success with Marco and his quest to become the Doge of Venice.

As the head of a patrician family, one of select few elite clans who steer the oligarchical republic, I was faced with earning Marco enough respect among his fellow family heads that they’d consider voting him to the top of the succession list, thus securing his power whenever the current Doge got the boot. Pulling that off, while also not kowtowing to the requests and schemes of those same peers, isn’t easy. A large cocktail of factors go into determining whether another noble likes you enough to vote in support of your succession. There’s your patrician rating, which is upped by doing things which specifically show you to be good at this Italian noble house lark, such as establishing trade monopolies for the republic, gaining the Pope’s favour, or having close relatives who also hold key positions in the republic. However, how close a friendship you have with the target and how their character traits align with you also play a huge rule.

Thus it was that, at one point, Marco was best mates with the current Doge and had a decent patrician rating, but had only amassed 68 of the 72 points needed for said Doge to vote for him because the Doge was generous, and therefore didn’t care for Marco’s ambitiousness. That same Doge either liked or respected Marco enough to grant him a council position and the status of regent, but he would not back Marco to replace him permanently for love nor money. This is the same man who liked Marco so much that when I pulled off that not-so-sneaky murder on another noble house head in order to inherit the keys to the Proveditorate of Zara, the Doge was instantly willing to pardon me and prevent Marco suffering any punishment for the crime.

Not even a couple of serious plagues hitting Venice, which at one point had all three people ahead of Marco in the queue suffering from smallpox, could get him on that throne. Eventually, it took a bit of lucky timing when his Doge mate died of old age - a few years after beating smallpox - to finally install Marco as Doge. Given that delayed promotion, as of writing I’ve not yet had enough time in the big chair to give a proper verdict on how it differs from regular feudal rulerdom in the long run.

What I can do instead is tell you a bit about the fun of council votes. The same pool of voters who in a republic like Venice, get a say in who succeeds the ruler, also vote on the passage of laws and decrees. One proposal you can put forward is a vote to change who exactly is allowed a seat in this voting pool. It defaults to just being heads of patrician families, but can be extended to include all vassals, or tightened up so only the Doge’s councillors or the ruler themselves have a say, that being the way despotism lies. As you can imagine, passing a change to who can vote can drastically change the state of play, either tightening up the number of folks who might oppose or support you, or bringing new folks into play in a fashion that dilutes individual power, but gives you more pieces on the board to mind or manipulate to your advantage.

I’ve found it suitably tough to orchestrate successful votes to change laws and customs which medieval Italian noble families, expectedly, have firmly entrenched stances on. Getting rid of checks and balances, such as the requirement to vote on banishments or wars - so the Doge can rule as a despot - has proven one such challenge, as has changing laws governing men being prioritised over women. I tried to pass equal succession as a substitute for men being put first while playing as Speronella, but met an insurmountable wall of resistance due to the region’s culture and faith. So, all I could do was try to put my young male heir in the best position possible to make the sorts of gains I couldn’t.

Much easier to weasel into place, either through some canny swaying or the offering of hooks, are laws giving state backing or other priority to trading in certain types of goods. Alcohol, for instance, which certainly hasn’t hurt the profits of Marco’s two wine trade routes.

Thus, cash continues to flow through the tentacles of my trade empire in sizable globs. As he’s now Doge, I’ve got Marco using the ample funds of Venice’s treasury to build a new piazza and have him focused on making the city’s already-strong trade league even more formidable, by attracting new members from the few other northern Italian blocs.

While I’m fairly sure there are folks who’ll find Silk and Silver's constant barrage of trade route notifications annoying, or that their power-grabbing skills and sheer multitude of money-making means combine to make playthroughs less challenging than they could be, so far I've found it another enjoyably sonorous string to Crusader Kings 3’s bow. Above all else, playing as a merchant gives more reason to invest in stewardship skills beyond just maximising your tax yields, while the republican politics of the DLC's main region fleshes out rewarding intrigue-focused builds. Diplomacy and martial skills are less key, but still have their place at times.

If you’re an absolute stickler for historical accuracy, you may well find other things to gripe about, but aside from the Guelph and Ghibelline conflict fading into the background, I’ve found plenty to like in my quest to become top Doge.

How Much Does The World's Largest Commercial Engine Cost?

📅 Nov 16, 2026  |  Category: Business  |  Read Source →

The headline figure that the incoming GE Aerospace 134-inch GE9X turbofan is worth $42-45 million is misleading. Understood as a list price, it is ok, but compared with the market value of a GE90 or Trent XWB, it is not. While the engine is likely to be the most expensive engine on the market, it is still likely to belong to the same tier as the competing Trent XWB-97, with the difference in value measured in single digits of millions of dollars, not tens of millions.

It is also incredibly difficult to disentangle the price of an engine from its financing, airframe, status as a bundled spare, long-term maintenance contracts, and bulk orders. It is almost unheard of for an airline to walk into a GE Aerospace showroom, point to a large aero engine, and pay upfront for one engine with no maintenance, tooling, spare components, or other services. Here is how much a GE9X is worth in 2026, attempting to avoid apples-to-oranges comparisons.

The GE Aerospace GE9X is the world's largest commercial jet engine by diameter, and by thrust produced during test runs, it is not the world's largest by certified thrust. The world's largest aeroengine by certified thrust is its predecessor, the GE90-115B. The GE90-115B was built to power the Boeing 777-200LR, Boeing 777-300ER, and Boeing 777F freighter. It comes with a certified thrust of 115,300 lbf at sea level.

Its GE90-94B counterpart is certified for 93,700 lbf and powered the previous Boeing 777-200 and Boeing 777-300 generation of Triple Sevens. GE Aerospace says it has test-run the new GE9X generation of the GE90 family at 134,300 lbf. It claims that this is the "highest thrust of any commercial jet engine." But producing the highest thrust in test runs and actually being certified for that are two different things.

The GE9X has a certified thrust of 110,000 lbf, placing it at 5,300 lbf less than its predecessor. GE Aerospace's new variant will provide the incoming Boeing 777X family with 5% fuel-specific fuel consumption improvement versus "any twin-aisle engine available" (read: Rolls-Royce Trent XWB), and offers up to 10% specific consumption improvement versus the older GE90-115B. It achieves an approximate 10:1 bypass ratio.

Safe Fly Aviation writes, "Aircraft engine values are among the most important metrics in commercial aviation finance. A single powerplant can cost more than a private jet, and in today's market, some engine types are worth more than the aircraft they power." According to its analysis, the General Electric GE9X is the world's most valuable engine at around $42–45 million at list price. It says, "Its list price of $42–45 million per engine exceeds the list price of many private jets, making it the pinnacle of aircraft engine values."

For reference, it lists the GE90-115BL as having a "Full-Life value" of over $32 million and a Half-Life Market Value increasing by 60% since 2023. The only other extra-large modern engine is the Rolls-Royce Trent XWB, "valued at approximately $25 million per unit." There are some glaring issues with these comparisons, and they do not offer an apples-to-apples comparison.

The GE9X is not yet on the market, as the Boeing 777X is yet to enter service. Therefore, the only data point is its list price, while the market values for the other two engines are being used. I have an accounting background, and this is a terrible comparison. List prices are little more than brochure listings where manufacturers and sellers often inflate the true market value of their products. The market value, what companies actually pay, is much more consequential.

Discover why the Boeing 777X depends entirely on the GE9X, the world’s largest and most advanced commercial jet engine ever built.

In its H2 2026 update, IBA states, "Current widebody engine values continue to grow strongly due to new aircraft production delays. Existing engine retention remains high, MRO demand is strong, and spare engine supply is limited." The value of an engine is not just its performance or the cost of manufacture, but also its scarcity. This is the classic economics 101 of supply and demand. Currently, demand is high, and supply is constrained, driving up costs.

Put another way, the delay of the Boeing 777X and the fact that the GE9X is not yet in service is making GE90-115BL and Trent XWB engines more valuable. Airlines are being forced to keep their older GE90-powered 777s in service for longer, which necessarily includes the engines. Low spare engine availability for the GE90-115BL is driving up the value of the engine.

GE Aerospace (revenue-sharing partners: IHI Corp., Safran, MTU Aero Engines)

From $18 million+ (line-fit for Emirates) to $37 million (standalone spare engine)

While the A350 is a new aircraft with no airline seeking to retire them, the fact that it is one of the only larger widebodies available, given production constraints of the 787 and no 777Xs, is driving up their value. IBA states that the GE90-115BL full-life values exceed $35 million, and its long-term lease extensions are being observed between $250,000 and $300,000 per month. Meanwhile, MBA Aviation lists the H1 2026 lease rates for the Trent XWB-97 with a market lease rate of $287,000.

In commercial aviation, virtually no airline or lessor pays the published Original Engine Manufacturer (OEM) list price for a factory-new widebody aero engine. In other words, the GE9X is not really worth $42 million. GE Aerospace can slap any value that it claims to value it at, but that is divorced from the reality of the market. The 'real' price is the 'discounted' price that airlines and lessors actually pay for them.

As a rule of thumb, when engines are purchased as part of a new aircraft order, especially on competitive twin-engine aircraft, OEM's routinely provide 'concessions' (aka discounts) of 40% to 46% or more off their inflated catalog list prices. However, the discount is typically less on aircraft like the A350 or 777, where Rolls-Royce or GE Aerospace are exclusive engine suppliers. That said, there are still typically substantial discounts.

The true value of the GE9X will become much more apparent when it is in service and starts appearing in secondary markets. Until then, there is simply a lack of data compared with the XWB and GE90. The value of the engine also varies by whether it comes as part of the aircraft or separately. This is similar to a car. It is typically much cheaper to purchase a car fully assembled and ready to go than purchasing each individual part, including the engine, and then self-assembling it.

The largest and most powerful commercial engine ever built, the GE9X is by no means cheap

Speaking of list prices per seat (not engines per se), Sirius Capital Aviation writes, "list prices were far higher than any realistic estimate of actual prices (typical discounts were already over 50%) but they did provide a limited market signal of relative prices." Put another way, the list price of $42 million for the GE9X is a starting figure that needs to be discounted to find out what it is really worth. It is absolutely not a number that can be compared in good faith with the market price of a Trent XWB-97.

Another issue is that it can't even be compared with XWB list prices, as Rolls-Royce does not publish standalone engine list prices and Airbus ceased publishing its annual aircraft list price catalog in 2018. Using a reverse calculation, the implied list value for the XWB would likely fit into the $35 to $40 million range. This is back-of-the-envelope math and should not be taken too seriously. Given that the GE9X is the sole engine for the 777X, an expected discount off list price of around 35% to 40% can be expected.

Importantly, the GE9X may be the only engine on the 777X, but the 777-9 still has to compete with the XWB-powered A350-1000. If this is the correct range, then the real implied market rate of around $22 million to $28 million per engine can be expected. It is possible that 35-40% is too steep, and so the market price may be higher. In other words, the value of new XWB and GE9X engines may be within a reasonably modest margin of each other.

Also, engines are seldom just purchased alone. They are part of bundles that include other engines, the aircraft themselves, spares, and service agreements. Engine manufacturers may sell engines at or even below manufacturing cost, as their margins are made up on the lucrative 15–25-year-long servicing of the engines. Spare engines purchased directly from the OEM generally get smaller discounts than line-fit installed engines.

Even looking at announced contracts is problematic. For example, GE announced Singapore Airlines had ordered 22 more GE9X engines in 2022 for $2.8 billion at list price. That works out to more than $100 million per engine, but it also includes a 12-year maintenance contract (called TrueChoice). The price of engines likely varies considerably by airline.

Emirates has placed orders for around 45% of the entire global 777X backlog. This shifts much of the bargaining power to Emirates from Boeing and GE. Much of the program is reliant on Emirates (and Qatar), and Emirates knows it. While the numbers are not available, it is reasonable to assume Emirates is purchasing GE9X engines for much less than Silk Way West, which is ordering two aircraft with four engines.

The Leonid meteor shower peaks Nov. 17-18 sending shooting stars and fireballs across the autumn sky as Earth passes through a stream of debris left by Comet 55P/Tempel-Tuttle.

Up to 15 meteors could be spotted per hour under perfect dark sky conditions on the night of Nov. 17-18, according to the International Meteor Organisation. NASA, meanwhile, has given a more conservative rate of less than three meteors per hour — time will tell.

Leonid meteors appear to race away from a point of origin, or radiant, in the constellation Leo, which glistens high above the southeastern horizon in the hours before dawn in mid-November. Both Jupiter and Mars will be separated by just a few degrees in the constellation Leo on the night of the peak, making it particularly easy to find in 2026!

The best time to spot Leonids is in the hours before dawn on Nov. 18, when the radiant will reach its highest point in the southeastern sky, maximizing the amount of meteors that may be seen skimming through Earth's atmosphere. By then, the waxing moon will have set, leaving darker skies and a better chance of spotting fainter meteors.

Shooting stars that appear close to the radiant will strike Earth almost head-on, leaving the shortest trails. Instead, find a patch of sky a short distance away from the constellation Leo, where the meteors will be at their most impressive, and give your eyes 20-30 minutes to fully adapt to the dark.

As if shooting stars weren't enough, the Leonid shower is also known for spawning dazzling fireballs and long-lasting Earth-grazing meteors, which strike our atmosphere at an oblique angle, leaving long trains as they skim through before passing back out into space.

The shower is also capable of producing magnificent meteor storms when Earth passes through particularly dense trails of debris left behind by Comet 55P/Tempel-Tuttle, which orbits the sun approximately every 33 years. Sadly, we'll have to wait a bit for the next big show. According to the Planetary Society, another Leonid meteor storm could occur as early as 2031, while a study released in August 2026 predicts potentially spectacular displays in 2033 and 2034.

Instagram announced a change on Monday that may spell the end for users being nudged to visit a creator’s “link in bio.” At least when they’re promoting products sold on Amazon.

Starting today, creators and businesses can attach multiple Amazon affiliate links to their Instagram content—up to 30 per video or 20 per regular feed post. (Affiliate links let creators earn a commission on any resulting sales.) To participate in this Amazon program, creators must be US-based adults with a professional account that has over 1,000 followers.

Instagram’s parent company Meta dropped this shopping-focused change right before the holiday season really kicks off. As creators look to cash in on Amazon's sales, you might expect an avalanche of affiliate posts as you sip hot cocoa and scroll through Reels. But Instagram claims this won’t be the case.

“We don’t expect a sudden influx of shopping content from this launch,” Instagram spokesperson Megan Buckley tells WIRED. “Instagram’s recommendation systems will continue to recommend content based on people’s interests and what we think they’ll enjoy and find valuable. This update makes creators’ product recommendations easier to shop.”

For years, Instagram users could attach a single external link to their bio information. This led to the rise of bundlers like Linktree, where creators could create simple one-page sites with multiple links to content, like shopping recommendations or news articles. In 2023, Instagram expanded the number of links allowed in profiles to five. This most recent update could change how it feels to scroll Instagram, moving more shopping nudges from Amazon storefronts hidden in the bio to direct links on the viral videos.

In an example shared by Meta, a woman recording a Reel leans into her camera as she applies makeup. The caption reads: “My favorite lip tint these days!” Above the creator’s name, a small image tile pops up with the Amazon product. Tap the product and a page opens inside Instagram with the price. Tap one of the listed items, and Instagram will bump you over to the Amazon app or website to complete your purchase.

This change comes as social media shopping habits continue to expand and evolve. TikTok, one of Meta’s primary competitors, has been quite successful in expanding users’ purchasing habits through the app. Around this time last year, an analytics firm estimated that $19 billion worth of products were sold globally through TikTok Shop, putting it roughly the same size as eBay. Unlike TikTok, however, Instagram will not take a cut of the sales (at least for now). Reddit is also experimenting with how users shop heading into this holiday season, recently announcing Reddit Reviews, which uses generative AI to summarize user sentiment about products.

For over a decade, Instagram developers have searched for ways to streamline how users spend money through the app. In 2016, WIRED wrote about how the social media app added select commerce links from brands like Warby Parker, a fairly novel attempt at the time to get people to buy stuff through social media. A few years later, the Instagram experience morphed into something like a “digital mall” with in-app checkouts.

This latest attempt moves links from creator bios and places them directly in users' feeds. While this could be a boon for creators, the platform’s implementation could annoy users, who are already inundated with ads online. We’ll see if this change clicks.
