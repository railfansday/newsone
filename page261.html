Prince William and Kate Middleton’s Full Relationship Timeline

The last few years have seen a lot of enormous changes in the British Royal Family, an entity known more for tradition than rapid growth. After the loss of Queen Elizabeth II, who ruled over the U.K. for most of the 21st century, some people may be looking for some stability. If there’s any couple in the family who offers that, it’s Prince William and Kate Middleton.

William and Kate have had what appears on the outside to be a bit of a fairytale romance, from the storybook wedding to the picturesque family of five they have now. While they’ve faced hard times like everyone else, there’s something about them that has captivated people since the beginning of their relationship. Here’s a look back at their journey, from college sweethearts to royal power couple.

Prince William and Kate Middleton met while both studying at St. Andrews University in Scotland. Rumor has it that William was won over after seeing Kate modeling at a student fashion show for charity (a show for which he reportedly paid £200 for a front-row seat).

Other rumors suggest that her reason for being at St. Andrews was entirely related to meeting her future husband.

The Crown historian Robert Lacey wrote in his 2020 book Battle of Brothers: William and Harry—The Inside Story of a Family in Tumult, that Kate was trying to manifest her future relationship with William when she was only 19.

“Kate was very strategic,” Lacey said. “She gave up her place at one of Britain’s premier universities, Edinburgh, and opted for another one [St. Andrews] that probably didn’t have the same academic standard. She had already been to Edinburgh to get her flat accommodations sorted out but switched universities and switched her courses to history of art because that was William’s. She delayed her own education by a year and even takes the chance she won’t get into St. Andrews.”

She was not the only person making these choices. Applications to the school reportedly went up by 44 percent after it was announced that was William’s choice of university. They might not all have been marriage hopefuls, but some probably were like Kate.

Hello! magazine reported that Prince William was moving from his dorm into a flat with three college friends, one of them “a pretty 20-year-old brunette” who turned out to be Kate Middleton.

He allegedly started out as just her friend, before seeing her walk in a fashion show in this see-through dress on March 26, 2002.

“She’s there and about to walk down the runway on the fashion show, and she strips off an extra layer of clothing,” Lacey explained. “Here is a very strategic lady who knows what she wants and has got it.”

William and Kate were photographed together for the first time during a ski trip in Klosters, causing then-Prince Charles to threaten to sever ties with The Sun, the outlet that published the photos. Charles was said to have been “very disappointed” by the breach in an agreement between the press and Buckingham Palace that ensures royal children can continue their education without having their privacy intruded upon.

Both William and Kate graduated from St. Andrews. The Middletons later shared a photo of them together on the day.

The couple attended Camilla’s daughter Laura Parker-Bowles’ wedding, marking one of their first big public appearances together. It also sparked speculation that they were serious enough to one day walk down the aisle themselves.

In 2007, William and Kate took a time-out and broke up for a couple of months. Kate took a vacation to Ireland with her friends afterwards, while William awkwardly fielded questions about “how things are going” with Kate.

Kate later said in a 2010 interview after their engagement that in hindsight, she thinks it was healthy for them to have time apart.

“I think I at the time wasn't very happy about it, but actually it made me a stronger person...You find out things about yourself that maybe you hadn’t realized,” she said. “Or I think you can get quite consumed by a relationship when you’re younger. I really valued that time for me as well although I didn't think it at the time, looking back on it."

By July of that same year, rumors began to swirl about a possible reconciliation as Kate was seen attending a Princess Diana tribute concert—though she sat two rows behind William with her sister, Pippa.

The two were seen together again, back where it all began: the ski slopes. Photos show Kate and William together in Klosters as sources told newspapers that they were very much back together.

William graduated from his RAF training, with Kate at his side.

William proposed to Kate while on a trip to Kenya, with Princess Diana’s sapphire engagement ring. “We had a little private time away together with some friends, and I just decided it was the right time really,” William later said in their engagement interview. “We’d been talking about marriage or a while, so it wasn’t a massively big surprise, but I took her up somewhere nice in Kenya.”

William and Kate got married in a fairytale ceremony at Westminster Abbey witnessed by nearly 72 million people worldwide. The bride did her own makeup, the groom wore sweat pads so that he wouldn’t sweat out of his uniform, and Ellie Goulding performed at the reception.

In marrying William, Kate gained the title the Duchess of Cambridge, the Countess of Strathearn, and Lady Carrickfergus.

Extra-Fluffy Cinnamon Rolls

📅 Dec 22, 2022  |  Category: Automotive  |  Read Source →

At first glance, these homemade cinnamon rolls have all the classics: buttery, golden-brown dough, a gooey cinnamon-and-brown-sugar filling, and a generous swoop of cream cheese frosting. But a couple of smart moves make them especially fluffy, with a tender crumb that stays soft well into the next day (assuming there are any left).

One is tangzhong, a Chinese technique that cooks a small portion of flour and milk into a thick paste before it’s added to the dough. The precooked starch helps the dough hold on to more moisture, resulting in pillowy, supple cinnamon rolls.

The other is brown butter, which gives the filling a nutty, toasty edge. The tangy cream cheese frosting balances all that richness; add the optional lemon zest for extra brightness.

Can I make these cinnamon rolls the night before?

Yes. You can refrigerate the dough after its first rise or assemble the rolls in their baking pans and refrigerate them before the second rise. Cover tightly with plastic wrap to prevent the dough from drying out. The next day, let the chilled dough sit at room temperature for about an hour before proceeding with the recipe, making sure the shaped rolls have doubled in volume before baking.

This is a soft, moisture-rich dough, but it should become smooth and elastic as you knead it. For the most consistent results, weigh your flour rather than measuring by volume. Also, make sure the tangzhong has thickened into a paste before adding it to the dough: You should be able to see the tracks of your whisk across the bottom of the saucepan. Avoid adding excessive extra flour, which can make the finished rolls dense.

Can I make these cinnamon rolls without a stand mixer?

Yes, though kneading this soft, enriched dough by hand takes considerable time and effort. Several readers have reported success doing so. If using a stand mixer, keep an eye on the motor during the extended kneading time. If it begins to strain or overheat, stop the machine and finish kneading by hand.

Can I use active dry yeast instead of instant yeast?

This recipe calls for instant yeast, which can be mixed directly into the dough. Active dry yeast generally requires activation in warm liquid and may affect rising times, so we recommend sticking with instant yeast for the recipe as written. Whisk ¾ cup whole milk and ¼ cup (31 g) all-purpose flour in a medium saucepan until smooth. Place saucepan over medium heat and cook, whisking constantly, until mixture is thickened and whisk lines are visible on bottom of pan, about 2 minutes. Scrape tangzhong into the bowl of a stand mixer and let cool.

Add 1 large egg, one ¼-oz. envelope instant yeast (about 2¼ tsp.), ⅓ cup (67 g) granulated sugar, 1 Tbsp. Diamond Crystal or 1¾ tsp. Morton kosher salt,  and remaining 1½ cups whole milk to tangzhong and whisk to combine. Add remaining 5½ cups (688 g) all-purpose flour. Fit bowl onto stand mixer; fit mixer with dough hook attachment. Mix on low speed until combined, about 1 minute. Increase speed to medium-low and mix, scraping down sides of bowl occasionally, until dough is smooth and elastic, 18–24 minutes. Pinch off a golf-ball-size piece of dough and stretch between your fingers as thin as possible without breaking. If you can see light through the dough, it’s ready. If not, continue mixing a few more minutes, then try again.

Add ½ cup (1 stick) unsalted butter, room temperature, cut into pieces, to dough and mix on medium speed until incorporated, about 2 minutes. Continue mixing until dough pulls away cleanly from sides of bowl and is no longer tacky to the touch, 5–7 minutes longer. Remove bowl from mixer and cover with a damp kitchen towel. Let sit in a warm spot until doubled in volume, 60–90 minutes.

While the dough is rising, cook ½ cup (1 stick) unsalted butter in a small saucepan over medium heat, stirring occasionally, until butter foams and milk solids turn dark amber, about 4 minutes. Transfer to a heatproof bowl and let cool slightly. Add 1 cup (packed; 200 g) dark brown sugar, 2 Tbsp. ground cinnamon, 2 Tbsp. heavy cream, and ½ tsp. Diamond Crystal or ¼ tsp. Morton kosher salt to butter and mix to combine.

Butter two 9"-diameter cake pans. Punch down dough and transfer to a lightly floured surface. Stretch dough into a rough rectangle; if dough feels stiff, let sit 5–10 minutes to allow gluten to relax. Arrange dough with short side facing you and roll out to a 24x13" rectangle. Spread filling over dough in an even layer.

Starting at edge closest to you, tightly roll up dough into a log; roll log seam side down. Using a sharp serrated knife and long sawing motions, trim ends of log; discard. Using serrated knife or, preferably, unflavored dental floss, cut log in half crosswise, then cut each half into 6 equal rounds. (If using dental floss, slide floss underneath dough, then cross over top and pull ends through.) Nestle 6 rounds, cut side down, in each prepared cake pan. Cover with damp kitchen towels and let sit in a warm spot until doubled in volume, about 30–50 minutes.

Place a rack in middle of oven; preheat to 350°. Bake rolls until golden on top and puffed (don’t be alarmed if they rise dramatically in the center), 25–30 minutes.

Meanwhile, using an electric mixer on medium speed or a whisk, beat 4 Tbsp. unsalted butter, room temperature, and 8 oz. cream cheese, room temperature, in a large bowl, scraping down sides of bowl as needed, until combined. Add 1¼ cups (138 g) powdered sugar, 1 Tbsp. heavy cream, 1 tsp. lemon zest (if using), and ¼ tsp. Diamond Crystal or Morton kosher salt and beat until mixture is smooth and sugar is incorporated. Taste and beat in more powdered sugar (up to ¾ cup) if desired. Thin with more cream as needed, a teaspoonful at a time, until frosting is thick but spreadable.

Let cinnamon rolls cool in pan 5 minutes; spread frosting over.

In the endless iterating that goes into devising a menu for a holiday dinner, there’s one finishing touch that really makes or breaks the vibe: the drinks. The wine and beer will, of course, flow — at these festive events, a bottle of Champagne or sparkling rose is likely to make an appearance — and it’s easy enough to throw together a gin and tonic or screwdriver. But nothing makes a holiday gathering feel chic, totally put together, gloriously intentional, like a batch cocktail, made-to-order martini, or gussied-up punch.

Now, we all have our preferences when it comes to strength, spirit, and format. The host’s bandwidth is also a consideration; do you want to make a lot of one thing, or tailor your options to your guests’ preferences? Those questions are for you to answer, but our staff is here to help by providing this rundown of six tried, vetted, and loved drinks to make at your own celebration.

The French 75 — the effervescent classic cocktail made with gin, Champagne, and lemon — is already one of my easy-drinking favorites, and I like it even better in punch form, accented with an aesthetically pleasing ice block of cheery lemon slices. What makes it 77 instead of 75? The substitution of St-Germain (or any elderflower liqueur) for simple syrup, adding just a tiny touch of herbal complexity. It’s perfect for New Year’s Eve, but just as refreshing at Thanksgiving or any other holiday table where you want something light and zesty. —Hilary Pollack, deputy editor

I grew up guzzling Broguiere’s eggnog, but I love the more tropical combination of rich, creamy coconut milk, rum, and cinnamon in coquito; the Puerto Rican holiday cocktail was first described to me as “eggnog, if it went on vacation,” and I haven’t looked back since. There are two schools of thought on a post-dinner drink: You could go for a bracing digestif to “aid digestion,” or you could indulge in a liquid dessert that swaddles you in sugar, fat, and everything nice. Together, a whole egg and syrup (in this case honey syrup) build a comforting base, while an entire 1.5 ounces of cocktail bitters (as opposed to the usual dash or two) ensure you don’t entirely fall asleep in your glass. Angostura is a great option, delivering some classic holiday spice, but any style of cocktail bitters would probably work, as would an amaro. Dry shaking (shaking the liquid ingredients without ice) is key to achieving a frothy texture, and don’t forget the half-ounce of whiskey, which punches up the rest of the flavors. —Nick Mancall-Bitel, editor

Engineered to contain flames and burn fuel more completely, smokeless fire pits deliver ambient warmth with a lot less smoke in your eyes than a traditional campfire. They achieve this through two points of combustion: The first happens at the bottom, where airflow feeds burning wood; the second occurs at the top, where the fire’s smoke and gas output are burned off. And while no smokeless fire pit is 100 percent smokeless, if you use best practices (like burning dry wood) they come pretty darn close.

We tested smokeless fire pits in our New York and Pennsylvania backyards, building fires rather than judging them on specs alone. Whether you want a smokeless fire pit to heat your outdoor space or be more of an ambient seasonal moment is an important consideration. For long nights by the fire with family and friends, you’ll likely want to invest in a larger model that measures 24 inches in diameter. Ideally, the smokeless fire pit should work right out of the box, with limited-to-no setup, besides setting the pit on a base. It should also break down easily. If you’ll be storing it between fires or seasons, a model that’s easy to disassemble, carry, and put away is key.

Burning pellets instead of wood was the closest we got to a truly smokeless fire during our testing. If you plan to use your fire pit regularly, easy ash removal should be a deciding factor. The last thing you want to have to do is pick up and tip a heavy steel drum over a trashcan in order to empty it of ash. Otherwise, you’ll need a shovel to scoop out the ash.

Material affects how long your new pit will look the way it does on arrival. Painted or powder-coated steel usually costs less, but can scratch and chip with repeat use.

Whatever finish you choose, keeping your smokeless fire pit empty of ash and covered when it’s not in use will help extend its life. To find the best dual-fuel generator for your home, campground, or worksite, consider the factors listed below, including fuel efficiency, fuel tank capacity, power output, and control panel configuration.

Dual-fuel generators have an isolated gasoline tank with a fuel capacity that ranges from as small as 2 gallons to more than 10 gallons. The larger the fuel tank on the generator, the longer the generator can provide gasoline-based power. But, as tank size increases, so does the size of the generator.

Fuel capacity does not affect how much propane you can use with your dual-fuel generator. Propane gas is stored under pressure in canisters, which attach to the generator with a hose and pressure regulator for direct fuel consumption.

The power output on dual-fuel generators is measured in watts. Manufacturers’ specs include peak power output and running power output. Peak power output refers to the maximum number of watts the generator can provide at any one time. Running power output equals the average number of watts produced during use.

Dual-fuel generators generally produce a power output from 1,000 watts to over 12,000 watts, depending on their size. Gasoline produces a higher wattage than propane, so many manufacturers of dual-fuel technology break wattage into propane peak and running outputs and gasoline peak and running outputs.

For greater power output needs, users may need to consider a standby generator rather than a portable dual-fuel option.

You can start your dual-fuel generator with an electric start switch or a recoil cord.

Many dual-fuel generators produce 50 to 90 dB of noise, depending on fuel type (gasoline runs louder than propane) and power output. The most common range is between 65 dB and 75 dB, comparable to the sound of normal conversation.

The quietest dual-fuel generators combine dual-fuel technology and inverter technology to create a dual-fuel inverter generator.

Dual-fuel generators are typically large and heavy, with hoses or tanks that make them difficult to carry and move. Thus, some manufacturers include wheel kits for tilting the generator back and rolling it to the desired location.

You may also be able to purchase a wheel kit separately to add to your existing generator or replace an older wheel kit that doesn’t work well anymore. Either way, a wheel kit makes transporting a portable dual-fuel generator much easier.

Lift hook bars are handy on jobsites, allowing workers to attach the generator to a hook-and-pulley system and raise or lower it instead of carrying it up or down stairs or ladders. In industrial settings, this feature helps raise the generator onto a walkway or platform.

A lift hook bar will increase the generator’s weight (and likely the price), so don’t look for a generator with this feature unless you truly plan to use it.

Most dual-fuel generators are between 22 inches long by 22 inches wide by 22 inches high and 35 inches long by 30 inches wide by 30 inches high. They typically weigh between 100 and 250 pounds.

If you plan to move the generator frequently, figure size and weight prominently into your decision. Make sure the model you choose fits into your vehicle so you can transport it as needed. Remember, however, that larger, heavier motors are also the most powerful. You’ll want to find the right balance of size, weight, and power to meet your needs.

Gasoline and propane each have their pros and cons as fuel options. Gasoline is easier to find, but propane costs less. Propane is a better fuel source in hot summer weather, while gas burns better in the cold. A dual-fuel generator lets you choose one or the other based on immediate circumstances.

Using both fuel sources, one after the other, greatly increases a generator’s runtime. Users can run to the gas station to refuel while the generator keeps providing power on propane.

Before buying a dual-fuel generator, consider the answers to the following frequently asked questions.

A dual-fuel generator operates like a single-fuel generator, except it can burn propane and gasoline to produce electrical energy. It converts mechanical energy produced by the burning fuels to force the movement of electrical charges and generate electricity.

While some single-fuel generators can be adapted for use with natural gas, this isn’t the case with dual-fuel generators. Unless specially designed, dual-fuel generators do not run on natural gas and cannot be adapted for this purpose.

Propane gas is less expensive than gasoline, nearly impossible to spill, and burns cleaner, resulting in less wear on the engine over time. Running a dual-fuel generator on propane will save money but slightly decrease the machine’s peak and running watts.

Runtime is determined by fuel efficiency and fuel tank capacity. On average, a standard dual-fuel generator runs about 8 to 10 hours on propane.

If power is the goal, then the DuroMax dual-fuel generator is one of the best options to help ensure the campsite or essential home appliances have power for hours.

Generators running on gasoline are typically louder than those running on propane since burning propane requires less energy.
