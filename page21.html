New Dungeons & Dragons Live-Action Series Officially Replaces Netflix's Forgotten Realms

📅 Nov 21, 2026  |  Category: Entertainment  |  Read Source →

The Mists are apparently rolling into Netflix.

A live-action Dungeons & Dragons series based on Ravenloft is in development at Netflix, giving the tabletop gaming franchise’s most famous horror setting its own shot at TV. The project, simply titled Ravenloft, comes from screenwriter John August, Alfonso Cuarón, and Hasbro Entertainment, and is described as the successor to Netflix's other, now-canceled D&D project, The Forgotten Realms.

While Dungeons & Dragons is often associated with taverns, dragons, treasure, and quippy adventuring parties, Ravenloft is the game’s gothic nightmare corner. It’s the place where D&D trades high-fantasy heroics for haunted castles, cursed lands, tragic monsters and the sense that there's always something lurking in the fog.

Ravenloft has been one of D&D’s most iconic settings for decades, dating back to the original 1983 adventure built around Count Strahd von Zarovich. Strahd remains the setting’s defining villain — he's a vampire lord, tragic monster, and tyrant trapped in the mist-shrouded land of Barovia.

The setting later grew into the Domains of Dread, a collection of horror realms ruled by Darklords who are often imprisoned in worlds shaped by their own sins and obsessions. The premise gives the TV show a lot of flexibility in the stories it tells, bouncing between gothic horrors, cursed folklore, and actual nightmares within the one setting.

That flexibility is a big part of why Ravenloft has endured. While Curse of Strahd remains the best-known modern entry point for many D&D players, newer books like Van Richten’s Guide to Ravenloft and Ravenloft: The Horrors Within have expanded the setting beyond Barovia, turning it into a toolkit for nearly every flavor of horror D&D can support.

According to a Deadline report, the Ravenloft project is also part of a broader push to expand D&D outside the core tabletop game. Netflix had also been developing The Forgotten Realms, though Deadline reports Ravenloft is now positioned as its successor. Meanwhile, a sequel to Dungeons & Dragons: Honor Among Thieves remains uncertain, even though co-writer and co-director Jonathan Goldstein recently said a script exists.

D&D’s reach has also been boosted by projects that don’t look like traditional tabletop products. People are still playing, modding and arguing about Baldur’s Gate 3 years after its 2023 launch, while Critical Role has helped turn actual play into a major entertainment lane of its own, with live shows, animated adaptations, and a huge audience beyond the table.

Arteta sweating on Raya’s fitness after Arsenal’s thriller with Newcastle

📅 Nov 21, 2026  |  Category: Sports  |  Read Source →

Syndicated reporting provided by Stats Perform and reviewed by the SoccerNews editorial team.

Mikel Arteta was left sweating over the fitness of goalkeeper David Raya after the Arsenal shot-stopper was forced off in Saturday's 2-2 draw against Newcastle United.

Raya was replaced in the 70th minute by Kepa Arrizabalaga at St. James' Park after clutching his left arm and looking in some discomfort after a challenge with William Osula.

By the time Raya was replaced, Arsenal found themselves 2-1 down after goals from Harvey Barnes and Malick Thiaw overturned Eberechi Eze's 33rd-minute opener.

However, Arsenal hit back 12 minutes from time through Noni Madueke, ensuring they remained six points clear of second-place Brighton at the top of the Premier League.

Arteta, however, remained concerned about the fitness of his goalkeeper, who has kept the joint-most clean sheets (seven, level with Bart Verbruggen) in the league this season.

The Gunners face Borussia Dortmund in the Champions League on Tuesday before welcoming Enzo Maresca's Manchester City to the Emirates Stadium five days later.

"Unfortunately, he had to come off, which is never good news, and he will get assessed now," Arteta said of Raya's injury.

"He will probably get an MRI scan tomorrow, to see what exactly it is. At the moment, I don't know."

Arsenal's result saw them end a 21-game run of Premier League games without a draw (W18 D3) since a 2-2 draw with Wolves back in February.

In an entertaining affair in the second half, Arsenal were edged in the expected goals (xG) battle, registering a total of 1.48 to Newcastle's 1.73 from one fewer attempt (12 to 13).

Newcastle had just three shots (0 on target), one touch in the opposition’s box, and won 46% of their duels in the first half, while facing 11 shots themselves (three on target).

In the second half, they had nine shots (three on target), 14 touches in the opposition’s box, and won 52.5% of their duels, facing just two shots from Arsenal (one on target).

And Arteta was left to rue a glut of chances while Arsenal were on top in the first half, conceding: "My feeling is that we have lost two points today. I say 'we', because we were exceptional in the first half.

"The level of aggression and intensity we put into the defensive aspect, the quality we showed with the clarity to understand the jumps and the way they were making every adjustment man-to-man, the aggression to attack the right spaces, and we should have been three or four up.

"When you don't do that, then in the second half, you know the opponents will try to react, and that’s what they did to us.

"You have to increase the level to cope with that, rather than stay at the same level. Right from the beginning, we're not at the races – we start to lose duels, start to lose second balls, we give the ball away and have really bad circulation balls against their press, and we give them belief.

"We did both, giving them a goal from a throw-in that was very disappointing, and then we gave them a corner, and left their best set-piece player free – we get punished.

"After that, we came back to being that exceptional team; we could break the press, run, create infinite situations where we could score a goal, we scored the second one, and we had infinite situations with overloads to go and kill the game and win it.

"We lacked that moment of magic to win the game in the end."

Simeone thrilled with Atletico’s position despite lack of goals from strikers

📅 Nov 21, 2026  |  Category: Sports  |  Read Source →

Syndicated reporting provided by Stats Perform and reviewed by the SoccerNews editorial team.

Diego Simeone has called for perspective amid concerns over the form of Atletico Madrid's strikers, insisting he is "excited" by his side's progress despite their lack of goals.

Ahead of Sunday's Madrid derby against Getafe at the Estadio Coliseum, Simeone reiterated his confidence in his forwards, pointing to the contributions of Atletico's other attacking players as a reason for optimism.

Indeed, Julian Alvarez, Jonathan David and Alexander Sorloth have combined for just four LaLiga goals between them this season, leaving much of the goalscoring burden on Atletico's wide players, with Alex Baena (five goals), Giuliano Simeone (four) and Alejandro Grimaldo (four) leading the way.

Despite their strikers' struggles, Atletico sit third in LaLiga, five points behind second-placed Real Madrid and leaders Barcelona.

"On the goals, it's all about perspective," Simeone said. "I'm excited that we're where we are without the strikers scoring.

"Hopefully, the goals will come, and then we can build on all the good things we're doing.

"We're going to face Getafe, who are a very tough opponent on their home turf. We need to play a strong game and take the match to where we can hurt them.

"We need to maintain what we've been doing and continue managing our playing time so that we can show our ability to compete at the level we need to."

The Atletico boss also provided updates on defenders Robin Le Normand and Cristian Romero, both of whom have featured regularly for Atletico this season.

"We have a lot of confidence in [Le Normand]," Simeone added.

"We have a very good relationship, we often talk about what's been happening to him. In football, mistakes are more damaging to defenders, but with his personality he'll do well.

"Cuti Romero also is working very hard to recover as quickly as possible. Even without playing, he's very important. We need the best Cuti Romero."

Getafe, meanwhile, are 15th ahead of Sunday's kick-off, though Jose Bordalas' side remain unbeaten at home in the league this season (W3 D3), and have not suffered defeat across their last eight LaLiga matches.

Enes Unal has scored five goals in 12 games against Atletico in LaLiga – his highest tally against a single opponent in the top-flight.

Lee Kang-In has found the net three times in eight LaLiga outings against Getafe.

Against no other opponent has he scored more goals across his career in Europe's top five leagues, along with three against Angers for Paris Saint-Germain in Ligue 1.

Atletico head into this clash as favourites, having won 10 of their 13 away matches against Getafe in LaLiga (D2 L1).

Los Colchoneros have not managed more away wins against any other opponent in the league (also 10 against Celta Vigo).

Simeone's side also arrive in strong away form, having won their last four LaLiga matches on the road. They have only managed five or more consecutive away victories in a single campaign on two previous occasions – in April 2014 and February 2021, both of which were title-winning seasons.

Simeone himself boasts an impressive record against Getafe, having won 22 of his 27 LaLiga meetings with them (D4 L1). Victory on Sunday would see him equal Luis Aragones' record for the most wins by a manager against a single opponent in the competition, with the latter having beaten Espanyol 23 times.

Getafe, though, have taken points from three of their last seven league meetings with Atleti (W1 D2 L4), having done so in just two of the previous 20 (D2 L18).

Bordalas' side will also take confidence from their recent form at the Coliseum, where they are unbeaten in eight LaLiga matches (W5 D3) – their longest such run since going 11 games without defeat between May and December 2023 (W7 D4).

‘That was Newcastle DNA-par excellence’ – Jaissle delighted after Arsenal draw

📅 Nov 21, 2026  |  Category: Sports  |  Read Source →

Syndicated reporting provided by Stats Perform and reviewed by the SoccerNews editorial team.

Matthias Jaissle believes he saw "Newcastle United DNA-par excellence" in their second-half display against Arsenal.

The Magpies held the Gunners to a 2-2 draw at St. James' Park on Saturday, after recovering from a dismal first-half performance.

After Eberechi Eze gave Arsenal a 1-0 lead at the break, Harvey Barnes and Malick Thiaw turned things around for Newcastle, though Noni Madueke salvaged a draw for the reigning champions.

Newcastle had just three shots (none on target), one touch in the opposition’s box, and won 46% of their duels during the first half, while facing 11 shots themselves (three on target).

In the second half, they had nine shots (three on target), 14 touches in the opposition box, and won 52.5% of their duels, facing just two shots from Arsenal (one on target).

Barnes' equaliser was the first goal the Gunners have conceded when leading in the Premier League this season, and Jaissle was delighted with how his players responded after the break.

Realty Income Pays Dividends Every Month. Here's What You Can Expect $10,000 Invested to Pay Monthly in 2027.

📅 Nov 21, 2026  |  Category: Business  |  Read Source →

Realty Income has paid a monthly dividend every month for more than 56 years.

By Jennifer Saibil – Nov 21, 2026 at 6:33PM EST

10 stocks we like better than Realty Income ›

If you're looking for a dividend stock, Realty Income (O +0.64%) is one of the best. It's a real estate investment trust (REIT), a business structure for real estate companies that pays out 90% of income as dividends, which is why REITs are often on best-dividend-stock lists.

Realty Income has an excellent track record, a high yield, and plenty of reasons to be confident about its future success. If you invest $10,000 right now, here's how much you can expect to get in dividends in 2027.

Realty Income has supported its top dividend by running a massive real estate business that leases properties to large essential retailers. These are chains you know and love and likely shop at, like Walmart (WMT +1.51%), Home Depot (HD +0.70%), 7-Eleven, and Dollar General (DG -2.23%).

These are stores with well-established brands and strong cash flow, making them reliable renters. They have multi-year leases, some as long as 20 years, and Realty Income's occupancy rate is a high 98.8%. It has maintained a rate between 97% and 99% since 2000.

Grocery and convenience stores make up almost 20% of the portfolio, providing a resilient revenue stream, but the company has also branched out into other areas as it expands and diversifies. The U.K. is one of its largest markets today, accounting for more than 15% of total properties, and Wynn Resorts (WYNN -2.07%) has become one of its biggest customers. Industrials make up a healthy 17% of the portfolio, and Realty Income is also getting started in data centers, which make up 1%.

The company has more than 15,500 properties in 96 industries, and it's always buying new properties to keep growing. It has a $5.5 billion revolving credit facility, and it's raised more than $2 billion so far this year from various sources. It has a large pipeline of quality properties to choose from, and expanding beyond retail offers it more opportunities.

Realty Income has paid a dividend monthly for the past 677 months, or more than 56 years. It's one of the few dividend stocks that cuts you a check every month.

It also raises its dividend quarterly rather than annually, which is unusual among great dividend stocks. It has raised the dividend for the past 117 quarters, or more than 29 years.

At the current price, the dividend yields 5.6%, an excellent yield for a reliable dividend stock.

Realty Income is a dividend play, and the stock hasn't changed much over the past 10 years. However, now has been a particularly challenging time for the company as interest and mortgage rates remain high.

Since its business model involves borrowing lots of money, higher interest rates mean it has to pay more to service its debt.

While shares are down 7% over the last six months, it doesn’t appear to be in any danger, and it’s flourished across a range of environments for more than 50 years, paying its dividend without missing a monthly payment.

As of its latest raise last week, Realty Income pays $3.288 in annualized dividends, up from $3.258. That's a small change of only 0.9%. If it continues like that for the next four quarters, by the end of 2027, it should be $3.408 in annualized dividends. The average of the two is $3.348, and divided by 12, that's an average monthly payment of $0.279.

If you invest $10,000 today at a share price of $57.66 (at the time of writing), that gets you 173 shares, and next year, you'd get about $48.27 every month.

That may not seem like much, but it's reliable and will continue to rise. It also highlights the need to save for retirement to ensure a livable monthly income.

German e-invoicing for developers: XRechnung and ZUGFeRD via REST API and MCP

📅 Nov 21, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Posted on Nov 21 • Edited on Nov 21 • AI-assisted

From 2027, many German businesses have to send structured e-invoices to other businesses, and from 2028 almost all of them. Receiving them has been mandatory since 1 January 2025. If you build software that creates or processes invoices for German customers, this is now your problem too.

This post covers what counts as an e-invoice, the two formats you will meet, how to validate them, and how we generate both formats from a single invoice object in our own API. Disclosure up front: we build MaxRechnung, a German invoicing app, and the examples in the second half use our API.

Small businesses under § 19 UStG ("Kleinunternehmer") are exempt from sending, but they still have to receive. Small-amount invoices up to €250 and travel tickets are exempt as well, and B2C invoices are not affected.

A PDF is not an e-invoice under German law, even if it is generated by software. An e-invoice is a structured, machine-readable format that complies with the European standard EN 16931. The law also allows other agreed formats such as EDI, as long as the data can be extracted in a way compatible with EN 16931. In practice, though, you will meet one of two formats:

Legally, the XML is what counts. If the PDF and the embedded XML disagree, the XML wins. That also means you have to archive the original file, not a printout or a re-rendered PDF. Strictly, the XML is what must be kept; the PDF part only matters if it carries additional relevant information, but keeping the original file covers both.

Two open validators cover almost everything:

If you build an invoicing feature yourself, run these validators in CI against a set of fixture invoices. The schematron rules change with every XRechnung release, and you want to notice before your customers' accounting software does.

In MaxRechnung, an invoice is created once as a structured object. XRechnung and ZUGFeRD are two renderings of the same data, and once an e-invoice is finalized, its XML is frozen. The flow over the REST API (/api/v1) looks like this.

Setting e_invoice_formats makes finalizing enforce the full EN 16931/XRechnung rules and freezes the XML. Leave it out only for invoices that don't need to be e-invoices (B2C, for example). The sender (name, address, tax ID) is taken from your account settings; pass a sender object to override it. Seller contact name and phone (BT-41/BT-42) always come from your account settings. A draft may be incomplete, so this call succeeds even if those settings are still empty.

The Idempotency-Key makes retries safe: the same key with the same body within 24 hours returns the original response instead of creating a second invoice, while the same key with a different body returns 409 conflict. Use something stable like your order ID.

Finalizing checks the mandatory invoice fields required by § 14 UStG. If something is missing, you get a 422 validation_failed with the affected fields in details (on a fresh account without sender data, for example sender.name, sender.address and sender.tax_id), and nothing is finalized. After that the invoice is immutable; corrections go through a separate corrections endpoint, which is what German bookkeeping rules (GoBD) expect.

This returns XRechnung 3.0.2 (CII). With Accept: application/json you get a JSON wrapper with the result of our pre-check against the XRechnung rules: valid (true/false), an errors array for rule violations and a warnings array for hints, e.g. a missing seller contact (BT-41/BT-42) or buyer e-mail address (BT-49). The raw XML download carries the same verdict in the X-EInvoice-Valid header. The free-text unit Std. from the example ends up as unitCode="HUR" in the XML. If you set e_invoice_formats in step 1, finalizing has already enforced these rules and you get the frozen file. For invoices created without it, check valid/errors or the X-EInvoice-Valid header: the call still returns 200 so that existing integrations don't break.

The full reference, including a cURL import for each endpoint, is at maxrechnung.de/developers/api, and the OpenAPI 3.1 spec is at https://maxrechnung.de/api/openapi.json.

The clip did give fans a closer look at some of the main cast in action, including Dominic McLaughlin, Alastair Stout, and Arabella Stanton as Harry, Ron, and Hermione, respectively, Janet McTeer as Minerva McGonagall, and John Lithgow as Albus Dumbledore, with the latter already earning plenty of conversation on social media.

The hype for Harry Potter is unsurprisingly sending fans back to the original film series. At the time of writing, the time-travel-based third story in the franchise, Prisoner of Azkaban, is one of the ten most-watched movies on Prime Video worldwide. The film follows the manhunt of Gary Oldman's wrongfully imprisoned Sirius Black, similar to the iconic Harrison Ford thriller The Fugitive, and is widely considered the greatest film in the series.

After Chris Columbus laid the foundations of the Wizarding World in the first two installments, acclaimed visionary director Alfonso Cuarón took over for this third film, giving viewers the most immersive experience in the whole franchise. Cuarón reshaped Hogwarts, connecting notable locations with dynamic camerawork so that the entire castle felt more real than ever.

The film's quality was repaid at the box office, with a reported $130 million budget turned into a global haul that fell just short of $800 million.
