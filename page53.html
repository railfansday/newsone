iFixit teardown shows how Apple's glowing PROLED MacBook logo works

📅 Nov 11, 2026  |  Category: Other  |  Read Source →

The new laptop goes the extra mile to recreate the iconic feature.

iFixit has shared its teardown of Apple's first touchscreen MacBook Pro and besides bemoaning the company's half-hearted commitment to user repairability, it also showed off the physical components that make the laptop's glowing logo work. The new lid design is one of the MacBook Pro with PROLED XDR Display's many charms, alongside its tandem OLED display and lighter weight.

Older LCD MacBooks featured a physical cutout that let the display's backlight illuminate the logo, but since OLED screens don't require backlights, Apple had to come up with a different solution to bring the design element back. iFixit found that the company uses a light strip with four LEDs layered behind the display, whose light is reflected through a diffuser to illuminate the logo. The Apple logo itself is also coated "like a two-way mirror," according to iFixit, so that it can appear metallic and reflective when the logo is off, but still let light through when the LEDs are on.

Apple ditched the glowing logo in 2016, so it's a bit ironic that the company waited until it stopped using backlit displays to bring it back. It used to be a cool bit of branding before, but now the company may see the glowing logo design as an important way to distinguish its most expensive laptop from the kind we peons are forced to use.

The rest of the internal changes to the laptop are more expected. Apple was able to achieve the new MacBook Pro's lighter weight by using less aluminum in the base and lid of the laptop and including a slightly smaller trackpad. The laptop's hinge was also reworked to make the display wobble less when you tap the screen. Those changes don't make it any easier to fix, though. The laptop is still complex to disassemble: For example, iFixit says that you have to remove the laptop's logic board to switch out its USB-C ports. Plenty of other components, like the MacBook's memory and the HDMI port, are also soldered in place. Anyone dropping up to $15,000 on a laptop probably isn't worried about doing their own repairs, but Apple's thin and light (and glowing) design comes with some tradeoffs, as always.

As Cole Hauser prepares to continue his work on screen as Rip Wheeler in Season 2 of Dutton Ranch, he’s also pursuing other big projects that he can be involved with both in front of and behind the camera. In fact, he seems to be taking a page out of Taylor Sheridan’s playbook as he sets his sights on a book-to-screen adaptation of Code Name: Pale Horse.

Now, while Hauser will still be acting, and that includes in this new project, like Sheridan, he’s clearly making moves to do more work behind the scenes. According to Deadline, the Rip actor, his American Outlaw Entertainment and Constantin Film will be taking on this adaptation of Pale Horse, which is based on a memoir by Scott Payne and Michelle Shephard about Payne’s time “embedded deep within the country’s most violent subcultures.” It will be written by Hauser’s producing partner Randall Batinkoff.

Payne spent 28 years as an FBI undercover agent, and his book tells stories about his time infiltrating the neo-Nazi white supremacist group, The Base. It also details Operation Roadkill, which was a two-year assignment involving the Outlaws Motorcycle Club in Massachusetts. Hauser called him a “real-life hero” and explained why he was drawn to this project:

These are the type of stories that I am drawn to as a producer and as an actor. Scott Payne is a real-life hero, with the kind of moxie and edge you would need to risk your life to fight evil. I am looking forward to producing this movie and getting a chance to bring him and his story to life on the screen.

Now, this is the first time Hauser has been cited as a producer; however, he has been an executive producer before. He’s listed as an EP on Dutton Ranch, 2022’s Panama and 2014’s Bad Country. Overall, Pale Horse serves as a big next step for him, as it’s said that this project could be a “potential starring vehicle” for him.

The reason I’m invoking Taylor Sheridan's name amid all this is twofold. For one, it shows a big step Hauser is taking to be more involved in his projects. Ever since Sheridan stepped away from acting full-time, he has put himself in positions to have a lot of say in his projects, from the Yellowstone shows (though not as much these days) to shows like Lioness and Landman.

Secondly, while Sheridan loves his cowboys, he also has a tendency to gravitate toward stories about operatives, agents and espionage. Just look at his movies, like Sicario, and his shows, like Lioness, and you’ll see what I mean. Clearly, like his Yellowstone boss, Hauser is a fan of both cowboys and agents.

Now, at the time of this writing, there isn’t much information on when we might see Pale Horse. However, it’s very clear that Hauser is looking forward to working on it. Along with that project, he’s also notably working on a new season of Dutton Ranch. While the first season of the Yellowstone sequel series came with its challenges, he’s looking forward to playing Rip again. So, while we wait for all that to happen, you can see Cole Hauser play Rip Wheeler, a character Taylor Sheridan is responsible for, by streaming Dutton Ranch with a Paramount+ subscription.

I spend way too much time on wikis reading about spin-off games made for handheld and mobile devices I will probably never play (there was a whole trilogy of Silent Hill: Orphan games that have lived up to their name by being orphaned on old phones). The Elder Scrolls Travels are a prime example—games made in the gap between Morrowind and Oblivion. The third in the series, Shadowkey, came out on Nokia's N-Gage handheld—a cross between a GameBoy Advance and a mobile phone that lasted two years before being discontinued in 2005.

This first-person RPG is about to return thanks to Dan Whelan, whose Shadowkey Reforged project will let us play Shadowkey in widescreen with controller support and upscaled graphics. You can choose to play in classic or modern mode, but you can also toggle individual features one by one. Modern mode isn't just a graphical upgrade as it also adds features from the later mainline Elder Scrolls games like stamina-based sprinting, stealth, and ragdoll physics.

This is one of those times where I prefer the original game's look, as the brutally abrupt draw distance leaves a lot to your imagination while the modernized version seems to show enemies looming unresponsively in the near distance. And it's not like the upscaled character models actually look good, despite being beyond what Nokia's taco phone was capable of displaying.

While Shadowkey was never particularly well-ranked in the pantheon of Elder Scrolls games, people did praise its use of the Morrowind soundtrack, which Shadowkey Reforged will continue. Point it to your Morrowind install and it'll borrow both music and sound effects from the best Elder Scrolls game.

To answer the obvious question, Whelan says in the FAQ: "I used AI to assist with coding and building the engine, and with debugging. I used AI image generation combined with Photoshop and some manual sprite editing to create most of the modern, higher resolution artwork."

Whelan plans to release Shadowkey Reforged on November 11, which is just 18 hours away at the time of writing. You'll be able to download Windows, Mac, and Android versions from the website, though you'll need the original game's files from your Nokia N-Gage as well, or whatever abandonware site you frequent.

Frankly, without the Tel'tak, Earth and the SG-1 crew would've been absolutely screwed multiple times, which is why the modest ship 100% deserves this brand-new, authentically designed collectible release arriving in 2027.

Master Replicas is officially bringing one of Stargate SG-1's most dependable ships to life with a brand-new Tel'tak die-cast replica, currently available for preorder ahead of its anticipated May 2027 release.

Although the project is still in early development without a confirmed premiere date and fans are only cautiously optimistic for the time being, it's encouraging news following the previous cancellation. Between a potential return to television and fantastic new collectibles like the Tel'tak, it's an exciting time to be a Stargate fan.

Ex Machina Is Now Streaming For Free On Tubi

Before Civil War and Annihilation, Alex Garland unleashed Ex Machina on the world. The writer/director takes pages from the likes of The Terminator, Westworld, and, even in a small way, Frankenstein. The idea of one's creation becoming sentient has been a benchmark of sci-fi and horror films since their inception. With Ex Machina, Garland molds the themes of playing God, exploitation of women, and humanity's eventual demise into a 108-minute mission around the greatest fears of humankind regarding A.I. It's both a confrontation with reality and a plea that it's not too late to turn back.

Garland had the kernel of the idea when he was 11 or 12, and his parents bought a home computer. "I did some basic coding on it, just trying to understand stuff," he told The Et Cetera, adding that he "would input programs that were kind of a Q&A, and this film traces itself back to that." His fascination with sentience incubated in his brain until he began writing the story that eventually blossomed into Ex Machina, one of the greatest sci-fi thrillers of all time.

Alex Garland's Ex Machina storms onto Tubi, the free, cinephile streaming platform known for snagging classic and modern films for its expansive library. Men, also written and directed by Garland and starring Jessie Buckley, can be found on the service, as well. Ex Machina sits comfortably next to other sci-fi/tech horror films such as Robocop, A-X-L, A.I. Rising, and The 6th Day.

Ex Machina is purposely slick, owing to frequent Garland collaborator Rob Hardy's cinematography. Hardy is most known for his subsequent work on Men, Annihilation, and Civil War. Most recently, he teamed up with director James Watkins for Clayface. His techniques and visual style serve the film well and mirrors the cold, mechanical story about a rogue robot that gains bodily autonomy. That polish accentuates Garland's storytelling approach with moody silver and white tones permeating the screen.

Looking back now, Ex Machina emerges as a prescient warning. We (collectively) should have been paying attention, particularly when other futuristic media like Her, M3GAN, Companion, Soulm8te, and the hit TV show Black Mirror do not exist in a vacuum. But screaming alarms fall on deaf ears. If you look around, Ex Machina feels ripped from the evening news. Headlines such as "Man Proposed to His AI Chatbot Girlfriend Named Sol, Then Cried His 'Eyes Out' When She Said 'Yes'" indicate the harrowing reality of machines slyly exploiting human loneliness, as well as our empathy.

When Blue Book programmer Caleb (Domhnall Gleeson, who went on to star in Black Mirror's "Be Right Back" episode) wins a contest to join CEO Nathan Bateman (Oscar Isaac) at his secluded estate for a top secret project, the film tempts the viewer that high-tech might not be so bad after all. That insidiousness soon spills over as the robot, named Ava (Alicia Vikander), begins exhibiting remarkable awareness. Caleb conducts a series of tests through conversations with Ava, and Nathan hopes to understand the latest model of his Android creation to better fine-tune the specs for the next iteration.

During a power outage, Ava whispers to Caleb that Nathan can not be trusted. She plants seeds of doubt, nurtured by Caleb's innate curiosity and inescapable feelings of longing. She feeds on his human nature as a way to manipulate and enact a plan of escape. While Caleb conspires with Ava to hatch the scheme, he ultimately has no control over her. Both Nathan and Caleb become victims of the A.I. system, and Nathan at least pays a deadly price for his obsession with playing God. It also doesn't help that Caleb describes the creation process as god-like.

Like Black Mirror, Ex Machina isn't a story about some far-off reality centuries from now. It's here, and that should terrify us.

Iconic Character Officially Confirmed For Buffy the Vampire Slayer's 2027 Return

📅 Nov 11, 2026  |  Category: Entertainment  |  Read Source →

As Buffy the Vampire Slayer and Angel head towards their 2027 return, it's been confirmed that several fan-favorite characters will return. However, a major unanswered question means that fans don't know exactly what version of a Buffyverse icon they'll be getting.

Beginning with the 1992 movie starring Kristy Swanson, Buffy the Vampire Slayer was rebooted into an iconic 1997 TV series starring Sarah Michelle Gellar as the titular hero. Now, the story will continue, though fans aren't yet clear on where the new narrative will pick up from.

One thing fans do know is that fan-favorite characters including Spike, Faith and Tara will return. But with some of them dead by the conclusion of the original TV series and others transformed over the course of the narrative, fans don't quite know what to expect...

In March 2027, Dynamite Entertainment will publish Buffy the Vampire Slayer #1, from Kelly Thompson, Stephen Byrne, Lee Loughridge and Jeff Eckleberry. The bumper-sized issue will begin a new narrative spread across the Buffy the Vampire Slayer series and a new Angel series, from Thompson and Giulia Giacomino.

Now, thanks to a press release from Dynamite and recent interviews by Thompson, fans know some details of which characters will return. Character designs shared by Dynamite (below) have confirmed several members of the Scooby Gang, including Spike, Dawn, Tara, Faith and Anya, with preview art confirming Angel's Fred and Thompson ruling out Oz for the time being.

Meanwhile, Dynamite promises the "comics debut of a beloved Buffy fan-favorite character" who is represented by a silhouette on the cover art shared so far. While these announcements are Manna from Heaven to longtime Buffy fans, major questions have been raised about the timeline and how it affects the cast.

Thanks to details like Tara being alive and Xander having both eyes, fans have been asking questions about how the comics fit into the TV series timeline. The question is a big deal for Spike, who only gains a soul late in season 6, significantly altering his outlook and motives.

The first issue's summary referencing Buffy's death has fans speculating that the comics will pick up after season 5 of the original show, though this would mean the majority of TV Angel wouldn't be in canon. The summary reads:

Eisner-winning writer KELLY THOMPSON (Absolute Wonder Woman, Black Widow) and superstar artist STEPHEN BYRNE (Wonder Twins) present a vision of BUFFY THE VAMPIRE SLAYER you've got to read to believe! Featuring the comics debut of a beloved Buffy fan-favorite character!

Into every generation, a slayer is born. And Buffy Summers may have broken the mold. Though Buffy's powers were varied and great, it was the love and devotion she inspired that was perhaps her greatest power. And when she gave her life to save the world, the people who loved her couldn't live with that. This is that story.

Speaking to AIPT, Thompson acknowledges that she's been deliberately cagey about the timeline, while implying the comic WILL respect the show timeline up to a certain point, saying:

I’m not pinning it down. I do think that anyone who’s paying attention to the sketches and stuff that have been out, Fred is in one sketch, and Dawn is there, too. So these things suggest it has to be at least a certain point. It’s a terrific non-ness.

Thompson also promises that Buffy the Vampire Slayer #1 will act as an "atom bomb" for the characters, suggesting that while the comics will likely begin at a certain point within the TV timeline, it won't necessarily feel the need to fit into the gap between that and future seasons, likely charting its own course in terms of ongoing continuity.

One area in which this is likely to be noteworthy is Tara's characterization. Thompson notes that due to the groundbreaking nature of Willow and Tara's relationship at the time, Tara wasn't given the same compelling flaws as other characters - something that can be remedied in the new comics. Thompson says:

I don’t want people to come and think they’re getting ‘The Tara Show.’ She’s a supporting character at best in this first arc. We’ve got to build to [more interesting characterization], especially with the big reveals that are in issue #1.

From what fans know so far, it's clear that the new Buffy the Vampire Slayer and Angel series are going to be an explosive return to the franchise, coming from a writer who knows it inside and out and isn't afraid to take big swings with this new era.

At the same time, the series' sense of independence from canon and where it fits into it is likely to lead to character regression in some places and evolution in others. Fans will find out exactly how these characters will change when Buffy the Vampire Slayer returns in 2027.

Buffy the Vampire Slayer #1 is coming March 10 from Dynamite Entertainment.
