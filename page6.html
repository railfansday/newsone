Democrat Scott Colom speaks in Jackson, Mississippi, after winning the U.S. Senate race. Colom defeated Republican incumbent Cindy Hyde-Smith in an upset, becoming the state’s first Black senator since Reconstruction.

Mississippi's Republican Gov. Tate Reeves, former President Bill Clinton and former Vice President Kamala Harris were among those to congratulate Democrat Sen.-elect Scott Colom on his victory in the Magnolia State's U.S. Senate contest.

More than a week after Election Day, The Associated Press on Wednesday called the race for Colom, the district attorney for Mississippi Circuit Court District 16, defeating President Donald Trump-backed incumbent Republican Sen. Cindy Hyde-Smith.

"We may have had to count One Mississippi, Two Mississippi, but Blue Mississippi sure has a nice ring to it! Scott, you put your heart and soul into this race because you believe in the promise of your state’s people. And they believed in you right back. I’m honored to have stood alongside you. Congratulations!" Clinton said in a Wednesday post on X.

Colom is the first Democrat to win election to a Mississippi U.S. Senate seat since 1982; the late Sen. John Stennis, who served in the U.S. Senate from late 1947 until early 1989, was the last Democrat to serve as a U.S. Senator in the state.

"Congratulations to Senator-Elect @scott_colom. I hope to collaborate as we continue our very important work to support Mississippi families, grow jobs and wages for our people, and improve educational outcomes for our kids!" Gov. Reeves wrote in a post on X.

Colom shared the governor's post and wrote, "Thank you for the congratulations @tatereeves. I also look forward to working with you on the issues that matter to our state. Together, there’s nothing our beautiful state can’t accomplish." He added the hashtag, "#MississippiMatters."

Colom will be the third Black person ever to serve as a U.S. senator in Mississippi, the state's first Black senator since the 1800s and the first Black person popularly elected to serve as a senator from the state, since the prior two men were selected by the state legislature prior to the adoption of the 17th Amendment in the 20th century.

"The power is with the people, and the people of Mississippi are with Scott. @scott_colom makes history today as the first Black senator elected in Mississippi since Reconstruction. I saw firsthand when campaigning with him in his hometown that he is a brilliant leader and dedicated public servant who will work tirelessly to serve every Mississippian with pride. Congratulations, Scott," Harris wrote in a post on X.

ZEC has corrected approximately 41% from its $1,695 all-time high and is testing its 20-week EMA band.

Zcash ETFs recorded $29.78 million in November net outflows, with assets falling to $562.16 million.

Zcash's active developer count has roughly doubled in two years, despite broader industry contraction.

ZEC price is facing another uncomfortable test after falling below $1,000 in November. The latest decline has raised concerns about whether Zcash can defend its broader bullish structure, particularly as the token retests its 20-week EMA band. Interestingly, this isn’t the first time ZEC has experienced a brutal correction after reaching new highs. Its history suggests investors shouldn’t dismiss another 75% drawdown scenario.

The previous market structure offers some uncomfortable comparisons. Since 2021, ZEC had traded inside a descending triangle, repeatedly testing the $95-$100 support zone. Expectations of a breakout increased during the first half of 2022. Instead, the structure broke down.

What followed was an unusually long consolidation stretching from the second half of 2022 through the first half of 2025.

Then came the turnaround. ZEC reached an all-time high of $740 in the second half of 2025 before profit-taking triggered a correction exceeding 75%, eventually bringing prices toward $200.

During Q1 2026, another descending triangle emerged. This time, the breakout worked. By the end of Q3 2026, ZEC had established a new all-time high of $1,695. Now, November’s decline has pushed the token below $1,000 again.

The current correction stands approximately 41% below the latest all-time high. Despite the decline, the broader uptrend remains intact, with price hovering around the 20-week EMA band.

However, historical comparisons aren’t particularly comforting. ZEC previously dropped from $380 to $95 in 2021 and from $740 to $200 in 2025. Both corrections represented declines approaching 75%.

If selling pressure continues, $740 and $550 emerge as potential support areas. A deeper breakdown could expose the $400-$440 region, representing another substantial correction from the latest peak. These are potential downside scenarios, not confirmed price targets.

Institutional flows aren’t helping the immediate picture. US spot Zcash ETFs recorded $14.61 million in outflows on November 11, their largest daily withdrawal since October 15, according to SoSoValue data.

Funds experienced withdrawals on six of the last nine trading days. November’s net outflows consequently reached $29.78 million, while total net assets declined to $562.16 million, their lowest level since September 4.

The reversal is notable because late October delivered the strongest weekly inflows since mid-September.

Here’s where the bearish price action gets interesting. Grayscale research indicates Zcash’s monthly active developer count has roughly doubled over two years, even as the broader cryptocurrency developer population declined.

Approximately 70% of active Zcash developers have more than one year of crypto experience, compared with 58% across the wider industry.

Grayscale believes this development momentum could eventually support ZEC’s valuation. However, the market hasn’t rewarded that progress yet.

For now, ZEC price faces competing signals: weakening ETF flows, a substantial correction, and improving developer participation. Whether the 20-week EMA holds could become particularly important. If selling accelerates, the historical 75% correction pattern makes $740, $550, and potentially $400-$440 levels worth watching.

The 2026 NFL season has reached its midpoint, and all 32 coaches who began the season in charge of their respective franchises remain in place.

As every NFL fan knows, that will not last long. Last season, 10 of the league's 32 teams made coaching changes. NFL owners are growing ever-impatient as they look to quickly build Super Bowl contenders, so leashes are shorter than ever for coaches.

That's why many will be on scorching hot seats during the second half of the season. And while none have been dismissed yet, a couple may fail to finish out the season, while numerous others will see their tenures end on "Black Monday," the day after the final game of the regular season.

But who will NFL owners and decision-makers be eyeing among the league's next crop of top coaching candidates? A few prominent retreads are on the market, along with the usual plethora of young, offensive-minded coaches.

Here's a look at the top coaching candidates to watch as the 2027 NFL coach hiring cycle draws ever nearer.

Tomlin is easily the biggest name on the NFL's coaching carousel for 2027. The 54-year-old led the Steelers to a 193-114-2 record across 19 seasons in Pittsburgh and won a Super Bowl while in charge of the franchise. He famously never had a losing season, and his steady performance will certainly endear him to NFL organizations looking for a proven culture-builder to improve their operations.

The lone question is whether Tomlin will want to return to the sideline after stepping down from the Steelers following the 2025 NFL season. He is currently working as a studio analyst for NBC and could be happy to remain in that role moving forward.

Unlike Tomlin, McDermott's intentions are known. The 52-year-old wants another crack at being an NFL head coach after leading the Bills to a 98-50 regular-season record across nine seasons in Buffalo.

McDermott is a strong, defensive-minded coach and has shown an ability to bring in quality offensive coordinators, with Joe Brady being among the best hires of his tenure. His track record leading the Bills to consistent success – eight playoff appearances in nine seasons – should appeal to NFL owners, even though McDermott was never quite able to guide any of his Bills teams to the Super Bowl.

McDermott will also be helped in the interview process by the fact that he is not currently employed by an NFL team. That will allow any team that fires their coach in-season to immediately check in with McDermott and gauge where he stands among the top candidates on the market.

The Vikings started the 2026 NFL season with a 6-2 record despite sporting an offense that ranks 30th in EPA per play, per the NFL's Next Gen Stats. Their success has largely been thanks to Flores' defense, which has established itself as the league's best.

Flores' aggressive, blitz-heavy attack has helped Minnesota post the NFL's best pressure rate while ranking second league-wide in defensive EPA per play behind only the Tampa Bay Buccaneers. The Vikings are surrendering a league-low 14.5 points per game and proved, by containing Josh Allen on "Monday Night Football" in Week 9, that they can be a championship-caliber defense.

With that in mind, any NFL organization looking to improve its defense should be interested in bringing Flores aboard. His system has proven transferable across all of his NFL stops, so he is practically guaranteed to turn whatever team he coaches into a top-10 defense.

That said, Flores is still embroiled in a discrimination lawsuit against the NFL, and it isn't clear whether that legal process will make any teams apprehensive about hiring him.

Udinski is just 30 years old, but he's a rising star in the coaching ranks. He has helped the Jaguars become one of the NFL's best and most efficient offenses during the 2026 season, and his ability to scheme players open should make him a hot commodity on the coaching market.

Udinski would be one of the NFL's youngest-ever coaches, but NFL owners have been more willing than ever to follow the Sean McVay blueprint and take chances on young coaches. That could give Udinski a path toward earning a coaching job even with just seven years of NFL coaching experience.

Doyle is in a similar boat to Udinski. He's just 30 years old but has enjoyed a rapid rise over the last two seasons, finding success working as Ben Johnson's offensive coordinator in 2025 before getting a chance to call his own plays with the Ravens this season.

Doyle's ties to Johnson, who has rapidly turned the Bears into a Super Bowl contender, should make him a popular interview during the 2027 coach hiring cycle. He could also improve his candidacy if he's able to effectively lead the Ravens through injuries to quarterbacks Lamar Jackson and Tyler Huntley and give Baltimore a chance to make a playoff push with Jacoby Brissett at quarterback.

Brothers Matt LaFleur and Mike LaFleur are both NFL coaches. Could Klay Kubiak and Klint Kubiak be next? The latter Kubiak brother has performed well in his first season with the Las Vegas Raiders while the former has been a part of Kyle Shanahan's staff for six seasons.

Kubiak has performed well since being elevated to offensive coordinator in 2025. He led the 49ers to a top-10 offense in both scoring and yardage in his first season in the role and is on pace to do the same in 2026. He has helped Shanahan guide San Francisco to success amid a plethora of injuries at receiver while Brock Purdy has thrived under his tutelage, completing 69.2% of his passes for 4,147 yards, 37 touchdowns and 15 interceptions in 17 starts.

Purdy's success should be enough to get Kubiak interviews; and if NFL teams get the sense he can develop into a quarterback whisperer, they may pull the trigger on hiring him.

Many believed Scheelhaase might earn a coaching job during last year's hiring cycle. At the time, he was coming off a successful season as the Rams' pass game coordinator and had helped lead Matthew Stafford to an MVP season.

Scheelhaase wasn't ultimately hired, but he earned a promotion to the Rams' offensive coordinator role. The Rams have gotten off to a disappointing 5-4 start, but their offense still ranks fourth in yards per game and ninth in EPA per play, per the NFL's Next Gen Stats. That, plus his ties to Sean McVay, will be enough to get Scheelhaase more looks during the 2027 cycle.

It's also worth noting Scheelhaase was a college quarterback at Illinois, so he has experience playing the position. That could prove valuable for teams looking for a coach who can develop a young quarterback into a star-caliber player.

Scheelhaase isn't the only Rams assistant who should be a hot commodity. Shula got plenty of interviews last season after leading the Rams to a top-10 mark in defensive EPA per play and has only continued to raise his stock in 2026.

The Rams presently rank fourth in defensive EPA per play and have allowed the sixth-fewest yards and eighth-fewest points this season. Shula has achieved those results despite missing star edge rusher Myles Garrett for most of the season, and he has helped develop players like Nate Landman into key pieces of the team's defense.

Also, for those wondering, yes: Chris Shula is related to Don Shula. He is the grandson of the legendary Miami Dolphins coach.

Durde has spent the last three seasons as Seattle's defensive coordinator under Mike Macdonald. He has helped Macdonald turn the Seahawks into one of the league's best defenses amid their run to a Super Bowl 60 win over the New England Patriots.

The Seahawks had the NFL's top-scoring defense last season and have allowed the fewest yards thus far in 2026. That will help Durde get looks during this coaching cycle, and so will his and Macdonald's abilities to coach up their young players – like former undrafted free agents Ty Okada and Drake Thomas – and turn them into key role players.

Campanile has spent the last two seasons as the Jaguars' defensive coordinator. He has created a defensive identity in Jacksonville, one focused on attacking downhill and stopping the run. That has paid dividends with the Jaguars allowing just 89.3 rushing yards per game in 2026, fifth-fewest in the league.

NFL owners looking for a hard-nosed, defensive-minded leader could find themselves gravitating toward Campanile. His aggressive defensive style figures to transfer well to his next landing spot, provided he has the personnel needed to stop the run.

The Falcons rank sixth in pressure rate during the 2026 NFL season. This is despite their top pressure-generators being the little-known duo of Brandon Dorlus (29) and Cameron Thomas (26).

Those pressure numbers are a feather-in-the-cap of Ulbrich, who has done well to help an undermanned unit perform at a higher-than-expected level. That's not surprising, given the 49-year-old frequently got the most out of the defenses he coached with the Jets as part of Robert Saleh's staff from 2021-24.

All told, there's a reason the Falcons were adamant about keeping Ulbrich as their defensive coordinator even after switching coaches following the 2026 season. His motivational skills are strong, and his ability to develop defensive talent should earn him interviews this offseason.

Graham has spent eight seasons as an NFL defensive coordinator for four different teams. He is finally enjoying high-end success with the Steelers, who have helped lead to a top-seven mark in yards allowed, points allowed and defensive EPA per play.

Graham, 47, has earned several head coaching interviews during his career and figures to once again draw interest this offseason. His experience across six different NFL organizations and a Super Bowl ring with the Patriots as a defensive assistant could eventually be enough to get him his first chance to be an NFL head coach.

West Ham tracking Sheffield United star alongside six Premier League clubs

📅 Nov 12, 2026  |  Category: Other  |  Read Source →

Sheffield United midfielder Sydie Peck has been among the standout performers for Chris Wilder’s side this term. The SUFC ace is no stranger to transfer interest, and his displays mean rival clubs are regularly keeping tabs on his situation.

A new report from TEAMtalk states Championship rivals and summer admirers West Ham are among those monitoring Peck ahead of a potential January swoop. Also interested in the 22-year-old are six Premier League clubs: Newcastle United, Sunderland, Brighton & Hove Albion, Crystal Palace, Brentford and Bournemouth.

It is added that the Blades are ‘aware’ of the interest in Peck and hope to keep him, but there is an ‘increasing expectation’ that exit opportunities arise in January. The England youth international is currently under contract until 2028, and separate reports have said that a new deal is being eyed.

Peck’s impressive displays have seen his stock surge. He’s a regular in the England U21s setup and his hat-trick against Preston North End showed he has potential to add goals to his game from midfield too.

It means that belief in his ability to step up to the Premier League is growing, and Sheffield United’s uphill battle to break into the Championship promotion fight may well speed up the process. However, Peck himself is in no rush just yet.

Asked about his future after summer transfer speculation, the SUFC star expressed a desire to repay the faith shown in him by Wilder and the first-team and academy coaching staff at Bramall Lane. Speaking to The Star in August, Peck said:

“I joined when I was 16 and the gaffer’s given me every debut since. From Premier League to Championship and in the cup. The staff as well, Mickey Collins. I’ve got a lot of stuff to pay back. They’ve helped me become the player I am today.”

Those comments certainly don’t show a burning desire to move on to a higher level. His loyalty would indicate he has little intention of leaving as early as January too, despite his progress and the growing interest.

Amid this new report and fresh claims from TEAMtalk, Peck’s own words would suggest he is unlikely to exit as early as January.

Peck’s comments aren’t the only reason West Ham could have a tough time persuading him to join. The Hammers spent big on a new midfielder in Arne Engels in the summer, and with all of Soungoutou Magassa, Edson Alvarez, Mohamadou Kante, Tomas Soucek, Divine Mukasa and Lewis Orford on the books, there is not a burning need for another player in this position.

Peck will want to go somewhere he is a regular starter, just like he is at Sheffield United, and that may not be the case with the Hammers.

Furthermore, given his commitment to SUFC, it would be a surprise to see him leave mid-season for a club at the same level. If he is to be prised away from the Blades, it seems more likely that Peck heads for the Premier League.

Bitcoin’s rate-cut hopes face a $5 diesel problem in 2027

📅 Nov 12, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Cheaper crude may not bring the fuel-price relief Bitcoin needs: damaged refineries are keeping the 2027 diesel forecast above $5 a gallon.

The US Energy Information Administration raised its forecast for next year’s retail diesel price by 12%, reducing the fuel-cost relief it previously expected. That leaves less room for falling fuel costs to cool inflation and bring the lower interest rates that could help Bitcoin, even as the agency projects cheaper crude in 2027.

The EIA’s November 10 outlook puts the 2027 US diesel average at $5.03 a gallon, up from $4.49 in its October 6 forecast. That is an upward revision to the same annual forecast, rather than a 12% increase in observed pump prices.

Diesel is still projected to fall from a forecast $5.25 average in 2026. EIA also expects Brent crude to average $86 a barrel in 2027, down from a projected $97 this year. Inputs were finalized November 5, so the outlook does not specifically account for subsequent market events.

Cheaper crude does not guarantee a matching decline in finished-fuel costs. EIA assumes petroleum-product supply constraints will continue through the end of 2027, supporting wider crack spreads: the gap between refined-product prices and crude costs.

The agency attributes elevated refining margins to damaged refineries in the Middle East and Russia, which have reduced product exports. It expects disruptions to be concentrated early next year and constraints to ease thereafter, while warning that unplanned outages and deferred maintenance could disrupt refinery operations.

US refinery crude inputs are forecast at 16.5 million barrels a day in 2027, against 16.7 million this year, despite similar utilization. Running the available refineries hard therefore does not necessarily mean processing more crude.

Merchandising For Bots: The Critical Questions Retail Leaders Aren't Yet Asking

📅 Nov 12, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Zohar Gilad is cofounder and CEO of Fast Simon.

In a recent survey, 24% of shoppers said they would be comfortable letting an AI agent shop for them. While AI-driven traffic to online stores is still a small share of visits, it’s growing quickly.

These numbers will undoubtedly increase. So what happens when AI becomes the intermediary between a brand’s catalog and the shopper?

Because the technology is developing so rapidly, anyone claiming to have a definitive answer or playbook on merchandising for AI is suspect. The more useful exercise is to ask harder questions about what happens to product discovery when an AI system starts deciding which products a shopper sees.

Here are four issues that AI-savvy retailers should be thinking about now.

Traditional SEO taught marketers to compete for visibility inside a ranking system: Identify search intent, optimize pages, earn authority and improve your position.

Now, a new system has emerged: AI (or generative) engine optimization (AEO/GEO).

Unlike keywords, AI prompts are essentially infinite and personalized, and countless different prompts can be clustered around the exact same consumer intent. Consider these requests:

• Find me a breathable, floral dress for under $200.

• Give me a romantic, cottage-core dress appropriate for a guest at a summer wedding.

• I’m going to an outdoor wedding next month, and it’s going to be 85 degrees; what dress will look elevated but won’t show sweat?

Each prompt could lead AI to recommend the same product or three different ones. With open-ended prompts, there’s little predictability.

The answer isn’t to optimize for every possibility, but to make the underlying product information understandable to AI. Then it can connect a product to the many different ways a shopper might describe it (fabric, weight, seasonality, occasion, etc.).

That creates a new merchandising question: How do you structure your catalog to map to these infinite systems? And when the system deciding what gets surfaced is contextual and constantly changing, how do you reliably earn product visibility?

AI agents are well-suited for the shopping that consumers view as bothersome chores: groceries, household essentials and even the frustrating process of finding a holiday gift for Aunt Mathilda.

Conversely, shopping for high-consideration items, like a designer handbag, involves a desire to dream, be inspired and browse. Many shoppers actively enjoy this process and wouldn’t want to hand it off to an AI agent.

That prompts the question: Which parts of the shopping experience would a consumer actually want to delegate?

AI’s impact will likely be most immediate in the “chores” space. Merchandisers selling commodities should consider what happens when AI can compare products on price or availability without exposing the consumer to the brand experience.

Which parts of your catalog become commodities once an AI agent strips away the marketing and compares products side by side? Even for commoditized items, how will descriptions, origin countries and imagery influence the agent’s final selection?

The more important question might be what happens on the consumer’s end. If discovery is part of your value proposition, what does an AI agent remove from the brand experience?

Traditional search lets consumers scroll through and click on numerous options. But as AI search models try to catch up to human discovery habits, they distill endless results down to three to five recommendations.

When presented with AI’s narrowed choices, humans default to convenience, accepting what AI shares. If ChatGPT presents three options for the best running shoes, the shopper is unlikely to look elsewhere for a fourth option.

So how does a brand secure its place in the coveted golden set of results when AI is significantly limiting consumer choice?

In traditional search, a retailer competes for page-one visibility and relies on the shopper to browse. In AI search, the retailer may need to earn consideration before the shopper ever sees the product.

That could create a new competitive real estate, a small shelf of products that AI deemed worth showing. And if AI systems are increasingly determining which products matter, how will this narrowing of options alter market competition?

Agentic commerce is making it easier for AI systems to access structured product information, compare products and eventually complete transactions on a shopper’s behalf.

That creates an uncomfortable question for merchandisers: If AI controls the interface, what happens to the careful curation they’ve built into the catalog?

We’re already moving in this direction. For example, Shopify’s model context protocol (MCP) server displays a brand’s entire catalog to AI agents, leaving all the product discovery power in the hands of AI platforms.

In this instance, a retailer’s products may be more accessible to AI, but accessibility isn’t the same as merchandising.

Merchandising is about deciding which products to emphasize, how to group them and what story to tell around them. An AI agent can potentially bypass much of that curation and make its own decisions about what to show the shopper.

To exert more influence over the shopping process, brands may need to deploy their own AI to interface with the shopper’s AI.

But what will that look like? Will merchants use their own AI to negotiate with or even manipulate the consumer’s shopping agent? How can retailers use technology to restore their product discovery power?

The transition toward merchandising for bots is imminent, but a one-size-fits-all approach will fail.

The first question for retailers is what they’re willing to give up when AI becomes the intermediary between their catalog and the customer.

As AI shopper agents threaten to remove some of the merchandiser’s control, retailers should resist the urge to immediately rewrite their strategies based on unproven theories. The brands poised for success are those that are actively interrogating their catalog’s purpose, data structure and defensive posture against AI intermediaries.

📅 Nov 12, 2026  |  Category: Business  |  Read Source →

Tofu took the crown in a showdown of 20 protein-rich foods judged on health, affordability, availability, and their toll on the planet.

Protein-rich foods can look very different when cost, access, health, and environmental impact all enter the picture. In a study published in Frontiers in Sustainable Food Systems, Dr. Chris Macdonald developed a framework that combines these four considerations to compare commonly consumed protein sources.

Among the 20 foods assessed, tofu took the top spot, while cheddar cheese finished last. The results followed a clear pattern: plant-based foods occupied the entire highest-scoring fifth, and animal-based products made up the lowest-scoring fifth.

Tofu’s lead came from strong results across both parts of the assessment. It earned a perfect 16 points for environmental performance, placing in the best-performing fifth for greenhouse gas emissions, water pollution, land use, and freshwater use. It also scored 20 out of 24 points for health and accessibility, including full marks for providing all nine essential amino acids.

Together, these measures explain why the rankings go beyond protein content alone. They weigh the environmental costs of producing protein alongside nutritional factors and practical considerations such as price and availability.

The paper argues that ratings focused on one feature can leave consumers with an incomplete picture. A product’s performance in one area does not explain its trade-offs elsewhere. Macdonald presents the framework as a way to help people understand those competing considerations and the food systems their purchases support.

As the paper states, “Against deliberate corporate obfuscation and marketing myths, the goal of a holistic score is not to create a new label, but to foster a new literacy, one that empowers us to see the reality of the systems we are funding.

The research builds on Macdonald’s work at the Better Protein Institute, which examines how much protein people need for optimal health, how to shift consumer behavior effectively, and where investment could support future protein sources. By questioning common assumptions and starting from first principles, that work addresses some of the most frequently Googled questions about dietary protein.
