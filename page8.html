45% of US Independent Arts Publications Face Existential Crisis, Report Finds

📅 Nov 13, 2026  |  Category: Business  |  Read Source →

In addition to declining funding, the survey identified a disturbing correlation between censorship concerns and financial precarity.

Nearly half of independent publications covering arts and culture in the United States said their business models will not be sustainable without “profound changes” to the current funding landscape, according to a report from Critical Minded and the Dorothea and Leo Rabkin Foundation released this week.

The new survey notably identifies a disturbing correlation between censorship concerns, including external pressure from advertisers and sponsors, and financial precarity. The 41% of respondents who said they faced free-speech pressures or threats in the last two years were more likely to report funding declines, throwing into stark relief how what outlets choose to cover — or not — may determine whether they can pay staff and contributors, or keep the lights on altogether.

“Support for Arts and Culture Journalism: A Survey of Independent Publications in the United States” polled 141 independent outlets that cover the arts on issues impacting the field and strategies used to address them. The anonymized respondents are based in 34 states and the District of Columbia, with the highest concentration (38.2%) in the Northeast, and reach an average annual audience of about half a million.

New report claims TikTok Live is fastest-growing platform for game streaming

📅 Nov 13, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

As TwitchCon descends upon San Diego, a different hub for video game streaming is celebrating its ascendance. TikTok Live is the fastest growing platform in that category, according to a new report published by the GameSquare subsidiary Hatchet.

Hatchet (previously known as Stream Hatchet) dispensed its TikTok Live status report as part of a biannual update related to the ByteDance-owned platform. During the first half of 2026, gaming streams on TikTok Live pulled in 2.5 billion hours of watch time. That traffic gave TikTok Live a 14% share of the game streaming market, pulling it even with the Stake.com-affiliated platform Kick.

Previous Hatchet reports have characterized Twitch and YouTube Gaming‘s dominant positions within the streaming landscape, but the firm’s latest dispatches have shown that TikTok Live is gaining on its market-leading rivals. While TikTok Live watch time grew 96% year-over-year, Twitch and YouTube Gaming’s respective market shares both went down.

A stunning seven-bedroom penthouse at Palazzo Della Luna on Fisher Island, which originally hit the market in Miami Beach for $50 million in June 2025, sold on Oct. 28, 2026, for $37.8 million.

Chinese AI labs are training models on Nvidia's Blackwell despite export controls, says ex-OpenAI engineer

📅 Nov 13, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Forensic report finds Blackwell's fingerprint in DeepSeek, Moonshot AI, MiniMax and Ant Group models

US export controls are not stopping AI labs in China from using restricted Nvidia chips, according to a report from a former OpenAI engineer.

Will DePue, who left the AI giant earlier this year to launch Unemployment Labs, an AI research consultancy, published forensic evidence showing that "DeepSeek, Moonshot AI, MiniMax and Ant Group have gained access to Nvidia's Blackwell GPUs, despite restrictions on exporting these chips to China, and are using that hardware to create their AI models."

"This sounds insane, but I discovered that almost every AI model carries a hidden signature of the GPU that trained it," said DePue in a social media post. "When a model is first set up, the starting values of its weights are laid out in a pattern that depends on the exact GPU. Each combination of GPU and seed number produces a unique pattern – a sort of fingerprint of the hardware used to create the model. Traces of that pattern survive training into the published model."

It doesn't sound that insane. The resignation of Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang followed from revelations that he had an affair with Chinese businesswoman Alice Huang, who, in 2023, founded Singapore-based Megaspeed International. That company is reportedly under investigation by US authorities for allegedly smuggling Nvidia chips to China.

Huang's departure coincides with a renewed push to tighten US chip export controls. House Speaker Mike Johnson supports the idea. And there are at least four bills awaiting consideration that aim to limit China's access to leading edge AI tech: the AI Overwatch Act, the Chip Security Act, Match Act, and the Cloud Security Act.

On Monday, US Senators Jim Banks (R-Ind.) and Elizabeth Warren (D-Mass.) cited Jensen Huang's "relationship with a suspected Chinese spy" as a justification for passing the AI Overwatch Act, which would codify the Trump administration's existing chip export controls. The Trump administration on Thursday said it's not sure whether Alice Huang was a spy, an allegation denied by Beijing and currently under investigation by the FBI.

Whatever the case, the current rules clearly are not working. Only a few weeks ago, Lava, an Israel-based security company, said it had found "32 Nvidia B300 GPUs, part of Nvidia's Blackwell Ultra generation, through publicly exposed monitoring endpoints with IP addresses geolocated to China."

These GPUs, Lava noted, are subject to US export controls [PDF]. The security biz said its findings followed from the exposure of monitoring systems intended for internal infrastructure management that were publicly exposed.

The analysis published through DePue's consultancy, "Top Chinese Frontier AI Models Carry the Fingerprint of Export-Controlled Nvidia Blackwell GPUs," takes a different approach: analyzing the published weights of various models from Chinese AI labs.

As described in the report, model weights – the parameters that change in relation to training data – must be initialized with some value. One method for doing so involves a pseudorandom number generation (PRNG) algorithm called Philox. The algorithm starts with a seed value that produces the same set of initial values whenever it's run.

Using PyTorch, initialization can be done by creating multiple GPU threads that work in parallel, with the help of Nvidia's cuRAND library. And because this process is not truly random, it's possible to select a chip and a seed value, generate the initial weights for that hardware and compare that pattern to a model's published weights.

"I searched more than six trillion combinations of chip layout and seed number and matched them to open-weights models," said DePue. "In doing so, I found conclusive evidence that six Chinese AI models – including DeepSeek-V4-Pro, Kimi K3, and MiniMax-M3 – were initialized on Nvidia Blackwell systems. Four of the models match Grace Blackwell, the GB200 and GB300 parts in Nvidia's flagship liquid-cooled rack-scale systems."

Noting that this is the first time MiniMax and Ant Group have been publicly linked to usage of Nvidia's Blackwell, he also cited several limitations to his approach. One is that usage of Blackwell GPUs doesn't prove where the GPUs were located, so it's possible some of these Chinese labs used this hardware through US providers – a loophole the Cloud Security Act aims to close.

DePue's report includes initial seed values that linked DeepSeek-V3 to Nvidia's Hopper and DeepSeek-V4-Flash to Blackwell, for those who wish to attempt to reproduce the results. DuPue claims that multiple senior researchers, both independent and inside frontier AI labs like OpenAI and Anthropic, have confirmed the result.

Coincidentally, OpenAI and Anthropic have each complained about Chinese labs copying their models through distillation, a pastime also noted by the US Cybersecurity and Infrastructure Security Agency. ®

Nanofat Is Dermatologists’ Best-Kept Secret for Younger, Fresher Skin

📅 Nov 13, 2026  |  Category: Health  |  Read Source →

It’s not an injectable filler, and it’s not fat transfer. It’s a secret third thing you’ll want to try.

The last few years have been unkind to our facial fat. We sliced out our buccal pads to create sunken-in, razor-sharp cheekbones. We zapped our skin with thermal devices that accidentally melted fat away in pursuit of boosting collagen. We injected our stomachs with fat-loss GLP-1 medications, leaving our faces looking hollow. Droopy. Structurally unsound. As it turns out, facial fat is critical infrastructure—not just for looking younger, but for your skin's overall health and quality. And while you can’t choose where you lose or gain fat, you can choose to have a doctor siphon it, remix it, and reinject it.

“Adipose tissue is a rich source of growth factors and progenitor cells, and re-injecting fat is probably one of the best ways to regenerate the full potential of healthy skin I've seen in the last decades,” says Patricia Ogilvie, a Munich-based dermatologist and member of the Dior Reverse Aging Board. “There are trends that are not really worth being a trend. I think the restaging of the importance of adipose tissue is very justified.”

You’re probably familiar with fat transfer, a procedure that’s been around for decades. In simple terms, fat transfer takes adipose tissue from one area of your body, like your flanks, liposuctions it, and moves it to another area, like your breasts or cheekbones. “With traditional fat transfer, it’s about restoring volume. And because it’s your own living tissue, it can integrate beautifully and give you very natural-looking results,” says dermatologist Sheerene Idriss, medical director and founder of Idriss Dermatology in New York City. “But what’s interesting is that fat isn’t just fat. It also contains regenerative cells, including adipose-derived stem cells, and growth factors that may help improve the surrounding tissue over time. Nanofat is where it gets really exciting.”

Nanofat is part of a new class of regenerative aesthetics offered by dermatologists and plastic surgeons, treatments that don’t just make you look better, but rather address the signs of aging from the inside out by stimulating collagen or repairing tissue. Imagine taking that same fat from a traditional fat transfer and processing it into a fine, milky liquid rich in your own stem cells and growth factors, then injecting or microneedling it back into your face—that’s nanofat.

“Instead of using it to restore volume, you’re using it to potentially improve skin quality itself,” says Idriss. “These cells and growth factors communicate with surrounding tissue and can stimulate processes involved in collagen production, tissue repair, and blood vessel formation. So you’re not just trying to fill a wrinkle. You’re trying to improve the skin itself—its thickness, resilience, and texture—so that wrinkle potentially becomes less noticeable.”

The best areas for nanofat injections include under the eye, a notoriously risky area for hyaluronic acid fillers, and the cheekbones. “The ideal patient for nanofat is someone who's starting to notice thinner, crepier skin, fine lines, or changes in skin texture, especially around the eyes, neck, or chest, and wants to improve skin quality rather than add volume,” adds Idriss, who both has had the treatment done and offers it in her practice. Plus, Ogilvie notes, nanofat doesn’t attract water the way fillers do, so it won’t cause under-eye puffiness (a common post-filler complaint). But because nanofat doesn’t restore volume as much as it reinvigorates your skin, you can inject it almost anywhere you’re noticing signs of aging.

“We do it under tumescent local anesthesia, so you’re awake the entire time, and recovery is usually pretty quick,” says Idriss. “I personally had nanofat and fat transfer in June and was back at work the next day. The biggest downside is some soreness at the fat extraction site, which usually lasts a few days, and you’ll want to avoid strenuous exercise for about a week. Because there’s no general anesthesia, you’re also avoiding the grogginess and recovery associated with being put under.”

Like any cosmetic treatment, it has risks and downsides. Experts note that nanofat isn’t the right treatment for anyone looking to restore significant volume loss or seeking instant results. “Improvement in skin quality can vary from person to person, and it takes months to see the full results,” Idriss adds. “There are rare but serious risks, including infection, vascular complications, and even vision loss when injecting certain areas of the face.” But patients who have tried the procedure say the recovery is quick and easy, with results compounding over weeks, though they ultimately fade away faster than traditional injectables.

Nanofat injections sit somewhere between microfat and hyaluronic acid fillers. Microfat can be lumpy and unpredictable when injected, sometimes leading to lopsided results. Plus, in any fat transfer, only a small amount of fat survives. Fillers, on the other hand, offer long-lasting precision and volume restoration, but patients are tired of looking overfilled and unnaturally plump. Nanofat helps your skin look naturally healthier, fresher, and firmer, at least for a while. After all, it’s made from you.

“I look at this as something you can do periodically throughout your life, but what you do in between treatments is just as important. And that’s why I don’t believe in demonizing any one treatment, including filler,” says Idriss. “When used appropriately, for the right indication, these treatments can all work beautifully together. It’s about knowing what you need, when you need it, and when enough is enough.”
