San Francisco Proclaims Fromm Day as the Fromm Institute Turns 50

📅 Nov 19, 2026  |  Category: Science  |  Read Source →

More than 960 students enrolled at the Fromm Institute this fall. In a student survey, 63 percent of respondents said they felt less isolated.

SAN FRANCISCO, Nov. 19, 2026 /PRNewswire/ -- On her lunchtime walks through the University of San Francisco in 2012, Max Ferman kept seeing the same groups of older adults talking and laughing as they walked from Fromm Hall to the University Center. They disappeared in the summer and came back in the fall. "Who were these happy old people!?" she recalled wondering.

Ferman became a Fromm student in 2022 and shared that story on behalf of her fellow students at the Fromm Institute for Lifelong Learning's 50th anniversary gala on Nov. 14, 2026. Students, faculty, university leaders and supporters gathered at the McLaren Center, where the Institute received a proclamation from Mayor Daniel Lurie Office declaring Nov. 14 Fromm Day in San Francisco.

San Francisco proclaimed Nov. 14 Fromm Day as the Fromm Institute marked 50 years of lifelong learning.

Fern Warner attended the Institute's first session in 1976 and still takes classes today. Betty Dvorson, who is about to turn 101, keeps coming to class because learning makes her happy. In a Fromm Institute student survey, 82 percent of respondents reported greater life satisfaction, 63 percent said they felt less isolated and 52 percent reported forming new social networks.

Executive Director Derek Leighnor described how those relationships take shape. A conversation that starts in class can turn into a standing lunch date or a friendship that lasts for years. When the pandemic closed classrooms in 2020, Fromm students learned to meet online so they could keep studying together and stay connected.

University of San Francisco President Salvador D. Aceves invited guests to picture an 18-year-old undergraduate heading to a first-year seminar while a 90-year-old Fromm student walked to class. Hanna and Alfred Fromm's founding vision, he said, broadened whom the university could serve and gave younger and older students a chance to learn from one another.

Rabbi Brian Lurie, chair of the Friends of the Fromm Institute Board of Directors, thanked university leaders, the board and Leighnor for sustaining the Institute.

Hanna Fromm expected about 50 people at the first open house. About 150 enrolled in the first year. This fall, more than 960 enrolled. Fifty years after that first open house, Fromm students are introducing more San Franciscans to the program at neighborhood farmers markets.

Enrollment is now open for the Fromm Institute's Winter session, which begins Jan. 11, 2027. Adults 50 and older can explore courses and enroll.

About the Fromm Institute for Lifelong Learning

Founded in 1976 at the University of San Francisco, the Fromm Institute offers daytime courses for adults 50 and older, on campus and online. Students explore art, history, literature, science and other subjects without grades, exams or homework. fromminstitute.org

New Ukraine talks including Europe to be held 'soon' — Merz

📅 Nov 19, 2026  |  Category: World  |  Read Source →

Germany's Chancellor Friedrich Merz says European leaders could take part in fresh Ukraine talks on "neutral ground." The UK and EU countries have urged a seat at the table in meetings between the US, Russia and Ukraine.

German Chancellor Friedrich Merz said on Friday he believes new talks regarding Russia's invasion of Ukraine with European leaders taking part will occur "soon."

"Of course we are prepared to hold talks with Russia, we have always said this. These talks are now set to take place soon," Merz said after a meeting of Ukraine's allies in Berlin. He did not give a concrete date for the next talks, but said Europe's interests would be represented by Germany, France, the UK, Poland and Italy.

Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskyy was present at the Coalition of the Willing summit in the German capital, along with British Prime Minister Andy Burnham, French President Emmanuel Macron, US President Donald Trump's son-in-law Jared Kushner and US Special Envoy Steve Witkoff.

Merz said the talks including Europe would take place on "neutral ground," such as the United Arab Emirates.

Merz along with other European leaders have been critical of meetings between US, Ukrainian and Russian representatives regarding the conflict, arguing that Europe needs a direct seat at the negotiating table.

Russia's invasion of Ukraine began in February 2022, with the Trump administration prioritizing an end to the war.

Trump took part in a bilateral meeting with Russian President Vladimir Putin in Shenzhen, China on Friday, with Trump saying the two leaders would discuss the war in closed-door talks. Putin acknowledged that Europeans want a seat at the table, and said Russia invited European leaders to Moscow.

"We understand that discussing issues linked to security in Europe is pointless without Europeans," Putin said, while rejecting any military cooperation between Europe and Ukraine.

Trump also took part in a historic trilateral meeting with Putin and Chinese President Xi Jinping.

The Trump-Putin-Xi meeting raised fears that the US, Russia and China are looking to carve up the world into spheres of influence. It drew comparisons with the February 1945 Yalta Conference, where US President Franklin D. Roosevelt, UK Prime Minister Winston Churchill and Soviet Union leader Joseph Stalin discussed Europe's future as Nazi Germany's defeat was imminent in World War II.

Merz handed out pieces of the Berlin Wall to leaders at the Coalition of the Willing summit, saying the Yalta structure had brought "oppression, insecurity and a lack of peace."

"This is exactly what Yalta stood for," Merz said.

GTA 6 Ultimate Edition Has A Car Upgrade You Can Unlock Right From The Start

Like other installments in the long-running and beloved series, "Grand Theft Auto 6" is as much about the autos as it is about the grand theft. Sure, the game has a campaign, and the open-world setting makes it tons of fun to run around and commit crimes. But for some people, the biggest draw has always been Rockstar's sweet, sweet, non-licensed but clearly real-world-inspired cars (and planes, bikes, and boats, of course). Throughout the game, you'll find and unlock car customization options of all types, from decals to lighting and beyond.

If you want one of the coolest rides in all of gaming, though, you don't have to work very hard. You just have to shell out the extra $20 to get GTA 6 Ultimate Edition. Doing so gets you early-game access to the coveted and beautiful Ganado Retro Build. This is an exclusive customization of player character Jason's personal '70 Ganado, a Ford Ranchero-like pickup truck that's become an instant classic. Of all the cars of GTA 6, the Ganado Retro Build is especially noteworthy because of what it replaces. It's a major glow up from the well-loved, drab-colored Ganado Jason starts the game with.

While Ultimate Edition owners are spoiled with perhaps the best-looking car in the game, they still have to work for it just a bit. No matter which version of the game you buy, you start out with Jason's base Vapid Ganado, which isn't in the best condition. To upgrade to the iconic Retro Build, you'll have to drive the Ganado to a location called Coastal Auto Care, found in Key Lento. Just don't forget to fill up on the way, because cars in "Grand Theft Auto 6" don't have infinite fuel. The player is introduced to Coastal Auto Care early on via a mission, and you can find it on your map by filtering for "Mod Shops."

You can upgrade your Ganado during the "Just a Scratch" mission, or you can visit Coastal Auto Care any time after completing the mission to apply the upgrade. Once you've driven into the garage's entrance, you get the car customization screen. This screen offers you a choice of different customization options, like cosmetics, bodywork, performance, and interior. Instead of choosing any of those, select the "Pro Builds" tab, which offers you the choice between full kits and wheel kits. For Ultimate Edition players, the Ganado Retro Build kit is found under "Full Kits," and it's free to apply. Unfortunately, this classic design and slick color palette is specific to Jason's Ganado, so you can't bring in other cars and apply the Retro Build kit to them.

Once you're cruising around in the sickest car in the game, you'll want to practice defensive driving. GTA 6 has an advanced physics engine for more realistic car crashes, and nobody wants to see such a beautiful car smashed to bits in a fender-bender.

If you're the type of person who likes to try out every car possible, you'll have your work cut out for you in "GTA 6." There are over 300 different rides you can find throughout the course of the game. Some, like the Declasse Akane (inspired by the Chevy Sonic) are pretty unexciting, albeit still fun to commit major crimes in. Others are breath-takers that can help under-thrilled enthusiasts live their exotic and classic driving dreams.

Take the '95 Grotti Cheetah, inspired by the Ferrari Testarossa. This is another Ultimate Edition-only vehicle, but boy is it sick. If you figure you'll probably never drive a real Testarossa without renting one in Vegas, driving the Cheetah around is a fun second-best. Another eye-grabbing option is the obnoxious green Canis Kamacho, inspired by the Jeep Crew Chief 715 concept. It's a solid in-game off-roader that's available in the base game.

For players with aspirations that extend beyond the asphalt, the Shitzu Squalo is a special boat available to owners of the Ultimate Edition of the game. It's based on the Christ-Craft Stinger 390x and can be found at the docks of Washington Beach. It has a striking pink and blue gradient color scheme, and despite its name, it's not squalid at all. You'll also get access to biplanes, jets, helicopters, and even airboats as you play the game, allowing you to explore the world in whatever style fits your fancy.

Posted November 19, 2026 | Reviewed by Gary Drevitch

Poison Ivy may be the only Batman villain whose cause many readers quietly agree with.

In her origin story from Batman comics, Dr. Pamela Isley (Poison Ivy) was once a passionate scientist. However, she was injected with toxins that merged her with plant life. She can control vegetation and feel the pain of plants directly.1 Driven by empathic distress, she fights environmental abuse and political negligence. Archetypally, she embodies both the grief and the fury of a wounded Earth.

Her grief reflects emotions many of us share; the research literature captures how people commonly feel sadness, fear, anger, helplessness, and profound grief in response to ecological loss.2 This is closely tied to the experience of solastalgia, or the anguish of watching a cherished home environment deteriorate until it no longer offers the solace it once did.

Environmental grief and climate-related trauma are natural and instinctive responses to climate breakdown.3 They arise from tangible losses of ecosystems, wildlife, homes, jobs, and communities. They also arise from losses that are harder to see, such as diminished trust, a loss of faith in the future, and a deeper psycho-spiritual disconnection from Earth itself.

Poison Ivy is also a figure of political grief. She is driven by moral outrage at governance that permits deforestation, pollution, and the exploitation of the natural world. Environmental grief often intersects with political grief, especially as climate change and environmental breakdown places a far greater burden on disadvantaged communities and developing countries.4 Subsequently, growing numbers of people are questioning values that give personhood to corporations but not to the natural world.

Poison Ivy accurately shows how these losses can be felt on an intensely personal level, one that penetrates us to the core of our being.

Grief, hopelessness, and despair can push us into survival states marked by defensiveness, psychological inflexibility, and absolutist thinking.5 Over time, these experiences can lead people toward extremist positions, social alienation, and political polarization.6 Poison Ivy’s impulsive, destructive choices do not create meaningful collective change. Her over-identification with plants causes her to lose touch with her own humanity.

In troubling times, it can be tempting to imagine that we need a figure like Poison Ivy. This supervillain fantasy will not solve the ecological crisis, though, or meaningfully comfort our grieving hearts.

The world has already experienced enough destructive authoritarianism that violates all traditional cultural wisdom. Violence will only make problems worse, and coercion entrenches polarization and resistance.6 Tragically, supervillains embody the same cycles of harm they claim to oppose. Without an ethical framework, supervillains impose rigid ideologies that oppose cooperation, respect, dignity, and mutual understanding.

The environmental crisis and the ongoing tear in our political systems evoke a great deal of fear, which is exactly why we need superheroes. The Justice League from DC Comics is a diverse group of superheroes who unite against threats too large for any one of them to handle. Despite differing ideologies, they preserve a sense of common humanity.

Adopting a stance of common humanity does not require compromising deeply held values or excusing abuses of power.6 Instead, it means focusing on our intention to connect rather than our need to defend. Many Indigenous worldviews express this through four guiding values: relationship, responsibility, reciprocity, and redistribution.7

Over 40 years ago, Roger Walsh urged us to face collective existential threats from a place of psychological self-awareness rather than fear or shadow.8 External change is most effective when it is fuelled by inner transformation and by tending to our own unresolved losses. Furthermore, crises benefit from level-headed individuals who are respectful, strive to minimize harm, utilize ethical frameworks, and maintain unwavering integrity. In other words, people who can embody and psychologically integrate the qualities of a superhero are what the world needs.

When we can metabolize our widespread environmental and political grief, we create inner space and presence. That space awakens us to life, love, and interdependence, and to the ability to re-imagine the future. No one is also meant to face this grief alone. Our collective moment of reckoning has arrived, and we need all the superheroes we can muster.

This new collection uses superheroes and the multiverse to explore how non-death loss impacts our psychology.9

A Hiker Fell off a Cliff in Big Bend National Park. Then Rescuers Had to Leave Him There Overnight.

📅 Nov 19, 2026  |  Category: Other  |  Read Source →

Sitting in far West Texas along the Rio Grande, Big Bend is more than 100 miles from the nearest major city (Photo: National Guard)

A 19-year-old hiker is safe after falling off a cliff and staying the night on a narrow ledge in Big Bend National Park, one of the most remote national parks in the Lower 48.

According to a news release, on November 13, the man, whose name authorities have not released, fell ten feet when his gear gave way out in an area inaccessible to ground-based search and rescue responders. Rescuers reached the area just after 9 P.M. and called the Georgia Army National Guard medical evacuation crew assigned to the Joint Task Force-Southern Border. The organization works with U.S. Customs and Border Protection to monitor the shared border between Texas and Mexico, an area central to a heated national debate over the Trump Administration’s border wall construction.

The helicopter crew had taken off, but with limited lighting and winds approaching 35 miles per hour, and a string of storms moving into the area, the Black Hawk medevac crew made the difficult decision to scrub the mission.

“The crew agreed it was not safe and we had to make the unfortunate decision to go back and try again in the daylight,” Chief Warrant Officer 2 Gregory Hiett, the aircraft commander, said in a statement. “It was an extremely difficult thing to do, knowing the patient was going to be stuck out there for the rest of the night.”

Throughout the night, park responders remained on-scene and on the ground.

Just after 9 A.M. on November 14, a second helicopter mission launched with a hoist rescue team. Flight medic and technician Holly Johnson descended 150 feet on a cable, determined the hiker was uninjured, and secured him in a rescue vest to lift him into the aircraft. The mission was finished by 10:45 A.M., and the hiker was transferred to Big Bend Regional Medical Center.

“During a complex hoist operation in rugged, inaccessible terrain, our medevac crew successfully reached and evacuated an injured hiker when no ground rescue option existed, delivering the patient safely to higher medical care,” Lt. Col. Teresa Parrotta, commander of the 29th Combat Aviation Brigade, Maryland Army National Guard, said in a statement.

Sitting in far West Texas along the Rio Grande, Big Bend is more than 100 miles from the nearest major city. It’s rugged, isolated, and enormous, spanning 800,000 acres with sheer 1,500-foot limestone cliffs. The National Park Service warns visitors to expect unreliable cell service, sparse infrastructure, and primitive roads—conditions compounded by the intense Chihuahuan Desert sandwiched between the Chisos Mountains. Reaching a hiking trailhead is challenging because roads frequently wash out from flash flooding, and many backcountry trails require high-clearance, four-wheel-drive vehicles.

Chucho Valdés Turns 85 & Keeps Rocking His 88 Keys

📅 Nov 19, 2026  |  Category: World  |  Read Source →

The Cuban pianist talks with Billboard about his new album UNCUT: "Being in love with the piano and with music, it makes you feel like there's no such thing as age."

Chucho Valdés celebrated his 85th birthday on Oct. 9 with a party at his house (replete with musicians, of course); with a series of concerts across the U.S. during which he played both solo and with an ensemble featuring  jazz notables like saxophonist Joe Lovano, bassist John Patitucci, drummer Marcus Gilmore and percussionist Roberto Jr. Vizcaino Torre; and with a solo album, UNCUT, released prior to his big day. The album was a particularly oversized achievement for Valdés, a towering musical figure with a towering height (6-foot-5) to match. UNCUT — released on indie InnerJazz (part of InnerCat Music, Valdés’ label since the early 2000s) — is an eight-track set recorded completely live by Valdés on his favorite Steinway grand, with no cuts, no edits and no second takes. It’s the kind of exercise in musicianship, inspiration and joy that’s allowed Valdés to remain an essential figure in both the jazz and Latin music worlds; a man whose music spans countries, generations and genres.

Reaching back to his origins as the son of another legendary pianist, Bebo Valdés, Chucho has traversed the musical world from classical to experimental jazz, famously creating Cuban experimental group Irakere alongside Paquito D’Rivera and Arturo Sandoval, then launching into other realms with abandon, collecting seven Grammys and seven Latin Grammys along the way.

“Imagine. The piano is the soundtrack of my entire life. I was born listening to it and then I learned it,” says Valdés today.

In celebration of his monumental year and his 85th birthday, Valdés spoke with Billboard.

To what do you attribute the fact that you can still tour, write and compose at 85 years old?

I really think it’s because of my passion for learning. Also, the thrill of sharing and connecting with the audience that’s listening. The healthy ambition to create different things, being in love with the piano and with music. It makes you feel like there’s no such thing as age in this sense. It’s incredible. It’s as if you’re still young.

UNCUT is not your first solo piano album?

I have others. But it had been a long time since I made a solo piano album, and this one was truly improvised. I just sat down to play whatever came to mind. But yes, I’ve done a solo album before. Once, back in 1986, I was traveling from Germany to Austria, and they proposed I do an album, but there was a catch: there was a flight that left at 1 p.m., and the time from the studio to the airport was an hour. So I had one hour to record, from 11 a.m. to 12 p.m. I said, “I’m not sure this is going to work,” but it worked out. It’s called “Lukumí?”

What’s your earliest memory of the piano?

My first memory of the piano is from when I was very, very, very small—since I can remember. Seeing my father sitting and playing Lecuona, but also playing jazz. And to me, it seemed impossible to do because I was a five-year-old kid.

Was there ever a moment when you didn’t want to be a pianist?

No. Always, always, my dream was to learn how to play the piano. Inspired by my father, but also because of myself. I wanted to be a pianist.

If you didn’t play the piano, what instrument would you play?

I think I would play the double bass or the cello. The cello because it sings so beautifully. To me, it’s the most expressive instrument.

What do you feel most comfortable with: jazz, classical music or Latin jazz?

I swear, listen carefully, I swear. It’s just sitting at the piano without a plan, without premeditation, and starting to play whatever comes to mind in the moment. That’s the thing that gives me the most joy.

What has jazz taught you that classical music or Cuban music hasn’t?

They’re different things. Cuban music is very rich in rhythms. Years ago, it was extremely rich in rhythm, but it was somewhat limited harmonically. The rhythm of Cuban and Afro-Cuban music was rich, but the harmony of the tumbaos [repeating piano patterns] was quite simple. It was beautiful but a bit limited. Jazz, on the other hand, offered a much richer harmonic field and a much broader harmonic landscape. As I started to mature, what I did was mix the two: adding jazz-influenced harmonies to Cuban music and mambo.

Imagine. Ayayay. The piano is the soundtrack of my whole life. Because I was born listening to it, then I learned it, went to the conservatory, and fell in love with classical music. There are so many geniuses, but there are some who touched me deeply. I’m in love with Bach, Mozart, and Beethoven, but the Romantic period with Chopin touched me a lot, a lot, a lot. And other classical composers like Schubert and Schumann. But Impressionism, the era of Ravel and Debussy, was another musical period that I loved. And when we arrive at the Neo-Romantics, Rachmaninoff is my life. That concerto [the gods] was written by the gods. It’s a beauty.

Yes. When I’m at home and with my family, I have about three sessions. One after breakfast, one at noon, and the one I enjoy the most is at night, when no TVs are on and there’s peace and quiet. But whenever I can, I sit down and play, and I enjoy it. When you love that instrument, time doesn’t exist.

Your wife is a percussionist. Was it important to have another musician by your side?

I think so. Because there’s human communication between partners, but also, when it becomes a connection, she can understand what I want and what I’m doing. So we share better. My son started with the piano, then the guitar, then the flute, and then singing. But when he started playing percussion, that’s when he found his path.

You just finished your solo piano album and a tour with a jazz trio. Those are very different formats. How do you prepare for each one?

That’s something else. They are such, such different worlds. When you’re alone, you’re free from everything. You just have to start playing whatever comes to mind. When you’re in a trio, you’re accompanied by a bassist and a drummer, and you have to think differently. You have to think about how to create a teamwork dynamic, where we’re speaking the same language and giving space to the other musicians. At this point in my life, it comes automatically because I’ve done it so many times. But it’s not the same. It’s the same as playing with a big band. In a big band, the piano is a supporting instrument. You have to add details exactly where they won’t interfere with the band. I learned that from my father and from the orchestras I played in back in Cuba. Every world is different. But sitting there alone, I’m on cloud nine.

Right now, I’m working on a symphonic project. I already have a repertoire, but I’m going to enrich it. For example, my version of ‘Mozart a la cubana’ is being expanded for a full orchestra. It’s Cuban Mozart, but suddenly it turns into something rhythmic, while still respecting the melody. I have something so beautiful, which is Gabriel Fauré’s “Pavane.” Ayayaya, I love it so much. Fauré came before Debussy, but he was already entering the realm of Impressionism. There’s such a beautiful part, and every time I play it, there’s a standing ovation at the end. Always, always. So I’m going to include that.

When you’re not inspired, where do you go?

Sometimes the piano and I kind of fight. Because sometimes I say, I’m over-practicing, and I don’t want to play it. And it says to me, “Fine, don’t play me.” And sometimes I’ll go three days like that. But when I sit down again, it makes me pay for it – without mercy. One finger over here, another finger over there.

What do you think when people call you a genius?

I’m not a genius at all. I’m just a person who loves music and was raised with a sense of respect and always learning something new. And if that results in someone thinking that it’s genius, I love it.

In a life full of memorable moments, what’s the one that’s most engraved in your memory?

Let me tell you. We were in Cuba in 1978, and leaving Cuba was practically impossible for a Cuban jazz group. Our dream was to meet the great jazz artists, but to meet them personally… And we were lucky in 1978 to get a contract with Columbia, and our debut in the United States was on June 25, 1978, at Carnegie Hall. It was the first time a Cuban jazz group played at a jazz festival in the United States. And two of the jazz pianists I was obsessed with performed there: Bill Evans and McCoy Tyner, as well as a lady who was an expert in blues, Mary Lou Williams. That was a moment I will never forget. It had been 18 years since I had seen my father because he lived in Sweden, and I couldn’t go to the United States. But my father flew from Sweden to New York, and I knew he was in the audience. And after that concert, we hugged for an hour and cried and kissed. That was the greatest moment of my life. I was 19 years old when my father left. And the emotion of knowing he flew from Sweden, and I was playing but didn’t know where he was sitting, was something incredible. The dream of my life.

I’m not going to lie. I buy a lot of information. I buy a lot of music books, of course. But the truth is, I spend all my money on clothes. I love it. Louis Vuitton, Gucci, Robert Graham. I have a collection of shirts.

Who, alive or dead, would you like to share a bottle of wine with?

With Bill Evans. He often brings me to tears. He was such an emotional, sincere pianist. Wow. I was lucky that day in Carnegie Hall, I met Bill Evans, and when they told me, “This man wants to meet you,” and he reached out his hand, I hugged him crying and told him that I listened to him all the time. He was and still is my favorite pianist.

“This game is such woke slop,” wrote one blue-check user on X who has posted almost nothing besides negative reactions to the game for the past week. The post included a screenshot of a fully optional GTA VI mission that describes how the Lucia character will appreciate a flower delivery and indicates with a heart icon that the action will strengthen feelings between her and Jason.

Other choices you can make as Jason to get closer to Lucia (sex is very much on the table) include picking up groceries, getting a haircut or beard trim, gifting her a piece of jewelry, and repainting walls in the house.

The concept of role-playing as a nice and non-performatively masculine boyfriend has come as a rude shock to those more interested in explosions and mayhem. The right-wing streamer Zack Hoyt, known online as Asmongold, was dumbfounded to find himself unclogging a sink in a scene where Jason is trying to collect back rent from a tenant who refuses the money until the plumbing is fixed. “I actually have to fucking fix this bitch’s sink?” Hoyt asked on stream. “Why can’t she do this?” Elsewhere, when running errands for Lucia as Jason, Hoyt referred to GTA VI as a “cuck simulator.” (That Hoyt thinks caring for a romantic partner makes someone a cuckold is perhaps an unfortunate glimpse into his dating history.)

More frustrated responses to the massively popular GTA VI came from looksmaxxing influencer Braden Peters, aka Clavicular, (the game required too much “simping for girls,” the streamer, currently facing multiple rape charges, complained on X), anti-woke gamer activist Mark Kern, aka Grummz (he called the housework “mundane crap”), and misinformation superspreader Dom Lucre, who falsely claimed that Rockstar had specifically made it impossible to open fire in a gay nightclub. (Washington Post critic Gene Park pointed out that the club in question was not LGBTQ-specific and that the mechanics of the game don’t allow you to shoot anyone in any kind of club.) These comments came alongside the predictable gripes over supposedly “overweight” female characters with insufficiently large breasts.

If the Tates, incels, and other far-right reactionaries of the world are so bent out of shape, it’s probably because whereas GTA once punched down at marginalized groups like sex workers and transgender people, bigots and self-styled alpha males are now the butt of the joke. The Vice City of GTA VI is bristling with parodies of toxic manosphere content and crypto scams. Random characters on the street will spout MAGA-coded conspiracy theories and call you a “libtard” or “commie socialist” if you walk into them. These touches seem to have made the game into “a bug zapper for chuds,” as one Bluesky user put it. (That hasn’t stopped Republicans and Trump allies from trying and failing to score political points with the game.)

Yet the provocation of asking guys to consider acts of service for a virtual girlfriend could prove the most revolutionary element of GTA VI.

It’s not that games like The Sims haven’t explored this territory before, and Rockstar’s own Red Dead Redemption franchise paid similar attention to quotidian detail. In those frontier-set narratives, your cowboy character had to make coffee over campfires and even clean up manure in barns. By addressing characters’ most basic needs—you can even manage personal hygiene by showering—GTA VI recalls some of the slower and quieter aspects of that franchise.

What’s truly remarkable, though, is that adult men who grew up indulging their most selfish and sociopathic instincts in the older GTA games have had their nostalgia for those escapades cleverly complicated with real-life problems. They can still steal cars, sell drugs, and rise through the ranks of a seedy underworld. That will always be the appeal of the series, which is now asking eternally aggrieved culture warriors to consider a vital question: What if you didn’t have to do it alone?

Resolve AI’s SRE-1 model finds root causes of production alerts as AI agents go on call

📅 Nov 19, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Incident remediation company Resolve AI Inc. has announced the launch of SRE-1, a new model that has been trained to find the root cause of production alerts, and Primary On-call, a new service that puts its artificial intelligence agents on call ahead of human engineers.

The new model, announced Wednesday, reflects the company’s argument that while AI has transformed how software gets written, it has barely touched how software gets run. According to Resolve AI, production has no public training corpus to match the published code that models learned from, as incidents at each company stay private.

SRE-1 is a 27 billion-parameter model built on open weights and trained by a team of 16 AI researchers over a final run of 7.4 days on up to 512 graphics processing units. Measured on the same incidents before and after training, which used reinforcement learning alone, the pass rate for a single investigation rose from 6.9% to 38.8%.

Resolve AI used Snowflake Inc.’s Cortex Training, a serverless service for customizing open-weight models, to build SRE-1. Snowflake Senior Vice President of Engineering Vivek Raghunathan said Resolve AI was the first design partner for the service.

In testing on 49 difficult live production cases, each run five times, an ensemble of five SRE-1 investigations matched the strongest frontier model tested at a lower cost, according to the company. Resolve AI also benchmarked its complete system on 178 production alert investigations, solving about twice as many incidents as general-purpose coding agents.

Primary On-call puts that research to work on the critical path. Each page is investigated across telemetry, infrastructure, code and operational knowledge, according to the company. Depending on what it finds, the agent can close the alert, mitigate the problem where a team’s policies permit or escalate to a human. Once it has acted, the agent checks the fix against the production baseline, monitoring it until recovery holds.

The agent also links related alerts and investigations into a shared view of each underlying problem. For teams wary of handing over the pager straight away, Resolve AI offers a shadow mode that records the call the agent would have made on each page.

Up to 80% of paged alerts in production are said to be handled by Primary On-call without escalation. Customers have reported a tenfold improvement in mean time to resolution, along with root-cause accuracy of up to 95%, according to Resolve AI.

Salesforce Inc. is among the early users. Bhupesh Patel, a software engineering principal architect at Salesforce, said that in the first few weeks of dark launch testing, the agent closed more than 70% of the CRM Analytics team’s paging alerts “without waking an engineer.”

“No engineering team can keep up with how fast code and patches land in production,” said Resolve AI Chief Executive Spiros Xanthos. “The answer is agents that are accurate and fast enough to sit on the critical path, and that takes a model built for production.”

Primary On-call is now generally available. Access to SRE-1 is limited to Resolve AI’s own platform.

Resolve AI is a venture capital-backed startup that has raised more than $190 million to date, including a $40 million extension to its Series A round in April, led by DST Global and Salesforce Ventures, that valued the company at $1.5 billion. Notable customers include Coinbase Global Inc., DoorDash Inc., Figma Inc., MSCI Inc., Nasdaq Inc., SAP SE and Zscaler Inc.
