Analysis: Airbus A350 Passengers Often Don't Feel As Tired After Flying Long-Haul Compared To A Boeing 777

📅 Nov 08, 2026  |  Category: Business  |  Read Source →

George Mutch & Michael Derrer Fuchs | Shutterstock, Simple Flying

Passengers stepping off a 12-hour flight may feel noticeably better after traveling on an Airbus A350 than after making a similar journey aboard an older Boeing 777. The difference is not simply a matter of seat comfort or airline service. The A350 was designed around a newer cabin environment, incorporating lower pressurization altitude, improved humidity retention, and noise-control measures intended to reduce the physical strain of spending many hours in the air.

These features have a credible scientific basis for mattering, though the evidence requires qualification. No major controlled study has directly compared passenger fatigue after equivalent A350 and 777 flights. Aircraft configuration, airline operating practices, seating density, sleep opportunity, and individual health all influence how travelers feel. Nevertheless, the A350 offers several potential advantages over many earlier 777s, particularly on very long sectors. Those benefits can make the journey less taxing, even though they cannot eliminate jet lag.

One of the A350's most consequential comfort features is its ability to maintain a lower cabin altitude. Although the aircraft may cruise above 35,000 feet, its pressurization system keeps the cabin environment at a much lower equivalent altitude. Airbus specifies a cabin altitude of 6,000 feet (1,828 meters) for the A350, a level made possible in part by its carbon-fiber-reinforced plastic fuselage. Older Boeing 777 variants generally operate within the conventional commercial-aircraft limit of 8,000 feet (2,438 meters) at maximum operating altitude. This is a regulatory ceiling, not a guarantee that every 777 flight remains at precisely 8,000 feet. Actual cabin altitude changes during a flight and varies with operating conditions. Even so, the potential difference between a 6,000-foot A350 cabin and an 8,000-foot cabin is physiologically meaningful.

A 2007 study published in the New England Journal of Medicine examined 502 volunteers during simulated flights in a controlled hypobaric chamber. Researchers exposed participants to cabin pressures equivalent to altitudes ranging from 650 feet to 8,000 feet. At the highest simulated altitude, average oxygen saturation fell by as much as 4.4 percentage points compared with the lowest condition. Participants also reported more discomfort, including fatigue and muscular discomfort, at 7,000 to 8,000 feet. Those differences emerged after three to nine hours of exposure.

The researchers did not find that the lower oxygen levels caused acute mountain sickness or measurable impairment in sensory and psychomotor testing. Their conclusion was more specific: maintaining a cabin altitude of 6,000 feet or lower could reduce passenger discomfort on long flights. This supports the A350's approach, but it does not prove that every traveler will arrive less tired or that the aircraft prevents fatigue. Pressurization performance varies by generation and operating conditions. Nevertheless, the A350's lower target gives it a tangible advantage in an area where scientific research has established a relationship between cabin altitude and passenger discomfort.

Cabin humidity is another factor that can influence how comfortable passengers feel after a long flight. The air supplied to aircraft cabins at cruising altitude is extremely dry. Relative humidity on many long-haul flights commonly falls between 10% and 20%, well below levels people experience indoors on the ground. Low humidity does not normally cause clinically significant dehydration in healthy passengers. It can, however, dry the eyes, nose, throat, and skin, making a long journey feel less comfortable. Increasing cabin humidity improved nasal comfort while reducing ocular, nasal, and skin symptoms.

The A350's composite fuselage helps address an engineering constraint behind cabin humidity. Traditional aluminum structures are more vulnerable to corrosion when exposed to sustained moisture, so aircraft manufacturers have historically limited humidity to reduce the risk of condensation and structural deterioration. Composite materials are less susceptible to that form of corrosion, giving designers greater flexibility to manage moisture levels.

Airbus says the A350 combines its carbon-fiber structure with improved humidity levels during cruise, contributing to lower passenger fatigue. Independent aviation-health research also notes that newer composite aircraft, including the A350 and Boeing 787, can retain more moisture than older designs. Cruise humidity around 15% has been reported in some cases. This does not mean every A350 cabin remains at 15% to 20% throughout a flight. Humidity varies with passenger numbers, ventilation rates, and operating conditions, and it is not routinely monitored in commercial cabins. Passengers may notice fewer dry-eye symptoms or less irritation after many hours, particularly when improved moisture retention accompanies lower cabin altitude. Humidity alone is unlikely to determine whether someone arrives exhausted, but it can remove one source of discomfort that becomes more noticeable as flight time increases.

The A350's reputation as a 'passenger favorite' masks a more complex truth for flight crews, whose rest experiences can vary widely.

Noise is less straightforward to compare because measurements depend on where they are taken, which engines power the aircraft, the phase of flight, and the seating area. Airbus describes the A350 as the quietest aircraft in its category and says its noise-optimized airframe and engine nacelles can make it up to nine decibels quieter than competing aircraft. The Rolls-Royce Trent XWB engines, aerodynamic design, and cabin insulation contribute to its acoustic environment.

Independent measurements offer a more restrained picture. Researchers found that the differences between the several widebody aircraft measured were relatively small, within approximately three decibels. The A350 was not the quietest aircraft in that particular sample, with the Airbus A380 recording the lowest levels. These findings show why manufacturer comparisons should not be interpreted as a universal passenger experience. Even small reductions in background noise can help on a long flight. A steadier acoustic environment may make it easier to read, relax, or sleep, especially for passengers sensitive to engine noise or cabin activity. Noise also interacts with other conditions: someone in a quiet business-class suite may rest better than a traveler seated near a galley, regardless of aircraft type.

The A350's cabin environment does not make it superior in every aspect of passenger comfort. The 777 has a slightly wider fuselage. That extra space can matter, especially in economy class, where airlines decide how to divide the cabin among seats, aisles, and sidewalls. Seat width depends heavily on the carrier's layout, as the 777 can be configured with nine seats across in economy or with ten seats across. The same principle applies to the A350, where a common ten-abreast economy arrangement can reduce individual seat width. Overall, aircraft dimensions therefore do not tell passengers exactly how much personal space they will receive.

Singapore Airlines provides a useful illustration of how the comparison changes by cabin. Its 777-300ER economy layout can offer wider seats than its A350-900, making the larger Boeing a potentially better choice for travelers who prioritize shoulder room. Yet the A350 may be preferable for passengers who value a lower cabin altitude, a more modern acoustic environment, and potentially less dryness on long sectors. The airline's actual seat map and aircraft assignment matter more than a simple manufacturer-versus-manufacturer ranking.

Premium cabins introduce further variables. Seat design, privacy, bedding, service standards, and the ability to lie flat can have a greater effect on rest than small differences in cabin pressure or noise. A well-designed 777 business-class seat may be more comfortable than a poorly configured A350 cabin, while a spacious A350 suite can make the journey feel substantially easier. Passengers should compare the specific airline product rather than assuming that the aircraft model alone determines comfort.

The aircraft provides the physical environment, but airlines determine many of the conditions passengers experience within it. Cabin temperature, lighting schedules, meal timing, service interruptions, seating density, and crew procedures can all influence rest. An A350 operated with bright cabin lighting during intended sleep hours or frequent service interruptions may offer less restorative rest than a carefully managed 777 flight. Passenger circumstances, including sleep before travel, departure time, stress, and seat location, also shape how travelers feel on arrival. Someone seated beside a busy galley may experience more disruption than a passenger in a quieter window seat.

For travelers choosing between comparable flights, the A350 may provide a more favorable environment for a long journey. However, aircraft substitutions and differences in airline configurations make it worth checking the scheduled aircraft and seat map before departure. It is also necessary to distinguish general travel fatigue from jet lag, which occurs when the body's internal clock falls out of alignment with local time after crossing time zones.

The A350's advantage over older 777s may also become less pronounced as Boeing introduces the 777X. The forthcoming aircraft family is being designed with a quieter cabin, improved humidity, cleaner air, and other passenger-focused upgrades. However, Boeing's often-cited 6,000-foot cabin altitude figure currently applies to its BBJ 777X business-jet offering at maximum cruise altitude. It should not automatically be treated as the confirmed specification for every future commercial 777X configuration.

Ultimately, passengers may arrive feeling less tired after flying on an A350 than on an older 777, particularly when flights are similar in duration, schedule, and cabin class. The strongest evidence concerns cabin altitude: controlled research links exposure to 7,000- and 8,000-foot cabin conditions with greater reported discomfort over time. The A350's 6,000-foot cabin target gives it a credible physiological advantage, while improved humidity and noise control can further enhance the experience. However, no evidence establishes a universal fatigue difference between the two aircraft. As the 777X introduces newer cabin technologies, the comparison will increasingly depend on aircraft generation and airline execution. Manufacturers will need to make cabin comfort more consistent if they want passengers to experience a reliably better arrival.

'Zlatan Ibrahimovic Ended My England Career in 10 Minutes - I Never Played Again'

📅 Nov 08, 2026  |  Category: Sports  |  Read Source →

While plenty of attention is devoted to Zlatan Ibrahimovic's character and his larger-than-life personality, his ego wouldn't have been nearly as effective if he hadn't also been one of the best players on the planet during the prime of his career. The Swede was a phenomenon in front of goal, ranking among the greatest strikers in football history, and wherever he played, he rarely struggled to find the back of the net.

Ibrahimovic represented some of the biggest teams in world football throughout his career and ranks among the most decorated footballers of all time when it comes to trophies won. He played for Barcelona, Inter Milan, AC Milan, Paris Saint-Germain and Manchester United, alongside several other notable clubs. His imposing frame was matched by remarkable agility and speed, meaning there were few defenders in the beautiful game capable of keeping him quiet for an entire match. Having retired in 2023, the striker finished with 949 appearances for club and country, scoring 558 goals in the process.

Facing the superstar during his prime could have serious consequences for a player's career too, as one England centre-back discovered when his international career was effectively brought to an end after going up against Ibrahimovic.

Zlatan Ibrahimovic says his former Manchester United teammate deserves credit for Cristiano Ronaldo’s success at Old Trafford.

After several seasons impressing for Stoke City in the Premier League, Ryan Shawcross received his first-ever call-up to the England team in 2012. Supporters had been pushing for the defender to get an opportunity with the national side for some time and he was finally handed his chance, but his debut came against Sweden. He was tasked with trying to deal with Ibrahimovic and, by his own admission, things didn't go according to plan. In fact, the incident turned him into a source of jokes within his family. Via quotes shared by the Telegraph, Shawcross said:

"I’m the butt of the joke in my family, just because the 10 minutes I had in international football was ruined by one man. They are consistently saying that Zlatan ruined my career! I had a chance and I don’t think many other defenders have stopped him. I was given 10 minutes to show what I could do and probably didn’t provide a good enough performance. I was against one of the best strikers in the world who was on a hot streak at the time and these things happen."

The centre-back was able to look back on the episode with good humour at the time, but he never earned another England appearance for the rest of his career. That was unfortunate, considering he enjoyed a strong spell in the Premier League and had arguably done enough to deserve another opportunity.

Zlatan Ibrahimovic previously had tattoos on his body that are no longer there - here's the story behind it.

He never managed to join one of England's biggest clubs, but for much of his career, Shawcross was a top-level defender who held his own against some of the Premier League's finest strikers. After progressing through the Manchester United academy, he spent time on loan with Royal Antwerp and Stoke before eventually making his move to the latter permanent. The Potters were playing in the Championship at the time, but they soon secured promotion to the top flight with Shawcross at the centre of their defence.

From there, he helped Stoke establish themselves as a midtable Premier League side, with the club remaining in the top flight for 10 seasons before eventually being relegated to the Championship. Throughout his Premier League spell, Shawcross was highly impressive and attracted interest from several clubs over the years. No move ultimately materialised, however, and he stayed with Stoke for 13 years.

Across that period, the centre-back made 453 appearances for the Potters, including 317 in the top flight. He was a hugely important servant to the club, but departed in 2021 to join Inter Miami. His stay there lasted just one season before he retired, and he never represented England again. It's fair to say that Ibrahimovic may have played a role in that.

Man City Signed the ‘English Messi’ - He Made 3 Appearances in 6 Years

📅 Nov 08, 2026  |  Category: Sports  |  Read Source →

Eleven years after being touted as the 'English Lionel Messi' and subsequently being signed by Manchester City for £12 million, Patrick Roberts is plying his trade in the Championship for Birmingham City.

Born in Kingston upon Thames in south-west London, Roberts joined Fulham's academy at the age of 13. After making his debut for the Cottagers at 17, City opted to sign the wide man for £12 million in the summer of 2015.

He went on to make three appearances for the Citizens' first team, before a succession of underwhelming loan spells saw him sold to Sunderland in 2022. After four years on Wearside, Roberts swapped the Premier League for the second tier again, joining Birmingham permanently in the summer.

He's now an important figure in Chris Davies' team, who are vying for promotion back to the big time. From being tipped to lead City to major honours to being compared to Messi, it all could have been so different for Roberts.

After making waves in Fulham's academy, Felix Magath called Roberts up to a matchday squad for a Premier League fixture in March 2014, when the winger was just 17. He'd just signed his first professional contract, and would go on to make his senior debut a week later against the club he'd eventually sign for, Manchester City.

Introduced as a 55th-minute substitute against the north-west outfit, Magath showered the then teenager with praise after this surprise inclusion, describing the player as an 'extraordinary talent' (via the Mirror).

Naturally, these complimentary words sparked excitement amongst Fulham fans about the level of talent they might have on their hands.

Roberts would go on to make 17 appearances for the West Londoners in the Championship in the following season. Despite failing to score in any of these outings, City bought into the hype around the starlet, forking out £12 million to bring him to the Etihad in the summer of 2015.

It was at this point that Roberts' nickname in the Fulham academy became common knowledge. His peers in the youth ranks at Craven Cottage had called him 'Mini Messi', which led to many branding him the 'English Messi' when he signed for the Citizens (via the Mirror).

As well as being tipped for greatness by Messi, Roberts himself referenced his likeness to the Argentine, citing the legendary playmaker as his idol in February 2016 (via PlanetFootball).

"It’s a conscious thing for me to be like Messi," he said.

"I’ve watched him for years and he’s my idol. You try to copy what he does, and it also comes naturally to me as well. Because I’m left-footed, I tend to move the same way as he does. In games, I try to replicate what he does. I’ve studied him on YouTube and when La Liga is on I watch that all the time. My view is that you watch the best to learn from the best."

It's fair to say that Roberts didn't live up to this tag. After spending six months on the periphery of Manuel Pellegrini's squad, making just three appearances for the Premier League giants, he was shipped out to Celtic on loan.

This sparked a series of temporary stints, which lasted seven years and included stops at Girona, Norwich City, Middlesbrough, Derby County and Troyes, before he finally parted ways with City in 2022.

While Roberts hasn't lived up to the potential he once promised, it would be unfair to dismiss his career as a failure. Most players who are compared to some of the game's greatest players when they are young don't get close to replicating their idol's accomplishments, and the ex-City prodigy has certainly achieved a lot more than most.

Finally settling at Sunderland in 2022, after enjoying an impressive loan spell with the Black Cats in the back half of the 2021/22 campaign, Roberts shone for the north-east club for several years in the Championship.

Lamine Yamal may have won, but some former Ballon d'Or winners didn't even include him in their top three.

He made 147 appearances on Wearside, playing a key role in their promotion charge in the 2024/25 season. The former wonderkid was then granted a solitary Premier League appearance for Regis Le Bris' side before making the switch to Birmingham.

Since joining the Blues, he's scored four goals and established himself as a core member in an improving team that is now looking to return to the top flight for the first time in 15 years.

This Semiconductor ETF Just Had Its Worst Day Since March 2020

📅 Nov 08, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

The drop hastened the iShares Semiconductor ETF's slide into a bear market.

Technology stocks were drubbed on Thursday, Nov. 5, as investors priced in the impact of Tuesday's midterm election results.

Amid a blue tsunami that saw Democrats take control of both chambers of Congress and a host of gubernatorial victories, fears are rising that data center construction could stall and artificial intelligence (AI) regulations could enter the spotlight. Semiconductor stocks are epicenters of the calamity.

The iShares Semiconductor ETF just posted its worst intraday slide in over six years. Image source: Getty Images.

Just look at the iShares Semiconductor ETF (SOXX +3.51%). The second-largest exchange-traded fund (ETF) in the semiconductor category slid 12.1% on Thursday, notching its worst intraday decline since March 2020, when stocks slumped into a bear market due to the coronavirus pandemic. Let's see if this ETF's weakness is a warning sign or a buy-on-the-dip opportunity.

Unfortunately, the iShares ETF's Nov. 5 slide isn't a one-off event. It comes on the heels of an 8.2% drop on Oct. 30. That is to say, in the span of about a week, this ETF has had its fifth- and seventh-worst intraday showings ever, and that's saying something, because the fund is more than 25 years old. The fund is now down 30% over the past month and trades nearly 38% below its 52-week high. In short, it's in a bear market.

Yes, election results make for an easy scapegoat for chip stocks' recent misfortunes, but there are also some stock-specific issues hindering this ETF. For example, Qualcomm (QCOM +5.53%), the fund's eighth-largest holding, tumbled on Thursday after reporting a 26% decline in fiscal fourth-quarter smartphone chip sales.

Micron Technology (MU +5.12%) is another offender hailing from this ETF's roster. That stock, the iShares fund's fourth-largest component, plunged 17% on Thursday amid fears that memory chip shortages could pinch the broader tech sector and that, if data centers stall, demand for memory chips will slump. Memory chip demand is pertinent to the iShares ETF because the fund holds several other memory names in addition to Micron.

Add in election results that some experts say represent a "total repudiation" of AI, and these are perilous times for semiconductor stocks and related ETFs.

There's no denying that "bloodbath" is an accurate description of the week for semiconductor stocks, but in turbulent times, perspective is most valuable. On that note, investors interested in kicking in the tires on the iShares ETF or individual chip names may be heartened to know that it's not uncommon for markets to immediately overreact to election results before cooler heads prevail over the subsequent weeks and months.

Second, and admittedly this is a wild card, anti-AI/data center stances make for good campaign trail fodder, but post-election reality is different for politicians. That reality includes the burning desire (in most cases) to win the next election, and railing against cash-rich tech companies that can fund opposition in primary or general elections may not be the ticket to future political victory for this year's winners.

So there may be an opportunity with chip stocks and ETFs, and the silver lining is that prospective buyers acting in the near term will benefit from better pricing than was available just a few weeks ago.

TL;DR: Circle says it froze roughly $1.1 billion in USDC connected with the reported Binance exploit while working with law enforcement. The stablecoin issuer stresses that a wallet freeze does not decide asset ownership or whether funds should ultimately be forfeited.

The response to the enormous reported Binance breach has exposed a difficult but essential distinction in digital finance: tracking money is not the same thing as freezing it, and freezing it is not the same thing as recovering it for a victim.

In a November 5 statement, Circle said it had frozen approximately $1.1 billion in USDC associated with the incident, in coordination with law enforcement. It described an attack involving more than $6 billion in digital assets, while emphasising that the investigation and identification of relevant funds remain ongoing.

USDC is issued by a company that operates an administratively controlled token system. Under certain legal processes, the issuer can freeze specified addresses so tokens cannot move in or out. Bitcoin, by contrast, does not have an issuer with an equivalent global address-freezing switch. That difference can matter enormously in the first minutes after an attack.

But it also raises governance questions. Who is authorised to request a freeze? What evidence is required? How quickly can an incorrect restriction be lifted? Circle says it acts on valid legal process and does not decide for itself whether a crime occurred, whether a wallet’s owner committed an offence or who is entitled to the assets. Those questions belong to law enforcement and the courts.

This makes it important not to count frozen funds as already returned to customers. A frozen balance may remain subject to lengthy investigations, ownership disputes and judicial decisions. The amount is an intervention figure, not a completed reimbursement total.

Modern crypto breaches move across bridges, exchanges, token pairs and custody services. Attackers may split assets among many addresses and convert one token into another to make intervention harder. Defenders need analytics capable of tracing flows across those boundaries, along with responsive institutions that can take action where legal powers exist.

An earlier NewsBTC report on Aave freezing new tBTC activity illustrated how different controls can be used to reduce exposure within a protocol. Circle’s move is not the same kind of action, but both show why the ability to intervene is a design choice with trade-offs.

For investigators, the key issue is coordination. An address identified in one jurisdiction may be connected to a stablecoin issuer or exchange in another. Delays can give attackers more time to swap tokens and fragment holdings. Moving quickly is therefore useful, but speed cannot replace careful identification and due process.

The full scope of the incident, ultimate recoveries and allocation of any frozen funds will require additional verified disclosures. Blockchain traces alone can also create false impressions when commentators assign an address to a person without sufficiently strong evidence. Circle’s account is an issuer’s report of its own intervention, not an independently completed forensic audit of every transfer.

The development offers a lesson beyond this incident. Dollar stablecoins can provide rapid payment settlement and targeted interdiction, yet their compliance controls concentrate important operational powers in issuers. Users, institutions and regulators need to understand both sides of that bargain.

Patrick Stewart's '90s Fantasy Movie With Neve Campbell Is Streaming For Free

📅 Nov 08, 2026  |  Category: Entertainment  |  Read Source →

An adaptation of Oscar Wilde's 1887 short story, Sydney Macartney's "The Canterville Ghost" aired on ABC on January 27, 1996, and, perhaps surprisingly, featured notable central roles for both the late legend Sir Patrick Stewart and Neve Campbell. Indeed, 1996 was a banner year for both actors, with Stewart appearing in the film "Star Trek: First Contact" and Campbell headlining two of the best teen movies of the 1990s over the same 12-month period (namely, "The Craft" and "Scream"). Stewart, of course, was already riding high professionally by that point. But for Campbell, this was the final vault before proper stardom following her breakout role on the hit series "Party of Five."

It should be noted that Wilde's original story is a light tale about a haunted mansion, and Macartney's TV movie — which is currently streaming for free on platforms like Tubi and Prime Video — affects a similarly lighthearted tone. Wilde's source material concerns the Otises, a family of two parents and three children who move into Canterville Castle, despite warnings that it is haunted. Sure enough, its ghost tries to frighten them off, but the Otises react with mere annoyance. For example, when Mrs. Otis spots a bloodstain on the carpet, she dryly states, "I don't at all care for blood-stains in a sitting-room."

Macartney's adaptation transposes the action to the modern day. In the movie, the Otises inevitably have to deal with the specter of Sir Simon de Canterville, played by Stewart. Campbell, meanwhile, plays the young Virginia Otis, and the story is told largely from her perspective. As for Sir Simon, he eventually reveals the trauma that left him in his ghostly state, which has to do with his beloved dead wife, Eleanor.

The TV movie version of "The Canterville Ghost" adds a few fun flourishes to the story. The Otis family patriarch, Hiram, is played by Edward Wiley, and there comes a scene where Virginia and the Ghost, having begun communicating, have to convince Hiram that ghosts are real. In a nod to Sir Patrick Stewart's past with the Royal Shakespeare Company, Virginia and the Ghost get Hiram's attention by staging a scene from "Hamlet" wherein Sir Simon plays the Ghost of Hamlet's Father. Fittingly enough, Stewart would later portray both the Ghost of Hamlet's Father and Claudius in a celebrated 2008 BBC production of the play opposite David Tennant.

Initially, as mentioned, Sir Simon is determined to scare away the Otis family to remain in his sad, ghostly state. Soon after moving in, however, Virginia finds a secret passageway inside the Canterville Castle leading to a chamber where Sir Simon hangs out during the daylight hours. Soon, she and her brothers befriend Sir Simon, and we learn that, despite his intense demeanor, he isn't so terrible or scary. (Virginia's brothers even startle Sir Simon with a fake ghost puppet they construct themselves.) At the same time, Virginia develops a crush on Francis, the Duke of Cheshire (Daniel Betts), a local nobleman who plays a larger role here than he does in Oscar Wilde's original story.

The one Starlink perk that gives it an edge over traditional internet

📅 Nov 08, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Why satellite internet has a unique advantage over terrestrial providers.

It's debatable as to whether Starlink markets its services properly. Anyone who's seen some of the company's ads probably understands the basics, namely that, regardless of which Starlink setup you think best suits you, you're getting the internet via a satellite dish instead of a cable or phone line. Also, if you choose the right plan, like one of the wildly different $55 plans, you can get internet access virtually anywhere in the world. However, until you dig a little deeper, many details remain mysterious, such as important specs like expected speed.

One of those gaps is why you might want satellite internet when you're not roaming or otherwise isolated. In a country like the US, UK or Canada, it's rare to find a community that doesn't have at least some basic landline option. Given that Starlink can get quite expensive with its equipment costs, there has to be serious incentive to sign up.

For some consumers, there is, and it's an adaptability that traditional internet lacks. It's baked into the nature of Starlink, and it could save your bacon if you value resiliency over raw speed. There are some important exceptions, which we'll get into.

Starlink's network is based around clusters of small satellites inserted into low Earth orbit (LEO). As of October 2026, there were over 11,000 working satellites, flying at altitudes ranging from about 205 to 311 miles (330 to nearly 500 kilometers). SpaceX continues to launch more, whether to expand the network or replace malfunctioning units. Essentially, there should always be a cluster overhead at any given time.

The most obvious advantage of this is that whereas traditional internet connections can be knocked offline for hours by a single cable or server going down, it's rare for Starlink to suffer a similar fate. If one satellite fails, another in the same cluster should be able to pick up the slack. Even sightline obstacles aren't necessarily an issue, since the cluster approach means there's almost always a satellite with a viable angle as long as your dish is positioned properly.

As mentioned, this coverage is only slated to get better. SpaceX recently filed an application with the US Federal Communications Commission to operate up to 100,000 of its Gen3 satellites, following up on a recent approval to expand Gen2 numbers as high as 15,000. If you can afford to install and maintain a dish, your internet access could hypothetically become bulletproof, short of power outages or other rare disruptions.

On paper, Starlink dishes should be extremely resilient. Current-generation models can melt snow and endure temperatures from -40 to 110 degrees Fahrenheit (-40 to 43 degrees Celsius). Properly mounted, they can supposedly withstand heavy rain and winds in excess of 99 miles per hour (about 160 kilometers per hour).

In practice, things don't always work perfectly. Snow takes time to melt, and there are regular, brief disruptions while your dish switches from one satellite to another. The possibility of prolonged outages remains, whether because of extreme weather, some systemic problem on Starlink's end or issues with the rest of the internet. It hardly matters how many satellites are in the sky if a particular trunk of the internet is having trouble with its local ground-based infrastructure.

Some prospective users may want to actively avoid Starlink altogether. Apart from the many controversies involving founder Elon Musk, those momentary signal blips can be terrible for real-time activities like online gaming. In a shooter like Fortnite or Counter-Strike, a split second can mean the difference between landing a headshot or taking one.

You open your phone to check the time or a quick message. The next thing you know, an hour has passed and you’ve scrolled through endless celebrity drama, cat videos, awful news stories, influencer rants, and whatever else the algorithm decided to throw at you.

Even though you probably don’t want to keep wasting your time and energy on this mind-numbing content, you do it again the next day.

Doomscrolling, the habit of spending excessive amounts of time consuming content on social media, has become incredibly widespread. A survey from last year found that 64% of Americans say they doomscroll.

Researchers have warned that doomscrolling can negatively affect several aspects of your well-being, including your mental health and attention span. Spending long periods scrolling can lead to brain fatigue, difficulty focusing, and disrupted sleep. And if a lot of the content you’re consuming is negative or stressful, it can leave you feeling disheartened, anxious, and emotionally drained.

It’s hard to break the cycle of doomscrolling, but there are plenty of apps that provide content that’s engaging and productive.

Of course, you could always read a book or go for a walk (we have a guide on how to stop doomscrolling), but this list is for when you have a few spare minutes and want something to do on your phone that isn’t endless scrolling.

Finch is a self-care pet app that’s designed to help you build healthy habits. You care for a cute virtual pet by completing small daily goals, such as drinking enough water, going on a walk, and practicing mindfulness. You can write about your day, complete a breathing exercise, or send good vibes to a friend by adding them to the app.

The more self-care you do, the more your pet thrives. The app offers a fun way to trade mindless scrolling for small moments of self-care.

Finch is available for free on iOS and Android.

Libby is a free reading app that connects you to your local public library’s digital collection of ebooks, audiobooks, and magazines. All you need is a library card to borrow thousands of titles, which makes it easy to turn screen time into something productive.

You can explore a new book during a commute or while waiting at a doctor’s office, turning moments when your fingers might otherwise itch to scroll into opportunities to read.

Libby is available for free on iOS and Android.

If you want to take a break from doomscrolling and try something creative, Dudel Draw might be a good app for you. Each day, the app gives you a random shape that you turn into a drawing. These daily shapes vary from basic geometrical forms to more complex and abstract designs.

You can also get your friends in on the creativity with some fun competition by comparing your different creations, which could provide a nice break from sending each other endless TikToks or Instagram Reels.

If you still want to feel connected to the world without scrolling through social media, you can check out Radio Garden. The app lets you listen to over 25,000 live radio stations from across the globe.

Once you open the app, you’ll see green dots marking cities and towns. Tap any dot to listen to radio stations broadcasting from that location. You can add favorites or search for radio stations, countries, and places.

Radio Garden is free, but it also offers an ad-free premium plan for $2.99 per month. The app is available on both iOS and Android.

Elevate is an app designed to help improve focus, memory, reading, math skills, and other cognitive abilities. It includes over 40 games to train different abilities you use in everyday life, from reading faster to comparing prices more efficiently.

You can track training streaks and compare your mind’s performance over time.

The app offers a free version with access to three games per day, or a yearly subscription of $39.99 for unlimited access. It’s available on both iOS and Android.

Vocabulary is an app that helps you learn new words every day. You can pick your difficulty level and choose categories that interest you, like emotions, the human body, business, and more. Each word comes with a definition, example sentences, and a guide on how to pronounce it.

The app also includes mini-games to help you review the words you’ve learned. You can set a goal of how many words you want to learn each week and create a regular learning routine.

Vocabulary offers a free trial, and then costs $4.99 per month or $59.99 per year. It’s available on iOS and Android.

If you’re a geography nerd looking to test your knowledge or just want to improve your geography skills, Seterra is perfect for you. The app features over 300 different games to test your map skills. You can test your knowledge of world flags; discover oceans, seas, and rivers; and explore mountain ranges and volcanoes across the globe.

Seterra lets you track your progress across categories and see leaderboards for top scorers for each game.

The app is free and available on both iOS and Android.

The app costs $5.99 per month for unlimited access and archives, but some games like Wordle, Strands, and the Mini Crossword are available to play for free.

NYT Games is available on both iOS and Android.

The app uses visually engaging mini‑games to help you build vocabulary and common phrases in more than 45 languages, with bite‑sized lessons designed to be completed in about five minutes a day.

Drops is designed for both beginners and experts who want to grow their foreign language vocabulary.

The free version of the app offers five-minute lessons per day. You can get unlimited access and premium features for $11.99 per month or $79.99 per year. The app is available on both iOS and Android.
