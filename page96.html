West Ham took too long to sack Nuno and now must trust Parker to do what he does best | Jacob Steinberg

📅 Nov 04, 2026  |  Category: Business  |  Read Source →

Forget speculation over how a Parker-led side would fare in the Premier League and let the man who has taken three clubs up do his work again

It has taken West Ham six months to come to their senses and sack Nuno Espírito Santo, but at least the decision has arrived early enough to revive their hopes of automatic promotion.

Dithering was no longer an option after the dismal performance against West Brom last Saturday.

West Ham, eighth in the Championship and four points off the top two, had to act after a second consecutive home defeat. They are in danger of slipping into a battle for a playoff spot and needed to move on from Nuno after just over a year of his confused, passive football.

Nuno did a terrible job after replacing Graham Potter as manager in September 2025. The 52-year-old had 33 games to save West Ham from relegation and failed. He has the most expensive squad in the Championship but his side have not lived up to their billing as title favourites and their stuttering form has exposed the folly of not sacking Nuno at the end of last season.

David Sullivan, then the club’s largest shareholder, wanted a change. But Daniel Kretinsky favoured continuity and looked to capitalise on the shifting power dynamics in the boardroom. Karren Brady had stepped down as vice-chair in April – Karim Virani replaced her in an interim role – and there was further upheaval when Sullivan resigned as joint-chair and a director of the club to fight what he described as “false allegations” regarding his personal conduct.

Sullivan bears most culpability for West Ham’s relegation. He still owns a 40% stake but is no longer on the board and West Ham are better off without him dictating key decisions. Equally, though, where Sullivan was not mistaken was in wanting Nuno gone. Kretinsky got his first big call wrong. At least the Czech billionaire has given Nuno’s replacement time to get the team back on track. The task falls to Scott Parker, who has been on the market since leaving Burnley last April. Parker is regarded as a “safe” appointment and has been on West Ham’s radar for a while.

Some wanted the former England midfielder in the summer and his name was mentioned again when West Ham failed to win their first three league games. Patience was wearing thin. A subsequent run of four wins in a row swiftly proved a false dawn.

West Ham have not had the right mindset. They have two wins from their past six games and have often started playing only after going a goal down. A 6-0 win against Wrexham was a highlight but victory at Lincoln by the same scoreline came with the caveat that the Imps received two red cards before half-time.

There was no identity under Nuno. West Ham are unbeaten on the road and won 1-0 at the then table-topping Swansea last month but it has been hard to tell Nuno broke the Football League transfer record with the £22m signing of the Belgium midfielder Arne Engels from Celtic. Even Jarrod Bowen, who was convinced to sign a new deal in the summer, has been off colour. Nuno’s tactics mean the winger, West Ham’s top scorer, is often crowded out.

West Ham were lopsided against West Brom. Enough was enough. Kretinsky, who is not at every game, was unimpressed when he attended West Ham’s 3-2 defeat by Southampton a week earlier. He pays attention to the views of Bowen, Tomas Soucek and Mark Noble, the technical director and former club captain.

Nuno, whose substitutions were almost as weird as his starting lineups, had lost the players. The mood darkened after the West Brom game. West Ham were easy to play through because of Nuno’s reluctance to use a holding midfielder, an issue exacerbated by his refusal to use Edson Álvarez or Soungoutou Magassa after both sought moves during the summer.

Magassa’s exile has caused disquiet. Not only is a young asset being devalued, a fragile defence is going without enough protection. West Ham have a lot of No 8s but they need a No 6. It is an area for Parker to address. He will also need to work out whether to stick with Mads Hermansen in goal, and the jury is out on summer signings.

One source questions whether West Ham targeted players equipped to handle the intensity of the Championship. Divine Mukasa, the 19-year-old midfielder, is talented but inconsistent. Manor Solomon and Edvin Austbø have flattered to deceive on the left wing. Joël Veltman, the 34-year-old Dutch right-back, has had fitness issues. Adama Boiro, a 24-year-old left‑back on loan from Athletic Bilbao, has not been seen since a cameo against Derby in early September. The three strikers signed by Nuno across two windows have toiled; Pablo Felipe, Taty Castellanos and Joël Piroe have five league goals between them this season.

Nils Koppen, the head of recruitment appointed in the summer, will have to be busy in January, but Parker will hope to have made his impact before the window opens. He is not known for expansive football but he is proven at this level. Parker won promotion with Bournemouth, Burnley and Fulham. The 46-year-old’s track record is clear and it helps that he has an association with West Ham.

Parker, whose all-action playing style made him a fan favourite, knows how difficult it can be in east London. He played in some pretty bad West Ham sides, experienced relegation in 2011 and arrives with the club in a tricky spot again.

It is not the moment to start worrying about Parker’s questionable record as a Premier League manager. What do West Ham do if they go up? Do they stick with Parker? Do they make an instant change? Well, how about they get promoted first? The rest can wait.

Pokémon Officially Gives Charizard A New Look For 30th Anniversary

📅 Nov 04, 2026  |  Category: Other  |  Read Source →

Pokémon has been celebrating its 30th anniversary all year long, and that shows no signs of stopping anytime soon. Even this month alone, there's a mysterious countdown to something on November 30. Previous months have seen the release of the 30th Celebration set in the Pokémon TCG, an Adidas collaboration, and the releases of FireRed and LeafGreen for Nintendo Switch and Switch 2. For most franchises, that might be it, but for Pokémon, that's barely scratching the surface.

And thanks to these collaborations, we're getting to see artists and designers put their own unique spins on beloved Pokémon. From plushes and shirts, to shoes and jewelry, the Pokémon of Gen 1 have been featured on a lot of merchandise throughout 2026. We've gotten Gengar gaming chairs, Mewtwo double-banded rings, and skateboard decks with fan-favorites like Eevee, Pikachu, and Gyarados.

As one of the most famous Pokémon from the Gen 1 era, Charizard has also gotten its fair share of merchandise for the 30th anniversary of the series, including its very own Adidas sneakers and a plush designed by Sean Wotherspoon. The ever-popular Fire-type was also featured on 30th Celebration cards, and is a core part of the new McDonald's Happy Meals.

Now, Charizard is getting another new look thanks to the "Evolve to Charizard Ring" by Enso. The ring features Charmander, Charmeleon, and Charizard, which are etched into the ring, but Charizard is the main star. The ring itself is made of medical-grade silicone, and it's currently up for sale in a number of adult sizes.

Available on the official Pokémon Center storefront, the "Evolve To Charizard Ring" by Enso is priced at $44.99. This is a ring for adults, with a sizing chart available; however, Size 13 is currently sold out. The other sizes are still available at the time of this writing. If you're a fan of the Gen 1 Fire-type starter evolutions, or know someone who is, then this could make a great gift ahead for the upcoming holiday season.

In addition to the Charizard ring, there's one featuring Gengar (also for $44.99), as well as a double-banded premium ring for Mewtwo for $129.99. The three rings from Enso are just some of the latest merchandise released to coincide with Pokémon's 30th anniversary. With only a couple of months left in 2026, these might also be some of the final items of the year.

For the most part, the 30th anniversary has been focused on the Gen 1 games. That makes sense. After all, those are the games that are actually turning 30 this year. That has meant older Pokémon have gotten the chance to shine, but it has also put an increasing spotlight on longtime favorites like Charizard, Pikachu, Gengar, Eevee, and Pikachu.

With the holidays approaching, Pokémon has also launched a holiday collection, where you can expect to find additional merchandise starring some of these fan-favorites from the Kanto region. In the meantime, it's been great to see all of these new designs released for Pokémon that many fans have adored for three decades now.

New Louie Barry connection should seriously excite Sheffield Wednesday fans

📅 Nov 04, 2026  |  Category: Sports  |  Read Source →

A new era is underway at Sheffield Wednesday following the appointment of Dave Challinor as the club's new manager.

After a mixed start to life back in League One, Wednesday parted company with Henrik Pedersen last month, and it has not taken long for the South Yorkshire outfit to find his replacement.

It has been a frustrating few weeks for Wednesday on the pitch, with back-to-back away defeats at Notts County and Doncaster Rovers spelling the end of Pedersen's tenure before interim manager Matt Hamshaw oversaw consecutive home draws against Cambridge United and Blackpool.

However, while it was a disappointing afternoon for the Owls as they were held to a 1-1 draw by struggling Blackpool on Sunday, the headlines were instead dominated by the appointment of Challinor, who was confirmed as the club's new manager on the morning of the game.

Given his links to Wednesday's sporting director Simon Wilson, who he worked with previously at Stockport County, it had long been rumoured that Challinor would be taking over at Hillsborough at some stage following his departure from Edgeley Park in June.

Challinor spent almost five years in charge of the Hatters, during which time he led them from the National League to League One, and he missed out on promotion to the Championship in what proved to be his final game as his side were beaten 4-1 by Bolton Wanderers in the play-off final at Wembley.

The 51-year-old, who has also had spells with the likes of AFC Fylde and Hartlepool United, holds the remarkable record of finishing in the play-offs or higher in each of his 15 full seasons as a manager, and he arrives with Wednesday sitting ninth in the League One table, just two points from the top six.

It is fair to say that Challinor's appointment has been well-received by Wednesday fans, with many feeling that the seven-time promotion winner is the right man to lead the club back to the Championship.

Challinor's impressive track record will no doubt equally excite the Owls dressing room as they prepare to begin work under their new manager, but one player in particular may be thrilled about the change in the dugout.

Louie Barry worked under Challinor on three separate occasions at Stockport during his loan spells from Aston Villa, and the forward produced the best form of his career across his various stints at Edgeley Park.

Barry linked up with Challinor for the first time in the 2023-24 season as he joined the Hatters on a season-long loan, and he scored nine goals and provided four assists in 22 appearances to help the club to the League Two title.

After the success of his first loan, Barry returned to Stockport in the summer of 2024 ahead of their return to League One, and he took his form to another level in the third tier, netting 16 goals and registering three assists in 24 games before being recalled by Villa in January.

The Hatters secured a third loan deal for Barry in January, and while injury limited him to just 10 appearances in the second half of last season, he still managed to score four goals, including netting in the second leg of the play-off semi-final victory over Stevenage.

Barry made a promising start to his Wednesday career after his permanent move from Aston Villa this summer, scoring three goals and providing one assist in his first six appearances, but he was ruled out for three months after sustaining an ankle ligament injury in early September.

As he continues to make good progress in his recovery, it has been suggested that Barry could make his return earlier than expected after the November international break, meaning Challinor may not have to wait too long to have the 23-year-old at his disposal, which is a mouthwatering prospect for Owls supporters.

While Barry has started to debunk the theory that he cannot deliver outside of Stockport since his arrival at Wednesday, he did struggle to hit the same heights in his other loans away from Aston Villa, failing to register a single goal contribution in a combined total of 13 appearances during injury-hit spells with Hull City and Sheffield United in the Championship in 2025.

The Owls have won just two of their nine league games during Barry's absence, and they have only scored 11 goals in that time, with five of those coming in the victory over AFC Wimbledon last month, so the forward has certainly been missed.

Is Dave Challinor the right man for Sheffield Wednesday or should the Owls have considered other options? Tell us in the comments below!

Such was Barry's influence in the early weeks of the season, there is every chance that Pedersen may still be in a job had he not lost his main creative spark, but Wednesday supporters will be confident that Challinor can unlock more from him than their former manager would have been able to.

If Challinor can help Barry to rediscover the explosive form he produced on a regular basis at Stockport, he could be celebrating an eighth career promotion with the Owls come the end of the season.

Last year, it seemed that some of the biggest brands had declared the death of the opulent, magical holiday advert. AI slop would do, they decided.

Coca-Cola's AI Christmas ad was one of the most controversial, while McDonald's pulled its festive effort due to the backlash. This year, it seems we're seeing a strong counter movement, with big brands making a point of their commitment to handcrafted art.

First, there was the glorious Prada Christmas ad, directed by former Disney animator John Pomeroy. Now Levi's has pulled out the stops for a very different animated gem, showing that it's not just luxury brands that see the value of human craftsmanship in advertising.

Levi's Let’s Get Fuzzy stop-motion holiday campaign was produced by the London-based production studio Blinkink . The animation begins as a play on horror movies, making it a fun segue from Halloween. A monster interrupts a group of friends' cosy festive retreat in a snow-covered cabin. But Fuzzyeti's discovery of festive jumpers makes him quickly succumb to the holiday spirit.

The medium of painstaking stop-motion animation was chosen to highlight Levi's heritage of craftsmanship. As shown in the making of video below, a six-person team spent more than 2,000 hours over six and a half weeks building and painting the sets, including three cabins at different scales, along with 400 miniature trees, and four faces for each character.

The clothes are miniature replicas of real Levi's products – a stylist was on hand to ensure accuracy. They were moved for every frame of the animation to make them feel life like, but requiring four hours of work of every second of animation.

A reference edit was made with actors and the directors themselves acting out the roles (including a specialist monster actor for Fuzzyyeti). This was then used as the guide for the animators.

Levi's has form in animation, from the psychedelic 2D short The Stranger in 1971 to Clayman with Shaggy’s Boombastic in claymation in 1995. Today, the decision to use such a laborious handcrafted process makes a bigger statement, creating added distinction and value amid so much AI-generated content.

Your local food truck might not worry about their ugly AI flyers if they can do the job for cheap. But for big brands, proof of work like the behind-the-scenes video above has become as important as the finished advert. Avoiding the embarrassment of an 'AI content' label isn't enough, they're taking steps to proactively signal that an advert was human made.

Hermès and Ralph Lauren are just two fashion brands that have been doing this by mentioning the people behind their ads in the captions on social media. Naming the creative team behind a campaign has become a signal of craftsmanship, and the implication is clear: human-made art is seen as a sign of a premium brand.

Yet, that’s exactly where the Slate Truck lands with its $24,950 starting price. For an all-electric pickup, I have to admit I’m impressed by the genuine real-world capability it delivers on paper: a rear-mounted motor pushing 181 horsepower and 195 lb-ft of torque, a 65 kWh battery pack, 1,500 pounds of payload capacity, NACS DC fast-charging compatibility, and an estimated 205 miles of range.

While those specs might look modest next to six-figure behemoths like the Rivian R1T, Chevrolet Silverado EV, and GMC Sierra EV, you can't ignore just how accessible that price point is. But after getting up close with the Slate Truck in person, a few details caught me completely off guard. Here’s what stood out the most.

Prior to seeing the Slate Truck in person, I already knew it was scaling things back with crank windows and manual seats. Even in 2026, I still come across a handful of cars with manual seats — though that’s usually reserved strictly for the passenger side, and it's rarer still to find both seats manual.

So while manual adjustment wasn’t a shock, I was taken aback to find that the side mirrors are entirely manual, too. I can live with crank windows and mechanical seat levers, but manual side mirrors? The Slate Truck I saw featured a limited-edition wrap created in collaboration with Smarties. Yes, the candy rolls you probably remember from childhood.

Compact pickups are already at a disadvantage due to their smaller footprint, and in a two-seater like the Slate Truck, cabin space is especially tight. That might not be an issue if the Slate Truck is strictly kept to quick local errands, but plenty of drivers will want to take it on longer road trips. Fighting driver fatigue starts with comfortable ergonomics, so the lack of recline here makes long stretches behind the wheel tough to manage.

I’ve seen deeper, longer beds on full-size pickups, but the Slate Truck’s usable cargo space rivals compact pickups I’ve tested, like the Hyundai Santa Cruz.

The biggest challenge the Slate Truck faces is convincing buyers the company will actually be around for the life of the vehicle. Given its ultra-aggressive pricing, I’m genuinely surprised the EV maker is offering such a generous 10-year, 110,000-mile battery and powertrain warranty.

TL;DR: The Solana Foundation has described Microscope, an open-source monitoring approach for teams managing on-chain programmes and infrastructure. An alert is not a transaction blocker.

Operational tooling rarely generates the excitement of a new token launch, yet it can determine whether institutions trust a network enough to hold meaningful value on it. Teams want evidence that they can detect unexpected actions before the problem expands. Monitoring is one part of that process, not a substitute for preventive controls.

The Solana Foundation has described Microscope, an open-source monitoring approach for teams managing on-chain programmes and infrastructure. The toolkit is designed to help operators inspect instructions, transactions and alerts while connecting observations to common monitoring workflows. It is an engineering tool, not an announcement that every Solana application now receives automatic protection against attacks. The dated original disclosure is the starting point for the figures and product scope discussed here.

In a complex blockchain application, important activity can be hidden within technical transaction instructions that a general-purpose dashboard does not explain well. Monitoring tools try to decode those events and route relevant signals to people who can respond. This is useful for treasury movements, contract administration and unusual operational behaviour, but good alerts depend on choosing the right thresholds and maintaining the integrations.

The wider lesson is that on-chain transparency is not the same thing as operational comprehension. Transaction data may be public while a business still struggles to identify why an event matters or who should investigate it. A self-hostable tool can give teams more control over their monitoring stack, provided they manage uptime, access and alert delivery responsibly.

There is a meaningful gap between a fast transaction and a complete financial service. Solana infrastructure can settle digital assets, but an actual payments or securities product must also manage eligibility, legal rights, local currencies and exceptions. The best reporting follows the full workflow rather than assuming the chain’s speed is the entire product.

For additional perspective, it is worth reading our existing reporting on Solana’s Samsung Wallet rollout and user test and the latest Solana developer and payments coverage. They explain related market structure and should not be mistaken for evidence that every new proposal is already live.

An alert is not a transaction blocker. Microscope does not automatically fix a vulnerable programme, reverse an executed transfer or eliminate the need for security audits. False alarms can consume attention, while incomplete alert definitions can miss harmful activity. A monitored environment still requires tested incident procedures and clear accountability.

Distribution can be an advantage for blockchain applications, but the user experience needs to justify a change in behaviour. Developers also need monitoring and controls to support systems that operate continuously. A credible launch is one that describes available features, supported participants and how success will be measured after the initial announcement.

Watch for adoption in real applications, public documentation and integration examples showing how teams respond to alerts. The most useful evidence would involve measured detection speed and fewer unresolved incidents, rather than the number of dashboards deployed. For Solana developers, monitoring belongs alongside code review and permission design as a basic operating requirement.
