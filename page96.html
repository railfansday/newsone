Coinbase Borrow is now available in New York, letting residents borrow against their crypto through Morpho on Base.

Crypto exchange operator Coinbase has expanded its onchain crypto loan product to New York residents, completing its rollout across all 50 U.S. states.

The launch follows approval from the New York State Department of Financial Services, a Coinbase spokesperson told The Block.

Asked when Coinbase applied and received approval, Jacob Frantz, head of Coinbase's revenue and investment products, said the company had been working with New York regulators since the product launched last year.

"We have been working closely with regulators in New York since launching Coinbase Borrow last year, so that New Yorkers can also access cash without having to sell the crypto assets they believe in," Frantz told The Block. He did not provide application or approval dates or details on the scope of the approval.

With Coinbase Borrow, eligible New York residents can borrow USDC against supported crypto held on Coinbase. The borrowing limits are up to $5 million against bitcoin, $1 million against ether and $100,000 against Solana, subject to eligibility and other applicable limits, the Coinbase spokesperson said.

Customers access Coinbase Borrow through Coinbase, with Morpho providing the onchain lending infrastructure on Base.

The product launched in January 2025 and has since originated more than $3.4 billion in loans. More than $1.7 billion remains outstanding, backed by over $3.2 billion in collateral, Morpho said.

Coinbase has added more collateral options since the launch, most recently tokenized gold via Paxos' Pax Gold (PAXG). It has also introduced fixed-rate loans through Morpho Midnight, allowing borrowers to agree on a rate and maturity before taking out a loan.

Those features are not all available in New York. At launch, customers in the state can borrow against BTC, ETH and SOL, the Coinbase spokesperson said.

Fixed-rate loans and other collateral options, including tokenized gold PAXG, Zcash's ZEC, Hyperliquid's HYPE, XRP and staked assets, are not yet available there, the spokesperson added.

Coinbase also offers a separate USDC lending product through Morpho, where users can earn returns by allocating their USDC across onchain lending markets. That product holds more than $500 million in deposits, according to Morpho, but is also unavailable in New York.

Asked about plans to bring those products to the state, the spokesperson said: "We're excited to continue partnering with regulators to bring better financial services to New Yorkers."

FAA pauses installation of new routers after ground stop

📅 2026-11-17  |  Category: Business  |  Read Source →

The Federal Aviation Administration said Tuesday it would pause the installation of new routers after problems with the technology led the agency to issue a rare nationwide ground stop on Friday.

Tens of thousands of flights were affected by the ground stop, which halted departing flights for more than four hours. It was the longest halt to U.S. flight departures since 9/11.

Transportation Secretary Sean Duffy said Sunday that an overloaded router installed in southern Arkansas as part of an upgrade to a system overseen by L3Harris Technologies appeared to overwhelm systems that feed radar and other data for air traffic control.

The Wall Street Journal first reported the news about the pause.

The FAA and Department of Transportation have said L3Harris was working to convert the communications network from copper wires to fiber optic.

Democratic members of the Senate Committee on Commerce, Science and Transportation this week asked the FAA and the Department of Transportation for clarity on what went wrong.

"Identifying the device that failed does not establish why it failed," the senators wrote in a statement. "Modernization cannot come at the expense of operational safety."

The agencies have said they are working to figure out the root cause of the issue and have reiterated that there is no indication of a cyberattack or outside threat.

The senators also asked for a briefing with the FAA, L3Harris and Juniper Networks, which is making the new devices, by Monday. They asked the companies to lay out why the operational systems lacked sufficient redundancy and answer other questions about what led to the outage.

CNBC has reached out to L3Harris and Juniper Networks for comment.

Duffy on Friday called for Congress to approve $50 billion in funding for upgrades to the aging U.S. air traffic control system, more than he has previously asked for. Democratic lawmakers pushed back on the request, citing unspent money from existing funding, which includes Congress' approval last year of $12.5 billion for airport and air traffic control improvements over five years.

Aging equipment and a shortage of air traffic controllers have for years angered airline executives, who have complained about the cost of resulting disruptions.

U.S. officially loses measles elimination status amid ongoing outbreaks

📅 2026-11-17  |  Category: Business  |  Read Source →

The U.S. on Tuesday lost its official designation as a measles-free nation, more than 25 years after it eliminated the highly contagious virus.

The Pan American Health Organization announced the decision, which came after an independent panel of eight scientific experts convened last week to consider the country's epidemiological evidence. The director of PAHO, the regional branch of the World Health Organization, made the final call that measles is endemic — or always present — in the U.S. following 12 months of continuous transmission.

In other words, measles is spreading regularly in the country again, with ongoing outbreaks sparking new ones.

"The commission concluded that endemic measles transmission has now been reestablished in Bolivia, Mexico and the United States, and recommended that their measles elimination status be withdrawn — a recommendation which I have accepted," Dr. Jarbas Barbosa, PAHO director, said during a news briefing on Tuesday.

It's a devastating step backward for American public health, as declining childhood vaccination rates and vaccine hesitancy fuel outbreaks across the country that have sickened thousands of people and led to a tragic string of preventable deaths. Among the deaths where vaccination status was reported, all were unvaccinated.

More than 5,400 measles cases had been reported nationwide in 2026 as of mid-November, marking the country's worst outbreak in more than three decades. States have confirmed 10 deaths associated with the disease so far in 2026: seven in Pennsylvania, one in New York and two in Wisconsin.

The commission found that out of 52 U.S. states and territories it tracked, 48 experienced measles outbreaks during the reporting period between January 2025 and January 2026, PAHO Assistant Director Dr. Rhonda Sealey-Thomas said during the briefing. But Barbosa said countries are already taking action to control outbreaks, such as strengthening vaccination, surveillance and outbreak response efforts.

The U.S. in particular has ramped up vaccination efforts to close immunity gaps, particularly in areas with low immunization coverage experiencing outbreaks, said Dr. Daniel Salas, executive manager of PAHO's Special Program for Comprehensive Immunization. He said PAHO expects the U.S. to present its corrective action plan in the coming weeks, outlining strategies to regain its designation.

In a Tuesday release, the Centers for Disease Control and Prevention said it will continue to ensure all communities have the resources to prevent and respond to outbreaks and is committed to working with states and other local partners to restore the U.S.'s measles elimination status.

CDC Director Dr. Erica Schwartz said in the release that the measles elimination designation "is not what protects Americans. Vaccination does." She said the agency has invested $100 million in state and local health departments to reach communities where transmission is occurring.

Last week, Schwartz told Reuters that the agency is going to be "doing the same exact thing that we've been doing today" if the U.S. loses its measles-free status.

The decision also poses a new test for the Trump administration and its Health and Human Services Secretary Robert F. Kennedy Jr., who has taken steps to change recommendations for the childhood measles, mumps and rubella, or MMR, vaccine and has a long history of questioning its safety without evidence.

CNBC previously reported on how the return of measles in the U.S. exposes weaknesses in the nation's public health system that raise concerns about the return of other eliminated diseases. The resurgence of measles exposes eroding trust in health institutions and growing challenges in persuading people to protect themselves, their children and their communities.

Public health experts previously told CNBC that the return of measles ultimately boils down to fewer children getting vaccinated.

The MMR vaccine is about 97% effective against measles after two doses, while a single dose is 93% effective, according to the CDC. Yet data aggregated by the CDC shows that vaccination coverage for that shot among U.S. kindergarteners fell to 92.4% in the 2025-2026 school year, which is below the 95% threshold public health officials generally recommend to prevent sustained transmission.

The American Medical Association urged the public to get vaccinated after PAHO's announcement.

"We must treat the current measles outbreaks as an urgent call to rebuild trust in science-based recommendations and restore strong, fully-funded public health infrastructure to protect families and communities and prevent the return of diseases once eliminated," AMA President Dr. Willie Underwood said in a statement.

In a separate statement, the pharmaceutical industry's largest lobbying group called on policymakers to provide "clear, accurate information about the seriousness of measles infection and the effectiveness of vaccines against it, restore and sustain high vaccination coverage in every community, strengthen vaccination efforts, and invest in disease surveillance and rapid outbreak response."

"We have the tools to eliminate measles again and what we need now is the commitment to use them," said Dr. Michael Ybarra, chief medical officer of the Pharmaceutical Research and Manufacturers of America.

Measles isn't just a public health threat — it's also a huge financial burden on the healthcare system, CNBC reported.

Based on this year's case count, a new tool developed by researchers at Johns Hopkins University estimates an economic cost of nearly $83 million for the outbreaks. That total includes $56 million in public health response costs, almost $25 million in lost productivity and more than $1.8 million in direct medical expenses.

The U.S. now joins Canada, Spain and the U.K., which all recently lost their measles-free status after a review by experts at the World Health Organization. While the U.S. dropped out of the WHO, it remains a member of PAHO, its Americas branch.

GitHub Pilot Turns AI Security Findings Into 55 Open Source Fixes

📅 Nov 17, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

GitHub reported on November 17, 2026, that a security pilot with Microsoft produced 55 fixes across six open source projects as of November 15, 2026. The Codename MDASH trial filtered 9,791 possible issues into 99 private reports, each checked by researchers and accompanied by a proof of concept and suggested fix.

Fourteen of the fixes had already been included in releases. The projects were AutoGPT, Caddy, Express, FastMCP, ImageMagick and Netty. Maintainers decided which findings to fix, how to fix them and when to ship, with reports and patches staying on GitHub.

The design addresses a maintenance problem created by cheaper AI scanning: duplicates and reports that are not actual vulnerabilities already consume maintainer time, while remediation still requires human judgment. GitHub wants researchers to consult maintainers before pointing another model at their projects and to submit patches alongside any findings.

Microsoft’s Codename MDASH system scanned each project with its MAI Cyber model and suggested fixes. A GitHub Security Lab AI triage agent, built on tailored Copilot skills, removed duplicates, checked validity and focused on high-impact bugs. Seven Microsoft FORGE Lab researchers then manually checked every remaining finding before reports went to maintainers through GitHub private vulnerability reporting.

The first fixes shipped about a week after the initial scan, according to GitHub. Caddy’s maintainers fixed all 22 reports they received in a week or less. ImageMagick, maintained by two people, also received 22 reports; in one case, its maintainers pushed a fix in 15 minutes after a summary identified the exact lines of code and showed that an apparently familiar issue was different.

At AutoGPT, a report arrived three days after maintainers shipped a broken check and pointed to the right line. They took the fix further than the suggested patch, wrote it within a day and had it in production less than a week later. Across the pilot, maintainers used some suggested fixes as written and adapted others.

A Netty maintainer described the remaining constraint: “Finding vulnerabilities is no longer our bottleneck. Fixing them is the hard part, and that depends on maintainers.”

GitHub described the project sample as small, the conclusions as early lessons and the manual review as not yet scalable; researchers spent about two days per project checking findings by hand. Low-quality reports still reach maintainers every day.

GitHub also identified private-fork CI/CD testing and maintainer access to cyber-capable models as remaining needs. The tests would verify fixes on private forks, and model access would help maintainers verify a proof of concept before and after remediation. Its recommended standard for AI-generated reports is to check each finding against the project’s existing knowledge, threat model and security policy, report privately, include a suggested fix and let maintainers set the pace.

GitHub plans to bring these lessons into private vulnerability reporting and security advisories. It will offer AI security scans with findings checked before private reporting, plus GitHub Copilot Max, to all alumni projects of the GitHub Secure Open Source Fund and extend the support to future Fund sessions. GitHub and Microsoft are also working to expand researcher review while preserving those checks.

For AI-assisted vulnerability disclosure, the pilot’s trade-off is concrete: automation can reduce a flood of raw findings to a manageable private queue, while expert review and maintainer control determine whether those reports lead to patches. Expansion hinges on preserving that standard without returning the triage burden to maintainers.

How reverse mortgage LOs can help borrowers to self-service

📅 Nov 17, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Reverse mortgage servicers are investing in borrower portals, mobile apps and smarter phone systems to reduce call volumes. Celink is launching ReverseLink soon, while Onity is expanding digital draw requests and emphasizing closing checklist steps for loan officers.  AI Summary

Reverse mortgage servicers are investing in portals, mobile applications and smarter phone systems so borrowers can manage their loans without contacting a human customer service representative. Servicing executives say that loan officers can play a key role in whether these tools actually work.

Gail Balettie, senior vice president of client satisfaction at Celink, and Ryan McIntire, director of reverse mortgage servicing controls at Onity Mortgage, outlined their companies’ self-service offerings during a panel discussion moderated by Chris Downey of CrossCountry Mortgage at last week’s National Reverse Mortgage Lenders Association (NRMLA) Annual Meeting & Expo.

“In today’s world, where we are conditioned to some extent to be able to go online, get immediate satisfaction, to do things without talking to somebody, the more we can do that, the better we can reach our customers,” McIntire said.

Balettie announced at NRMLA Annual that her company’s mobile app, ReverseLink, will be launching “in the next few weeks.” Its design is similar to Celink’s website and borrowers will receive an invitation to download it. The company is the largest independent subservicer of reverse mortgages and has an estimated market share of about 75% across federally insured Home Equity Conversion Mortgages (HECMs) and proprietary loans.

McIntire told the audience that Onity‘s servicing app is already available on iPhone and Android devices and mirrors its borrower portal. He stressed the importance of providing familiar technology to senior homeowners so “they don’t feel like they’re having to learn something all over again.”

Celink’s soon-to-be-released app will allow clients to accomplish multiple tasks, Balettie said. They’ll be able to request payoff quotes, make voluntary payments, upload forms and download up to two years of statements. The app automatically generates confirmation that an uploaded form has been received, cutting down the number of calls needed for this purpose.

“They can request a line-of-credit draw, and if they do so, they’re going to get their funds a day or two faster than the typical five-day turn time, because it comes in an automated fashion,” she added.

ReverseLink also has a number of FAQs and educational videos, covering subjects like how to manage the account online, what happens to the reverse mortgage when the borrower dies, how to shop for insurance coverage and how to file a claim, and how to find help with small home repairs.

Onity’s portal ties into the company’s system of record, McIntire said. It shows three months of transactions and includes an introduction package that’s tailored to the structure of the loan — for example, any funds set aside for required repairs. Onity is also in the process of building fully digital draw request forms. Today, borrowers have to download and complete these forms before returning it via email or fax, but the goal is to process e-signed draw requests within a few business days, McIntire said.

“Obviously, information that’s available to the borrower via this site is very sensitive. We obviously, as servicers, have to be very cognizant and mindful of fraud. So it does have multifactor authentication that just helps provide that additional sense of security,” he added.

Borrowers commonly contact their loan officer, rather than their servicer, when they have questions about their loan. Balettie said that LOs can take an “ABCD” role to approach to providing assistance in this area.

Making sure a servicer has the correct contact information for a borrower may seem obvious, but it doesn’t happen with perfect uniformity. “We get about 70% of our loans that board with an email address. I want to see that be 100%,” Balettie said. “I don’t know very many people who don’t have an email address in this day and age.”

Balettie sought to distinguish between the alternate contact on the loan application and an authorized third party. The latter may discuss the account but cannot change contact information or request a draw or payoff. Conversely, servicers use the alternate contact only when they cannot reach the borrower, she said.

In response to an audience question, Balettie said that loan officers are welcome to call a servicer on their client’s behalf, but to receive a relevant response, they must be listed as the authorized third party. This is especially important for brokers, who lose access to information once a loan is sold. “We have privacy laws we have to follow,” she added.

A power of attorney can act for a living borrower, but legal authority ends at death, the panelists noted. Downey urged originators to execute the authorized party form at closing and warned that without it, payoffs for a borrower “can be a nightmare.” Having this information on file removes the need to hire an attorney to search for heirs after the borrower dies.

Balettie said originators should collect a canceled check and the direct deposit form at closing. Borrowers without a check can provide a redacted bank statement or bank letter with their account and routing numbers. Due to security concerns, servicers don’t allow bank details to be added online, but they’ll accept faxed or emailed documents, and closing is the best time to gather them.

AWS Brings OpenTSLM Time-Series Reasoning to SageMaker JumpStart

📅 Nov 17, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Amazon Web Services announced on November 17, 2026 that OpenTSLM-Flamingo Gemma 3 4B is available in Amazon SageMaker JumpStart, AWS’s machine-learning model hub. Customers can deploy the 5.24-billion-parameter model to a real-time SageMaker AI inference endpoint within their own AWS environments.

OpenTSLM interprets numerical measurements alongside text such as maintenance records, market news and engineers’ observations, then returns natural-language explanations and answers. The JumpStart package pairs Gemma 3 4B with a time-series encoder and cross-attention layers, which let the language model attend to encoded signals. Its weights occupy about 10.5 GB of GPU memory when stored in bfloat16.

The OpenTSLM research paper distinguishes this Flamingo architecture from SoftPrompt. Both treat time series as a native input modality, but SoftPrompt adds learned time-series tokens to the language model’s sequence, while Flamingo uses a Perceiver Resampler to produce a fixed-size latent representation and gated cross-attention to integrate it. The original evaluation covered ECG question answering, sleep staging and human activity recognition, giving the managed release a research foundation rooted in medical and wearable data.

The separate OpenTSLM repository is MIT-licensed and supplies fine-tuning code, with pretrained checkpoints available through the project’s Hugging Face organization. It identifies the project as a collaboration between Stanford University’s Schmiedmayer Lab and ETH Zurich’s Agentic Systems Lab.

AWS describes Nexthink and Aionic Labs as building toward proactive assistance through Spark, Nexthink’s personal IT agent, so employees can receive help before asking for it. The workflow combines recent CPU, memory, disk, application-stability and connectivity measurements with timestamped events, device configuration and software versions. The model supplies a state summary with supporting signals, a recommended check and action, and an expected outcome that Spark can verify.

AWS’s low-disk-space example is illustrative. The model connects falling free space, a rising disk queue and more frequent application hangs to symptoms such as slow performance or difficulty saving files. Spark checks whether an IT-approved cleanup is appropriate, shows the employee what would be removed and seeks confirmation before acting. It then checks the result and offers escalation to IT support if the problem persists.

The endpoint standardizes each series to a mean of zero and a standard deviation of one, while supplying the original mean and standard deviation as text so the model receives both shape and scale. Real-time calls have a 60-second limit and a 6 MB request-body cap. AWS reports roughly 20 generated tokens per second on the default instance; instances are processed sequentially, and asynchronous inference is recommended for longer-running work.

Flamingo’s fixed-size representation bounds what reaches the language model, but it does not give the complete system unlimited memory or guarantee that a brief event remains identifiable in a very long recording. AWS advises testing representative normal and abnormal windows, retaining sampling resolution for short events, aligning related channels and including units, sampling intervals and observation periods in the context. The payload cap and the model’s useful analysis window are separate constraints.

AWS’s adaptation workflow uses TimeNet, which provides a common dataset format and Python API with connectors and immutable versions. TimeNet’s documentation explicitly limits its scope to data; training, inference, model definitions and evaluation metrics are outside it. Renting isn’t always a stepping stone to homeownership. Multifamily expert Virginia Love of Entrata explains how financial priorities, flexibility and the true cost of ownership are reshaping renters’ decisions.

The story we’re telling ourselves about renters is outdated, Virginia Love, industry principal at Entrata, told me recently.

Entrata is a property management operating system built to improve operational efficiency at multifamily communities. In its recent “Resident Pulse” survey, conducted in partnership with YouGov, the company asked renters about their financial priorities, attitudes toward homeownership, definitions of success and other lifestyle factors.

That gives Love a bird’s-eye view of the state of renting and, by extension, homeownership.

“I feel like people think that renters are less-than,” she said. “I think the real story is, people are trying to do what’s right for them and what’s right for their family.”

With homeownership increasingly out of reach for people of all ages, it’s time that real estate agents rethink the role of renters in the housing market. Entrata’s insights can help.

Love said that renters are prioritizing healthy financial hygiene and making decisions based on a more complex economic environment, reducing their existing financial obligations before pursuing longer-term financial goals.

“They are eating out less, they’re shopping less, they’re spending less, they’re switching to cheaper brands,” she said, with the goal of prioritizing emergency savings, debt reduction and other budgetary line items.

For previous generations, the initial barrier to homeownership was a down payment, Love said. Now, it’s the rising costs of everything that comes after the initial upfront purchase price: the mortgage payment at today’s higher rates, insurance, maintenance, repairs and other expenses. Managing the unexpected requires greater levels of financial stability.

“It’s then I have insurance, then I gotta take care of the yard. Then what if the tree falls on the driveway?” Love said.

Beyond the cost of rent vs. buy, maintenance, amenities and the ability to relocate can have real value for renters, especially when they’re working to pay down debt, pursuing educational goals, navigating career shifts or deciding where and when they’re ready to settle down. What they’re paying for, Love said, is freedom.

Love describes Gen Z in particular as pragmatic about money and more willing to take a gradual path toward major financial milestones.

For those who plan to buy, “they’re absolutely fine with starter homes,” Love said. “And I don’t think that that is something that we’ve seen for a while. Everybody kind of wanted to go from zero to 60, and this is a generation that’s willing to take those steps.”

She explains that some renters are working toward a purchase while others prefer to invest, maintain flexibility or avoid being tied to one address.

Real estate agents often approach renters with an ingrained belief in the superiority of homeownership, but it’s not right for everyone. Spend time upfront with renters to find out what matters to them, and don’t just push them toward homeownership automatically, Love said.

Stop assuming buying is the client’s next step: Renters aren’t necessarily buyers who simply haven’t saved up a down payment yet. They may have other financial priorities, like paying down student loans, building an emergency fund, investing for retirement or prioritizing flexibility. Ask what a client’s financial priorities are before assuming homeownership is the goal.

Talk about the full cost of homeownership: Down payments aren’t the whole story here. Monthly payments, rising insurance and repair costs, home maintenance and other expenses can affect whether, and when, buying makes sense. Make sure you’re sharing updated costs of ownership along with the upfront costs, and be realistic about affordability.

Ask what payment feels comfortable, not just what a lender will approve: A client’s maximum purchasing power isn’t necessarily the same as their target budget. Understand the buying budget that matches their comfort level, other financial considerations and personal priorities before locking in on a price range.

Understand that delay isn’t the same as giving up: Some renters may aspire to own a home, but they’re not necessarily in a rush. Maintain relationships over the long haul without turning every conversation into a homebuying pitch.

Recognize that renting has value beyond the monthly payment: The benefits of renting — maintenance support, amenities, flexibility and freedom — may outweigh those of homeownership, even though they don’t build equity for the renter. Understand what the renter values before presenting ownership as an obvious upgrade or superior choice.

“I think the real story is, people are trying to do what’s right for them and what’s right for their family,” Love said. By understanding timing, goals and price sensitivities, agents can help potential clients evaluate their options without prescribing the outcome ahead of time.

Situational Awareness Dumps Bitcoin Miners After Citadel Sale

📅 Nov 17, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Situational Awareness, the artificial intelligence hedge fund run by former OpenAI researcher Leopold Aschenbrenner, cut its bitcoin miner holdings sharply in the third quarter, leaving about $80.8 million across three stocks and exiting three others, a Monday filing shows.

The San Francisco firm still held 3,019,029 CleanSpark shares, worth $38.9 million at Sept. 30, 1,934,618 Bitdeer shares, worth $20.3 million, and 527,864 IREN shares, worth $21.6 million. Those are the only miner lines left, a sliver of the $1.83 billion in six miner stocks the fund listed at June 30.

The deepest cut was IREN. The fund held 9,474,099 IREN shares, worth $433.3 million, at midyear. The Sept. 30 stake is about 94% smaller by share count. CleanSpark was trimmed about 75%, from 12,276,139 shares worth $178.6 million. Bitdeer was reduced about 44%, from 3,439,450 shares worth $54.6 million. Bitdeer’s reported value fell further than its share count, to $20.3 million, as the stock also declined.

Three names disappeared from the filing entirely. The fund had held 26,008,473 Core Scientific shares, worth $665.6 million, 17,100,000 Riot Platforms shares, worth $468.2 million, and 9,133,726 Hive Digital shares, worth $33.2 million, at June 30. None of those lines appear in the new report. The filing does not say whether those sales were part of a late-July portfolio sale, later trades, or both.

Aschenbrenner published a long 2024 essay, “Situational Awareness: The Decade Ahead,” arguing that artificial general intelligence was approaching faster than most investors appreciated, and that computing power and electricity would be the tight constraints. He founded the fund that September around that view, looking at technology and energy companies that AI would help or hurt.

The miner stakes fit the same idea. A March profile described the fund as betting that electricity and computing capacity may be the most valuable assets in the AI build-out, pointing to miners that were turning high-density facilities and power contracts toward AI workloads. IREN has signed a multiyear cloud contract with Microsoft. CleanSpark has struck a $6.6 billion data-center lease. Listed miners have also been operating under tight economics this year, with bitcoin often trading below estimated production costs.

The cuts landed after a brutal July. The fund sold the bulk of the fund’s public stock portfolio to Ken Griffin’s Citadel late that month after heavy losses on AI-related holdings, Reuters reported, citing people familiar with the matter. Those sources said the fund was forced to unwind most of its public book. In a letter to investors, Aschenbrenner said the portfolio was down an estimated 67% in July, that the fund had come “closer to permanent capital impairment than is acceptable,” and that it had sold a portion of its public holdings in a block trade to remove all leverage and close its shorts. The unaudited letter still put year-to-date performance at plus 80%. Aschenbrenner said the fund was not shutting down and had preserved its private investments.

The new 13F shows how much of the public book was left by Sept. 30. Reported long holdings fell from $20.24 billion, across 26 lines, at June 30, to $1.58 billion, across 23 lines, three months later. That decline should not be read as the fund losing 92% of its capital. A 13F reports only long positions in U.S.-listed stocks and certain options. It leaves out cash, shorts and private investments, and option lines are valued at the underlying shares, not the premiums paid. Reuters’ sources said that after the Citadel deal the fund would hold a book of roughly $10 billion, including stocks and private investments such as a stake in Anthropic.

What remains is concentrated. The largest common-stock line is now SharonAI, 8,070,950 shares worth $440.7 million. Aschenbrenner also listed call options on Bloom Energy, SanDisk, Nebius and a memory ETF. The three leftover miner stakes are a stub of the midyear book. Whether those positions were trimmed further after Sept. 30 will only be clear when the fourth-quarter 13F arrives in mid-February.

Mortgage rates jump to 7.58% as inflation lifts bond yields

Mortgage rates rose sharply last week, with 30-year conforming loans at 7.58% and jumbo loans at 7.87%. Hot inflation data and higher yields pushed Fed hike expectations above 93%, while builders continued offering rate incentives for new homes.  AI Summary

Mortgage rates skyrocketed last week on the back of rising inflation and bond yields, erasing some of the recent affordability gains for potential homebuyers.

At HousingWire‘s Mortgage Rates Center on Tuesday, rates for 30-year conforming loans averaged 7.58%, up 23 basis points from one week ago. Rates for 30-year jumbo loans jumped 31 bps to average 7.87%, while rates for 30-year loans through the Federal Housing Administration (FHA) rose 15 bps to 7.37%.

HousingWire Lead Analyst Logan Mohtashami wrote over the weekend that “last week wasn’t good for mortgage rates or the bond market” due to hawkish statements from Federal Reserve officials and hot inflation data from the U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics (BLS). Additionally, the U.S. conflict with Iran and potential tariff increases against Mexico propelled long-term Treasury rates and mortgage rates upward.

Interest rate traders took notice. According to the CME Group‘s FedWatch tool, more than 93% expect benchmark rates to increase by 25 bps at next month’s Fed meeting. That’s up from 76% one month ago. It would be the second hike of the year by the Fed and put the federal funds rate at a range of 4% to 4.25%.

Kyle Bass, business production manager at Refi.com, pointed to Mortgage Bankers Association application data showing that recent downward moves in rates had positive impacts on loan demand. But with visible volatility in rates on a day-to-day basis, he said, preparation between loan officers and borrowers is needed to lock in the lowest possible rate and get lower monthly payments.

“Borrowers who benefit are the ones who prepared before the number moved. A shadow application, with income documents, statements and credit in order, lets a homeowner ask what their current mortgage could do for them the day rates cooperate. Waiting for a better number only works if you are ready when it arrives,” Bass said.

A report released Tuesday by Realtor.com shows that the new-home segment is giving the typical buyer lower rates and better affordability than existing homes. Homebuilders, who are also under financial pressure, continue to offer rate buydowns and other incentives as they seek to offload their inventory.

Realtor.com reported that the average mortgage rate for a new-home buyer in the third quarter was 5.01%, more than a full percentage point lower than the 6.15% rate for an existing-home buyer. That translates to a difference of $258 in monthly payments for someone buying a $400,000 home with a 10% down payment.

“Builders have adjusted to a market where affordability is still a major hurdle, but that adjustment isn’t always visible in the listing price,” Joel Berner, senior economist at Realtor.com, said in a statement. “Reduced mortgage rates can help make a new home more affordable for buyers while allowing builders to hold asking prices relatively steady.”

The report noted that reduced rates are often temporary and only last for the first few years after origination. But even when comparing 30-year fixed rates, those who bought a new home in the third quarter saved, with average rates of 6.19% compared to 6.68% for existing home-buyers. On the same $400,000 purchase with 10% down, the new-home buyer saves about $117 per month in interest.

“Mortgage-rate incentives can make a meaningful difference in what a buyer pays each month, but it’s important to understand how long the lower rate lasts,” Berner said. “A temporary buydown can provide relief early on, while a lower rate fixed for the life of the loan offers a different kind of savings.”

Selma Hepp, chief economist at Cotality, said in a statement that tariff-driven increases to building materials are putting pressure on the new-home segment. In turn, this winds up being felt by consumers through higher home purchase prices. Regardless of the type of home being purchased, rising insurance premiums and property taxes are also straining budgets.

“For housing, the key question is not simply whether mortgage rates decline, but whether they fall enough to meaningfully improve purchasing power and bring more buyers and sellers back to the market,” Hepp said.” Until then, I expect activity to remain relatively subdued, making the spring homebuying season an increasingly important test of whether the market can finally turn a corner.”

While housing market professionals continue to grapple with fewer home sales and lower mortgage origination volumes, the U.S. economy as a whole continues to show resilience to inflation, tariffs and geopolitical uncertainty.

Melissa Cohn, regional vice president at William Raveis Mortgage, pointed to last week’s Producer Price Index (PPI), which showed 6.5% inflation on an annualized basis in October. It was the largest year-over-year increase since November 2022. That came only days after the Consumer Price Index showed monthly inflation of 0.4%.

“Unfortunately, the CPI number that we got earlier this week has set off bond yields, and bond yields were probably up almost 25 basis points from where they were at the end of the previous week,” Cohn said Friday in a statement. “It’s not just because of the CPI number, but because of expectations about the Fed, where members are saying, ‘Well, we’re definitely going to have to hike rates.’ We know that the market’s expecting it.

“All of the benefit that we got in mortgage rates over the course of the past three weeks was basically just thrown out the window again, because mortgage rates are really tied to the rate of inflation. We’re back on the inflation wagon.”

Federal Reserve Chair Kevin Warsh offered some forward-looking remarks on Monday during a jobs and productivity conference in San Francisco, saying that the central bank continues to look closely at the development of “super intelligence” and its impacts on the global and national economies.

“At the Fed, our role in all this is neither advocacy nor opposition. Our responsibility is to recognize that the widespread adoption of this technology will have significant impact on the economy, with important implications for monetary policy down the road,” Warsh said.

Senate Poised to Vote on Affordability Package that Hunters, Anglers Worry Is a Trojan Horse for Public-Land Rollbacks

📅 Nov 17, 2026  |  Category: Other  |  Read Source →

Published Nov 17, 2026 1:09 PM EST

Get the hottest outdoor news from Outdoor Life.

The U.S. Senate plans to vote this week on a bill aimed at easing America’s affordability crisis and fighting climate change that hunting and fishing groups worry will also erode many of the nation’s bedrock conservation laws.

The complex and winding 400-page Bipartisan American Affordability and Jobs Act focuses on lowering energy costs, something both Republicans and Democrats have championed and and that helped drive Democratic gains in the midterm elections. BAAJA also, however, ties jobs and household bills with curtailing public-lands protections. And in a twist that’s perplexing many conservation groups, the bill is co-sponsored by Sens. Martin Heinrich (D-N.M.) and Mike Lee (R-Utah), two lawmakers known for being on nearly opposite ends of the public-lands spectrum.

BAAJA is largely focused on permitting reform, something the country hasn’t tackled in decades, according to the Bipartisan Policy Center. In practice, the legislation would raise the standard for whether projects approved by one administration can be revoked by a future administration.

That means an industrial solar project permitted by one president, for example, could not be stymied by a future president intent on preventing renewables. It also speeds up permitting processes and cuts red tape for everything from energy projects to landscape-scale changes like prescribed fire.

But sportsmen and conservationists say the bill would not just expedite climate or environmentally friendly projects. BAAJA would also make it much harder — or potentially impossible — for an administration to overturn leases or approvals for projects as controversial as hyperscale data centers on public lands or mines in the same watersheds as sensitive wilderness areas. It’s a bill that prioritizes development, many observers say, at the cost of the very “red tape” that has been responsible for conserving many of the country’s most priceless fish, wildlife, and wild places.

“I understand this is a big, bipartisan bill — it has some things in it I would rather it not have. But I negotiated the deal based on what was actually possible to get signed into law,” Heinrich wrote in an email to Outdoor Life. “Because when I say climate change is an existential threat, I mean it. And I will not let an opportunity to do something about it pass by.”

Conservationists like Ingrid Lyons, executive director of Save the Boundary Waters, understand the need for stability around permitting development. The whiplash between issuing and revoking permits for public land development projects every time an administration changes has likely hurt the U.S.’s ability to move forward with important energy projects, including in renewables like wind and solar.

But she fears this bill could instead cement the creation of controversial projects like a copper-sulfide mine at the headwaters of the Boundary Waters Canoe Area Wilderness or a copper-molybdenum-gold mine in the salmon-rich waters of Alaska’s Bristol Bay. Permits for projects like these have been denied, among other issues, because of environmental risk. If permits are reissued in similar scenarios, legislation like BAAJA could prevent future administrations from stopping development in places that truly need protection.

“What about landscape certainty?” Lyons says. “We’re overlooking in some ways the impact to communities that are relying on protection and not just projects.”

The conservation community has become increasingly vocal in its opposition to the bill since Heinrich and Lee partnered with Sens. Sheldon Whitehouse (D-R.I.), and Shelley Moore Capito (R-W.Va.), to introduce the legislation in late September. Lee has a clear track record of supporting public land sales and undermining existing public-lands conservation, including 2025’s attempt to include federal land sales in the One Big Beautiful Bill.

A lengthy version of BAAJA is before the Senate as of Tuesday. The current bill includes a variety of red flags for hunters, anglers, tribes, endangered-species advocates, and other conservationists.

The Theodore Roosevelt Conservation Partnership acknowledged the need for permit reform but said in a statement that “progress should not come at the expense of the conservation safeguards and public review that give hunters and anglers a voice in how our lands and waters are managed.”

For Lyons, it comes down to the potential unintended consequences of sweeping legislation like BAAJA.

“We need to be exceptionally aware of the implications of this bill, which could be that a lot of these really beloved places could have projects many communities have worked really hard to oppose cemented into place.”

Many in the conservation community were surprised by the bill’s sponsors, given Heinrich’s history as a sportsman and conservationist who regularly defends public lands and sensitive places, and Lee’s general position as a proponent of development and public-lands sell-off.

But in Heinrich’s statement to Outdoor Life, he emphasizes the impact of climate change on our planet, including the places where people hunt, hike, bike and camp. Independent modeling, he said, predicts the bill will lead to a 20 percent reduction in power sector emissions, or the “equivalent to taking 39 coal plants offline for 10 years.”

“You cannot protect a trout stream without paying attention to the warming temperatures and declining flows that threaten it,” he wrote. “You cannot protect wildlife habitat while ignoring the impacts of increasingly severe drought and wildfire. We need to reduce emissions, and that means getting more clean energy onto the grid.”

In an Oct. 30 op-ed in the Albuquerque Journal, Heinrich explained how New Mexico’s SunZia Wind and Transmission project took 17 years to permit and build.

He also pushed back on what he called misinformation about the bill, stating he doesn’t believe data centers should be on public land. The bill also requires large scale data center operators to pay the full cost of electricity consumed by the facility.

The Izaak Walton League’s conservation director, Kate Miller, thinks each of the bill’s sponsors are acting in good faith and trying to do what is right for their constituents. Many projects, including those aimed at restoring forests and wildlife habitat, have been held up by litigation. Something like BAAJA could move those forward.

But Miller would have liked to see these issues tackled individually instead of in a massive bill. And while she hopes that, if the bill passes, the administration will respect existing laws, the past two years has brought an onslaught of changes to most of the nation’s bedrock environmental laws including the Clean Water Act, the Endangered Species Act, and the National Environmental Policy Act.

“We want to make sure what comes out is not something that sacrifices those core protections that allow us to have healthy waters and habitats,” Miller says. “That we don’t sacrifice one for the other.”

Meanwhile, both industry and conservation groups say they’re working to lobby lawmakers for changes and amendments.
