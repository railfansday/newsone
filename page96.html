The Leonid meteor shower peaks overnight on Nov. 17-18, sending shooting stars racing across the night sky from the direction of the constellation Leo.

The Leonid meteor shower peaks Nov. 17-18 sending shooting stars and fireballs across the autumn sky as Earth passes through a stream of debris left by Comet 55P/Tempel-Tuttle.

Up to 15 meteors could be spotted per hour under perfect dark sky conditions on the night of Nov. 17-18, according to the International Meteor Organisation. NASA, meanwhile, has given a more conservative rate of less than three meteors per hour — time will tell.

Leonid meteors appear to race away from a point of origin, or radiant, in the constellation Leo, which glistens high above the southeastern horizon in the hours before dawn in mid-November. Both Jupiter and Mars will be separated by just a few degrees in the constellation Leo on the night of the peak, making it particularly easy to find in 2026!

The best time to spot Leonids is in the hours before dawn on Nov. 18, when the radiant will reach its highest point in the southeastern sky, maximizing the amount of meteors that may be seen skimming through Earth's atmosphere. By then, the waxing moon will have set, leaving darker skies and a better chance of spotting fainter meteors.

Shooting stars that appear close to the radiant will strike Earth almost head-on, leaving the shortest trails. Instead, find a patch of sky a short distance away from the constellation Leo, where the meteors will be at their most impressive, and give your eyes 20-30 minutes to fully adapt to the dark.

As if shooting stars weren't enough, the Leonid shower is also known for spawning dazzling fireballs and long-lasting Earth-grazing meteors, which strike our atmosphere at an oblique angle, leaving long trains as they skim through before passing back out into space.

The shower is also capable of producing magnificent meteor storms when Earth passes through particularly dense trails of debris left behind by Comet 55P/Tempel-Tuttle, which orbits the sun approximately every 33 years. Sadly, we'll have to wait a bit for the next big show. According to the Planetary Society, another Leonid meteor storm could occur as early as 2031, while a study released in August 2026 predicts potentially spectacular displays in 2033 and 2034.

Instagram Is Bringing Amazon Affiliate Links Directly to Reels and Posts

📅 Nov 16, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Instagram announced a change on Monday that may spell the end for users being nudged to visit a creator’s “link in bio.” At least when they’re promoting products sold on Amazon.

Starting today, creators and businesses can attach multiple Amazon affiliate links to their Instagram content—up to 30 per video or 20 per regular feed post. (Affiliate links let creators earn a commission on any resulting sales.) To participate in this Amazon program, creators must be US-based adults with a professional account that has over 1,000 followers.

Instagram’s parent company Meta dropped this shopping-focused change right before the holiday season really kicks off. As creators look to cash in on Amazon's sales, you might expect an avalanche of affiliate posts as you sip hot cocoa and scroll through Reels. But Instagram claims this won’t be the case.

“We don’t expect a sudden influx of shopping content from this launch,” Instagram spokesperson Megan Buckley tells WIRED. “Instagram’s recommendation systems will continue to recommend content based on people’s interests and what we think they’ll enjoy and find valuable. This update makes creators’ product recommendations easier to shop.”

For years, Instagram users could attach a single external link to their bio information. This led to the rise of bundlers like Linktree, where creators could create simple one-page sites with multiple links to content, like shopping recommendations or news articles. In 2023, Instagram expanded the number of links allowed in profiles to five. This most recent update could change how it feels to scroll Instagram, moving more shopping nudges from Amazon storefronts hidden in the bio to direct links on the viral videos.

In an example shared by Meta, a woman recording a Reel leans into her camera as she applies makeup. The caption reads: “My favorite lip tint these days!” Above the creator’s name, a small image tile pops up with the Amazon product. Tap the product and a page opens inside Instagram with the price. Tap one of the listed items, and Instagram will bump you over to the Amazon app or website to complete your purchase.

This change comes as social media shopping habits continue to expand and evolve. TikTok, one of Meta’s primary competitors, has been quite successful in expanding users’ purchasing habits through the app. Around this time last year, an analytics firm estimated that $19 billion worth of products were sold globally through TikTok Shop, putting it roughly the same size as eBay. Unlike TikTok, however, Instagram will not take a cut of the sales (at least for now). Reddit is also experimenting with how users shop heading into this holiday season, recently announcing Reddit Reviews, which uses generative AI to summarize user sentiment about products.

For over a decade, Instagram developers have searched for ways to streamline how users spend money through the app. In 2016, WIRED wrote about how the social media app added select commerce links from brands like Warby Parker, a fairly novel attempt at the time to get people to buy stuff through social media. A few years later, the Instagram experience morphed into something like a “digital mall” with in-app checkouts.

This latest attempt moves links from creator bios and places them directly in users' feeds. While this could be a boon for creators, the platform’s implementation could annoy users, who are already inundated with ads online. We’ll see if this change clicks.

Gemini rolling out 66 new regional voices on Android and iOS, including 10+ UK dialects

📅 Nov 16, 2026  |  Category: Food  |  Read Source →

Following a preview at Google I/O, Google is rolling out 66 new regional voices to the Gemini app on both Android and iOS today. The expansion introduces tailored voice options across U.S. English, U.K. English, Indian English, Hindi, Brazilian Portuguese, Indonesian, and Korean.

For users in the UK, the update brings over 10 distinct English voice options covering a wide range of regional cadences. The lineup includes Geordie, Scouse, Brummie, Scottish, Welsh, Northern Irish, Modern RP, and Multicultural London English (MLE).

This might help reduce the issues that sometimes still occur when English accents, in particular, morph into a hybrid of Australian or even American that has been going on for a while with Gemini Live. We have not yet been able to test to confirm this has been resolved with the update. As someone with a regional English accent, this extra representation with Gemini voices is quite welcome.

According to Google, these voices are tuned for natural pronunciation, pitch, and everyday local rhythm rather than exaggerated stereotypes. Because the focus is on cadence over slang, Gemini will sound like a local without filling its text responses with forced street slang. Once selected, your chosen voice carries across the entire app, applying to both Gemini Live conversations and standard text-to-speech playback when tapping the “Listen” icon on chat responses.

You can preview and select the new options today by tapping the three-dot menu inside Gemini Live, or by heading into the Gemini app settings and selecting “Gemini’s voice.” It’s worth noting that the voices aren’t explicitly labeled by city or region in the app menu, and Gemini isn’t programmed to self-identify which accent it is using if you ask it directly.

Instead, Google notes that locally rooted prompts about food, culture, or sport, such as asking for the best pub in Sheffield for a Sunday roast – my suggestion is not a pub; it’s Turnip & Thyme on Eccleshall Road, FWIW. Or another suggestion from big G is where to find proper ingredients for a traditional Scouse stew; the company says these will naturally bring out the warmth and rhythm of the new dialects.

I’d suggest trying to ask where to get the best pattie and chips in Hull or where to get the best pint in Leeds (it’s Whitelocks).

Photos reveal scale of Trump’s White House ballroom, drawing criticism

📅 Nov 16, 2026  |  Category: World  |  Read Source →

Earlier this month, Trump’s White House ballroom and military bunker laid its final structural steel beam, with politicians and critics weighing in on the scale of the project.

Recently taken images of the extension, which show the structure with massive concrete shear walls, steel beams, and columns, along with a partially enclosed roof structure, have drawn attention to the scale of the building.

No sign of its planned neoclassical ornamentation has yet been implemented.

Officially called the East Wing Modernization Project, the structure dwarfs the Executive Residence and West Wing.

Trump announced the laying of the last steel beam in the superstructure on 4 November, the day after the national midterm elections.

“This is a special structure,” Trump said on a tour on 4 November, according to NBC News. “It’s going to be not only one of the biggest, but it’ll be the most beautiful ballroom, I think, anywhere in the world, and they’ve been trying to get it for 150 years.”

It says everything about Donald Trump that his gilded ballroom dwarfs the size of the People's House—and it says everything about the American people that they repudiated him loudly and clearly last Tuesday. pic.twitter.com/ou23LpAes8

Since then, there has been a renewed wave of criticism online over the project, ranging from outrage to humour.

Former senator and presidential candidate Hillary Clinton wrote on X (formerly Twitter), commenting on the size of the structure and noting the midterm election’s “repudiation” of Trump.

“It says everything about Donald Trump that his gilded ballroom dwarfs the size of the People’s House—and it says everything about the American people that they repudiated him loudly and clearly last Tuesday,” wrote Clinton.

Congressman Jim Clyburn took a similar stance in a statement on Facebook, noting that the ballroom was “much bigger” than the rest of the White House.

Architecture critic Kate Wagner took a more humorous approach, noting that the ballroom is not neoclassical in construction. Wagner compared it to a Ramada Inn, a chain of hotels in the US, that they will “glue pediments to”.

“[It’s] an outcome designed in a lab to make everyone angry. possibly the most trump building ever built,” wrote Wagner on X.com.

The ballroom has been controversial since Trump demolished the White House’s West Wing in October 2025. The National Capital Planning Commission approved the project in April.

It has faced legal challenges, with US District Judge Leon issuing a stay in response to a lawsuit led by the National Trust for Historic Preservation.

the fact that trump's ballroom is clearly not neoclassical in any way shape or form is possibly the funniest moment in architectural history

In August, the Supreme Court allowed the project to continue, though the dissenting opinion said it was “likely unlawful”.

Despite the legal challenges, construction has continued, with Trump’s team leaning on the national security aspects of the underground bunker.

The administration has also floated plans to add a screening facility at Sherman Park – a small park to the southwest of the White House. Trump also installed a paved helipad on the White House lawn.

What it will take for CMOs to reach the next frontier of AI-driven growth

📅 Nov 16, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Join us Dec. 1-3 in Nashville for the Digiday Programmatic Marketing Summit

CMOs at the largest enterprise brands across consumer goods, financial services, B2B and more are all struggling with the same thing: more AI technology has not translated into more business growth. They’re stuck chasing efficiency gains, funding tools that optimize tasks inside functional silos, running pilots that go nowhere and delegating AI decisions to IT teams.

But a few CMOs are doing it differently. Eric Schwartz, CMO of The Clorox Company, told me his marketers are building AI systems that produce highly optimized product content. They’re encoding brand voice, compliance guardrails and seasonal context into agents that work across every brand in their portfolio. Their goal isn’t to save time on doing what human writers can already do. Instead, they want to codify, scale and orchestrate the expertise that increases relevance, engagement and conversion with consumers. That’s a growth play.

This is an operating-model decision that the CMO has to own: give marketers the strategic, system-building work and shift execution to AI agents. That takes much more than a chatbot built for a solo work experience.

“I tell everyone that tries to pitch me about AI capabilities that the last thing I need is a 14th chatbot to save our marketers time,” Schwartz said. “What I need is interoperable functionality.”

Schwartz isn’t alone. A vanguard of marketers are charting the frontier of AI-native marketing. To do this requires three fundamental shifts.

Employees are becoming more efficient with AI, but report that leaders aren’t asking them to reinvest the productivity gains into strategic work; they’re just expected to produce more output. Boston Consulting Group found that 66% of frontline employees receive limited or no guidance on using saved time, and more than half don’t reinvest it into strategic work.

Measuring AI success solely on efficiency invites efficiency’s reward: a smaller budget.

For example, one CMO’s AI-powered content system is saving the company 500 hours a month. But the company’s CFO saw the dashboard and asked: “If marketing can do this with 500 fewer hours, why does the budget stay the same?” The dashboard was supposed to prove value. Instead, it made the case for a cut.

A marketing team at a regulated financial services company chose a different approach. The team was losing 360 hours a week to legal review bottlenecks, manual multi-channel content creation and constant product updates across 800-plus references. The team built a system with compliance encoded into the workflow and distribution automated across 25 channels. The result: 360 hours freed up weekly, 90% less editing time and zero agency spend.

The marketing team reinvested those hours into producing an award-winning ad campaign, not producing more of the same content. In a category where most consumers can’t name three financial lenders, breaking through the sameness drove brand recall, pipeline and market share. The system handled the production, but the humans did the work that won.

The AI opportunity isn’t time saved. Frontier CMOs reinvest that time in architecture, curation and orchestration: designing agentic workflows, encoding brand voice and steering the system to drive growth. The humans who know best what makes their brand different turn saved time into a real advantage.

Historically, marketing and revenue have operated on separate tracks. In B2B, marketing fills the top of the funnel and hands off to sales. In consumer brands, marketing creates content while commerce manages the shelf. In financial services, brand marketing and the advisors who own the relationship rarely connect. In most organizations, no single team owns the moments that decide the sale.

Sixty-seven percent of B2B buyers now prefer a rep-free experience, according to data from Gartner. Consumers and bank customers are doing their own research too. In financial services, buyers are asking AI tools for recommendations before they ever talk to an advisor. These buyers aren’t waiting for a handoff.At the frontier, there are no handoffs. AI agents carry customer data, market signals and brand and compliance context between marketing and revenue teams in real time. Context doesn’t die at a cross-functional boundary. It compounds.

Schwartz described what this looks like at Clorox. The company’s media ecosystem spans retail media, e-commerce and traditional retail, and the goal is to connect performance back to sales — or to the signals that point to sales. Most marketing organizations stop at creating and deploying campaigns. Schwartz wants that signal flowing back to show where to lean in and scale and to shape what gets created next. By closing that loop, a media budget compounds into growth instead of disappearing.

A CMO recently told me his organization has 40 active pilots, all marked green. Everything worked in the lab, but nothing survived contact with the organization. IT loved the tools, but marketing couldn’t use them.

This happens when AI adoption is delegated to IT. The CMO sponsors the budget but doesn’t own the transformation. In Writer’s 2026 survey, more than half of CMOs (52.3%) reported “significant knowledge gaps” about AI. Gartner predicts that by 2027, lack of AI literacy will rank among the top three reasons CMOs are replaced.

At the frontier, the CMO owns the entire marketing AI agenda: selecting tools, redesigning workflows and deciding what should remain human-led. That means making AI adoption a cultural shift, not a software rollout. And that can’t be delegated.

Schwartz is an example of what this looks like in practice. He made AI adoption his own rather than delegating it to an innovation team or task force.

The CMOs making progress are changing how the work gets done, moving from traditional marketing operations toward a new model of driving growth with AI. They aren’t simply doing the same work with AI layered on top, but reimagining foundational structures, systems and strategies for a new era of marketing. The frontier isn’t a place a CMO arrives at. Clearpool’s move from CPOOL to CLEAR is reaching the point where a project timetable becomes an exchange-access issue. Its self-custody migration opens on November 17, while some trading venues have scheduled restrictions before that date.

The distinction matters because holders do not all follow the same process. A token in a personal wallet and a balance on an exchange may represent similar market exposure, but the organizations responsible for converting them are different.

Clearpool’s November 12 migration guide sets a one-for-one conversion and a February 17, 2027 deadline. It identifies CLEAR as an ERC-20 token on Ethereum. The guide separates holders using their own wallets from those whose exchanges will handle the migration.

That separation is more useful than treating the announcement as a universal instruction to transfer tokens. Exchange support, snapshot rules and the blockchain on which a balance is held can affect the appropriate process.

The guide is the informational starting point for verifying those arrangements. The existence of a public migration should not make unsolicited messages or lookalike websites credible. A legitimate project announcement can be copied by an impersonator without changing its appearance very much.

KuCoin’s notice schedules CPOOL deposits and withdrawals to close at 04:00 UTC on November 16, followed by trading bots at 07:00 and the CPOOL/USDT market at 08:00. The exchange says it will convert eligible balances automatically.

Its notice includes specific eligibility conditions: a minimum of 0.1 CPOOL and covered spot-account balances. Pending deposits or withdrawals at the snapshot are excluded. It also warns that later deposits of the old token will not be supported.

These details show why the project deadline alone is insufficient. An exchange can stop accepting an asset months before the broader self-custody window ends. Its reopening schedule for the replacement asset is another separate milestone, which KuCoin says will be announced after the swap.

A holder exchanging 1,000 old tokens for 1,000 new tokens preserves the number of units in that transaction. The conversion does not establish the new market price, total supply or the holder’s percentage interest in the network’s token economy.

Clearpool’s September expansion proposal paired the migration with treasury recapitalization and additional allocations. That document is background to the economic design; the November migration guide supplies the operational dates. The two should be read for their different purposes.

NewsBTC’s examination of Aave’s proposed division of lending revenue illustrates a related analytical point: an administrative parameter or token count is only one part of the underlying economics. Funding, incentives and realized activity still determine how a protocol develops.

Clearpool has linked its broader plans to institutional credit and expansion toward the XRP Ledger. Completing a token conversion would be an operational milestone, while launching products and attracting borrowers would require separate evidence.

That distinction also appears in NewsBTC’s reporting on financing tokenized collateral. A working token or interface is one component; enforceable claims, usable liquidity and dependable servicing make the financial product useful.

For this week’s transition, the immediate questions are concrete: whether exchanges apply their published rules, when replacement balances appear and when the new markets reopen. Those answers will reveal the quality of the migration more clearly than a change of ticker alone.

Paul Rudd And Seann William Scott's R-Rated Comedy Is Worth Revisiting 18 Years Later

📅 Nov 16, 2026  |  Category: Entertainment  |  Read Source →

Studios abandoned big comedies years ago. But in the 2000s and early 2010s, we really had it good. This was the age of the Judd Apatow comedy, in which the director not only delivered such modern classics as "The 40-Year-Old Virgin" and "Knocked Up," but influenced almost every comedy released during the era. Alongside the main Apatow offerings you had those which he produced such as "Anchorman: The Legend of Ron Burgundy," or those made by his collaborators, such as "Superbad." Then, there were those films that featured Apatow associates but were made without his direct involvement. The brilliant "I Love You, Man" is one example which, despite being criminally overlooked, remains one of Paul Rudd's best films.

As does 2008's "Role Models," which saw Rudd team up with Seann William Scott for a film that, like pretty much every comedy movie released at the time, was defined by the improvisational, R-rated humor that had made Apatow's films so successful. This particular movie, however, paired Apatow allies Rudd and Christopher Mintz-Plasse with former members of sketch comedy troupe The State in director David Wain and writer/actor Ken Marino.

As someone who watched "Role Models" when it came out, I simply cannot believe this movie is 18 years old. Despite being less well known than the aforementioned "Knocked Up" and its ilk, "Role Models" became instantly beloved by me and my friends upon its release. Almost two decades later, it's strange to think that an entire generation has grown up without films such as this shaping their sense of humor. If you happen to be part of that group, now's the time to acquaint yourself with this  oft-overlooked comedy. If, like me, you remember "Role Models" fondly, you'll be happy to hear it holds up.

Like so many of the best comedies of the 2000s, "Role Models" succeeds because despite being an R-rated comedy with frequent gross-out gags and unapologetically immature humor, it's also a surprisingly tender story with its heart in the right place.

Paul Rudd plays Danny who, alongside Seann William Scott's Anson Wheeler, works as a promoter for the energy drink "Minotaur." Danny feels deeply unfulfilled and has become deeply cynical as a result, spending most of his time correcting people on their speech. This causes his girlfriend, Beth (Elizabeth Banks), to break up with him, after which Danny loses it during a presentation at a high school. His freak out leads to both he and Anson being assigned to community service at Sturdy Wings, a big brother-style program where the pair become mentors to two "littles."

Anson is paired with Ronnie (Bobb'e J. Thompson) a foul-mouthed kid with no friends, while Danny is given Augie Farks (Christopher Mintz-Plasse), a teenager obsessed with his medieval LARP group. At first, Danny and Anson are merely going through the motions. But over time they develop a bond with their "littles" — one which just might melt Danny's cold, cynical heart.

The secret star of "Role Models" is Joe Lo Truglio, who plays Kuzzik, a particularly dedicated LARP-er who, if you watch the bloopers, made it almost impossible for Rudd to get through any of his scenes without breaking. But there are a ton of standout performances here, from A.D. Miles as overly earnest Sturdy Wings counsellor Martin Gary to Ken Jeong as the self-serious LARP king Argotron and Jane Lynch as a former addict turned Sturdy Wings leader.

"Role Models" hit theaters in November 2008 and grossed a solid $92.6 million on a $28 million budget. It also garnered positive reviews, earning a 77% "certified fresh" rating on Rotten Tomatoes. Dana Stevens of Slate found "Role Models" to be "strewn with enough serendipities to make it a worthy variation on the dudes-grow-up genre," while the San Francisco Chronicle's Peter Hartlaub noted how, while Seann William Scott and Paul Rudd were "from two different moviemaking cliques" they ultimately "get along fine in a Bing Crosby/David Bowie kind of way."

Meanwhile, Rafer Guzman of Newsday noted how Rudd, who co-wrote the script with David Wain, Timothy Dowling, and Ken Marino, had "clearly learned a few things working on Judd Apatow comedies." By that point, Rudd had appeared in Apatow's "The 40-Year-Old Virgin" and "Knocked Up," as well as several Apatow-produced projects including "Anchorman" and "Forgetting Sarah Marshall." As such, he was well-established as one of the most prominent figures of the 2000s R-rated comedy boom.

But The State boys certainly brought their own flair to the film and the pairing of theirs and Rudd's sensibility along with Scott's lovable knucklehead charm made for a hilarious and surprisingly heartwarming experience that deserves to be remembered as fondly as some of the more well-known comedies of the era. In 1980, Thompson crossed from Hawai’i to Tahiti by voyaging canoe using neither a compass nor modern instruments, becoming the first Native Hawaiian to do so in six centuries.

They rely on the stars, natural cues and the lessons of the late Mau Piailug, a master navigator, along a 2,400-mile route their ancestors sailed. Piailug, who was raised on Satawal in Micronesia, taught Native Hawaiians traditional navigation.

Manley said in a director’s statement: “I came to see a deep burden and ‘kuleana’ that rests upon the shoulders of Nainoa Thompson, who has spent the last 50 years of his life fulfilling a promise made to his teacher, Micronesian master navigator Mau Piailug, to prevent navigation from going extinct.”
