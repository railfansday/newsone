Qatar’s Film Committee and Watermelon Pictures Forge Partnership to Co-Produce a Slate of Films From the ‘Global South’

📅 Nov 21, 2026  |  Category: Business  |  Read Source →

Qatar’s Film Committee and Watermelon Pictures have announced a strategic partnership to develop and co-produce a slate of films “championing visionary filmmakers and compelling stories from the Global South and beyond with an emphasis on culturally authentic storytelling and broad international appeal,” they said in a statement.

The Global South is a geopolitical and economic term that broadly includes countries in Africa, Latin America and the Caribbean, most of Asia, and most of Oceania. Related Stories Behind Election Night Scenes, Fox News Launches New Multimedia Era ‘GTA 6’ Upsets Conservatives: Fox News Tells Fans to ‘Grow Up’ as Clavicular and More Manosphere Influencers Complain About Lucia

The Qatar Film Committee is an official body that is part of the Media City Qatar hub and is tasked with driving growth of the country’s entertainment industry.

Founded in April 2024 by brothers Badie Ali and Hamza Ali, Chicago-based Watermelon Pictures has gained worldwide attention in its short time in business with releases such as Rashid Masharawi’s “From Ground Zero,” Annemarie Jacir’s “Palestine 36” and Cherien Dabis’ “All That’s Left of You,” which were shortlisted for the best international feature Oscar, as well as Kaouther ben Hania’s Oscar-nominated “The Voice of Hind Rajab.”

The partnership between Qatar’s Film Committee and Watermelon was announced on the second day of the Doha Film Festival’s Industry Days, which are running from Nov. 19-21.

The plan is for the pact to spawn a slate of projects developed by Watermelon spanning comedy, television, genre and drama. Financial terms were not disclosed.

“Combining Qatar’s commitment to world-class filmmaking, growing production capabilities, and investment in the creative economy with Watermelon’s access to leading talent, established distribution infrastructure, and deep understanding of what resonates with audiences, the collaboration aims to produce commercially compelling films with high production values and international appeal,” the statement said.

The plan envisions that these projects will shoot mainly in Qatar or be structured as Qatari co-productions. They will tap into the country’s growing production ecosystem and the Qatar Screen Production Incentive rebate offering up to 50% in cash, “creating opportunities for local crews, businesses and creative talent while strengthening Qatar’s position as an international production hub,” it noted.

“Through this partnership, we want to give filmmakers the resources to tell stories rooted in their communities and bring them to audiences around the world,” Hassan Al Thawadi, the Qatari lawyer who chairs the Qatar Film Committee, said in the statement.

For Watermelon Pictures, the partnership marks a significant leap into larger-scale, mainstream production, backed by its distribution expertise.

Bayern Munich confirm Harry Kane surgery as England captain issues statement from hospital bed

📅 Nov 21, 2026  |  Category: Business  |  Read Source →

England captain Harry Kane has undergone successful surgery on an ankle ligament injury which is expected to keep him sidelined for three months.

The recently crowned Ballon d’Or winner started for Bayern Munich as they drew with Cologne on Friday evening, but was forced off after just 20 minutes.

He was caught in a late tackle by Joel Schmied as he looked to latch onto a Michael Olise cross and immediately went down clutching his lower leg. He attempted to play on, but after spending a few minutes as a virtual bystander, he signalled to the bench that he could not continue.

It was confirmed at full-time he had suffered a tear to the syndesmotic ligament in his ankle, with Bayern head coach Vincent Kompany predicting he would miss around three months.

And on Saturday morning the club confirmed he had undergone successful surgery on the injury, with the ball now rolling on a lengthy period of rehabilitation.

Kane later shared a photo of himself on the edge of a hospital bed, bearing a broad grin and brandishing two thumbs-ups despite wearing a protective boot on his left foot.

He wrote: “The highs and lows of life! Obviously very disappointed to get this injury but as always it’s an opportunity to show resilience and improvement going into 2027. I’ll be back fresh and ready to go for the business end of the season!

“‘Tough times don’t last, tough people do’ - my guy [NFL legend Julian Edelman] told me this one!

“Thank you for all the messages! Alles gut!”

Ankle injuries plagued Kane throughout his 14-year stint at Tottenham, but he has largely managed to stay fit since moving to Munich in 2023.

It remains to be seen whether Kane will be fit in time to lead England out in the Nations League quarter-finals in March 2027. The two legs of that tie will be played on March 27 and 30.

The projected three-month rehabilitation period suggests he will be back in contention in late February, and Thomas Tuchel will hope he can get back fit and firing in time to don the captain’s armband in Oslo.

Kane has been fielding plenty of well wishes. England’s official Instagram account replied to his post confirming his succesful surgery, writing in a comment: “Wishing you a speedy recovery, Harry!”

Chelsea and England forward Morgan Rogers has also joined Joshua Kimmich, Manuel Neuer and Dayot Upamecano in sending their support to the 33-year-old.

Social Security's Trump Bump-Led 2027 COLA Is the 5th Largest in 35 Years -- but There's a Dark Side to This Outsize Increase as Well

📅 Nov 21, 2026  |  Category: Business  |  Read Source →

Though Social Security checks are about to get bigger, the projected timeline for sweeping benefit cuts may have accelerated.

By Sean Williams – Nov 21, 2026 at 6:36AM EST

For Social Security’s nearly 72 million traditional beneficiaries -- retired workers, workers with disabilities, and survivors of deceased workers -- there are few, if any, announcements more anticipated than the annual cost-of-living adjustment (COLA).

Social Security's COLA is the near-annual "raise" given to beneficiaries to account for inflation (i.e., rising prices). If the price for the goods and services that seniors regularly purchase kept climbing without an adjustment in benefits, the buying power of Social Security income would steadily decline over time.

Social Security's announced 2027 COLA offers something of a mixed bag for recipients. On the one hand, it's set to deliver one of the largest raises over the last 35 years. This larger payout comes courtesy of President Donald Trump and a handful of his policies.

President Trump's policies offer a good-news/bad-news scenario for Social Security. Image source: Official White House Photo by Molly Riley.

But this outsize Trump bump isn't without its consequences. Larger payouts in the new year can make the program's precarious financial outlook that much worse.

On Oct. 14, the Social Security Administration announced that monthly payouts would increase by 3.6% in 2027. For the average retired-worker beneficiary, their monthly payout is expected to climb by $75. Meanwhile, the typical worker with a disability can expect their monthly Social Security check to rise by $59 in the new year.

A 3.6% raise is well above the norm. It marks a tie for the fifth-largest percentage increase over the last 35 years, surpassed only by COLAs of 4.1% (2006), 5.8% (2009), 5.9% (2022), and 8.7% (2023).

It's also the sixth consecutive year in which recipients have received at least a 2.5% cost-of-living adjustment. It's been 30 years (1988-1997) since benefits rose by at least 2.5% for six straight years.

While modest inflation associated with a growing U.S. economy results in a Social Security raise most years, next year's outsize COLA was pumped up by two of President Donald Trump's policies.

New from the New York Fed: "By February 2026, tariffs had contributed 2.9 pp to goods price inflation, and without them goods prices would have fallen slightly." About one quarter of every point in higher tariff rates shows up in consumer prices within one year. • A 10% across-the-board tariff yields a 2.6% rise in consumer goods prices after one year. • About two thirds of the increase comes from pricier imports, which get passed through quickly. • Another third comes from U.S. made goods, as producers face higher input costs and less import competition. This takes longer, about 6-12 months, to filter through.

To begin with, each of the last two Social Security COLAs has been influenced by Trump's tariff and trade policy. Adding duties to imports directly raises their price and can increase manufacturing costs for domestic producers.

A recently published report by three New York Federal Reserve economists writing for Liberty Street Economics ("How Fast Do Tariffs Pass Through into Consumer Prices?") found that Trump's 2025 tariffs increased goods price inflation by an estimated 2.9 percentage points by February 2026. Without tariffs, the NY Fed economists estimate that goods prices would have otherwise fallen.

The other major policy contributing to Social Security's 2027 Trump bump is the Iran war. Closing the Strait of Hormuz has resulted in the largest energy supply disruption in modern history, sending gas and diesel prices notably higher.

With Trumpflation (inflation driven by Trump's policies) becoming entrenched in the U.S. economy, it's quite possible that these policies provide a third consecutive year with a Trump bump in 2028.

Unfortunately, outsize Social Security COLAs come at a potentially steep cost to America's leading retirement program.

Every year since 1985, the Social Security Board of Trustees Report has warned of a long-term unfunded obligation. In simpler terms, the projected income to be collected in the 75 years following the release of a report is expected to be insufficient to cover outlays (primarily benefits, but also the administrative costs to oversee Social Security). As of 2026, this long-term unfunded obligation had ballooned to $29.3 trillion.

However, this isn't the most pressing concern for the program or its nearly 72 million traditional beneficiaries.

According to the latest Trustees Report, the Old-Age and Survivors Insurance trust fund (OASI) is forecast to exhaust its asset reserves by the fourth quarter of 2032. The OASI's asset reserves represent the excess income collected since inception that is invested in special-issue, interest-bearing government bonds, as required by law.

US Old-Age and Survivors Insurance Trust Fund Assets at End of Year data by YCharts.

The positive news is that Social Security isn't going bankrupt and is in no danger of halting benefits. More than 91% of the program's income is collected through the 12.4% payroll tax on earned income (wages and salaries, but not investment income). As long as Americans keep working, the payroll tax will continue generating income for disbursement to eligible beneficiaries... even if the OASI's asset reserves are depleted.

But just because Social Security isn't going bankrupt, it doesn't mean everything is peachy. If the OASI's asset reserves run dry over the next six years, as predicted, it would indicate that the current payout schedule, inclusive of annual COLAs, isn't sustainable. Sweeping benefit cuts of up to 22% may be necessary for retired workers and survivors of deceased workers.

The concern is that Social Security's Trump bump may have materially altered the trajectory to the OASI's asset reserve insolvency.

When the Trustees Report issues its short- and long-term outlooks, it takes into account a long list of variables, including birth rates, death rates, net migration rates, and even annual COLAs. The Trustees assume that annual COLAs will be modest -- but as we just saw, a 3.6% annual raise is anything but average.

Social Security's outsize 2027 raise risks draining the OASI even faster than initially forecast.

President Trump's "Big, Beautiful Bill" already moved up the projected OASI asset reserve insolvency timeline by one quarter (from the first quarter of 2033 to the fourth quarter of 2032). It's entirely possible the 2027 Trump bump further accelerates the timeline to sweeping benefit cuts.

Rosalía Takes the Statement Hat Trend to the Skies

📅 Nov 21, 2026  |  Category: Business  |  Read Source →

Is it a bird? Is it a plane? No, it’s Rosalía, wearing one of the most spectacular headpieces of the season.

On Friday night, the Spanish singer arrived at the LOS40 Music Awards in Barcelona with an enormous white dove perched above her head, its wings dramatically outstretched as though caught mid-flight. The sculptural creation came courtesy of Robert Wun’s fall 2026 couture collection, and was paired with a custom ivory Maison Margiela gown, featuring a plunging halter neckline and delicate pleating. With her dark hair pulled back and her bleached blonde halo, the avian accessory was given plenty of room to spread its wings.

It’s a particularly pertinent choice of chapeau for the singer, whose Lux era has been steeped in religious imagery and otherworldly fashion. The dove, of course, is a traditional symbol of peace and the Holy Spirit. Rosalía has already experimented with all manner of heavenly headwear, from the Margiela nun-inspired headpiece on her album cover to the theatrical hats worn throughout her Lux tour. This might be her most divine accessory yet.

As it happens, the dove has been rather busy lately. Just days earlier, Whitney Peak wore a cobalt-blue gown from the same Robert Wun collection to the New York premiere of The Hunger Games: Sunrise on the Reaping. Her dress featured two sculptural white birds appearing to suspend the fabric in midair. Wun, whose fantastical couture designs regularly blur the line between fashion, costume, and sculpture, is becoming something of a go-to for stars seeking genuinely captivating red-carpet creations.

Consider Elle Fanning, who recently embraced the gloriously eccentric style of her Hunger Games character Effie Trinket in a pink Robert Wun couture gown, complete with a matching pillbox hat topped by a miniature ballerina. Fanning has also worn a saucy red satin creation by British milliner Rachel Trevor-Morgan.

And she’s in excellent company. Dua Lipa recently championed the artsy chapeau in a frayed Chanel couture number, while Anya Taylor-Joy opted for a gigantic pink Jacquemus hat that framed her head like a halo. Even winter’s more practical accessories have taken a more eccentric turn: Hunter Schafer has been championing furry, archival Undercover headgear, while Taylor Russell has collaborated with Ilana Blumberg on some particularly fabulous fuzzy hats.

For Wun, the theatricality is very much the point. His fall 2026 couture collection, titled Child's Play, explored childhood and fairytales through a procession of fantastical characters, from Snow White to the Big Bad Wolf. Working with milliner Awon Golding, the designer produced a series of elaborate hats and sculptural headpieces that seemed to defy the laws of gravity.

After seasons of slicked-back buns and minimalist accessories, fashion’s most adventurous dressers are evidently ready for something a little more playful. The message? There’s no such thing as too much drama up top. And as Rosalía has demonstrated, sometimes the best accessories have a life of their own—and take flight.
