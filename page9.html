Grand Theft Auto 6 has a wide variety of encounters that can crop up across its central locales. While most are tied to a specific area or mission, some of the game’s most memorable are those that can occur anywhere within a given zone, adding tons of random madness to Leonida while free-roaming.

Tales of streakers were some of the first leaks to breach GTA 6, and those on the hunt may be a bit disappointed.

Many of these quests are direct references to other pieces of crime fiction, from subtle hints to straight-up placing the protagonists in the situation from the films. One such quest sees either Jason or Lucia held at gunpoint by a stand-in for Tom Cruise’s character from Collateral. If you want to trigger this event and get out alive, make sure to read on!

This encounter is a standout, not appearing anywhere on the map and not being accessible until midway into Chapter 3. Once you’ve gained easy access to Vice City and start heading toward Vice Beach, sitting in any car alone for an extended period gives a suited stranger a chance to enter the back seat suddenly.

He’ll proceed to stick up the protagonist, forcing them to start driving. From here on, failure to comply with his orders will lead to him shooting the player, not enough to kill outright, but enough to push you over the edge if you’re already wounded.

Your next task is to run a red light, any red light you can find. Thankfully, the agent is rather patient as long as he can tell you’re looking for a light or waiting for one to change. Once it’s clear you can follow instructions, he’ll mark an area near the park in Vice Beach for you to drive to, a trek depending on where you started the mission.

Make sure you don’t slow down too much or try to turn on the radio. Your passenger wants a nice, quiet ride to his destination. Once you arrive, things change fast, giving you a split-second decision.

Once you arrive at the location, the agent will explain that this entire charade was to put the protagonist’s face and car on a police suspect database for running that red light. Now that you’ve made it to his destination, we’re about to be in real hot water, as he guns down a nearby guard protecting a small drug lab.

He insists you leave, but if you’re feeling spiteful or profit-focused, you can easily follow him inside. Without a gun to your head, he’s as easy to take down as any other enemy, plus he’ll have already cleared the guards inside and outside the lab.

Make sure to keep your car running by double-tapping your exit button, and quickly pocket everything he stood to gain before heading out. He wasn’t kidding, the cops are already on their way, so whether you’re willing to snag some for yourself or just want to hightail it, make sure you leave fast.

I’d fully recommend hitting the lab, as it’s easily a 10k payout for very little effort. There are also a few handy guns to pocket if you need the hardware. The Jeep parked outside sells for a pretty penny as well, giving you an excuse to swing by Randy and continue his questline.

Mauricio Pochettino Slams 2 Referee Mistakes in Liverpool 4-0 Man Utd

📅 Nov 23, 2026  |  Category: Sports  |  Read Source →

New Manchester United manager Mauricio Pochettino is convinced the referee made two huge mistakes in his side's 4-0 defeat to Liverpool on Sunday afternoon.

All week, the former USMNT boss had been warned about just how difficult the task of taking over a Red Devils side currently languishing in the Premier League drop zone would be. Their latest defeat means United have now won just one of their opening 11 league matches of the season, while Pochettino has endured the worst start of any Man United manager in 100 years.

Pochettino's honeymoon period lasted just 10 minutes at Anfield, as Dominik Szoboszlai added insult to injury by smashing home the resulting free-kick following Leny Yoro's controversial red card for denying Alexander Isak a clear goalscoring opportunity.

That flashpoint has been the main talking point since the final whistle, with a VAR expert weighing in on the dismissal. However, Pochettino remains convinced his side were on the wrong end of another major decision from Sam Barrott, before the Reds ran riot with further goals from a resurgent Florian Wirtz, Rio Ngumoha and Victor Munoz.

The Argentine has called the decision to send Yoro off an 'unfair situation', which he brought up with the officials at full-time at Anfield. However, he was also quick to mention another incident that many supporters may have forgotten about.

Before Szoboszlai's opener, inside the first five minutes, Bryan Mbeumo was bundled over in the box after battling shoulder to shoulder with Jeremy Jacquet - and Pochettino believes there was enough contact for a penalty to be given. He said (watch his interview and the incident below):

"If you go two minutes before or one minute and a half before, the situation of Jacquet and Mbeumo, if you compare that, tell me how we are going to talk now about the team? Yes, of course, it's painful because it's 4-0 in the end and you lose the game, like we play more than 80 minutes with 10 men in an unfair...

"Of course, we need to be critical, but also the penalty to Mbeumo, that the action maybe was a mistake also of Liverpool, because we can make a mistake, no? But we were punished badly, and they were not punished. That is the truth."

It has since been confirmed that VAR did review the incident, but officials are believed to have deemed there was insufficient contact to warrant sending Barrott to the pitchside monitor or overturning the on-field decision themselves.

While sections of the United support, along with Pochettino, feel hard done by, another interpretation is that the Cameroon international may have simply kicked his own leg rather than been fouled, with Jacquet using his pace to get across him and block his route to goal.

There were some damning statistics that came out of Sunday's rout, and perhaps none summarised United's disastrous start to the season better than the fact they've conceded more goals than any other team in European football, having now shipped - or rather, 'invited' - 34 goals in all competitions.

The Argentine quickly learned the mess he's tasked with cleaning up.

On top of that, they now sit 19th in the Premier League table, just a point above rock bottom. There was no new manager bounce like there was with Michael Carrick, and how Pochettino will get a tune out of the current squad is anyone's guess - he has nine games to navigate with an average of three days rest each time before the January window opens.

But even then, will the top brass at Old Trafford give him the players he wants or deserves to work miracles? His predecessors would have something to say about that.

I discovered Daniel Coyle through this episode of Dr. Amantha Imber’s great podcast. He overflows with useful ideas. One of his books, The Talent Code, explores how top performers excel. In a video I like, he summarizes some of its most interesting takeaways.

In his conversation with Imber, Coyle offers practical ways to move beyond small talk: Ask better questions, try small community rituals, and notice what gives people energy.

3. Curious Minds at Work

Host: Gayle Allen
Standout episode: David Epstein on the Power of Limits

David Epstein references Dr. Seuss and Apple legend Tony Fadell. His anecdotes spotlight historical figures and contemporary ones. In this interview he draws on themes from his latest book, Inside the Box: How Constraints Make Us Better, to explain how limits help with creativity.

Allen is a superb interviewer who asks concise questions and avoids putting herself at the center of the conversation. Subscribe to her podcast for interviews with top contemporary nonfiction writers.

Notable quote: “Useful constraints . . . force you to clarify priorities.” —David Epstein

4. The Happiness Lab

Host: Dr. Laurie Santos
Standout episode: How to Stop Work From Taking Over Your Life

I struggle to stop working sometimes, so this episode gave me a lot to ponder. I liked psychologist Guy Winch’s suggestion to treat dreaded tasks like pebbles in our shoes and remove them right away. Thinking of them as nuisances may be helpful in combating procrastination. The episode also suggests planning restorative micro-breaks and building an end-of-work ritual.

Notable quote: “We’re taking the 15-minute task, say, 15 minutes of unpleasantness, and we’re smearing it over an entire week.” —Guy Winch

5. The Knowledge Project

Host: Shane Parrish

Like Ezra Klein’s, Parrish’s podcast is excellent partly because of the host’s expertise and partly because he picks great guests. Morgan Housel, the interviewee on this episode, is an entertaining storyteller whose first and third books are about the psychology of money. I liked his second most: Same as Ever: A Guide to What Never Changes.

6. Acquired

Hosts: Ben Gilbert and David Rosenthal
Standout episode: Trader Joe’s

Each episode of Acquired explores a single company. Episodes are three to four hours long. The hosts say they spend 100-plus hours doing deep research on each of the eight episodes they publish per year.

The depth and quality of their research, reporting, and storytelling have drawn in more than a million listeners. Sponsors pay $500,000 and up, according to a profile of Acquired in The New York Times. There’s even a Harvard Business School case study about their rise.

Their marathon episodes challenge the idea that consumers want only short media. This Trader Joe’s episode lasts three hours and 26 minutes. That’s longer than many shoppers spend in the store all year.

Listening to Acquired feels like reading an interesting book. The hosts weave lively details about each company they cover into a conversational narrative. Their sharp analysis helps explain how successful ventures stand out.

They detail why Trader Joe’s avoids coupons, ads, and sales; how its founder scouted locations for his first stores and overcame early cash-flow problems; and how the company avoided risky expansions overseas and online.
