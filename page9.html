West Ham Taty Transfer Talks Could Open Door To Goalkeeper Deal

According to a number of reports, River Plate are exploring a move for Valentín Castellanos, with an initial loan proposal understood to be one of the options being discussed. The Argentine giants are believed to be assessing whether a temporary agreement could be structured with West Ham contributing towards a portion of the forward’s wages to help make the deal financially viable.

The Argentinian side are also open to exploring a deal in which they would acquire Castellanos while West Ham retain a percentage of his future transfer rights, reducing the upfront fee required.

While the prospect of regular football in South America may appeal to the player, any negotiations are unlikely to be straightforward.

A Taty transfer to River Plate could result in Beltran moving in the opposite direction.

While West Ham are understood to be open to parting ways with Castellanos, the club would want a significantly higher transfer fee than the figures currently being considered by River Plate, creating a sizeable gap between the two clubs’ positions.

Our trusted transfer source suggests it’s unlikely that the Argentinians can afford the striker, as teams over there don’t have any money, yet reaffirmed the striker is very much up for sale.

From West Ham’s perspective, agreeing to subsidise wages while also accepting a heavily discounted transfer fee makes little financial sense unless there are performance-related add-ons or future sell-on clauses that could bridge the gap.

The situation remains at an early stage and there is no indication that a breakthrough is imminent. However, River Plate’s interest appears genuine, and negotiations could continue if the Argentine club can move closer to West Ham’s expectations.

There are also suggestions that Santiago Beltrán, as reported by Gonzo yesterday, is of interest to West Ham, which opens up the possibility of the goalkeeper being included in any deal.

For now, the ball is firmly in River Plate’s court. If they want to bring Castellanos back to South American football, a significantly improved proposal will be required before West Ham consider doing business.

Destiny brought Mauricio Pochettino to Man United - but one huge question remains

📅 Nov 12, 2026  |  Category: Sports  |  Read Source →

The Argentine has come close to becoming Manchester United manager on three separate prior occasions. Now the stars appear to have finally aligned, but is it too late for either the man or the club?

On Sunday evening, Mauricio Pochettino got the call he had been waiting for.

He and long-time assistant Jesus Perez had frequently asked about Manchester United over the last decade. They would often contact friends in the game who were connected to Old Trafford, looking to find out what was happening, whether there could be an opening.

Pochettino came very close to taking one of those openings on at least three occasions: 2018, 2021 and 2024. Now, elements might finally have aligned.

It is a call that others at Old Trafford have long waited for, too. Back when they had a much publicised dinner in 2016, Sir Alex Ferguson essentially told Pochettino he should be the United manager. The Argentine was enthralled at that meeting by stories from one of the game’s great glory eras, and a sense of magic he had felt just standing in Old Trafford had been deepened.

Pochettino had been especially taken by Ferguson’s account of rebuilding United from the 1980s, initially drawing comparisons with what he was doing at Tottenham Hotspur. Now, he’s set to take on that exact same job, if one that has become so much bigger than in 2016. It is arguably even bigger than in 1986.

Pochettino, however, is fired by the idea of being the man to finally fix United. It has become one of the great managerial quests. The United hierarchy have ultimately gone for him because of that personality, and his standing. They don’t want a coach who hasn’t worked at this level. They want someone ready. Both the Glazers and Sir Jim Ratcliffe can sense that, since the two sides of the United ownership have known Pochettino for years.

If this is an appointment that always felt destined, though, that does raise one big question. Is this the right moment for it? Is it like Jose Mourinho, in that it came too late? Is it typical of modern United in that sense? Is it the same old “magical thinking” of hoping a famous name saves them, when the game has changed and the actual problems are now so structural?

Pochettino probably should have gone to United in 2016 or 2018, when he was at a peak in terms of being one of the most exciting coaches in football. The club did consider a move in the summer of 2016, but sources say an exit clause was allowed to expire. How different things could have been.

Old Trafford insiders are now talking about the value of “a name”, but the hierarchy did pass on Pochettino in 2024, when they initially considered sacking Erik ten Hag before ultimately deciding to stick with the Dutchman. His interview wasn’t seen as convincing as that of Thomas Tuchel or Roberto De Zerbi. Those who know him also say he is closer to being “a manager” now, in terms of a wider outlook, rather than the on-the-grass coach that so drove Spurs.

The very fact United are negotiating to pry him from the United States national team, without wishing to be disrespectful, also illustrates how his stock has recently dropped. Some close to Pochettino would nevertheless put that down to necessary choices at certain moments. Taking charge of the hosts of the biggest World Cup ever, that had so much pomp around it, was too enticing. Before that, he probably should have pushed to go to United or Real Madrid in 2018 but he was convinced he could do something special with Spurs. He’d put too much into it.

And that does point to one of the other great twists of fate in modern football, where Pochettino’s profile could be completely different. His Tottenham team were genuinely one of the best in Europe over 2016-18, and one of the great Premier League teams. To achieve 86 points in 2016-17, on what was then only the sixth highest wage bill in English football, was genuine alchemy. It was immense overperformance, fostered by a side that could just overrun you.

We also now know that the wage bill of the eventual champions for that season, Chelsea, had in part come from signings that had been secured by payments against the rules. The punishment from that guilty verdict still looks weak. There is a strong argument that Chelsea should have been stripped of the 2015 and 2017 titles. Pochettino himself said as much in a wide-ranging interview with The Independent and other media in September: “I’ve said that, that we should win one title, one Premier League. We should have been awarded it… for sure, we deserve to be champions for that year.”

Had that justice been done, Pochettino would not just be a “name” who created an exciting Spurs once. He would be an English champion, and one responsible for one of the most historic titles given the much wealthier competition. A great institution would have won just their third title, and first in 56 years.

Both he and Spurs could have had their futures transformed, as well as their place in history. It would have been a modern equivalent of what Ferguson himself did with Aberdeen.

Now, he wants to follow Ferguson in a different way. This is the job he has been waiting for.

A certain apathy towards the US job was displayed in that September interview when he spoke about how his contract didn’t necessarily mean he’d stay the full term.

For all of that, and how their World Cup campaign ended in the chaos of Donald Trump’s disruptive interference in the Falorin Balogun case, there were very impressive spells when the US really looked like a classic Pochettino team. The way they ripped into Paraguay was reminiscent of Spurs 2016-18, right up to some of the flourishes. And that was in a much more limited and restricted environment than a club team.

This is the other thing about just taking the job, too. Regardless of United’s current problems, and perceptions of where Pochettino is in his career, he is energised by this. He has that bite. As one figure who knows him well says, “he has got the IP, he has got the brand, he has got the experience”.

Having already worked for Chelsea and Paris Saint-Germain, he’s been at big clubs with demanding owners. He has the mettle for that. And he now looks set to have the big job he’s always wanted.

Scout Motors has 212,000 vehicle reservations as $2 billion South Carolina plant nears completion

📅 2026-11-12  |  Category: Business  |  Read Source →

BLYTHEWOOD, S.C. — Electric vehicle startup Scout Motors has notched roughly 212,000 reservations for its upcoming EVs as the company nears completion of its new $2 billion plant in South Carolina, CEO Scott Keogh told CNBC.

The reservations are for the Volkswagen-backed company's Terra pickup truck and Traveler SUV, which are expected to start full production next year before deliveries begin to customers in 2028.

Reservations do not necessarily translate to sales, as the U.S. automotive industry has learned during the past decade, especially with highly anticipated all-electric trucks such as the Ford F-150 Lightning, Tesla Cybertruck and others that garnered strong interest that didn't lead to meaningful numbers of purchases.

Keogh said he expects Scout could roughly double those reservations by the time customer production begins, but he said the more important thing is to convert early interest into actual sales as production nears.

"It's been a consistent drumbeat," Keogh said during an interview at Scout's plant site outside Columbia, South Carolina. "I think we could easily be up over 400,000 with reservations without doing too much effort. The big thing for us is how much of the initial production can we 100% presell."

Keogh said achieving an order book of 12-to-18 months "would be great" and something he thinks the company can do before production. Scout on Thursday said construction is "in its final phase."

Scout's vehicles will be offered as all-electric versions as well as electric hybrid variants, known as extended-range electric vehicles, or EREVs, that include an engine as a backup generator for the electric motors and batteries.

Scout, a former American vehicle brand from 1961 to 1980, was expected to exclusively offer EVs in a bid for the German automaker to expand its presence in the U.S. However, slower-than-expected adoption of EVs and higher costs led the company to decide to offer EREVs as well.

Keogh said the EREVs and SUV are dominating reservations thus far, with roughly 87% choosing EREV models and 75% being SUVs.

Given the interest signaled by reservations, Keogh said the company is expected to launch with the EREVs first, followed by the EVs. He also said the company plans to launch with mid-level models first, a deviation from many companies that begin with their highest-priced models.

"The No.1 thing for me is I want to go get the best volume as soon as possible," Keogh said. "In my mind, it's how quick can you get to 100,000 units? It's at 100,000 units where you can start to punch through."

His comments come months after Rivian Automotive received criticism online for launching its R2 SUV with its highest-priced models first, despite that being a common practice across the automotive industry.

Keogh said Scout's South Carolina plant is about 85% complete, but supporting infrastructure and a $300 million supplier park continue to be installed.

Reservations for Scout's vehicles initially opened in October 2024, when it revealed the Traveler and Terra, which feature sleek, yet rugged, exterior appearances that pay some homage to the brand's historic models.

2 Memory Stocks to Buy and Hold Through 2030

📅 Nov 12, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Memory chip stocks were hot in 2026, but they will continue to be top picks through 2030.

Memory stocks have had quite the year in 2026. Two of the best performers are Sandisk (SNDK +13.69%) and Micron (MU +11.89%), which are each among the top five best-performing stocks in the S&P 500 (^GSPC +1.31%) this year.

Sandisk holds second place, rising around 270%, while Micron has risen about 150%. However, I think both of these stocks are due for more over the next few years, and all investors have to do is listen to what they're saying.

I think these two will be great stocks to buy and hold over the next few years, and could deliver monster returns.

Memory chips are required for many devices to function. Long-term data storage utilizes NAND memory, such as in solid-state drives (SSDs). Computing units need DRAM memory to function, and these are being deployed heavily in data centers for computing power.

While memory chips are necessary, there isn't much to differentiate one memory chip from another. With no competitive advantage, memory chips basically became a commodity. As a result, pricing depends heavily on supply and demand. This makes memory chips a cyclical industry, and investors have historically given companies in these fields deep discounts to avoid losing vast sums of money when the cycle turns.

That's what the market is betting will happen for Micron and Sandisk. With the AI build-out in full force and consuming all available memory, demand for chip production capacity for their products has never been higher. With production capacity sold out, prices have also skyrocketed. This has led to ludicrous growth rates for each company, but the market isn't valuing them fully because of their cyclical nature.

For reference, the S&P 500 trades at 19 times forward earnings, so if both stocks just had a market-average valuation, they would rise nearly 5 times in value. But because the market is betting on a downturn, they're cheap.

Both of these companies are taking steps to reduce the cyclical nature of their businesses. Instead of selling products on the open market, both are now entering into long-term agreements with their clients. Sandisk calls this its "New Business Model" (NBM), and a large chunk of its revenue is coming from these agreements. Micron is doing the same thing and has locked in approximately 35% of revenue through 2030 with these types of contracts.

Why is this a big deal? It raises the floor for the companies. Instead of dealing with a significant pricing decline as demand falls when the AI build-out ends, there are floor pricing levels with their clients that keep the business running well. So, Micron and Sandisk may not see the massive pricing highs they're seeing now ever again, but they won't experience the lows that result in the stocks not being highly valued. This means the market should reprice the stock accordingly, which could lead to massive upside in the near future.

Furthermore, the industry has been sold out for many years, and Micron told investors that market conditions in 2027 and 2028 will likely be tighter than in 2026. The reality is that the memory space will be tied up for multiple years, likely through 2030, and these two stocks will make for excellent investments during that time. There's a good chance memory stocks will become a hot commodity sometime in the near future, and that makes now the perfect time to load up on them.

Richard Avedon, Fashion Guru, Does Old Age

📅 Nov 12, 2026  |  Category: Entertainment  |  Read Source →

A moving, incisive photography exhibition in Toronto and Montreal explores the way of all flesh.

Left: Richard Avedon, Gloria Swanson, actor, New York, September 4, 1980, gelatin silver print. Center: Richard Avedon, Jacob Israel Avedon, father of Richard Avedon, Sarasota, Florida, October 7, 1969, gelatin silver print. Right: Richard Avedon, The Duke and Duchess of Windsor, Waldorf Astoria, suite 28A, New York, April 16, 1957, gelatin silver print.

© The Richard Avedon Foundation, courtesy of the Museum of Modern Art, New York, Committee on Photography Fund

Old faces — some seem to crease and crumble as we watch — were a specialty for Richard Avedon (1923–2004), the photographer famous for his fashion work for Vogue, Harper’s Bazaar, Revlon, and Elle, magazines not known for plugging geezers. I didn’t know this, so I learned something new in my first minutes in Richard Avedon Immortal: Portraits of Aging, 1951–2004, finishing its tour next month, when it goes to The Image Center in Toronto and the Montreal Museum of Fine Arts. Avedon was a pioneer in presenting cool, stark, tough beauty — he gave us a nude Nastassja Kinski with a boa constrictor licking her earlobe, Brooke Shields in jeans, and lots of movie stars and potentates at their peaks. But old age? Shades of decadence are one thing, but decrepitude?

The Image Center is Canada’s premiere photography museum and part of Toronto Metropolitan University. The exhibition there is similar to Montreal’s edition and displays 100 Avedon portraits. Avedon died more than 20 years ago, and the show spans 50 years, so the subjects are from another era. That said, old age is timeless as well as time-limited. On view are superstars such as Gloria Swanson, Ronald Reagan, the Duke and Duchess of Windsor, and the Dalai Lama. Then there are the period pieces like Dorothy Parker, Oscar Levant, Ezra Pound, and Coco Chanel.

Elizabeth Warren’s Plan to Abolish Credit Cards

📅 Nov 12, 2026  |  Category: Business  |  Read Source →

Senate Minority Leader Chuck Schumer (D., N.Y.), and Sen. Elizabeth Warren (D., Mass.) host a roundtable on housing affordability and accessibility on Capitol Hill in Washington, D.C., January 7, 2026.

Elizabeth Warren, never short on annoyances, thinks that interest on consumer credit cards is too high.

The average rate currently hovers around 21 percent. Her solution: Pass a law to cap interest rates at 10 percent. She says she has a bill ready to go, and like-minded lawmakers have signaled their support for controlling rates.

Like any other price ceiling, an interest cap would create a shortage of the product it attempts to make more affordable. With only a tiny fraction of credit cards now offering interest at 10 percent or less, capping rates at that level would force lenders to dramatically restrict accounts — requiring borrowers to put up collateral or limiting access to people with exceptionally good credit — or eliminate them outright.

To progressives, interest on credit cards is nothing but usury — the exploitation of poor borrowers who can’t afford to pay their bills on time. Warren’s sales pitch implies that, under her bill, people would enjoy the same credit just at a lower cost. In reality, almost everyone would see their credit cards disappear.

Regrettably, Warren may also find allies for her proposal among Republicans. She claims she is open to working with President Trump, who called in 2024 for a temporary cap on credit card interest of “around 10 percent.” Senator Josh Hawley (R., Mo.), a right-wing progressive of sorts, recently joined hands with Bernie Sanders to translate that thoughtless musing into legislation.

Interest rates are not some arbitrary number. They are the price for lenders to provide liquidity on the promise, but never the certainty, that it will be repaid. To issue loans through a credit card, banks must first obtain the money by borrowing it themselves, either from depositors or other financial institutions. Interest must be paid on that debt and passed through to cardholders.

It’s true that rates on credit cards, as well as other consumer loans, are typically much higher than those for mortgages and businesses. That is because personal credit is one of the riskiest kinds to extend. Unlike secured loans, repayment depends wholly on borrowers’ creditworthiness. That often fails to hold up: 13 percent of credit-card balances are seriously past due, and approximately 5 percent of debt is never repaid. Higher interest rates exist to compensate for these losses.

Rates also vary based on individual creditworthiness. Borrowers with poor credit history, or people just starting to build their history after leaving childhood or moving to the country, have the most to lose from interest caps. They must accept costlier rates to obtain any credit whatsoever, and if lenders can’t adjust for their added risk, they will simply be cut off. Those in need of funds will instead have to turn to pawnshops or illegal loan sharks.

The credit card industry could always use more competition to keep interest rates at their fair market level. Ironically, the Dodd-Frank Act that Elizabeth Warren championed likely reduced the availability of consumer credit by increasing lenders’ regulatory burden. Warren also despises credit reporting agencies for tracking borrowers’ repayment histories, even though they replaced a far more discretionary and intrusive system in which bankers evaluated applicants’ personal reputations. For someone so appalled by alleged discrimination in finance, she should love objective credit reports.

By and large, Americans like their credit cards and wish to keep them. In trying to rescue consumers from their own financial decisions, Warren would deprive them of their plastic and consign them to infinitely more treacherous alternatives.

The Fight Over Obamacare’s Phantom Enrollees

📅 Nov 12, 2026  |  Category: Health  |  Read Source →

The Trump administration is right to take on the problem of improper enrollment.

Administrator for the Centers for Medicare and Medicaid Services Dr. Mehmet Oz speaks during a press conference on anti-fraud initiatives at the White House campus in Washington, D.C., September 22, 2026.

More than a dozen Democratic senators are criticizing the Trump administration’s efforts to address the problem of phantom enrollments in Obamacare. In a letter to insurers, the senators accuse the companies of “colluding” with the administration to terminate coverage for more than 760,000 enrollees. Their criticism gets insurers’ financial incentives backward and overlooks the extensive safeguards the Centers for Medicare and Medicaid Services (CMS) employed before terminating these highly suspicious enrollments.

Insurers have little incentive to drop phantom enrollees. For years, insurers have been the biggest beneficiaries of the expanded Obamacare subsidies — reaping tens of billions of dollars in higher subsidies. By making millions of plans free to consumers, Democrats created powerful incentives for improper and phantom enrollment. Given these incentives, the administration was right to scrutinize suspicious enrollments.

An Intellectual History of California’s Proposition 40

📅 Nov 12, 2026  |  Category: Other  |  Read Source →

How a French academic research project and an elite public university nearly took down the Golden State’s venture capital economy with a wealth tax

People attend a rally in support of Proposition 40 and in opposition to Propositions 41 and 42 ahead of the midterm elections in Los Angeles, Calif., October 5, 2026.

On November 3, California voters narrowly rejected Proposition 40, a 5 percent tax on the net worth of the state’s billionaires. That outcome was not assured. You Can Buy T-Shirts Glorifying Mass Shootings Almost Anywhere Online

📅 Nov 12, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

On the day he carried out the Columbine High School massacre, 18-year-old shooter Eric Harris wore a white T-shirt with the phrase “Natural Selection” printed on it in black lettering. His fellow assailant, 17-year-old Dylan Klebold, wore a black T-shirt with the word “Wrath” printed in red across the chest.

Ever since, this apparel has been closely linked with mass murder, part of an ever-growing mosaic of niche allusions to deadly attacks on schools, places of worship, government buildings, and social gathering spaces. While you might not think twice about a person wearing one of the shirts Harris and Klebold chose for their assault on their classmates, let alone recognize the reference, there’s an online community that revels in this aesthetic, using it as a means to valorize and motivate nihilistic violence.

According to a report publishing later this week from the Institute for Strategic Dialogue, a research organization that analyzes extremism and online threats, statement shirts associated with such horrific acts are widely available from at least half a dozen internet retailers.

ISD found sellers peddling “Wrath” and “Natural Selection” tees modeled after the Columbine shirts on TikTok Shop, Redbubble, Temu, Etsy, AliExpress, and Walmart. Shirts with the slogan “Humanity Is Overrated”—like the one worn by a teen gunman who killed nine people at his high school in Finland in 2007—turned up on AliExpress, Temu, and the TikTok Shop, according to the report.

In a statement, a representative for TikTok US said: “Discriminatory and offensive products are prohibited from sale on TikTok Shop and will be removed.” A representative for Redbubble said that the marketplace prohibits “content that glorifies or trivializes violence” and that it had removed listings for “Wrath” and “Natural Selection” shirts flagged by WIRED.

None of the other retailers immediately replied to a request for comment.

Katherine Keneally, ISD’s senior director of threat analysis and prevention, tells WIRED that these clothes are promoted by a cohort known as the True Crime Community, or TCC. Not to be confused with fans of podcasts and documentaries about unsolved or unusual crimes, the TCC “is a predominantly online fandom that is rooted in an obsession with mass killers, regardless of the mass killer’s motivation,” Keneally says.

The TCC’s fascination with the lore around specific killers can lead to dissection of details as minor as what they had for breakfast on the morning of their attack, Keneally says. But the statement shirts offer a convenient way to covertly endorse such massacres, with most people outside the in-group none the wiser.

“What we see from attackers is that they are often trying, in many ways, to mirror previous attackers, but putting their own sort of spin on it,” Keneally says. The shirts are used to “signal interest and glorification of the violence, and also can serve as a sign of someone's plans to engage in something, engage in some sort of attack,” she adds. The ISD’s report cites the cases of a 13-year-old arrested in South Africa last month after allegedly stabbing a 6-year-old while wearing a “Wrath” shirt and an 18-year-old accused of fatally shooting two people in a California library in June, who was apprehended wearing a “Natural Selection” tee.

The ISD analysis of the sale of shirts connected to past shootings is part of a planned series by the nonprofit covering various warning signs of future attacks. The idea is to give parents, teachers, and law enforcement more guidance in understanding when a young person may have intent to harm others, particularly because “within the TCC, we are seeing predominantly youth and minors being involved,” Keneally says.

Adolescents aren’t just engaging with this content in “the darker corners of the internet,” Keneally says. “It's happening very openly across mainstream and fringe platforms.” The prevalence of the clothing across an array of online shops is an indication of how the TCC’s extremist messaging flies under the radar.

Meanwhile, as ISD revealed in its research, younger affiliates of this diffuse collective are trading tips on how to disguise the meaning of paraphernalia tied to mass shooter fandom. One screenshot in the report shows a TikTok user asking their followers, “How do y’all convince your parents to buy the natural selection shirt,” with commenters advising that they pretend “Natural Selection” is the name of a movie, a video game, or a brand. One person replied: “My parents don’t know what tcc is so i can buy it without any problems.”

TCC enthusiasts have also made it clear in their product reviews of the T-shirts that they admire perpetrators of deadly gun violence. In their five-star feedback on a “Wrath” shirt, a customer wrote “we love dylan klebold!” Another attached an anime-style cartoon of Eric Harris in the “Natural Selection” shirt on their review of same, noting that they were hiding it from their mom but that it was “so cute” and “good quality too.” A third person ominously noted that they would use their “Wrath” shirt “to become a bit more popular at school.”

Keneally acknowledges that there’s a “fine line” to consider with clothing that may suggest an unhealthy fixation on infamous killers, since even the best-known examples are not offensive or illegal in themselves. Context also matters: The TCC has an affinity for the German industrial band KMFDM because they were a favorite band of the Columbine shooters, but a fan wearing a KMFDM shirt could have no idea of the connection.

While a teen’s purchase of the most flagrant TCC shirt doesn’t help them commit an attack or necessarily indicate the planning of real-world violence, Keneally says, adults should weigh it against other factors. “If they have access to weapons or maybe another student has heard them talk about committing violence, then it can help serve as a warning sign and provide an opportunity for intervention,” she says. And wearing the shirt at all communicates a likely need for mental health support.

As for the retailers that facilitate the sale of this apparel, Keneally believes that if they adequately enforced their established safety policies, much of it would be taken down. The clothes are something that youths seek out to be “a part of” the True Crime Community and fuel this toxic subculture, she says. “It shouldn't be that easy to access.”

Why Engineering Productivity Metrics Keep Misleading CTOs

📅 Nov 12, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Elton Chan is the Co-Founder of Second Talent, a solution that connects global tech leaders with AI-native engineering talent across Asia.

​Engineering teams have more ways to measure developer activity than ever. CTOs can track pull requests, commits, deployment frequency, review times and AI tool usage.

But a busy dashboard can still leave one question unanswered: Is the team delivering better software, or producing more work that someone else has to process?​

I’ve seen how AI coding tools can help engineers produce a first version faster, but the time saved during implementation can get absorbed by code reviews, test updates and integration issues. Even if the team appears more productive when measured by code output, the time it takes to move a change from development to a reliable production release often hasn’t improved.

To solve this, engineering leaders need to measure the entire delivery process, not just the speed of individual tasks.

​A team can write more code and still ship features at the same pace. Developers might open twice as many pull requests, only for reviewers to spend more time checking them. More code enters the pipeline, but the team still has to test it, integrate it and deal with whatever breaks.

The 2025 DORA report found AI adoption is associated with higher software delivery throughput, while also finding a negative relationship with delivery stability.

For a CTO, the practical question is what happens between writing code and getting a change safely into production. If implementation speeds up but testing, review or integration falls behind, the team may be generating work faster without delivering proportionally more value.

Lines of code, commit counts and tickets completed are easy to count. They are also easy to misread.

A developer who splits one change into 10 commits can look more active than someone who delivers the same change in two. A team can close more tickets by breaking work into smaller pieces without improving its delivery rate.

The SPACE framework offers a broader approach, looking at satisfaction and well-being, performance, activity, communication and collaboration, and efficiency and flow. Its central point is straightforward: No single metric captures developer productivity.

CTOs do not need to measure everything in the framework, but they should avoid drawing conclusions from one number in isolation. A rise in completed tickets means little without knowing whether the work reached users, met quality expectations and reduced the problems the team was hired to solve.

A pull request waiting three days for review is a different problem from a feature waiting three days for a product decision. A test environment that fails unpredictably creates a different delay from code that repeatedly needs rework.

Yet a dashboard showing that delivery has slowed down may not tell a CTO which of these problems is responsible.

Look at where work waits and where it has to be repeated. Review wait times can expose a shortage of reviewer capacity, while rework and failed changes can point to testing or quality problems. Long lead times between a task starting and reaching production can reveal delays across several teams.

The response should follow the evidence. Hiring more engineers will not clear a review queue if nobody has time to review. Adding another testing tool will not fix a team that receives changing requirements halfway through implementation.

AI tools can shorten implementation time, but that saving needs to be assessed alongside the work that follows. If developers spend less time writing code and more time checking generated output, resolving integration problems or fixing regressions, measuring coding speed alone gives CTOs an incomplete picture.

The 2025 DORA research finding that AI adoption is associated with higher throughput but also with lower delivery stability offers a useful starting point. Leaders evaluating their own AI investment need to examine both sides rather than relying on adoption rates or developers’ reported time savings.

To do so, compare delivery before and after introducing a tool. Look at how long changes take to reach production, how often they cause problems, how much rework they generate and whether the team is delivering outcomes that users need.

Then, ask engineers where the time saved has gone. Their answers can help explain changes in the metrics.

If implementation time falls while review queues and rework grow, the next investment may need to be in testing, review capacity or the development workflow rather than another AI tool.

A useful engineering dashboard should help leaders understand how work moves from a business need to a reliable release. A small set of measures can cover delivery speed, change failure rate, review delays, rework and the outcome the work was meant to achieve.

These measures should be read together and at the level where teams can act on them. They are useful for finding constraints and deciding where to invest. They are much less useful as a league table ranking individual engineers by commits, tickets or lines of code.

Instead of trying to make a dashboard look more productive, understand whether the engineering organization is delivering better software, where work is getting stuck and what needs to change.​​
