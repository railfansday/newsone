Forget ‘Outlander,’ Netflix’s 25-Episode Fantasy Thriller Is a Near-Perfect Binge

📅 Nov 26, 2026  |  Category: Entertainment  |  Read Source →

Romance has always been an exceedingly popular genre, but one can argue that more cultural phenomenon-level television shows are enjoying their moment in the sun than ever before. Take modern offerings like Outlander, Bridgerton, and Heated Rivalry, all of which took the world by storm during the last decade. Their individual success comes back to the same collective reason why small-budget rom-com movies once consistently reigned over the box office, and why the assorted sub-genres under romance's umbrella remain some of the highest-selling novels in the world. At the genre's best, its immersive nature lends itself to supporting the thematic priorities for which many underrepresented demographics hunger for: empowerment, escapist wish-fulfillment, the revelatory tangle of intimacy, and harmless theatrical melodrama.

Romance's increased mainstream prevalence extends, albeit on a smaller scale, to A Discovery of Witches. Based on New York Times bestselling author Deborah Harkness' All Souls book series, the 2018 small-screen adaptation surged in popularity once all three seasons hit Netflix in 2024. Although A Discovery of Witches doesn't revolutionize the conventions surrounding fantasy romance, it does excel at creative pivots, memorable interludes, and the TV version of what it feels like to devour a luscious gourmet meal.

A Discovery of Witches fuses elements of supernatural romantasy, historical drama, and conspiracy mystery thriller. Opening in the Gothic-tinged halls of modern-day Oxford University before spreading across the luxurious United Kingdom and European countrysides, Harkness follows Diana Bishop (Teresa Palmer), a historian, scientist, Ivy League professor, and an orphaned witch.

Four love stories, four completely different ideas of what it means to be somebody's person. This is the one that matches how you actually fall, and what it would ask of you.

You do not fall for strangers. You fall for the person you have already been talking to for years, which is the slowest and by some distance the most durable way anybody has found to do this.

You want a partner, not an audience. Somebody who wants their own life as badly as you want yours, and who would never ask you to be smaller to make the relationship easier.

The intensity was never the problem. The secrecy is. You are drawn to a love that lives entirely in private, and to the particular ache of wanting somebody you are not supposed to want.

You do not do this cautiously. When it happens for you it is immediate and total and it rearranges everything, and you would not accept a smaller version of it.

Diana has steadfastly avoided practicing any magic, even for convenience's sake, since her parents' unsolved murders. She also rejects associating with the larger community, aside from her loving aunts (Alex Kingston and Valarie Pettiford). Of course, her best-laid plans go right out the window as soon as an unexpected discovery throws her into the crosshairs of a brewing war spearheaded by malignant narcissists, not to mention an enigmatic and unfairly handsome vampire named Matthew Clairmont (Matthew Goode).

If you don't respond to what's essentially the vampire-and-witch version of Romeo and Juliet with enthusiastic approval, then A Discovery of Witches might glide headfirst into abundant silliness. If you approach it with the right mindset, however, its sincerity and self-awareness are key to the entertainment factor. The series treats the story seriously without taking itself too seriously. It's the kind of comfort-watch indulgence where the cheesiness tastes as scrumptious as the world's best and biggest nacho platter — two top-notch forbidden love stories for the price of one, with all the pleasing aesthetics (velvet shadows, billowing fog, envy-inducing period costumes, steamy rendezvous teetering on the knife's edge between desire and danger) and emotional roller-coaster loops that are required (a frail social order, kidnappings, near-death experiences, sworn enemies becoming in-laws).

Overall, A Discovery of Witches' premise, payoff, apocalyptic impetus, and personal stakes all nicely dovetail. Harkness staunchly honors the internal structure and mechanics of her worldbuilding, braiding together historical figures from Elizabethan-era England, alchemy, rumored witchcraft, and Wiccan spirituality with her own magic system and a biology-based supernatural mythology. The metaphors for prejudice and segregation can lean simplistic and heavy-handed, but in select cases — as long as the execution isn't gratingly clumsy or doesn't perpetuate harmful stereotypes — it's more responsible to lightly, loosely reckon with applicable subtext than ignore its implications.

Stay tuned for more yearning in the Scottish Highlands.

The All Souls books began as a trilogy before Harkness expanded the series into five novels and counting. If there's any justice, A Discovery of Witches' rights-holders will stage a continuation sooner rather than later. The found-family dynamic between the stellar character actors (familiar faces abound for fans of My Lady Jane, Rome, and Game of Thrones) is too good to waste. The same goes for Palmer and Goode's thrumming chemistry, especially since Diana's judicious academic expertise, steel-lined backbone, and emotional vulnerability make her a blazing beacon of well-defined, take-no-prisoners heroism. Until a revival graces our screens, A Discovery of Witches' three seasons offer all the beguiling decadence of a wrapped-and-ribboned Christmas gift. Sometimes, we crave the cozy simplicity of a thrill that's capable of flashing its sharp incisors.

All three seasons of A Discovery of Witches is now available to stream on Netflix.

Joshua Zirkzee insisted Manchester United must focus on their own performance rather than pointing fingers at controversial refereeing following their 2-2 Champions League draw against Sporting Lisbon on Wednesday night.

The tie in Lisbon was marked by a series of contentious calls from Polish match official Szymon Marciniak, prompting former United captain and BBC Radio 5 Live pundit Ashley Young to describe the officiating as "a disgrace".

Marciniak disallowed Amad Diallo’s first-half equaliser by blowing his whistle before the ball hit the back of an empty net, having wrongly judged Youri Tielemans to have fouled Goncalo Inacio in the buildup.

The United midfielder’s frustration over the booking was compounded when he received a second yellow card in the closing stages of the match.

The referee also ruled out a late winner for United after being directed to the pitchside monitor, deciding goalkeeper Kaique Pereira had been fouled by Patrick Dorgu.

However, speaking in a post-match interview with MUTV, Zirkzee said: "In my opinion, there were definitely some key decisions there that didn’t really favour us.

"We shouldn’t look at the referee, we should look at ourselves instead, but it definitely has an impact on us, you can’t deny that.

"But the team that we have, we should come here and not look at the ref and make sure that we get the win, regardless of the decisions that he makes."

Marciniak had also awarded Sporting a dubious first-half penalty after Lisandro Martinez made slight contact with Geny Catamo, though Senne Lammens saved the effort.

"Obviously, we did come here to win, so in a way, we’ve got some positives to take," Zirkzee said.

"But with our recent form, of course, we’re disappointed to not win. Some positives to take, we’ve just got to keep moving forward, keep going together, keep putting in the work and move on to the next game."

Making his first start of the campaign, Zirkzee expressed his gratitude to the supporters, adding: "I’m very happy. Every game you play for this club is an honour.

"I’ve been here for some time and it’s been a while since I started a game, so I’m very happy, I just wish we could have got a win.

"For where we’re standing at right now, to have a fanbase like that is just incredible.

"All we can do is get up the next day and make sure the next time they travel with us and the next time they watch our game, we repay it, so that’s all we can do for now."

United, who sit 19th in the Champions League standings, fell behind at Estadio Jose Alvalade to a Luis Suarez spot-kick. They responded through Bruno Fernandes’ double – the first coming from the penalty spot – before Suarez seized on a late error from Lammens to secure a draw.

Mauricio Pochettino will now aim for his first win in charge of United when they host Brentford in the Premier League on Saturday.

5 of the Greatest Sci-Fi Shows for ‘Dungeon Crawler Carl’ Fans, Ranked

📅 Nov 26, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Waves of new fans are picking up the Dungeon Crawler Carl series every day, which means waves of people with the dreaded "book hangover" looking for their next fix after A Parade of Horribles. Dungeon Crawler Carl is a fantasy story at its heart, though it uses sci-fi elements and aesthetics just as much as classic fantasy tropes. If you find yourself wanting something similar to pick up after DCC, a lot of science fiction novels will miss the mark. However, sci-fi TV shows tend to have a similar blend of humor, action, and heartfelt character work. These TV shows may also be strong indications of how the upcoming DCC TV adaptation will work, especially when it comes to shows that have been adapted from books.

It's worth noting that the Dungeon Crawler Carl TV series will be produced by Fuzzy Door — the production company of Seth MacFarlane. MacFarlane has already produced a live-action sci-fi series called The Orville, which is a parody of Star Trek and other shows of its kind. Watching that might be a bit of research ahead of the DCC premiere. Beyond that, the five shows below are the most likely to appeal to Dungeon Crawler Carl fans after finishing all eight books.

If your favorite part of Dungeon Crawler Carl was the irreverent humor, Futurama should probably be your next stop. The animated series takes a satirical approach to high-tech sci-fi, though as the series goes on it delves surprisingly deep into its own world-building. It was created by Matt Groening, co-creator of The Simpsons, and it follows a young man who was cryogenically frozen and transported to a distant future. He lands a job as a courier working for his own descendant, and his travels around the planet and the galaxy lead to all kinds of hijinks.

Like DCC, Futurama has some genuinely emotional moments. It manages to dig deep into the pathos of its characters despite its silly tone, and it deals with some complex social issues as well. The show is streaming now on Hulu and it's definitely worth a try for anyone who laughed at Princess Donut's quips.

Switching gears, few people describe Apple TV's Foundation as silly or irreverent, but the massive, chaotic scale of its story fits well with Dungeon Crawler Carl. The series is based on Isaac Asimov's Foundation series, though it takes quite a few liberties in the adaptation process. The series is about the collapse of a galaxy-spanning empire, and humanity's attempts to salvage what it can as the dark age encroaches. There are three seasons of Foundation streaming now on Apple TV, with a fourth expected soon.

Rick and Morty is a natural choice for Dungeon Crawler Carl fans, and the two probably have quite a bit of crossover already. Like DCC author Matt Dinniman, the Rick and Morty writers hold nothing sacred, yet they take the consistency of their worldbuilding mechanics very seriously. The show touches on every sci-fi and fantasy trope in the book, and while it's funny, it also asks viewers to confront existential questions head on.

The most significant difference between these two stories is their worldview. DCC has resonated with many people because of its defiant, triumphant nature. No matter how much the dungeon throws at Carl and his friends, they fight back with righteous fury. Rick and Morty, on the other hand, has a tendency towards a jaded, macabre outlook. One is not necessarily better or more honest than the other, but that might be a fundamental barrier for fans enjoying both stories.

For all the traps and monsters in Dungeon Crawler Carl, the dungeon A.I. could still learn a thing or two about psychological torture from Lumon Industries. Apple TV's Severance is about a biotechnology corporation that asks employees to undergo a mysterious medical procedure known as "severance," which splits their psyche into two parts. The mind changes personas based on proximity to the company's headquarters, so employees do not remember anything that happens to them at work. Their alter egos — "innies" — do not know anything about their lives outside the office. This theoretically protects company secrets and frees the employees' main personas from experiencing tedious days at their desks.

There's so much we still don't know about Severance since the story is ongoing, but it's already clear that the show shares a disdain for corporate power with DCC. Both are about resisting oppression despite insurmountable odds, and in that way they are likely to have a huge crossover in fandom. Severance has two seasons on Apple TV now, with a third season now in post-production. There's no word yet on when the show will be back.

Finally, nothing has blurred the lines between a game and a real-life struggle quite like HBO's Westworld. The series introduced us to a futuristic theme park populated with life-like androids that behaved like normal people from the old west, allowing visitors to live out their fantasies as cowboys and pioneers — for better or worse. Over the course of four seasons, the show's scope steadily expanded to show us more about the park, its visitors, and eventually the world beyond. It's a particularly perfect binge for fans of Dungeon Crawler Carl book five, The Butcher's Masquerade and book seven, This Inevitable Ruin.

Sadly, Westworld can be tricky to find these days since it has been removed from HBO Max. The series was released on Blu-ray, so hard copies are available in some places. It can also be purchased digitally on PVOD platforms. As for Dungeon Crawler Carl, there are eight books available now in print, digital, and audiobook formats. The ninth book is coming soon, with no release date yet, and the TV adaptation is in the early stages as well.

That's to be determined. Right now, he's aiming to be the former, but Spider-Man would probably classify him as the latter.

Who is this new villain, and what is his deal? Well, the name says it all: "Scavenger." He has assembled a super-suit out of discarded pieces of gear from past Spider-Man villains, from the Vulture's wings to Jack O'Lantern's pumpkin headgear, and more.

Underneath it all, though, he's just a guy named Waldo Wagman (channeling some classic Marvel alliteration), and in the opening bout of his feud with Spidey, he doesn't exactly make a heavyweight name for himself. But he does live to fight another day, and surprisingly gains an advantage that could help him next time.

Warning! Spoilers ahead for Spectacular Spider-Man #6!

Spectacular Spider-Man #6 is really the Scavenger's story. It introduces him during a flashback to "a few years ago," taking advantage of Spider-Man and the Avengers' battle with giant robots controlled by Doctor Octopus to do some felony shoplifting. Waldo quickly gets more than he bargained for, though, when he stumbles across the Tinkerer's lab and "some of the deadliest weapons in New York."

Waldo lies low for a while, presumably practicing operating his new gear, and coming up with his supervillain name. The issue follows his erstwhile criminal debut as he tries to taken down an armored truck. This guy is definitely not Neil McCauley from Heat. What he is, though, is desperate to make some kind of name for himself, and that makes him extremely dangerous. As soon as Spider-Man shows up on the scene, Scavenger gleefully escalates from armed robbery to attempted murder.

"I get to be the guy who killed Spider-Man," he shouts as he aims a powerful laser at the hero. That goes about as one would expect.
