Matt LaFleur was right: Kirk Cousins will be the starting quarterback for the Minnesota Vikings against LaFleur's Green Bay Packers on Sunday at Lambeau Field.

Vikings coach Kevin O'Connell officially confirmed Cousins, who was acquired via trade with the Las Vegas Raiders on Tuesday, will be the starting quarterback for the Vikings on Sunday. The Vikings also ruled out five players on the final injury report, including starting linebacker Blake Cashman, starting tight end T.J. The Packers were expecting Cousins to start. Minnesota also won't have receiver Jauan Jennings, and Murray is officially out for the second straight week because of a hamstring injury.

Now in Baltimore, the well-traveled Brissett prepares to fill in for another star quarterback

📅 Nov 13, 2026  |  Category: Sports  |  Read Source →

OWINGS MILLS, Md. (AP) — Jacoby Brissett's next challenge is to fill in for a two-time MVP in Baltimore.

If anyone should be used to that assignment, it's Brissett. The 33-year-old has been a journeyman for the past several seasons, but has plenty of experience replacing standout quarterbacks.

"I think he'll operate our offense at a level required to be successful. We're really excited about the addition, and he's just a very mature pro that I think will bring a leadership style to our offense from the quarterback position," Ravens coach Jesse Minter said. "He'll get the ball out on time. He'll be able to kind of run the whole thing. He'll be a leader. He'll hold guys accountable."

Brissett is set to start for the Ravens on Monday night against the Los Angeles Chargers. Baltimore traded for him after Lamar Jackson and backup Tyler Huntley both had to go on injured reserve. Now in his 11th season, Brissett is one of the game's most experienced understudies. Rarely a team's top choice entering the season, he nonetheless has started 73 games, often in place of some pretty big names.

"You are around a lot of different people, a lot of different systems, different situations, every year," Brissett said. "So just being able to be flexible, that's what's kept me in this league for so long, and this is no different."

Brissett was drafted by New England in 2016, the year Tom Brady was suspended four games in the "Deflategate" aftermath. When Jimmy Garoppolo went down with an injury, Brissett suddenly found himself starting a couple of games less than a month into his career.

Indianapolis traded for Brissett the following year, with Andrew Luck recovering from surgery. Brissett ended up starting 15 games that season. When Luck abruptly retired two years later, Brissett was next man up for Indy, again starting almost every game.

"You have to have grace for yourself, obviously, and then confidence that you wouldn't be in that position if the organization, the team, the coaches, the players didn't think that you can handle it," Brissett said. "Then obviously, you take it day by day, and I think you slow the time down while everybody else is moving fast around you."

In 2021, he made five starts for Miami in place of the injury-plagued Tua Tagovailoa. A year later, Brissett started Cleveland's first 11 games when Deshaun Watson was suspended following sexual misconduct accusations.

In 2024, Brissett was back in New England, starting the first five games while the Patriots held off on making rookie Drake Maye the No. 1 quarterback. Last year, Brissett replaced injured Kyler Murray and made 12 starts for Arizona. With Murray out of the picture this year, Brissett started the first eight games for the Cardinals before being demoted and then quickly traded.

"It was obviously a tough situation. Then you get the news like the next day or two, or whatever it is, and then you kind of have to get your mind wrapped around another situation," Brissett said. "So, it's kind of all over the place, but having those extra days kind of helped me settle my emotions down and then obviously refocusing and figuring out what the next steps were."

Now he's in a familiar role — replacing a franchise quarterback as what the team hopes will be a short-term fix. Baltimore announced Friday that Jackson's type of thumb injury typically takes six to 10 weeks to heal. Brissett's job is to keep the Ravens (4-5) in playoff contention for as long as he's needed.

"He's a guy who's played 11 years, so he's seen a lot of football," offensive coordinator Declan Doyle said. "He'll let it rip. He can really drive the ball. Those are all things that I'm really excited about with him."

Jesus Owono Banned For 12 Games After Jesus de Miguel’s Death

📅 Nov 13, 2026  |  Category: Sports  |  Read Source →

FC Andorra goalkeeper Jesus Owono has learned his punishment after he was sent off in an incident that eventually led to the death of CD Tenerife forward Jesus de Miguel.

Tenerife and Andorra met in a Spanish Segunda Division match on November 2, but the match had to be abandoned in stoppage time after De Miguel suffered a serious injury.

Jesus Owono has broken his silence after the tragic passing of CD Tenerife forward Jesus de Miguel.

The striker was looking to run onto a long ball over the top when he was met by Owono in a horrific collision. Despite making it to hospital, De Miguel's death was confirmed the next morning.

Owono was sent off for the challenge, but immediately showed his panic when he realised the severity of De Miguel's injury. The 25-year-old is reported to be struggling with the incident, which saw his fellow player die at just 30 years of age.

Spanish police opened an investigation into involuntary manslaughter, but it is believed Owono is unlikely to face legal action. He has, however, now been handed a lengthy ban from football.

According to Marca, the goalkeeper will now serve a 12-match suspension for his part in the tragic event. The Disciplinary Committee of the Royal Spanish Football Federation (RFEF) released the following statement:

"The Disciplinary Committee, as an independent body within the federation's structure, has agreed to suspend Jesús Lázaro Ferrer Owono, a player for FC Andorra, for twelve matches for his actions during the CD Tenerife – FC Andorra match. Twelve matches is the maximum penalty allowed by the Disciplinary Code for the events described in the referee's report.

"The referee's report is the only document in the disciplinary proceedings file, and the Committee must determine the sanction based solely on this information. The report describes a violent action, with the leg raised and excessive force used, while the ball was in play. In light of these circumstances, the Committee has determined that the events fall under Article 102 of the Disciplinary Code.

"Furthermore, the subsequent consequences of the action, which are also documented in the referee's report and are public knowledge, have been taken into account to increase the sanction to the maximum level permitted by the regulations. Under no circumstances may the Committee exceed the threshold expressly stipulated in the applicable article.

"The decision of the Disciplinary Committee may be appealed to the RFEF Appeals Committee within ten working days."

Sotheby’s Hedreen Auction Proceeds Will Repay Loan, Not Fund University Museum: Report

📅 Nov 13, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

A $300 million art donation to Seattle University has been revealed to be more complex than originally reported.

On Monday, Sotheby’s will begin selling approximately 50 works from the collection amassed by Seattle real estate developer and philanthropist Richard “Dick” Hedreen, previously billed as the “largest art gift ever” to a US university. Hedreen said in September that the sale did not mean the promised gift was falling through. Rather, he suggested, the proceeds would help furnish Seattle University’s new art museum, where the collection was eventually expected to hang. Related Articles As Art Week NYC Kicks Off, Galleries Have Millions of Dollars of Art to Sell--And No Shortage of Naras Paris Court Orders Sotheby's to Return Nazi-Looted Pissarro Painting to Jewish Heirs

Records newly reported on by The Seattle Times, however, tell a different story: The works are being sold not to fund the museum, but to pay off a debt secured against the collection — a debt Hedreen says stands between him and fulfilling his pledge to the university.

What was originally framed as a donation, in reality, was a pledge: Hedreen, whose firm R.C. Hedreen Co. owns the Grand Hyatt among other Seattle properties, retained the right to buy and sell works from the collection until the gift was finalized. The Sotheby’s auction is expected to generate at least $100 million across a series of sales. As of writing, the overall value of the artwork set for Seattle University is unclear.

The sale spans an expansive sweep of art history. Works by Willem de Kooning, Rashid Johnson, Amy Sherald, and Jonas Wood will be offered alongside Old Masters by Bronzino, Jacopo Pontormo, Élisabeth Vigée Le Brun, and Thomas Gainsborough. Leading Sotheby’s offerings is Roy Lichtenstein’s Reflections on “Painter and Model” (1990), estimated at $20 million to $30 million. Cecily Brown’s 12-foot-wide Untitled (Shipwreck) (2017), estimated at $7 million to $10 million, is another highlight.

A news release from Seattle University stated: “Hedreen has given the university the renowned Hedreen Collection (named for himself and his late wife Elizabeth ‘Betty’ Ann Petri Hedreen, a Seattle University alumna) valued at $300 million, along with $25 million in seed funding to develop the museum.”

However, the Seattle Times reported that records show that the “seed funding” was actually provided through the private sale of a de Kooning painting. Estimated in 2024 to be worth $25 million, the work fetched $17 million.

The Sotheby’s sale proceeds will instead go toward paying off an outstanding loan secured against the collection. The borrowing dates to 2012, when Hedreen first pledged several artworks, including a pieces by Francis Bacon, de Kooning and David Hockney, as collateral for a Goldman Sachs loan. In 2025, that financing was replaced with a Bank of America loan secured by approximately 44 major artworks.

R.C. Hedreen Co. President David Thyer confirmed that the Sotheby’s sale is tied to financial pressures in the hotel and real estate markets. Income generated by the company’s properties, he said, is insufficient to repay the loan “in any reasonably foreseeable time frame.”

ARTnews has contacted Sotheby’s for comment.

Richard Hedreen’s son, Guy, said in a statement that his family’s “commitment to Seattle University has not changed.” His father, Guy added, “is not selling works from his collection in order to spend money on himself” or his business. Rather, the auction reflects his commitment to the university “by removing a debt that prevents him from translating his pledge intentions into an outright gift.”

Construction on the new art museum, originally scheduled to begin this past summer, has been pushed to 2027 as the project awaits permits. Designed by local architect Tom Kundig, the museum is intended to permanently house the Hedreen collection, alongside rotating exhibitions and academic programming.

Scott K. McClellan, Seattle University’s vice president for university affairs, said the school continues to believe the collection “presents a significant opportunity for the university, community and our broader vision for the arts.” He reiterated the “shared intention of Mr. Hedreen and the university” that Seattle University ultimately receive a collection of museum-quality works commensurate with the collection that formed the basis of the original commitment.

McClellan added that it had been “reasonable” for the university to expect Hedreen’s loan to be paid off by 2028, when the museum was originally scheduled for completion.

Should Government Officials Be Banned From Prediction Markets? House Oversight Says Maybe

📅 Nov 13, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Should Government Officials Be Banned From Prediction Markets? House Oversight Says Maybe

The chairman of the House Oversight Committee is raising the prospect of barring government officials from trading on prediction markets, citing concerns that insiders are using nonpublic information to profit on platforms like Kalshi and Polymarket.

In a staff memorandum released Friday, the committee detailed an investigation into insider trading by government officials, military personnel and people holding security clearances who have placed bets using information not available to the public.

The probe gathered nearly 1,700 pages of documents and included five bipartisan staff briefings from prediction market platforms about how they detect and report suspicious activity.

Chairman James Comer framed the issue around public trust. “Americans are rightly concerned about public corruption,” he said, arguing Congress “has a duty to ensure our laws adequately prevent insider trading” and prohibit officials “from profiting from nonpublic information.”

He said lawmakers must weigh “additional reforms to prevent public corruption and restore the American people’s trust in government.”

The memo’s central recommendation is that Congress consider new restrictions on federal personnel, ranging from an outright ban on trading prediction markets to narrower limits on specific contracts, such as those tied to government decisions.

Applying such rules across all branches, the committee argued, would reinforce that government employees shouldn’t financially benefit from access to nonpublic information, and would remove even the appearance of it.

The memo stressed that existing insider trading laws already apply to prediction markets. It cited the Commodity Exchange Act, as amended by the STOCK Act, which bars federal employees, members of Congress and others from trading commodity futures, options, or swaps on nonpublic information drawn from their positions.

The committee credited the platforms themselves for policing their markets, noting that most reported insider trading incidents were first detected and flagged to regulators by the platforms. It singled out Polymarket, which recently hired a former law enforcement expert and rolled out surveillance software called “PolyGraph” built with analytics firm Chainalysis, and Kalshi for strengthening their detection protocols.

The scrutiny follows a string of cases involving alleged insider trading by public officials and government employees.

A U.S. soldier was charged in April over alleged Polymarket trades tied to a military operation that resulted in the capture of Venezuelan President Nicolas Maduro. Then, in August, the CFTC fined a former White House teleprompter operator for betting on presidential speeches.

The push also lands amid broader pressure on the sector, with the CFTC moving to assert authority over prediction markets in its fight against several states, while Sen. Elizabeth Warren sets her sights on the industry as the incoming Senate Banking chair.

Hollywood CEO Paydays May Get Harder to Track as Federal Regulator Proposes Disclosure Revamp

📅 Nov 13, 2026  |  Category: Entertainment  |  Read Source →

Fewer executives will be tracked, and less data will be shared if the SEC's proposed disclosure changes move forward.

It’s no secret that Hollywood CEO pay is often a hot-button story.

Chief execs in media, entertainment, and technology are annually among the most highly compensated people on Earth, with mid-eight-figure pay packages a common occurrence and nine-figure paydays increasingly common.

But those days might be ending soon. Not the paydays, just the disclosures, should new rules from the Securities and Exchange Commission go into effect.

The SEC on Friday proposed a revamp of its executive compensation disclosure rules, simplifying things while also changing what those disclosures will look like. The result will be that pay is disclosed for fewer executives, with only the CEO and CFO likely to be disclosed should the rule change pass (no more COO, general counsel or other C-suite pay), while the way the pay packages are disclosed shifts. Movies Renny Harlin, Fixture of '90s Blockbusters, Launches Production Company Focusing on New Directors

In addition, the threshold for perquisites is being raised from $10,000 to $150,000, meaning that some perks like club memberships will no longe require disclosure, and companies will get more discretion to determine if something is a perk at all, such as CEO security costs.

At the heart of the changes are simpler disclosures, with three tables: “Earned Compensation Table, Granted Compensation Table, and Status of Equity Awards Table” with a particular emphasis on salary, bonus and equity grants.

“As applied to executive compensation disclosure, a panelist at last year’s roundtable aptly analogized current Item 402 to an ice cream parlor that carries rum raisin and maple walnut; flavors desired by only a single customer,” SEC chairman Paul Adkins said in a statement about the proposed changes. “The proposed amendments would, if adopted, revise Item 402 to focus on an objective standard regarding needs of the reasonable investor, rather than on the specialized tastes of a specific investor.”

Executives, investor relations and corporate communications teams are surely cheering the move, which will result in less gargantuan numbers than previous disclosures. Some of those changes may be warranted: After all, equity may not end up vesting if certain metrics are not achieved, and a high strike price can invalidate whatever value is in some shares. Those are things that CEOs have complained about for years: The news coverage has a headline number, but their actual take-home could be lower (or much higher!) depending on the performance of the shares.

The new disclosure structure will eliminate the paper gain spikes associated with unvested equity, and likely to be closer to what an executive really takes home, though if the old model sometimes overstated thing, the new disclosure may understate things, if the stocks consistently rise.

There will also be changes to the employee-to-CEO pay ratio, which focuses it on U.S. full-time employees and could make for more favorable comparisons.

And the changes to perquisite reporting mean that things like security, parking, country club memberships and even some private jet use could no longer be disclosed, all of which would be music to CEO ears. Even if those disclosures sometimes help tell a bigger story.

Jensen Huang Steps Down as CEO. Is Nvidia Still a Buy?

📅 Nov 13, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

A slew of extramarital affairs and security risks have shaken Nvidia's investment thesis to the core.

Nvidia (NVDA -2.01%) has been tethered to the spotlight since Nov. 8, when founder and then-CEO Jensen Huang admitted to having an affair with Alice Huang.

Alice Huang (no relation to Jensen) was an executive at a Singapore-based Nvidia customer. And reports indicate that U.S. officials and the FBI are investigating the relationship because Alice Huang is suspected of being a Chinese intelligence operative. On Nov. 10, Nvidia hired a prominent New York law firm to initiate an independent investigation into the affair.

The situation escalated after market close on Nov. 11, when the New York Times reported that Jensen Huang had a romantic relationship with Nvidia's vice president of healthcare, Kimberly Powell. On Nov. 12, reports surfaced of a possible third affair with a second employee. That same day, Nvidia published a press release, announcing that Huang had resigned, with CFO Colette Kress taking over as interim CEO.

As of market close on Nov. 12, Nvidia is down a staggering 27.5% in the last month. Here's how the recent news surrounding Huang impacts Nvidia's investment thesis and whether the growth stock is still a buy.

Jensen Huang was arguably the most important CEO in the world. He co-founded Nvidia with Chris Malachowsky and Curtis Priem 33 years ago and possesses a rare balance of technical, strategic, marketing, and leadership skills.

I recently read The Nvidia Way: Jensen Huang and the Making of a Tech Giant by Tae Kim. It's an excellent book that serves as both a history of Nvidia and a biography of Huang. And the biggest takeaway I had from the book is that Huang is arguably more integral to Nvidia than Steve Jobs was to Apple (AAPL +0.39%), Bill Gates was to Microsoft, or Jeff Bezos was to Amazon.

Tim Cook was able to catapult Apple's market cap up severalfold, even after Jobs's passing, by leveraging Apple's integrated ecosystem of products and services. Nvidia is different. When it comes to artificial intelligence (AI) in the semiconductor industry, competition is fierce, and innovation is paramount. Huang has made several key decisions throughout the company's history. And without them, it's highly unlikely the tech stock would be where it is today.

Huang was a firm believer in parallel computing and the graphics processing unit (GPU) -- a term Nvidia helped coin when it debuted the GeForce 256 in 1999 -- and labeled it the world's first GPU. He was also adamant about CUDA, which is Nvidia's software architecture used by developers with Nvidia GPUs.

By integrating hardware and software, Nvidia paved a runway for repeat business in an industry where a hot product can leapfrog the moats of even the most entrenched industry leaders. After all, Huang saw this in the early days of Nvidia, when the company almost went bankrupt multiple times because it developed products too infrequently and bet the farm on a single launch.

Huang also has an impeccable work ethic and top-to-bottom devotion to Nvidia -- arguably to a fault. Nvidia's organization structure positioned Huang like Jupiter, surrounded by dozens of direct reports that orbit him like moons. This wasn’t just so Huang could keep tabs. It's so that he can contribute through spontaneous bursts of creativity.

To complement this workflow, Nvidia has whiteboards scattered throughout meeting rooms for ideation and real-time problem-solving. The knowledge and willingness to break concepts down, start from scratch, and hold frequent open discussions have become essential parts of Nvidia's corporate culture. And it remains to be seen if that will persist under new leadership.

Huang has also been a firm proponent of AI. And it's a key reason why Nvidia was able to shift its end markets from gaming, professional visualization, and automotive to data centers in just a few years. Gaining a foothold in AI was one thing. But Nvidia has continued to push the bounds of innovation through incredibly impressive product upgrades.

Nvidia could have easily taken its foot off the gas after announcing its Grace Blackwell architecture in March 2024. Blackwell unlocked massive efficiency improvements and cost reductions in AI training. But Nvidia really outdid itself with Vera Rubin, which it announced in January 2026.

Rubin's full-stack platform includes six chips, allowing Nvidia to control a larger share of data center infrastructure. Rubin's cost reductions and efficiency improvements compared to Blackwell are so massive that hyperscalers are lining up at the door to place orders. On the Aug. 26 Q2 fiscal 2027 earnings call, Nvidia said that it began shipping Rubin in August and expects it to account for 20% of its Q3 data center revenue, which it will report on Nov. 17.

Reports of three affairs, an internal investigation, an FBI probe, and now Jensen Huang resigning as CEO directly impact the investment thesis. Until we get answers on the extent of the damage and the next long-term CEO, the stock simply isn't as strong a buy as it used to be -- even with a valuation that's ridiculously cheap for such a high-quality business.

I wouldn't sell Nvidia simply because the company's operating leverage and market position are impossible to ignore. But it's hard to imagine Nvidia without Jensen Huang's leadership. The situation would be different if Huang had a clear successor, but there's nothing of the kind at Nvidia, which poses a major key-person risk.

At the very least, Nvidia must overhaul its organizational structure. Poor character aside, I'd expect any replacement to fall short of Huang's caliber as a CEO. And that's legitimately concerning for investors.

As tempting as the sell-off looks at first glance, investors are better off waiting for the dust to settle. I'd much rather buy the stock at a higher price with greater clarity than bottom-fish in waters this murky.

Nakamoto Sells More Bitcoin (BTC)

📅 2026-11-13  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

David Bailey's Bitcoin treasury company, Nakamoto Inc. (NASDAQ: NAKA), has reduced its Bitcoin (BTC) holdings by another 184 BTC, according to its latest quarterly financial results.

The company, which has made Bitcoin central to its corporate strategy, held 4,283 BTC as of Sept. 30, down from 4,467 BTC at the end of June.

According to Bitcoin Treasuries, Nakamoto sold 184 BTC during the third quarter of 2026, bringing its total holdings to 4,283 BTC. The company currently ranks 21st on the Bitcoin 100 corporate treasury ranking.

The latest financial filing shows that Nakamoto generated approximately $13.2 million in additional proceeds from digital asset sales during the quarter. However, the filing does not separately identify the 184 BTC reduction as a single sale.

Despite the reduction in its holdings, Nakamoto's remaining Bitcoin was valued at approximately $357.9 million at the end of September.

This is not the first time Bailey's company has reduced its Bitcoin holdings in 2026.

In March, Nakamoto sold approximately 284 BTC for $20 million to support working capital requirements and fund operations following its acquisitions of BTC Inc. and UTXO Management.

The sale took place at an average price of roughly $70,422 per Bitcoin, significantly below the company's original acquisition cost.

During the first quarter, Nakamoto also received approximately 43 BTC in premiums from its derivatives strategies before selling around 40 BTC.

The company made another substantial Bitcoin sale in June.

According to its second-quarter financial results, Nakamoto sold approximately 600 BTC for 35.6 million USDT.

Together with proceeds from certain Bitcoin derivative positions, the transactions generated approximately $48 million.

Nakamoto used $45 million of those proceeds to partially repay its Bitcoin-backed loan from cryptocurrency exchange Kraken, reducing the outstanding principal from 210 million USDT to 165 million USDT.

The latest reduction in Bitcoin holdings comes as Nakamoto continues to manage its substantial debt obligations while expanding its operating businesses.

As of Sept. 30, approximately 3,030 BTC were pledged as collateral against the company's debt, leaving 1,253 BTC unencumbered.

On Nov. 11, Nakamoto extended the maturity of 60 million USDT of its Kraken loan from December 2026 to December 2027. The company now has no scheduled principal repayments remaining in 2026.

Interestingly, Nakamoto reported a net profit of $85.5 million for the third quarter, primarily benefiting from a substantial increase in the market value of its Bitcoin holdings.

However, its adjusted operating loss reached $15.8 million, highlighting the ongoing challenge of generating sustainable earnings beyond Bitcoin price appreciation.

Bailey has maintained that Nakamoto intends to build businesses capable of generating revenue throughout different Bitcoin market cycles.

The company's latest Bitcoin holdings reduction illustrates how corporate treasury strategies can involve selling cryptocurrency to meet financing and operational requirements, rather than simply accumulating BTC indefinitely.
