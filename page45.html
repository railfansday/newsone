Fintech giant Plaid opening Seattle engineering office, with plans to double local headcount

📅 Nov 10, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

by Kurt Schlosser on Nov 10, 2026 at 10:28 amNovember 10, 2026 at 10:28 am

Plaid is opening an engineering office in Seattle’s Westlake neighborhood in January and plans to double its local workforce to about 80 people by next summer.

The San Francisco-based fintech company says the new 11,466-square-foot space will serve as a hub for its technical teams, with most open Seattle roles focused on machine learning, data science and engineering.

Plaid said in a blog post that it chose Seattle because much of the cloud and AI technology behind modern financial infrastructure was built in the region. The company is developing AI models trained on its network to understand patterns in how money moves.

Plaid already has about 40 employees in the Seattle area, who currently work remotely, The Seattle Times reported. “There’s just great talent in Seattle, so it’s a little bit of a no-brainer for us,” Meghan Welch, Plaid’s chief people officer, told the Times.

Plaid’s careers page lists more than 50 open roles that include Seattle as a possible location, though most are also open in San Francisco, New York or other cities. About half are in data, engineering or security, with the rest spread across sales, legal, finance and other teams.

Founded in 2013, Plaid was valued at $8 billion in an employee share sale earlier this year, down from a 2021 peak of $13.4 billion. The company says more than 150 million people have used its technology to connect their financial accounts.

Plaid is led by co-founder and CEO Zachary Perret.

The new office will have views of Lake Union, an outdoor deck and event space, along with three catered meals a day, according to the company.

Unai Emery's actions after Aston Villa drew 4-4 with Manchester United on Sunday were disrespectful.

That is the opinion of former senior Villa scout, Bryan King, speaking exclusively to Football Insider.

The Spaniard was seen walking off the pitch before the final whistle had sounded, after his side had conceded two stoppage-time goals to throw away a 4-2 lead.

Emery was asked after the game why he had left, and claimed in a fiery interview that he thought that the game had ended when the six minutes of additional time were up.

There have been several instances of the Villa manager failing to hide his emotions at the end of games that have not gone his team's way over the years.

It has been reported that Emery did shake Michael Carrick's hand before making his exit on Sunday, but twice last season did the Spaniard leave before exchanging formalities with his opposite manager.

Emery was clearly emotional in his post-match interview, and understandably so after his team's collapse.

Villa had been two goals ahead twice in the game, inexplicably throwing away their lead on both occasions.

It has become a concerning trend for Emery to deal with, with draws against Fulham and Brentford also seeing them give away the lead.

"He got the hump. There's a few like that in the Premier League, you see," King told Football Insider when asked about Emery's reaction on Sunday.

Billy Joel Is Returning to the Stage—Months After Selling His Long Island Estate and Revealing Brain Disorder

📅 Nov 10, 2026  |  Category: Business  |  Read Source →

Billy Joel is returning to performing—months after he offloaded his Long Island estate and nearly one year after revealing a brain disorder diagnosis.

The hitmaker, 77, revealed in a Nov. 9 social media post that he would be making his on-stage comeback.

“Florida Atlantic University will welcome singer, songwriter, and composer Billy Joel for Billy Joel: An Evening of Questions & Answers…And a Little Music,” he penned on Instagram, alongside a snap of himself singing.

The post revealed that he would be performing on Dec. 14, adding that tickets would be exclusively available to currently enrolled students at FAU.

This will mark Joel's first official performance since he announced on May 23 that he would be calling off his tour in order to focus on treatment for normal pressure hydrocephalus (NPH)—which occurs when there's a buildup of fluid in the brain, causing difficulty walking and balancing, confusion, and forgetfulness, per the Cleveland Clinic.

According to a statement released by the singer's spokesperson on Instagram, his condition has been "exacerbated by recent concert performances, leading to problems with hearing, vision, and balance," forcing him to bow out of his planned tour dates.

“Under his doctor’s instructions, Billy is undergoing specific physical therapy and has been advised to refrain from performing during this recovery period. Billy is thankful for the excellent care he is receiving and is fully committed to prioritizing his health," the announcement continued.

The statement added that the "Piano Man" singer was thankful for his fans' "support" and that he "looks forward" to performing when his health is in a better place.

"I'm sincerely sorry to disappoint our audience, and thank you for your understanding," Joel added in a quote.

At the end of the statement, fans were reassured that they won't need to take any action to receive their refunds, adding that their payments will be automatically refunded to the original form of payment.

The concert dates that were affected by the news include shows in New York City; New Orleans; Toronto; Santa Clara, CA; Salt Lake City; Washington, D.C., and Liverpool.

Months after he announced his diagnosis, his daughter, Alexa, gave fans a long-awaited update on her dad's performance schedule, telling the Hollywood Reporter that he is a "trooper."

She noted that her father was looking forward to returning to Carnegie Hall on March 19, where his daughter will be belting out his hits at a tribute event.

Alexa explained, “I said, ‘Dad, you better be there! This is Carnegie Hall. Everyone’s paying tribute to you, and it’s an amazing group of artists.' He was like, ‘Let me mark that down.’ He’s very organized with his little calendar. He was so excited when he first played Carnegie Hall in the ‘70s, so it’s exciting for him to come back decades later and see amazing artists and his daughter pay tribute to him. It’s a full-circle moment."

The 40-year-old singer-songwriter also noted that her father's health is much better.

"He’s doing physical therapy regularly, and he’s doing great," she revealed. "He’s lost weight as he’s on his diet. I’m so proud of him. He’s such a trooper, so resilient and committed to being healthy and proactive. He’s a fighter. He’s always been a fighter and talks in his documentary about how life’s like a fight.”

She added, "He played it down. He knows I have anxiety and am a neurotic worrier, so he was, like, ‘Everything’s fine. It was just a little trip.’ When I saw the footage, I was crying. But then I went with him to the doctors, and we’re on top of everything. I just tell him to stay healthy.”

“Once a performer, always a performer! But health comes first. I said, ‘If you’re going to perform again, please stay seated at the piano. No throwing the microphone stand around!’" she revealed.

Alexa's update on her father's health came mere weeks after he finally offloaded his Long Island estate.

Joel had been trying to sell a large portion of his Long Island estate since March 2025, when it was listed for $39.9 million, having initially put the entirety of the property, which is made up of three parcels, on the market for the even higher price of $49 million.

However, after months with no concrete offers, the asking price of the property's main residence was slashed, first to $29.9 million, then to $25 million.

In 2025, he sold the seven-bedroom, nine-bathroom gatehouse for $7 million. On March 6, 2026, Billy sold the main property—dubbed "Middlesea"—for $23.2 million, as well as a pair of adjoining lots that brought the total to $35.75 million.

He purchased his Long Island sanctuary in 2002 for $22.5 million before picking up two adjoining plots.

The main residence, which comes with 14.6 acres, was formerly on the market for $25 million, while the two adjacent plots were listed for $3.5 million each.

When deciding to separate the areas, Emmett Laffey, CEO of Berkshire Hathaway HomeServices Laffey International Realty, said, "The new price reflects the splitting of the two buildable waterfront lots, so the gross price remains the same."

“When [Billy Joel] bought this 20 years ago, he bought the main house. And then as he lived there, he bought the adjacent two lots, which are buildable lots, and he purchased a gatehouse. Now we’re selling it like he bought it—in pieces. The smartest way to sell this incredible land and property is the way he purchased it," Laffey previously told the Post.

Laffey noted that many super "fans" have already attempted to snap up the property and have put in several offers.

“You do have some extreme fans. It is very special. It’s a very famous home to everyone on the North Shore of Long Island," he said.

The main house sprawls over 20,000 square feet and comes with five bedrooms, six full baths, two half-baths, a playroom, a spa and hair salon, a bowling alley, and a wine cellar.

There's also an indoor pool, which Billy had covered up so he could use the space as a music room, because of its excellent acoustics.

The estate also features a three-bedroom beach house, a three-bedroom guest apartment, and a four-bedroom gatehouse, which has since been sold.

Other luxe amenities include a floating dock and boat ramp, two outdoor pools, and a helipad.

Billy previously insisted that he would still call Long Island home, but would be spending more time in Florida.

Although Billy and Alexa tend to focus on music, they have previously exchanged real estate, with him having sold his Manhattan property, a two-bedroom condo, for $4.5 million to Alexa.

Despite passing the dwelling to his own family member, the singer didn't hold back with the price, earning an impressive $1.2 million profit on the $3.3 million he paid for the Elizabeth Street dwelling in 2010, when he purchased it from his then-wife, Katie Lee.

Property records now reveal that the two-bedroom, two-bathroom dwelling has been transferred to Alexa following a $4.5 million transaction, as first reported by Crain's.

The home, which sits at 211 Elizabeth St., spans 1,600 square feet. Pre-staged photos revealed dark herringbone floors, black crown moldings, and a dark-toned chef's kitchen, according to Curbed.

Transferring the property into his daughter's name was a formality for the most part, as it has been reported that Alexa has been living in the dwelling since 2010.

Data center developers brace for new reality as election backlash sinks in

📅 Nov 10, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Challenges, breakthroughs and big ideas in the race to protect our planet. SEE MORE

by Lisa Stiffler on Nov 10, 2026 at 10:25 amNovember 10, 2026 at 10:26 am

Data center developers already knew they had a public relations problem. This month’s midterm elections showed how much of a political problem it has become.

Voters approved data center restrictions in 44 of 45 local measures across a dozen states. Candidates who support stronger regulation of the facilities won 32 of the 34 gubernatorial races called so far. And political campaigns reportedly spent more than $380 million on TV ads referencing data centers, according to the Associated Press.

The results capped a year of accelerating resistance as tech giants plan to spend hundreds of billions of dollars on new facilities.

So what comes next? More outreach, more transparency and the expectation that states and local governments will pass data center rules, said leaders in the Pacific Northwest.

“We’ve been working and adapting our approach to meet the moment,” said John Wilson, chief financial officer of Sabey Data Centers, in an interview. “There used to be a time where we would say that our track record could speak for itself, and that’s clearly no longer the case.”

Communities nationwide have raised concerns about the facilities’ effect on rising electric bills, their water use for cooling electronics and the noise they generate.

Broader fears about artificial intelligence are also stoking the pushback, including worries that it will replace workers and appropriate human-created content, and that it can provide erroneous information, foster unhealthy relationships with users, or facilitate scams or terrorist attacks.

A renewed effort to regulate data centers is expected in January in Washington state — home to the headquarters of Sabey, Microsoft and Amazon.

Earlier this year, state Rep. Beth Doglio, D-Olympia, pushed legislation that included ratepayer protections, transparency on environmental impacts and clean energy requirements. Microsoft and the national Data Center Coalition opposed the bill, which failed.

Doglio has been meeting with stakeholders, including tech companies and data center developers, and will try again in the coming legislative session under noticeably different conditions. Fellow lawmakers, she said, will take on AI’s intersection with privacy, education, labor and other issues.

“It’s going to be a very tech-heavy session,” Doglio said.

Before voters weighed in this month, tech companies launched efforts to assuage public fears.

In January, Microsoft released its “Community First AI Infrastructure” initiative, which includes promises to pay the company’s full power costs, reject local property tax breaks, replenish more water than it uses, train local workers and fund AI education and community programs.

Last month, Amazon announced its “Built Together” program, pledging to invest $1 billion over five years in the U.S. communities where it operates data centers, including support for education and civic projects. The initiative offers similar commitments to Microsoft’s on transparency and covering its energy costs.

Wilson said Sabey also works with communities to understand and meet their needs. When a proposed campus in Indianapolis triggered significant pushback, the company brought community members to Quincy, a central Washington data center hub, to tour a Sabey facility and hear from locals about their experience.

“It requires a lot of patience. It requires a lot of transparency in our communication,” Wilson said. Sabey’s Decatur Technology Park in Indianapolis is moving ahead, and the company aims to bring it online in 2030.

But the even bigger challenge now is statewide moratoriums and data center bans — a scale of resistance that is difficult to address even when there is local support.

Oregon Gov. Tina Kotek in October directed a state agency to pause processing and issuing permits for new and expanding data centers until July 2027. Umatilla, where Sabey is developing its first Oregon campus, declared a local state of emergency over the potential loss of investment and revenue, the Oregon Capital Chronicle reported.

Even with the surge in resistance, “we’ve been able to find our way through on every project that we’ve had,” Wilson said. “I’m knocking on wood as I say that.”

Corporate commitments alone may no longer be enough to win public confidence, said Brad Smith, Microsoft vice chair and president, in a GeekWire interview. Communities increasingly want laws requiring all data center developers to build responsibly — a shift he called a “watershed” moment, “but one we anticipated.”

“[P]eople want to see everyone who builds a data center do it in a responsible way,” Smith added, “and they feel that the only way that one can obtain that result is to have some new laws that are put in place.”

Come next year, that could include Washington and impact local tech giants, Sabey and others. Baxtel, a data center industry research firm, lists 29 Microsoft facilities operating in Washington and four under construction, with Sabey operating eight facilities.

Amazon plans to build its first large-scale data centers in Washington, with proposed campuses in Walla Walla County and the Tri-Cities. Amazon did not respond to repeated requests for comment on this story.

Doglio said the next version of her bill will be more stringent. “We have to have a strong bill,” she said, adding that the public and members of her party are calling for it. Microsoft did not publicly specify which provisions triggered its opposition to the previous bill.

Wilson hopes any legislation, in Washington or elsewhere, allows flexibility and recognizes that data centers vary widely in size, impact and application. Sabey provides data center space and infrastructure for customers ranging from major cloud companies to corporations and community organizations.

“Describing all data centers as one thing is just not accurate,” Wilson said, “and it doesn’t understand the realities of the industry itself.”

Smith expressed optimism that U.S. lawmakers can find a middle ground that meets the needs of communities and tech companies.

“There’s plenty of room to draft laws that enable people to go forward and build responsible data centers without chilling the economic contributions these data centers make, especially to local communities,” he said.

Oura is buying a clinical AI and on-demand doctor startup for $625 million

📅 Nov 10, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Free daily briefing on global business news.

The all-cash deal is expected to close before year-end, and Counsel's CEO will become Oura's chief clinical AI officer

Oura agreed to acquire Counsel Health, an AI-native primary care company, for $625 million in cash, the company said on Tuesday. The price is subject to adjustments, and the deal is expected to close before the end of the calendar year, subject to customary closing conditions.

Counsel combines medical AI with in-house physicians, the company said. Oura members can start from an Oura insight or chat with Counsel's AI, and the conversation can escalate to a board-certified Counsel physician within minutes, all inside the Oura app. Counsel physicians can provide diagnoses, prescriptions and referrals.

"Oura helps members understand their physiology and, with Counsel, they can take action on their health across a range of needs, from quick questions and prescriptions to lab results, chronic care, and referrals," Oura CEO Tom Hale said in a statement.

Counsel's AI draws on connected medical records, prior interactions, clinical guidelines and biometric data, the company said. Counsel will keep serving enterprise partners, including health plans, health systems and employers, after the acquisition. Oura said that could bring Counsel's model to people outside its base of over six million members.

Hale told Bloomberg the purchase is Oura's largest to date "by multiples." According to Bloomberg, Counsel CEO Muthu Alagappan will become Oura's chief clinical AI officer, and Counsel's 70 employees will join him. Counsel's physician bench, which is made up of doctors who work on demand, stands at roughly 50, according to Bloomberg, and Alagappan said he expects it to expand. Counsel has been available within Oura's app since June.

Counsel has so far reported raising $36 million, according to STAT.

Oura announced the deal alongside preliminary fiscal fourth-quarter results. Revenue for the quarter ended Sept. 30 grew 153% from a year earlier to $533 million. Adjusted EBITDA was $87 million, a 16% margin. Fiscal 2026 revenue was about $1.75 billion, up 92% year over year. Oura said the figures are unaudited and may change as it completes its year-end close and audit.

David Shuman, Oura's executive chairman, cited that revenue growth and the membership base as the basis for the investment. "Oura tells members what's happening in their bodies, and Counsel helps them act on it," Shuman said in a statement.

Oura postponed its planned Nasdaq $NDAQ initial public offering on Sept. 29, citing uncertainty in the IPO market. The offering had targeted a raise of up to $2.2 billion. Hale told Bloomberg he had nothing new to share about when a listing might happen or whether Oura would revive it.

Poland’s Parliament Votes 240-202 to Let Officials Freeze Crypto Before Court Review

📅 Nov 10, 2026  |  Category: Business  |  Read Source →

Poland's Sejm passed a sanctions bill Friday that lets the interior minister freeze bitcoin and bank funds of people on a national list, and a court appeal would not pause the freeze.

Poland’s lower house of parliament voted 240-202 on Friday to let the interior minister freeze crypto assets, bank accounts and other property of people placed on a national sanctions list, before any court reviews the order.

The Sejm, Poland’s lower house, passed the Act on Restrictive Measures at its 68th sitting, with one member abstaining and 17 not voting. The Senate received the bill Monday and has 30 days under Article 121 of the Constitution to adopt, amend or reject it, after which it would go to President Karol Nawrocki.

The law, a government bill that also implements European Union sanctions rules, treats crypto assets as financial means that can be frozen, using the definition in the EU’s Markets in Crypto-Assets regulation. A freeze, as the text defines it, is meant to stop any movement, transfer, use or other change that would let the holder benefit from the assets.

The freeze applies only to people and entities the interior minister adds to a public national list, either on the minister’s own initiative or at the request of agencies that include the financial watchdog, the central bank, the financial intelligence unit and the internal security service. Grounds include supporting a country already under EU sanctions, serious human rights abuses, or initiating or financing information manipulation that poses a serious threat to Poland’s national security.

A listing on that last ground requires a showing of a real and serious threat to Poland’s interests, borders, security or elections. The bill carves out ordinary journalism, political speech and publishing, unless that activity is a knowing campaign of the kind the law describes.

The interior minister’s decision takes effect the day after it is posted in the ministry’s Public Information Bulletin and is immediately enforceable. There is no request to the minister to reconsider. A listed person may file a complaint with an administrative court, but the law says the decision remains in force until a judgment becomes final, and it sets aside the usual rule that would let a court pause a challenged order.

The measures also bar making funds or property available to listed persons and can exclude them from public contracts. In some cases, the economy minister may appoint a temporary administrator to sell frozen assets or, under a separate order, take them over for the State Treasury.

The Poland bill does not target ordinary bitcoin holders in Poland. It reaches crypto assets owned, held or under the control of people and firms on the sanctions list. Banks, exchanges and other parties face administrative fines if they fail to freeze those assets or make them available, and compliance is checked by the financial intelligence unit and the Polish Financial Supervision Authority for the firms they already oversee.

The text does not describe a way for officials to obtain the keys to coins held in self-custody. The practical enforcement path in Poland runs through exchanges, custodians and other obliged institutions that can actually stop a transfer.

The bill still has to clear the Senate and Nawrocki’s desk. The president refused to sign a separate crypto-assets market bill the Sejm passed on May 15, and lawmakers in September failed to override that veto, voting 241-198. He had also vetoed earlier versions of that market bill, arguing the rules went too far.

That earlier fight was about licensing and supervision under the EU’s crypto-assets rulebook, not about a sanctions freeze. Whether the Senate trims the pre-court freeze, and whether Nawrocki will sign a sanctions law that reaches bitcoin, is the question the bill now leaves open.

This comes as the company also agreed to acquire River One to enter institutional asset-backed financing and announced a $50 million share repurchase program, running through December 2027.

While medical cost trend remains a pressure point for health insurers, leading companies cited some positive trends, too, heading into the final months of 2026.

UnitedHealth Group was once again the most profitable company among payers in the quarter, bringing in $4.1 billion in profit for Q3. That's up from a $2.3 billion haul in the prior-year quarter.

Through the first nine months of the year, profits were $15.9 billion at UHG, rising from $12 billion a year ago.

The healthcare giant has been in the midst of a critical financial turnaround since mid-2025, and in the most recent quarter, it highlighted some of the progress that it's logged over the past year at one of it's biggest pain centers: Optum Health.

When the company posted an uncharacteristically poor quarter to start 2025, Optum Health was cited as one of the main pressure points. During its most recent call, Optum CEO Patrick Conway, M.D., said the transition centered on a "continued return to value-based care principles" that sit at the core of Optum Health.

UnitedHealth also led the way on revenue in Q3, posting $112.7 billion. That's down slightly from $113.2 billion the company reported in the third quarter of 2025.

CVS Health had the second-highest profit and revenue in Q3, bringing in $2.2 billion in earnings for the quarter. This marks a significant reversal from the prior-year quarter, when the company posted a nearly $4 billion loss.

CVS' revenue in the quarter was $108.4 billion, increasing from $102.9 billion in the third quarter of 2025.

The company's financial performance wasn't what dominated the headlines in Q3, however, as CVS shocked the industry in announcing plans to split its pharmacy business off and form two companies. The break-up is expected to close in the first half of 2028, and would establish CVS Pharmacy as its own company while Aetna and Caremark would remain together as CVS Health.

CVS CEO David Joyner said that the shift represents "a structure and plan that are built to win and represent the best path to create value over the long term."

The Cigna Group and Elevance Health were on par with one another in Q3 on profits, both earning $1.4 billion. Cigna wins out on revenue, though, with $73.7 billion, while Elevance reported $51.4 billion.

By comparison, Cigna posted $1.9 billion in profit and $69.7 billion in revenue in Q3 2025.

Core to the third quarter conversation was the company's newly-unveiled Lead to One healthcare transformation strategy, which puts an emphasis on building a personalized experience for its members and customers.

Cigna said it plans to lean on all of the segments under its umbrella to drive toward meeting individual member needs.

Elevance Health, meanwhile, increased its profit slightly year over year from $1.2 billion in Q3 2025. Revenues in the prior-year quarter were $50.7 million.

The company was one of several insurers in the quarter that noted positive signs in its Medicaid plans. While its leaders told investors that 2026 remains a "trough year" for its Medicaid business, improvements are likely in the cards for 2027.

That trend is supported by state rate setting that more fully accounts for the rising cost of care and the acuity of people enrolled in Medicaid, executives said. The member mix is only expected to increase in acuity as states roll out work requirements at the beginning of this year.

Similarly, Centene CEO Sarah London said the company sees an opportunity for "long-term growth" in the Medicaid market as part of its Q3 report. She noted similar trends around rate setting from states.

London added that the insurer has made significant progress on focus areas for improvement, such as behavioral health.

Centene posted a $71 million loss in the quarter, slimming its losses significantly from $6.6 billion in the prior-year quarter. That massive loss a year ago was backstopped by a one-time impairment charge of more than $6 billion.

Revenues in Q3 were $50.6 billion, up slightly from $49.7 billion in the third quarter of 2025.

Humana offered color on early signals in the Medicare annual enrollment period, which suggests that the insurer is poised for further growth in Medicare Advantage for next year. CEO Jim Rechtin said sign-ups through the early parts of the window indicate 2027 membership growth.

The insurer has had momentum around new sign-ups and has also done well in recapturing enrollments that were impacted by market exits, Rechtin said.

Humana posted a $162 million profit in the third quarter, down from $195 million a year ago. Revenues were $41.15 billion, growing from $32.6 billion in the prior-year quarter.
