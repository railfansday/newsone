Wayne Rooney made Wrexham & Preston North End claim - It's unlikely anytime soon

For the next month or so, a fair portion of the population with a love of football will be intrigued to see how Wayne Rooney fares on ITV hit show, I'm A Celebrity, Get Me Out of Here.

The 41-year-old is one of the most iconic strikers ever produced in English football, having skyrocketed to fame with his iconic goal for Everton against Arsenal in 2002.

In his playing days, Rooney became Manchester United's all-time leading scorer whilst winning several trophies and individual accolades, and after a brief return to Merseyside, found himself donning the colours of Derby County in the Championship.

The former forward then earned praise for his managerial spell at Pride Park as a youthful Rams side competed valiantly in the face of adversity, and would have survived were it not for a 21-point deduction.

The less said about Rooney's dugout spells at Birmingham City and Plymouth Argyle, the better, but during the three spells, he would have learned plenty about what it takes to succeed in one of European football's toughest divisions.

However, a claim made earlier in 2026 by the former England great is certainly looking pear-shaped at this moment in time.

Following his dismissal at Home Park in December 2024, Rooney is yet to return to management but has become a regular fixture on our TV screens with Amazon Prime, as well as his analysis on Match of the Day.

He is also a regular pundit on The Overlap with Sky Sports duo, Gary Neville and Jamie Carragher, where major talking points are discussed and analysed at length, as well as being in conversation with supporters.

Earlier this year, it was put to Rooney by Carragher as to what his 'ideal' Premier League would look like, and in doing so, the former pinpointed three clubs he would like to see make the 'big time in the coming years'.

Two of those were Wrexham and Preston North End, who both reside in the Championship right now, with the Red Dragons certainly one of the EFL's most ambitious clubs under Ryan Reynolds and Rob Mac's ownership, while North End are one of the country's most historic clubs, but have not played top-level football for 65 years.

The North Wales outfit's story has gained plenty of traction, as their Hollywood owners have invested heavily in various aspects of the club, particularly on the pitch.

With Phil Parkinson at the helm, Wrexham achieved an unprecedented three successive promotions from the National League to the Championship, before adding further depth and quality last season with the likes of Callum Doyle, Issa Kabore, Lewis O'Brien, Nathan Broadhead, Kieffer Moore and Josh Windass.

Although the club have certainly spent more than the sums of cash coming into the Racecourse Ground in player sales, the strategy very nearly worked for a fourth-straight season, but a slow start saw Parkinson's side narrowly miss out on a play-off spot.

However, there haven't been many indications that promotion will be achieved this season due to an inconsistent start, in which experienced players such as Broadhead and Windass have suffered injury misfortune, as they sit 15th in the table despite a return of four points from six against high-flying Middlesbrough and Wolves before the international break.

The Lilywhites have, in recent times, developed a knack for finishing mid-table in the Championship whilst being criticised over a lack of ambition, but that didn't appear to be the case this summer.

Although a Middle-East takeover is yet to be approved, such feats, as well as the club-record signing of Alfie Devine from Tottenham were two key reasons for optimism in Central Lancashire in pre-season.

However, a malaise which saw Preston win just six of their final 23 matches last season following a positive start continued into this campaign, with Paul Heckingbottom replaced by Deepdale cult hero, Graham Alexander, last month.

That said, the Scot is yet to turn the tide and has won just one game out of seven thus far, albeit those three points came before the international break against Southampton.

As such, Rooney's hopes for Wrexham and Preston to reach the Premier League anytime soon are in serious jeopardy, with North End appearing more likely to be overtaken by the third team he wishes extreme luck towards - Stockport County.

The Hatters have, like the Red Dragons, risen from the depths of non-league in recent seasons to have such lofty ambitions under owner, Mark Stott.

Even the exit of current Sheffield Wednesday head coach, Dave Challinor, hasn't derailed County's progress despite suffering play-off agony in the past two seasons, with Jimmy McNulty's side currently second in League One, as only a two-goal swing separates themselves and Huddersfield Town right now.

Charlie Austin reacts to 'strange' Swansea City manager development

📅 Nov 20, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Ex-Premier League and EFL striker Charlie Austin has branded the decision for Vitor Matos to leave Swansea City for Strasbourg as "strange," but does admit that the French club can be a "stepping stone" to bigger things.

The Portuguese boss's departure hasn't been formally announced by Swansea just yet, but Strasbourg themselves revealed that he'd joined their side to replace the outgoing Hugo Oliveira.

In the space of a little under a year, Matos took the South Wales side from 20th in the Championship to a top-half finish last season. Then, he took them to the league's summit during the opening stages of this campaign, before a run of four defeats in five saw them slip down to sixth.

Nevertheless, he had Swansea playing an incredibly attractive, high-intensity brand of football, while also being controlled in possession, too, and it was universally agreed that the Swans had their best chances of promotion in around six years under him.

Strasbourg currently sit 13th in Ligue 1 with three wins through their opening 10 games. They aren't in Europe, and some critics would say that the challenge of getting a team like Swansea back into the Premier League would be more satisfying than taking over a struggling team in France.

However, Strasbourg are under the BlueCo banner, the multi-club ownership group which mainly oversees Chelsea, and the French top-flight side is often seen as the little sister, and sometimes even a feeder club, to its Premier League siblings.

Speaking on his "Chaz Kicks Off" YouTube podcast show, former Burnley, QPR and Southampton striker Charlie Austin spoke about how now being under that umbrella could open doors for Matos that Swansea simply couldn't.

"The Swansea manager has departed and gone to Strasbourg, which is strange because they're in a good position," he said.

"But he's obviously been promised the world [by Strasbourg]; it's the stepping stone, isn't it? And we've all seen where it could lead. We saw it with that PE teacher who went to Chelsea, et cetera, so it opens up new things."

While a slightly disrespectful way to refer to him, the "PE teacher" that Austin referenced was Liam Rosenior, who was given the Chelsea job back in January following the departure of Enzo Maresca.

The 42-year-old had spent the previous season and a half at Strasbourg, leading them to a seventh-placed finish in his only full year at the club, and left them in seventh midway through the 2025/26 campaign.

At Chelsea, despite a promising start, seeing only Arsenal beat his side for the first two months of his time at the club, things quickly unravelled, and he was sacked in late April. He's now back in France, and has Paris FC sitting third in Ligue 1, three points off their neighbours PSG atop the table.

Like Matos, Rosenior was in a Championship job before moving to Strasbourg, and was shortlisted for Manager of the Season in his year at Hull City. The two are following a similar path, and the Portuguese boss may have taken the job with that potentially in mind.

The master limited partnership (MPL) again raised its dividend by 12.5%, meaning $10,000 is generating even more income for investors.

By Todd Shriber – Nov 20, 2026 at 2:30AM EST

One of the epicenters of above-average dividend yields and, in many cases, alluring dividend growth is the energy sector, specifically pipeline stocks.

MPLX (MPLX -1.55%) is certainly part of that conversation. This master limited partnership (MLP) stores and transports natural gas and oil in the Marcellus Basin in eastern North America and the Permian Basin in Texas, among other locations.

It's one of the most impressive dividend growth stories among large-cap midstream equities, and that's saying something because that asset class is ripe with high yields and stout payout-growth trajectories.

MPLX made good on a promise to lift its distribution by 12.5% and the payout growth could continue. Image source: Getty Images.

In conjunction with its third-quarter earnings report released on Nov. 3, the company announced its third consecutive 12.5% annual dividend increase, putting it in a strong position to honor its previously announced pledge to grow its distribution by 12.5% annually through at least 2027.

With the latest dividend increase announcement, MPLX yields an eye-catching 8.5%, and its yearly payout in dollar terms vaults to $4.84 per share. So that means investors who owned $10,000 (and all shareholders, for that matter) of this midstream stock before this month are getting raises.

Based on the Nov. 13 closing price of $57.25, $10,000 buys 174.6 shares. Let's simplify the math and call that 175 shares. With the new payout of $4.84 a share, 175 shares generate $847 in yearly income. That's pretty good.

It's even better for investors who don't need that income right now. They can -- and probably should -- consider the merits of dividend reinvestment, particularly if the pipeline operator makes good on its pledge to grow its payout by 12.5% again next year.

Figure it this way: With a $10,000 stake, even if the stock were to trade flat for a year, the $847 in dividends investors will receive over the next 12 months would buy another 14.7 shares based on the Nov. 13 closing prices.

It might not sound like much, but that's nearly another $72 added to an investor's compounding mix with MPLX. Plus, based on the company's $1.5 billion in third-quarter distributable cash flow, it's generating enough cash flow to cover its shareholder rewards obligations.

I’ve reviewed every model of the Endorphin Speed and I’ve always been a big fan. While that’s still true with the Speed 6, this category contains some of the best running shoes available, and there are some lighter, bouncier fast trainers I rate very highly, like the New Balance SC Rebel v1 and Dynafish Xiaonian.

The Saucony Endorphin Speed 6 costs $180 in the U.S. and £180 in the U.K., which is a $5 price rise on the Endorphin Speed 5. It’s an expensive shoe, though not out of line with other plated trainers like the Hoka Mach X3 and New Balance SC Rebel v1.

Saucony has added 2mm of foam to the midsole of the Endorphin Speed 6, which has a stack height of 38mm at the heel and 30mm at the forefoot, maintaining the 8mm drop of past models.

It weighs in at 8.5oz, which is 0.5oz lighter than the Endorphin Speed 5 in my size, a welcome drop for a speed-focused shoe.

The Endorphin Speed 6 has a simple mesh upper that has a little bit of padding around the tongue and collar, but mostly focuses on being lightweight and breathable.

It’s my preferred kind of upper on any shoe, since it’s not oppressively padded on the tongue or overly stiff around the heel, and I found the fit comfortable and secure throughout all my runs.

The Endorphin Speed 6’s main changes are in the midsole, which is made from a new foam called PWRRUN PBX, which is meant to be lighter and bouncier than the PWRRUN PB that has been used on all past models of the shoe.

There’s also the higher stack and a redesigned nylon plate in the midsole, which aims to create a smoother, faster ride, with the Speedroll rocker on the shoe also contributing to this.

The changes to the midsole are noticeable on the run, though not as dramatically as I expected when looking at the spec sheet for the Speed 6.

Rubber is used sparingly on the bottom of the Speed 6 to keep the weight down, but patches cover the forefoot and heel of the shoe, and in my experience Saucony’s pelletized midsole foams are durable even when the foam is exposed on the bottom of a shoe.

Grip hasn’t always been a positive feature on the Speed line, but the Speed 6 provided me with reliable traction in wet conditions on both the road and the track during my testing.

After the very pleasant surprise of the original Endorphin Speed, which I ran over 300 miles in, I’ve found that the line has delivered a reliably similar experience with all of the models since.

There have been updates to make it more stable and comfortable, but ultimately all five versions of the Speed to date have had a smooth, fast and versatile ride feel.

This is still true of the Endorphin Speed 6 for me, and while that’s mostly a positive in that it performed well across a variety of runs in my testing, some might have hoped for a bigger change to challenge rivals like the SC Rebel v1 and cheaper, lighter options like the Adidas Adizero Evo SL and Dynafish Xiaonian.

I used the shoe for a couple of easy runs, two progression runs moving from easy to tempo pace, and a threshold track workout running 4 x 8min reps, and it felt great throughout.

The rockered midsole pushes you smoothly through your footstrike, and while the midsole foam isn’t as soft and springy as some, it does give a good amount of energy return off the toes.

I also found the Speed 6 to be quite stable and comfortable for easy runs, which is not always the case with plated trainers like this. I could happily use it for pretty much any kind of run, though it’s not as fast as a true carbon plate racer, or as comfortable as a max-cushioned cruiser.

To compare it directly to the Endorphin Speed 5 I did a short run wearing both shoes, and while the Speed 6 is a little bouncier and more comfortable, especially under the forefoot, I still felt the two shoes had a similar ride feel.

The Speed 6 is still a bit firmer than most super-trainers, and not as comfortable as more cushioned shoes under the toes. Some will like this more direct ride feel, but I appreciate the extra softness and bounce of a shoe like the SC Rebel v1.

If you’re a long term fan of the Endorphin Speed line the Speed 6 will be a winner. It’s a mild improvement on the previous models with extra cushion and bounce, and delivers the versatile performance you expect from the line.

There is certainly a case for getting the Speed 5 instead in sales in my opinion, but the Speed 6 is a slightly better shoe overall and a successful update.
