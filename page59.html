As Buffy the Vampire Slayer and Angel head towards their 2027 return, it's been confirmed that several fan-favorite characters will return. However, a major unanswered question means that fans don't know exactly what version of a Buffyverse icon they'll be getting.

Beginning with the 1992 movie starring Kristy Swanson, Buffy the Vampire Slayer was rebooted into an iconic 1997 TV series starring Sarah Michelle Gellar as the titular hero. Now, the story will continue, though fans aren't yet clear on where the new narrative will pick up from.

One thing fans do know is that fan-favorite characters including Spike, Faith and Tara will return. But with some of them dead by the conclusion of the original TV series and others transformed over the course of the narrative, fans don't quite know what to expect...

In March 2027, Dynamite Entertainment will publish Buffy the Vampire Slayer #1, from Kelly Thompson, Stephen Byrne, Lee Loughridge and Jeff Eckleberry. The bumper-sized issue will begin a new narrative spread across the Buffy the Vampire Slayer series and a new Angel series, from Thompson and Giulia Giacomino.

Now, thanks to a press release from Dynamite and recent interviews by Thompson, fans know some details of which characters will return. Character designs shared by Dynamite (below) have confirmed several members of the Scooby Gang, including Spike, Dawn, Tara, Faith and Anya, with preview art confirming Angel's Fred and Thompson ruling out Oz for the time being.

Meanwhile, Dynamite promises the "comics debut of a beloved Buffy fan-favorite character" who is represented by a silhouette on the cover art shared so far. While these announcements are Manna from Heaven to longtime Buffy fans, major questions have been raised about the timeline and how it affects the cast.

Thanks to details like Tara being alive and Xander having both eyes, fans have been asking questions about how the comics fit into the TV series timeline. The question is a big deal for Spike, who only gains a soul late in season 6, significantly altering his outlook and motives.

The first issue's summary referencing Buffy's death has fans speculating that the comics will pick up after season 5 of the original show, though this would mean the majority of TV Angel wouldn't be in canon. The summary reads:

Eisner-winning writer KELLY THOMPSON (Absolute Wonder Woman, Black Widow) and superstar artist STEPHEN BYRNE (Wonder Twins) present a vision of BUFFY THE VAMPIRE SLAYER you've got to read to believe! Featuring the comics debut of a beloved Buffy fan-favorite character!

Into every generation, a slayer is born. And Buffy Summers may have broken the mold. Though Buffy's powers were varied and great, it was the love and devotion she inspired that was perhaps her greatest power. And when she gave her life to save the world, the people who loved her couldn't live with that. This is that story.

Speaking to AIPT, Thompson acknowledges that she's been deliberately cagey about the timeline, while implying the comic WILL respect the show timeline up to a certain point, saying:

I’m not pinning it down. I do think that anyone who’s paying attention to the sketches and stuff that have been out, Fred is in one sketch, and Dawn is there, too. So these things suggest it has to be at least a certain point. It’s a terrific non-ness.

Thompson also promises that Buffy the Vampire Slayer #1 will act as an "atom bomb" for the characters, suggesting that while the comics will likely begin at a certain point within the TV timeline, it won't necessarily feel the need to fit into the gap between that and future seasons, likely charting its own course in terms of ongoing continuity.

One area in which this is likely to be noteworthy is Tara's characterization. Thompson notes that due to the groundbreaking nature of Willow and Tara's relationship at the time, Tara wasn't given the same compelling flaws as other characters - something that can be remedied in the new comics. Thompson says:

I don’t want people to come and think they’re getting ‘The Tara Show.’ She’s a supporting character at best in this first arc. We’ve got to build to [more interesting characterization], especially with the big reveals that are in issue #1.

From what fans know so far, it's clear that the new Buffy the Vampire Slayer and Angel series are going to be an explosive return to the franchise, coming from a writer who knows it inside and out and isn't afraid to take big swings with this new era.

At the same time, the series' sense of independence from canon and where it fits into it is likely to lead to character regression in some places and evolution in others. Fans will find out exactly how these characters will change when Buffy the Vampire Slayer returns in 2027.

Buffy the Vampire Slayer #1 is coming March 10 from Dynamite Entertainment.

A Trip Through Toyota’s New GR Garage Experience Center

📅 Nov 11, 2026  |  Category: Sports  |  Read Source →

From Ivan Stewart’s Baja-winning off-road trucks to the monstrous Toyota Eagle MkIII, this collection chronicles nearly 70 years of Toyota racing history.

Toyota’s motorsports resume is a lot longer than you might expect. Long before Gazoo Racing was stamping GR badges onto Corollas, Supras, and the occasional RAV4, Toyota was racing across the Australian Outback, battling American muscle cars in IMSA, and building some of the most successful prototype racers ever to cross the finish line.

Near the Toyota Experience Center in Plano, Texas, the automaker has gathered an impressive collection of vehicles from its racing past. There’s a little bit of everything here: a 1957 Toyopet Crown that helped launch Toyota’s international racing ambitions, a legendary desert-racing pickup, a Le Mans prototype packing 1000 horsepower, and even a few cars from more recent spec series.

Here’s a look at some of the machines that helped build Toyota’s motorsports legacy at the new GR Garage Experience Center.

From the moment you walk into Toyota’s GR Experience Center, it’s obvious nearly every inch of this place has been curated to celebrate the brand’s motorsports history. Racing memorabilia, suspension components, and die-cast cars decorate the walls and many office rooms at the unassuming facility. It’s equal parts museum and shrine that honors the Japanese company’s obsession with racing.

Toyota’s international motorsports career began not with a World Rally car, but with this. In 1957, a Toyopet Crown entered the Mobilgas Round Australia Rally, an extraordinarily demanding endurance event covering thousands of miles of Australia's unforgiving landscape. The Crown finished 47th overall and third among foreign entries. They didn’t take home the trophy for first place, but surviving the 19-day endurance trial was plenty to brag about. More importantly, the experience exposed weaknesses Toyota would address in future vehicles.

Long before the Supra, there was the 2000GT. Toyota enlisted legendary American racer and car builder Carroll Shelby to develop the beautiful sports car for SCCA competition in 1968. Only three were prepared for the program, with Scooter Patrick driving the No. 33 car pictured here. The 2000GT’s 2.0-liter inline-six produced roughly 200 horsepower in racing trim, and the Shelby-prepared Toyotas proved surprisingly competitive against Porsche. Patrick finished second in the C-Production championship standings.

Don’t let the Celica badge fool you. This is about as closely related to a production Celica as a NASCAR stock car is to the sedan it resembles. Developed by Dan Gurney’s All American Racers, the IMSA GTO Celica used a tube-frame chassis, rear-wheel drive, and a heavily turbocharged inline-four engine producing as much as 600 horsepower. In 1987, Chris Cord and Toyota captured the IMSA GTO drivers’ and manufacturers’ championships. Not bad for a highly dramatized economy car.

Few vehicles are more immediately recognizable in off-road racing than Ivan “Ironman” Stewart’s red-and-white Toyota trucks. Built by Precision Preparation Inc. (PPI), these machines helped Stewart become one of the most successful drivers in desert racing history. Across his career, Stewart accumulated 84 victories and 10 championships, including 17 Baja 500 wins and three Baja 1000 victories. His nickname came from his willingness to tackle grueling desert events without a co-driver. Just imagine spending hundreds of miles bouncing around inside this thing. Sadly, most of us have only driven one of these via the Ironman “Ivan” Offroad Racing arcade game.

My absolute favorite in the room, and easily the most intimidating. Developed by Dan Gurney’s All American Racers, the Eagle MkIII competed in IMSA’s outrageously fast GTP category during the early 1990s. A turbocharged inline-four engine produced more than 800 horsepower, while its sophisticated aerodynamics generated enormous downforce. With Juan Manuel Fangio II leading the charge, the Eagle helped Toyota capture consecutive IMSA GTP manufacturers’ championships in 1992 and 1993. At one point, the Eagle won 17 consecutive races. It was so dominant that very little competition remained.

The Lexus LFA was already an engineering spectacle, thanks largely to its screaming 4.8-liter V-10. But Toyota wasn’t content to let its supercar spend its life sitting in collectors’ garages. The LFA became an important part of the early Gazoo Racing effort, participating in grueling endurance races at the Nürburgring. The program also helped Toyota develop its philosophy of using motorsports to improve road cars, with company chairman Akio Toyoda personally participating in racing and vehicle development.

Lexus decided that another 10 horses, sharper suspension, quicker shifts, and a gigantic fixed carbon-fiber wing were needed for the LFA. And they were right. The resulting Nürburgring Package was originally limited to just 50 examples. In 2011, one lapped the Nürburgring in 7:14.64, an astonishing time for a road car of its era. Just to the right of the LFA sits a Toyota Supra MkIV, the very one Toyota master driver Hiromu Naruse used to teach Akio Toyoda how to hustle a car.

Before Toyota finally conquered the 24 Hours of Le Mans, it built this legendary hybrid prototype. The TS040 combined a 3.7-liter V-8 with electric motors driving both axles, producing approximately 1000 horsepower. Rather than conventional batteries, the car used supercapacitors to store energy recovered during braking. Toyota won both the World Endurance Championship manufacturers’ and drivers’ titles in 2014, before introducing an extensively revised version of the TS040 for 2015.

The Lexus RC F GT3 seen here, yawning with its giant grille, has spent many years competing in motorsports. Its 5.4-liter V-8 engine helped Vasser Sullivan Racing become a formidable competitor in IMSA, despite facing newer, turbocharged rivals. The team captured the 2023 GTD Pro championship, with victories at Watkins Glen and Long Beach. It followed that up with a class victory at the 2024 12 Hours of Sebring. 2026 was the RC F’s final year in IMSA, and it will be replaced at the 24 Hours of Daytona in January by the GR GT3 race car.

The Supra’s return to the street also brought a new Toyota race car. Developed for customer racing in the GT4 category, the GR Supra GT4 shares its 3.0-liter turbocharged inline-six with the road-going Supra, but adds suspension, brakes, aerodynamics, and a stripped-out interior that’s only tolerable in race cars. Its initial specification produced approximately 430 horsepower, subject to racing balance-of-performance regulations. Unlike the wild prototypes elsewhere in this collection, the Supra GT4 remains closely related to something you might drive to work.

Think of the GR86 Cup Car as the affordable end of Toyota’s motorsports spectrum. Built for Toyota’s single-make GR Cup racing series, which began in 2023, the car retains the production model’s 2.4-liter flat-four engine but replaces its manual transmission with a six-speed sequential gearbox. The goal is to provide drivers with relatively accessible, closely matched machinery that emphasizes driving talent instead of checkbook size. Plus, it’s one of the few cars in the museum that you can also drive in iRacing.

Developed for SRO’s TC America touring-car championship, the GR Corolla TC starts with Toyota’s 300-hp, turbocharged 1.6-liter three-cylinder engine and retains the GR-Four all-wheel-drive system. Interestingly, it also uses the production car’s eight-speed automatic transmission. The transformation includes upgraded suspension, Alcon brakes, a roll cage, and aggressive aerodynamic bodywork.

Institutional investors will be able to access Bitcoin and Ether trading through an expanded relationship between 24X and Standard Chartered. In a November 10 announcement, 24X Bermuda said the banking group will extend its prime-brokerage role from foreign-exchange products to digital assets on 24X’s multi-asset technology platform.

TL;DR: 24X says Standard Chartered is expanding its prime-brokerage relationship to cover BTC and ETH. The arrangement is aimed at institutional counterparties accessing crypto and FX through one execution infrastructure. It changes the trading and counterparty workflow for eligible participants; it is not a retail exchange rollout.

The company’s stated model allows an institutional participant to face Standard Chartered as its central counterparty while using 24X for execution, matching, liquidity configuration and straight-through processing. That separation matters. The participant’s commercial relationship and credit exposure are handled differently from the technology that matches orders.

Traditional foreign exchange and spot cryptocurrency trading do not have identical market structures or risk profiles. FX markets have established bank relationships, while crypto markets operate around the clock and rely on a patchwork of exchanges, brokers and custody providers. An integrated platform can simplify some operational tasks, although it still requires clients to understand the product-specific terms.

The release says 24X already offered FX, non-deliverable forwards and spot services. BTC and ETH are the additional digital-asset instruments named in the expanded partnership. It would be misleading to claim the agreement immediately covers every token, derivative or jurisdiction.

A recognized bank acting as a central relationship may appeal to institutions that want more familiar onboarding, credit arrangements and oversight. It does not remove Bitcoin volatility, market liquidity problems or the possibility of disruptions in the surrounding infrastructure. Nor should the word ‘prime’ be used as a shortcut for saying all trades are insured or guaranteed.

Institutions will need to evaluate exposure limits, settlement timing, custody arrangements, collateral terms and what happens during market stress. The distinction between an execution venue and the organization responsible for a trading obligation is especially important for crypto, where the history of market failures has made counterparty concentration a persistent concern.

NewsBTC previously looked at Ripple Prime’s effort to bring traditional markets into 24-hour trading workflows. That is a distinct product and operator, but it reflects the same strategic demand: customers increasingly want to manage digital and conventional exposures without jumping between disconnected systems.

The announcement establishes the partnership expansion, but it does not provide a full ledger of live client trades, volumes or fee terms. Commercial success depends on whether eligible counterparties find the combined trading and brokerage model attractive enough to move business from existing venues.

As tokenized securities and digital assets enter more established markets, investors may become less focused on whether a blockchain is present and more concerned with the legal machinery behind settlement. Our report on the NYSE’s tokenized securities infrastructure partners describes why those arrangements matter. For 24X, the next proof point will be active institutional adoption rather than the breadth of the announcement alone.

Now, she's leading one of the fall season's sneakiest hits.

Directed by Alex Huston Fischer and Eleanor Wilson, "Wicker" opened in theaters on the weekend of October 23, pulling in a solid $5.7 million domestically on a reported $10 million budget. That's not bad, but it debuted at number five on the charts, and it's easy for movies like this to slip by pretty quickly. However, it's managed to hold very well, even as "Godzilla Minus Zero" ruled the box office this past weekend.

As of this writing, "Wicker" has earned $20 million domestically to go with $9 million internationally for a $29 million running total. It hasn't dropped more than 24% on a single weekend, either, meaning that it still has a lot of gas in the tank. Thus, on top of already being in a good position thanks to its reasonable budget, it's on track to profit theatrically before VOD and streaming are even factored in.

Sad to say, that's not the case all too often for smaller, non-franchise movies these days. The film's backer Black Bear has also had a rough start as a distributor in Hollywood, with its Sydney Sweeney vehicle "Christy" flopping at the box office in high-profile fashion last year. This is the hit Black Bear needed.

"Wicker," as it were, centers on an ostracized fisherwoman (Colman) who commissions the local basketmaker (Peter Dinklage) to weave her a husband (Skarsgård). But when this creation proves unexpectedly extraordinary, jealousy and upheaval threatens their quiet village.

It's good news for the movie business in general when a new distributor can emerge on the scene and find success — especially with Warner Bros. and Paramount having recently merged under one roof. Add in Disney having already absorbed Fox in 2019, and Hollywood continues to shrink. So, as much as Black Bear has struggled in the past, it's starting to figure things out.

Indeed, it's been tough sledding for the studio up to this point. Recall that Henry Cavill and Jake Gyllenhaal's action movie "In the Grey" bombed at the box office earlier this year after Lionsgate dumped it and Black Bear picked it up. Matthew McConaughey's "The Rivals of Amziah King" didn't pan out for the studio, either. But if it can emulate the success it's finding with "Wicker" (which is easier said than done, admittedly), there's reason to be optimistic.

Broadly speaking, both theaters and the Hollywood system could use more sleeper hits like this. 2026 has had a lot of bright spots at the box office, among them "Obsession" and "Project Hail Mary," but having smaller movies that can thrive is key to a healthy marketplace. Brad Pitt's survival thriller "Heart of the Beast" also became a sleeper hit while you weren't looking, but that was an expensive movie with an A-list lead. It's not quite the same thing.

Instead, "Wicker" feels closer to Disney turning "Barbarian" into a sleeper hit in 2022. That, in turn, paved the way for director Zach Cregger to make hits like "Weapons" and "Resident Evil." So, yes, we could absolutely use more movies like "Wicker." At the right place and with the right premise, more original, non-franchise ideas can still thrive.

You’ll be able to access Visual Intelligence from a new tab in the tablet's Camera app.

Apple users willing to install beta versions of its OS updates can get their hands on upcoming features sooner than most, as long as they're comfortable contending with the possibility of buggy software or apps breaking. And one such feature is the Siri Mode that's already available in the camera app on supported iPhones. It's also coming to compatible iPads as part of iPadOS 27.3 and will let you ask Siri AI questions about things you point your tablet's camera at.

We got our first look at this mode when Apple properly unveiled Siri AI at its Worldwide Developers Conference in June this year. It basically brings the company's Visual Intelligence tool for asking questions from photos to the camera app. But there's one distinguishing feature that's unique to iPads which isn't available on iPhones.

On iPads, Visual Intelligence includes Apple Pencil support. So, much like with Google's Circle to Search feature, you can snap a photo of something with your iPad, use your Apple Pencil to draw a circle around an item and learn more information about the object with the help of Siri AI.

It's worth noting that Visual Intelligence is already available on compatible tablets — iPad Pro and iPad Air with the M1 chip and newer, and iPad mini with the A17 Pro or A20 Pro chipset. As things stand, you can access it in the Camera app by long-pressing the Camera Control button or pressing the Action button if you've set it up to activate the feature (and your iPad has an Action button, of course).

In iPadOS 27.3, you'll have another way to access Visual Intelligence in the Camera app. All you'll have to do is swipe through the options in the viewfinder (i.e. photo, video and portrait modes) until you get to the Siri tab.

The company is expected to release the public version of iPadOS 27.3 in December. However, you can try it on your tablet right now, because Apple released the first beta in early November.

To install the iPadOS 27.3 beta, open the Settings app on your iPad, then tap General > Software Update. If you haven't already done so, you'll need to enable beta updates. Tap the Beta Updates option, then iPadOS 27 Public Beta. After you go back to the Software Update tab, you should be able to install the iPadOS 27.3 beta.

Before you install the beta, it's best to create a full backup of your iPad using iCloud, a Mac or a Windows PC. Beta builds aren't the finished product, meaning they're more susceptible to bugs and errors. Backing up your data should be standard practice anyway, but it's especially advisable to do that before installing a beta version of an operating system, just to make sure your files and other information stay safe.

If you don't feel comfortable enough to install a iPadOS beta, that's perfectly fine! The public version of the OS update is likely coming soon. You can check out the Siri tab on your iPad's Camera app at that point.
