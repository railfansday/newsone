AG Blanche, FBI’s Patel dodge questions about Jensen Huang investigation

📅 2026-11-24  |  Category: Business  |  Read Source →

Attorney General Todd Blanche and FBI Director Kash Patel on Tuesday dodged questions about the conclusions of an investigation President Donald Trump cited to push Nvidia's board to bring back Jensen Huang as CEO.

Asked by a reporter whether the Department of Justice had investigated Huang and the results of that probe, Blanche said, "What the president said is that he talked to a lot of people going into the decision that he made to encourage the board to reinstate him."

"And so I'm not going to get into communications I have with the president," Blanche said at a news conference in Washington, D.C.

Huang was reinstated as CEO of Nvidia on Monday morning, less than two weeks after resigning amid allegation had had affairs with a purported spy for China and two Nvidia employees, as well as concerns that advance semiconductor chips made by his company had been diverted to China in violation of U.S. law.

In an interview with CNBC on Monday, Trump was asked what led him to personally confirm to Nvidia's board that Huang was not a national security risk.

"We've done a very thorough investigation, and the investigation showed that he did, I mean, he had bad judgment having to do with somewhat unrelated incidents, but he did nothing wrong," Trump replied.

Trump in a Truth Social post on Monday said the U.S. government had no reason to believe Huang's return to the company "poses any risk to National Security," and that Blanche had confirmed the government "does not possess evidence that he has engaged in any unlawful conduct."

Nvidia, in its statement Monday announcing Huang's return, said, "The Board received input from senior levels of the United States government, and based on that input, the Board is confident that Mr. Huang's reinstatement poses no national security risk."

Blanche on Tuesday said, "Nvidia is a good corporate citizen."

"Obviously, if Nvidia was the target, or was the subject of some criminal investigation, or they were obstructing or thwarting our investigation, then that wouldn't be the message that the president is saying to the American people," Blanche said.

Patel did not answer a reporter's question of whether the FBI has spoken to Huang, and whether the FBI had been made aware of an assessment by U.S. intelligence agencies that Huang's reported paramour, Alice Huang, was an asset for China's spy agency.

Jensen Huang is not related to Alice Huang, who was an executive at Megaspeed, a Singapore-based customer of Nvidia's semiconductor chips.

The DOJ and Commerce Department on Nov. 17 said they were investigating Megaspeed for potential diversion of Nvidia chips in violation of U.S. export law.

Asked how the DOJ could rule out Jensen Huang having potential criminal liability given the fact that the investigation remains ongoing, Blanche replied, "So, when you say the export investigation is still ongoing, I don't know what you're talking about."

"We have hundreds, if not more, export investigations going at any time at the Department of Justice," Blanche said.

<a href="https://www.fiercehealthcare.com/payers/fewer-choices-available-enrollees-aca-exchanges-rwjf" hreflang="en">Fewer choices available to enrollees on ACA exchanges: RWJF</a>

📅 2026-11-24  |  Category: Business  |  Read Source →
Fewer choices available to enrollees on ACA exchanges: RWJF" style="max-width:100%; height:auto; border-radius:8px;" loading="lazy">

The number of regions where patients have limited choices for Affordable Care Act plans is on the rise, according to a new analysis.

The Robert Wood Johnson Foundation report found that for the 2027 plan year, there are more than 200 counties in which there is only one insurer available, and close to 1,000 where there are just two plans available for consumers to choose from.

This makes for an increase of 50% from the 2026 plan year, with more than a third of counties having limited options on the ACA's marketplaces for 2027.

Most states (39) will have at least one county where plan choices are limited for enrollees. For about half of these states, this is the norm and aligns with 2026, but for the rest, this marks a significant shift, the RWJF researchers wrote in the report.

"For the individual market as a whole, the decline in participation is sobering but not a fatal blow," per the report. "The presence of federal and some state subsidies provides an important backstop that will likely prevent a complete meltdown."

"Yet the prospect of a growing number of counties with little insurer participation is grim, and likely will not bode well for premium increases, morbidity or enrollment going forward," according to the report.

The foundation cites a recent analysis from Wakely that found the individual market declined by 16% in the first six months of 2026. The end of the enhanced premium tax credits, cost pressures and growing administrative burdens are likely to contribute to worsening trends in this space.

For instance, ACA marketplace exits from Cigna, CareSource and Medica are having a significant impact in Iowa and Indiana for the coming plan year, the researchers found. In the latter case, most Indiana counties have two or fewer plan choices.

However, there are some bright spots, RWJF researchers wrote. There are certain insurers participating in the exchanges who have beefed up their footprints, and there is a smattering of new entrants. Oscar Health, for example, is planning the largest growth, entering a new state and adding new counties in some of the states it already serves.

Some insurers, like Centene and UnitedHealthcare, are making strategic exits in some markets while entering others, for a neutral impact on their footprints overall.

Insurers like Antidote Health and Peak Health are also entering new regions—even if on a limited basis in some cases—which can help staunch the bleeding.

Blue Cross Blue Shield plans also often play a key role in limited markets, as in close to 80% of counties with a single insurer, that payer is a Blues plan. In addition, in 12% of counties, the single insurer is Elevance Health, which operates plans as Anthem Blue Cross Blue Shield.

"This suggests that were it not for the somewhat unique role of Blues plans as a backstop, the threat of a bare county might be higher," per the report. "Single-state Blues, in particular, are under social and political pressure to participate in much if not all of their state, and do not feel as free to exit unprofitable counties as are publicly traded insurers."

<a href="https://www.fiercehealthcare.com/payers/voters-expect-lawmakers-address-healthcare-affordability-access-poll" hreflang="en">Voters expect lawmakers to address healthcare affordability, access: poll</a>

📅 2026-11-24  |  Category: Health  |  Read Source →
Voters expect lawmakers to address healthcare affordability, access: poll" style="max-width:100%; height:auto; border-radius:8px;" loading="lazy">

Half of voters said they are paying more this year in premiums and out-of-pocket costs, according to a new national poll.

Lake Research Partners and Bellwether Research & Consulting surveyed 1,000 voters between Oct. 20 and Oct. 28 on behalf of the National Health Council, and 50% said their premiums and out-of-pocket costs are higher this year compared to last year. Among those with commercial insurance, 63% said their premiums have risen, and 60% said their out-of-pocket costs have grown year-over-year.

In addition, 25% of those surveyed said they had a claim for a prescription denied, and 24% said they waited more than two weeks for a prior authorization decision. Twenty-one percent said that they struggled to find a specialist.

Healthcare costs and the broader economy were major factors in Democrats' significant victories in the latest midterm election. And the NHC poll finds that voters expect Congress to act on addressing the costs that they face.

Most of the surveyed voters (78%) said they believe that the healthcare system warrants major changes or a complete overhaul, and 84% said they would support a candidate that would make it a priority to reform the healthcare insurance system.

However, 74% of those surveyed rated the effort from elected officials to address affordability and access as either fair or poor.

“Holding onto a plan isn’t the same as being protected by it. Patients are watching their costs rise and their coverage change, and they’re worried about what that means for their families down the road,” said Randall Rutta, CEO of the National Health Council, in an announcement. “The new Congress has a real opportunity to change that, and patients across the country will be watching.”

The survey highlights a number of potential policies that would have broad voter support. For example, 91% of those surveyed said they would support eliminating surprise medical bills and policies to require price transparency.

In addition, 90% said they would back efforts to reduce out-of-pocket costs, while 89% said they favor requiring health plans to cover treatments that were prescribed by doctors. Eighty-eight percent support efforts to increase screening and early detection for medical risks.

"Voters aren't asking lawmakers to talk about health care differently," said Christine Matthews, president of Bellwether Research and Consulting, in the release. "They want to see it work differently. Reform is a priority they expect the new Congress to deliver on."

U.S. is preparing to raise tariffs on China, the E.U., and 14 other nations

📅 Nov 24, 2026  |  Category: Other  |  Read Source →

The U.S. Trade Representative is expected to unveil results of its industrial subsidies investigation as soon as Tuesday

Allen J. Schaben / Los Angeles Times via Getty Images

The U.S. is getting ready to hike duties on imports from China, the European Union and 14 other economies in response to their industrial subsidies, according to The Wall Street Journal.

U.S. Trade Representative Jamieson Greer could announce the results of his office's investigation as soon as Tuesday, according to Bloomberg. The timing could change, according to The Wall Street Journal. Tariff rates and an effective date are unclear, according to Bloomberg.

The probe falls under Section 301 of the 1974 Trade Act, a provision that lets a president act alone in levying tariffs in response to unfair trade conduct, according to Bloomberg. The Office of the U.S. Trade Representative opened the investigation in March, following the high court's invalidation of President Donald Trump's earlier worldwide tariffs.

Greer has said that state backing, subsidies among it, has left the targeted economies with more industrial production capacity than they need. He has said that this lets companies in those economies undercut competitors abroad on price, which hurts American manufacturers. The U.S. Trade Representative's office declined to comment, according to The Wall Street Journal and Bloomberg. The White House did not respond to requests for comment from The Wall Street Journal or Bloomberg.

The new tariffs are expected to apply to the bulk of goods coming from the targeted nations, among them China, Japan, South Korea and the E.U.'s 27 member states, according to The Wall Street Journal. Steel, aluminum and automobiles are expected to be left out because they are already subject to duties under another law tied to national security.

Administration officials have said they plan to set rates close to the ones Trump put in place during 2025, ahead of the Supreme Court's invalidation of his initial round of tariffs in February 2026, according to The Wall Street Journal. Countries with trade agreements, including Japan, the E.U. and South Korea, are expected to face rates of 15% or lower. Vietnam and India could be hit with steeper rates that reflect the agreements they struck with Trump.

China is expected to be charged a steeper tariff than many of its peers because Trump officials have described its subsidies as especially far-reaching, according to The Wall Street Journal. The administration could add wording that would let it increase tariffs if a country's overcapacity in industry grows worse or if the country fails to change its policies during later talks.

The new duties are expected to stack on top of tariffs announced in July over forced labor, according to The Wall Street Journal. Those tariffs, which run from 10% to 12.5% on about 60 economies, are being contested in a lawsuit before the Court of International Trade, according to Bloomberg.

<a href="https://www.fiercehealthcare.com/providers/academic-hospital-heads-petition-congress-2-year-delay-cms-340b-cuts" hreflang="en">Academic hospital heads petition Congress for 2-year delay to CMS' 340B cuts</a>

📅 2026-11-24  |  Category: Business  |  Read Source →
Academic hospital heads petition Congress for 2-year delay to CMS' 340B cuts" style="max-width:100%; height:auto; border-radius:8px;" loading="lazy">

Dozens of academic health system and teaching hospital chief executives are turning to Congress in a bid to blunt a controversial subsidy cut coming to Medicare Part B in the new year.

Earlier this month, the Centers for Medicare and Medicaid Services locked in plans to reduce reimbursements for outpatient drugs dispensed by participants in the 340B Drug Pricing Program during the 2027 calendar year.

Specifically, the agency said it will move from the current rate of average sales price (ASP) plus 6% down to ASP minus 28%, with a corresponding 7.5% increase in non-drug outpatient payments to maintain budget neutrality. CMS said the change will reduce Original Medicare drug payments by an estimated $4.35 billion and drug payments from beneficiaries by $1.1 billion during the policy’s first year.

Hospital groups—particularly those that chiefly represent nonprofit or safety-net hospitals—have denounced the plan’s disproportionate effect on hospitals participating in the decades-old subsidy program, as well as a gift to for-profit hospitals that do not participate but will benefit from the budget-neutral offset.

With their formal opposition to the policy’s proposal set aside by CMS, 95 executives whose organizations are members of the Association of American Medical Colleges have now penned a letter to leaders of the House and the Senate pleading for a two-year delay to the rate cut’s implementation.

“This policy will exacerbate already negative Medicare margins at a time when teaching hospitals are already grappling with rising financial challenges resulting from inflation, workforce shortages, manufacturer-driven restrictions on the 340B program and an expected rise in uncompensated care costs,” they wrote.

The executives acknowledged that the final policy’s rate of ASP minus 28% is “slightly smaller” than CMS’ initial proposal of ASP minus 33.4%, but said the change was still a threat to the country’s healthcare safety net. They noted the cuts represent a nearly $5.5 billion reduction that would be disproportionately shouldered by AAMC-member teaching hospitals—who comprise 17% of all impacted hospitals—but will see almost $3 billion of that total.

Those funds are necessary to finance critical care services at academic hospitals including trauma care, inpatient behavioral care, transplant surgery and labor and delivery, the signatories warned lawmakers. They also noted that their institutions are major referral hubs that accept “the most complex transfer cases from lower acuity settings, such as rural and community hospitals,” in a way that for-profit hospitals and others who will benefit from the final rule do not.

“While we understand that Congress has an interest in improving the 340B program, and are broadly supportive of meaningful transparency and accountability for covered entities, we also strongly believe that cutting Medicare payment to 340B hospitals is not the appropriate policy mechanism to achieve these goals,” they wrote. “Therefore, we respectfully request that you support a two-year delay in the [Outpatient Prospective Payment System] rule’s payment cuts for 340B-acquired drugs.”

Among the letter’s 95 signatories were the heads of Cedars-Sinai, Stanford Health Care, Yale New Haven Health, Tampa General Hospital, Rush University System for Health, Mass General Brigham, Nebraska Medicine, RWJBarnabas Health, NewYork-Presbyterian, UNC Health and School of Medicine, OHSU, Jefferson Health, Penn Medicine, Vanderbilt Health and Froedtert ThedaCare.

The academic health systems and hospitals’ turn to lawmakers comes after their rebukes of CMS’ 340B policy have fallen on deaf ears. The agency and broader Trump administration have often sided with critics of the subsidy program on related initiatives like a pilot rebate program, citing 340B’s rapid growth and contribution to increased healthcare spending. A Health and Human Services Office of the Assistant Secretary for Planning and Evaluation policy brief defending the rate cut made those views crystal clear with claims that the program has incentivized provider consolidation while doing little to serve patients’ needs.

Meanwhile, Congress, spurred by public pressure to reduce healthcare costs, has proposed its own legislative fixes to 340B. Though each bill aims to preserve the program and subsidize safety-net care, some are more friendly to the hospital industry than others.

Jensen Huang is back as the CEO of Nvidia, and is now tasked with selling the company's artificial intelligence vision while also defending himself in Washington.

As Nvidia employees celebrated the return of their leader to the company's headquarters, Huang told the gathered crowd, "I'll be a better CEO the second time around."

Huang will need to be a better CEO to head off the issues surrounding Nvidia and the broader AI industry exposed by Huang's exit. The challenges include a proliferating roster of investigations and hearings from Congress, regulators and state-level officials, as well as technical and competitive questions about how long Nvidia's currently dominant graphics processing units can remain on top.

"There's nothing prohibiting him from doing what he does on his day job," said Katten chair and former federal prosecutor Gil Soffer. But investigations take significant prep time, Soffer said. "You don't just walk in cold."

At Nvidia's GTC conference in Washington, D.C., next week, the company will attempt to convince customers, partners and suppliers that it is developing the future of AI. At the same time, lawmakers will be pressing its newly reinstated CEO over what he knew and when about Megaspeed executive Alice Huang.

"People will want to see that it's business as usual, which hopefully for Nvidia shareholders will mean that Jensen Huang announces some new exciting products," said Babson College associate professor Peter Cohan.

Right now, Nvidia has Ian Buck, vice president of hyperscale and high-performance computing, filling the slot. A representative for Nvidia declined to answer multiple questions about whether Huang would appear at the company's event.

Anthropic announced on November 24, 2026 that Skills over MCP is in public beta in Claude and Claude Code, while Tasks and Elicitation are generally available and File Uploads will roll out soon. The additions let connected servers supply workflow guidance, run lengthy jobs, request user input and, once File Uploads arrives, exchange files with Claude.

The features are built around the open Model Context Protocol (MCP) and its extension system, allowing supporting servers to work in compatible clients beyond Claude. Anthropic reports more than 700 million monthly MCP SDK downloads and over 10,000 MCP connectors for Claude, describing the ecosystem it is asking to implement the new server capabilities.

Tool names and descriptions tell Claude what a server can do; skills add guidance on how to handle particular use cases. A server can publish best practices, step-by-step workflows or playbooks tailored to a user’s account or organization alongside its tools. Claude retrieves a skill only when needed, and updates arrive with the server, so users do not install anything separately.

The Skills extension documentation defines a skill as a directory containing a SKILL.md file and optional supporting files, with discovery and retrieval handled through MCP’s existing Resources primitive. Hosts may not fetch files ahead of need. Before use, they must verify each file’s byte size and SHA-256 digest and compare the instruction file’s frontmatter with the approved manifest; changed files require fresh approval. SDK and host support across clients is still being implemented.

The beta is on by default for Pro, Max and Ultra plans, available to Team plans after an administrator enables it, and listed as coming soon to Enterprise. Anthropic’s examples include Shopify for designing and updating online stores, HubSpot for customer onboarding and Atlassian for organization-specific analysis. A forthcoming Carta skill is intended to draft and issue equity from an uploaded document.

Tasks and Elicitation are available to Pro, Max and Ultra plans in Claude and Claude Code and are rolling out to Team and Enterprise organizations. Tasks lets a server run a time-consuming operation or a full agent loop in the background while Claude continues other work and retrieves the result later. ZoomInfo uses it for deeper account and contact research that previously required several prompts.

The Tasks documentation says the server returns a durable task handle instead of blocking the connection. Clients can poll for progress and resume with the same task ID after a disconnect or restart. States cover working, input required, completion, failure and cancellation, and a task can request input before proceeding. Cancellation is cooperative, so the server acknowledges the request without being obligated to stop the underlying work.

Elicitation lets a server pause for user input through a form or link before continuing. Supabase uses it to confirm paid-project costs and destructive SQL, Railway to ask before potentially destructive changes, and GitHub to request a repository name before deletion. The Elicitation specification separates structured form input from URL-based interactions and bars servers from using forms to request passwords, API keys, access tokens or payment credentials. Clients must identify the requesting server and provide decline and cancel options.

Anthropic plans File Uploads for Claude and Claude Code on Pro, Max, Ultra and eligible Team and Enterprise plans. It will allow PDFs, spreadsheets and images to pass to MCP servers without going through the model’s context window.

The File Uploads repository makes its earlier-stage status explicit: the extension is experimental, is not part of the MCP specification and has not been proposed for inclusion, so breaking changes are expected. Its design keeps file bytes out of model context and JSON-RPC messages. A server marks file arguments, the client uploads missing bytes and retries, and a tool can return a download target in result metadata.

Anthropic also reports that on-demand tool search can reduce the context used by tool definitions by more than 85% in multi-server setups. That efficiency focus connects the separate additions: skills load only when needed, tasks avoid holding a connection open, and file transfers are designed to keep bytes out of model context. The result is a broader coordination layer for agent work, whose reach depends on servers and clients implementing the same extensions and on the file-transfer design stabilizing.

Anorak Speeds Up Reference-Guided AI Video Without Retraining the Model

📅 Nov 24, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Anorak’s PCRG technique cuts repeated work in reference-guided video generation, delivering a 1.49× to 1.57× speedup in tests on MiniMax-H3.

A video generator asked to follow reference footage can spend a surprising share of its time revisiting details that barely change: an empty sky, a flat wall, or a stretch of road. In a release on November 24, Anorak said its AI lab had developed a way to cut that repeated work. The method improved end-to-end generation speed by about 1.5 to 1.6 times on two benchmarks in the team’s research preprint, without retraining the underlying model. A separate result that exceeded twice the original speed combined the method with another optimization.

The distinction matters for anyone evaluating faster video generation. The authors measured one model, a version of MiniMax-H3 already optimized with eight denoising timesteps and 8-bit quantization. The findings suggest there was still meaningful room to reduce computation in an already streamlined system. They do not show that the method halves generation time across video models or makes them responsive enough for live interaction.

Reference-guided generation lets a creator provide more than a prompt. An image can supply the appearance of a character, while a rough animation can show the motion or composition a finished clip should follow. In the architecture studied by the researchers, the model represents those references as tokens and examines them alongside the tokens for the output it is generating. Reference tokens account for roughly half of that combined sequence in the configuration described in the paper. Longer sequences are expensive because full attention compares tokens with one another; the number of those comparisons grows roughly with the square of sequence length.

Yet not every part of a reference contributes equally. A patch of sky that barely changes from frame to frame adds less new information than a moving subject. There is an important exception: a static detail can still be central to what the creator asked for. A logo on a moving car may be visually repetitive across the reference but essential to the shot.

Anorak calls its method PCRG, short for pruning and caching of reference tokens for video generation. It uses two signals the system already produces. The first indicates whether the compressed visual information is repetitive. The second, drawn from the part of the system that reads the visual inputs and prompt together, helps identify which pieces of the reference are relevant to the output.

The method keeps processing tokens that add distinctive information. It processes repetitive but relevant tokens once, then reuses part of that computation as the clip is refined. It drops repetitive tokens that the system identifies as less relevant before those refinement passes start. Instead of asking the model to do fewer rounds of refinement, the method reduces how much reference information it has to reconsider in each round. That difference explains why it can be combined with some other speedups.

The researchers reported a 1.57× end-to-end speedup on their V2V-Bench test suite and 1.49× on the public VACE-Benchmark. Their VBench quality measure declined by 0.24% and 0.17%, respectively. Combining PCRG with Veda reached 2.15× on V2V-Bench. These are author-reported results, and a small change in an aggregate score cannot rule out errors in an individual clip.

For Anorak, the research is part of a broader effort to make generated footage respond to players. Workflows that repeatedly adjust a reference or generate alternative takes could benefit even if the technology falls short of real-time play. How far the gains extend is an open question: a workflow with short or little reference material has less redundant input to remove, and a model that handles references differently may not see the same improvement. The next useful evidence would be a comparison across more model families and longer, more varied inputs, with timings and visual quality reported together.

Interhuman AI Tests How Models Read Social Signals Beyond the Transcript

📅 Nov 24, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Social Signal Bench tests how multimodal models interpret observable cues in audio and video without treating human judgments as certain.

A pause in conversation can signal hesitation, uncertainty, or simply that someone is thinking. A transcript may not record the pause at all. Interhuman AI’s Social Signal Bench, publicized on November 24, asks AI models to identify observable signals like these from video and audio, then scores them against the judgments of trained human raters. Its most useful feature is how it handles an awkward problem: two trained observers can watch the same clip and reasonably come away with different labels.

The research preprint describes a video track with ten signals, including interest, skepticism, and confusion, and an audio track with six. A model can predict more than one signal in a clip. The held-out test sets contain 2,372 video clips and 1,094 audio clips. Interhuman has made the dataset card and training data publicly available, while keeping the expert-labeled test material separate for hosted evaluation.

In a conventional exact-label score, confusing hesitation with uncertainty is simply an error. So is confusing hesitation with agreement. Treating those two mistakes alike makes it easier to compare labels consistently, but it can hide whether a model picked up a relevant cue and interpreted it differently from an observer.

Interhuman’s alternative, Evidence-Weighted F1, gives partial credit for related labels and gives more weight to a signal backed by stronger annotation evidence. Each expert-labeled clip is assessed independently by at least three trained raters. Their labels capture when a behavior was visible or audible, how strong it appeared, and how certain the observer was about the label. Agreement among raters is useful, but disagreement isn’t automatically discarded. If observers had different reasonable readings, the scoring method can retain evidence for each.

That does not mean a model can earn an easy score by listing everything it might have seen. The benchmark pairs predictions with reference labels one to one and penalizes unmatched predictions. The choice of how closely different labels are related still comes from the authors’ taxonomy. A different definition of which mistakes matter most could change what a good score looks like, which is one reason exact-label scores remain useful alongside the new metric.

The benchmark fits into Interhuman’s broader work on models that process conversations as audio and video. The company’s technical write-up describes outputs that include a predicted signal, a confidence level, and a short account of the evidence. A keyword search would miss an example like a speaker agreeing verbally while slowing down or pausing; the benchmark asks whether models can recognize behavioral cues rather than rely solely on the literal words.

But the human labels reflect what observers perceived. They cannot establish whether a speaker really felt uncertain or why they paused. Culture, speaking style, the setting, and the short length of the clips all affect interpretation. The public dataset is primarily English online video with one clearly visible speaker; the held-out sets broaden that selection but do not establish that results transfer to every language, community, or interaction. The dataset terms also rule out unconsented surveillance and high-stakes uses such as hiring or workplace monitoring.

The immediate value is a better way to test how multimodal models respond to ambiguous evidence, particularly when a system might otherwise treat a transcript as the entire conversation. It also clarifies what a high score cannot tell us: a model may be good at matching trained observers on selected clips without reliably knowing what a person intended. That gap is precisely what makes a carefully bounded benchmark useful.

Epochal Launches Time Portal to Test Forecasting AI on Web History

📅 Nov 24, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Epochal, a new AI company, introduced itself on November 24, 2026 with Time Portal, a point-in-time web environment for AI agents. The company says the archive spans more than 1 trillion pages and is designed to teach systems to forecast without exposing future outcomes.

The announced team includes Guodong Zhang, Zihang Dai and Manuel Kroiss, key members of xAI’s founding team, alongside Alex Bocharoff and Frank Luan. Time Portal, Epochal’s first product, was built in 90 days.

The company’s premise is that today’s models absorb the internet with many outcomes already visible, whereas people learn by making decisions before knowing the results. Epochal wants agents to rehearse that process across thousands of historical days, using the web as it existed at each selected moment rather than as a single training corpus filled with hindsight.

Epochal reports that Time Portal tracks more than 7 trillion URLs across over 30 years of internet history, with minute-level version granularity on the most dynamic pages. Its three components have distinct jobs: search finds historically available pages, fetch retrieves a specific URL, and sandbox supplies a Linux environment gated to the same instant.

The sandbox addresses ways an agent could notice the simulation without reading a future page. Its system clock is set to the chosen instant, while the Ubuntu version, system libraries, language runtimes and command-line tools match that period. Package mirrors and network requests also respect the cutoff, placing commands and web browsing inside the same historical environment.

To assess how closely the simulation matches live deployment, Epochal ran forecasting agents on 347 live questions from prediction markets Kalshi and Polymarket. A few weeks later, it replayed those questions through Time Portal with each cutoff set to the original forecast time. Separate replays gave agents either the live web or no web access.

In Epochal’s own evaluation, the correlation between Time Portal replay errors and original live forecast errors was 0.98, compared with 0.16 for live-web replays after resolution and 0.49 without web access. The Time Portal replays showed no significant deviations in predictions from the historical runs. Live-web agents could look up resolved outcomes, reducing errors to nearly zero, while no-web errors were larger and only loosely tracked the originals. The experiment therefore measures historical fidelity: how closely a replay matches the original run.

An engineering post describes a strict retrieval rule: fetch returns the newest capture at or before the requested instant and returns a miss if none exists. A later snapshot is never substituted because even the closest future copy could reveal information that was unavailable at the time. The engine also marks disappeared pages as absent, collapses duplicate content within and across URLs, and adjusts ranking as the meanings of terms and facts change.

Developers can start with a limited version for free through the API, console playground or SDKs. The API documentation says the playground does not require an API key, while API calls use an Epochal key and an Accept-Datetime header containing a past timestamp. Python and Rust clients, a command-line interface and an MCP server are also provided. When a sandbox stops, its filesystem and command output are discarded.

For Epochal, Time Portal is infrastructure for a broader effort to train AI through decisions and observed outcomes rather than static historical text. Its archive and strict cutoffs address how forecasting experiments can be staged without hindsight. Whether work in these reconstructed pasts produces better predictions on unresolved events is the central question for the company’s approach.
