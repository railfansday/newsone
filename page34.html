The Aircraft Replacing The Chinook

📅 Nov 08, 2026  |  Category: World  |  Read Source →

As it stands, there is technically no official replacement for the Boeing CH-47 yet under US Army development. Significant progress on the Sikorsky UH-60 Black Hawk’s successor has taken place in recent years, however. That is the Bell V-280 Valor, a tiltrotor under development that has key differences from its older cousin, Bell-Boeing’s V-22 Osprey.

The lack of a direct successor for the heavy-duty whirlybird has led to some speculation that the Army may consolidate its fleet, with the backbone model being the Valor (officially designated MV-25). Concerns about the CH-47F’s survivability in contested airspace and vast domains like the Pacific lend credence to the possibility of a total phase-out.

The Chinook’s unrivaled heavy-lift capability and outstanding track record make it a favorite with the special forces communities of the US Department of Defense. The Army’s Chinook fleet numbers more than 470 aircraft and is expected to serve as the primary heavy-lift rotorcraft for many more years to come.

Since debuting in 1961, the Chinook has never been toppled as the apex of tandem-rotor helicopters. The soviets experimented with tandem-rotor designs, but favored different configurations for heavy lift. Even the US Navy and Marines’ CH-46 has been retired for a decade. The Chinook is still being modernized, as the Block II variant offers a gross weight increase of 54,000 pounds, upgraded fuel systems, and rotor blades.

Honeywell has developed the new T55-714C, with 20% more power and 8% better fuel efficiency than previous powerplants. Vertical Mag quoted General John Murray in 2021, saying that no replacement had been planned yet, and the Chinook remains a critical asset for Army Special Forces, among other missions:

“The Future Attack Reconnaissance Aircraft and the Future Long Range Assault Aircraft are the two plans, programs we have working right now. There is nothing working for a replacement for the CH-47.”

The first CH-47F Block II Chinooks reached the Army in 2024, and the first of a scheduled 465 helicopters was delivered in July of that year, according to World of Aviation. Some compare the CH-47 to the equally enduring B-52, for being so good at what it does that there simply is no replacement. Perhaps it will soldier on into the distant future, but let's explore the latest challenger and if it has what it takes to fill the Chinooks’ place on the flightline.

The Army’s Future Vertical Lift program sought to develop the Future Long Range Assault Attack, or FLRAA. The winning platform was Bell Textron’s V-280 Valor tiltrotor. The new aircraft will initially supplement the UH-60 Black Hawk fleet but will likely replace the aging Sikorsky completely over time.

Although the V-280 is cut from the same cloth as the Bell-Boeing Osprey, engineers have implemented key differences from the start. Aside from a smaller overall footprint, the V-280 uses a V-tail configuration rather than the V-22’s twin vertical fin tail. While the V-22’s nacelles, which house both the engine and gearbox, fully rotate, only the V-280’s props and gearbox move while the engines remain fixed during transition between flight modes.

The V-280 also features a different clutch to the V-22’s problematic design, as it operates more like a conventional helicopter. The Osprey’s transmission has been linked to fatal hard-clutch engagements on more than one occasion and requires somewhere in the ballpark of 45 days to replace, according to Military.com.

The Bell V-280 Valor is a next-generation tiltrotor aircraft developed specifically by Bell-Textron for the US Army's FLRAA program. The V-280's defining feature is its ability to transition between helicopter-like vertical flight and airplane-like forward flight by tilting its rotors and drive shafts. It offers significant improvements in speed, range, and maneuverability over the UH-60.

This allows vertical takeoff and landing like a helicopter while providing speed and range comparable to a fixed-wing aircraft. Flying as fast as 300 knots, the Valor is almost twice as fast as the Chinook, which tops out at 170 knots. The Valor also surpasses the Chinook in range by a long shot.

Under combat load, the CH-47 can only go about 400 NM, while the V-280 can fly 800 NM on a single fill-up without any aerial refueling. The Valor can perform fast-rope sling-loading like the UH-60 or CH-47, but armament has yet to be publicly revealed. A key area where it falls short of the CH-47 is, inevitably, raw payload.

The V-280, or MV-25 as it recently became designated, has under half the sling-load capacity at 10,000 lbs compared to the Chinook’s 26,000 lbs. The Valor can’t compete with the Chinook in weight or volume capacity, which is the crux of the issue when it comes to replacing the venerable CH-47.

Like its Bell-Boeing V-22 Osprey predecessor, Valor’s tiltrotor design is a radical departure from conventional rotorcraft. The MV-25 is equipped with two Rolls-Royce AE 1107F engines, which are of the same powerplant family as used in the C-130 Hercules, Global Hawk drone, and V-22 Osprey.

The V-280 gearbox design has been refined from the V-22's design into a simpler, lighter, and more easily maintainable design. This new system eliminates the need for a mid-wing gearbox, which cuts the total number of gearboxes from five to four, versus the Osprey.

The design allows for a straight wing design that reduces complexity and eliminates a number of failure points. The V-280's stationary engines result in weight savings, thanks to fewer parts, and the lower empty weight also allows for better performance and fuel efficiency.

Its simplified mechanical system also improves reliability and maintenance, reducing the notoriously long downtime seen with the Osprey as well as operational costs overall. The Valor is superior to the Chinook in the area of efficiency, and likely around the same level of complexity. Still, the question of operation costs and maintenance intensity remains unclear.

The MV-25 has the potential to revolutionize US military operations, particularly in the Pacific. There, its speed, range, and survivability are greatly superior to the CH-47 in long-range air assault missions. Its ability to take off and land vertically like a helicopter and transition to airplane-like flight profiles allows for rapid deployment and maneuverability that the Chinook can’t match in the vast, island-dotted Pacific.

This would be particularly important in any potential conflict with China. Here, long distances and limited infrastructure are major challenges for any conventional rotorcraft. The Valor greatly exceeds the capabilities of the Black Hawk in this scenario. It will enable the Army to project power across greater distances in the Pacific, like the South China Sea, more quickly and efficiently.

The MV-25 can operate from austere locations like its predecessors, and its long range can overcome logistical challenges in the vast and dispersed island chains of the Pacific. Improved survivability features make it more difficult for adversaries to target. The primary strength is speed, but it also features radar cross-section reduction and infrared suppression to minimize its detectability.

In potential conflicts with near-peer adversaries with advanced air defenses, the Valor will significantly improve the survivability of air assault operations. That also allows for more dispersed and decentralized operations over extended distances. That adaptability and flexibility make it a well-suited platform for the Pacific theater.

The US Army is undergoing a significant transformation in its aviation branch as a whole. The future will prioritize unmanned systems and more long-range crewed platforms. The Valor is one of the top priorities, as it will replace the Black Hawk, which is the current backbone of Army Aviation. The Army is also investing in long-range precision munitions for its aircraft to match its tactics to faster platforms.

The Army is rapidly working toward affordable, reliable, and lethal uncrewed aerial systems in the field. Deploying drone swarms or executing pinpoint strikes from units integrated with drones at the brigade level will evolve the Army’s strategy to the next level. The 'Replicator' initiative, headed by the Defense Innovation Unit, aims to field thousands of low-cost, autonomous systems.

The new devices would be attritable drones that counter adversary threats as well as provide man-portable intelligence, surveillance, and reconnaissance with strike capability. In a future of drones at the tip of the spear and tiltrotor workhorses as the backbone of the vertical lift fleet, it's hard to say if the CH-47 will still have a place.

Its raw payload-carrying power has no equal in current or planned platforms, which may see it live on in a way the B-52 has endured. On the other hand, it may simply be unneeded, as tiltrotor technology improves and other aircraft like the Defense Advanced Research Projects Agency Liberty Lifter are introduced.

10 Greatest Opening Lines In Fantasy TV History, Ranked

📅 Nov 08, 2026  |  Category: World  |  Read Source →

Fantasy television has a unique challenge when introducing audiences to a new series. Unlike shows grounded in reality, fantasy stories must establish unfamiliar worlds, explain their rules, and convince viewers to invest in characters whose experiences might be entirely impossible. While some shows spend episodes establishing their premises, others accomplish something remarkable with just a few words.

The best opening lines in fantasy television do more than introduce a story. They establish atmosphere, foreshadow conflict, and reveal themes that eventually define the series. Whether delivered through an ominous conversation or unforgettable narration, these introductions can become as recognizable as the shows themselves.

Some beloved fantasy series have especially memorable introductions because their opening words gain significance as their stories progress. What initially seems like a simple statement can eventually reflect everything the characters experience, making those moments more meaningful during subsequent rewatches.

From supernatural procedurals to adventures involving magic and mythology, fantasy television has produced outstanding opening moments. However, the best examples capture the spirit of their shows while giving audiences something worth remembering long after the final episode.

NBC's Grimm establishes its relationship with traditional fairy tales through a deceptively simple exchange between detectives Hank Griffin and Nick Burkhardt: "Oh, you're a happily ever after guy." Hank's observation about Nick's romantic outlook initially feels like harmless banter, but it's much more meaningful once the show's supernatural premise emerges.

Grimm takes stories traditionally associated with happy endings and transforms them into something darker. Nick discovers that fairy tale monsters are real, and his responsibility as a Grimm frequently jeopardizes his relationships and personal happiness.

The opening line introduces the contrast defining the series. Nick might believe in happy endings, but his new reality makes it much harder to experience one.

Although not technically the first spoken line of The Magicians, "It's happening," from the premiere's opening conversation between Eliza and Dean Henry Fogg makes an immediate impression. Viewers have virtually no context for understanding what Eliza means, but the urgency surrounding their exchange suggests something dangerous is approaching.

The conversation establishes that powerful individuals already understand the supernatural threats awaiting the central characters, even before those characters discover their magical abilities. It also foreshadows the Beast's terrifying arrival.

Unlike fantasy stories that initially present magic as an exciting escape from ordinary life, The Magicians immediately suggests that the supernatural world carries considerable danger. Its mysterious warning establishes those stakes before audiences understand the larger conflict.

After an intense opening battle against a monstrous kikimora, Geralt of Rivia delivers a surprisingly understated observation in The Witcher's premiere: "Today isn't your day, is it?" His first words establish the dry humor and emotional restraint that make Henry Cavill's portrayal of the monster hunter so entertaining.

Rather than celebrating his victory or expressing relief at surviving, Geralt treats the encounter like another inconvenience in his difficult profession. The contrast between the fight's brutality and his casual response makes the moment memorable.

This introduction establishes that Geralt isn't a conventional fantasy hero. He approaches extraordinary circumstances with remarkable practicality, and his humor frequently emerges from his apparent indifference.

Netflix's The Sandman introduces its supernatural mythology through narration from Dream, also known as Morpheus. His opening words, "We begin in the waking world..." challenge the assumption that the physical world represents the entirety of human existence, introducing audiences to a realm most people experience only while sleeping.

The narration establishes the essential distinction between the waking world and the Dreaming while helping viewers understand Morpheus' enormous responsibility.

What makes this introduction effective is its philosophical approach to fantasy storytelling. Rather than simply presenting an unfamiliar supernatural environment, The Sandman encourages audiences to reconsider something they experience every night, transforming the ordinary act of dreaming into something extraordinary.

Outlander begins with Claire Randall making an observation that initially resembles the introduction to a missing-person mystery: "People disappear all the time." However, her words take on a double meaning once audiences discover that her disappearance involves traveling through time.

Claire's narration presents disappearing as an ordinary occurrence before revealing the extraordinary circumstances of her experience. Her unexpected journey from the 20th century to 18th-century Scotland fundamentally changes the life she expected to lead.

The statement becomes increasingly meaningful as Claire navigates relationships separated by centuries. Throughout Outlander, disappearing isn't always about leaving a particular location. Sometimes, it means losing access to an entire life that can never be recovered.

HBO's Game of Thrones opens with members of the Night's Watch investigating an unsettling discovery beyond the Wall. When Will says, "We should start back. They're all dead," suggesting they abandon their mission after discovering dead wildlings, the audience is introduced to the dangers lurking beyond the Seven Kingdoms.

The fantasy umbrella covers a wide range of TV shows, but only some have stood out as the very best, further influencing the genre as a whole.

The statement establishes the supernatural threat that eventually overshadows the political conflicts dominating much of the series. Viewers quickly discover that, beyond the Wall, death doesn't necessarily mean the end.

While Game of Thrones became famous for shocking betrayals and struggles for the Iron Throne, its opening words emphasize another danger entirely. The White Walkers represent an existential threat that makes humanity's political rivalries seem considerably less important.

The opening narration of Buffy the Vampire Slayer establishes one of television's most recognizable supernatural mythologies. Before audiences meet Buffy Summers, they learn that a single young woman is selected to protect humanity from vampires, demons, and other dangerous creatures. "In every generation, there is a Chosen One."

The introduction establishes the enormous responsibility placed on Buffy's shoulders. However, its significance grows as the series explores the consequences of being chosen for a destiny she never asked for.

Throughout Buffy the Vampire Slayer, the expectations surrounding the Slayer frequently conflict with Buffy's desire for an ordinary life. The show's eventual decision to challenge the idea that only one girl should carry this responsibility makes its original introduction even more meaningful.

Prime Video's The Lord of the Rings: The Rings of Power opens with Galadriel delivering a line that captures an important theme in J.R.R. Tolkien's mythology: "Nothing is evil in the beginning." Rather than introducing an established conflict, her narration encourages audiences to consider how darkness emerges within a world that wasn't always corrupted.

The opening is especially appropriate for a prequel exploring events thousands of years before The Lord of the Rings. Audiences already understand the devastating consequences of Sauron's influence, making the origins of evil particularly significant.

Galadriel's words also emphasize how admirable intentions can become corrupted. It's a fitting introduction to a story where the line between good intentions and dangerous decisions becomes increasingly blurry.

Few fantasy series have an opening narration as recognizable as BBC's Merlin. Delivered by the Great Dragon, Kilgharrah, the introduction establishes the legendary world of Camelot while emphasizing that the kingdom's destiny rests on someone's unexpected shoulders. "In a land of myth and a time of magic..."

The narration transforms the familiar legend of Merlin into a story about a young man discovering his abilities. Rather than presenting the wise wizard traditionally associated with Arthurian mythology, the series follows someone whose extraordinary destiny remains largely unrealized.

As Merlin's relationship with Arthur Pendragon develops, the opening narration becomes more meaningful. It reminds audiences that ordinary decisions can have enormous consequences, particularly when someone is responsible for protecting a future that hasn't happened yet.

Avatar: The Last Airbender delivers arguably the most effective opening narration in fantasy television history. Through Katara's introduction, audiences learn about the four nations, the Avatar's responsibility to maintain balance, and the devastating war transforming their world. "Water. Earth. Fire. Air. My grandmother used to tell me stories about the old days..."

This opening is impressive because it efficiently establishes a complicated mythology without overwhelming viewers. Within moments, audiences understand the central conflict, the missing Avatar's importance, and why his return represents humanity's greatest hope for peace.

However, the narration's greatest strength comes from Katara herself. Rather than presenting an impersonal historical account, Avatar: The Last Airbender establishes its mythology through someone personally affected by the war.

Her belief that the Avatar can save the world becomes the fantasy series' emotional foundation. Even after witnessing Aang's complete journey, those opening words retain their significance, reminding viewers that one person's hope made everything that followed possible.

How long can you expect your PC RAM to last?

📅 Nov 08, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Individual RAM needs may be the biggest factor to lifespan

We probably don't need to remind you that RAM prices are out of control, spiraling into the sky like a broken rocket because of the unending growth of AI data centers. Companies like Apple have had to raise the cost of existing devices to keep up profit margins, and newer products are often launching with eye-watering price tags. Valve's Steam Machine costs over $1,000 — which is in line with a comparable gaming PC, but it was originally intended to be much cheaper, probably closer to $750. There's no telling how expensive Sony and Microsoft's next consoles will be.

This makes clinging onto the RAM you have particularly important. Replacement memory could potentially be unaffordable, never mind buying or upgrading to an entirely new computer. In fact, companies like Intel are even backtracking to support cheaper DDR4 RAM, despite DDR6 already being on the horizon. The good news here is that in terms of durability, your RAM will probably survive until you buy a new PC, regardless of how many years away that might be. Whether you'll tolerate the performance barriers of older modules is another matter. The demands of PC apps are always evolving, after all.

While it's tough to track down hard figures, PCWorld claims that on average, the material longevity of RAM should fall anywhere between 3 and 15 years. That's obviously an extremely broad range. It's very plausible, however, when you consider the range of factors involved. For one, there's initial manufacturing quality. While it's hard to go wrong with any of the major brands out there — such as G.Skill, Samsung and Corsair — there's always the possibility of a defect, since quality control is never perfect. If you turn to an obscure brand, you're taking an even bigger gamble.

More significantly, different users put RAM under different workloads. In a PC mostly tapped for browsing, email, streaming and light gaming, there isn't much strain. But if you're handling hours of tasks like video editing, AI processing or photorealistic 3D gaming, you're naturally stretching your RAM to the limits. All of the electricity for reads and writes generates waste heat, slowly wearing down your RAM's circuitry.

Some people take their RAM to the limits and beyond by overclocking it. This boosts performance, but can push heat past intended design tolerances, at least without additional cooling. Ambient conditions play a part as well, say if your CPU and GPU are generating a lot of heat, or voltage stability is inconsistent. Lastly, there's the potential for quasi-random failures such as static shock. Your PC should always be well-grounded.

Realistically, older DDR4 RAM is plenty fast for most people. DDR5 can deliver meaningful improvements in tasks like gaming, yet even then, CPU and GPU speeds are going to have a deeper impact. Also, quantity can matter more than quality. If you're doing a lot of multitasking, you're easily better off with 32GB of DDR4 than 16GB of DDR5. Google Chrome alone can consume over 8GB of RAM if you have a dozen or so tabs open.

It's really the link between RAM and CPU performance that's the killer. Normally, motherboards designed for the latest CPU sockets are also built with DDR5 RAM in mind, and they're not backwards compatible with DDR4, let alone DDR3. Opting for a DDR4-compatible motherboard may place serious limits on CPU speed unless you specifically hunt down one of the recent options (like Intel's) designed to circumvent the memory crisis.

The gist is that if you want to stay on the cutting edge of computing, you may be forced to upgrade the kind of RAM you're using long before what you've got expires. Even five years can be an eternity in PC evolution, never mind 10 or more. It's only the people putting their PCs through the wringer — whether in terms of prolonged workload or excessive overclocking — who should seriously be worried about sudden death.

The 5 Scariest Books of All Time, According to Stephen King

📅 Nov 08, 2026  |  Category: Entertainment  |  Read Source →

It’s interesting to find out what scares one person, and compare their fears to those of another person, perhaps naturally, but then when you're looking at someone who is usually the person who causes fear, then finding out what scares them (if anything) is even more intriguing. Enter Stephen King (no, wait, not like that!), who probably doesn’t need much of an introduction, not just at this stage, but really any stage in the last several decades. His earliest published novels came out in the 1970s, and so many of his initial classics belonged wholly or in part to the horror genre.

You'd be missing out on great books like 11/22/63, much of The Dark Tower, and most of Different Seasons if you only read King’s works of horror, but sure, horror is the genre he’s most associated with for honestly fair reasons. He’s also read and absorbed a lot of horror made by other people, as the non-fiction book Danse Macabre (1981) demonstrates, but more recently, he gave his top 5 scariest books to USA Today in an interview. It’s subjective, and it’s also not the same as Stephen King saying they're the five “best” scary books ever written, but it’s worth looking at what makes the arguable master of horror himself feel fear (no one is safe from being affected by a scary story, it seems).

In the past, Stephen King has also been asked about which stories of his he views most favorably, and he made some unexpected choices there, and also, he highlighted something recent at the time he was asked: 2021’s Billy Summers. Maybe he likes doing that, and it is interesting to not just go straight back to the classics and/or the heavy-hitters, so he could've been trying to do something like that again when one of his five picks was the (at the time of writing) very recent Trad Wife. It’s a 2026 novel about very contemporary things, as you might guess from the title, because the idea of a “tradwife” is a more online and modern-day thing, but it’s all about wanting women to be traditional wives, as things might've been in older days that some people choose to romanticize.

Trad Wife has divided people to some extent, if you go over the other reviews out there, but Stephen King is clearly a champion of the book.

That’s probably the gentlest and least provocative way to define it without rattling anyone. So, the book’s about a journalist who wants to document someone who appears to be a “tradwife,” and she does that while on the property she lives on with her family, but then things slowly get strange and more horrific, though potentially not in the ways you're expecting. Trad Wife has divided people to some extent, if you go over the other reviews out there (admittedly there aren’t a ton, seeing as it’s still a fairly new release), but Stephen King is clearly a champion of the book, being confident enough to label it the best horror novel of 2026.

More of an established heavy-hitter now, Stephen King is a big fan of The Silence of the Lambs, which was a sequel to Red Dragon. Now, Red Dragon got a movie adaptation in the form of Manhunter, but the film version of The Silence of the Lambs wasn’t really a sequel to that film, and then there was a prequel to The Silence of the Lambs (1991) that was called Red Dragon about a decade later, but that one wasn’t great. Oh, and you get some Red Dragon in the TV show, Hannibal, though chronologically, that one didn’t have enough seasons to get to The Silence of the Lambs material.

Anyway, point is, you can not read all that much, and you still probably know the deal with this series, to some extent, and you likely have some familiarity with Hannibal Lecter and what a great character he is. The Silence of the Lambs was obviously a great movie, but the source material is also remarkable, and it’s one of those great cases where a high-quality book seamlessly became a high-quality movie (something that cannot be said about every Stephen King novel that’s made its way to the big screen, but at least there'll always be Stand by Me, Misery, and The Shawshank Redemption, among others).

It’s fair that some of Stephen King’s supernatural horror books get a lot of attention, but just as skilled is what he often manages to do when he tackles psychological horror (and sure, when he has the two collide, like in The Shining, that can also be phenomenal). He singles out The Other as a psychological horror book that frightened him to a particularly great extent, and with it being published a few years before King’s first published novel, you can probably assume it had some kind of influence on his own work, or at least some of his subsequent books.

At the center of The Other is a pair of twins, with one of them being very troubled and the other being seemingly anything but, and the overall novel does increasingly unsettling things with that premise. It’s a slow-burn without ever running the risk of feeling too slow, and there is also something worth celebrating about a book that can prove frightening while sounding, on paper, a bit ordinary or even too straightforward. The Other just does a lot of stuff right, and going into the things it does right would run the risk of ruining things. Just trust King and the other people who are fans of this one (it was very popular back when released, but is perhaps a little more obscure nowadays than the other older books King’s highlighted as being the most frightening to him).

Oh, and now this one isn't obscure by any metric. The Exorcist was a big deal as a book, for a little while, and then it was a potentially even bigger deal as a movie, with that movie getting made not long after the book came out. A similar thing happened with The Godfather novel being the book equivalent of a blockbuster, and then the movie was made rather fast, and ultimately became even more culturally significant. With The Exorcist, though, the source material here is very much worth reading even if you’ve seen the movie, since there are ideas it explores – and sights it describes – that prove more unsettling when they're placed in your head, versus seeing/experiencing those things on a screen.

And that’s saying a lot, because the movie is still absolutely surprising and effectively horrifying with what it shows and explores. Guess it’s the case that you’ve got a winning work of horror either way, with The Exorcist (1971) being potentially the definitive demonic possession novel, and The Exorcist (1973) almost definitely being the definitive demonic possession movie. Also, there is a blending of more supernatural (or otherworldly) horror with psychologically troubling stuff in The Exorcist and, once more, the interplay just serves to make both those strands of horror hit even harder.

The Exorcist is singled out in polls on Goodreads as being one of the scariest novels of all time, among those voting, and Lord of the Flies is one you'll often see in such polls, too. And hey, it’s relevant here, because it was one of King’s five picks when he was asked about books that scared him. It’s interesting, because Lord of the Flies isn't outwardly a horror book, probably more definable as a survival story, or maybe like a small-scale dystopian novel without real sci-fi elements, but it’s horrifying without necessarily being full-on horror. And of course it is, because it’s about young boys getting stranded on an island and finding themselves clashing over various things, all in increasingly violent ways.

People tend to read Lord of the Flies when they're younger as well, which has an effect on it being more frightening, like if it marks the first time you're forced to confront some really troubling ideas. Stephen King himself has written some pretty great books that are comparable to Lord of the Flies, like The Mist (technically a novella, with it involving people trapped in a supermarket), and Under the Dome (in which a dome does indeed come down over a very small town, trapping people under it). Also, King’s love for Lord of the Flies is apparent in his fiction beyond looking at the stories of his that seem a little similar premise-wise, since Lord of the Flies itself is an important part of the novella “Low Men in Yellow Coats,” mostly in the way it has a strong impact on that story’s young protagonist.

Spalletti: ‘Juventus wallow in doubt and lose confidence’

📅 Nov 08, 2026  |  Category: Sports  |  Read Source →

Luciano Spalletti reiterated Juventus ‘can’t seem to be consistent’ and ‘lose confidence, so we wallow in doubt’ after a 2-2 draw with Fiorentina, but remained enigmatic on penalty appeals.

The Bianconeri had to fight back twice for a point in Florence this evening, as they went behind within four minutes to a Mateo Pellegrino header, then once Nick Woltemade had equalised with his first Serie A goal, Alieu Njie had restored the Viola advantage.

Gleison Bremer rose to meet a Douglas Luiz corner kick for the final 2-2 result, although both teams had late complaints against the referee.

“We tend to drown in a glass of water sometimes, and lose confidence in the way we play,” Spalletti told DAZN Italia.

“We conceded so early, but then reacted well, we just can’t seem to be consistent throughout a match, as a team or as individuals. We do really well for 10 minutes, then are lacking in something else the next, we aren’t consistent.”

The Njie goal raised eyebrows, because while Guglielmo Vicario had made the initial save, his teammates weren’t reactive enough to clear, allowing the Fiorentina player to bundle in the loose ball.

“In some moments we decided not to make decisions, we just stay there and accept the situation, without taking action or the initiative. Doing something takes away the doubt, but when you wallow in the doubt, you never manage to be in control of a situation,” confessed Spalletti.

It was a new trident attack this evening with Jeremie Boga and Randal Kolo Muani taking the wings, leaving Woltemade as the centre-forward.

“We made this choice because Fiorentina are aggressive, and seeing as we don’t build out too well from the back, at times we need to be more direct going for the centre-forward. It is not his greatest strength, holding up the ball, as he likes to combine with his teammates, but he’s the best at it in the squad.

“Kolo Muani is doing better, and with these fitness levels and rediscovered verve, you can put him anywhere and he’ll deliver a performance. He moves around a lot more and seems more engaged with the rest of the team.”

Radu Dragusin’s goal was disallowed for a push on Bremer, while Juve had penalty appeals rejected for a potential Joao Mario handling offence.

“I don’t know, do you have an alternative question?” replied Spalletti with a smile when shown the replay of the incident.

“I didn’t need a replay, I already saw it on the pitch.”

The result means that Juventus go into the break for international duty in seventh place, falling six adrift of Milan and the Champions League qualifying spots.

A tally of 18 points after 11 rounds is their worst start to a Serie A season since 15 points in 2021/22 under Max Allegri.

Patrick Mahomes to get more tests on right elbow after Sunday's injury

📅 Nov 08, 2026  |  Category: Sports  |  Read Source →

Chiefs quarterback Patrick Mahomes didn’t miss any offensive snaps until garbage time in Sunday’s 35-12 win over the Jets, but it looked like he might have suffered a serious injury late in the first half.

Mahomes lost the football when he took a shot to the right elbow from Jets first-round pick David Bailey and he went to the sideline holding his arm in a way that led to fears of a serious injury. Mahomes grimaced while throwing on the sideline, but returned to the game after the Jets missed a field goal and then led the team to 21 points in the third quarter to put the win away.

After the game, Mahomes said at his press conference that it was a scare for him and he didn’t feel great but “if I’m able to play, I’m gonna play.” He said that he wasn’t “throwing the ball the way I wanted to” after halftime, but felt better as the game went on. Mahomes finished the game 23-of-31 for 242 yards and two touchdowns.
