Phil Neville Names 4 Players Who Could Get Man United Relegated

📅 Nov 09, 2026  |  Category: Sports  |  Read Source →

Former Manchester United defender Phil Neville has named the four players currently putting both Michael Carrick’s future as manager of the Old Trafford side and the Red Devils’ Premier League safety at risk.

His comments came in the aftermath of United’s dramatic 4-4 draw with Aston Villa on Sunday evening. Although many - including Rio Ferdinand - praised Carrick and co. for twice coming back from two goals down to snatch a point, the result did little to steady the ship at M16, either on or off the pitch, despite a lap of honour at full-time.

Defensive fragility was at the heart of United’s problems this weekend as they slipped into the relegation zone. However, having won just one of their last 10 matches across all competitions, and with supporters staging an anti-Glazers protest at Old Trafford, it is clear that the issues run much deeper.

The Neville brothers dissected the problems at the club during a segment with USA Sports. While Gary focused more on the behind-the-scenes issues, Phil pulled no punches when discussing four players in particular.

Relegation for a club of Man United's size feels impossible. That was until now, with the Red Devils sitting 18th in the Premier League, just a point above cellar dwellers Crystal Palace.

At one stage over the weekend, United were sitting rock bottom, and Neville has named the four players he thinks would be at fault for the club's demise if the worst-case scenario did unfold. He said (watch the full segment below):

"No, this is going to continue [United's relegation struggle]. This team, I think Gary said it, [Noussair] Mazrouai, [Leny] Yoro, [Ayden] Heaven, [Patrick] Dorgu, finished the game.

"That back four gets you relegated. That back four gets you the sack as a manager. And that back four cannot keep Man United in the Premier League."

"It's rinse and repeat, isn't it?" Gary then replied. "Amorim, Ten Hag, Ole Gunnar Solskjaer, Carrick, Jose Mourinho, Louis van Gaal, David Moyes, Ralf Rangnick - some of them are world-class managers who have had success, there are a lot of promising young managers there, ex-players who know the club as well.

The Red Devils' boss is feeling the pressure as United head into the international break in the relegation zone.

"None of it has worked, because if you recruit badly, if you’ve got poor performance department, poor recruitment department, you’ve not got leadership that is strong right through the spine of the football club, you will fail."

He added: "Every organisation in the world will fail if you’ve not got an aligned leadership from top to bottom - your board and your ownership right the way through to the person who is stood on the pitch trying to play football. There are real issues within that football club, you’ve got people doing those jobs who have never done those jobs before in senior positions."

United have made their worst start to a league season for 40 years and have not won in the Premier League for more than 70 days following the draw against Villa this weekend.

While Carrick may have started his tenure well in January this year, having picked up more points than any other manager between then and the end of the 2025/26 season, he now looks destined to become the seventh permanent manager to come and go since Sir Alex Ferguson retired in 2013.

The fact he is not alone in coaches starting well at United before going down a well-trodden path of demise proves there are plenty more issues than just the one in charge of the team itself. Recruitment has long been underwhelming - something Lisandro Martinez publicly criticised before being silenced recently - and there is no rhyme or reason to the hierarchy's direction going forward.

United's decision-makers can choose to part ways with another failing ex-player, but who they turn to, and whether Carrick's replacement can handle the pressure any better, is very debatable.

4 Action Movie Masterpieces That Define The Entire Genre

📅 Nov 09, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

The action genre is an exciting and evolving subsection of cinema that has continued to develop in exciting ways since the dawn of the medium. There’s no clear consensus on when the modern action film began because examples of high-wire setpieces and incredible stuntwork are much older than anything resembling modern blockbusters. The silent films of Buster Keaton and Charlie Chaplin featured remarkable precision and experimentation when it came to actual stuntwork, even if they wouldn’t technically be considered “action stars” by modern pundits. The Western genre, which has been an active part of the industry since the ‘30s, is also where a lot of “action stars” got their start; actors like John Wayne, Steve McQueen, Charlton Heston, Gary Cooper, and Yul Brennar were all accomplished dramatic actors who could do their own fight scenes.

Action cinema began to become more stylized in the 1960s, as the era of World War II adventure films started to sunset in favor of franchises like James Bond, which promised an increased degree of spectacle and theatricality upon each new entry. The ‘70s introduced a darker component to the genre with the rise of vigilante films like Rolling Thunder and the Dirty Harry series, which were more grounded in reality because they drew from contemporary anxieties.

The action genre is often defined by stars, as it seems as if each generation has a few major actors who continuously push the genre forward. Arnold Schwarzenegger and Sylvester Stallone dominated the ‘80s, and their reign continued well into the ‘90s, even if they found new competitors in Tom Cruise and Nicolas Cage. Although today there’s been action stars like Jason Statham and Gerard Butler who have essentially become genres on to themselves, there’s also an appeal in seeing older, more grizzled dramatic actors return to their pulpier roots, including Denzel Washington and Liam Neeson. There are still a few specific action films that have not only stood the test of time, but have a significant degree of influence on pretty much everything that has followed.

Raiders of the Lost Ark was an attempt to do for ‘30s adventure serials what Star Wars had done for science fiction, and producer/creator George Lucas knew that there was only one person who he could trust with bringing the character to life. Steven Spielberg had long since lamented that he was unable to direct a 007 film, as MGM had consistently turned him down because they were not accepting American filmmakers. It was famously when Spielberg and Lucas were on vacation during the opening weekend of Star Wars that they came up with the idea of what the Indiana Jones franchise would look like. Unlike Bond, Indy was a straight-laced professor with a deep knowledge of history and archeology, but he also had a strong desire for justice. Although multiple possibilities were floated for who could possibly take on the role, Harrison Ford eventually became the only real choice, and it’s perhaps the defining part in a career that includes some of the most beloved films of all-time.

Raiders of the Lost Ark is a film that Lucas described as having “just the good parts,” and that is evident within a perfect screenplay by Lawrence Kasdan. There is no fat on the script, as the story moves at a propulsive pace without ever feeling impersonal. Although there are several great installments in the series, Raiders of the Lost Ark remains the best because it has the most exciting MacGuffin, which brings to mind issues of philosophy and faith that add an existentialist level to the film. Many of the action scenes, such as the truck chase and the bi-plane fight, are absolutely brutal in their viscerality, but Spielberg did not forget to also make a film that is equally funny and romantic. The romantic chemistry that Ford has with Karen Allen is nothing short of remarkable, and it’s a major reason why Marion Ravenwood is the love interest that the series has kept coming back to.

Die Hard is a film that has come to represent an entire era of action cinema because of its bullet-proof premise of an ordinary man trapped in a confined location and forced to use his wits and wry wisdom to defend himself and get people to safety. Die Hard was such a massive cultural success that the next generation of action films could all be pitched with their comparisons to Die Hard; Speed was “Die Hard on a bus,” Con Air was “Die Hard on a plane,” Under Siege was “Die Hard on a boat,” and The Rock was “Die Hard in Alcatraz." What distinguished the original Die Hard from all of its imitators was the instantly iconic, brilliant performance by Bruce Willis as John McClane. Although at the time few thought that the guy from Moonlighting would be the next great action star, Willis understood the heart, humanity, and humor needed to play McClane, who somehow felt like a more relatable character than the types of roles that Schwarzenegger and Stallone were playing at the same time.

Die Hard’s genius was understanding that having a great villain was just as important as having a great villain, and cinematic antagonists rarely get better than Alan Rickman as Hans Gruber. Rickman had never acted in a film before, but became perfectly suited to be the cruel, sarcastic, and deviously clever criminal mastermind whose motivations aren’t exactly what they appear to be at first. The dynamic between Willis and Rickman is why Die Hard hits on another level, even if they don’t share that many scenes together. It’s also why none of the Die Hard sequels were able to quite live up to the promise of the first film; the best of the other films in the series is the third installment, Die Hard With A Vengeance, which honored Rickman by introducing Jeremy Irons to play his brother, Simon Gruber.

The Matrix came at a time in which the science fiction genre needed a boost of energy due to it being overrun by maximalist blockbusters and established franchises. The Wachowski sisters made a deeply philosophical warning about the rise of artificial intelligence that also drew from the philosophy of Plato and the type of martial-arts that was traditionally found in East Asian action cinema, as well as strong components of the cyberpunk aesthetic. The result was a groundbreaking achievement in worldbuilding and spectacle that both looked like something that cinephiles had never seen before and got them to question the nature of their reality. Due to the new rules for what its universe looked like, The Matrix was able to reinvent the way that fight scenes were choreographed in the digital age with cutting-edge techniques, such as the now-famous introduction of “bullet time.”

The Matrix is cutting edge as a work of slick, propulsive action filmmaking due to the inclusion of advanced and superhuman fight techniques within a tactical environment, but it is also a classic “hero’s journey” story that benefitted from the charisma of Keanu Reeves. Although Reeves had already proven himself to be a talented action star with Point Break and Speed, The Matrix perfectly cast him to play the avatar for humanity who has to accept his destiny to become “The One.” Although the later installments in The Matrix franchise got a little too wrapped up in their own mythology and strayed away from the emotional sincerity of the original premise, the first film remains a perfect masterpiece that has held up just as well today.

Mad Max: Fury Road was a film that came 30 years in the making, as George Miller had desired to make a fourth installment in the Mad Max franchise ever since he wrapped on the third entry, Mad Max Beyond Thunderdome, in 1985. Although a massive script was written that included an outline for the prequel film that he would end up making, Miller was constantly faced with setbacks and issues regarding funding; after 9/11 delayed shooting and Mel Gibson became too old to play the iconic titular character, it seemed like his dream would never come to fruition.

It was against all odds that Miller ventured into the Australian desert once more on a famously grueling film shoot in which wild rumors suggested that there was a massive disaster in place, and that skepticism didn’t let up until Mad Max: Fury Road debuted at the Cannes Film Festival in 2015 and solidified itself as one of the greatest films of the 21st century, regardless of genre. Mad Max: Fury Road is pure adrenaline and propulsiveness, as it is essentially one long chase. On a deeper level, Mad Max: Fury Road follows a drifter who gets caught up in a revolution, and the launching of Charlize Theron’s Furiosa as one of the defining action heroes of all-time took the great franchise to another level.

Polygon wants to move 70 million POL without the wallet holders’ signatures

📅 Nov 09, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

The proposed fork would make ownership proofs, later deposits and exchange accounting central to returning stolen coins.

Polygon's proposed recovery of roughly 70 million POL would move coins out of five hack-linked wallets without their holders signing a transfer. The plan would change balances through the network's rules, putting the authority to return stolen funds in the hands of governance and validators.

The November 4 proposal targets five addresses it links to stolen funds from security incidents affecting prediction market Polymarket and exchange Binance. Its dated reference tables attribute about 24 million POL to four Polymarket-linked addresses and 47 million POL to one Binance-linked address. The actual recovery would use balances at activation, not these dated reference amounts.

As of November 9, the published proposal still leaves the recovery fork's name, client release and activation block undecided. The November 5 governance agenda lists the proposal for discussion without recording approval. The separate Bor v2.11.0 release implements Uburu's StateReceiver changes, rather than this proposed recovery.

Under the proposed design, the upgraded software would read each listed address's balance immediately before activation, set it to zero and credit the same amount to the designated recovery address. This would happen before transactions execute in the activation block. Total supply would stay the same, and earlier chain history would remain intact. The proposal says Bor engineering must still confirm the design.

A single protocol rule would move native POL from five listed accounts to two company-designated recovery addresses. Approval and recipient verification remain conditions.

The rule would read each listed account's full balance from F−1. The recovery credit has not executed.

Before ordinary transactions in F, the five source balances would be zero and the designated recipients credited by the same amounts.

The proposed recovery changes balances at one block, so custody records need a separate adjustment.

Governance approval, recipient-control checks and Bor engineering confirmation are required. The activation block and recovery release remain undecided as of November 8.

That would place recovery authority in a combination of governance approval, the specified address list and the software rule validators adopt. The affected companies would designate the recipients. Polygon Labs would confirm the proposed proof of recipient control. The proposed sweep would not require a transaction signed by the source addresses.

Those are separate responsibilities. Tracing stolen coins to an address supports the case for including it; proving control of a recovery address checks where the coins would go. The proposal itself warns that misidentifying an attacker-controlled address could move a legitimate balance.

Its recipient-verification requirements call for proof of control, such as a signed message or small test transfer confirmed by Polygon Labs, plus written confirmation that the address belongs to the recipient. The proposal also excludes other tokens and a separate category of blocked addresses, limiting the proposed sweep to native POL at the five listed accounts.

The full-balance rule creates another ownership question: later deposits would move too. The proposal requires balances to be checked again before activation and any changes to be published.

Those checks would disclose which coins the sweep includes, but would not establish that every later deposit belonged to an incident victim.

For exchanges and custodians, the proposed movement would create a difference between the balance shown by an upgraded node and the balance reconstructed solely from ordinary transactions. The proposal explicitly flags this problem for explorers, indexers and accounting tools.

A reconciliation would therefore need to connect each affected source address, its balance before activation, the designated recipient and the corresponding credit at the activation block. For custodians, the rule implies a separate balance adjustment, since there would be no ordinary transfer transaction to match against it.

The planned checks address part of that burden: an Amoy rehearsal would test that supply stays constant and unrelated accounts remain unchanged, followed by a mainnet-fork replay. The proposal also calls for publishing the transaction-free balance change and a verification script after activation. AI Predicts PTSD Risk Based on Stories About What Brought Patients to the Emergency Room

📅 Nov 09, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

NEW YORK, Nov. 9, 2026 /PRNewswire/ -- An AI tool can analyze patients' narratives about injuries that sent them to the emergency room to predict who will later develop post-traumatic stress disorder (PTSD), a new study found.

About 43 million patients are admitted to a US emergency department (ED) each year with physical injuries (trauma), and more than a quarter of them develop PTSD, according to past studies. That makes the ED a checkpoint for catching the condition early, but clinicians face time constraints severe enough to rule out the use of standard screening questionnaires.

"PREVIA could open a window for timely care approaches, when preventive intervention is most effective."

Led by NYU Langone Health researchers, the new study found that the team's set of AI models â each analyzing the trauma narratives of 145 ED patients â correctly identified more than 95.4 percent of at-risk patients. In a head-to-head test, the AI tool, called PREVIA, flagged 100 percent of 30 cases from the same patient group that went on to develop PTSD, while an ED clinician specializing in PTSD correctly identified 89 percent of these cases.

"This is the first study to show that a patient's spontaneous answers to a single, routine care question â 'What brought you to the ED?' â carries enough of a psychological signal to forecast their PTSD risk a month later," said senior study author Katharina Schultebraucks, PhD, associate professor in the departments of psychiatry and population health at NYU Langone. "PREVIA can serve as an AI screener that identifies at-risk patients and explains why, without adding any time to intake in busy emergency rooms."

Published online November 9 in Nature Communications, the new work examined the capabilities of generative AI, which predicts options for the next word in a sentence based on how billions of people used that word in context. This sensitivity to language lets the models analyze a person's description of what happened to them to find patterns linked to their risk of developing PTSD, the researchers said. The patients' answers were recorded and transcribed for analysis.

"The emergency department is often the only point of contact a trauma survivor has with the healthcare system before symptoms become much harder to treat," added Dr. Schultebraucks, who is also co-director of computational psychiatry at NYU Grossman School of Medicine. "By identifying at-risk patients at that first encounter, PREVIA could open a window for timely care approaches, when preventive intervention is most effective."

The first month after trauma is a key window for finding those at risk for PTSD because many patients are no longer in contact with the hospital after that, the researchers said. Current questionnaires â such as the Peritraumatic Dissociative Experiences Questionnaire and the Posttraumatic Adjustment Scale â effectively forecast PTSD development, yet only about 28 percent of EDs routinely screen for PTSD at all, and only two-thirds of those use validated tools.

This likely reflects overcrowding and limited clinician time, typically about 15â20 minutes per patient, which forces clinicians to prioritize immediate medical concerns over lengthy mental health assessments, the study authors said.

PREVIA was designed as a pipeline of steps that use different AI tools based on their strengths (e.g., the Llama model tends to emphasize signs of PTSD risk, while Mistral highlights protective and contextual factors that could buffer against it). Specifically, the tool's first layer scans the language of a patient's narrative for symptoms that happen during the initial trauma, like loss of control or amnesia, as well as early symptoms that show up while a person is in the ED, such as feeling disconnected from the body. Both types of symptoms have been linked to later PTSD development.

The second layer of the team's AI tool also weighs risk factors such as pre-existing mental health problems or lower socioeconomic status, alongside protective factors such as coping skills or strong social support. A third AI model synthesizes these assessments into a structured summary, reducing the chance that any single model dominates the results. A final layer combines that summary with demographic information (e.g., age, sex), trauma type, and known PTSD prevalence rates to produce a risk score with a plain-language explanation.

Experiments confirmed that this three-layer design consistently outperformed simpler configurations across most performance metrics. Unlike AI that succeeds or fails for reasons unknown, PREVIA explains its reasoning and grounds predictions in established PTSD risk factors. Results with the tool were also validated by comparing them to diagnoses made for the same cases using Clinician-Administered PTSD Scale for DSM-5 (CAPS-5), the gold standard clinical interview for diagnosing PTSD.

"We built PREVIA on open-source models with no training or fine-tuning required," said Dr. Schultebraucks. "That means other hospitals can test it independently, without access to proprietary software or our patient data, which is essential for widespread adoption."

Along with Dr. Schultebraucks, authors of the study from NYU Langone were Victoria Mueller, Jyoti Yadav, Annie Farnsworth, Charlotte Hilberdink, Sapir Gershov, and Arieh Shalev in the Department of Psychiatry, Eric Oermann in the Department of Neurosurgery, and Stephen Wall in the Ronald O. Perelman Department of Emergency Medicine.

The study was funded by the National Institute for Mental Health grant R01 MH129856 (PI Dr. Katharina Schultebraucks).

Normal Solana transactions use recent blockhashes that expire after 30 seconds. Durable nonces replace those temporary references with stored values, allowing transactions to remain executable until the nonce advances.

The feature was designed for legitimate operational needs. Exchanges, custodians, and multisig organizations often require offline signing procedures that take longer than Solana’s normal transaction window.

However, attackers turned that convenience into an attack vector against Drift on April 1, 2026. They reportedly secured administrative approvals through air-gapped signing systems before allowing the transactions to sit for more than a week.

Once submitted, the approvals helped attackers take control of the protocol and drain at least $270 million. Drift subsequently attributed the attack to a North Korean-linked group.

Anza contributor Gabe Rodriguez filed SIMD-0723 on November 5. The proposal would require transactions to use recent blockhashes after activation. May 5, 2027, represents the earliest proposed mainnet activation date. The change still requires technical approval and migration planning.

Solana is also exploring replacement programs that could support offline signing without relying on durable nonces. These alternatives would introduce different authorization and transaction-failure considerations for custodians.

The proposal forces institutional users to reconsider how they authorize transactions across Solana. Meanwhile, questions about trust are becoming harder to ignore elsewhere in the ecosystem.

Patagonia’s original R1 fleece hoody became a classic because it was warm enough to wear for cold-weather workouts and breathable enough to absolutely hammer without holding moisture. The R1 Ultralight pushes harder in the latter direction. It was built not just to breathe but also to move with freeing raglan sleeves and underarm gussets.

Kid meltdowns are entertaining to watch as a bystander at the ski resort but unbearable as a parent. Nothing derails a family ski day quicker than an uncomfortable six-year-old in the parking lot. Poles are incredibly useful on giant climbs and equally annoying when you no longer want to use them. Black Diamond’s Distance Carbon Z FKT solves the second problem with ultralight carbon shafts and folding Z-Pole construction that collapses down to about 35 centimeters (14 inches) at the shortest size. A pair starts around 294 grams (10.4 ounces), which is light enough that carrying them across flats doesn’t feel like punishment for needing them uphill.

While it’s a physically huge gift, Old Town’s Discovery 158 is the sort of present that will change how an entire family spends summers. The 15-foot-8-inch hull is made from tough three-layer polyethylene and has more than 1,000 pounds of usable carrying capacity, enough for two paddlers, camping gear, a dog, and at least three wildly heavy cast-iron pots and pans. At 87 pounds, it’s decidedly not ultralight, but it’s stable, durable, and built for decades of lake time.

Cooking well while camping is one of life’s great pleasures, but that joy is significantly diminished if your kitchen is a hectic jumble of plastic bins spread across a picnic table. Cinch’s Kitchen Cruiser corrals the mess into one 43-pound aluminum box that unfolds into roughly 50 inches of cooking workspace with dual burners, counters, storage, and an integrated rechargeable water pump. It’s the ideal gift for the Type A camper with the entire operation folding back into a tidy, rectangular case that slips in the back of a truck when mealtime is over. “Yes, it’s heavy, and the legs are a pain to deploy. But I’ve decided this thing could weigh 100 pounds and I’d still find a way to cart it to every campsite,” says Outside associate gear director Jenny Wiegand after taking it camping this summer.

An 88-millimeter waist gives the Arcade enough platform for soft and chopped-up snow, while tip-and-tail rocker and a relatively tight turn radius keep it lively on groomers. A poplar core backed by a full Titanal layer adds stability when speed picks up. In other words, this ski will be fun in a few inches of fresh snow but will still hold an edge at speed on sheet ice. It’s on the luxury side of the backpacking weight spectrum at 850 grams (30 ounces), but those extra grams pay soft-touch dividends with a toasty 4.7 R-value and a stretch knit exterior that feels like a supple shirt on bare skin.

This inflatable touring kayak packs down small enough to check on a plane, then provides the length, shape, and rigidity needed for lakes, protected coastlines, and multi-day flatwater trips once inflated. A 13-foot sea kayak that you can check on a plane sounds like one of those outdoor-industry claims that require an asterisk. Alpacka nailed the construction and usability here, and it needs no asterisk.
