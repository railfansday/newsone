Zcash Is Getting a Major Upgrade in November. Could This Be a Catalyst for Further ZEC Price Gains?

📅 Nov 04, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

The red-hot cryptocurrency has a significant upgrade just days away.

Zcash (ZEC +14.01%), the largest privacy coin by market cap, is about to get a major technical upgrade around Nov. 5. The NU7 network upgrade will cut the time between blocks of transactions from 75 seconds to 25, making confirmations roughly three times faster.

Despite a rough year for the crypto market, Zcash has exploded in value, gaining 134% year to date. Let's take a closer look at the NU7 upgrade to see what it changes and whether it could drive further growth.

The main headline of the NU7 upgrade is the change in block time. Like other cryptocurrencies, Zcash records transactions on a blockchain, with each block containing groups of transactions. Blocks currently have a target confirmation time of 75 seconds, which will drop to 25 seconds after the upgrade.

This doesn't mean transactions go through in 25 seconds. Each exchange sets a required number of confirmations to fully verify a transaction, based on its risk tolerance. If an exchange waits for three confirmations, then transaction times on its platform would drop from nearly four minutes (225 seconds) to 75 seconds.

NU7 also changes Zcash's fee allocation. Starting at activation, 60% of each block's transaction fees are removed from circulation and placed in the network sustainability mechanism, a reserve that provides a security budget for the network. Miners keep the other 40% of transaction fees. Originally, 100% of block fees went to miners. Like Bitcoin, Zcash has a hard cap of 21 million coins, so the reserve prepares for a day when fees pay miners rather than newly issued coins.

In addition, the upgrade adds per-block limits on shielded transactions and shuts down Sprout, the blockchain's original privacy pool. Funds must be transferred out of Sprout before activation, or they'll become unusable.

Execution issues are the primary risk for the NU7 upgrade, especially since it's being activated on a faster schedule than usual. Zcash's rules normally call for activation to occur approximately three months after release of the first software for the new network. This provides exchanges, node operators, and miners with ample time to update their software before it expires.

NU7's deployment document waived the three-month requirement. A release candidate started running on Zcash's test network early last month, but the stable software for the main network only launched on Oct. 22. Anyone using outdated software could end up on a different chain.

The closure of the Sprout pool also presents potential issues. Any funds left there will be lost forever, and moving funds off that pool could temporarily expose transaction amounts.

NU7 is an important upgrade to the Zcash network, both shortening block times and setting aside fee revenue in a reserve. But I wouldn't expect it to have a big impact on the privacy coin's price.

Blockchain upgrades aren't surprises. They're scheduled in advance, giving traders time to buy in and often turning the actual activation dates into "sell-the-news" events. Zcash has also already been one of the most successful crypto investments this year, so an upgrade may not be enough to move the needle at this point.

For investors, it's still worth watching how activation goes. A clean switch to NU7 would be a positive sign, while a delay or glitch could weigh on sentiment. Either way, the upgrade's value is more likely to show up in how Zcash works than in its price.

Democrat Abdul El-Sayed defeats Republican Mike Rogers in key Michigan Senate race

📅 Nov 04, 2026  |  Category: Health  |  Read Source →

Abdul El-Sayed speaks to supporters at an Oct. 24 rally in Detroit. Spencer Platt/Getty Images hide caption

Midterms coverage: Live results | What to know | Stories around the countryStay connected: NPR Politics Podcast | NPR Politics newsletter | NPR app

DETROIT — Democrat Abdul El-Sayed has won the U.S. Senate election in Michigan, according to a race call by the Associated Press.

One of the most closely watched elections in the U.S., the race in Michigan tested how an antiestablishment candidate on the left would fare in a perennial Midwest battleground.

El-Sayed will be the first Muslim American to serve in the Senate.

The 42-year-old former public health official defeated former Republican Rep. Mike Rogers. Rogers lost his first bid for the Senate in 2024.

Harnessing anger over the cost of living and corruption, El-Sayed galvanized young and infrequent voters by focusing on passing single-payer healthcare and curbing the influence of corporate money in politics.

He is not a democratic socialist, unlike several candidates whose insurgent campaigns gained traction in primaries this cycle, though his campaign was backed by some of the biggest stars on the political left.

His positions on Israel alienated some Jewish voters, including comments after a truck-ramming attack at a synagogue preschool in metro Detroit — for which he later offered an apology — and campaigning during the primary with controversial left-wing streamer Hasan Piker.

Those were among the dynamics during a heated primary contest that turned into a stand-in for many of the broader debates fueling the direction of the Democratic Party after two years in the political wilderness.

In the primary, El-Sayed narrowly defeated Rep. Haley Stevens by about 1 percentage point, despite Michigan heavyweights like Gov. Gretchen Whitmer and retiring Sen. Gary Peters backing his opponent.

Mike Rogers speaks to supporters during a campaign event at Hoffman Farms Winery on Oct. 9 in Highland Township, Mich. Chip Somodevilla/Getty Images hide caption

In the general election's closing days, Stevens reversed course and announced she was endorsing Rogers, after initially saying she would support the Democratic ticket, underscoring the rocky path to uniting the Democratic coalition in Michigan. After the primary, polls showed a tight race with Rogers, despite Democrats having held the Senate seat since 1979.

During the campaign, El-Sayed criticized his opponent and other Republicans, including President Trump, for targeting him with Islamophobic rhetoric.

Trump early on endorsed Rogers, who has embraced some of the president's false claims about widespread election fraud, despite previously condemning them, including after the Jan. 6, 2021, attack on the U.S. Capitol. As November neared, Rogers tried to distance himself from some of the president's policies, including the war in Iran.

El-Sayed, a Michigan native who ran the health departments in Wayne County and the city of Detroit, first arrived on the political stage in 2018 with an unsuccessful bid for governor.

Now as Democrats wrestle with their party's identity, El-Sayed has signaled that he intends to be a prominent voice, especially given Michigan's prime position in the presidential nominating process in 2028.

Trumper’s Remorse: Voters Look at the Last Two Years, Ask for a Redo

📅 Nov 04, 2026  |  Category: Entertainment  |  Read Source →

With crushing wins in the House and Senate, America wants to Replayola everything since 2024.

Even the most forgiving retailers tend to draw a line on a return policy of 90 days. Come after that, you’re out of luck. But voters across America on Tuesday took a look at what they purchased two Novembers ago and said: “How about two years; would you accept that? The merchandise is a little battered, but the tags are intact.”After months of political fighting both substantive and surreal, with debates over ideology and assessments of buttocks, American voters Tuesday delivered as strong a rebuff to a Republican president as has been delivered in four decades, turning the House of Representatives and Senate over to Democrats and essentially saying “you see if you can stop him.”Democrats won in swing states and they won in red states; they won in states where Trump had been ascendant and states where Republicans never gave up control.From Maine to Texas, Kansas to South Carolina — hell, as of this writing even Mississippi was in play — as new Democratic Senators sprung up everywhere. They won so often cable news pundits ran out of water metaphors for a blue wave.Democrats gained, as of this writing, a whopping 41 seats in the House, far more than the three they needed to win control. But it was the Senate — where a flip was far from assured and in fact unlikely just weeks ago — where the most drama abided.A need for four flips was filled, then it soon turned into five, then six. States where Democrats hadn’t won a Senate seat in 38 years (Texas) or in 94 (Kansas). States where Trump won by double digits (Ohio) and states where Strom Thurmond presided for half a century (South Carolina).And, maybe most unlikely, states where Democrats hadn’t beaten a Susan Collins in 30 years (Maine).As of this writing, Democrats had flipped seven Senate seats with an eye on an eighth in Alaska and a ninth in Mississippi. They were holding on to all of the blue seats, including Michigan, called by CNN for Abdul El-Sayed at 2:30 am local time.There have been major shifts like his before, most notably in 1994 for Republicans and 2008 for Democrats. But both of those events occurred because of a galvanizing personality in the winning party — Newt Gingrich in 1994 and Barack Obama in 2008. What was remarkable about this sweep is that it didn’t come from one central charismatic figure but a slew of grassroots personalities who understood their individual constituencies, state-by-state: James Talarico and Josh Turek and Annie Andrews and Adam Hamilton. (Also, speaking of Talarico and Hamilton: it’s nice Democrats finally figured out what the Republicans did decades ago: overtly Christian candidates play.)And, of course, there was the Trump remorse. There was a political figure who mobilized the electorate — just in the negative. How fittingly 2026 that the figure the candidate who influenced voters was one people hated, Donald Trump and ICE and Iran and the fact that prices still seem to be going up while the only thing the president is bringing down is the East Wing.Perhaps the best modern comp with all this is to 1986. That Election Day 40 years ago, Democrats also scored like this in the midterms — also in the second term of a fading Republican icon, netting eight seats against Ronald Reagan.This election season has been one of the most entertaining, if also the most divisive in recent memory, as a showman president disliked even by his own party paved the way to a series of colorful showdowns.Books can be written about all the dynamics, but fights over Middle East policy roiled and at times threatened to break apart the Democratic party — right up until the final days with Haley Stevens’ shocking defection in Michigan. And yet somehow it all led to history — the first openly gay male senator in New Hampshire’s Chris Pappas, the first Black female governor in Keisha Lance Bottoms.Meanwhile, Republicans tried to look toward the future while grappling with the Donald Trump present, accepting his campaign bids even as they kept him out of nearly all of their ads. (Democrats, on the other hand, were all too happy to tether their opponents to the president.)And through it all the president himself often seemed to distract himself with random talk on a campaign trail where he was only ambivalently wanted.The Democrats will have some mountains ahead, not least including schisms in their own party; how will the Darializa Chevalier and Melat Kiros types in the House play with the mainstream types led by new speaker Hakeem Jeffries, and in the Senate how will Peggy Flanagan and Abdul El-Sayed, should his lead hold, meld with Midwestern personalities like Turek and Hamilton and majority leader Chuck Schumer.They will also have to decide how much to lean into inquiries and investigations, as this is not what voters sent them to Washington for — it’s the economy, stupid, as Bill Clinton’s (and James Carville’s) words continue to redound.But the bigger questions lie with Republicans, who now must decide in the minority how to oppose Democrats — and how much to genuflect to a man they have spent much of the past decade paying fealty to, now newly irrelevant. Perhaps the person most confused today, strategically: J.D. Vance. “Numbers [in transfer fees] are changing at the moment, but we won’t pay ridiculous or stupid [prices] because we want to be a solid and serious club. No financial risks will be taken for successes.

Once activated, a lawyer evaluating a client’s position could draw on applicable awards, treaty practice and institutional rules, then test the resulting research against documents in the matter’s Harvey Vault. The matter remains in Harvey, and findings can be carried into a memo, pleading or witness-preparation outline. Each authority links back to its source document on Jus Mundi’s platform.

Those results can be reviewed in the context of the matter’s pleadings, exhibits and transcripts. Pinpoint citations support checking the underlying material before an answer is presented to a tribunal. Recurring disputes work covered by the integration includes preparing a Request for Arbitration, checking authorities while drafting, comparing exhibit bundles with precedent and identifying clauses that rely on superseded international rules.
