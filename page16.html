HBO Max’s New 6-Part Rom-Com Is a Masterclass in Breaking the Genre’s Rules

📅 Nov 20, 2026  |  Category: Entertainment  |  Read Source →

Amanda M. Castro is a Network TV writer at Collider and a New York–based journalist whose work has appeared in Newsweek, where she is an Associate Editor, and The U.S. Sun, where she previously served as a Senior Consumer Reporter.

She specializes in network television coverage, delivering sharp, thoughtful analysis of long-running procedural hits and ambitious new dramas across broadcast TV. At Collider, Amanda explores character arcs, storytelling trends, and the cultural impact of network series that keep audiences tuning in week after week.

Born and raised in Puerto Rico, Amanda is bilingual and holds a degree in Communication, Film, and Media Studies from the University of New Haven.

Romantic comedies tend to follow a familiar formula, where two people meet, fall in love, face a few obstacles, and eventually find their way back to each other. The best ones might even convince you that love can survive just about anything. But what happens when the relationship falls apart before the story really gets going? That's the question at the heart of Break Clause, a new British romantic comedy that finds plenty of laughs, awkward encounters, and genuine heartbreak in the aftermath of a breakup. Now streaming on HBO Max, the six-part series makes a compelling case for why sometimes the hardest part of ending a relationship isn't letting go. It's figuring out who gets to keep the apartment.

Created by Jess Bray and directed by Alice Snedden, Break Clause stars Samuel Bottomley as Ben and Lara Ricote as Lil, a young couple whose plans for a fresh start in London fall apart almost immediately. After four years together at university in Leeds, Lil lands a job in the capital and convinces Ben to make the move with her, but Ben has been keeping a secret. After a drunken indiscretion on their final night in Leeds, he eventually confesses to cheating on Lil, leaving the couple newly single just as they've signed a rental agreement.

Unfortunately, neither can afford to just pack up and leave. With six months of the lease to run, Ben and Lil have no choice but to become roommates, creating an uncomfortable situation where both must live separate lives while sharing a living space, kitchen, and bathroom facilities. And that's where Break Clause excels: instead of treating a break-up as the end of a romantic story, it shows the complications that follow.

One of the story's strongest points is that it clearly shows the ridiculousness of Ben and Lil's situation while still acknowledging the feelings beneath it. Because of this, the show can turn the storyline by focusing on two people who have already broken up instead of uniting them, as most romantic comedies usually do. They are no longer close, but they still find it hard to stop acting like a couple, at least for a bit.

Four love stories, four completely different ideas of what it means to be somebody's person. This is the one that matches how you actually fall, and what it would ask of you.

You do not fall for strangers. You fall for the person you have already been talking to for years, which is the slowest and by some distance the most durable way anybody has found to do this.

You want a partner, not an audience. Somebody who wants their own life as badly as you want yours, and who would never ask you to be smaller to make the relationship easier.

The intensity was never the problem. The secrecy is. You are drawn to a love that lives entirely in private, and to the particular ache of wanting somebody you are not supposed to want.

You do not do this cautiously. When it happens for you it is immediate and total and it rearranges everything, and you would not accept a smaller version of it.

Notably, the idea of getting used to failure also appears inthe breakup. Generally, when people go to a different city, they think that they are starting a new chapter of life. However, Ben and Lil face unemployment and financial issues and conclude that their relationship was not as strong as they thought. The rental contract complicates the situation by creating ethical problems for everyone involved.

The story's financial aspect adds another layer to the main conflict. Ben, who is trying to become a writer and struggles to find work, starts looking for food in garbage bins to save money and even considers participating in clinical research. Lil, meanwhile, has already secured a position at an architectural firm, creating an imbalance that becomes another source of tension between them. Ben’s negative attitude, tied to their financial situation, can sometimes spark arguments and problems in their relationship.

The central relationship wouldn't work without the two actors playing it. Bottomley and Ricote give Ben and Lil enough chemistry to make their shared history believable, even when they're at their least compatible. Ben is withdrawn, insecure, and often too preoccupied with his own shortcomings to recognize what's happening around him. Lil is much more outgoing, approaching life with an enthusiasm that occasionally gets her into trouble. Their personalities make for an entertaining contrast, particularly when they're forced to deal with the same uncomfortable situations.

One unique performer in the cast is Ricote. She plays Lil in a way that captures the character's positive, contagious energy instead of portraying her as a peculiar person with a variety of characteristics. Although her positivity is lovable, it also creates room for mistakes. For instance, when Lil tries to attend a street dance but then realizes that nobody else is interested, it makes the character so unique. While Bottomley often explores Ben's awkwardness and embarrassment, he also shows his attempts to reconcile with life in a ridiculous yet realistic way. Both make their characters more memorable and show why it matters whether they find a solution.

There's plenty of material here beyond whether Ben and Lil will reconcile, from disastrous dinner parties to uncomfortable reunions with university friends, Break Clause finds comedy in everyday situations that become considerably more complicated when your ex is still living across the hall. Its humor can be wonderfully strange and deadpan, but Bray also understands when to give a scene space rather than turn every uncomfortable moment into a punchline.

Ben and Lil are not at home just waiting for their lease to end; they are trying to come to grips with what “growing up” means when the future they planned for no longer exists. The world around them has changed, and their friends are moving on with their careers and their lives, which makes them think about the future and their relationships with those who were once dear to them.

This six-part comedy turns ridiculous movie pitches into some of the year’s funniest television.

In the end, the series resorts to clichéd romantic comedy tropes it seems to want to avoid at the beginning, but that doesn't have to bea flaw. The mere potential for reconciliation gives the narrative an emotional arc, even as Ben and Lil navigate new relationships and increasingly muddled emotions. The issue is not only whether they still love one another but also whether they should even want the kind of relationship that they had.

One hand on the controller, one on the pizza roll: How America fed itself through GTA VI’s $3.79 billion launch night

📅 Nov 20, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

The biggest video game launch in years left millions of players with a practical problem: how to eat without putting down the controller.

Rockstar Games, a unit of Take-Two Interactive, said the game brought in an estimated $3.79 billion in retail sales worldwide on its first day, which it called the biggest entertainment launch in history. With that demand, there are surely some tired people holding onto those controllers but hungry for a snack.

It seems like the delivery apps already planned for it. Uber Eats picked several of its Grand Theft Auto VI launch-day deals for food that could be eaten with one hand. In the U.S., the list included buy-one-get-one McNuggets at McDonald’s, boneless wings at Wingstop, pizza at Papa John’s and Whopper Jr. burgers at Burger King.

“We even picked some of our food deals with one-handed eating in mind, so fans can keep their focus on the game,” Uber President and COO Andrew Macdonald said in a statement to Fortune.

Gamers ordered accordingly. Between midnight and 5 a.m. on launch night, Uber Eats orders in Miami, the real city behind the game’s Vice City, rose more than 30% from the prior two Thursdays, Macdonald said on X. Nationwide, orders of Red Bull Watermelon rose 95%, Totino’s Pizza Rolls 54% and Ghost Sour Strips 46%. Uber told Fortune it had no separate data on one-handed food orders.

DoorDash reported a similar pattern. Between 9 p.m. and 2 a.m. ET, orders for chips, burgers, fries and pizza each rose more than 10% compared with the average of the six prior Wednesdays, the company told Fortune. Energy drinks rose more than 20%. Miami led all major markets, with late-night orders up nearly 25%, and Philadelphia, Detroit and Atlanta also saw double-digit increases, according to DoorDash’s data dashboard. DoorDash said it could not compare the food increases with its overall order growth.

Some of the biggest jumps were for gear, not food. Orders for controllers and gaming accessories rose 450% on DoorDash, HDMI cables 440% and headsets 70%. Searches for GTA VI on the app rose more than 940% in the week before launch.

“GTA 6 is more than a game launch. It’s a cultural moment that fans have been waiting years for,” Gina Igwe, DoorDash’s vice president of brand, creative and consumer marketing, said in a statement to Fortune.

Players who weren’t playing were watching. In its first two days, GTA VI drew more livestream watch time than any game has in its entire launch week, more than Elden Ring, Hogwarts Legacy and Cyberpunk 2077 drew in their first weeks combined, according to Streams Charts.

Total Twitch watch time rose 61% compared with the two days before launch, the firm said, while World of Warcraft and Counter-Strike lost about 31% of their Twitch watch time and Fortnite fell 60% on YouTube. Kick’s “Just Sleeping” category rose 176% as streamers ran round-the-clock GTA VI marathons.

Restaurant chains also moved to get a share. Popeyes is selling a Spice City sandwich, named after the game’s setting, through Nov. 22 and told players to order it through its app “between missions.” McDonald’s began taking delivery orders on Discord, the chat app many gamers use while they play. DoorDash ran offers with Wendy’s and the Cheesecake Factory, and Grubhub offered $10 off to its first 1,000 customers.

Analytics firm Sensor Tower expects the game to drive more than 50 million additional visits to U.S. food delivery and restaurant apps in its first 30 days, Reuters reported.

Neither company put a dollar figure on launch night. A rough Fortune estimate suggests the extra takeout spending was small next to the game itself. Uber and DoorDash together handled about $680 million in delivery orders a day worldwide in the third quarter, based on their earnings reports. If late-night hours make up roughly a tenth of that, and launch night lifted those orders 10% to 25%, the extra spending comes to roughly $7 million to $17 million. That’s about 1% to 2.5% of a normal day’s delivery business for the two companies, and less than half a percent of the $3.79 billion Rockstar says the game brought in on day one.

That shift fits what economists say about events like this one. Much of the money moves from one kind of spending to another rather than appearing from nowhere. “People just rearrange how they’re spending their free time,” Victor Matheson, an economist at the College of the Holy Cross, told Fortune before the launch.

Restaurants also had fewer hands. Missed restaurant shifts rose 92% week over week on Nov. 19, according to data from scheduling platform Harri shared with Fortune, covering 280,000 shifts. One in 14 shifts went unworked, compared with one in 27 the Thursday before, while sales at those restaurants fell less than 2%.

Grand Theft Auto VI came out Nov. 19 on PlayStation 5 and Xbox Series X|S, more than 13 years after its predecessor. Before the launch, a widely shared estimate claimed workers skipping their jobs to play would cost the U.S. economy $1 billion, a figure Matheson told Fortune he doesn’t believe. Office attendance barely moved. Wi-Fi logins at the more than 250 U.S. locations of Industrious, a coworking company owned by CBRE, fell about 5% on launch day compared with the previous three Thursdays, the company told Fortune.

Before Harry Potter entered theaters in 2001, a highly praised children's novel was adapted into a film. The feature had a star-studded cast and earned praise from fans and critics alike. 30 years later, the feature has captured a new audience and become a streaming hit.

The film in question is Matilda, a children's fantasy movie based on Roald Dahl's book of the same name, starring Mara Wilson as Matilda, a young girl who discovers she has powers. Unlike Stephen King's Carrie, a horror story that also follows a young girl with powers, Matilda uses her newfound abilities to help others. The book came out in 1988, and the film adaptation, which also starred Danny DeVito (It's Always Sunny in Philadelphia), Rhea Perlman (Barbie), and Pam Ferris (Harry Potter and the Prisoner of Azkaban), was released in 1996.

Recently, Matilda became a streaming hit on HBO Max, landing at #4 on the platform's Top 10 Movies Worldwide, sitting between The End of Oak Street and The Legend of Tarzan. Since its release, Matilda has grossed $47 million worldwide and earned a Certified Fresh critics score of 90% and an audience score of 73% on Rotten Tomatoes, plus a 3.8-star rating on Letterboxd.

Four worlds and four completely different ideas of what a good life looks like. This is where you belong, what it would ask of you, and what you would get back in return.

You belong somewhere old enough that the mountains have names and somebody can still tell you who gave them. Middle-earth is for people who think the right thing is worth doing even when it will not be finished in their lifetime.

You want the sea, a crew, and absolutely nobody telling you where you are allowed to go. The Grand Line is the world for people whose main ambition in life is finding out what is over there.

You would rather be feared and useful than liked and useless. The Continent is a world where the monsters are the straightforward part of the job and the people are where it all gets complicated.

You want the myths to be real and loose in the actual world, and you want to find out at about thirteen that you were never anywhere near as ordinary as everybody kept telling you.

Roald Dahl was a British author born in 1916 and died in 1990, known for his children's books. Matilda is one of many books in Dahl's collection adapted to screen. Another popular entry is Charlie and the Chocolate Factory, a 1964 book that received two film adaptations and a prequel. The first came out in 1971 as Willy Wonka & the Chocolate Factory and starred Gene Wilder as Willy Wonka. It was adapted again in 2005, starring Johnny Depp as Willy Wonka and Freddie Highmore as Charlie Bucket. Then, in 2023, a prequel to the 1971 feature was released, called Wonka, starring Timothée Chalamet as a young Willy Wonka who has yet to build his chocolate factory. In 1996, a film adaptation of James and the Giant Peach was released, based on Dahl's 1961 novel of the same name, but it was a box-office failure, earning only $28.9 million. There's also The BFG (Big Friendly Giant), a children's book that was first published in 1982. The book received two film adaptations. The first came out in 1989, and the second, directed by Steven Spielberg, came out in 2016 and earned nearly $200 million worldwide.

The Internet Archive's workers are unionizing

📅 Nov 20, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

They asked founder Brewster Kahle to voluntarily recognize the union, but will go to an NLRB vote if needed.

Workers at the Internet Archive, home to the Wayback Machine, are unionizing. After signing up to join the Communications Workers of America (CWA), they're asking founder Brewster Kahle to voluntarily recognize the union. As a backup, they've filed for a National Labor Relations Board (NLRB) election. Kahle hasn't responded publicly yet.

Wired reports that over 80 of 124 eligible US workers signed on. None of their asks are radical: better pay, additional benefits that apply to everyone and paths for career development and promotion. Organizing committee member Danielle Leard tied the unionization campaign to the Internet Archive's mission, stating that job security, benefits and livable wages go "hand-in-hand with sustaining the Archive long-term."

There's a recent precedent from another Bay Area nonprofit. Wikimedia Foundation workers joined the CWA in September, but only after the foundation refused to voluntarily recognize the effort and it went to an NLRB vote. Wikimedia workers ultimately voted in favor by a count of 158 to 14.

The Internet Archive has had a rocky few years. In 2023, a judge ruled that it violated copyright law by lending ebooks, and it suffered a major cyberattack the following year that left the Wayback Machine out of commission for weeks. Adding to the troubles, many news outlets began blocking the Archive's crawlers, fearing its archival copies could be used as a workaround for AI scraping. But the news hasn't been all bad: the US Senate designated the Internet Archive as an official US government document depository in 2025.

Pfizer's Launched and Acquired Drugs Grew 32% in Q3 — and 3 Other Reasons to Buy the Stock Before 2027

📅 Nov 20, 2026  |  Category: Business  |  Read Source →

Pfizer remains deeply unloved on Wall Street, but it is slowly turning a corner.

By Reuben Gregg Brewer – Nov 20, 2026 at 5:15PM EST

Pfizer (PFE -0.51%) faces a large patent cliff, and investors worry it won't be able to offset the revenue it loses as older drugs face generic competition. That's a problem, but the drugmaker is aware of it and is making important strides in its efforts to address the issue. The big story, however, is that this issue is pretty normal for a drug company.

In fact, revenue from recently launched and acquired drugs rose 32% in the third quarter of 2026. That shows the pharmaceutical giant's efforts are bearing fruit. But there's more reason to be excited, including the company's strong balance sheet, its large dividend, and management's commitment to continue paying that dividend. Here's what you need to know.

One of the biggest worries about Pfizer is its upcoming patent expirations. Two big ones are Eliquis in 2028 and Vyndamax in 2031. But patent expirations are a normal part of the pharma sector. And research and development efforts in the highly technical and intensely competitive healthcare sector don't always align perfectly with a company's patent expiration schedule. It's just a fact of life.

The 32% revenue growth Pfizer achieved from launched and acquired products is evidence that the company is seeing some success in its efforts to address the issue. And it sets up three more reasons to consider buying the stock.

Pfizer ended the third quarter of 2026 with $1.8 billion in cash and $12.4 billion in short-term investments. That gives the company significant firepower to support its business through this patent-expiration cycle. In fact, the company's cash hoard is slightly higher than at the start of the year, so it isn't rapidly burning through its savings.

Despite the company's results with new drugs and its financial strength, investors are deeply negative on Pfizer's stock. That's highlighted by its huge 6.1% dividend yield as of this writing. To put that yield into context, the S&P 500 index (^GSPC -3.70%) yields roughly 1.1%, and the average pharma stock yields 1.3%.

The negativity highlighted by the elevated yield is echoed by more traditional valuation metrics. Pfizer's price-to-sales and price-to-book ratios are below their five-year averages. And while the price-to-earnings ratio is elevated, it is largely due to one-time charges. Looking at the whole picture, Pfizer's stock looks attractively priced.

Given the lofty yield, Pfizer is likely to be most appealing to dividend-focused investors. But right now earnings aren't covering the dividend. That's not a great sign, but one-time charges are part of the issue. If you look at cash flow, the company can still support the dividend. That's highlighted by a cash dividend payout ratio of roughly 85%. While a lower number would be better, there's still some room for adversity. And don't forget about the company's large cash pile.

Both of these facts help support management's strong backing of the dividend. Indeed, one of Pfizer's key capital priorities is to "maintain and grow our dividend." Obviously, the company's priorities could change, but it wouldn't make a statement like that if the board of directors wasn't on board with protecting the dividend.

At the end of the day, Pfizer is facing very real headwinds today. But it is an industry-leading company with a long history of success. If history is any guide, it will muddle through this difficult period. The success it is having with new drugs, its financial strength, attractive valuation, and dedication to its large dividend are all strong reasons for investors to take a deep dive before 2026 is over. If you wait too long, the opportunity here may pass you by.

The last New Music Friday before Thanksgiving is full of treats.

Thirteen years after Grand Theft Auto V took over the gaming world, Rockstar Games has unleashed the sixth iteration of the wildly popular video game. To celebrate, Atlantic Records oversaw Grand Theft Auto VI: The Album, a sprawling compilation album that brings the video game’s high-octane, Miami-set world to life across hip-hop, pop, country, alternative, dance, Latin, Afrobeats and more. While Cardi B, Morgan Wallen and Travis Scott headlined the compilation soundtrack with key pre-release singles, the full 34-track set also features contributions from SZA, Dua Lipa, Zara Larsson, Lana Del Rey, Robert Glasper, Camila Cabello, Omar Apollo, JENNIE, Fcukers, Burna Boy, Rauw Alejandro, Gunna, Young Thug, JT and Justice.

Virgil van Dijk delivers future update amid Liverpool contract uncertainty

📅 Nov 20, 2026  |  Category: Sports  |  Read Source →

Virgil van Dijk is unsure where he will be playing his football next season, with his Liverpool contract due to expire in the summer.

The Dutchman, 35, has plied his trade at Anfield since 2018 and is now a two-time Premier League champion.

He will be out of contract in June, though, and has revealed in a new interview with Sky Sports that it remains unclear whether he will extend his stay. He is, however, clear that he expects to continue his playing career for several more years.

Per reports from BBC Sport, talks regarding a new deal are underway but an agreement is not close, and no resolution is expected before the new year. From January, Van Dijk will be free to sign pre-contract agreements with clubs outside England.

“The main thing for me is that I feel good and I'm looking after myself and I still have many more years in my opinion,” said Van Dijk.

“Whatever's going to happen after this season, we'll see.

“At this moment of time, if anyone says that or put out that they know what I'm going to do next season, it's untrue because at this moment of time, speaking to you, I've got no idea either.”

He continued, explaining that he will remain fully focused on on-pitch matters as he awaits clarity on his contract situation.

“But I'm relaxed with it because if you know me, I only focus on what I can control and that's at the moment the Manchester United game. Then we'll focus to the Champions League and whatever happens in the new year coming, we'll see then.

"But it's been an incredible journey, almost 10 years this summer and it's been a big part of my life and I'll forever be grateful for anything that happened over those years.”

He added, matter-of-factly: “If it's getting extended, we'll see then. If not, then I'm going to say goodbyes in the summer.”
