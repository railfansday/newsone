California targets Nvidia in China AI chip probe

📅 Nov 19, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

California has served an investigative subpoena on the AI chip designer Nvidia in the wake of a scandal that ended in the departure of longtime CEO Jensen Huang.

The state's top law enforcement officer, attorney general Rob Bonta, said he is probing whether Nvidia or Huang's actions resulted in China obtaining advanced chips or confidential information, adding that such actions could violate California law if the company is found to have mislead investors and the public.

The BBC has contacted Nvidia for comment.

"Nvidia is in a messy, transnational situation but the underlying principle of our corporate securities law is clear: Companies cannot mislead their investors," Bonta said on Thursday.

Nvidia has its global headquarters in Santa Clara, California.

Earlier this month, it emerged that Huang had an extramarital relationship with a suspected Chinese spy, Alice Huang, who was an executive at the Singapore-based data centre company Megaspeed.

Some members of the US Congress have urged the Department of Commerce to investigate whether Megaspeed, a major Nvidia customer, diverted Nvidia chips to China.

Megaspeed has previously said it is aware of reports "containing assertions about our business, supply chain and global operations that are not supported by verifiable evidence".

Nvidia shares have plummeted 22% since the New York Times reported on the affair in early November.

Earlier this week, the company's board chair Harvey Jones said Nvidia had no intention of reinstating Jensen Huang after the longtime Nvidia boss and co-founder issued a statement saying he would be "honoured" to return, as some tech leaders including Elon Musk campaigned on his behalf.

Have Portugal found Cristiano Ronaldo's replacement? Ex-Man City star Carlos Forbs impressed Erling Haaland and stunned Wales this week in No 7 shirt - just over a year after being brutally axed via WHATSAPP

📅 2026-11-19  |  Category: World  |  Read Source →

Published: 18:03 EST, 19 November 2026 | Updated: 18:07 EST, 19 November 2026

As far as auditions to replace Cristiano Ronaldo go, Carlos Forbs' couldn't have gone much better.

His winner against Wales on Tuesday certainly looked and felt like a Ronaldo goal, especially from early in the former Manchester United star's career.

Right flank. Dribbling at pace. Ben Davies back-tracking. A quick flap inside with the left boot. Shoot. Goal. He only needed 11 minutes since coming on to change the game - and potentially his career trajectory.

Portugal's No7 later insisted he did not feel the 'weight' of the shirt, but the jersey is pregnant with meaning. Ronaldo wore No 7 for the national team for 20 years, inheriting it from the legendary Luis Figo. Its next permanent incumbent matters deeply in the Portuguese footballing psyche.

Forbs has not inherited it full-time yet, as a source within the Portugal camp has confirmed to Daily Mail Sport. He appears locked in a battle with Rafael Leao, who was supposed to have it this international break until he missed out through injury.

But now Forbs - a chap who went to school in Leeds and was praised by Erling Haaland while at Manchester City - is the name on fans' lips.

Carlos Forbs is gunning to be Portugal's new full-time No7 after Cristiano Ronaldo's retirement - and his audition against Wales went swimmingly

Ronaldo held the shirt for 20 years, having inherited it from the legendary Luis Figo

They are already calling him 'CF7' and media outlets across the world hailed the 22-year-old, a relative unknown for plays for Club Brugge. 'The new national hero,' is how leading Portuguese outlet O Jogo described him. No pressure, kid.

His form is attracting attention on British shores. Rangers were interested in him over the summer, as were Lyon. Daily Mail Sport has been unable to verify recent reports that the likes of Arsenal, Aston Villa, Everton and Newcastle are keen.

But it would be no surprise to see him in the Premier League one day. Stardom has long felt like it might be on the cards for Forbs. Born in Sintra to Bissau-Guinean parents, he started off playing futsal and was picked up by Sporting Lisbon's academy, just like Ronaldo. His family left for England when he was just 11, and it wasn't long before Manchester City came calling.

The attacker spent eight years in the City academy and was considered a gem. In 2022-23 he was named Premier League 2 player of the season after a stunning campaign which saw him score 29 goals and provide 18 assists in all competitions.

He had a knack of scoring hat-tricks - not a bad habit to adopt. In one match he scored four against Manchester United like it was child's play. It felt like nothing could go wrong for the winger, who copied Ronaldo's lesser-known 'nap' celebration to mock the Red Devils. A player who looked up to the likes of Raheem Sterling and Phil Foden didn't look far off joining them in the first team.

He was rewarded with chances to train alongside Pep Guardiola's seniors, and Kyle Walker and Bernardo Silva were noted as being particularly welcoming. Erling Haaland was impressed with him. Watching a montage including the youngster's goals, he said: 'Is that Carlos? I've been training a lot with these guys. Carlos is good.'

But a debut never came due to the competition at City and in 2023, yearning for opportunities to play in the big-time, he moved to Ajax in a £17million deal.

'I didn’t have to leave, but in the summer, clubs came along where I knew I had more prospects,' he told AD in October 2023, while he was still known as Carlos Borges.

Forbs, previously known as Carlos Borges, was a gem in Manchester City's academy but never got a first-team debut

He was shipped off to Ajax but struggled to nail down a starter spot and was harshly cast aside

'The media made me big, but I didn’t really feel like I was getting many opportunities in Manchester. I didn’t know if that would change this season. Of course, I already knew that Ajax focuses on youth. When I heard the plans, I thought OK, I want to try my luck at this club.

'A number of staff members at Manchester City also wanted to see me make the final step after ten years. Me too, at the club where I grew up, just as it was my parents’ dream. But I concluded that it was even better to take this opportunity.'

But things didn't work out for Forbs in the Netherlands. Playing for such a dominant side against low blocks did not suit his electric pace and direct dribbling, even though he had managed at Manchester City, who had a similar philosophy.

He was a bit-part player in his first season and, in his second, was sent on loan to Wolves, where he had little impact in 10 Premier League appearances. While he had more game time under Gary O'Neil, he did not fit into Vitor Pereira's 3-4-2-1 system, which favoured wing-backs, when the Portuguese replaced the Englishman.

By the summer of 2025, Ajax had seen enough, and dispensed of Forbs in brutal fashion. Technical director Alex Kroes informed seven players, including him, over WhatsApp that he was 'no longer welcome' at the club's training ground. Former Arsenal man Chuba Akpom was among them, too.

If Forbs wanted to train, he had to wait until the first team were done. Even if it was a day of double sessions, he had to stay away until both were over. With three years remaining on his contract, his space in the car park had reportedly been removed. Access to the gym and canteen was only permitted when the first team were finished.

A once-dream move had soured within two years and he was cast away savagely, joining Belgian side Club Brugge - and that is where his career has been reborn.

Early into his Brugge career, in November 2025, he announced himself in Europe by scoring two goals and providing an assist against Barcelona in the Champions League.

Forbs has shone for Club Brugge and scored twice against Barcelona last season

'Today is a dream come true,' he said after being awarded man of the match against Barca. It was the sort of night his youth coaches had known he was capable of.

After 10 goals in all competitions last season, he is already on five for this campaign and growing his reputation. He is on three Portugal caps now - he made his debut last year - and seems to have the trust of new manager Jorge Jesus.

When pressed if he was the new permanent Portugal No7 on Tuesday, Forbs was coy. 'I try to do my best,' he said.

Then he added: 'We know it's a big, blessed number and I hope to continue wearing this number and scoring many goals.'

It remains to be seen whether he will take on the mantle of Ronaldo, who scored a world record 146 goals for Portugal. The longer-term favourite is Leao, an established star who has won Serie A with AC Milan. But Leao has hit a rough patch in the last couple of years and as his stock wanes, Forbs' star is only rising.

California attorney general issues Nvidia subpoena in probe of China chip diversion

📅 2026-11-19  |  Category: Business  |  Read Source →

California Attorney General Rob Bonta issued an investigative subpoena to Nvidia on Thursday as part of a probe of whether the company's advanced semiconductor chips ended up in China in contravention of U.S. export restrictions, his office said.

The probe comes a week after former Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang resigned after allegations he had multiple affairs, including one with Alice Huang, a suspected Chinese spy. Alice Huang, who is not related to Jensen Huang, was an executive at Megaspeed, a Singapore-based company that is being investigation for potentially diverting restricted Nvidia chips to China.

"The conduct of Nvidia and its former CEO raises bright red flags, including about the ways in which the company may have misled investors," Bonta said in a statement.

"My office is investigating whether Nvidia or Jensen Huang's actions resulted in China obtaining advanced chips or confidential information — and if Nvidia misled investors and the public about this risk, in violation of the law," Bonta said.

"Nvidia is in a messy, transnational situation but the underlying principle of our corporate securities law is clear: Companies cannot mislead their investors," the attorney general said.

Nvidia did not immediately respond to a request for comment on Bonta's subpoena.

Nvidia shares are down 22% in the nine trading days since Huang admitted to an extramarital affair. The stock is on pace for its seventh straight weekly loss, shedding 38% of its value over that stretch.

Anthropic was supposed to save the AI trade. What went wrong?

📅 2026-11-19  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Anthropic's historic IPO — the largest in U.S. history — delivered a big win for the company and its investors. But it failed at another task: reviving animal spirits in the AI trade.

The Nasdaq Composite closed down 2.25% amid a broader market pullback with the S&P 500 declining 2% to 7,175.

Investors had been looking forward to the Anthropic IPO as a "clearing event" — reviving the tech sector after months of choppy trading, the Nvidia espionage scandal, and growing concerns that AI chip sales to Asia are likely to slump as Washington cracks down on potential violations of export controls.

Some of those worries resurfaced Thursday as a short seller, Hunterbrook Media, alleged in a report that firmware sold by chipmaker AMD had turned up in U.S.-blacklisted companies. AMD stock closed down more than 7%. The chipmaker told CNBC it complies with U.S. law.

Adding to the worries, the House Foreign Affairs Committee released documents showing that a subagency of the Commerce Department approved the sale of more than 879,000 AI chips to Chinese companies. That also raised the prospect of an Asia sales slowdown under stricter export controls.

Many investors and analysts continue to express confidence in the AI trade, noting that Anthropic's successful market debut — the stock closed up 17% from its IPO price and raised more than $81 billion — bodes well for capital spending across tech.

But investors acknowledged that this is a high-tension moment and said it's time to be more strategic about the trade.

Dan Niles, founder of Niles Investment Management, described himself as a "circumspect bull," making the comparison to market conditions in 2022 when the big indices tanked in the back half of the year.

"You could have still made money that year. You just needed to have the right combination of longs and shorts," he said.

Part of the challenge, Niles said, has been a price war between the two cutting-edge frontier labs, Anthropic and OpenAI. That has posed a threat to the overall revenue and profit potential of AI and the capex going into it. Fierce competition on price is producing collective losses between the two top companies, he notes.

"The combined revenue growth for OpenAI plus Anthropic went from growing 75% sequentially in Q2 to 48% in Q3," he told CNBC. "The combined losses went from $7.9 billion to about $8.4 billion from Q2 to Q3."

While uncertainty is high now, some resolution may be on the horizon if a greater degree of commercial and regulatory clarity emerges over the next few months.

Trading experts also told CNBC that the tech sector's troubles Thursday were a more serious matter than Anthropic simply siphoning capital from other parts of the market.

"There's a reset of AI in general," said JJ Kinahan, senior vice president at Cboe Global Markets.

Investors for years have profited in tech by buying the dip, he said, but "there's been an attitude change that [you] can't buy things blindly and they'll all go up." Nvidia's ongoing declines have shaken confidence in the buy-the-dip strategy, he said.

Anthropic's next few trading days will be a key gauge of the market's strength. Friday will be a "wild card," Kinahan said. It could be a "final flush out" for the market, or a bounce that leaves the market in "no man's land" while volatility remains elevated.

A better gauge may be Monday, when options on Anthropic start trading, providing insight into whether retail investors will back the company in force — providing more of a vote of confidence in the AI trade.

"If people think we're going up [on Anthropic] they'll buy stock, but retail particularly will just buy calls," he said. "There's a bigger test coming Monday."

For now, investors continue to sell AI-related stocks.

Neoclouds, the generally highly indebted AI-dedicated computing providers at the outer edges of the AI trade, were down substantially Thursday, with CoreWeave closing down more than 6%.

Hyperscalers such as Alphabet and Amazon.com, viewed as relatively defensive AI bets, also finished lower.

One bright spot: Cybersecurity stocks continued to rally, with Palo Alto Networks and CrowdStrike both closing up. AI is finding security holes across software infrastructure, creating a scramble to patch the vulnerabilities before hackers can exploit them.

— CNBC's Daren Fonda contributed reporting.

Gap names new Athleta CEO, lowers full-year guidance

📅 2026-11-19  |  Category: Business  |  Read Source →

Gap Inc. on Thursday announced Ashley Kechter will be the next president and CEO of its Athleta brand, effective Dec. 14.

Kechter comes to Gap from activewear retailer Vuori, where she was global president and oversaw the brand's global business strategy, marketing and financials. She was previously global brand president of Fabletics and began her career at Gap before that.

"I'm excited to return to Gap Inc. and partner with [Gap CEO Richard Dickson] and the team to strengthen our connection with customers and shape the brand's next chapter," Kechter said in a statement.

The announcement comes after Lululemon poached former Athleta CEO Maggie Gauger to be its chief product officer last month.

"Clearly, we're disappointed with Athleta's performance, and we recognize there's a lot of work ahead," Dickson told CNBC. "We had been in rebuild mode, but now we're really in turnaround mode."

Dickson said the company is focused on getting back to its fundamentals: fabrications, feature, form, function and fit. Dickson added that Kechter will be conducting a "comprehensive review" of the brand to reposition Athleta for future success.

While the execution of its strategy has thus far "fallen short," Dickson said this new approach will allow Athleta to fully realize its potential.

Gap also reported mixed fiscal third-quarter earnings on Thursday and posted flat overall comparable sales. Wall Street had been expecting a comparable sales increase of 1.3%, according to StreetAccount.

Net sales grew 2%, though demand softened in October and early November due to warmer weather, the company said. Store sales increased 1%, while online sales increased 3%.

Gap also said it's recorded a $420 million adjustment to cost of goods sold and a $21 million adjustment to interest income from tariff refunds so far this year.

The company lowered its full-year net sales outlook to be "roughly flat," down from previous guidance of up between 1% and 1.5%. Gap is also now expecting adjusted earnings per share of between $2.29 and $2.34, down from an earlier expectation of between $2.35 and $2.45 per share.

Dickson said in an interview that much of the lowered guidance was due to warmer weather in the first two weeks of the quarter, but he said Gap is expecting "meaningful acceleration" in shopping ahead of the peak holiday season.

"We're monitoring the trends, and we're going to remain agile as we respond to the changing business trends," Dickson said.

Here's how the company performed in its fiscal third quarter compared with what Wall Street was expecting, based on a survey of analysts by LSEG:

For the quarter, Gap reported net income of $311 million, or 86 cents per share, compared with $236 million, or 62 cents per share, the year prior. Sales rose slightly from $3.94 billion the year prior.

At Athleta, net sales were down 14% compared with last year, coming in at $220 million. Comparable sales for the banner sank 15%, which the company attributed to "limited traction" from efforts to modernize its merchandise and marketing.

The company's namesake brand saw 9% comparable sales growth for the quarter, with net sales of $1.1 billion, marking an 11% increase compared with last year. Dickson said it was the brand's 12th consecutive quarter of positive comparable sales.

"Gap is really firing on all cylinders, and the consistency of the brand is really giving us confidence in the brand's ability to continue its momentum," Dickson said.

The Old Navy banner saw flat comparable sales for the quarter and net sales of $2.3 billion, up 1% compared with last year. It's the first earnings report since the brand, which has been experiencing sluggish sales, named Michael Francis as its new CEO last quarter. Francis began his role at Old Navy on Nov. 2.

Banana Republic saw its net sales sink 3% compared with last year, which the company attributed to delayed demand for cold-weather categories and a women's fall assortment that fell flat. Wall Street had been expecting growth of 1.5%, according to StreetAccount.

Dickson said the brand has since introduced new products and seen demand "rebound" as cooler weather has set in across the country.

"We've been encouraged by the early customer response, and we should see sequential improvement in the fourth quarter," he said. "There's some work to do here, but with peak selling still ahead, I'm confident that the team is focused on the right areas and [will] ultimately position Banana well for growth."

In an interview, Mudryk blamed the Ukrainian FA (UAF), claiming the substance entered his body while on international duty.

'This drug could only have got into my body where I was, and that's Ukraine,' he said.

But Shevchenko, president of the UAF, described the accusations as 'baseless slander'. Shevchenko added: 'His representatives repeatedly approached the UAF with a proposal to find an employee who would falsely take responsibility for the meldonium found in his doping sample,' he told Tribuna. 'Such a proposal contradicts the principles of our work.'

Shevchenko said investigations had failed to establish how the substance entered Mudryk's system. He added that national team staff complied with Chelsea's request not to give Mudryk medication, and that two of his personal specialists were allowed at training camps.

Mykhailo Mudryk (left) has been accused of 'baseless slander' by Ukraine FA (UAF) president Andriy Shevchenko after he blamed his failed drugs test on the national team's setup

Mudryk's representatives have been approached for comment.

'Loud statements have been made about the UAF and the Ukrainian national team. This is baseless slander against our people, who do their work honestly and professionally,' the UAF president, who also played for Chelsea during his time, said in a statement to The Athletic.

I trust them, because throughout all this time there have been no incidents in the team that could make anyone doubt their professionalism. If Mykhailo has evidence to back up his words, let him provide it. If no such evidence exists, I expect him to publicly retract his accusations and apologise to the people whose honesty and professionalism he has called into question.'

In his interview, Mudryk - who is yet to add to his 28 caps for his country since returning from his ban - laid the blame at Ukraine's door.

'I had never heard of a drug like meldonium, never seen it, never come across it in my life. I only learned about it when they told me the news. I definitely have ideas. It's a drug you can only get in Eastern Europe,' the winger said.

'This drug could only have got into my body where I was, and that's Ukraine. There are no such drugs in England. It supposedly stimulates your heart and helps you, as far as I understand, handle physical load better.

'You can't believe it's true. You can't believe this is happening to you. If I'd ever taken anything anywhere, I'd have understood that this conversation might happen one day.

SharpCloud’s POLI beta uses portfolio data to answer questions and model changes, but keeps approval with the people running the projects.

Lumivero has opened a beta of POLI, a set of AI agents built into its SharpCloud portfolio management software. The company introduced POLI on November 16 and says beta access began November 17 for customers and prospects. The agents can answer questions about projects and risks or explore the consequences of a proposed change. A person has to confirm a change before it is applied to the portfolio.

The design targets project portfolios, not investment trading. A company running several factory upgrades, for example, might use it to ask which work depends on a delayed delivery. The answer depends on connections the team has already recorded in SharpCloud. If the shared supplier or affected milestone is missing, the model cannot be relied on to know about it.

Teams typically keep schedules, risks and dependencies in separate systems. A project may appear on track in its own schedule even though a prerequisite is slipping elsewhere. SharpCloud gives teams a way to represent those relationships explicitly. In January, Lumivero announced its acquisition of SharpCloud, identifying portfolio visualization and the ability to model how one change might affect multiple projects as reasons for the deal.

POLI works on that connected portfolio model. Its five default agents are Getting Started, Comparison, What-If Impact, Story Summary and Product Knowledge. They cover different jobs, from helping a new user get oriented to comparing items or summarizing a portfolio view. Comparison can make the differences between projects easier to see. What-If Impact asks a different question: if a project assumption changes, what other parts of the modeled portfolio could be affected?

The distinction matters because dependencies are often more useful than a ranking. If a delayed equipment installation pushes back the training associated with it, the important result is not just that a milestone moved. It is which people need to adjust their plans and which assumptions they need to verify. Scenario analysis helps identify those questions without asking the system to decide what the business should do.

Lumivero says What-If Impact is read-only and does not change the data it evaluates. The company also says POLI respects existing user permissions, shows the basis for its answers and leaves proposed changes for people to approve. AI is opt-in per SharpCloud instance. These are vendor-described controls, not independent findings about how accurately the agents work on messy or incomplete data.

Human approval is most useful when an explanation can be checked against the original records. A plausible answer may use a valid but outdated estimate, or a dependency that no longer applies. A reviewer needs to see which records the assistant used and know who maintains them. An approval step provides a point of control, but it does not fix stale inputs or make every recommendation correct.

Lumivero’s own capital project decision-making report says few surveyed respondents would act on an AI recommendation without significant review. The survey comes from the vendor and does not verify POLI’s performance. It does, however, illustrate the adoption problem the product is meant to address: teams want help pulling together scattered information while retaining responsibility for any decision based on it.

Early users can test the agents on familiar examples where the project team already knows the outcome, then compare the answers with the underlying records and repeat the test with a different access level. That would show where POLI helps reduce the time spent cross-checking schedules, and where missing records still require manual investigation. The initial beta is best understood as a way to query and test a portfolio model, not a replacement for the people accountable for it. Lumivero lists separate analysis and controls modules on its roadmap for the first half of 2027; those modules are not part of the current beta.

Form & Function Consulting has launched Lever, an AI platform designed to give professional services firms a consistent view of their operations across disconnected systems. Announced on November 12, the product combines operational data with company-specific business definitions so leaders can ask questions about staffing, revenue forecasts and project profitability through conversational interfaces.

The interesting part is not another chatbot answering financial questions. It is the work required before those answers become useful. A sales system can record a promising deal while a delivery team sees insufficient capacity to fulfill it. An AI assistant needs more than access to both databases to explain what that combination means for the business.

According to the launch announcement, Form & Function starts with information from customer relationship management, professional services automation and finance systems. It also gathers documents and the knowledge employees use to interpret those records. Its Lev chatbot supports stakeholder interviews that capture how a firm actually operates.

That onboarding feeds a context layer describing how work is sold, staffed, delivered and measured. The goal is to connect questions that otherwise fall between departments: whether the sales pipeline supports a hiring decision, which engagements offer the best margins, or how a delayed investment would affect projected cash flow.

This distinction matters in services businesses because similarly named metrics can conceal different assumptions. Available capacity might exclude time spent selling, while a revenue forecast might depend on both a deal’s likelihood of closing and when the resulting work can be delivered. A plausible answer based on the wrong definition can be more misleading than an obvious error.

The company’s Lever product page demonstrates Lev answering questions, generating visual output and working inside Slack. It also shows access through other AI clients, including Claude and ChatGPT, using Model Context Protocol, or MCP. Rather than requiring every interface to reconstruct the firm’s business logic, the approach supplies a shared foundation for those interactions.

Tanium’s AgentSight launch gives security teams a view of AI tools installed on employee devices, while agent activity tracking and broader enforcement remain on the roadmap.

Tanium introduced AgentSight at its Converge conference in Las Vegas this week, giving security teams a way to inventory AI software running on employee devices. The company says the first capabilities are available now in the Tanium console. They identify local models, coding agents, AI extensions and Model Context Protocol (MCP) servers across Windows, macOS and Linux, then associate each item with a device and the person who installed it.

That launch is narrower than the long-term product Tanium described. The first release answers what is installed and where. The company is still developing parts of the system that would explain what an agent did, flag unusual behavior or apply a policy across many devices. For organizations choosing how to govern AI, the distinction matters: a complete inventory is valuable, but it does not establish that an agent’s actions are authorized.

An enterprise can govern a company-wide AI subscription and still have limited visibility into the assistants staff install on their own workstations. An approved coding tool might sit alongside several extensions, a local model and a connector to another service. A software inventory lets IT see which devices are running those components and gives security staff a starting point for tracking down who installed them. It also helps explain why approving one service does not automatically account for every tool that ends up in a workflow.

Tanium says its initial visibility is available in on-premises and air-gapped environments as well. That matters for teams whose devices cannot send telemetry to a cloud service. The company also says cost tracking in Tanium Asset shows where AI software is deployed and what it costs. An endpoint view is useful for these questions, but it is not a substitute for records from browser-based services, cloud systems or the applications an agent uses remotely.

In the launch announcement, Tanium described a Converge demonstration of a Shadow AI page in Atlas where security teams can see changes in the inventory and ask questions in plain language. The company described additional plans to rank findings, provide explanations, and tie agent activity to the originating session and prompt. The demonstration and roadmap should not be mistaken for proof that those features are generally available or that they cover every agent or service.

Tanium also plans to let security teams define policies centrally and detect violations before they turn on enforcement. That sequence matters operationally. A local tool that looks unusual in the inventory might be part of a legitimate engineering or accessibility workflow. Reviewing those cases with the people who own the affected systems can help organizations distinguish authorized exceptions from unmanaged software without disrupting routine work.

The voluntary NIST AI Risk Management Framework puts AI risk in the context of how systems are used, not simply what is installed. For an enterprise, that means linking a device inventory to the people, permissions and business processes behind the entries. It also means deciding what information should be collected and who can review it, especially if future activity records include prompts that may contain confidential work.

The near-term test for AgentSight is whether security teams can keep an accurate inventory as employees adopt and change tools. The more demanding test is whether businesses can connect that visibility to useful decisions: which tools are approved, where an exception is justified, and who should review behavior that falls outside policy. Tanium has shipped the first part of that work. The rest will depend on both the product roadmap and how companies put it to use.
