The Landing, Beloved Los Angeles Gallery with a Keen Eye for Pairing Historical and Contemporary Artists, Closes After 14 Years

📅 Nov 20, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

The Landing, a beloved Los Angeles gallery whose program was defined by its unique pairings of historical and contemporary artists, announced that it had closed. Its last exhibition was a one-night only solo for Maxwell Hendler staged on October 29.

On the gallery’s website, founder and owner Gerard O’Brien wrote, “the Landing Gallery at 5118 W Jefferson Blvd is now closed. We are no longer programming at this location. We are deeply grateful to the artists, collectors, collaborators, and community who have been fellow participants in our engagements. It has been a privilege to champion extraordinary artists, present ambitious exhibitions, and create a space for meaningful exchange and discovery.” Related Articles Two More Vienna Galleries Close, Citing 'No New Collectors' and 'Challenging' Financial Landscape Prairie, Chicago Gallery That Put Conceptualism Ahead of Commercialism, Closes After Nearly a Decade

In an Instagram post, Gerard O’Brien put it more simply, “End of an era … 22 years of Gallery spaces in Los Angeles, Reform Galley 2004-2019 … the Landing Gallery 2012 -2026 … onwards. thank you Los Angeles❣️”

O’Brien founded the Landing in 2012 as an off-shoot of his design-focused Reform Gallery. “I never really thought there was a big difference,” he said of the distinction between art and design. He chose the name for its original location, a spot within Reform: “he cleared out the landing atop a short staircase in his furniture gallery and dedicated it to shows of art, bringing in work by artists both contemporary and historical to display just above his design wares,” as a 2016 blog post by book publisher Phaidon reported.

In 2015, O’Brien decided that it was time for the Landing to grow, opening in a 2,800-square-foot gallery space on Jefferson Boulevard with a solo show for the late California artist JB Blunk.

Over the years, the Landing would become known for its pairings of historical and contemporary artists. At the time of its closing, its roster included four deceased artists (Jeremy Anderson, Richard Bowman, Leslie Kerr, and Fred Reichman) and five living contemporary artists (Yevgeniya Baras, Ryan Fenchel, Matt Phillips, Jonathan Ryan, and Amanda Valdez).

The gallery was also known for regularly staging off-kilter group exhibitions, with titles like “Rat Bastard Protective Association” (2016), “The Useful and the Decorative” (2017), “New Iconography: Artists Raising Children” (2021), and “Consensual Ennui (2023). The first of these, “3 Women” (2016) took its name from a 1977 Robert Altman film as a way “to create a cross-generational dialogue that explores the intersections of craft and fine art, influence and homage,” per a release. It featured the work of Lenore Tawney from the 1960s and ’70s paired with textile pieces by Tanya Aguiñiga and paintings by Loie Hollowell. The exhibition marked the first time Tawney’s work had been exhibited in LA in nearly 50 years.

In an Artforum review, Erin Kimmel wrote, “The Landing’s handsome, restrained exhibition showcased the exquisite detail of [Tawney’s] works, which reward prolonged scrutiny with seemingly boundless formal revelations.”

To mark its 10th anniversary at its Jefferson Boulevard space, the Landing mounted a two-part anniversary exhibition. The first part, which opened in November 2025, focused on contemporary art and design and featured the work of Ruth Asawa, Wallace Berman, Bruce Conner, Lyne Foulkes, and Doyle Lane. The second part, which opened this past April, featured gallery artists as well as others who had exhibited there, including Brenda Goodman, Sam Jablon, and Lukas Geronimas.

In a release for part one, O’Brien wrote, “10 Year brings together the feeling of the earliest days of the Landing Gallery when two years of exhibitions took place inside Reform Gallery on Melrose Avenue. Exhibiting art in a salon style in dialogue with decorative arts is how tL began.”

The Landing’s closure is the latest in an extended wave of closures that began in mid-2025. Los Angeles galleries have particularly been affected, with Murmurs, the Box, LA Louver, and Blum all shuttering, along with the LA outposts of Marian Goodman Gallery and Tanya Bonakdar.

In his statement on the gallery’s website, O’Brien added, “While our gallery doors have closed, our commitment to art and artists continues. Watch this space for news of future offsite exhibitions, activations, and special projects. Thank you for being part of the Landing’s ongoing narrative.”

Texans vs. Colts prediction: Houston looks to bounce back on 'TNF' vs. lowly Indy after rough Week 10 loss

📅 Nov 20, 2026  |  Category: Sports  |  Read Source →

If not listed, please contact your TV provider.

In the Week 11 edition of "Thursday Night Football," the Houston Texans play host to the division rival Indianapolis Colts.

Houston enters this game coming off a dispiriting loss to the Cleveland Browns that dropped the Texans to just 3-6 on the season and dealt a significant blow to their playoff hopes. The Texans need to start stacking wins, and they need to do it right now if they want to make a run at a back-end postseason spot.

The Colts, meanwhile, snapped a five-game losing streak with a close win over the Miami Dolphins last week. They didn't exactly light the world on fire, though, and have the look of a team that is just playing out the string after their series of trades ahead of the deadline.

Will the Texans bounce back, or will the Colts make it two in a row? We'll find out soon enough. Before we break down the matchup, here's a look at how you can watch the game.

The Texans defense has been one of the most disappointing units in the league this season. Houston ranks 20th in yards allowed, 18th in points allowed and 22nd in EPA per play, according to Tru Media. The Texans checked in first in yards, second in scoring and third in EPA per play a year ago, and were expected to again field one of the top defenses in the league this season after returning almost all of their key pieces.

Instead, Houston has struggled to contain opposing passing attacks, repeatedly leaking on the back end despite putting pressure on quarterbacks at an above-average rate. Players like Kamari Lassiter have regressed significantly, while Reed Blankenship has been a bust as a free-agent signing.

Luckily for the Texans, they are about to face one of the most inept passing offenses in the NFL. Daniel Jones has been entirely unable to drive the ball in intermediate and deep areas of the field since returning from his Achilles tear, and it has neutered the entire aerial attack. Jones has completed a career-low 59.6% of his passes at a career-low 5.6 yards per attempt, with just seven touchdowns against a league-high 12 interceptions.

The Colts also traded away both Josh Downs and Keenan Allen last week, leaving them with Laquon Treadwell, Ashton Dulin, Anthony Gould and Deion Burks as their main wide receivers. There is not an imposing player in the bunch. The Texans should be able to shut down this passing offense with relative ease -- especially if they can get pressure on Jones, who can no longer evade the rush like he did last season. He's already been sacked more times than he was a year ago despite playing three fewer games.

The Colts have turned the dial down on Jonathan Taylor's workload in recent weeks, having him split snaps and carries with rookie Seth McGowan, including in the red zone. Taylor played 81.2% of the offensive snaps through the Colts' first six games, according to Pro-Football Reference, but is at just 66.5% of the snaps since then, with McGowan's usage ticking up.

Taylor has also not been as effective this season (career-low 4.2 yards per carry) as in recent years, which isn't surprising given the total lack of a passing game and the Colts' offensive line issues. Those issues should persist on Thursday, with both of Indianapolis' starting tackles out for this game. It's fairly difficult to run on the Texans to begin with (4.0 yards per carry allowed), and it should be even more so with the Colts in their current state.

C.J. Stroud is playing the best ball of his career, including his rookie year. Stroud checks in sixth in the NFL in EPA per dropback, according to Tru Media, despite the fact that he's been working with an injured and rotating pass-catcher corps for much of the season.

The Colts are an easy bunch to throw on, checking in 27th in the league in net yards per attempt allowed (which accounts for sack yardage), and they won't have Charvarius Ward in this game and may not have Sauce Gardner, either. Nico Collins should see favorable matchups on the outside and be able to feast all night long. The likes of Tank Dell and Xavier Hutchinson could get in the mix as well, though they tend to rotate more heavily in and out of the lineup and therefore see fewer targets than Collins.

It's also quite easy to run on Indy, which has leaked 4.8 yards per carry on the season, 29th in the NFL. The Texans, though, have been entirely unable to run the ball, with neither David Montgomery (who is set to return from injury on Thursday night) nor Woody Marks (who is out with a concussion) able to establish anything resembling success on the ground. Montgomery was averaging a mere 2.7 yards per carry before his injury, and Marks has been at only 3.7 per carry in Monty's absence.

This is likely to be a game where the Texans lean heavily on Stroud to establish a lead, then turn to the ground game to run the clock out, in what should be a relatively easy matchup given the state of the Colts' roster. So long as the Texans keep Stroud well protected early on, that strategy should be plenty successful, even if it doesn't necessarily result in a blowout win because the Texans themselves aren't that explosive of an offense.

I just don't think the Texans should be favored by 12.5 points over anybody.

It's an honor to be the answer to a question on Jeopardy!, and I'd assume it's an even higher honor to be the answer for Final Jeopardy! So, with that in mind, it's always fun to see how people react to being the subject of a question featured on the show. Case in point: Ice-T had a very funny take when he found out that someone didn't know he was the answer to Final Jeopardy!.

Final Jeopardy! can literally make or break someone’s future on the long-running game show. The clues can range from history to literature to, yes, even pop culture. On the episode from the 2026 TV schedule that aired on October 28, Ice-T was the answer for the final round. Two people got it right, but one person was wrong. Ice-T addressed the moment while appearing on the Today Show and revealed just what he thinks of the whole ordeal:

I’m in a lot of crossword puzzles, because Ice-T, I-C-E-T, is a weird spelled thing. But Jeopardy, I’ve been in Jeopardy before, but Final Jeopardy. …. [They asked] who put out an album, Home Invasion, and also is the longest-running character in television. And the guy said Tim Allen. I think he was thinking about Home Improvement, right? But the two women got it right, and I only care about the women [laughs]. So, you know, he lost, and they won. But that’s how it goes. But Final Jeopardy!’s a big deal.

Of course, being a clue on Jeopardy! is a big deal, even if it does sting when someone does not know the answer. And as Ice-T pointed out, he’s been in plenty of clues over the years. That being said, it’s a lot different when you’re in Final Jeopardy!

Overall, I love that Ice-T wasn’t too hurt over being mistaken for Tim Allen, and instead focused on the fact that the two women got it right, which is all he cares about. I'm also very entertained by the image of Tim Allen trying to rap.

Anyway, the clue was for the category “Current TV Streaks,” and it just shows that even if a clue seems easy, it may not be for a lot of people. For host Ken Jennings, there was one category that made him nervous when he was competing, and it was surprisingly hockey. While he wound up being one of the biggest Jeopardy! winners of all time and no one has yet to beat his 74-game streak, it sounds like that still haunts him. So, everyone has their blind spots.

Meanwhile, that Final Jeopardy! clue made me realize something truly insane. Despite the fact that Law & Order: SVU is in its 28th season, it’s wild to me that Ice-T has been playing Odafin “Fin” Tutuola for almost three decades. He’s been on the NBC drama since the second season and is one of the longest-running actors in the Law & Order franchise. Luckily, he doesn’t have any plans to leave any time soon. While he has been worried that he’d be written out as the show has had to make room for Kellie Giddish, that has not been the case.

Hopefully, the next time Ice-T is an answer for Final Jeopardy! everyone gets it right. However, even if it’s just the women, I’m sure he will be just fine. In the meantime, fans can catch him on Law & Order: SVU on Thursday, December 3, at 9 p.m. ET on NBC, streaming the next day with a Peacock subscription.

According to NerdWallet, the program's most recent change took place on April 2, fine-tuning the mile-earning schedule for today's economy. Basic Economy officially no longer earns travelers miles, and it has reduced miles per dollar earned at each status level.

Premier 1K, United's top tier of earned status for passengers, grants a variety of benefits, amenities, and privileges onboard and in the airport. Premier 1K unlocks United's most comprehensive set of publicly available elite benefits, including priority pre-boarding, complimentary Premier Upgrades cleared as early as 96 hours before departure, three free checked bags, Economy Plus seating at booking for the member and up to eight companions, access to expanded award availability, a dedicated Premier phone line, and a bonus of 280 PlusPoints that can be used to request upgrades on eligible flights.

There are two main ways to achieve such status, including flying on United or United Express operated flights in a class other than Basic Economy, and with credit card spending with United's co-branded Chase credit card lineup. The exclusivity comes at a price, and not just anyone may qualify for Premier 1K, let alone United's entry level status, Premier Silver. For the 2026 qualification year, United Airlines illustrates on its website that earning Premier 1K status requires either 28,000 Premier Qualifying Points (PQP) or a combination of 22,000 PQP and 60 Premier Qualifying Flights (PQF).

PQPs are generally earned based on how much a traveler spends on United-operated flights, preferred seating, upgrades, and select partner airline tickets. The requirement makes Premier 1K one of the most expensive top-tier status levels among major US airlines.

A PQP measures the money you spend on airfare and eligible credit card purchases (typically earning 1 PQP per dollar spent on base fares). A PQF counts each individual flight segment you take on United, United Express, or eligible partner airlines. PQFs include layover segments. For instance, a round trip flight with a layover in each direction equals four PQFs earned.

The qualification requirements have steadily increased throughout the loyalty program's history. Following the merger with Continental Airlines and a broader airline industry trend toward revenue-based loyalty programs, United has increasingly prioritized spending over simply logging miles flown. Today, how much a passenger spends matters significantly more than how far they travel.

According to NerdWallet, one cannot simply earn Premier 1K status simply through spending on one of United's Chase cards. While a strong supplement in the pursuit of Premier 1K, it is not enough alone to suffice. United requires a four PQF minimum in order to qualify for any premier status, including the entry level Premier Silver.

While Premier 1K may traditionally be associated with road warriors flying hundreds of thousands of miles annually, United's current rules allow members to earn the vast majority of the required 28,000 PQP through credit card spending. The absolute maximum utilizing credit card spend requires 28,000 PQP, plus the four-flight minimum. Depending on the specific Chase card in use, one can earn 1 PQP per $20 spent or 1 PQP per $15 spent, translating to $560,000 or $420,000 in credit card spend annually.

In practical terms, a traveler pursuing status exclusively through credit card spending must supplement their spending with four PQF segments on United or an authorized partner airline. Business travelers whose employers pay for frequent premium cabin trips often reach the threshold naturally, whereas leisure travelers typically require a more deliberate strategy involving credit card spending and frequent travel.

This is certainly bad news for United MileagePlus members.

While MileagePlus has evolved significantly since its 1981 launch, the core philosophy remains centered around recognizing travelers who consistently choose United throughout the year. That philosophy is reflected in the program's modern qualification structure, where spending and engagement with the airline carry greater weight than mileage alone.

Million Miler status adds another layer of value for long-term United customers. Unlike annual status qualifications, Million Miler recognition rewards cumulative lifetime flying with the airline. However, becoming a Million Miler is solely based on miles flown on a United Aircraft, not credit card spend. Overall, travelers considering a long-term loyalty strategy may therefore view Premier 1K as only one component of a broader relationship with United, especially compared with travelers who simply leverage status matches between competing carriers.

At Delta, travelers earn Diamond Medallion status exclusively through Medallion Qualification Dollars (MQDs), with a threshold of $28,000 MQDs during the qualification year. Meanwhile, American Airlines awards its highest published tier, Executive Platinum, after earning 200,000 Loyalty Points, which can be accumulated through flights, co-branded credit card spending, shopping portals, hotel partners, and numerous non-flying activities. As a result, many loyalty experts consider American's program the easiest top-tier status to earn because it offers the greatest number of pathways that do not require extensive flying.

Applying for Spousal Social Security Benefits in 2027? 3 Things You Need to Know.

📅 Nov 20, 2026  |  Category: Business  |  Read Source →

Spousal benefits have some unique rules that are worth knowing before you sign up.

By Kailey Hagen, CFP® – Nov 19, 2026 at 7:00PM EST

Spousal Social Security checks tend to be a lot smaller than retirement benefits, averaging just $988 in October 2026 compared to $2,092 for retirement checks. But they can still make a meaningful difference to your household's budget in 2027.

Spousal benefits have a few rules that make them different from retirement benefits, and it's important to understand them if you plan to apply for these checks next year. Here are three of the biggest differences you should be aware of.

You must be at least 62 or older to claim a spousal Social Security benefit. But even at this age, you can’t sign up if your partner is not yet receiving the partner's own retirement benefit. If you're eligible for a retirement benefit on your own work record, you may claim this in the meantime. Otherwise, you have to wait for your partner to apply first.

It's important that you and your spouse are both on the same page about when each of you will apply so that you can maximize your household income. If you haven't already talked through your plans, now is a great time to do so before you try to apply for Social Security benefits in 2027.

Spousal benefits are also available to ex-spouses if you were married to a qualifying worker for at least 10 years before divorcing and you haven’t remarried. Your ex's remarriage will not affect your eligibility to claim benefits on the ex's work record.

Benefits for ex-spouses work almost exactly the same as benefits for current spouses, except you don't need to wait for your ex to sign up for benefits first. As long as you've been divorced for at least two years and are 62 or older, you can claim a spousal benefit on your ex's work record, provided it's worth more than your own retirement benefit.

Social Security retirement benefits continue to grow for every month you delay your application until you qualify for your maximum checks at age 70. But spousal benefits only grow until you reach your full retirement age (FRA). This is 67 if you were born in 1960 or later.

Make sure you sign up by this point at the latest, unless you can’t because your spouse hasn't applied yet. If you fail to sign up by your FRA, contact the Social Security Administration right away to get the process started and to see if you qualify for any retroactive benefits.

Most first-time buyers put down less than 20%. Their median down payment was 9% in the National Association of Realtors’ 2026 buyer survey.

Twenty percent is only the point where a conventional loan, one without government backing, stops requiring private mortgage insurance (PMI), which protects the lender if you stop paying.
