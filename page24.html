Middlesbrough have been without their key attacker for their last two Championship outings against Wrexham and Watford respectively. They are sat joint-top of the table along with Birmingham City and Stoke City after 15 fixtures as they eye promotion to the Premier League.

Lankshear, 21, joined Boro in the summer from Tottenham Hotspur and has since been prolific. He has made 16 appearances in all competitions this season and he has fired 15 goals already.

Boro’s decision to land the England youth international has worked out for all parties involved. He has significantly bolstered Hellberg’s attacking department and has a bright future ahead of him in the game.

Middlesbrough seriously lacked a proper number nine in the last campaign. They probably would have finished in the top two if they had Lankshear at their disposal.

He has adapted well to life with his current club and they will be delighted with his progress. He penned a long-term deal running until June 2031 when he arrived back in late July.

Middlesbrough need to get back on track against Bristol City. Their upcoming opponents have been in poor form and pressure is growing on their manager Michael Skubala.

Having Lankshear back will boost Boro’s chances of getting a victory. It will be interesting to see if they start him from the off or bring him off the bench.

Hellberg has other options such as David Strelec and Kyle Joseph. The latter was snapped up from Hull City in the summer but he has struggled to make an impact so far and hasn’t been able to find the net yet in the league.

Worse Than 2008? Peter Schiff Issues Terrifying Market Crash Warning

📅 2026-11-20  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Noted economist and longtime Bitcoin critic Peter Schiff has sounded the alarm about an upcoming financial apocalypse, warning that the crisis could be worse than the historic 2008 market crash.

On Nov. 20, Peter Schiff shared a series of troubling X posts warning about the potential for significant losses in the U.S. stock market, real estate, and cryptocurrencies amid escalating financial system stress.

The economist shared a video with a very alarming headline: "The Next Crash Will Be Worse Than 2008."

In one of his most recent posts on X, Schiff argued that the recent gold price downtrend might signal a grim outlook for other key asset classes.

"It's probably a bad sign that gold is falling this much. It probably means that the stock and real estate markets are going to fall even more in percentage terms," Schiff cautioned.

According to Peter Schiff, gold's recent price behavior could be signaling deflation. As a result, this might lead to a more severe financial crisis.

However, Peter Schiff is convinced that the Federal Reserve will step in to prevent this financial apocalypse.

"Gold is signaling deflation. But asset deflation would bust the government and the banking system, so the Fed will pivot to avoid it," the economist explained.

Simply put, Schiff assumes that the Federal Reserve will pivot and introduce monetary stimulus to prevent a financial meltdown.

In another startling prediction, the economist warned that the U.S. stock market might wipe out all its gains since President Donald Trump took office.

"There's a good chance that the U.S. stock market is about to give back all the gains since Trump took office," the economist predicted.

He also warned that there might be a recession. As a result, Trump will likely point to Democrats and their wins in the recent midterm elections as the reason for this recession.

Interestingly enough, Peter Schiff's bearish outlook also applies to Bitcoin (BTC), which has already suffered significant losses amid the overall market crash.

On Nov. 18, Peter Schiff shared several alarming market indicators. According to his data, the Nasdaq has dropped 12.25%. AI infrastructure company CoreWeave has plummeted 64%.

Bitcoin, on the other hand, has plunged 33.5% from its 2026 peak.

Additionally, the economist noted that the U.S. housing market index had dropped to 25, its lowest level since 2012. This shows that the economic issues go well beyond AI stocks.

"Still think this is just about AI?" he asked.

james turrell fills new york gallery with floating cubes and glowing walls of light

📅 Nov 20, 2026  |  Category: Travel  |  Read Source →

the legendary light artist brings floating cubes and luminous walls to almine rech's tribeca gallery in new york.

James Turrell has filled Almine Rech’s Tribeca gallery in New York with luminous forms that seem to extend from the walls and hover in midair. Titled Something Old, Something New, Something Borrowed, Something Blue, the exhibition brings together over fifteen works, and even includes new installations that revisit some of the artist’s earliest experiments with light and perception.

A rectangular opening glows violet against a darkened wall. Elsewhere, a luminous cube appears to float where two walls meet, with its sharp edges suggesting a solid object suspended in space. The room itself becomes difficult to read as the light alters viewers’ sense of depth and distance.

The show is on view through January 30th, 2027, and marks Turrell’s first presentation with Almine Rech in the United States. It arrives nearly four decades after their first collaboration in Paris, and returns to ideas that have occupied the artist since the 1960s.

Among the new works is the installation Shallow Space, part of a series in which James Turrell uses illuminated apertures to create unexpected depth within a wall. At first, the luminous surface can appear completely flat, like a field of color. As the viewer’s eyes adjust, the opening reveals an actual space beyond the wall, although its dimensions remain difficult to judge.

In Raemar II (2026), a glowing rectangular form casts violet light across the surrounding surfaces. Color spreads beyond its edges and gradually dissolves into the room. From a distance, the work resembles an illuminated painting, although its relationship to the wall is far more ambiguous.

The exhibition also introduces new pieces from the artist‘s Glassworks series, which uses precisely programmed LEDs behind etched glass. Colors change gradually across these luminous surfaces, and the apparent depth of each work shifts with them. Some passages of light appear dense and opaque, while others seem to recede far beyond the physical dimensions of the piece.

Another new installation, Afrum Violet (2026), returns to James Turrell’s Cross Corner projections. A sharply defined violet form occupies the intersection of two walls, where the geometry of the room helps create the illusion of a three-dimensional cube. The surrounding surfaces absorb a softer wash of purple, while the central form retains its remarkably precise edges.

Turrell began experimenting with these projections in 1966 at the former Mendota Hotel in Ocean Park, California, where he converted the building into a studio. By directing light onto the walls at specific angles, he discovered that a simple projection could appear to have weight and volume. A shape formed by light could become surprisingly convincing as a physical presence.

Six decades later, the strange effect remains. From one spot, the illuminated planes read as a solid cube. Move through the room, and the relationship between the shape and its architectural setting begins to change. The installation asks viewers to spend time with something as familiar as a corner and reconsider exactly what they are seeing.

Up to 100,000 young people from the EU would be allowed to travel, work and study in the UK per year under a new youth mobility scheme set to be agreed on Friday, the BBC understands.

The same number of young Britons would be allowed into Europe under the scheme, expected to be approved at the UK-EU summit in Brussels. Downing Street has not commented on the details, but said talks were "down to the wire" on Thursday.

The prime minister has said a "Channel action plan" on immigration could also be struck, while negotiators are seeking agreements on food trade rules and carbon emissions pricing.

Conservative leader Kemi Badenoch has accused Andy Burnham of "reopening the divisions" of the Brexit referendum.

A new youth mobility scheme would aim to make it easier for young people to work and travel both in the EU and UK.

The UK already has schemes in place with 13 non-EU countries including Australia, Canada and India.

In 2024, the EU proposed allowing 18 to 30 year-olds to work or study in the EU and UK for up to four years.

Labour, which was then in opposition, rejected the deal - arguing it amounted to reinstating freedom of movement.

Since coming to government, the party has suggested it would consider a more restricted scheme with a time limit, a cap on the numbers coming to the UK and a ban on participants from claiming benefits.

The UK's request to limit numbers has proved to be a sticking point in negotiation ahead of the summit, along with the EU's request that EU students should pay the same tuition fees at UK universities as domestic students.

Speaking to broadcasters on Thursday evening, Burnham was asked if there were any circumstances in which the UK would walk away from a deal on Friday.

"There would always be those circumstances because I'm in this to get the best deal for Britain, always," he said.

"But what's in it for us is the possibility of lower food prices and I can't turn my back on that, lower costs for business, cutting red tape, and a really big win potentially of more collaboration on immigration - a Channel action plan - which is a really big deal if we can land that tomorrow.

"So I hope people can understand why I have pushed this because it could be a really good deal for Britain."

Burnham previously said the UK and EU could link up their law enforcement efforts, including sharing more data and criminal records of foreign nationals.

The prime minister's spokesman has said if a deal were secured, the government would introduce legislation to Parliament next week to implement changes.

An agreement on trade could remove checks and paperwork faced by food companies exporting to the EU.

The government argues this could add Â£5bn to the economy and cut food prices.

However, it could mean the UK will have to follow certain EU rules on products, for example on gene-edited crops or labelling.

Burnham has also said the summit would allow the UK to raise concerns about the Made in Europe scheme, which is designed to protect industries from unfair Chinese competition.

Burnham has previously suggested he could commit Labour to building a closer long-term future relationship with Europe, including asking to rejoin the organisation as a full member - however, he has ruled out major changes before the next election.

The party's 2024 manifesto promised not to return to the EU, the single market, customs union or freedom of movement, which allows EU citizens to live and work elsewhere in the bloc without a visa.

Earlier this week, former deputy prime minister David Lammy urged the prime minister to go into the next general election promising to rejoin the EU.

However, the subject has divided his cabinet with one minister warning of the costs that would come with reversing Brexit.

Reform UK leader Nigel Farage, speaking to The Sun, external, said he does "not believe for a minute" the country would "ever vote to rejoin" the EU.

He added: "I think what Burnham is doing is playing a very cynical political game. He's trying to pick up votes from the Lib Dems, pick up votes from the Greens, and using it as a massive distraction from all the problems that he can't deal with."

Speaking to the BBC's Today programme on Thursday, Badenoch said she wanted to see easier trading with the EU, but "not trade at any cost".

She also said the new deals could see UK companies having to follow EU rules, even those that do not trade with the bloc.

Appearing on BBC Breakfast on Friday, Reform UK leader Nigel Farage described the agreement as a "very, very expensive and costly error" and said Burnham has no "democratic mandate to do any of this".

He added that the Labour party manifesto was clear that the UK would not go back towards the single market and asked: "Where is our democracy?".

Liberal Democrat leader Sir Ed Davey told BBC Newsnight that while he agreed with Burnham on "the harms of Brexit", he did not think the prime minister was "going to go anywhere near far enough".

"We need to go further and faster," he said, including returning to freedom of movement - which Sir Ed said would help tackle immigration and boost the UK economy.
