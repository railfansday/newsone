Alphabet Stock Just Hit a 1-Year Low. Why Is Google's Parent Borrowing So Much Money?

📅 Nov 21, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Alphabet has added more than $78 billion of long-term debt this year, and it isn't done.

By Jonathan Ponciano – Nov 21, 2026 at 6:00PM EST

Friday was an ugly day for tech stocks. The Nasdaq Composite fell 5.3% and finished the week down 10%. Nvidia (NVDA -14.01%) dropped 14% after Reuters reported that the SEC is looking into its accounting. And Alphabet (GOOGL -2.70%)(GOOG -2.59%) didn't escape. The stock was up nearly 4% early in the day and then reversed, closing at $265.29, down 2.7%. That's its lowest close since October 2025. Shares are now 34% below their record close of $402.62 on May 13, which was also their 52-week high, and 15% lower than where they started the year.

And perhaps most importantly for the stock and its sentiment right now: Alphabet has been focused on borrowing more money.

Alphabet raised about $10.9 billion in two bond sales this month. On Nov. 5, it closed a €6 billion sale of euro bonds (about $6.7 billion), split across seven series that mature between 2028 and 2045. Two weeks later, it priced C$6 billion of notes in Canada (about $4.2 billion). The longest of those matures in 2066 and pays 6% per year, meaning Alphabet will pay interest on it for 40 years.

The filings say the money is for "general corporate purposes, which may include the repayment of outstanding debt."

These deals cap a big year of borrowing. Alphabet's long-term debt was $125.1 billion at the end of September, up from $46.5 billion at the end of 2025, according to its latest quarterly report. And that doesn't count the November bonds.

It has sold stock, too. In June, Alphabet announced an $80 billion equity raise, including a $10 billion private sale to Berkshire Hathaway (BRKB -0.36%). Berkshire paid $351.81 for each Class A share. At Friday's close, that stake is down about 25%.

Alphabet spent $132 billion on property and equipment in the first nine months of the year, more than double the $63.6 billion it spent a year earlier. In June, the company said it expected to spend $180 billion to $190 billion for all of 2026, and that spending in 2027 would "significantly increase."

That spending has outrun the cash the business brings in. Alphabet's free cash flow, the cash left over after capital spending, was negative $9.1 billion in the third quarter and negative $5.9 billion in the second, according to its earnings release. As recently as the fourth quarter of 2025, it was positive $24.6 billion.

The business itself is still growing quickly. Third-quarter revenue rose 25% to $127.6 billion, and Google Cloud revenue more than doubled to $30.7 billion. That cloud growth is a big part of why Alphabet is building so fast.

Alphabet isn't in financial trouble. It ended September with $265.1 billion in cash and marketable securities, and its bonds are rated AA+ by S&P, just one step below the top rating. Borrowing at roughly 3.7% to 6% to build data centers can make sense if those data centers keep filling up.

The risk is what happens if they don't. Alphabet is spending as if demand for AI computing will keep climbing for years, and it's using borrowed money and new shares to do it. Investors are starting to question that kind of bet across the industry, which is a big reason the stocks are falling.

For long-term investors, the stock is far cheaper than it was in May. Two numbers to watch are free cash flow and Google Cloud growth. If cloud revenue keeps doubling, the borrowing will look smart. If it slows while the spending keeps rising, today's lower price may not be the bottom.

📅 Nov 21, 2026  |  Category: Business  |  Read Source →

The Pittsburgh Steelers are 6-3 and still well positioned to make the NFL playoffs because of a favorable schedule.

For that reason, the 38-18 loss to the Cincinnati Bengals on Nov. 15 was not a total disaster and could plausibly be written off as just a bad day against a red-hot offense led by leading MVP candidate Joe Burrow.

At least that might be true if the team's most highly paid players were producing at the levels they're paid to. They decidedly are not, and until they are, then the Steelers still profile as an upper middle class AFC team capable of reaching the postseason, but probably not winning once they get there.

Receiver DK Metcalf, who's in Year 2 of a five-year, $150 million deal, is the chief offender. Through nine games, he's on pace for the least productive season of his eight-year NFL career. It would break the lower threshold that he established last just year, his first in Pittsburgh, when he had 850 yards.

Pro Football Focus data faults him for six drops, a figure that feels generous given how frequently he fails to come down with catchable balls from quarterback Aaron Rodgers.

The contested catch numbers encapsulate the profound struggles better. PFF credits him with receptions on just 10 of 25 opportunities in 2026. That's a 40% contested catch rate, which is the worst in the NFL among receivers with at least that many opportunities. Only eight out of 38 receivers with at least 50 targets are worse overall.

Within that cohort, Metcalf ranks 33rd in yards per route run at 1.24; 32nd in yards after the catch with 125; and dead last in raw catch percentage when targeted at 50.7%.

By no metric has he been the reliable No. 1 option that he's being paid to be. Still, for $150 million, you expect a player to be able to transcend his circumstances more often than Metcalf has.

Linebacker/pass rusher T.J. Watt is also worthy of scrutiny. In two games against Cincinnati this season, the Steelers' $42 million man this season generated zero pressures on Burrow. If this franchise is going to win its first playoff game since 2016 come January, the guys making the most need to begin playing like it. Otherwise, another disappointment like Sunday night's could be the start of a long winter once again.

Why Delta Air Lines Quietly Added ETOPS Certification To Its Boeing 737-900ERs Even Though They Don't Fly Transatlantic & Pacific Legs

📅 Nov 21, 2026  |  Category: Business  |  Read Source →

In 2011, Delta Air Lines announced an order for 100 Boeing 737-900ERs, the largest variant of the 737 family. It later ordered another 20 units in 2015 and another 10 in 2017, bringing its total to 130, and the airline acquired another 33 second-hand aircraft after COVID. They first started arriving in 2013, and the large, efficient 737-900ER has become a workhorse for Delta's domestic network. The type, however, isn't known for exceptional range and flies only within North America. Yet, these aircraft are certified for ETOPS-180 operations.

ETOPS stands for Extended-range Twin-engine Operational Performance Standards (or alternatively, Engines Turn Or Passengers Swim), and ETOPS certification demonstrates that a twin-engine aircraft may safely fly more than 60 minutes from a diversion airfield. The number denotes how far the aircraft may fly from an airfield in minutes. Although the 737-900ER does not operate any transoceanic flights, Delta has chosen to maintain ETOPS certification on some of its 737-900ERs, but not all of them.

Modern twinjets are certified by regulators with an ETOPS type design approval, but this doesn't mean that a specific aircraft is ETOPS-certified. Planes need to be individually approved for ETOPS operations, as the regulator needs to verify that the aircraft features special equipment mandatory for overwater operations and that the airline has procedures in place that comply with ETOPS practices. An airline could even choose to have just one aircraft certified for ETOPS, or it could certify its entire fleet if desired.

The ETOPS certification process is expensive, and the extra equipment adds weight to the aircraft. Therefore, airlines generally only certify aircraft with ETOPS if the aircraft will regularly operate overwater flights to make the investment worth it. As an example, all of Delta's widebodies are ETOPS certified, but many of its short-haul aircraft are not. Notably, however, the 737-900ER lacks the range to operate transoceanic routes and is primarily used for domestic services.

But although the 737-900ER is not generally known for having long range, the aircraft is still capable of operating longer services. This includes transcontinental flights or services to the Caribbean.

While ETOPS is not generally necessary for Caribbean flights, it can permit more efficient routings in some cases, and the 737-900ER is still commonly deployed on the carrier's routes to this region. In addition, while the role of the 737-900ER has been changing as Delta receives a higher number of new narrowbody aircraft, the carrier formerly had a majority of its 737-900ERs ETOPS certified.

Today, Delta only maintains a small subfleet of roughly 20 737-900ERs with ETOPS certification, while the remaining are not certified for extended overwater operations. However, many of these planes formerly featured equipment for ETOPS operations and were maintained to ETOPS-180 standards. Delta later removed the extra equipment from many of its 737s and no longer holds them to ETOPS standards.

The 737-900ER was Delta's newest and most economical large narrowbody in the mid-2010s. With such a large order, the Atlanta-based carrier intended to deploy the 737-900ER throughout its network on some of its longest routes within North America. Some of these are Caribbean services that would require ETOPS certification for more efficient routings; hence, most of the 737-900ERs got ETOPS certification. In addition, Delta also originally intended to use the 737-900ER for some of its services to Hawaii from the West Coast, which necessitates ETOPS-180.

ETOPS equipment and maintaining ETOPS certification for an aircraft is expensive, so Delta has removed it from most 737s as the airline uses the 737 on fewer overwater routes today. Part of this is because the carrier now has a large Airbus A321neo fleet, which has been deployed on longer Caribbean routes due to its excellent fuel efficiency. The other reason is that the previously planned Hawaii services didn't work out, and Delta pulled the 737s from these operations.

Southwest Airlines has just 138 ETOPS-certified aircraft in its fleet of over 800 aircraft.

Until the arrival of the 737-900ER, Delta primarily relied on the far less efficient 757 for its routes from the West Coast to Hawaii. In 2016, the airline began scheduling the 737-900ER on flights between Seattle and Honolulu, while the 737 began flying to Kona shortly afterwards. However, the airline would end up pulling the 737-900ER off of these routes, instead replacing it with the 757 again, as these routes pushed the 737-900ER too far past its payload-range limitations.

The 737-900ER can and has operated longer routes than these Hawaii services, even for Delta itself. However, operating an ETOPS flight requires extra fuel reserves; in the case of the Hawaii routes, the aircraft needs to carry enough fuel to essentially turn around midway, as there are no diversion airports between the West Coast and Hawaii. The fuel reserves add significant weight, cutting into payload, and the 737-900ERs were frequently payload-restricted when flying to Hawaii, hurting revenue.

United Airlines also faced this same issue when operating the 737-900ER to Hawaii, but the Chicago-based carrier had a smaller 757 fleet than Delta, and most of its examples were configured with lie-flat business class seats. Delta, with its large subfleets of domestic-configured 757s, was able to switch the routes back to the 757s, which cost more to operate but also earned more revenue. Today, Delta mainly relies on the A321neo for its Hawaii routes, while United relies on the 737 MAX, neither of which faces these issues.

Delta Air Lines uses the A321neo, 737-900ER, and 757 for routes to the Caribbean and Hawaii. Because its network of overwater routes within North America is operated by several types, the airline maintains subfleets of planes with ETOPS certification and planes without. Only about half of the A321neos are certified for ETOPS operations, as well as select 737s and 757s. As a whole, just over 100 of Delta's 828 narrowbodies are certified for ETOPS, because this is all that the airline needs.

United Airlines, however, certifies all of its 640 737s and 61 757s for ETOPS operations. The 737s are the airline's primary aircraft for Caribbean and Hawaii routes, while the 757-200 is heavily used to Europe. The 757-300s are also still used on several of United's flights to Hawaii. Still, the carrier has significantly more ETOPS-certified aircraft than it likely needs; it even maintains ETOPS certification on its 12 737-900s, which mainly operate short domestic trips out of Houston. This is a costlier approach, but what it allows is operational flexibility.

If an ETOPS-certified 737 goes out of service, United can just swap it for another 737 without complicating the rest of its schedule. If one of Delta's ETOPS-certified 737s is taken out of service, the carrier has to swap it out for another ETOPS-certified 737, of which it has far fewer. Delta is maintaining separate subfleets of A321neos, 737s, and 757s, which is a less flexible arrangement regarding operational disruptions and spares. By certifying all of its 737s with ETOPS, United avoids this problem.

United doesn't maintain ETOPS certification on its A319-100, A320-200, or A321neo fleets, and so it essentially designates one aircraft type (the 737) for Caribbean and Hawaii runs, while the A320 family is restricted to the US mainland. The catch is that this approach is more expensive upfront.

The A350 has the highest ETOPS rating of 370, meaning it can fly up to six hours and 10 minutes from the nearest usable landing strip.

ETOPS certification is not generally required for flights from the US to the Caribbean. However, there are some airways southeast of Bermuda to the Eastern Caribbean that take an aircraft more than 60 minutes away from a diversion airfield, and if Bermuda is unavailable as a diversion point, then planes need to hug the Florida coastline. While ETOPS certification is not necessary for Caribbean flights, it does offer benefits through more direct flight paths, and it is necessary for Hawaii operations.

To actually be certified for ETOPS operations, an aircraft needs to be maintained according to a special ETOPS program. It needs to be equipped with additional communications and navigation equipment, and an ETOPS-qualified mechanic is required to complete a service check before the flight. The engines must be inspected by two separate technicians, and the aircraft's maintenance logs also denote it specifically as being ETOPS-certified.

ETOPS is meant to be a thorough process that ensures a twinjet can safely fly far away from a diversion point due to having only two engines. Because it's expensive, airlines generally only certify aircraft with ETOPS if the plane will routinely be used on overwater routes. Even if a route doesn't necessarily require it, ETOPS can offer better routings that save time and fuel. Today, 737s and A321s now routinely operate overwater routes to Hawaii or across oceans, and this would have been unimaginable 40 years ago.

Zlatan Ibrahimovic Named English Icon He Wished Played in Europe

📅 Nov 21, 2026  |  Category: Sports  |  Read Source →

Over the years, Zlatan Ibrahimovic earned himself a fearsome reputation. On the pitch, he was never afraid to clash with anyone – including teammates and managers.

In retirement, as a pundit, he's been just as sharp-tongued when appropriate. However, that doesn't mean he's afraid to dish out deserved praise. Indeed, the Swedish icon – who won league titles with Ajax, Inter Milan, Barcelona, AC Milan, and Paris Saint-Germain across his distinguished career – is known for occasionally recognising greatness.

During his playing days, he actually expressed disappointment that one 'fantastic' icon, who just so happened to be one of the best English footballers of all time, never left to play for a club in Europe.

Zlatan felt Ronaldo 'took all the glory' for himself.

The man in question is Steven Gerrard. The Liverpool legend will forever be remembered as one of the greatest midfielders in football history. Most famously, he won the Champions League with the Reds and also earned 114 caps with England.

While many European giants would have loved to have signed Gerrard, he remained committedto Liverpool during his prime years. Ibrahimovic lamented this, once claiming he wanted to see the Englishman play abroad.

In 2012, ahead of the Three Lions' clash with Sweden, with Gerrard set to win his 100th cap, Zlatan spoke about how much respect he had for the midfielder:

"He's a fantastic player, always someone who plays at a high level. He's a good fighter and has good skills also and is strong with the ball.

"He feels more like an international player than a normal English player. Whenever I play against Steven the coach always says, 'Be careful with that player because he can make the difference.'

"He is respected throughout Europe but I would like to see Steven in, of course Liverpool is a big club, but in a big international club. I would like to see that also for England because it is very exciting when someone goes abroad and shows who he is in another competition.

"For me a fantastic player can make a difference in every country, wherever he plays, and I'm pretty sure that Steven would do that also. Of course, he is playing in one of the best leagues in the world now but he's a fantastic player."

Ibrahimovic was also asked to clarify what he considers a 'typical English player' to be, the then-PSG forward replied:

"Someone with a big heart, always fighting, aggressive, who never gives up. For me Steven Gerrard has more international skills than a normal player. England are used to playing 4-4-2 and the big teams in the Premier League are playing 4-3-3 with a lot of foreign players. It is developing like every other competition and every other country. They are a fantastic team with fantastic players."

Sadly for Ibrahimovic, and many football fans, the Swede didn't get his wish. Gerrard would never play for a European club, spending the bulk of his career at Liverpool.

However, the Englishman would leave Anfield in 2015 to spend one season in the United States. Coincidentally, he joined LA Galaxy, the same MLS side Zlatan would also later play for. Their paths did not cross in America, though, with the striker joining Galaxy in 2018, two seasons after Gerrard retired.

Ibrahimovic also came to England for a while, playing for Manchester United from 2016 to 2018. During his time at Old Trafford, he did at least get to share the pitch with another English icon he 'liked more than anyone', Wayne Rooney.

Kicker Charlie Smyth has been elevated to the New Orleans Saints' playing roster and has signed a new deal with the NFL franchise.

Florida’s invasive pythons don’t always stay on the ground, and about 1 in 7 adult sightings were up in trees

📅 Nov 21, 2026  |  Category: Science  |  Read Source →

A USGS study found that invasive Burmese pythons in Florida sometimes climb trees, especially in winter, raising concerns for nesting birds.

Burmese pythons invading southern Florida are not primarily tree-dwellers, but some of them climb. Among 188 radio-tracked adults, about 14 percent, or roughly 1 in 7, were found in a tree or shrub at least once, according to the U.S. Geological Survey (USGS).

For 168 juveniles, the share was about 17 percent. Across more than 4,000 observations, however, fewer than 3 percent placed a python in a tree or shrub.

Both age groups were most likely to climb in December and January, the cooler months of southern Florida’s dry season.

Each observation came from locating a snake through radio telemetry, not from a chance sighting. Tracking ran from November 2017 to December 2024 in Big Cypress National Preserve.

Lisa McBride, a biologist at the USGS Fort Collins Science Center, is the study’s first author.

A public USGS data release lists 112 tracking events in trees or shrubs, involving 56 different pythons.

The first python was observed in the Everglades in 1979, but no more were seen until 1995. The species now breeds in the preserve and is spreading through south Florida.

Burmese pythons, one of Florida’s invasive reptiles, are notoriously difficult to find. By some estimates, the probability of detecting an individual python can be less than 5 percent.

Adults in trees were seen basking, resting, brooding eggs or gathering with other pythons. Juveniles rested, basked or moved between tree limbs.

Most adults found in trees during the peak climbing months were basking, a behavior that lets reptiles absorb heat from their surroundings.

The researchers suggest pythons may use trees and shrubs at that time of year to maintain a high body temperature.

In February 2022, one adult male basked atop a Brazilian peppertree about 16 feet (5 meters) above the ground. It then climbed to the crown of a cabbage palm, about 23 feet (7 meters) up.

A female brooded her eggs in a fallen, hollow cypress tree about 6.5 feet (2 meters) above the ground in May 2022.

A juvenile female rested on a pond apple tree about 5 feet (1.5 meters) up that same May.

A USGS video shows a juvenile female moving across the branches of a dahoon holly tree in the preserve.

The higher share among juveniles may reflect an ontogenetic shift, a change in habitat use as an animal grows, the researchers suggest.

The probability of finding a python in a tree generally fell as its weight rose. Smaller snakes may move through branches more easily than heavy adults.

Still, trees with strong branches, such as Brazilian peppertrees and live oaks, supported some of the largest pythons tracked.

Adults caught in Florida average 6 to 9 feet (1.8 to 2.7 meters), according to the Florida Fish and Wildlife Conservation Commission (FWC). The species can reach 19 feet (5.8 meters), however.

Native from India to lower China, the Malay Peninsula and some islands of the East Indies, Burmese pythons are semi-aquatic and excellent climbers.

The exact route of their introduction is unknown. Pythons likely escaped from a breeding facility destroyed during Hurricane Andrew, and pet pythons have likely been released in and around the Everglades.

The months when adult pythons were most likely to be in trees overlap with the nesting season of several wading-bird species. Earlier studies documented pythons preying on nests of great egrets and white ibises in the Everglades.

No python was seen preying on birds in trees during the tracking. The USGS cautioned that the overlap does not mean tree use is causing bird declines.

Rather, it identifies another behavior that could bring an invasive predator into contact with vulnerable animals, and an area for continued research.

More than 25 percent of captured pythons have birds in their stomachs.

Before 2000, mammals were encountered frequently during nocturnal road surveys in Everglades National Park. Road surveys from 2003 to 2011 then recorded a 99.3 percent drop in raccoon observations as pythons spread.

Opossum observations fell by 98.9 percent and bobcat observations by 87.5 percent. The same surveys failed to detect rabbits.

Some reports put the south Florida python population anywhere from 5,000 to 180,000 snakes, though the best estimates so far place it closer to 30,000.

Almost 28,000 wild Burmese pythons have been removed from Florida’s environment and reported to the FWC since 2000. The 2026 Florida Python Challenge alone removed 280, with 907 participants from 30 U.S. states, Canada and Vietnam.

The South Florida Water Management District also pays agents an hourly rate to hunt pythons. They earn $50 for each snake up to 4 feet (1.2 meters), $25 for each additional foot (0.3 meters) and $200 for each verified active nest.

Seasonal data on tree use “could improve the effectiveness of removal efforts by management agencies,” the authors wrote.

Knowing when pythons climb could also help scientists understand how the snakes interact with the Greater Everglades ecosystem.

The study was published in the journal Journal of Herpetology.

Directed by Luca Bercovici, "Ghoulies" was released in 1984. It came out mere months after the stone-cold '80s classic that is "Gremlins" ruled the box office. Now, nobody can say that "Ghoulies" was as successful or as well-liked as "Gremlins," but releasing a film about little chaotic creatures around that time was undoubtedly a recipe for success.

The movie follows a young man and his girlfriend as they move into his parents' old mansion, where his satanic father is buried. When the young man performs a ceremony described in one of his father's black magic books, he unwittingly unleashes small, demonic creatures. Hargitay plays Donna, one of the guests at the house who encounters these little monsters.

"Ghoulies" is often accused of being a "Gremlins" rip-off, but, in actuality, the movies were competing to see which could make it to theaters first. In a 2018 interview with Love-It-Loud, Bercovici explained how it all went down.

"We were in production at the same time. We were curious about them, and they were curious about us. They were so curious about us, in fact, that Warner Bros. briefly sued to stop us from using the name — they lost. Part of the reason that 'Gremlins' came out first, is that Charlie Band ran out of money halfway through shooting, so a few months went by as we all scrambled for the money, allowing 'Gremlins' to open first."

Released in January 1985 and produced for a budget of just a few hundred thousand dollars, "Ghoulies" did big business (as much as $35 million at the box office, by some accounts). That paved the way for a string of sequels, including 1987's "Ghoulies II," 1991's "Ghoulies III: Ghoulies Go to College," and 1994's "Ghoulies IV." Good or bad, it made no difference. These movies were very successful.

Mind you, it's not remotely uncommon for well-known actors to get their start in less-than-prestigious horror movies. Oscar-winner Leonardo DiCaprio made his feature film debut in the 1990s creature feature "Critters 3," while Jennifer Aniston, who later broke out on "Friends," similarly made an early career appearance in "Leprechaun." It happens all the time.

Interestingly enough, Hargitay auditioned many times for a lead role on "Friends," only to be denied. She was also fired from the 1995 "Power Rangers" movie. The early '90s were not terribly kind to Hargitay. But she eventually made her way to "SVU" and, it almost goes without saying, that worked out pretty darn well for her.

Six products and services Google discontinued in 2026

📅 Nov 21, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Here's a rundown of some Google tools that ended up in the graveyard this year.

Google is inherently data-driven, and that extends to its products and services as well. The search giant has a tendency to shut down services when it feels they're not serving its broader goals, and this has led to the discontinuation of products as diverse as Google Reader, Google+, Stadia, Google Glass, Google Hangouts and dozens of other utilities over the last 15 years. 2026 is no different, so let's take a look at everything Google discontinued this year.

Google Assistant has been phased out in favor of Gemini on mobile devices. Google initially targeted the end of 2025 for the transition, but pushed the deadline to September, 2026. This means Assistant is no longer available on mobile devices, Wear OS smartwatches, headphones and earbuds, or vehicles using Android Auto projected from a user's phone. It's still usable on older devices like Nest Audio, Nest Mini, the original Google Home, Google Home Max and cars with Google built-in.

Google Assistant debuted in 2016 (in the short-lived chat app Allo, of all things), and was integrated into much of the broader Android ecosystem, including mobile phones, tablets, smart home devices and vehicles. It provided a hands-free way to control smart home lights, play music, call contacts, and look up information on the web. Those tasks will now be handled by Gemini.

The Pixel Tablet was discontinued in September 2026; the link to its listing now takes you to the Google Store's home page, and Google removed tablets as a category from its site navigation. Google's tablet ambitions have felt half-hearted at times, but the Pixel Tablet sought to change that. The tablet debuted in 2023 for $499, and it came with an 11-inch 60Hz LCD panel, decent hardware and reliable battery life. Google sold the tablet for as little as $250 during the 2025 Black Friday sale.

Its key selling point was the included Charging Speaker Dock, which effectively turned the tablet into a Nest Hub Max. The dock contained a dedicated speaker and served as a Cast target, allowing users to cast music and podcasts to the device. With the discontinuation of the Pixel Tablet, Google no longer has a tablet of its own; there were leaks pointing to a successor with a 120Hz panel, but that never materialized. With Google now focusing on Googlebooks, it may be a while before we see another Pixel Tablet.

Google ended production of the Nest Mini and Nest Audio in June 2026, with both products now discontinued. The Nest Mini proved particularly popular after Google announced it in 2019 for just $50, and the smart speaker offered an affordable way to interact with Google Assistant and play music or control smart home devices.

Nest Audio debuted in 2020 for $99, serving as the successor to the original Google Home. It featured a fabric design and surprisingly solid audio quality via two drivers — a 75mm woofer and 19mm tweeter — with only the much larger and more expensive Home Max rivaling its sound quality in Google's speaker portfolio.

Even though the Nest Audio and Nest Mini are discontinued, Google will continue providing software updates for the foreseeable future. Google is now positioning the $99 Google Home Speaker as the go-to choice for anyone looking for a smart speaker. The Home Speaker has 360-degree sound, but it comes with a smaller 58mm driver, and features Gemini instead of Google Assistant.

As part of a broader rebranding initiative centered around Google Health, Google turned the Fitbit app into a unified health portal in May 2026. Alongside the change, Google also indicated that it was migrating Google Fit data over to Google Health.

Google is positioning Health as its go-to choice to monitor health and fitness data from existing Fitbit wearables, and newer devices like the Fitbit Air and Fitbit Edge use it from the get-go. Fitbit's Web APIs have also been wound down, so if you're using a Fitbit fitness band or smartwatch, you will need to install and use Google Health to monitor metrics. While Health has a lot of features — including an AI-assisted Health Coach — it lacks the social features that made Fitbit's app stand out.

In January 2026, Google confirmed that it was ending the service, noting that it didn't "provide helpful next steps," and that it would instead focus on "tools that give you more clear, actionable steps to protect your information online." While there are other monitoring services such as Have I Been Pwned, Google's Dark Web Report was notable because it was integrated into your Google account.

Gmailify was a feature that let you use Yahoo, AOL or Outlook email accounts with Gmail and access spam protection, search, automatic sorting and other services available within Google's email service. The feature debuted 10 years ago, but it was discontinued in Q1 2026; existing users will still be able to use it until January 2027, and then it will be wound down.

That said, Google still lets you access email from third-party accounts within Gmail for Android and iOS, and it uses the standard Internet Message Access Protocol (IMAP) protocol to fetch mails. The only issue is that you don't get some of the extras, like spam protection. In a similar move, Google ended the ability to fetch emails from third-party accounts using the Post Office Protocol (POP); you'll have to use the newer IMAP standard to sync your mails going forward.

In addition to these, Google discontinued several lesser-known initiatives in 2026. Google announced in July 2026 that the legacy Google Earth Pro desktop app would no longer be available to download after June 25, 2027. If you've used Google Earth Pro in the past and want to continue using it, all you need to do is download and install the app before June 2027, and it will continue to work even after the wind-down. Google is moving its focus to Google Earth on the web and Android/iOS, and while you can still view satellite imagery and Street View for free, it is introducing tiered subscription plans for access to additional data layers.

Also, Steam for Chromebooks, a beta program that allowed users to install Steam games on ChromeOS machines, ended in January 2026. Google shut down Pixel Studio, a dedicated app that launched with the Pixel 9 series and let you create AI-assisted imagery using text prompts, in March 2026. With image generation moving to Gemini, Google is directing users to its latest AI assistant instead. Google clearly likes building new tools and features, and while that means there's always something exciting on the horizon, the downside is that there's no guarantee the service you use today will be available a year or two down the line.
