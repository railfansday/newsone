Designers Say These 9 Unexpected Bed Styling Tricks Can Wake Up Your Whole Bedroom

📅 Nov 16, 2026  |  Category: Lifestyle  |  Read Source →

These pillow ideas make your bed feel intentional, collected, and joyful.

A standard pillow setup—two king pillows against the headboard, with two standard pillows in the front, and a square accent pillow in the center—is a snooze. Designers are treating the bed as an opportunity to showcase a bit of flair. Not to mention awaken the entire bedroom.

What kinds of changes are happening? Throw and accent pillows are now treated as if they are fashion accessories. We’re seeing everything from edgy studs to romantic ruffles in contrasting colors to graphic lines that hypnotize. In fact, mismatched pieces work great and can feel “intentional, collected, and completely joyful,” says designer Betsy Wentz.

Here, nine unconventional ways to play with your bed pillow arrangements.

If the rest of your bedroom is neutral, designers are saying it’s OK to go more bold with your bed pillows. In the above space, it’s impossible not to notice the hypnotizing hue of Yves Klein blue pillows on a striped duvet cover.

“There’s something about that particular blue,” says designer Kate Anlyan, founder of Yasu Home. “It’s so vivid and almost electric, but it also has a depth to it. I love how it cuts through all the other tones without feeling disconnected from the rest of the room.” The hue works as a counterpoint to the wood paneling in the background and provides a balance to the visual weight of the black headboard.

The interconnection between the contrasting stripes on the square pillows and the round velvet ball feels both fun and sophisticated, says designer Beth Diana Smith. The stripes feel especially striking, bringing a sense of energy and definition to the space, when set against the moody purple-brown Benjamin Moore's Cinnamon Slate wall color. To keep the rest of the bedding from competing, Smith tames the room with a muted light gray coverlet.

“Match up the headboard, throws, lamps and artwork with your throw pillows to balance out color throughout your room,” says designer Renée Gaddis. The encapsulated look creates instant cohesion. Starting with a bold hue, like a mustard yellow, will make your job easier because it has personality from the start.

Faux fur is a classic way to add rich texture, but you don’t need to reserve it for a château. Interior designer Mialani Collard uses a neutral linen palette and tactile embellishments that sandwich the fluff to make it less prominent, allowing the bed to work even in tropical climates like Florida.

When you want a home with casual living as the main objective, fill surfaces in a way that doesn't look too pristine or orderly. Interior designer Tyson Ness of Studio Ness mixed different patterns and colors for the bedroom pillows so there’s no “right way” to make the bed after it’s been used.

“The different combinations create a slightly different look each time, keeping the overall feeling light, airy, and totally casual,” Ness adds.

Choosing pillows that match the bedding is easy, but Cate Gutter, owner and principal designer of CWG Design, prefers to add a bit of je ne sais quoi when she styles the bed.

“This bedroom is tucked away in a mountain home here in North Carolina, and we wanted it to have a playful, nostalgic feel reminiscent of summer camp,” Gutter says. “We carried that inspiration into the pillow designs, giving them an almost handmade, craft-like quality that feels whimsical.”

The oversized pillows are made in Imogen Heath's Arty Check, a cheerful yellow check finished with a fuzzy green fringe. In front of it, a smaller bolster is a playful take on a traditional shape, but the muted, multicolored stripe with a contrasting ruffled flange bolsters extra personality. The added texture breaks up the monotony of solid linens.

“Studs are an unexpected detail on bed pillows because they’re so versatile,” says Sean Carter, senior interior designer at Sapphire Pear. “They look just as great on a neutral fabric as they do with bold colors, prints, and patterns.” The metallic element adds a little edge without overpowering the overall design.

Wentz is no stranger when it comes to combining patterns and layering different scales in the same color family. Look for prints with their own personality that won’t compete with each other.

“The smaller confetti and stick stripe pillows give your eye a place to rest against that big graphic headboard, so they feel like friends, not strangers,” Wentz says.

Pillows gone wild can still balance restraint. Creating serious contrast—in pattern direction—will step up your bedscaping game.

“The geometric patterns in the pillows complement the strong linear elements,” says interior designer Nicole Johnson. “The vertical wood slats draw the eye upward, while the horizontal lines of the bed and furnishings provide balance.”

Linear patterns in the pillows at a smaller scale create a picture-in-picture effect that won’t overwhelm the overall design.

Remembering a Random Packers Game: The Minnesota Vikings

The last time I wrote about a random game between the Green Bay Packers and Minnesota Vikings, I covered a 1999 matchup the Packers had won. The 2026 Packers proceeded to lose in spectacular fashion to open the season, and I think it's because I jinxed them. Following that same logic, I'm using my voodoo magic to conjure up a win for Week 10. This week's random Packers vs. Vikings game is the 90th meeting between the two teams, when the Packers lost 20-17 on November 21st, 2005.

The 2005 Packers were…pretty bad! They lost WR Javon Walker to a torn ACL in Week 1, and both RBs Ahman Green and Najeh Davenport were placed on IR with leg injuries. With a 2-7 record coming into their matchup against the 4-5 Vikings, the Packers had turned the ball over 20 times already. This was a far cry from the team who had won the NFC North at 10-6 the previous year while having two WRs with 1,200+ yards (Javon Walker and Donald Driver) AND a 1,100 yard rusher (Ahman Green). The team had already lost to Minnesota by 3 points in Week 7—it was time to right the ship.

On the Vikings' side, they had recently lost starting QB Daunte Culpepper to a catastrophic knee injury. Taking his place was veteran Brad Johnson, who had the Vikings on a bit of a hot streak after two straight wins. Losing Culpepper certainly wasn't ideal, but the Vikings were finding ways to win.

After a missed 49-yard field goal attempt by the Vikings on their opening drive, QB Brett Favre and the Packers offense responded by going 3 and out. The Vikings offense proceeded to do exactly the same thing. The first score of the game came on the Packers' next drive after a couple of big plays, including a 30 yard pass to RB Samkon Gado, and then a 15 yard pass to WR Donald Driver in the endzone.

The Vikings' next drive ended with an interception by S Nick Collins. Things were looking up for the Packers! Just kidding. Both offenses proceeded to combine for 6 straight punts. Favre then decided enough was enough—someone needed to score! So he threw a 51 yard pick-six to CB Dovonte Edwards.

With about 30 seconds left in the 2nd quarter, the gunslinger rattled off a 53-yard yard pass to Donald Driver, and the Packers found themselves with a 14-7 lead at halftime.

After more unsavory play by both offenses, the Vikings tied it up 14-14 in the 3rd quarter after RB Ciatrick Fason rushed for a 1 yard TD, capping off a nearly 9 minute drive. This would be the final touchdown scored by either team. The teams proceeded to trade punts and field goals, and with 3 seconds left, Vikings K Paul Edinger nailed the 27 yard dagger. Packers lose, 20-17.

Brett Favre finished the day 20 of 33 for 227 yards, 2 TDs, and 2 INTs. Tony Fisher led the team on the ground, finishing with a whopping 14 yards on 4 carries, while Donald Driver finished with 5 catches on 9 targets for 84 yards and 2 TDs.

Forget Salmon: This Fast-Cooking Fish Has Fewer Calories

📅 Nov 11, 2026  |  Category: Lifestyle  |  Read Source →

Salmon's rich intensity also means it generally has more calories, which can mean different things based on someone's dietary needs. If you're conscious of this and still want something that cooks up quickly (and deliciously), try flounder, a leaner, lower-calorie option that never lacks flavor.

Exact calorie amounts can vary quite a bit, especially when it comes to wild versus farmed salmon, but even the leanest ones usually sit between 130 and 200 calories per three-ounce serving. Flounder populations, however, have a more uniform lifestyle and consistently clock in at around 100 calories per three-ounce serving. That brown or gray layer of fat may help a salmon migrate and survive cold waters, but flounder are sedentary ambush predators that don't have nearly the same energy requirements. On your plate, this means a fillet of flounder is lean, clean-tasting, and quite mild compared to a richly marbled piece of salmon.

Because flounder is often thinner and far less fatty than salmon, that also means it cooks exponentially quicker. While a skin-on fillet can widen your margin of error, it's still much easier to render your tender fish into a dense, rubbery mess. Your best bet is to treat it like a lean steak and use a hot and fast cooking method, letting a bit of carryover cooking finish the center rather than trying to reach the perfect temperature in a pan or on the grill.

Flounder is delicate, light, and can easily lose its natural flavor if seasoned too heavily. While it's not exactly a beginner-friendly fish, the best ways to cook it are often straightforward and focus on timing and temperature, not technique. Hands-off methods that require little manipulation or rely on a sauce for flavor are usually ideal and keep your meat moist and tender.

If you haven't tried making a meunière yet, it's a classic seafood dish you should try at least once. Made by combining browned butter, lemon, and parsley, it's just flavorful enough to elevate your flounder without overwhelming it. Just about any French butter sauce, from beurre blanc to béchamel, is great with flounder because it adds a bit of richness and is a great vehicle for other tasty seasonings. Plus, they're flavorful enough that you'll only have to pan-sear each side of your fish, reducing the chance that it'll fall apart or overcook.

Flounder grows more delicate the more it's cooked, so preparation methods that rely on you only handling it when raw are also quite ideal. Baking is a precise method, and you only have to move the fish once to transfer it to a plate. On the other hand, flounder is fantastic when deep-fried, and the breading helps it hold its shape. You'll still want to use a large, slotted tool and a delicate hand when fishing it out, but all you need to do is batter and drop it into oil for a great result.

In a release shared by Tod’s Group, which owns Tod’s, Hogan and Roger Vivier, the company said that the decision in line with their decision to “embark on a strategic direction aimed at more decisively communicating the brand’s values, the distinctive character of its codes, and the artisanal quality of its products, as well as the Italian lifestyle”.

One of Star Trek's Best Characters Almost Didn't Exist Because of Gene Roddenberry

📅 Nov 09, 2026  |  Category: Lifestyle  |  Read Source →

Even though hundreds of notable actors have graced Star Trek with their presence, only one cast member can claim the distinguished title of "most prolific Trek contributor." With 288 onscreen appearances to his name, Michael Dorn's franchise résumé eclipses even his equally legendary Star Trek: The Next Generation co-stars and their Star Trek: The Original Series predecessors. One exquisite Season 3 episode heralds a crucial turning point for both Lieutenant Worf's evolution and Trek's approach to the Klingons — even though Dorn's record-setting, career-defining portrayal faced behind-the-scenes interference from the start.

Despite Worf's immediate intrigue as the first main Klingon character and the lone Klingon to join Starfleet, he spends Season 1 as an isolated background figure — a symbol of the Federation's peaceful alliance with their former antagonists. Once Worf replaces Tasha Yar (Denise Crosby) as the Enterprise's chief of security, he assumes a pivotal role and demonstrates perhaps the most substantial growth out of the entire ensemble. Fond of prune juice, Klingon opera, romantic poetry, and cosplaying American Westerns, Worf survives The Next Generation's seven-season run and its quartet of feature films, joins Star Trek: Deep Space Nine, and reunites with his original crew in Star Trek: Picard's final season as an elite Starfleet Intelligence agent — complete with white hair and a new propensity for witty retorts, meditation, and imbibing tea.

Yet Star Trek's architect, Gene Roddenberry, almost prevented this austere, enthralling, and multifaceted fan favorite from walking onto the Enterprise bridge. According to interviews collected by Mark A. Altman and Edward Gross for their book The 50-Year Mission: The Next 25 Years, Roddenberry wasn't interested in developing the Klingons beyond their restrictive origins as recurring foils whose aggressive nature employs racially-coded stereotypes. This sentiment led Roddenberry, in the words of creative consultant David Gerrold, to "adamantly" resist "[my suggestion of a Klingon first officer]. Gene vetoed it for four months." Even after Roddenberry conceded, executive producer Rick Berman revealed that "Michael Dorn wasn’t even guaranteed all episodes in the first season."

Six decades after The Original Series premiered, the Klingons are regarded as one of the canon's most prominent, recognizable, and sublimely realized alien species. Before The Next Generation Season 3 — the first installment to hit its creative stride — the spin-off had already opened the door for a more sensitive, if still imperfect, depiction. The character beats and plot threads established in the third season's seventeenth episode, "Sins of the Father," written by Ronald D. Moore and W. Reed Moran from a story by Drew Deighan, reverberate for years to come.

Moore displayed an early aptitude for crafting engrossing Klingon-oriented stories. Moore's collective influence and his collaboration with Moran on "Sins of the Father" expands the Klingon Empire into a culture thrumming with vibrant life. Their spiritual practices, feudalistic government, home planet (seen for the first time in "Sins of the Father"), language, and the immense value they place on battle prowess and warrior honor code surpass archetypal words.

Can a new live-action series maintain fans' passion for Star Trek, or will it be another disappointment?

Likewise, "Sins of the Father" grants Worf's underutilized potential a narrative purpose beyond the metaphorical. His upbringing as an orphan separated from his civilization and adopted by doting human parents exacerbates his conflicted duality. Worf strives to prove his worth by honoring his biological heritage and fulfilling his duty to Starfleet, and refuses to accept that he can't unify that contradictory schism. His Enterprise colleagues gradually accept him, while the Qo'noS-born Klingons dismiss him as a weakling who willingly betrayed his birthright.

In "Sins of the Father," Kurn (Tony Todd), the younger brother Worf believes had perished alongside their parents during the infamous Khitomar Massacre, visits the Enterprise to disclose their connection and employ his sibling's aid. Duras (Patrick Massett), the leader of a rival house and a prominent member of the Klingon High Council, has accused the brothers' late father, Mogh, of forming a treasonous alliance with the Romulans. What befalls an ancestor taints their entire bloodline, so Worf vows to prove his father's innocence. If his claim fails, then the sons of Mogh will both die by execution.

Assisted in person by an unwavering Captain Picard (Patrick Stewart) and from afar by the loyal, evidence-scouring Enterprise crew, Worf uncovers a nefarious conspiracy. Duras is framing Mogh to deflect suspicion from his own father, the real traitor, and Chancellor K’mpec (Charles Cooper), the High Council’s leader, has advanced the lie to avoid sparking a civil war. Worf's challenge was doomed long before he issued it.

Worf can’t fathom betraying his familial responsibility, let alone a major legal body condoning such injustice. Nevertheless, he understands the preventative reason behind their cover-up and accepts discommendation (a non-lethal ceremony where individuals endure public dishonor) as long as the Council spares Kurn's life. As Worf's fellow Klingons declare him a coward and turn their backs, leaving him abandoned and shunned, the outlier who studied his culture from afar models a far deeper comprehension of honor, sacrifice, integrity, and courage than the rulers who spout such values but fail to walk the talk.

Between the meaty material and Les Landau's direction, Dorn's magnetism, gravitas, and pathos enjoy their richest opportunity to shine to date. Roddenberry gave Dorn leeway to develop Worf as he saw fit, and the incisive actor-character synergy that elevates Worf into a series-stealing standout is on full display through Worf's dignified resolve, his steely determination, the strength of his principles, and Dorn's extensive range. Since Worf tends to be a man of few words, the ones he does utter carry lethally significant weight.

Meanwhile, his wordless micro-expressions carry enough gravity to sustain multiple planets. Dorn carries Worf through an array of demandingly complex emotions: repressing his fury over Kurn's needling comments, grappling with the appearance of his lone living relative, solemnly yet proudly shouldering the weight of his parents' legacy, his profound gratitude and admiration for Picard, and reconciling with how the Empire's corrupt leadership has shattered the idealized vision of his people Worf carries.

In Moore's 2021 interview with the Hollywood Reporter, he shared: "[Gene] didn’t really see Worf as a primary character. [...] And this was going to be the series' 100th episode on top of it. So, we had to fight somewhat to get the episode going." In true warrior fashion, the team's commitment ensured Worf's victory against every obstacle in his path. "Sins of the Father" guides Worf toward his endgame – an assured and complete identity — as an ethical soldier, a diplomatic ambassador, a father, a devout friend, and a widower. A defining moment in Star Trek history, it's a focus for Worf and Dorn that was long overdue.

While some people appreciate the natural look of a shadowy under-eye, many of us hope to find a solution—which is where the best eye cream for dark circles comes in. “Not all eye creams are created equal, because not all eye problems are equal,” says board-certified dermatologist Dr. Shereene Idriss. “When a person is looking to treat dark circles, the question is: Why do you have dark circles?”

The answer is different for everyone. For some people, it’s pigment, which can be genetic or the result of tiredness. For others, the skin under the eyes is so thin that the blood vessels underneath show through as a bluish or purple cast. If you’re experiencing puffiness, that can cast its own shadow, especially in the morning. Finally, as we get older, a little volume loss where the lower lid meets the cheek can make the whole area look darker than it really is.

That’s why when it comes to your eye cream, the ingredient list matters. Vitamin C, niacinamide and licorice root are good picks for pigment. Formulas that include retinol and peptides help thin skin look firmer and less see-through over time, and caffeine is every skin expert’s go-to for puffiness. Hollowing is the one thing a cream can’t fix, but the right formula can still make the skin around it look smoother and brighter.

Do eye creams actually get rid of dark circles?

Though eye creams can soften the appearance of dark circles, they’re (alas) not a one-swipe magic wand. “Eye creams can improve the quality of the skin around your eyes, but they can’t fix every undereye concern,” says Brauer. “Eye creams mainly help to moisturize and protect the skin around your eyes—one of the most delicate areas of skin on your face.
