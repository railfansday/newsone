CDT Southern Terminus Closure Lifted, Border Monument To Be Relocated

📅 Nov 13, 2026  |  Category: Other  |  Read Source →

The Continental Divide Trail’s southern terminus and southernmost mile have reopened following a temporary construction closure, according to the Continental Divide Trail Coalition (CDTC). This section of the trail had been closed in late October so that construction crews could widen an access road and construct a second border barrier at the terminus.

The first mile of the CDT falls within the Fort Huachuca New Mexico National Defense Area (NDA), a military zone created in spring 2025 when roughly 110,000 acres along the US-Mexico border were transferred from the Bureau of Land Management into Army control. Hikers seeking to access the trail’s southern terminus must submit to a background check and obtain an Army permit. Noncitizens must also have a US citizen escort through the NDA who also has an access permit.

“These events are unprecedented in the history of our trail system: the transfer of a critically significant element of our national scenic trail into the Department of Defense control, the conversion of the CDT Southern Terminus into a military and construction zone of momentous scale, and trail management partnerships that include land management agencies and state governments, but also the US military,” the CDTC said in a press release.

The CDTC received word today from US Army officials at Fort Huachuca that the trail is once again officially open, although construction activities are ongoing and hikers may still encounter traffic, road closures, and delays in the area. “This closure came at a critical time for southbound hikers,” the organization said. “Completing the 3,100-mile trail is a monumental achievement, and the construction activities at the border created dynamic changes to the permitted NDA access in place during the past year.”

The press release also indicates that the construction of the second border wall means the southern terminus monument will need to be relocated to an area outside of the border walls, and that access to the monument may be temporarily prohibited during construction and relocation. “CDTC does not know the exact timing of the relocation but will provide updates as available,” the organization wrote.

What 12 12 12's Title Really Means

📅 Nov 13, 2026  |  Category: Other  |  Read Source →

Apple TV's newest crime thriller is 12 12 12, so what exactly does that unusual title mean? Anthony Mackie and Jamie Dornan star in 12 12 12, an Apple TV heist series that premiered on November 13, 2026. Created by Jim Keeble and Dudi Appleton, the series currently has a 64% on Rotten Tomatoes.

12 12 12 follows Jack Strickland (Mackie), an FBI agent on the tail of a famed thief and the possible murderer of Jack's former partner, Paul Galvin (Dornan). After a Newark, NJ heist went sideways and Jack's agent friend was killed, his despair hardened into resolve and then into obsession.

Now, he is doing everything he can to catch Paul in the act. For his part, Paul hasn't slowed down, and he now has his eyes on 800,000 Swiss francs that a Russian criminal organization has recently placed into a vault in Zurich. As we watch the cat-and-mouse game, it becomes clear that Paul and Jack may be more alike than it appears.

It's a fairly standard setup for a heist story, with a few curveballs thrown in here and there. The biggest of these curveballs is the structure, which fans of a show like 24 will quickly understand. It's this conceit that sets the show apart from other ones in the genre, and it also helps explain the title.

At the top of each episode of 12 12 12, the audience is reminded of something allegedly in the FBI's Principles of Crime. First, a high-asset robbery, like the one Paul has done in Zurich, takes 12 months to plan. The actual execution takes 12 hours. Then, the police have 12 days to solve it before the thieves can erase their tracks.

The 12 12 12 of the title then comes from those principles. Not only are we reminded of these at the start of the episode, but the show is also structured so that we are jumping through each of these frameworks, sometimes viewing an event in the future before we see its build-up in the past.

The 12-month timeline is titled onscreen as "The Planning", the 12-hour one is "The Heist", and the 12-day one is "The Takedown". As we move through each of these different sections, more details are revealed to us that change the way we think about the future events we may have already seen.

The first two episodes of 12 12 12 premiered on November 13, 2026, and the series will release new episodes weekly until December 25, 2026, for a total of 8 episodes. At the moment, there are no plans for 12 12 12 season 2, so it's unclear what kind of ending viewers should expect.

Warhammer Survivors Puts Your Brain In A Mindless Blender Of Non-Stop Action

My quest to play every genre of Warhammer 40,000 video game has led me now to Warhammer Survivors. Since I’ve fallen and I can’t get out of the Warhammer brain-rot swamp, this grimdark palette swap of Luca Galante’s make the brain go brrr-like Vampire Survivors should be extremely my shit. But not only does playing it makes me feel like something is being extracted from me, be it time, mental energy, or life force, but the game is also just…not my scene.

I’ve never played Vampire Survivors or its roguelike deckbuilder spinoff, Vampire Crawlers, but I watched my husband surrender his life to the game last year so I have a basic understanding of how it works. Pick a character, kill an ever-growing, nigh-endless horde of enemies, get upgrades to increase your power, rinse and repeat.

What surprised and impressed me the most about WS is, for however much the game’s description makes it seem mindless, there’s some strategy to be had. How you move around structures, knowing when to deploy items like grenades or flamethrowers, and which upgrades to prioritize during a run, all required more brain power than I expected the game would demand. There’s an item called vuln oil which randomly spits out gobs of black sludge at regular intervals. Because it doesn’t proc as often nor do as much damage as my chainsword or bolter, I thought it was worthless and ignored it my first few runs. After being forced into the item from a treasure chest and seeing how it worked after a few upgrades, I’ve now decided it’s a must-have.

I’m not fond of the obvious dark pattern-ification inherent in the game, the way it uses psychological tactics to keep you playing. Getting a treasure chest in Warhammer Survivors and seeing coins spit out of it slot-machine style while jubilant music plays is extremely off-putting. I know why it’s designed like that. I know the emotion it’s trying to evoke. Yuck. Playing Warhammer Survivors also makes me feel like I’m actively losing my mind. Not going crazy, but like my brain is atrophying in real time. It involves a ton of focus, which I generally enjoy in a game, but it’s a passive kind of focus. Like with the awareness you need to drive a car, your brain is on but it’s not really engaged in any kind of problem solving.

But honestly, I can forgive all that. I’m not immune to dark pattern exploitation and want to spend a little more time with it to unlock the Sister of Battle character. I think my biggest, pettiest complaint about Warhammer Survivors is that…it’s just not the flavor of Warhammer I want. Firstly, your starting character is an Ultramarine and I’m sick to the back teeth of fucking Ultramarines in Warhammer video games. Space Marine, Space Marine 2, both Boltgun games and now Warhammer Survivors. Ultramarines are to Warhammer what the Yankees are to Major League Baseball. Yes they put up a bunch of Ws; they have the biggest, loudest fanbase; and they once counted among their number some legendary guys. But there are far more interesting factions out there worth paying attention to.

Secondly, and I really must stress this is a petty complaint, where’s the love for the Horus Heresy, man? At the beginning of the game you can choose to play in the 40,000 universe or in the sword-and-board variant, Age of Sigmar. I would have loved to get one extra option to play in the Horus Heresy universe. Walk with me here. You’re a little Salamander space marine, trudging around Isstvan V fighting off wave after wave of Death Guards, Emperor’s Children, World Eaters, and more. As you get stronger, you progress from regular space marines to space marines in terminator armor, then chaos corrupted space marines, then a first captain, then Horus himself. Do you know how hard that would go?? I was ready to scrap this entire blog because I thought I had unlocked my absolute favorite character from the Horus Heresy, the goodest boy Tarik Torgaddon, but when I realized his name was actually Tor Garadon I was crushed.

I know as a Horus Heresy girlie I’m spoiled for choice when it comes to books, animated series, and audio dramas. I know. But sometimes a girl needs a video game that will let her kill her mortal enemy, Horus Lupercal. Warhammer Survivors, then, isn’t the one she’s looking for.

Before or After the Snow? Here’s The Right Time to Salt Your Driveway This Winter

📅 Nov 13, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Salt your driveway like a pro this winter with these expert-backed tips.

The Centers for Disease Control report a 35 percent increase in outdoor slips and falls during the winter months. Icy sidewalks, stairs and driveways are largely to blame for this seasonal uptick. Icy driveways are not only dangerous to walk on but also make driving and parking difficult. Ice melt salt is one of the best ways to keep driveways clear, but how should you best use it? Should you apply it before or after a storm — or both? Two industry experts weigh in on the best way to salt your driveway for optimal results this winter.

It depends. Salting before a big storm with heavy snow and ice accumulation is a good idea, but timing matters.

Plan to apply salt several hours before a storm hits for best results. It creates a salty coating that prevents the initial snowfall from bonding with the driveway surface. Pre-salting reduces total snow accumulation and makes snow removal easier once the storm is over. Avoid doing it too far in advance, or if rain is in the forecast, as the salt can be washed or blown away before the snow arrives. It’s not necessary to salt before a light snowfall of less than six inches.

It’s always a good idea to salt after it snows, but don’t rely on it to replace snow removal efforts. First remove as much snow as possible with a shovel or snowblower, then use salt to clear away any remaining slush and snow.

The best time to salt is immediately after snow removal, before cold nighttime temperatures set in. “When applied before the temperatures drop, salt prevents ice from forming and ensures the snow does not freeze solid to the ground,” Angelika Klimorowski, a winter maintenance specialist at Online Rock Salt, says.

Apply salt before and after a big storm for best results, especially when heavy snow, sleet or freezing rain is expected.

“Pre-salting helps prevent ice from bonding to the driveway, while a second application can help break down any ice that forms,” Aaron Strickland, owner of Mr. Trim Lawn & Garden Services, says. “For a typical snowfall, both applications usually aren’t necessary.”

If only a few inches of snow is forecast, salting after snow removal is likely sufficient. Applying salt before and after a small storm is not only a waste of money, but can damage driveway surfaces, burn lawn edges and generate harmful storm runoff.

“The exact amount of rock salt that you need will depend on how big the area is that you are planning to de-ice,” Klimorowski says. A little goes a long way, and more is not better. A 12-oz. coffee mug filled with salt is enough to treat a 1,000-sq.-ft. driveway. Spread salt evenly, leaving about 3 inches between salt grains. Avoid overapplication, as it can degrade driveway surfaces and harm the environment.

A handheld spreader makes application easier and more consistent, especially for larger driveways. “We try to follow recommended coverage rates and use a spreader wherever possible,” Strickland says. “Too much salt wastes product and can damage surrounding grass and gardens.” If spreading salt by hand, always wear gloves and cascade it for even coverage.

Rock salt is only effective down to ground temperatures of 15 to 17 degrees. Don’t apply more salt if it’s not working, as it’s likely too cold out for it to work. “If your area tends to experience colder temperatures, you may need to find an alternative de-icing product to keep your surfaces safe,” Klimorowski says. Calcium chloride and magnesium chloride ice melt products are two common alternatives that remain effective at sub-zero temperatures.

Yes, rain will wash away salt rather than dissolve it. It then ends up in storm drains and potentially pollutes local bodies of water.

Rock salt stops working in temperatures below 10 to 15 degrees.

“While it is true that salt will work best if you apply it before the temperatures drop, there is no deadline, and you can always apply it later,” Klimorowski says.

There’s no guarantee salt will damage your driveway, but you should use it sparingly. Concrete driveways are particularly vulnerable to salt damage.
