Andrea Bowers Salutes the Climate Change Activists of the 1980s at Fondazione Sandretto Re Rebaudengo

📅 Nov 04, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Andrea Bowers’ solo exhibition “La Ragnatela” at the Fondazione Sandretto Re Rebaudengo in Turin, Italy, is unintentionally, aptly timed, given its focus on the politics of climate change: Last year, Italian Prime Minister Giorgia Meloni hit pause on solar panels on farmland, calling it a “threat to our food sovereignty.”

Bowers’ show spotlights the Italian anti-nuclear feminist movement and women’s camp La Ragnatela—“the spiderweb”, in Italian—that launched in 1983 in Comiso, Sicily, to protest the installation of US nuclear cruise missiles at the NATO base in Comiso. The show is a mini-retrospective composed of Bowers’ political activist work which was in dialogue with the movement. There are works by Bowers that she created in 2003, straight out of graduate school, as well as pieces produced in 2013, 2022, 2023 and four newly created artworks for the exhibition. Related Articles Melting Ron DeSantis Statue Makes a Point About Climate Change in Florida Collective Climate Action Implemented by Los Angeles Arts Institutions

Much of La Ragnatela’s history has been forgotten, so Bowers worked with curator Bernardo Follini to find and rebuild the network of Italian ecofeminists. Follini found three women, who are now in their 80s and 90s, who contributed documents and clippings to the exhibition.

“The social engagement part of my practice is the least known about or documented because the art world is more focused on objects, but this is what I’m most proud of,” said Bowers during a preview in Turin, dressed in head-to-toe black with her Botticelli-esque hair flowing freely.

The show opens with Defense of Necessity (2003), a quilt that nods to women’s traditional crafts, and it’s one of the first pieces that Bowers created as a political artist. On close inspection of the quilt, there are sowings of spiderwebs, a symbol for the Women’s Pentagon Action, an American grassroots ecofeminist and anti-nuclear movement that was simultaneously taking place in Italy by way of La Ragnatela and in England with the Greenham Common Women’s Peace Encampment. Defense of Necessity is held up as a fence in the show. “The fence is the site often where nonviolence is questioned whether it’s becoming violence. Activists who are involved in nonviolence are always saying, if we cut that fence, does it become violence? Or is this called an offense of necessity, which is an argument that smaller injustices, or you break small laws to protect the world from larger injustices,” explained Bowers. “It’s a legal term and because it has the word ‘fence’ in it, it’s a play on words and it’s poetic.”

On the other side of the fence are four vitrine cases, two of which feature a timeline of the La Ragnatela movement. The others display letter exchanges between the women in Comiso and their comrades in England, who eventually traveled to Comiso to help them protest. There are photographs of some of the women who were arrested for protesting, along with a letter from one of the women, who was detained for a long time and put on trial, during which she wore a T-shirt that says “question authority.” Bowers took the tagline and added it to Neon Spiderweb #3 (2026), a neon spiderweb with the words sitting on the outer string of a web—the artist also made three other neon works with such slogans: “A better end of the world is possible,” “Return our future” and “To be free from fear.” The political slogans of hope pour over into Political Ribbons (2026), brightly colored sheets of silky ribbons that repeat the mottos on the neon signs or feature new sayings: “People over profits,” “Outlaw women are the only hope for earth’s survival” and “Widen the web to break the nuclear chain.” Visitors are urged to take ribbons from the wall with them when they visit the show. Bowers explains that it’s a “simple way for people to take a position.” It’s a methodology that she’s used before, adding that to this day, she still gets a thrill out of seeing them in the wild or online.

Bowers’ pieces in the show command attention no matter their size. Her graphite on paper works: Diabloblockade, Diablo Nuclear Power Plant, Abalone Alliance, 198 (2003) and Women’s Pentagon Action, 1981, Detail of Woven Web Around Pentagon (2003) are hyperrealistic. the artist has recreated photographs that were given to her by her mother’s friends who were part of the Californian eco-group Mother for Peace. In Cardboard (2026), a larger-than-life cardboard with an “empowered Italian woman” facing her back to the audience holding a flag that says “Sun and wind,” the artist taught herself how to use an iPad to draw the image that’s been projected onto a thick cardboard.

The show at the Fondazione Sandretto Re Rebaudengo is sort of a homecoming for Bowers, as she last showed at the foundation in 1998 as part of a group show titled “Guarene 98.” Some 28 years later, her pieces from the Chandeliers of Interconnectedness franchise grace the halls of the space as visitors make their way to other exhibitions by Nan Goldin or Joseph Yaeger. The show, however, feels cut short. Perhaps Bowers has more to say than can fit the space she was given. (It would have been a better idea to give her two galleries to really demonstrate two decades of hard passion for the environment.)

Still, she managed to make a mark with Neon Opland (2026), another neon sign that imitates a doodle from a La Ragnatela poster of a woman kicking a missile with the words “No ai missile” written beneath. It’s somewhat phallic, a comical touch made more apparent by teenage couple who were flirting on a bench opposite its neon lights.

New TMNT Sequel Confirms Radical Redesign For Classic Series Villain

📅 Nov 04, 2026  |  Category: Entertainment  |  Read Source →

The Teenage Mutant Ninja Turtles’ latest series is in full swing, bringing Leonardo, Donatello, Michelangelo, and Raphael back in peak form. While only two installments have been released so far, it's already one of our favorite takes on the Turtles to date. Now, the series has gotten even better with the debut of a classic TMNT villain, who has received a totally radical new redesign.

September saw the launch of IDW Publishing’s Tales of the Teenage Mutant Ninja Turtles: Coastal Chaos #1, the first issue in writer Andrew Joustra and artist Louie Joyce’s new TMNT series. The title Tales of the Teenage Mutant Ninja Turtles may sound familiar to some fans, given the two-season animated series of the same name that premiered on Paramount+ in 2024.

Paramount+’s Tales of the Teenage Mutant Ninja Turtles was originally intended to bridge the gap between the first Mutant Mayhem (2023) movie and its highly anticipated 2027 sequel. However, the series was ultimately canceled amid Paramount’s broader restructuring following its merger with Skydance. Meanwhile, IDW Publishing brought this iteration of the Turtles to comics in October 2025 with Tales of the Teenage Mutant Ninja Turtles.

Now, this iteration of the Turtles has returned in Joustra and Joyce’s Coastal Chaos, and it’s nothing short of epic. While the series has plenty of strengths, it’s Joyce’s art that truly makes it stand out among TMNT’s ever-expanding bibliography. His art style is bright, cartoonish, and just downright fun, giving the four brothers some of their best redesigns in recent years.

With Coastal Chaos #2 out and available to read, we’ve just witnessed the legendary Armaggon, TMNT’s anthropomorphic shark villain, rendered in Joyce’s exceptional art style. And it’s totally Cowabunga awesome!

While Armaggon was teased in Teenage Mutant Ninja Turtles: Coastal Chaos #1, he didn’t make his full debut until issue #2, as we only caught brief glimpses of him in the first issue. Coastal Chaos #2 sees the Turtles confront Armaggon about his recent beach attacks, calling out the shark for attacking Mikey while he was surfing and targeting other beachgoers. It’s here that we get our first proper look at the villain’s redesign, and while the character is instantly recognizable, this is also one of Armaggon’s most distinctive looks to date.

Armaggon originally debuted in the Archie comics in the early 1990s as a shark mutant from the future. However, many fans will likely know him from the 2012 Teenage Mutant Ninja Turtles animated series, where he appears as a cybernetic alien shark bounty hunter.

Armaggon is typically depicted as a hulking, anthropomorphic shark. However, Joyce flips the character’s traditional design on its head by giving the villain a relatively scrawny appearance that toes the line of sickly. With twig-like arms, a slender build, and skin ravaged by scars, this is Armaggon as we’ve never seen him before. This interpretation is wholly unique while still retaining classic elements of the character, such as his cybernetic weaponry.

The sheer amount of detail Joyce puts into Armaggon’s appearance makes this one of the coolest interpretations of the villain to date. Armaggon’s role in the narrative is also exceptionally fun, as the story somewhat feels like a collision of JAWS and TMNT. We’re confident this is the direction Joustra is taking the story, given that the synopsis for Coastal Chaos #3 teases a very JAWS-esque situation:

The Turtle gang find themselves STRANDED AT SEA on a SINKING SHIP with a HUNGRY MUTATED SHARK hiding just out of sight, ready to make TURTLE SOUP, which he can do because he has BIG MUTATED ARMS. How are they going to get out of this one?! No, really — if you know, please tell them!

If you haven’t dived into Coastal Chaos yet, bump it up on your TBR list. This is a true Teenage Mutant Ninja Turtles story that delivers pure fun and good vibes, and Joyce’s art alone makes it more than worth the read.

AMC Theatres Sets All-Time Record for Quarterly Revenue, Thanks to ‘Spider-Man: Brand New Day’ and ‘The Odyssey’

📅 Nov 04, 2026  |  Category: Business  |  Read Source →

The blockbuster success of “Spider-Man: Brand New Day” and “The Odyssey” fueled a record-breaking quarter for AMC Theatres. Total revenue for the three-month period ended Sept. 30 hit $1.71 billion, up 31.6% from $1.3 billion a year earlier.

Those marked the largest returns ever posted by the world’s largest cinema chain, eclipsing the previous all-time high established in the second quarter of 2026, led by “Project Hail Mary and “Michael.” Prior to this year’s rebound, AMC’s quarterly revenue high-water mark was pro-COVID, set in the second quarter of 2019, when “Avengers: Endgame” was released and shattered any and all box office expectations. Related Stories 'Magic Atlas' Review: Surreal, Shapeshifting Animation Is a Sometimes Mystifying Technical Marvel 'Capturing Bigfoot' Review: An Astounding Documentary Uncovers the Truth

“In recent years, where other key players in our industry collapsed, at AMC we repeatedly instead have talked about recovery. Today, we can finally say that AMC has recovered,” AMC CEO Adam Aron said in a statement. “To our studio partners, lenders, business partners, employees and our millions of passionate shareholders, thank you for standing with us as we traveled through a long, and at times brutal, road. Mere survival was never the goal. Our goal ultimately was to thrive, and that is exactly and precisely where our focus now lies. We are highly energized by the tremendous opportunities ahead.”

Aron attributed the better-than-expected quarter to several factors, including the rising popularity of premium large formats, the A-List subscription service and a resurgent box office. This summer was the best since COVID, as “Spider-Man: Brand New Day” became the third-highest grossing movie in history with $2.5 billion and Nolan’s ‘The Odyssey” powered to an astounding $1.8 billion. Overall domestic theatrical revenue has already eclipsed $8 billion, a milestone the industry didn’t cross last year until December. With potential juggernauts like “Avengers: Doomsday,” “Dune: Part III” and “The Hunger Games: Sunrise on the Reaping” still to come on the schedule, this year will easily be the biggest at the box office in post-pandemic times.

Now when people are going to the movies, they’ve been more likely to indulge in popcorn and soda. AMC’s food and beverage revenue hit $589.5 million for the three-month period, up 30% from $451.8 million a year earlier. Meanwhile, ticket revenue rose to $954.5 million, a 33% increase from the $715.1 million a year earlier.

Losses also shrank for AMC. The company’s net loss was $109.3 million, compared with $298.2 million in losses in the third quarter of 2025. It logged a diluted loss per share of 12 cents, after reporting a loss of 58 cents in the prior-year quarter.

“Sometimes short but sweet is the best way to summarize performance,” Aron wrote in a five-paragraph statement. “We expect the remaining film slate to propel both the domestic and global box office to their highest full-year levels in seven years. We could not be more enthusiastic about AMC’s prospects as this year draws to a triumphant close.”

Political Ad Revenue Breaks Record For Sinclair, Lifting It To Better-Than-Expected Q3 Results

📅 Nov 04, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Sinclair cashed in on political advertising during the third quarter even before the “blue tsunami” officially reached shore. The broadcaster rode a 55% gain in the category compared with the previous midterm election in 2022 to post better-than-expected financial results.

The company said it sold $136 million in political ads during the July-to-September period, with the spending hitting an all-time record of $455 million in 2026, ahead even of the 2024 presidential haul of $405 million. The prior high for a midterm cycle was $333 million in 2022.

Total revenue increased 16% from the prior-year quarter, hitting $893 million. Adjusted EBITDA more than doubled to $213 million, with earnings per share of 60 cents swinging to the black after a 2-cents-per-share loss a year earlier. CEO Chris Ripley said more quarterly results are yet to be reported by rival companies, but it appeared that local TV stations as a sector maintained market share levels from prior political cycles. With live sports continuing to pace the overall linear landscape, some political campaigns opted for national broadcast buys rather than local stations, given the comparative expense profiles. That created the odd spectacle of East Coast sports fans being served PAC-funded spots backing Senate candidates in Texas or Iowa. Watch on Deadline

In 2015 and 2016, as Donald Trump was rising to dominance, Sinclair played a key role in his unforeseen triumph over Hillary Clinton. At the time, Sinclair was also known for more expressly right-of-center programming and “must-run” editorials. A decade later, and in the wake of Democrats’ stinging rebuke of Trump in the midterms, the climate has shifted. Still, Sinclair remains one of the primary local players, though it is now No. 2 station behind market leader Nexstar.

Rob Weisbord, COO and president of local media, told Wall Street analysts on the company’s earnings call that the political haul this year “bodes well for the upcoming 2028 election cycle, which will be here before we know it. Notably, 2028 will present a dual open primary for both parties, for the presidential nominations. So we expect early and aggressive spending as both parties will want to introduce themselves nationally. While our footprint is well positioned throughout many battleground states, we also believe that the entire broadcast sector did well during the election cycle.”

He added that management commentary at rival companies as well as industry forecasts provide evidence “of local broadcast television being a key delivery platform of political advertising.”

Como da record in Champions League: nessuno aveva mai segnato 9 gol in trasferta

Prima di questa sera, infatti, nessuna squadra era mai riuscita a segnare nove reti in trasferta nella competizione. Dal 1992 a oggi, cinque formazioni si erano spinte fino a quota sette: il Marsiglia nel 2010, lo Shakhtar Donetsk nel 2014, il Liverpool nel 2017, il Bayern Monaco nel 2019 e il PSG nel 2025. Soltanto il Bayern era riuscito a fare meglio, arrivando a otto gol nella storica trasferta di Barcellona del 2020. Un primato che sembrava destinato a resistere, almeno fino alla notte del Bollaert-Delelis, dove la banda di Fabregas ha alzato ulteriormente l'asticella, spingendosi dove nessuno era mai arrivato prima.

Con il 9-1 rifilato al Lens, il Como entra anche in un club esclusivissimo: quello delle squadre capaci di chiudere una partita di Champions League con otto reti di scarto. Un'impresa riuscita, prima dei lariani, soltanto a Liverpool e Real Madrid. I Reds ci erano riusciti nel 2007, travolgendo il Besiktas con un 8-0 ad Anfield, mentre i Blancos avevano replicato nel 2015, rifilando lo stesso risultato al Malmo al Bernabeu. Da questa sera, però, anche la squadra di Fabregas può vantare un posto in questa ristretta élite.

Con i dieci gol segnati allo Bollaert-Delelis, Lens-Como diventa anche la terza partita con più reti nella storia della Champions League assieme ai dieci gol sommati che ci furono in Barcellona-Bayern Monaco del 14 agosto 2020 (2-8) e alle spalle degli undici gol in Monaco-Deportivo La Coruña, terminata 8-3 il 5 novembre 2003 e dei dodici che si videro a Signal Iduna Park il 22 novembre 2016 tra il Borussia Dortmund e il Legia Varsavia, che finì 8-4.

It needed a goalline clearance from Michal Sadilek to prevent a rampant Arsenal inflicting more damage before half-time.

The hosts came within a few centimetres of being awarded a penalty when Emmanuel Ayaosi was brought down by Ezri Konsa on the edge of the area. The England defender and Rice both found themselves in the book as Arsenal struggled to recapture their rhythm from the first half without ever looking in danger of conceding.

Durbin Is Retiring, Marshall Just Lost—Why The Bill Threatening Your Credit Card Rewards Isn’t Dead

📅 Nov 04, 2026  |  Category: Business  |  Read Source →

Dick Durbin is retiring and Roger Marshall just lost his Senate seat. The two most prominent champions of the Credit Card Competition Act, the law intended to regulate credit card interchange which will mean far fewer rewards for consumers, will be gone in January. The bill threatening the economics of card rewards, and the airlines that depend on them, has support from President Trump and Vice President JD Vance was an original co-sponsor so it may come back with his presidential campaign. But the thing about this legislation is that it’s more useful as a way to raise campaign money from both retailers and banks than as something Congress actually passes. Departures of Durbin and Marshall haven’t made the Durbin-Marshall bill go away. Durbin identified Vermont Senator Peter Welch as his successor on the issue. The Airline Lobby Is Right To Worry In this week’s Airlines Confidential, Airlines for America CEO Chris Sununu warns that state initiatives interfering with card fees would eliminate points-funded travel. Banks buy miles because rewards help attract customers and their spending. By reducing margins on that spending, it’s less valuable to spend to encourage consumers to use the product. In markets where government has gotten involved in interchange rates we’ve seen: smaller initial bonuses higher annual fees annual caps on awards award chart devaluations (if each mile is worth less, an airline can still award one mile per dollar on spend despite receiving less money for it from the bank) It also means less revenue for the airline. In the third quarter, American Express represented all of Delta’s profit. American Airlines is barely breaking even even with several billion net from Citibank. We’d have much shakier airlines and fewer flights, and aspirational leisure destinations like Hawaii in particular would see less air service. What The Credit Card Competition Act Actually Does The current Senate bill, S. 3623, would require covered issuers with more than $100 billion in assets to enable at least two unaffiliated payment networks. At least one would have to be outside of Visa and Mastercard. Merchants get to choose the routing. That’s meant to put downward pressure on merchant payment costs by changing who controls which network handles the transaction. Cards issued in a three-party system, such as American Express issuing its own cards, are exempt from the routing requirement. Merchants benefit from accepting cards. They get more sales, and higher average sales. It’s often less expensive to accept credit cards than to take cash (due to employee theft, incorrect change, higher insurance costs). Retailers want the service cards offer, but want the government to force Visa and Mastercard to provide it for less. Trump’s promise of $1,200 in annual family savings was completely made up and inconsistent with the bill. Currently a majority of interchange gets rebated to the customer. Regulation of interchange has never lowered prices to consumers. And card payments, funded in large part by interchange, provide services to consumers beyond just payments (and borrowings). They often fund consumer protections – not just stronger dispute resolution than debit cards, but also extended warranty, return protection, and travel protections. The Bill’s Strongest Value Is As A Threat Back in July 2023, I argued that this was an especially useful issue for opening contribution wallets on both sides. Retailers have a large, concentrated interest in lowering their acceptance costs. Banks, payment networks and airlines have a large, concentrated interest in preserving the existing economics. An unresolved legislative threat gives both sides a reason to pour dollars into campaigns and political committees. I’d still bet on another round of proposals, threatened amendments and lobbying. Durbin and Marshall are leaving but the retailers and banks with billions at stake aren’t. Politicians still want the money. Year-end giving campaign launches for the AAN Research Program

📅 Nov 04, 2026  |  Category: Business  |  Read Source →

Support the urgent need for brain research funding

MINNEAPOLIS, Nov. 4, 2026 /PRNewswire/ -- The need for brain research funding has never been more urgent. That is why the American Academy of Neurology (AAN) today announced its year-end giving campaign for the AAN Research Program, inviting people to support the researchers working to find new treatments and cures for brain diseases and disorders.

One hundred percent of every tax-deductible donation will go directly to brain research.

"Scientific breakthroughs begin with researchers who have the ideas and expertise to pursue important questions," said American Academy of Neurology President Natalia S. Rost, MD, MPH, FAAN, FAHA. "The AAN Research Program gives all of us the opportunity to help advance high-quality brain research, bringing hope to people affected by brain disease."

Brain research is what makes it possible to prevent, diagnose, treat, and eventually even cure conditions of the brain and nervous system. It also advances our understanding of memory, learning, sleep, mental health, and healthy aging, helping people live healthier lives.

The AAN Research Program has funded brain research for more than 30 years, owing to its success the exceptional pool of talented researchers in the AAN community, the world's largest association of neurologists and neuroscience professionals.

Demand for funding, however, continues to outpace available resources. Hundreds of researchers apply for AAN grants. The AAN invested nearly $1.8 million in 2026, but millions in viable neuroscience research went unfunded.

"We know that excellent research proposals go unfunded," said American Academy of Neurology Chief Executive Officer Mary E. Post, MBA, CAE. "We want to make sure that we have the funding to support the research that may eventually lead to a cure for MS or a way to prevent Alzheimer's. Every contribution can help researchers pursue discoveries as they work toward improving brain health for all."

With neurologists leading the AAN Board of Directors, Science Committee, and Research Program Subcommittee, the AAN is guided by trusted experts who understand both the science and the real-world application of research.

Help advance research by making a tax-deductible gift on the BrainHealth.com "Donate to Research" page. Organizations interested in supporting the AAN Research Program may contact donations@aan.com.

The AAN Research Program is administered through the American Academy of Neurology Institute, the Academy's 501(c)(3) charitable organization.

Discover more about brain health at BrainHealth.com, a free public resource from the American Academy of Neurology. It offers a website, podcast, and books, connecting you with trusted information from the world's leading experts in brain health. Follow BrainHealth.com on Facebook, X, and Instagram.

The American Academy of Neurology is the leading voice in brain health. As the world's largest association of neurologists and neuroscience professionals with more than 44,000 members, the AAN provides access to the latest news, science and research affecting neurology for patients, caregivers, physicians and professionals alike. The AAN's mission is to enhance member career fulfillment and promote brain health for all. A neurologist is a doctor who specializes in the diagnosis, care and treatment of brain, spinal cord and nervous system diseases such as Alzheimer's disease, stroke, concussion, epilepsy, Parkinson's disease, multiple sclerosis, headache and migraine.
