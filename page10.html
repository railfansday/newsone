Millions could lose paid benefits due to Congress ignoring decades of Social Security warnings: experts

📅 Nov 21, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Andrew Biggs, a senior fellow at American Enterprise Institute, and Romina Boccia, Cato Institute’s director of budget and entitlement policy, warned that Congress’ 50 years of action have led Social Security to the cliff of insolvency.

A panel filled with top economists, including a Nobel Peace Prize winner, warned Congress decades ago that their reforms to Social Security could spell disaster.

Now, experts tell Fox News Digital that group from a bygone era was right, as the safety-net program hurtles toward a crisis that could affect millions.

"Social Security benefits were put on autopilot back in 1977," Andrew Biggs, a senior fellow of the American Enterprise Institute, told Fox News Digital. "Since then, Congress has done as little as it possibly could."

Senate Budget Committee Chair Ron Johnson, R-Wis., unveiled a report on the decades-old program that blasted it as a "Ponzi scheme," and warned that Social Security’s slide into insolvency began in the 1970s, when, despite warnings, lawmakers opted to tie benefit growth to wages rather than inflation.

President Jimmy Carter shakes hands with Louisiana Sen. Russell Long after signing the Social Security bill at the White House in Washington on Dec. 20, 1977. (Getty Archive)

Johnson wrote in his report that at the time, an expert panel was formed that warned lawmakers that if they were to tie benefits to inflation, "Social Security would likely be sustainable today."

Still, lawmakers at the time ran with their own amendments to the program, which were signed into law by former President Jimmy Carter in December 1977.

"What they said in 1977 was if you index benefit growth to wages, you're effectively building a solvency crisis into the program," Biggs said. "You are over-promising, you're going to build in benefits that the current system can't afford to pay, and you're committing future taxpayers to pay rates of taxes that the taxpayers in 1977 weren't willing to pay."

In the decades since, Congress has done little to change the program.

Romina Boccia, the Cato Institute’s director of budget and entitlement policy, told Fox News Digital that’s largely because lawmakers have not had the political will to do it.

She agreed with Johnson that Social Security was effectively a "Ponzi scheme," and said "there's this misconception that a lot of Americans think that Social Security is a retirement savings program."

"But for that, you would actually need to have savings occur in the system, and there would need to be assets that would be invested in earning returns," Boccia said. "That's not the case. We're talking about a pay-as-you-go program."

One of the side effects of Congress’ lack of action to rein in the program is that the wage growth formula relies on workers in the economy contributing with their paychecks.

Boccia noted that as the decades have gone on, "We've seen a severe decline in the number of workers compared to the number of retirees collecting benefits as a result of a decline in fertility."

Sen. Ron Johnson, R-Wis., makes his way to votes in the U.S. Capitol on Sept. 30, 2026. (CQ-Roll Call, Inc. via Getty Images)

Congress’ last real successful effort to reform Social Security came in the early 1980s, Biggs noted, when lawmakers were forced to step in to deal with the "funding crisis" hitting the program.

"They raised taxes. They increased the retirement age. So they did this with months to spare to fix Social Security," Biggs said. "It's not something they wanted to do, but they were forced to do."

"Now in 2032, we're going to face the same situation, where Congress will be forced to take action sometime before the trust fund runs out in 2032, not because they want to, but because they're forced to do it," he continued.

If Social Security’s trust fund runs dry by 2032, Americans’ benefits would be slashed and the onus of paying for the shortfall would add trillions more to the nation’s debt.

But Johnson, in his report, declared that the Social Security problem could not be fixed between now and then because of Congress’ inaction for the last several decades, and he suggested that the solution would be to repeal a possible benefits reduction as the program’s trust fund heads toward insolvency in just under seven years.

Biggs, who was part of former President George W. Bush’s effort to reform Social Security over two decades ago, said that had Congress taken action "25 years ago, you could fix Social Security in barely noticeable ways."

"There is no easy fix to Social Security today, and that stems entirely from the fact that every year, when Congress has had the opportunity to fix Social Security, they chose to duck the issue for political reasons and kick the can down the road," Biggs said.

Boccia disagreed with Johnson’s solution and contended that the real answer was one that lawmakers of yesterday and today would be hesitant to approach — raising taxes.

"The real way to think about this is that there's almost no way to imagine Congress would let those benefit cuts take effect," Boccia said. "It will be so politically toxic. So most likely they're going to try to find a different solution."

"The last time the trust fund was running out of money in 1982, Congress put together a commission and then voted on a bill that increased taxes and reduced benefits, including raising the social security retirement age," she continued. "The problem is that the funding gap now is much larger, more than two times bigger than it was in the ‘80s."

A dozen hybrid compressors can beat its score, but none relies on a neural network alone.

Tufa Labs’ tufazip has compressed 1GB of information from Wikipedia into just 105MB of space, losslessly, without using any hand-built models, according to a research post. The method uses a 163-million-parameter Transformer model as a predictor instead, and similar compression or decompression would take two Nvidia B200 GPUs about a week to manage. It’s the smallest result on the Large Text Compression Benchmark from any entry that uses a neural network as its only predictor.

We shrank 1 GB of Wikipedia to 105.39 MB, losslessly. Introducing tufazip: the best entry on the Large Text Compression Benchmark using a neural network as its sole predictor. Everything above it mixes a transformer with hand-built models.November 19, 2026

Researcher Tommy He built tufazip, and the full package, at 105.39MB after optimization, includes a small, 4.33MB pre-trained starter model — stored at low precision, or quantized, to save space — that’s needed to unpack the file. The full model is a 12-layer Transformer, which means there are 12 stages for each guess to go through. The starter stores just two already-trained stages, copying them back and forth to fill all 12 once the full model spins up in memory; each copy keeps learning on its own.

This doesn’t work like a traditional chatbot or cloud AI but is, rather, a self-contained language model that learns on the fly. Notable here is that this compression is lossless, which means there’s no risk of any data being lost in the process. This means that predictor updates must be “fully deterministic.”

Training happens in two stages. The starter, a 34-million-parameter model, is trained offline, before any compression begins. Then, the full model begins to learn online, by predicting each token and training on it, with the unpacking side repeating the same steps, with the model itself being rebuilt alongside the text. For this reason, the total size must include, besides the compressed text, only the unpacking program and the files it needs, including the starter, but not the full trained model.

There is no traditional, hand-built compression model that assists, setting it apart, Tufa Labs says, from all 12 entries that now rank above it on the benchmark, which mix a Transformer with hand-built models. Its first, online-only version ranked second when submitted in early September; the 105.39MB version was 13th as of the benchmark’s Nov. 19 update. Emphasis should be placed on this being a text-only benchmark, for those imagining media files.

While this sounds more exciting than, say, WinRAR, it’s for research purposes and not a product, and that’s underlined by what you’d need to actually run it. Currently, two Nvidia B200 GPUs — plus two Intel Xeon Platinum 8570 CPUs in the test machine — would need 631,548 seconds to compress and 645,748 seconds to decompress the 105.39MB version, or about 7.3 and 7.5 days, respectively, not counting the starter’s offline training.

The first version, which learned entirely online and came in at 105.98MB, took 647,595 and 630,136 seconds. Its GPUs peaked at 43.37 GiB of VRAM between them, and total memory use, GPUs included, peaked at roughly 68,525 MiB during compression and 73,748 MiB during decompression.

The experiment is fundamentally interesting because it shows, for example, that a smaller vocabulary can actually help: a 4K-word tokenizer beats both 8K and 16K. Giving Wikipedia’s XML markup its own dedicated tokens made the compression worse. Bigger isn’t always better, and that goes for model size, too, as a 267-million-parameter model did slightly worse than a 188-million-parameter model on this data set. With a smaller, 100MB version of the file, gains leveled off at about 77 million parameters. A run started from a bigger, six-layer offline model predicted better, with just a 99.17MB payload, but its chunkier weights made the total file larger than the online-only run.

Improving compression can be costly, as widening the window of already-processed text used for extra training, from 15 to 35 million tokens, only cut the payload 1.21% at the cost of 40% more compute. The key takeaways are that optimization isn’t always as simple as throwing more at the problem, and that trade-offs can be more expensive than expected. He frames that six-layer trade-off as the next research question: how much predictive quality a smaller model can keep, which makes the vanishing point a balance between predictive quality and model size for this benchmark.

The seasons are shifting, which means our feathered friends are about to embark upon a changing of the guard... so which is your birth month winter bird, you ask?

It's not a silly question, by any means. After all, some birds migrate south for winter, some arrive from further north, and year-round residents shift their habits. And, from the brilliant red cardinal perched against a snowy backdrop to the tiny dark-eyed junco hopping beneath a bird feeder, these seasonal visitors have plenty of personality.

With that in mind, then, what if every month had its own winter bird to call its own? What might it reveal about the people born under its wings?

Now, we’re already familiar with the idea of a classic birth month bird, not to mention the birth month owl and birth month bird of prey, but what might the unique traits that make each winter bird so wonderful say about our own personalities?

Some are bold and charismatic, others are quiet and contemplative, and a few are wonderfully independent. All, though, are beautiful in their own special way, and so much fun to watch as they go about their business over the colder months.

With that in mind, then, I've paired 12 fascinating winter birds with the personality traits commonly associated with each birth month, along with a few easy ways you can make your garden a more welcoming place for them. Time to grab yourself a motion-activated wildlife camera with night vision, like the GardePro on Amazon, then, and start reading...

If you're born in January, you're likely to be headstrong, disciplined and hardworking (all of which are, natural leadership qualities, just in case you fancied informing your boss). Naturally, then, your birth month winter bird is a wonderful reflection of that determination: the snow bunting.

These beautiful little birds are highly social during winter, often gathering in flocks to forage for seeds in open fields and along shorelines, while their ability to thrive in bitterly cold conditions is rather impressive for such a small bird.

Like January babies, basically, they don't shy away from a challenge; they meet it head-on! To help them do this, be sure to leave native grasses and seed-producing plants standing where possible, rather than tidying every corner of your garden.

One of the most iconic birds in the world, the striking snowy owl has brilliant white plumage, piercing yellow eyes and an unmistakable presence. They spend much of the year in the Arctic, but some travel south during winter, occasionally appearing in northern parts of the United States (although they tend to favour wide-open landscapes, including coastal dunes, fields and other exposed areas where they can hunt).

Often solitary hunters, February's birth month winter bird gets by on its own instincts and remarkable ability to spot prey from a distance. It's also wonderfully adapted to its harsh environment, making it a fitting symbol of independence, deep-thinking, and inner strength.

To help snowy owls out over the colder months, avoid using rodenticides that can poison birds of prey, turn your garden into an owl hangout (it only takes five plants), and always give any owl you encounter plenty of space.

March babies are often associated with introspection, creativity, compassion and a thoughtful nature. It makes a whole lot of sense, then, that their birth month winter bird is the dark-eyed junco, a small but charming visitor that brings plenty of life to colder gardens.

Sometimes nicknamed the snowbird, dark-eyed juncos tend to spend much of their time quietly hopping around on the ground, searching for fallen seeds beneath trees and feeders. As such, they're a lovely match for March personalities: observant, gentle and happy to enjoy the quieter things in life.

If you fancy seeing more in your garden, remember that juncos are particularly fond of feeding on the ground, so provide a sheltered feeding area with small seeds. Leaving native grasses and seed heads standing also gives them more natural foraging opportunities.

If you're an April baby, you're thought to be charismatic, energetic, straightforward and naturally confident. As such, there's perhaps no better bird to represent you than the unmistakable blue jay.

With its brilliant blue plumage, distinctive crest and loud calls, the blue jay certainly knows how to make itself noticed. These intelligent members of the crow family have bold personalities that often make them one of the most entertaining birds to watch, in fact.

Blue jays are also clever problem-solvers, not to mention never afraid to make their presence known. Plant native oak trees and other nut-producing species to provide natural food and shelter to help them out, and remember that they also enjoy unsalted peanuts , so you can occasionally offer some in a suitable feeder.

I know, I know; a wild turkey might not feel like the most glamorous birth month winter bird, but hear me out! May personalities are often described as sociable, talented, loyal and strong-willed. And wild turkeys? Why, they're highly social, often gathering in flocks during winter as they search for food, not to mention putting on some impressive courtship displays.

They're also resourceful foragers, searching through woodland floors for seeds, nuts, berries and insects. During colder months, they can be spotted scratching through leaf litter and searching beneath trees for whatever food they can find. To help yours out, then, take care to leave natural cover in place where possible, and avoid using pesticides that reduce the insects and other natural food sources they rely on.

Known for its melancholy-sounding coo, the gentle mourning dove is a familiar sight across much of the United States, often seen perched on telephone wires, fences and tree branches, or quietly searching the ground for seeds.

They're also known for their strong pair bonds, with partners often working together to raise their young, so it's easy to see why this bird suits June babies (especially as they tend to be such romantic dreamers). It's a birth month winter bird that perfectly aligns with all of that sensitivity and imagination.

Remember, mourning doves enjoy feeding on the ground, so provide a clean feeding area with something like sunflower hearts if you want to see them in your garden. Plant some native seed-producing plants and shrubs, too, to help provide natural food and shelter.

July babies are adventurous free spirits known for their cheerful and (ahem) slightly slightly eccentric personalities, not to mention their strong protective streaks when it comes to family. It was inevitable, then, that the snow goose would prove their birth month winter bird.

Known for their impressive journeys between breeding and wintering grounds, snow geese are often spotted travelling in enormous flocks that fill the sky with their distinctive calls. They are also strongly connected to family life; young birds travel with their parents, and family groups can remain together during migration and winter.

If you want to help your feathery soulmate out, remember that snow geese depend on healthy wetlands, grasslands and agricultural landscapes during migration and winter. Supporting wetland conservation and protecting natural resting and feeding areas, then, is one of the best ways to keeo them thriving.

It makes so much sense that August babies get the delightful red-breasted nuthatch as their birth month winter bird, as it sums up all their best qualities perfectly. Yes, this little bird might be small, but it certainly doesn't lack confidence. One of its most entertaining habits is climbing headfirst down tree trunks, something it does with remarkable ease as it searches for insects and seeds hidden in the bark.

During autumn and winter, red-breasted nuthatches also collect and store conifer seeds, helping ensure they have food available when conditions become more challenging. They're hardworking, resourceful and rather determined, making them a lovely match for August birthdays.

To help them out, try to plant native trees such as pine, spruce and fir where appropriate for your region. You can also offer sunflower seeds, peanuts or suet in a clean feeder, particularly during colder weather.

Intelligent, spiritual, organized and analytical, September babies tend to be more successful in life (genuinely; look it up!) and thus are represented by the pine siskin.

These small finches might be easy to overlook at first, but they're fascinating little birds. They are adaptable, clever, and brimming with plenty of resourcefulness, though, which is why you often seen clinging to the tips of pine branches and even hanging upside down as they extract seeds from cones.

Sure, they're rather unpredictable visitors, as their movements depend heavily on food availability; this means that some gardens might welcome large flocks one winter and see very few the next. To keep them flocking, grow native conifers and other seed-producing plants to provide natural food, as well as nyjer (thistle) seed and sunflower hearts in finch feeders.

As an October baby myself, I'm more than happy to see this charming wanderer as my birth month winter bird! Especially as, with its striking yellow, black and white plumage and enormous pale bill, this chunky finch is one of North America's most distinctive birds.

Evening grosbeaks are adventurous travellers, sometimes moving considerable distances in search of food during autumn and winter. They're also wonderfully sociable, often gathering in groups around food sources and making quite an impression when they arrive at a backyard feeder.

For October birthdays, they're a lovely symbol of persistence, confidence and a little good fortune. After all, spotting one can feel like winning a small birdwatching lottery (although you can boost your chances with a handful of black-oil sunflower seeds, which can be offered in a sturdy platform feeder).

Empathetic, generous, brave and deeply determined, November-born souls are represented by one of North America's most magnificent waterbirds: the trumpeter swan.

These enormous white swans are impressive in every sense, with long necks, powerful wings and a distinctive black bill. They are known for their strong pair bonds and devoted parenting, and their family groups can be particularly striking to see gliding across a winter lake. In fact, it's their loyalty, protective instincts and graceful strength that make them a beautiful match for November birthdays.

These aren't traditional garden birds, so you can help them by protecting local wetlands, ponds and freshwater habitats, and always taking care to avoid disturbing swans when they're resting or feeding.

Generous, patriotic, optimistic and willing to take a few risks? Yeah, obviously the birth month winter bird for December is the cardinal!

Known for their strong pair bonds and the way they communicate through a variety of musical calls, their cheerful appearance against bare branches and snowy gardens has led to them becoming something not unlike a symbol of hope during the festive season. In fact, many believe that if they're the first bird you spot on January 1st, you're owed 12 months of love, optimism, and luck for the year ahead.

If you want to help yours out, plant native berry-producing shrubs such as winterberry, serviceberry and dogwood to make your garden a one-stop destination for food, shelter, and nesting opportunities.

Whether you believe in the concept of a birth month winter bird or not, there's no denying that they serve as a lovely reminder of just how much wildlife is around us, even during the coldest months of the year.

And perhaps the best part is that you don't have to wait for your own birth month to enjoy these feathered visitors. By planting native trees and shrubs, leaving seed heads standing, providing clean water and maintaining safe feeding areas, you can help make your garden a welcoming winter refuge for your feathered friends throughout the year.

Give it a go, I say! You'll do your bit for local wildlife and you might even discover a new favorite feathered friend along the way...

Space Exploration Technologies (SPCX -7.69%) just reached another major milestone with its Starship program, and the outcome could have huge implications for where its stock heads by 2030. Specifically, Flight 15 launched Nov. 13, and SpaceX caught the upper stage the day after. However, the Super Heavy booster aborted its catch and was lost in the Gulf.

Overall, the catch could strengthen confidence in SpaceX's ambitions, while the lost booster could raise concerns around development timelines.

Here's what investors should watch and how the mixed result could affect SpaceX stock.

Starship is a heavy-lift launch vehicle designed to be fully reusable, enabling SpaceX to launch, recover, and fly its rockets again rather than building a new vehicle for every mission. Now that Starship's upper stage has been caught for the first time, the vehicle could be a future game changer for SpaceX as the company could change the economics of getting into space and back.

From there, if the company can make that process (reliably) repeatable, it could dramatically lower launch costs and increase the number of missions it can undertake. Plus, with a much larger payload capacity, Starship could potentially carry more cargo and satellites into orbit than existing rockets.

That could lead to cheaper launches and make previously uneconomical businesses more attractive, from satellite deployment and space-based communications to orbital infrastructure and lunar transportation.

Beyond that, Starship could also make it easier and cheaper for SpaceX to expand its Starlink business, allowing it to deploy more satellites, ramp up coverage, and increase network capacity.

Of course, the upper stage catch was only half of the planned double recovery. What does the lost booster mean for SpaceX?

SpaceX shares slipped about 0.6% on launch day, but have since edged higher. Even so, the lost booster could raise questions about development timelines, future launch costs, and the company's ability to deliver on its longer-term ambitions.

The unsuccessful booster recovery attempt could delay the timeline for achieving full reusability.

There's also a timing consideration. SpaceX has big ambitions for Starship, including its role in NASA's lunar program and the expansion of Starlink. Any delays would affect expectations surrounding both opportunities.

Still, the lost booster doesn't necessarily undermine the investment case for owning SpaceX. After all, Starship is a much more ambitious launch vehicle, so the development process is naturally more complicated. And if/when Starship finally becomes operational, it could provide the company with a new avenue for growth.

Today, SpaceX stock trades at a price-to-sales (P/S) ratio of about 61, and based on the latest reported sales data, that works out to $2.36 in sales per share. This high multiple may be justified, assuming revenue increases significantly as SpaceX successfully recovers Starship.

In a conservative case, if SpaceX generates $11.95 per share in sales, an 8-times compressed multiple could put shares near $96 by 2030, about 32% below today's level. At $17.49 per share in sales and a 12-times multiple, shares could trade near $210 by 2030, about 48% above today's level. Finally, in a more optimistic case, sales of $24.77 per share at 15 times sales could put shares near $372 by 2030, about 163% above today's level.

To be clear, these are scenarios, not promises, but they show where Starship's progress after Flight 15 could take the stock by 2030. In the end, the bigger opportunity is Starship's potential to make space transportation dramatically cheaper, opening up entirely new markets.

Harry Kane is targeting the "business end of the season" after undergoing surgery on a torn ligament in his left ankle.

Bayern Munich said the operation on the injury, sustained in Friday's 1-1 draw with Cologne, was a success but the 33-year-old is expected to be out for months as a result.

Kane wrote on social media: "The highs and lows of life! Obviously very disappointed to get this injury but as always it's an opportunity to show resilience and improvement going into 2027.

"I'll be back fresh and ready to go for the business end of the season!

"Tough times don't last, tough people do" - my guy @edelman11 (Julian Edelman, three-time Super Bowl winner with the New England Patriots) told me this one!

"Thank you for all the messages! Alles gut."

Kane - who became England's most-capped player last week - sustained the injury when he was scythed down in the area by Cologne defender Joel Schmied, who reached Michael Olise's low cross first but clattered into the Ballon d'Or winner's left leg in his follow-through at the back post.

The England captain writhed in pain and, although he gingerly got back to his feet and initially soldiered on, it was clear he could not continue and he hobbled off the field alongside two physios after treatment.

Kane had a quick exchange with Vincent Kompany, who was back in the Bayern hot-seat for the first time in six matches after being diagnosed with meningeal irritation last month, before heading down the tunnel.

After the match Bayern confirmed Kane had torn the syndesmotic ligament and "will therefore be sidelined for the next few months".

Kane earlier this month signed a new deal with the Bundesliga giants until June 2029 - reportedly worth in excess of £400,000 a week, making him the highest-paid player in Europe before bonuses.

Since arriving from Tottenham three years ago, Kane has bagged a scarcely believable 156 goals in 163 appearances in all competitions, helping Bayern win a league and cup double last season.

He went into the international break having a drawn a blank in his four previous matches for Bayern but he scored twice for England, including from the spot in their 3-2 Nations League win over Spain.

But after being forced off after 20 minutes at the Allianz Arena, replaced by Tom Bischof, Kane could now be facing a battle to be fully fit for England's two-legged quarter-final against Norway in March.

A three-month period on the sidelines would rule Kane out for 11 Bundesliga games, the DFB-Pokal round of 16 tie against Hertha Berlin, as well as all four of Bayern's remaining Champions League league phase matches.

Providing his recovery goes to plan, that would give the striker just over a month to prepare for England's Nations League quarter-final.

The Three Lions face Norway in Oslo in the first leg on March 27, before the return leg at Wembley on March 30.

Hailey Bieber’s It-Jacket Wardrobe Is Becoming Untenable

📅 Nov 21, 2026  |  Category: Business  |  Read Source →

Hailey Bieber must have some serious wardrobe storage. The Rhode founder has long been a devoted collector of excellent outerwear—oversized leather bombers, vintage furs, sculptural shearling, and the occasional extravagant Conner Ives robe—but her latest run of looks in New York has taken things to another level. At this point, we’re struggling to keep up with her ever-expanding collection of It-jackets.

The last few days have seen Bieber leave just about everybody else out in the cold. On November 17, she stepped out a Phoebe Philo shaggy Dancer jacket: cropped, voluminous, in mottled brown and gray shearling. She paired it with her trusty light-wash Levi’s Ribcage jeans, vintage black Manolo Blahnik sandals, and amber-tinted Gucci sunglasses. The jacket, now sold out, was the undisputed star of the outfit. It’s a tried-and-true Bieber styling trick and very much a formula to follow this season.

The following day, she dipped into Carolyn Bessette-Kennedy’s wardrobe playbook for an appearance at the New York Times DealBook Summit. Bieber wore a black Yohji Yamamoto jacket from spring 1998, paired with a white pencil skirt and black pumps. The same design was famously worn by Bessette-Kennedyin 1998. It was a major archival find.

But she wasn’t finished. Later, Bieber emerged in an ivory satin opera coat by New York-based Oriori Maison, framed by a dramatic swathe of voluminous gray-white fur, worn with black trousers, dark sunglasses, and her trusty Inez pumps from Reformation.

The designer behind the coat, Xiaoxi Zhang, tells Vogue that Bieber’s stylist contacted her directly to arrange the pull. For Zhang, whose designs explore Chinese heritage through a contemporary wardrobe, the moment is particularly meaningful. “I’ve always loved my cultural heritage, but I’ve never really worn traditional Chinese clothing,” Zhang shared. “Before making my first Chinese-inspired jacket, my intention was always to find a way to bring elements of Chinese culture into everyday fashion—to make heritage feel natural, modern, and wearable.”

Zhang made her first jacket in 2024, initially just for herself, rather than as a business venture. Discovering vintage quilts helped crystallize her approach. “It was the beginning of seeing my culture through my own perspective, rather than simply recreating the traditional,” she says. Her luxurious satin-and-fur creation has now found its way onto one of fashion’s most closely watched dressers. Her one-of-one pieces using sumptuous archival textiles are dropped via Instagram, if you want to emulate this excellent look.

By November 19, Bieber was back in black leather, arriving at Rigor Hill Market in a glossy, longline leather coat, relaxed trousers, and black shoes worn with white socks.

Of course, Bieber’s expansive love of leather outerwear is nothing new. Earlier this fall, she was spotted in a fringed leather blazer, while her collection of oversized bombers and sharply tailored jackets has long been the foundation of her off-duty wardrobe. What’s changed is the sheer variety: from minimalist archival tailoring to flamboyant, fur-trimmed opera coats, Bieber is evidently having fun with her outerwear, heralding a post-quiet luxury era that keeps things cozy.

And she’s certainly not alone. As Vogue recently observed, the personality coat is having a major moment, with Jennifer Lawrence embracing Colleen Allen’s chartreuse velvet housecoat, and other fashion favorites turning to extravagant silhouettes and trimmings.

If there’s one thing we’ve learned from Hailey Bieber’s fall wardrobe, it’s that you can never have too many great jackets. Though, at this rate, she may need another home to house them all.
