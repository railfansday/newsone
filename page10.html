Man United Supporters’ Trust underlines ‘depressing moment’ after Michael Carrick sacked

The supporters’ group said “responsibility for United’s problems... lie with the ownership” rather than Carrick

Want to bookmark your favourite articles and stories to read or reference later? Start your Independent Membership today.

The Manchester United Supporters’ Trust (MUST) has branded Michael Carrick’s sacking “another depressing moment” and argued the club’s problems rest primarily with its ownership, in a now-familiar plea to end the ongoing feeling of crisis at Old Trafford.

United sacked Carrick just 10 games into the Premier League season with the club in the relegation zone and having won just once in this domestic campaign.

The club’s hierarchy met minority owner Jim Ratcliffe in Monaco on Wednesday to discuss Carrick’s future and a statement on Thursday said that it had “come to the extremely difficult conclusion that a change is necessary in order to achieve our ambitions”.

Former Southampton, Tottenham and Chelsea manager Mauricio Pochettino, who currently manages the US men’s national team, is in line to replace him.

The club statement paid tribute to former midfielder Carrick as a “Manchester United legend” and the MUST statement concurred, saying “his standing among supporters remains undiminished”, but criticised the ownership for the spiral the club has found itself in.

It said: “Michael Carrick’s departure marks another depressing moment for Manchester United. We thank him for his service and for restoring hope during the second half of last season. His standing among supporters remains undiminished.

“The reason Manchester United fans have continued to support Michael Carrick in recent weeks is that they know that responsibility for United’s problems do not primarily rest with him or indeed his predecessors as manager. They lie with the ownership.

“The Glazers’ debt-funded takeover continues to burden the club. When Ineos came in they promised sporting excellence, but yet with repeated changes in football leadership we have seen anything but.

“It is time for the owners to explain themselves and show some accountability.”

MUST continued to criticise “aggressive ticketing policies” which “have further damaged relations with supporters”, arguing “supporters are being asked to pay the price for the owners’ mismanagement as well as their stadium ambitions through higher charges and diminished rights”.

It continued: “The unprecedented scale of the booing at Old Trafford, alongside public criticism from senior players in recent weeks, now points to a deeper deterioration. These are signs of a club becoming more divided, with the bond between supporters, players and those running Manchester United under increasing strain.”

Defender Lisandro Martinez criticised the club’s hierarchy for not doing more to support Carrick in the transfer market following the 2-1 Champions League defeat to Atletico Madrid last month, while dire on-pitch performances have resulted in protests against the ownership and loud booing of the players, although there were public displays of support for Carrick by fans at Old Trafford.

MUST concluded: “The owners must allow senior club management to change direction: show accountability, rebuild trust, listen to supporters and recognise that their loyalty is fundamental to football success.

“Another change of head coach cannot repair what the ownership continues to undermine.”

Football fan arrest numbers branded ‘unacceptable’ by policing chief

📅 Nov 12, 2026  |  Category: Sports  |  Read Source →

Please refresh the page or navigate to another page on the site to be automatically logged inPlease refresh your browser to be logged in

Police praised supporters who travelled to North America for their conduct but warned that wider football-related disorder remains a concern

Want to bookmark your favourite articles and stories to read or reference later? Start your Independent Membership today.

Latest arrest figures for fans watching last summer’s World Cup are "unacceptably" high despite excluding linked crimes such as domestic abuse, football’s policing lead has said.

Home Office data shows 357 arrests occurred in England and Wales linked to fans watching England matches during the North American tournament, compared to just 19 arrests at the event itself.

Chief Constable Mark Hobrough, National Police Chiefs’ Council (NPCC) lead for football policing, praised travelling fans for "exemplary" behaviour, but described conduct at home during the World Cup and 2025-26 domestic season as far more troubling.

He said: "Though the vast majority of supporters attending domestic fixtures in the UK are law-abiding and enjoy supporting their teams at great games, there still remain an unacceptable number who spoil the event for others.

"2,025 people were arrested for football-related incidents during the domestic season in 2025-26, and disappointingly this number is up from 2,004 in 2024-25.

"In addition, 357 people were arrested in England and Wales for football-related incidents during the 2026 World Cup alone.

"Sadly, these figures do not paint a full picture as they do not include many of the associated offences, such as domestic abuse, which often occur behind closed doors after the full-time whistle has blown and in many cases nowhere near any stadium or club with individuals who’ve not attended any game themselves.

"However, as the figures show, we are continuing to take action against those responsible, and moving forward, we will continue to work with our partners including the Crown Prosecution Service, the Premier League, the EFL and the Football Association to collectively make football a safe environment for the overwhelming majority of supporters who just want to enjoy the game."

Home Office figures for 2025-26, first released in July showing 1,963 football-related arrests or police actions, have now been revised to 2,025.

Total arrests across the 2025-26 season—including domestic games, the World Cup, and European fixtures abroad—reached 2,403, falling from 2,584 in 2023-24, the previous men’s tournament season.

Tournament-related arrests within England and Wales increased compared to 2023-24, a change the Home Office attributed to better recording and awareness among police forces.

Michael Carrick sacked by Man United with club stuck in relegation zone after latest crisis

📅 Nov 12, 2026  |  Category: Sports  |  Read Source →

Please refresh the page or navigate to another page on the site to be automatically logged inPlease refresh your browser to be logged in

United have failed to win in eight Premier League games and will spend the international break 18th in the table

Want to bookmark your favourite articles and stories to read or reference later? Start your Independent Membership today.

Michael Carrick has been sacked as Manchester United manager with the club sitting in the Premier League relegation places after 10 games.

A second Old Trafford comeback of the week, this time battling from two goals down twice to rescue a 4-4 draw with Aston Villa on Sunday, could not save Carrick’s job and he departs United just three months into the start of the season and with his side 18th during the international break.

United ended a nine-game winless run when they came from two goals down to beat Roma in the Champions League on Tuesday but they have not won in eight attempts in the Premier League and have just one top-flight win this campaign.

It represents a stark turnaround from Carrick’s positive impact as interim manager last season, when the former midfielder went beyond expectations after replacing Ruben Amorim in January, steadying the ship and guiding them to Champions League qualification.

Carrick’s position has been under threat since the damaging defeats against Como and Bournemouth last month, but he survived crisis talks with the United hierarchy and was given time to turn results around, but speculation over a change begun the day after Sunday’s chaotic draw and United States boss Mauricio Pochettino is expected to succeed Carrick having reportedly reached a verbal agreement with the club.

United have made contact with US Soccer over compensation for Pochettino with a reported figure of $10m (£7.4m) set to be paid to release him from his contract which was due to run through to the 2030 World Cup. His current staff are set to join him at Old Trafford and would require further compensation payments.

The timing of the November international break, with a full two weeks before their next game away to Liverpool in the Premier League, has prompted United to finally act and leave minority owners Ineos searching for a fourth permanent manager in three years.

Though Pochettino is the overwhelming favourite to become the next head coach, Eddie Howe is available following his departure from Newcastle in the summer while former Bayern Munich and Germany manager Julian Nagelsmann is also an option. Aston Villa’s Unai Emery and Brighton’s Fabian Hurzeler are admired but their clubs would demand significant compensation.

Manchester United confirmed the news of Carrick’s exit with a statement which reads: “Manchester United can confirm that Michael Carrick has departed his role as men’s first-team head coach.

“Michael rejoined the club in January and guided the team to a third-placed Premier League finish by winning 12 out of the remaining 17 games of the 2025/26 season, accumulating the highest points tally in the league during that period.

“After a disappointing start to the season, the club has come to the extremely difficult conclusion that a change is necessary in order to achieve our ambitions.

“Michael is, and always will be, a Manchester United legend. Just as he did as a player, he has conducted himself with the highest standards of dedication and professionalism throughout his time back with the club. His leadership and achievements last season will always be fondly remembered.

“The club intends to appoint a permanent successor as quickly as possible.”

Carrick initially returned to Old Trafford as interim manager – his second stint in caretaker role – after the dismissal of Amorim at the beginning of the year. He was rewarded with the full-time position after guiding United to third in the Premier League last term and securing a return to the Champions League.

United spent £155m on three new midfielders this summer – signing Carlos Baleba, Youri Tielemans and Andre Santos, after missing out on their top targets Elliott Anderson and Sandro Tonali – but failed to sign a back-up left-back to Luke Shaw or another forward to support the injury-prone Benjamin Sesko.

But defender Lisandro Martinez criticised the club’s hierarchy for not doing more to support Carrick following the 2-1 defeat to Atletico Madrid earlier this month while captain Bruno Fernandes questioned his manager’s tactics following the 4-1 loss at Como a week later.

Carrick had gambled by dropping captain Fernandes for the home match against Bournemouth but a dismal 3-0 defeat instead highlighted how a fractured dressing room has unravelled since the beginning of the season.

Carrick said Fernandes did not play against Bournemouth due to “pure football” reasons before holding clear-the-air talks with the Portuguese and selecting him to start as his captain in the game at Stamford Bridge.

Another defeat followed against Chelsea before United finally ended their losing run by beating Roma. The stirring win did not save Carrick’s job, however, even after another remarkable comeback against Aston Villa on Sunday.

United have conceded 30 goals in their 14 games this season and have won just three times – against newly promoted Ipswich in the Premier League and Sabah and Roma in Europe. They will travel to Liverpool on 22 November having spent the fortnight in the bottom three.

When will Mauricio Pochettino take charge of Manchester United?

📅 Nov 12, 2026  |  Category: Sports  |  Read Source →

Please refresh the page or navigate to another page on the site to be automatically logged inPlease refresh your browser to be logged in

Pochettino is the favourite to replace Michael Carrick as United head coach and could be in situ for the Premier League trip to Anfield

Want to bookmark your favourite articles and stories to read or reference later? Start your Independent Membership today.

Michael Carrick has been sacked as Manchester United manager just three months into the new season with the club sitting 18th in the Premier League table.

The Old Trafford hierarchy pulled the trigger on Carrick’s departure on Thursday morning after three days of speculation following a 4-4 draw with Aston Villa which proved to be Carrick’s final match in charge.

Thoughts now turn to Carrick’s replacement with former Tottenham, Chelsea and Paris Saint-Germain manager Mauricio Pochettino the odds on favourite to take over.

Pochettino is the current manager of the United States Men’s National Team (USMNT) but has reportedly reached a verbal agreement with Man United to sign of as their new head coach. Should his appointment be confirmed when will Pochettino officially begin his tenure?

Man United’s next match comes on Sunday 22 November when they travel to Anfield to take on Liverpool. It will arguably be the most difficult start possible for whoever takes over and it is a game United need to take something from or else they risk dropping further into the relegation zone.

The current international break would usually provide a new manager with time to train and see the squad in action but Pochettino, should he be given the job, is unlikely to have such a luxury.

That is because the United States have two Nations League matches to play on November 14 and 18 during this window and Pochettino is likely to oversee both fixtures.

The Argentine is still contracted to US Soccer until the 2030 World Cup though an early departure from his deal feels increasingly likely. United have made contact with US Soccer over paying compensation to release Pochettino with rumoured figures reaching as high a $10m (£7.4m).

Should an agreement be made, Pochettino could, in theory, leave the US before the scheduled games against Haiti in order to spend time with Man United’s squad ahead of the crucial clash at Anfield.

In that case he could arrive at Carrington either the same day or a day after being formally announced as the new head coach. That would give him a maximum of nine days to prepare the team if his appointment is confirmed today (Thursday 12 November).

The more likelier option is that Pochettino leads the USMNT for their two fixtures with Haiti and reports to Carrington shortly afterwards.

In that case, he could be in post at Man United as early as Wednesday 18 November though given the time difference between the US and the UK, Pochettino would arrive late in the afternoon or early evening.

Even so, that would give him three full days, Thursday 19, Friday 20 and Saturday 21 to settle into his new environment and have time on the grass with the squad.

Whether three days will be enough for the Argentine to inspire a new manager bounce out of United’s playing group remains to be seen.

‘Ghost Soldier’ Review: Jim Caviezel Is a Superheroic Soldier in an Uninspired, Retrograde Actioner

📅 Nov 12, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

William Eubank's patriotic exercise casts the 'Sound of Freedom' star as a former Special Forces man defending his small Wyoming town from foreign corporate overlords.

If “Walk Hard” was the parody that made it harder to take seriously subsequent music biopics, “MacGruber” did the same for a certain subset of action movies. Directed by William Eubank (“Underwater”), “Ghost Soldier” stacks up one-man-army cliches as early as the opening credits and continues as if no one’s ever told the story of an individual with a certain set of skills defending the sovereignty of blue-collar Americans from deep-pocketed and unscrupulous foreign interests. Jim Caviezel, who’s increasingly made himself a Hollywood representative for politically conservative ideologies with projects like “Infidel” and “Sound of Freedom” (not to mention a series of faith-based projects that began with “The Passion of the Christ”), does little to dispel that association as this film’s tireless, technologically advanced and morally upright title character. But Eubank’s glossy direction, paired with a merciless urgency (and complete lack of originality) in Chris Papsadero’s script, renders the story’s ripped-from-the-headlines details with too little dimensionality even for it to deliver the satisfaction of a good, bloody B-movie.

Caviezel plays Henry “Fitz” Fitzgerald, a former Special Forces soldier who retires to Valentine, Wyoming to live in relative anonymity while still designing advanced weapons for DARPA, the Department of Defense’s research and development agency. When “water monetization” company Brownstone begins buying up land around Midwest rivers and lakes for sale to AI companies for their data centers, its CEO (Mido Hamada) instructs his U.S. representative Ty Bell (Alexej Manvelov) to apply pressure — financial or physical — to coerce holdouts in Valentine.

Using enforcers Graves (Garret Dillahunt) and McNeil (Ricky Whittle), Bell tries to bully the nearby Hernandez family into yielding their property, first by declaring eminent domain and later by setting their home on fire. After befriending the Hernandez’ young son Jack (Cameron Gayden), Fitz steps in to protect them using a variety of cutting-edge weapons he has stored in a bunker below his own home. After Graves realizes that the former soldier will present a bigger problem even than his armed “security team” can handle, Brownstone sends in a well-trained group of mercenaries led by Mirko (Beau Knapp) to flush him out and deliver the remaining parcels of land. Aided by gun dealer Garry (Michael Ironside) and a sheriff, Heidi (Olivia Thirlby), who ran afoul of corrupt local leadership, Fitz announces plans to exact vengeance against bloodthirsty Mirko and drive Brownstone out of Valentine permanently.

Released Stateside on Veterans Day, “Ghost Soldier” clearly aims to pay tribute to America’s servicemen and women, and Papasadero’s script firmly sides with hard working citizens who too often in real life get crushed by the money and muscle of corporate interests. But even if the family Fitz is protecting from Blackstone is a Latino one whose patriarch died in combat, the film unnecessarily tries to generate patriotic sentiment by making the company’s CEO a ruthless Bahranian who doesn’t hesitate to bring in bloodthirsty soldiers to commit an extrajudicial killing or 10 in order to achieve his avaricious goals.

Such xenophobic dog whistles might be common (or even expected) in productions from The Daily Wire, but it’s more surprising in a wide release from Sony Pictures. But then again, nothing else in the film has been thought through in any depth, so such details feel more like the most direct means to an end — in other words, instantly establishing good versus evil — than an attempt to make an actual political statement.

Instead, the movie opens with dialogue in which Fitz in spoken of in comically hushed tones (“there’s only one guy for this task”) and then abdicates all other character development in service of making him seem like the ultimate badass. Strictly speaking, there’s nothing wrong with that, but most of what makes Fitz such an effective soldier is his arsenal of futuristic gear, from night vision goggles to clothing that camouflages him in plain sight to bullets that bend around objects, splinter into explosive fragments or use homing devices to hit their targets. That bag of tricks makes him more like Iron Man than a righteous, proletariat-defending warrior, and it undermines what, again, seems designed at least in part to be a rousing paean to those who serve.

The further problem is Caviezel, who successfully sells his role as a super-soldier but fails to inject any deeper humanity in Fitz beyond the fact that he befriends a young boy. In retrospect, it feels like Terrence Malick figured out his appeal back in “The Thin Red Line,” at least as far as military roles are concerned: He’s a cipher, a silhouette for the audience to project their feelings upon. That certainly helps amplify Fitz’s drive, focus and intensity, but what he achieves in the film’s inevitable conclusion feels less like justice than retribution — which rings hollow.

Remarkably, Eubank assembles a fairly impressive ensemble of character actors, including Dillahunt, Knapp, Ironside and Thirlby, as well as Shea Whigham as Fitz’s government liaison and Jaina Lee Ortiz as Jack’s widowed mom Sophia. Unfortunately, as either his friend or foe, they have little to do except fear or admire him. At 90 minutes, “Ghost Soldier” doesn’t have the patience or interest to populate Fitz’s world with anything but victims, worshippers or targets, which is unfortunate because it ends with the implication that the film’s distributor would love to expand his saga into a larger universe where he’s dropped into international conflicts to single-handedly resolve them.

There are, of course, dozens of stories — single-serving or franchise-built — about soldiers brandishing their skill set off the battlefield, so one more doesn’t materially impact that well-established canon. But the ones that endure are the ones that elicit an audience response, be it excitement, empathy or even tragedy. By comparison, “Ghost Soldier” has the spontaneity of a training exercise that’s been run too many times.

Bitcoin ETFs Bleed $1.4B in Five Days as Investors Cut Risk

U.S. spot Bitcoin exchange-traded funds have recorded five consecutive days of net outflows, as investors retreat from risk assets after a turbulent week for markets.

The funds lost some $1.44 billion between November 5 and November 11, including $299.9 million on Wednesday, according to SoSoValue data. That brings net outflows for November so far to $1.88 billion, after $520 million left the funds in October.

The outflows have coincided with a sell-off in AI and technology stocks. On November 5, when the Nasdaq Composite fell 5.5% in a single day, Bitcoin ETFs saw net outflows of about $430 million.

"This wave of outflows is the result of institutional investors actively or passively reducing their risk exposure following a decline in macro risk appetite, which is not yet sufficient to prove that Bitcoin's long-term allocation thesis has reversed," Tim Sun, senior researcher at HashKey, told Decrypt.

The five-day outflows amount to about 2.6% of the funds' $55.1 billion in cumulative net inflows, "far from a full-scale retreat," Sun said. But the funds' total net assets have fallen to $93.7 billion, down about 45% from a peak of $169.5 billion in October 2025, as both outflows and falling prices take their toll.

Bitcoin is trading at $73,358, down 0.87% over the past 24 hours, according to Decrypt data. On Decrypt Predict, users put a 40% chance on Bitcoin trading above $74,000 on Sunday afternoon, and a 94% chance that it will hold above $70,000. Sun estimated that the average holding cost for spot Bitcoin ETFs is around $81,000, meaning some ETF holders are now sitting on unrealized losses, and that some investors may cut their exposure further as they reassess their allocations at year-end.

Analysts say the bigger concern is a shortage of new buyers. In a note shared with Decrypt, Greg Cipolaro, head of research at BitGo, said the post-election bounce brought only $75 million into spot Bitcoin ETFs, while the supply of dollar-pegged stablecoins contracted 1.9% to $286.3 billion, "suggesting little fresh capital is waiting on the sidelines." Onchain analytics firm Glassnode found that total stablecoin supply fell by $14.2 billion in the 30 days through November 9, the fastest contraction since June 2022, Decrypt reported on Wednesday.

The basis trade, in which hedge funds bought spot ETF shares while shorting futures to capture the price gap, has also become less attractive because the basis has fallen below borrowing costs, prompting many funds to exit, Cipolaro said. That means ETF flows may now reflect directional demand for Bitcoin more directly than before, according to Sun.

Corporate buyers have also slowed. The premium of Strategy's stock over the value of its Bitcoin holdings has narrowed, making it less attractive for the company to raise capital to buy more, while MSCI's decision to exclude it from its indexes could cut off passive investment, Sun said.

"The most critical factor to watch right now is the persistent lack of buying demand itself, the impact of which on prices may be more significant than the outflow figures alone," he said.

Cipolaro said Bitcoin had fallen with risk assets but lagged their recoveries. "Unless it begins to participate in rallies and ETF demand strengthens, digital assets could remain exposed to further downside without benefiting from a broader risk asset rebound," he wrote.

Customer Tried to Scam LA Pizza Shop With AI Image

A pizza shop customer used AI to edit a photo of their pizza in a bid to get a refund — but it ultimately failed.

Masa in Echo Park, Los Angeles, is a popular neighborhood pizzeria renowned for its Chicago-style deep-dish pizzas.

KTLA reports that a DoorDash customer by the name of “Dani B” ordered a pizza from Masa but later demanded a full refund after Dani B sent a picture of a severely burnt pizza, claiming it was inedible.

The owner of the restaurant, Rhonda Reynolds, immediately suspected something was wrong. Masa says it takes great care over its quality control, and delivering a blackened pizza would simply never be allowed to happen.

Suspecting the customer had manipulated the image, Reynolds ran it through an app called “Is This AI” in an attempt to verify its authenticity. The program returned a 96 percent certainty that it was AI, citing “unnatural lighting effects on the pizza crust and inconsistent shadow directions within the pizza box.” The image doesn’t appear to contain a SynthID invisible watermark.

To be extra certain, Reynolds reviewed security footage taken inside the pizzeria, which captured the customer’s order being made in the kitchen. The footage showed that at the moment it was taken out of the oven and put into a delivery box, it did not appear to be burned at all.

“It’s the principle because it’s our reputation [being attacked] and it’s what we do here,” Reynolds tells KTLA.

“They went back and checked the footage,” says Jose Tapa, the employee who boxed the order. “The box clearly says the name of the person with the order number and [the pizza] was not what it was in their picture.”

A refund was initially sent to the scammer, but Reynolds successfully fought the claim and the refund was reversed.

Reynolds tells KTLA that this is the first time she’s ever encountered a scam involving AI tools. She worries that as AI tools become more advanced, small businesses like hers could increasingly fall victim to them and the scammers using them.

“We’ve been here 22 years and we’re considered a busy restaurant,” she adds. “It’s a struggle, that if this is a new frontier, we’re not gonna make it through to the other side if all of our time is spent [dealing with this]. So in that respect, it’s scary.”

Thierry Henry reveals how Olise chose France over England and explains why Bayern star is a "genius"

📅 Nov 12, 2026  |  Category: Other  |  Read Source →

Arsenal and France legend Thierry Henry got to know Michael Olise well in 2024, when he coached the French team at the Paris Olympics.

The Bayern Munich star could have opted to represent England, who, according to Henry, wanted to include him in their squad for the 2024 European Championship.

However, the 24-year-old had made up his mind to represent France instead.

“He convinced himself! That's what he did,” Henry said on the “Kampo” podcast.

“Michael called me every day about going to the Olympics, even though England wanted him for the European Championship. If he had said he wanted to play for England, he would have gone. I called him to ask if he wanted to come (to the Olympics). He told me: 'I'm there.'”

Henry went on to describe Olise's desire to play for the French national team as “extraordinary”.

“Michael Olise has loved the French national team ever since he was a little boy,” he added.

It didn't take long for the 49-year-old to realise that Olise, who has since earned 29 senior caps for France, was a special talent.

“Michael is different because he thinks differently. At some point, you shouldn't try to understand geniuses,” Henry said.

“Michael has this thing about him where you find yourself asking: ‘Why did you do that?’" Henry continued.

“It's very, very rare to see a player with such technical quality going forward who also wins back so many balls – even more than the defensive midfielder. Sometimes you even feel like telling him: 'Stop running. Save some energy for when we're attacking.' But he's a team player.

“As a coach, what are you supposed to tell him? Absolutely nothing. Would you tell a painter how to paint? Why hold back a genius?”

Olise and France face Italy in the UEFA Nations League on Thursday night.
