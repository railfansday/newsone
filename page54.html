Chanel Chance Eau Splendide Is My Favorite of All the Chance Perfumes. Here’s Why I Love It So Much

📅 Nov 19, 2026  |  Category: Entertainment  |  Read Source →

Allow me to make the case for wearing it year-round.

My mom’s Chanel No.5, a fixture in our bathroom cabinet, was the first perfume I ever knew by name. It’s sophisticated and timeless, much like most of the other best-of-the-best Chanel fragrances that have been released since No.5’s 1921 debut. And while I have nothing but respect for the classics, I’ll admit that some of the brand’s more traditional floral fragrances aren’t necessarily what I reach for on a regular basis. I’m usually more interested in something a little fruitier, fresher, and more playful.

Enter Chanel Chance Eau Splendide, the purple-hued 2025 addition to the brand’s beloved Chance fragrance collection. With a sparkling raspberry opening, a heart of rose geranium, and a soft, musky base, it promises a bright, youthful take on the classic Chanel scent profile. And as someone who spends a considerable amount of time sniffing new perfumes for work, I know things are serious when they earn an immediate permanent spot on my vanity.

After attending the launch event for Eau Splendide last April, I didn’t intend to make it my new go-to... but that’s exactly what happened. I got an early sample, started wearing it, and simply noticed that I was reaching for it nearly everyday. I quietly fell in love with it as it sneakily became one of my signature scents. Now, after wearing it pretty consistently for a year and a half, here’s my honest review of Chanel Chance Eau Splendide Eau de Parfum, including what it actually smells like on skin, how it wears throughout the day, and whether it’s worth the luxury price tag. Ahead, everything to know before adding it to your fragrance collection.

First things first: Chanel Chance Eau Splendide is a fruity floral, with a much brighter opening than you might expect from a traditional Chanel perfume. I’m not exactly surprised that I love it so much, given that fruity florals make up about 90 percent of my perfume collection.

The fragrance features a raspberry top note that gives it a juicy, slightly tart fruity sweetness. But rather than leaning into the saccharine, sugary territory of some fruity perfumes, Eau Splendide then introduces rose geranium as a heart note, which adds a fresh, rosy, and surprising-but-subtle minty moment. To me, this is how you construct a perfect fruity floral: Before it gets too sweet (and for me, headache-y), some fresh floral notes balance everything out.

But that’s not all: As the fragrance dries down, you’ll experience base notes of iris, cedar, and white musk that make for a soft finish. To me, it results in a scent that feels elegant, feminine, and sophisticated—very Chanel—without being particularly heavy.

Splendide is a fresh and light scent, but don’t think that means it’s not staying put on your skin throughout the day. It usually lasts about four to six hours on me, so while it’s certainly not the longest-lasting fragrance I own, about four spritzes of it gets me through most of the day.

With so many great Chanel fragrances, how does this stand up against the rest? If I had to describe the overall vibe, I’d say that Chance Eau Splendide makes me feel effortlessly put together, but it also has a playful streak to it. I smell the raspberry most prominently on my skin, though, as it gets later in the day, I also notice the iris and musk. Even though I have dozens of fruity floral perfumes, it’s different than any of the others in my fragrance wardrobe.

The first time I spritzed it, I did wonder if it was going to be too sweet for me. Ultimately, I found that the raspberry and rose geranium mix well together and don’t feel too sugary whatsoever. I also love how youthful it is, especially compared to some of the other Chanel fragrances out there. It’s super flirty, vibrant, and fresh, making it the perfect companion for daytime meetings, date nights, and more—I even wore it to Coachella this year. All in all, the one word I’d use to describe it is approachable. Is it the most complex perfume I’ve ever sniffed? No. But the scent, the vibe, and the longevity make it perfect for anyone from a first-time Chanel fragrance wearer (I think it’s a great introduction to the brand) to a fruity floral fanatic. For me, it’s an everyday bottle that takes me from day to night all year long.

On that last point: I can see why someone might call it a spring or summer perfume, given the notes. But there’s no rule that says you have to put away your fruity florals when temperatures drop. This is the kind of fragrance that works whenever I’m craving something bright and fresh.

Okay, so is Chanel Chance Eau Splendide actually worth the splurge? It’ll definitely be a re-purchase for me, and not just because it comes in a pretty purple bottle.

Here’s my case for giving it a chance—yes, pun intended. The juicy raspberry opening is playful without veering into candy territory, while the other notes give it that effortlessly polished Chanel feel. It’s the kind of fruity floral that doesn’t need to be retired the second sweater weather arrives. I say we let this one live its best life all 12 months of the year.

Of course, Chanel prices are Chanel prices (even the smallest bottle is $115), so I’d recommend giving it a test spray before committing to a full bottle—especially if you typically gravitate toward super-sweet gourmands or bold, spicy fragrances. But that being said, I’ve been wearing it regularly for well over a year and I’m nowhere near the end of my bottle, so it’ll last you a while. For anyone who loves a fresh, feminine scent with a little personality, Eau Splendide is definitely worth a sniff. That said, since it’s such an easy-to-wear, clean-meets-powdery-meets-sweet scent, I’d consider it a pretty safe blind buy if that sounds like the type of perfume you generally gravitate toward.

I’ve worn this to weddings, concerts, dinners... You name it, you can wear this perfume there. She’s effortlessly versatile, isn’t overpowering, and isn’t a snob—even though she’s polished, there’s no intimidation factor, and she doesn’t take herself too seriously. She’s a must-have for anyone, whether you’re newly dabbling in fragrance or someone who already swears by a classic fruity floral. Consider this review my formal petition to add it to a list of all-time best year-round scents.

Catharine Malzahn is a contributing beauty writer at Cosmo who covers all things beauty, including fragrance. For this story, she shared her experience wearing the Chanel Chance Eau Splendide Eau de Parfum, one of her go-to perfumes for year-round freshness.

DWTS’ Val Chmerkovskiy Makes Spicy Comment About Jenna Johnson's Exit

Dancing With the Stars pro Val Chmerkovskiy expressed his support for wife Jenna Johnson following their back-to-back eliminations by giving an NSFW shoutout to his “baddie” wife.

Val Chmerkovskiy and Jenna Johnson may not be adding another Mirrorball to their household, but they're still having a ball at home.

A day after Johnson and her partner Harry Shum Jr. were voted off Dancing With the Stars following his and his partner Jenna Dewan’s elimination the week prior, Chmerkovskiy showed his support for his wife with a steamy comment.

“Keep the trophy’s [sic],” the 40-year-old shared on his Instagram Stories Nov. 18 alongside a photo of Johnson from a red carpet event. “I get to watch this baddie brush her teeth naked every morning.” Chmerkovskiy—who shares son Rome, 3, with his wife of seven years—also thirsted after her by adding a drool emoji.

Johnson and Shum were shockingly sent home on the Nov. 17 semifinals after receiving perfect scores 30 out of 30 for both of their dances: a paso doble to Rihanna's "We Found Love” and a quickstep to Amy Winehouse's “Valerie,” in which Shum paid tribute to late Glee costar Naya Rivera.

While some believe viewers made a major misstep by sending the pair home, the duo slammed the negativity they said they received online, sharing they think it impacted their relationship with the rest of the season 35 cast.

“I hope to get to know them more,” Shum told E! News in an interview following their elimination Nov. 17, “because it's just been so chaotic.”

“We're a bit all trauma bonded,” Johnson chimed in, to which Shum added, “There's a lot of trauma bond just because this year has been a little crazy.”

“Social media is a little nuts,” he continued. “I hope they address that at a certain point, because I think there's a good amount of bullying that doesn't feel really good, and I think the rest of the cast feels it.”

Though Shum didn’t go into specifics, he and Johnson were spun into drama after Chmerkovskiy received a one-week suspension from the show following his now-infamous reaction to a critique judge Carrie Ann Inaba made after one of Johnson and Shum’s performances, in which he referenced Will Smith’s 2022 Oscar slap.

Shum seemingly referred to the fallout as he praised Johnson after they were voted off.

“I want to say you have been dealing with everything with grace, with the noise outside,” the Grey’s Anatomy star told his partner on air, “and teaching me the things that I have only dreamed of to learn, and this has been one of the most beautiful experiences. So, thank you so much.”

As DWTS prepares to tango into its season 35 finale, read on for a deep dive into the secrets of the ballroom…

How Much Do the Celebrities Get Paid to Be on Dancing With the Stars?

Citing multiple sources, Variety reported in 2019 that Dancing With the Stars contestants make $125,000 for the rehearsal period and first two weeks of the show. If they progress beyond that point, the outlet continued, they earn more money each week. At the time, sources told the publication stars could earn a maximum of $295,000.

However, Bobby Bones said he made more than this when he won season 27 with Sharna Burgess in 2018.

"That show pays OK," the radio personality said on a Sept. 2025 episode of Jason Tartick's podcast Trading Secrets. "Like, first episode, no money. Second episode, $10,000. I think it's like, $10,000, $10,000, $20,000, $20,000. It ends up being $50,000 an episode if you last."

Also receiving a base salary of around $110,000, Bones continued, "I ended up making close to $400,000 from that show."

ABC has not publicly confirmed any of these figures.

What Do the Pros Get Paid on Dancing With the Stars?

Similarly, little has been shared publicly about how much the pros get paid. But as with the contestants, it seems like the longer they're on the show, the more money they can make.

But even if a pro is eliminated in the first round, they're not leaving the ballroom empty-handed.

"You're guaranteed until a certain amount of weeks," Jenna Johnson said on a June 2025 episode of Kelly Stafford and Hank Winchester's podcast The Morning After. "I think there's different contracts though. I can't speak for everybody."

Do Pros Get Paid More If They Win Dancing With the Stars?

While Johnson—who won season 26 with Olympic figure skater Adam Rippon, as well as season 33 with The Bachelor's Joey Graziadei—says the pros don't get a larger paycheck if they take home what is now called the Len Goodman Mirrorball Trophy, she noted there's still an incentive to make it to the finale (besides bragging rights, of course).

"If you make it all the way to the end, you're getting paid the whole season, which is amazing, and you get a bonus on top of that for making it to the finale," she explained on The Morning After. "If you win, it's not like you win a $1 million and split it with your partner. You're just getting a cute trophy together."

Do the Troupe Members Get Paid as Much as the Pros on Dancing With the Stars?

On a 2022 episode of Trading Secrets, Lindsay Arnold recalled how her salary was cut "more than in half" when she was demoted from pro to troupe member.

How Are Pros and Celebrities Paired Together on Dancing With the Stars?

The pros get little input when it comes to being matched with a celebrity.

"You get no say," Lindsay said on a May 2025 episode of Maggie Sellers' Hot Smart Rich podcast. "It's very much just, 'Here's your partner. Make it work.'"

In fact, Jenna said the pairing is often a secret until the last minute.

"They really want to keep it a secret until you meet them live," Jenna said on The Morning After. "They want that genuine reaction. So I think people always think we know who we have and we're keeping it a secret. Absolutely not. They do not tell us. They really don't even want us to know the cast. It gets leaked a lot, but they want it very hush-hush until you walk in and meet your partner."

As for what the Dancing With the Stars team looks for when making these matches?

"It's based on height, build and personality and compatibility," former showrunner Rob Wade told E! News in 2015. "We don't pair people who aren't going to get on. It's too intense. It's not like The Bachelor or something, we couldn't do that. That would just be miserable experience for the celebrity, for us and the viewer. You don't want to see two people who don't like each other and, quite frankly, we have made that mistake pairing people up who didn't get on so well."

How Often Do the Pros and Celebrities Practice on Dancing With the Stars?

Put simply, a lot."Every day we have four-hour rehearsals," Rylee Arnold, who was partnered with Olympic gymnast Stephen Nedoroscik on season 33, shared on a September 2024 episode of the Lightweights Podcast With Joe Vulpis. "It’s either 9:00 a.m. to 1:00 p.m., 1:00 p.m. to 5:00 p.m. or 5:00 p.m to 9:00 p.m."But the work doesn't stop once the rehearsal wraps. Rylee noted she might then meet with producers or the creative team, work on choreography or study dance videos."Literally my whole life is devoted to it," she continued, "but it's my passion and it's what I love. So, it makes me so happy."Indeed, it's a no-days-off kind of gig."We have our show day on Tuesday and then it's Wednesday to Sunday, straight rehearsals," Rylee added. "And then Monday we have camera blocking and then Tuesday's show day again."

Do the Pros Get to Pick the Songs Each Week for Their Dance With Their Celebrity Partner?

"Mostly yes," Emma Slater and Britt Stewart revealed in a September 2025 Instagram video, "though it's a collaboration with producers."

What's the Process Like for Making the Costumes for Dancing With the Stars?

If you thought the quickstep was fast, just wait until you hear about the pace of the costume department."We meet with the set and lighting designers, dancers and talent and create a story [for each pair]," costume designer Daniela Gschwendtner told TV Insider back in 2017. "Then we sketch out ideas. We have five days, max, to make all the outfits. That’s half a day per costume, not including all the rhinestones. The fitting and trimming we do later. We have about 20 people in our department and then we have a separate tailor shop. It’s a big enterprise."In fact, costume designer Steven Norman Lee said pairs usually try on their costumes for the first time just hours before showtime. And while the department "might use a pair of pants again for the boys," he continued, everything is generally made custom each week.As Gschwendtner added, "We do reuse things for group numbers or promo shoots, but not for the competition. We don’t reuse things unless there’s a specific reason to do so. We try to keep everybody fresh and new in something different every week, so it stays interesting."

Lance Broadway, a former Major League Baseball pitcher-turned-actor who appeared in Marvel’s “Agents of S.H.I.E.L.D.,” has died at 43.

Broadway was found dead on Nov. 17 in an apartment complex stairwell in Dallas, Texas, according to the Dallas County Medical Examiner. His cause of death is pending autopsy results.

Broadway was a pitcher who played for the Chicago White Sox and New York Mets. He made his feature film debut in 2013’s “Olympus Has Fallen” as Secret Service Agent O’Neill under President Benjamin Asher (Aaron Eckhart). The 10 Best Songs on the 'GTA 6' Soundtrack, Ranked

📅 Nov 19, 2026  |  Category: Entertainment  |  Read Source →

To say there’s been a lot of hype and anticipation around Grand Theft Auto VI is like the understatement to end all understatements. Grand Theft Auto V came out more than 13 years ago, and it was pretty mind-blowing at the time, as was Grand Theft Auto IV about five and a half years before that. The series has always been praised for the freedom it gives you, and lots of that freedom has also made the series controversial, but the last three games have done a whole lot more than just feel like mindless fun.

They’ve been giant steps forward technologically while also dealing with some more dramatic things narratively. You get the best of all the worlds, because much of the gameplay is still heightened and you can keep on causing mayhem if you want to. Anyway, that’s skipping over a lot, and it’s not even touching on the music, but music has always been a big part of these games, even going back to the GTA games of the PS2/original Xbox era. And GTA 6 is no exception, having an impressive official soundtrack (34 original songs made for the game itself), and a score by Justice… and then, of course, all the licensed music. There is so much licensed music heard on the radio stations in this game, so highlighting some of the best songs from that huge soundtrack is only scratching the surface, but these are indeed some of the greatest tracks you can listen to – mostly when you're in a (probably stolen) car – while playing GTA 6.

On the nose, sure, but if you’ve played a GTA game, you're surely aware that it’s not always the most subtle series in the world. That’s especially so for the satire, and while this writer hasn’t played enough of the most recent game to compare the humor to the previous games, many of those previous games took a South Park approach to comedy, satirizing/parodying pretty much everything that could be a decent target.

So, it’s on-the-nose. And speaking of, there’s a song on the GTA 6 V-Rock FM station called “Lawyers, Guns, and Money,” and at least two of those things are very relevant to a game where you're stealing stuff and doing acts of violence. It’s a great song on its own (Warren Zevon has a deserved cult following, but probably deserves even more popularity), and it fits the mood of driving around GTA 6 remarkably well.

This one is also a bit on-the-nose, or maybe obvious, and then further, it is a somewhat cheesy song. But… GTA 6 is a love story, given you can switch between playing two characters who are in a relationship (one with its ups and downs, sure, but that relationship is a central point of the whole game). They're Jason and Lucia, rather than Jack and Diane, but “Jack & Diane” is a broad, sweeping sort of song that really works with GTA 6’s main characters and then also, a little more abstractly, the setting/style.

The game probably elevates the song here, since in some contexts, this John Mellencamp track can feel like it lays it on a bit thick in a bad way. Maybe you just have to be in a certain mood to really appreciate it or vibe with it, but the mood can quite easily be felt while playing GTA 6, and hey, if a game or movie makes a song better, and the song sounds great as part of the soundtrack, why not include it for a ranking like this one?

If not “Pink Pony Club,” then Chappell Roan’s signature song to date is probably “Hot to Go!”, even if it might not even be her best song. It’s a big one, though. And it is fun. It’s a good fit for Flash FM, which is the modern (or fairly modern, maybe like 21st-century) pop radio station for GTA 6. Something a little moodier like “Good Luck, Babe!” might not have fit the vibe quite as well.

It’s pretty wild how good The Rise and Fall of a Midwest Princess is, as an album, that “Hot to Go!” is surrounded by a handful of songs that are even hotter. Also, seeing songs this recent is pretty great, just for the GTA 6 soundtrack generally, since the game was in development so long that there might've been something of a fear of things (music included) feeling dated by the time the game eventually came out.

It’s risky taking a song that was made for another property and putting it in something new, but it can be done, as “If You Leave” and its inclusion in GTA 6 shows. Like, this one isn't just associated with the 1986 movie Pretty in Pink because that movie happened to use the song well, but the song itself was made for that movie’s soundtrack, not appearing on an Orchestral Manoeuvres in the Dark studio album first.

Confusingly, the song “Pretty in Pink” was not written for the movie, but the movie was named after that Psychedelic Furs song. Also, that movie’s soundtrack is much better than the movie (it also features tracks by the likes of New Order, Echo & the Bunnymen, and The Smiths). That’s thankfully not the case with GTA 6. It’s a great game that just so happens to also have a great soundtrack, thanks to songs like “If You Leave.”

Now for something a bit more recent, “Lost…” well, actually, it’s not so recent that it couldn’t have technically snuck onto the GTA 5 soundtrack. It’s one of the highlights off Frank Ocean’s 2012 album Channel Orange, and GTA 5 came out in 2013, so it could've potentially just snuck on. It’s also one of those cases where it might not be the artist’s very best song, but it’s up there, and it’s also one of the most appropriate for inclusion on a GTA soundtrack.

It would be awesome to blast “Pyramids,” sure, but that song’s 10 minutes long, and you're not often going to be in a car for that long, since you'll probably get distracted by something on foot or hijack another vehicle or something in that time. And there are plenty of great songs from Blonde, but that’s more of a full album-listening experience, meaning songs plucked from there and put onto a more fractured soundtrack might not hit quite the same.

This one feels a little special, since you do hear “Drive” very early on as a needle drop, like right when you're supposed to drive to a location. Again, it’s a bit on-the-nose, but it does feel like a spontaneous and cinematic moment. The drive that you do when “Drive” is playing isn't even that wild or anything, but it’s the first time you'll be driving at night, and it is quite a quietly powerful experience.

It’s the right song for that mood, and that makes it stand out a bit more, but hey, a soundtrack choice is a soundtrack choice. Oh, and the band is called The Cars, in case a song called “Drive” being in a game called “Grand Theft Auto 6” wasn’t already beating you over the head too much. Still, a great moment is a great moment, and this song is also just a flat-out great one, so all’s fair here.

Yes, yes, lots of pop and alternative/rock kinds of songs here, and those are the songs that sound best with the style and world of GTA 6, but there is a wider variety of music found here, as is the case with all the GTA games and their radio stations. The Chamber 106.6 has some heavier stuff, like metal and a bit of punk, with Metallica (admittedly about as popular as metal bands get) being well-represented.

There are three Metallica songs that can be heard on The Chamber 106.6, and “Master of Puppets” is probably the most exciting one to hear when you're driving at a very high speed, and especially so if you're driving fast and away from a bunch of cops. It makes an already vibrant and energetic game feel more so, and it’s also a hard-to-fault song in general, so it’s a great fit for GTA 6, obviously.

It’s weird to think about “Electric Feel” feeling nostalgic, since it came out around the same time as Grand Theft Auto 4, and that game is only two GTAs ago. Maybe time flies. Anyway, “Electric Feel” was popular, sure, but it also felt like it was just cool enough to be alternative, but then it was also punchy enough to not feel out of place on mainstream radio, so if you liked any kind of music about 18 or 19 years ago, there’s a good chance you dug this one.

As for other MGMT songs? Yeah, they're a good band, or duo, technically. There’s some good stuff beyond just the songs that became surprising hits.

And it has that synthy feel that makes it feel appropriate with GTA 6 and its Vice City (and beyond) setting. Sure, not as 1980s and synthy in the way that the ‘80s-set GTA: Vice City was, way back when that came out, but the whole location has clearly retained aspects of that era. As for other MGMT songs? Yeah, they're a good band, or duo, technically. There’s some good stuff beyond just the songs that became surprising hits, especially with the slightly more recent – and overall underrated – album Little Dark Age.

“This Is the Day” best and most succinctly lays out the appeal of the artist behind it. Said artist is The The, which has always been a headache of a band name, though it’s one of those bands that’s really just one guy (named Matt Johnson). Everyone else comes and goes, a bit like a karma chameleon or something. Anyway, The The isn't a household name, but “This Is the Day” is fairly well-known, partly because it was heard on the Guardians of the Galaxy Vol. 3 soundtrack, and now it’s made its way into GTA 6 as well.

It’s one of a handful of lengthy and emotionally complicated songs found on the pretty much perfect album Soul Mining, with this track in particular striking such an admirably unique tone. It sounds a bit different depending on how you're feeling when you hear it, which is always a great quality for a song to have. If you hear it on Vice City FM (which is probably the best radio station in the game) while driving when the sun’s coming up in game, it always feels great, not to mention truly cinematic.

“Just Like Heaven” comes from what might be The Cure’s most sprawling – and perhaps messiest – album, Kiss Me, Kiss Me, Kiss Me. “Messy” isn't bad, though; it’s more just that it’s eclectic to the point of being a little inconsistent, which is something that’s harder to say about some of the band’s other albums from the 1980s. But “Just Like Heaven” is a highlight, and so it making the cut for the GTA 6 soundtrack is cool (if you could only pick one Kiss Me, Kiss Me, Kiss Me track).

That album definitely has value alone for housing this song. “Just Like Heaven” does sound like that, being a truly euphoric pop song, maybe a little alternative, but not as alternative as most songs by The Cure. They went broad here, and they did it well (the same can be said for songs that aren’t found on any GTA 6 radio station, like “Boys Don’t Cry,” “Lovesong,” and “In Between Days”).

Florida Set To Execute 71-Year-Old Man Who Claims He Was Framed For Killing Pregnant Woman, Husband

📅 Nov 19, 2026  |  Category: Other  |  Read Source →

Florida is gearing up for a record-breaking 20th execution this year ... but the man set to be put to death Thursday evening has spent decades claiming he was framed for the brutal murders of a pregnant woman and her husband.

Thomas Mitchell Overton, 71, is scheduled to be executed by lethal injection right now at Florida State Prison ... more than 35 years after Michael and Susan "Missy" MacIvor were found murdered inside their Florida Keys home.

And here's the twist ... Overton has insisted for years that he didn't commit the murders, claiming police planted the DNA evidence used to convict him. His attorneys have also raised some questions about how that evidence was handled.

The case dates back to August 22, 1991, when Michael, 30, and Susan, 29, were found dead inside their home in Tavernier, Florida. She was eight months pregnant at the time.

According to court documents, Michael had been hit in the head and strangled, with masking tape covering his face. Susan was found bound with tape and strangled in the couple's bedroom, and investigators determined she had also been sexually assaulted. The couple's unborn baby also died.

Investigators say they discovered the home's telephone wires had been cut and a ladder was positioned against the balcony.

The murders went unsolved for years ... until investigators connected Overton to the crime through DNA evidence.

According to court records, investigators recovered semen from the couple's bedding and developed a DNA profile in 1993, but didn't have a suspect to match it to.

That changed in 1996, when investigators arrested Overton in an unrelated burglary case. Although he initially refused to provide a blood sample, investigators eventually obtained a court order to collect his blood for testing. Two separate laboratories then reported DNA results linking him to the semen found at the crime scene.

But Overton has always insisted he was innocent.

During his original sentencing proceedings, he told the judge, "I didn't commit the crime." But he didn't present evidence that could have helped him avoid the death penalty because he said he believed his conviction would eventually be overturned on appeal and didn't want to put his family and friends through the ordeal.

In his appeal to the Florida Supreme Court, Overton's attorneys argued that more DNA testing could potentially clear his name or identify someone else involved in the murders.

His defense also claimed police planted the semen evidence to frame him ... and pointed to some unusual details about how investigators handled the evidence.

Overton's lawyers also argued the serologist assigned to the case took crime scene evidence home, stored biological samples in his refrigerator, and even drove a mattress pad to a psychic for an inspection.

Overton's attorneys also pushed to have hairs found on tape that was used to bind Susan tested for DNA, arguing the hairs didn't appear to match Overton or either victim and could potentially point to the true perpetrator of the crime.

Despite those arguments, the Florida Supreme Court rejected his challenges, finding the defense hadn't established that the evidence was tampered with or that the additional testing justified relief.

Overton was convicted in 1999 of two counts of first-degree murder, killing an unborn child, burglary, and sexual battery. He was sentenced to death.

Now, more than three decades later, Overton is set to become the 20th person executed in Florida this year ... breaking the state's previous record of 19 executions in 2025.
