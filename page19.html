Liverpool 4-0 Man Utd: VAR Expert Casts Verdict on Leny Yoro’s Red Card

📅 Nov 22, 2026  |  Category: Sports  |  Read Source →

Leny Yoro's sending off in Manchester United's 4-0 defeat to Liverpool came in controversial fashion, and VAR expert Dale Johnson has weighed in on whether referee Sam Barrott got the decision right.

Regardless of the red card, this was a pitiful performance from United and the worst possible start for Mauricio Pochettino. He watched his side capitulate within eight minutes when Matthijs de Ligt, strangely handed a start, got caught out and Yoro raced back before barging into Alexander Isak.

Yoro sent Isak to the turf just outside the box, and Barrott deemed this to have been a red card offence due to the French defender being the last man. Most United fans aren't using the dismissal as an excuse for their team's fifth league defeat of the season, but many are questioning whether the challenge warranted such punishment.

Isak was played through on goal and the Liverpool striker took a heavy touch, sending the ball a fair distance away from him when Yoro made the challenge. VAR didn't feel the need to intervene and potentially downgrade the red card to a caution.

Johnson delved into Barrott's decision and explained that the referee had four key considerations to make over Yoro denying an obvious goalscoring opportunity. These are "the distance between the offence and the goal", "the general direction of play", "the likelihood of keeping or gaining control of the ball" and "the location and number of defenders and attackers".

Three of those factors were satisfied because Isak ran directly towards goal in a central position and United had no covering defender. The main question is the "likelihood of keeping or gaining control of the ball."

United will argue that Yoro's contact came after Isak's touch and therefore there is no case that the challenge caused the player to over-run the ball. Johnson suggested in his BBC Sport article that Barrott and VAR John Brooks had to assess whether Isak would definitely have got to the ball ahead of goalkeeper Senne Lammens.

Johnson added: "And that if he had, it would have been a good scoring chance. If there is any doubt, then it should not be a red card. It is a borderline case, and as such the VAR would not be expected to intervene for a clear and obvious error."

United were put to the sword thereafter when Dominik Szoboszlai scored the opener with the resulting free-kick (10'). Florian Wirtz added a second (13') before Rio Ngumoha (66') and Victor Munoz (83') sealed an emphatic victory for Andoni Iraola's men.

There were differing opinions on the red card amongst Sky Sports pundits as United legends Gary Neville and Roy Keane disagreed. Neville defended Barrott's decision and tore into Yoro for a "clumsy" challenge and "poor anticipation".

Keane, however, felt Yoro could feel hard done by. He was "amazed" to see De Ligt fail to read the flight of the ball, arguing that Isak's heavy touch meant he wasn't going to regain possession:

"Isak has done well, but I actually don't think he's going to get the ball... He's not getting the ball. There's no way he's getting the ball. It's clumsy from United, it's one straight ball over the top. You shouldn't be in so much trouble, but there's no way he's getting that ball. Absolutely not."

Manchester United legend was less than impressed after the final whistle against Liverpool

This is the second time this season Yoro has been sent off, compounding a miserable afternoon for United. Their loss to Liverpool leaves them sitting 19th after 11 games played, and they will be without the Frenchman for Brentford's visit to Old Trafford on Saturday unless they successfully appeal the decision.

Forget Plain Bacon: Add This Crispy Ingredient To Grilled Cheese For Sweet & Smoky Flavor

📅 Nov 22, 2026  |  Category: Other  |  Read Source →

Plain old bacon can elevate even the plainest grilled cheese, but it tends to be a bit one-note, contributing mainly salty, crispy protein. However, if you add candied bacon instead, that extra sweetness can transform a wide variety of cheeses and breads, especially if you take the time to prepare it with extra seasonings.

Candied bacon is a delicate recipe, but if you manage to make it without burning the sugars, you get an incomparably delicious ingredient for grilled cheese. The syrupy sweetness helps cut through the sodium, whether from aged parmesan or bread crusted with garlic salt, and the texture provides a nice contrast to gooey cheese. The smokiness, in particular, is a great flavoring agent that you can easily control by increasing or reducing the amount of bacon you layer on. The end result is a nice middle ground between turning grilled cheese into a dessert and keeping it exclusively savory.

Candied bacon can take a hot minute to prepare and cook, and while it only lasts about a week in the fridge, it stays good in the freezer for up to six months. Since all you have to do is let it thaw, then zap it in the air fryer for a few minutes at 380 degrees Fahrenheit, it's a superbly easy accoutrement to keep on hand for when you want something a little special.

The best part of using candied bacon is that while it may close a few flavor doors, it opens up so many more. You may not want to pair it with an herbed goat cheese or wine-veined cheddar, but it can substantially upgrade many of the tastiest underrated cheeses into something spectacular.

When considering what to add to your new sandwich, try to think about the types of cheeses you'd find in desserts. Something creamy and high in fat provides fantastic texture and an aromatic backdrop of flavor but still lets the candied bacon take center stage. Brie, Camembert, and mascarpone are all sublime with a bit of sugar, but when balanced with the savoriness of cured bacon, they become even better. Any pale, rich variety should do the job well.

You should always use a variety of cheeses in your grilled cheese, and a dash of something sharp and aged can balance out the powerful flavors of candied bacon. Microplaning some aged Manchego or provolone on top diversifies the flavors without overpowering them, and the extra-fine texture helps even harder cheeses melt more evenly. While you don't want these to be the main characters of your recipe, a bit of added nuttiness and earthiness can bring out the best in your bacon, even if you only add a tablespoon or two.

Here's when a Raspberry Pi 5 could be considered overkill

📅 Nov 22, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

The ongoing memory shortage has led to price increases across the tech industry, even affecting historically affordable computers like the Raspberry Pi. In early November 2026, Raspberry Pi CEO Eben Upton announced that the latest Raspberry Pi 5's 16GB variant was getting an $80 price hike. At $385 for the 16GB Pi 5, it's safe to say that the platform is no longer as affordable as it once was. The Pi 5's 2GB, 4GB and 8GB models saw price increases earlier in 2026 too.

But if you're looking to buy a Raspberry Pi today, you don't have to limit yourself to the newest Pi 5 board. Depending on your intended use case, you could save significant money by buying an older model. The Pi 4, in particular, is still a capable platform with plenty of power for both light desktop use and the typical projects you would run on a Pi. And you're not just saving on the board itself — the Pi 4 has more modest power and cooling requirements than the latest model.

The Raspberry Pi 5 is more than twice as powerful as the previous-generation Pi 4, but you'll only notice that difference in the real world if you use it as your sole computer. The Raspberry Pi 4's slower CPU means it's not as well-suited for web browsing or heavy workloads. But if you're planning to use the board as a springboard for hobbies like self-hosting, home automation or robotics, you're unlikely to find the Pi 4's performance lacking.

Unfortunately, the higher-end Raspberry Pi 4 models have also seen substantial price increases over the past year or so. Still, they remain slightly cheaper than the Pi 5 and more widely available. The Pi 4's official starting price is just $35 for the 1GB model, the same as the original Raspberry Pi that was released in 2012.

The Pi 4's less performant CPU also saves you money on power and cooling. It only needs a 15W USB adapter, which is far more common than the 27W USB-C PD adapter that the Pi 5 required. Additionally, the older Pi 4 runs cooler, and you don't need to add a cooling fan to the bill of materials for most projects. However, the Raspberry Pi 5 tends to exceed 176 degrees Fahrenheit (80 degrees Celsius) after a minute of heavy load. Without a cooling fan, the board then throttles the CPU to bring temperatures under control.

When you factor in the cost of a more capable power adapter and cooling hardware, the Pi 5 ends up costing more than the $45 starting price would have you believe. The Pi 4's CPU also benefits from cooling hardware, but it doesn't get hot as quickly and you can get away with a cheap, passive aluminum heatsink.

If you're willing to stomach the extra initial cost of a Raspberry Pi 5 and hunt for available stock, it's definitely the more future-proof option. The Pi 5's 1GB and 2GB RAM variants, in particular, are only slightly more expensive than the Pi 4, but whether those work for you depends on how much RAM you need from your Pi.

That said, the advantage to buying an older Raspberry Pi is availability. Many official retailers simply cannot keep the affordable Pi 5 models in stock, while you can easily buy a Pi 4 or 3B+ without waiting for a restock. The $35 Pi 4 is also available at Micro Center locations in the US, while you'd have to order the base model Pi 5 online and pay for shipping.

The used market is also flooded with older Pi 4 boards as people upgrade to either the latest model or a more powerful computer altogether. In my city, I see dozens of listings for Raspberry Pi 4 and 3B+ boards on Facebook Marketplace, all selling well below the current retail price. These deals often include power supplies, cases and other necessary accessories too.

Raspberry Pi boards are safe to buy second-hand as they're surprisingly durable. They don't have any moving parts and most people use them for relatively lightweight loads. Even if you use a Raspberry Pi as a media server, for instance, the board remains idle most of the time. With minimal power draw and heat generation, a Pi can easily last several years if not a decade. So buying a used Raspberry Pi isn't as much of a gamble as a pre-owned desktop.

Raspberry Pi's CEO has called the recent price increases a "difficult, but temporary" phase and committed to lowering prices when memory costs fall. However, memory manufacturers have warned of supply shortages extending into 2028 and beyond. Put simply, the latest Raspberry Pi may come down in cost anytime soon.

He played for Bolton Wanderers and Aston Villa - Then won Celebrity Big Brother

📅 Nov 22, 2026  |  Category: Business  |  Read Source →

Former Premier League star and Serbian international Sasa Curcic earned both fame in his home country and a substantial payout after becoming a reality TV star following his retirement from the game.

As professional footballers reach the end of their playing careers, many are left with difficult decisions over what to do with their futures. Even the luckiest of players only make it until their mid-to-late thirties in the professional game, and the question of what to do after playing contracts dry up is a very real one.

For some, coaching or scouting is an option, while for others there is a clear appeal to a post-playing career in the media. By taking these routes, former players can rely on the experience that they picked up over the decades during which they were involved in the game. Others enter into business, being able to invest as a result of their time in the game.

But for some players, what's missing is the roar of the crowd, and in the case of the former Aston Villa, Bolton Wanderers and Crystal Palace star Sasa Curcic, the former player found a whole new audience as a reality TV star once his playing days were over.

Sasa Curcic arrived in England having already impressed in his home city of Belgrade. Having first signed for OFK Beograd in 1991, he moved to Partizan Belgrade two years later, having already made his debut for the FR Yugoslavia — now Serbia & Montenegro — national team at the very start of his professional career.

In the summer of 1995, Bolton Wanderers had just been promoted to the Premier League and were looking to strengthen. Curcic was an obvious talent, so was signed by Bolton’s managerial team of Colin Todd and Roy McFarland as they sought to avoid a quick return to the second-tier.

Described by When Saturday Comes as "the greatest thriller at Burnden Park since the iconic Frank Worthington", Curcic was given a free role in the Bolton midfield, but he was unable to keep his team in the Premier League; they were relegated back in bottom place in the table.

But having tasted life in the Premier League with Bolton, Curcic wanted to stay there following the club's relegation, and at the start of the 1996-97 season he left for Aston Villa. It was a disastrous move. Curcic fell out with manager Brian Little, and he left the club in March 1998 for Crystal Palace with just 29 League appearances under his belt. The move from Bolton to Villa was later ranked by The Times as the 12th-worst in the first ten years of the Premier League.

But life at Selhurst Park proved to be no easier for him. He managed just 23 League appearances for Palace before leaving the club in 1999, with his spell in South London best-remembered for his protests against NATO bombing of Serbia while he was playing for Palace. After spells in Major League Soccer and in Scotland with Motherwell, he confirmed his retirement as a player in 2001.

Having picked up a total of 14 appearances for his national team, Curcic was still well-known in Serbia, and he capitalised on that when he entered the Celebrity Big Brother house for their version of the show in May 2007.

As one of thirteen contestants, Curcic proved that he was still able to get people on his side, and it was confirmed a month later that he'd won the competition, scooping a prize of €50,000 and earning himself some lucrative television commercial work as a result of it.

But fame wouldn't last for him. He left the set of another reality show called Farma — "The Farm" — after just a couple of weeks the following year and faded from view again, eventually resurfacing back in South London in 2015, forming an academy with connections to Crystal Palace. He also had a spell managing, back in his home country. Appointed into the head coach's position at the Belgrade-based club Borac Lazarevac in November 2021, he lasted six months in the position before leaving.

Always a bit of a wild child — Brian Little would later tell a few stories of the midfielder's eccentricities, including "hiring a double-decker booze bus with models for his Aston Villa teammates" — Curcic himself later admitted that moving to England in the first place had been a bit of a mistake: "My dream was to play for Partizan, my team. I should have stayed. I would have my own stand, I’d be manager."

It speaks volumes for the self-confidence that got him to the top of the game in England and his subsequent fall from grace that he continued to believe in the certainty that his career would have ended up so differently had he stayed in Serbia in the first place. And Bolton Wanderers fans of a certain age certainly still remember the role he played following their original return to the Premier League.

Mauricio Pochettino was immediately up against it in his first few days at this slow-motion car crash of a club - Man United's 4-0 loss at Liverpool showed how much he has to do: OLIVER HOLT

📅 2026-11-22  |  Category: Sports  |  Read Source →

Published: 14:08 EST, 22 November 2026 | Updated: 14:52 EST, 22 November 2026

A drone spied on his first training session, his star striker stayed up into the early hours obsessing over Grand Theft Auto the day he arrived, his best player has paused contract talks, his chief executive praised the Glazers and Sir Jim Ratcliffe, and the club banned four supporters for three years for throwing a tennis ball on to the pitch.

That was before the football started. And the fates had decreed that Mauricio Pochettino’s first game in charge as head coach of this misused, abused, diminished, fallen slow-motion car crash of a football club that was once Manchester United but which has lost all sense of its place in the modern football firmament was to be away to Liverpool at Anfield.

And then Pochettino picked a centre-half, Matthijs de Ligt, who had not started a Premier League game for more than a year. And De Ligt began the game by watching a long ball sail over his head as if it were a rare curiosity to be observed rather than to be dealt with and before you could blink, Leny Yoro had been sent off trying to clear up De Ligt’s mess.

So at least Pochettino got to see United set a new record. Yoro was sent off after seven minutes and 23 seconds and that has never been done more quickly by United in the league. So there was that. De Ligt went on, it is sad to report, to have such a dreadful game that it was tempting to watch it through your fingers. Not the best selection decision by the new boss.

And in the stands, Omar Berrada, the strangely bullish United chief executive, looked colder and colder and more and more pale in the chill and, beside him, Jason Wilcox, the director of football, slid deeper and deeper into his big coat until he nearly disappeared from view. Which, presumably, was what he was hoping to do.

Mauricio Pochettino was given a near-impossible task in his first game as manager of Manchester United

Leny Yoro (right) was sent off just minutes into their 4-0 defeat by rivals Liverpool at Anfield

The Reds dominated from then on, with teenage winger Rio Ngumoha one of the Reds' stars

And in the row behind them, Sir Alex Ferguson gazed down on what remained of the great club he had built. James Nesbitt, the esteemed actor who is Ferguson’s friend, sat next to him in the directors’ box. One of Nesbitt’s best performances was in The Missing. The United defence were starring in the remake.

And the Liverpool fans loved it. ‘You’re going down with the City,’ they sang at the United fans. ‘You’re getting sacked in the morning,’ they sang at Pochettino, as he stood, arms tightly folded on the touchline. When it got to 4-0, the Kop broke out another ditty. ‘Poch is at the wheel,’ they sang over and over again.

So, welcome to Manchester, Mauricio. Welcome to the sight of United consolidating their position one off the bottom of the Premier League. If they keep this going for another week, they will be in the bottom three of the top flight in December for the first time in more than 50 years. If Pochettino did not know it before, he knows it now: there is an awful lot of saving to be done on the red side of Manchester.

This is a relegation fight that United are in. Don’t kid yourself that it’s not. The last time they dropped out of the top flight in 1973-74, they won three games in their first 11 matches. This season, they have won just once in the first 11 and that was at home to Ipswich Town on August 30th. A lot of the teams around them have got something about them, too. They certainly have more spirit than this rabble.

In that context, it was not the best omen that this was the worst result in a United manager’s first game for more than 100 years. If Pochettino thought getting the USA to win the World Cup was a big ask, rebuilding United is up there with it. This is a club mired in dysfunction on and off the pitch. It is a Herculean task to rescue them and we are about to find out whether Pochettino has it in him.

It was strange seeing a new United manager anointed at Anfield as the crowd sang You’ll Never Walk Alone and the Kop flew its flags and the faces of Shankly, Paisley, Dalglish, Klopp and the rest stared down from the banner of all the bosses here who have won league titles. Pochettino stood on the sideline and drank it all in, right until kick off.

It was his misfortune that this is also a Liverpool team beginning to click. Florian Wirtz is beginning to express himself and that is a wonderful sight. Bradley Barcola and Alexander Isak are becoming a formidable partnership. Rio Ngumoha scared Diogo Dalot rigid. Liverpool are rising again as United flounder.

It did not take long for Pochettino to get his first look at the uselessness that has afflicted United for most of this season. Only seven minutes had gone when Ronald Araujo drove a searching ball over the top of the United defence. De Ligt watched it go and abrogated responsibility for it, expecting Yoro to cover. Isak sprinted after it and by the time Yoro finally reacted, it was too late. Isak was on to it.

Pochettino opted to start Matthijs de Ligt (right) - but he struggled throughout the game

Liverpool, meanwhile, are starting to click - and Florian Wirtz is at the centre of that rise

Isak got ahead of Yoro and pushed the ball forwards. It was a heavy touch and would have run on to Lammens but Yoro compounded his error by bundling Isak down on the edge of the area. He was sent off by referee Sam Barrott. Only seven minutes and 23secs had elapsed. It was the quickest sending off in United’s Premier League history. It was not a controversy. It was just a show of defensive incompetence.

A free-kick in that kind of position might as well be a penalty when Dominik Szoboszlai is standing over it. He lashed it low and hard past the edge of the United wall. Bruno Fernandes shook a half-interested leg at it. It whistled past him and bulged the corner of the United net.

Three minutes later, Liverpool were further ahead. Ngumoha embarrassed Dalot down the Liverpool left – not for the last time – and pulled a cross back for Wirtz. United were in chaos. Pochettino looked stunned. He brought on Harry Maguire for Grand Theft Auto aficionado Bryan Mbeumo after 22 minutes. Maybe it was a bitter pill for Mbeumo. Maybe it will have just allowed him to spend more time with his PlayStation. Practice makes perfect, after all.

There was frustration at Anfield. They wanted more than this. This was a chance to rub United’s noses in it, to inflict more misery on their hapless rivals. And yet, there was a sense they were letting them off the hook.

That feeling deepened when De Ligt made a dreadful mess of intercepting a lofted pass to Isak, missed his kick and fell over. Isak raced through but then blasted his shot hopelessly high and wide. Then Barcola turned De Ligt and hit another shot too high.

Liverpool could not keep missing all afternoon, though, and they finally got their third when Barcola skipped past Lisandro Martinez on the halfway line as if he wasn’t there and sprinted down the right. He squared the ball across the box to Ngumoha and Ngumoha slammed it unerringly past Lammens. Victor Munoz drilled home the fourth eight minutes from time and United’s humiliation was complete.
