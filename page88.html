How to get the Golden Scale in Zelda Ocarina of Time

📅 Nov 09, 2026  |  Category: Travel  |  Read Source →

The Golden Scale is a unique item in The Legend of Zelda: Ocarina of Time remake that allows Link to dive deeper. You can consider it an upgrade to the Silver Scale, an item that you obtain as young Link as you try to find Princess Ruto. However, unlike the Silver Scale, you can only get the Golden Scale after becoming adult Link.

If you would like to explore the depths of Hyrule and hunt for all the treasures hidden underwater, you should learn how to get the Golden Scale in the Ocarina of Time remake.

You obtain the Golden Scale by catching a fish longer than 60 cm in the Fishing Pond. To complete the challenge, you must travel south of Hyrule Field to Lake Hylia as adult Link. You will see that the lake is dry, and you won't be able to reach the Fishing Pond, located on the right side of the map, on your own. You will need to either ride a magic bean plant (that you need to plant as child Link next to the laboratory) or play the Scarecrow Song near the bridge at the front of the building, and then use the Hookshot on to it. Lake Hylia will also be refilled once you clear the Water Temple, so you can come back after that, too.

Once inside, talk to the Fishing-Pond Man and learn that you can earn a prize if "you catch a big enough fish." Rent a fishing rod for 30 rupees, allowing you to fish as many times as you want until you tell the Fishing-Pond Man that you're done. When preparing to cast your bait, we suggest that you aim at the middle of the pond where you can usually find big fish.

Big fish are more difficult to reel in because they resist for longer periods. Just be patient and keep moving the rod in the direction the fish is pulling. If you catch a fish that is smaller than 60 cm, you just need to toss it back and keep trying until you find the right fish.

However, if you get a fish that meets the requirements, keep it, and then go talk to the Fishing-Pond Man. Select the "I'm done" option and he will examine your fish, and you will receive the Golden Scale.

Zelda: Ocarina of Time’s Coolest Dungeon Hides a Secret Tribute to a Beloved Classic

📅 Nov 09, 2026  |  Category: Entertainment  |  Read Source →

Nintendo's highly anticipated remake of The Legend of Zelda: Ocarina of Time is finally here, and millions of players are reliving their childhood adventures or experiencing Link's journey for the very first time. Though the Nintendo 64 game has received a dramatic visual glow-up for the Switch 2, the remake meticulously preserves the spirit of the original — including an extended reference to a classic American novel that even Ocarina diehards may have missed the first time around.

[Ed. note: Mid-game spoilers ahead for The Legend of Zelda: Ocarina of Time. If you haven't retrieved the Master Sword yet, turn back now!]

After Link collects the three Spiritual Stones and returns to Hyrule Castle Town to claim the Master Sword, we move forward seven years in time. Link is now an adult, and in order to thwart Ganon's terrifying reign, he must awaken six sages throughout Hyrule by visiting their temples and claiming their power for his own. The first of these is located just outside Link's hometown of Kokiri Village, land of the fairy folk. Though most of his youthful pals don't recognize the grown-up Link, they urge him to investigate the Forest Temple and rescue their dear friend Saria.

When Link ventures into the Forest Temple, he discovers something altogether unexpected: an eerie, luxurious old mansion inhabited by ghosts. As he enters a central room flanked by colorful torches, four ghostly Poes appear to snatch away the flames. Venturing deeper into the mansion, Link discovers that these ghoulish gals are actually four sisters: Meg, Jo, Beth, and Amy.

Sound familiar? Those are the same names (and the same birth order) as the March sisters in Louisa May Alcott's novel Little Women. It's a timeless coming-of-age story about four sisters who have very different goals and ambitions in life, one that's been adapted countless times for stage and screen. The most recent adaptation is the 2019 film by Barbie and The Magician's Nephew director Greta Gerwig, which stars Saoirse Ronan, Florence Pugh, and Timothée Chalamet.

While you might be inclined to think this is some newfangled meddling with the localization, these were also the Poe Sisters' names in the Nintendo 64 original. (There's one exception: the second sister, Jo, was called "Joelle" in the 1998 game.) Naturally, there are plenty of "well actually" guys pointing this out in the comments of social media posts. But it's still a fun little Easter egg for players discovering it for the first time, whether or not they've played Ocarina of Time before.

Is Your Sodium Intake Impacting Your Hiking Performance? A Dietitian Explains Why It Matters.

📅 Nov 09, 2026  |  Category: Health  |  Read Source →

Take a look at the nutrition label on a freeze-dried backpacking meal, and you might be surprised by how much sodium it contains. Some meals pack more than 1,500 mg into a single pouch, roughly two-thirds of the recommended daily limit of 2,300 mg for most adults. That’s a lot for one dinner, but when you’re spending hours sweating your way up steep climbs, you’re also losing sodium along with water.

Sodium helps regulate fluid balance and supports normal nerve and muscle function. Yet there’s a contradiction in how you might think about it on the trail: you question the sodium in your freeze-dried dinner while adding electrolyte powder to your water bottle and snacking on pretzels, jerky, and cheese.

So, are you consuming too much sodium or not enough? The answer depends on what’s already in your food, how much you’re sweating, and how you’re replacing fluids.

You can pack nearly identical amounts of food and end up with dramatically different sodium intakes. I compared two realistic days of backpacking food, each containing commercial meals, protein bars, snacks, and other familiar trail staples. Both provide roughly 3,150 calories and weigh about 23 ounces dry, yet one contains 5,465 mg of sodium while the other contains just 1,975 mg.

That’s nearly 3,500 mg of difference before you add a single electrolyte packet. Some of the variation comes down to individual products: Mountain House Beef Lasagna contains 1,180 mg of sodium, compared with 340 mg in Good To-Go Pad Thai. Even switching pretzel flavors or protein bar brands can make a noticeable difference. Neither food bag is automatically better, but the comparison shows why it’s worth checking what you’re already carrying before deciding to supplement.

Health experts recommend limiting sodium for a reason. Consistently consuming more than your body needs can raise blood pressure, increasing your risk of heart disease and stroke over time. A particularly salty meal can also leave you thirsty or temporarily bloated as your body regulates its fluid balance. If you’ve been advised to limit sodium because of high blood pressure, kidney disease, or another medical condition, those recommendations still matter when you’re backpacking.

But eating a high-sodium diet day after day isn’t necessarily the same as eating salty meals during a strenuous backpacking trip. When you’re sweating heavily, you lose sodium you wouldn’t lose while sitting at a desk. That doesn’t mean every milligram of sodium in your food is necessary, or that you should add electrolytes without thinking about what you’re already eating. It simply means your activity and conditions matter when interpreting those numbers on the label.

Even if you’re hiking alongside someone on the same trail, you may be losing very different amounts of sodium. Sweat losses depend on two things: how much you sweat and how concentrated that sweat is. Research has found sweat sodium concentrations ranging from roughly 230 to 1,600 mg per liter. Temperature, exertion, clothing, heat acclimatization, and individual physiology all affect your losses, which means mileage alone tells you very little about how much sodium you’ve lost.

Here’s another wrinkle: losing 800 mg of sodium through sweat doesn’t automatically mean you need to consume 800 mg to replace it. Your body regulates sodium and water balance, and research suggests targeted sodium replacement matters more under certain combinations of prolonged exercise, substantial sweat losses, and fluid replacement. That’s why taking an electrolyte packet every hour isn’t necessarily appropriate. A packet doesn’t account for how much you’re sweating, how much you’re drinking, or how much sodium you’re getting from food.

Sodium doesn’t have to come from an electrolyte supplement to count. Salty snacks, meals, and electrolyte drinks all contribute to your intake, although when you consume that sodium can matter during long, sweaty days. Imagine spending ten hours hiking in warm weather, sweating heavily and drinking water but eating very little. At camp, you prepare a freeze-dried dinner containing 1,500 mg of sodium. That sodium helps replace losses and retain fluids during recovery, but it wasn’t available during the preceding hours of hiking.

Eating salty snacks throughout a prolonged, sweaty hike provides some sodium along the way instead of waiting until dinner. That doesn’t mean you need electrolyte powder in every water bottle. If you’re regularly eating pretzels, cheese, jerky, or other salty foods, you’re already consuming sodium during the day. On the other hand, if your trail menu contains relatively little sodium or you struggle to eat while hiking, an electrolyte drink may be a convenient alternative. What matters isn’t whether the sodium comes from a packet or a snack, but whether you’re adding something your food isn’t already providing.

It’s easy to assume that replacing the sodium you lose through sweat will protect you from exercise-associated hyponatremia (EAH), a potentially dangerous drop in blood sodium. But according to the Wilderness Medical Society, most cases occur primarily because you drink too much fluid relative to your losses, often combined with your body’s tendency to retain water during prolonged exercise. Sweat sodium losses can contribute under some circumstances, but taking electrolyte supplements won’t prevent hyponatremia if you’re overdrinking.

The guidelines recommend avoiding overdrinking, generally by drinking according to thirst or using fluid needs you’ve estimated from experience in similar conditions. They also recommend having salty foods or drinks available during long, hot activities. Headaches, fatigue, nausea, and muscle cramps aren’t reliable indicators that you need more sodium because they can have several causes. If you or a hiking partner develops worsening confusion, repeated vomiting, seizures, or collapses during or after prolonged exertion, seek urgent medical help rather than assuming more water or electrolytes will solve the problem.

There’s no simple answer. Your sweat losses vary with the conditions and your individual physiology. Even knowing exactly how much sodium you lose wouldn’t tell you how much to replace. For context, 1,500 mg per day is considered an adequate intake for most adults under ordinary conditions, while 2,300 mg is the general recommended daily limit. Neither is a backpacking-specific target. A food bag containing 5,465 mg isn’t automatically excessive for a strenuous hiking day, just as one containing 1,975 mg isn’t necessarily inadequate. And since research hasn’t consistently shown that extra sodium improves endurance performance, there’s little reason to chase a precise replacement target.

The bottom line? Hike your own hike. If you’re curious about your sodium intake, track what you eat and drink, the conditions you’re hiking in, and how you feel during and afterward. You might notice patterns that help you plan future trips, without getting caught up in replacing every milligram of sodium you sweat out.

Googlebook apps built by Googlers add macOS-like status bar shortcuts, keyboard commands

📅 Nov 09, 2026  |  Category: Other  |  Read Source →

Googlebooks are a bit starved of desktop-class features at launch, but one of Google’s pitches around this platform is how apps can be built for it, and the company’s own employees are showing off what that looks like with some clever apps that just so happen to add features that feel a lot like they’re replicating macOS staples.

In a Reddit thread, three Googlebook apps built by Googlers through Antigravity are shown off, and they’re pretty cool, regardless of how you might feel about the platform itself.

“BentoBox” is a utility that gives you control over the status bar on Googlebook, enabling you to fill the status bar with shortcuts, mini-apps, and more. It’s full of glancable information such as weather conditions, battery charging speeds, and more, but also includes useful tools such as being able to access a clipboard history, set a timer, and more. The app requires permissions including accessibility overlays, but it works like a charm.

I had no idea the status bar on Googlebook could even be used like this, and it really makes that space a lot more useful. Of course, you have to draw a comparison here to macOS, which also uses this space for app shortcuts and tools, though BentoBox offers a different type of customization.

BentoBox is available for download via Google Play for free.

The other Mac-like app here is “Booklight,” a launcher that’s basically Apple’s Spotlight, just for Googlebook. It has a transparent design and lets you search “apps, settings, and the web.”

Technically, Googlebook already can do this in its built-in search bar (in the app drawer), but Booklight has more customization and functionality, including being able to write out shorthand commands all in one sentence with the app then completing the command in the appropriate apps, as well as having a clearer UI for experiences such as typing in quick math equations. It’s also good at finding symbols (so you don’t have to remember the U+ codes that carried over from ChromeOS). Not one I found myself wanting to use, but the potential is there.

Booklight is also supposed to be on the Play Store, but it’s disappeared in recent days.

Both BentoBox and Booklight were developed by Alexander Kuscher, VP over Laptops & Tablets at Google.

The other app highlighted in Google’s thread was Wayfinder, a file manager app described as a “desktop-grade file manager.” The big bonus here is network file access for NAS boxes and other uses, but the app also just has a more advanced UX compared to Googlebook’s built-in Files app.

Googlebook’s app gap is a real problem at launch, but these apps do show there’s a lot of potential in the platform, it’s just a matter of getting developers on board – which itself will require Google and its partners to actually sell some of these laptops… classic chicken-and-egg situation.

A Republican congressman plans to force a vote in the House of Representatives this week to impeach Defence Secretary Pete Hegseth, alleging he has violated the US Constitution.

"By engaging in hostilities in Iran for more than 90 days without congressional authorization, Secretary Hegseth is breaking the law and must be held accountable," Representative Thomas Massie said.

The effort will likely fail, given Republicans will remain in power in Congress until January. But Massie says he wants to push President Donald Trump to fire Hegseth before Democrats can launch their own impeachment process.

Hegseth, who will hold a news conference on Tuesday about Massie's move, said he was not concerned.

Massie formally introduced the articles of impeachment in a resolution on the House floor on Monday afternoon.

"I'm doing this because it's the right thing to do," the outgoing congressman told reporters after introducing the articles.

When asked by reporters about the impending resolution earlier on Monday, Hegseth said: "We're not worried about that.

He adding he would hold a news conference Tuesday morning "on that very topic". Hegseth's last news conference was in May.

Massie, who lost re-election earlier this year, has been critical of Trump and the war in Iran since it started nearly nine months ago. He is known across the country for also pushing the administration to release federal files on the deceased sex offender Jeffrey Epstein.

The Kentucky congressman first introduced impeachment articles in September and is now pressing for a vote in the wake of Republicans losing control of both the House and Senate in last week's midterm elections. He has said his push to rid the administration of Hegseth is meant to prevent a Democrat spectacle next year.

"My effort to impeach Secretary Hegseth is a much gentler version of what's coming when the Democrats assume control of the House and Senate next year," Massie also said in a statement. "They will hold hearings and trials."

"President Trump should replace Secretary Hegseth now while he can still get a replacement confirmed in the Senate," he also said.

Impeaching a cabinet secretary is very rare. The impeachment must be approved by both chambers for the secretary to be removed from office. As with presidential impeachments, the House would first hold a vote on charges against Hegseth and the Senate then would conduct a trial to decide whether to acquit or convict.

The House has voted to impeach only two cabinet members in US history, but the Senate did not convict either one.

Now Massie has introduced his resolution, Speaker of the House Mike Johnson must schedule a time to consider it within two days. Along with voting on the resolution directly, the House has the options to vote to refer it to committee or to set it aside.

Massie will likely have the support of House Democrats, with that party's leader, Hakeem Jeffries, saying on Sunday that he is a "hard yes" on impeachment. But Massie would still need to win over members of his own party.

Massie told reporters he was not sure if the measure had sufficient backing to pass.

Peter Loge, a professor of public affairs at George Washington University, told the BBC that the impeachment measure is "likely to pass the House because the Republican majority is so slim".

"And there are enough angry Republicans that more than enough Republicans are gonna simply not show up to vote," he added.

Currently, there are 218 Republicans and 214 Democrats in the House.

The Senate requires a two-thirds vote to convict, a threshold that would be hard to meet in the current congressional term. When the Democrats take over in January, they still will not have enough seats to convict Hegseth without Republican support.

In the articles, Massie also accused Hegseth of intimidating critics of the Trump administration, kidnapping foreign leaders and ignoring congressional War Powers resolutions.

The House has passed a War Powers resolution - a measure seeking to halt Trump from taking further military action in Iran - multiple times this year, with Massie's support. The Senate also passed a War Powers resolution over the summer.

Loge said the chances that Hegseth would survive until the end of Trump's term are "pretty slim", but he expected Hegseth to be ousted instead of impeached.

"Secretary Hegseth is a big personality, and I'm not sure I'd want to be a big personality that's drawing attention from President Trump these days," Loge said. "That's not a way to guarantee job security."
