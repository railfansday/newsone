F1 Academy Is Coming to Apple TV, and Its Drivers Are Ready for Their Close-Up

📅 Nov 19, 2026  |  Category: Sports  |  Read Source →

The all-female racing series is getting a bigger platform in the U.S.

Women's Health may earn commission from the links on this page, but we only feature products we believe in. Why Trust Us?

Starting in 2027, Apple TV will become the exclusive U.S. streaming home of F1 Academy, the all-female racing series developing the next generation of talent.

Fans will be able to watch every qualifying session and race on Apple TV, bringing more visibility to a series that is quickly gaining momentum. It also expands Apple's partnership with Formula 1, which began its five-year U.S. broadcast deal with the streaming service in 2026.

For 19-year-old Austrian driver Emma Felbermayr, who represents the Audi Revolut F1 team in F1 Academy, the announcement is particularly exciting.

“Apple TV is such a big platform, and it's all about F1 Academy getting more attention,” Felbermayr told Women's Health in Las Vegas ahead of the Grand Prix. “It just makes the interest into the sport much bigger, and that's what we need. We need to have more girls interested in the sport so that we get more attention. In the end, we want to get seen by people."

Launched in 2022, F1 Academy is a Formula 4-level development series designed to create more opportunities for women in motorsport. And its impact extends beyond the racetrack.

At a private dinner in Las Vegas celebrating the Apple TV announcement, F1 Academy managing director Susie Wolff reflected on the series' rapid rise.

“It's incredible how the sport has shifted in the last five to seven years,” Wolff said. “Women have a huge part to play in that shift, and that has been part of the reason why F1 Academy has had so much success in the short space of time.” The dinner event, co-hosted by Wolff and F1 Chief Commercial Officer Emily Prazer, brought together women from across the worlds of sports, entertainment, and advocacy, including activist Malala Yousafzai and model Kelly Piquet.

That growth is personal for Felbermayr, who says F1 Academy has helped her become more confident both in and out of the car. Once a self-described shy person, the 19-year-old Austrian has learned to navigate interviews, limited track time, and the pressure of competing at a high level.

“I got so much better in the language of English. That's maybe just a small part of it, but just getting more confident in front of the camera, but also in the car,” says Felbermayr, who just signed on for a third F1 Academy season with Audi. “Every challenge grows yourself as a person, and I think that's something I really saw in the last two years.”

With seven race weekends on the 2027 calendar, including stops in Austin and Las Vegas, American fans will have plenty of opportunities to get acquainted with F1 Academy's rising stars. And with Apple TV bringing their races to a wider audience, more young girls will have the chance to see what's possible behind the wheel.

For a bunch of performance snobs, we are a surprisingly forgiving bunch at PC Gamer. Yes, if you try to insult XCOM, you're going to get a slap, but we've only just started playing GTA 6 (well, some of us) and we're mostly over the whole 'only runs at 30 fps' thing already.

It turns out, while we're happy to part with our money to buy expensive components to hit higher frame rates (and earn the right to brag about it), we're also just as happy to settle for less when a game does a lot of other things exceptionally well.

GTA 6 certainly does that. That's the impression I get from most members of the team that have played it so far. Though if Rockstar is listening, when you do get around to our precious PC version, we'd love nothing more than 4K60—maybe more.

I will no doubt appreciate frames above 100 fps when GTA 6 comes to PC. Heck, I'd even just appreciate going up to 60 fps. But in my handful of hours with the game thus far, I just have simply not been that bothered by it. Be it smart ways of masking low frames, or even just the slower, methodical control approach of the game, I'm less frustrated with my PS5 running the game at around 30 fps than I was recently playing Phantom Blade Zero at well above 40 fps on a handheld PC.

Maybe that's down to the world having a little bit of magic to it, maybe it's the engrossing tone and atmosphere, maybe it's just because it looks so darn pretty. Maybe it's because I haven't got to the game's most hectic moments yet and haven't really needed to hit a headshot with my console unable to keep up with me. I'll tell you how I'm getting on in another fifty hours. Cool, see you in three days, then?

I was dreading playing GTA 6 on my OG PS5—a dusty ornament that's been chilling out in my living room this last year doing absolutely nothing. I've got a nice gaming PC in my office (RTX 4090, Intel i9 13900K, 32 GB RAM), so it's been a long time since I've had to deal with games dipping below 80 fps. I have been spoiled.

The second I got to control Lucia, my fears seemed justified. Everything just felt sluggish. But that feeling didn't last. I played GTA 6 for around 5 hours last night (I am very tired) and I haven't thought about the frame rate once since that initial section. Whether I'm getting into shootouts with coke smugglers, speeding through the Keys at night, or bouncing around at sea, GTA 6 looks great and feels great.

I can still tell this isn't 60 fps, but it is smooth.

I can still tell this isn't 60 fps, but it is smooth. I'm sure I've encountered drops, but crucially I haven't noticed them. Sure, it's inevitably going to look better and run better on PC, but the lower performance on my PS5 quickly ceased to be an issue, and I'm not regretting playing it on console.

GTA 6 is the kind of game where an uncapped framerate would be nice, but it doesn't feel essential. Like Red Dead Redemption 2 before it—a game I also played at 30 fps before it launched on PC—its pacing is measured. It feels designed around the limitations of the consoles it's launching on, prioritising detail and depth over twitch-based combat and precise interaction. It's exactly what I'd expect from a modern Rockstar game. Also like Red Dead Redemption 2 before it, I still feel the sense of longing for what this game will look like on PC. Make no mistake: the version currently available is a preview for what GTA 6 will be at its best. It's great, but those who can wait will be treated to a superior experience.

It's also possible that the 30 fps cap will feel more restrictive once the online mode arrives—whenever that will be, and in whatever form it takes. It's one thing to handle a lower framerate within Rockstar's meticulously created campaign. It's quite another in a lobby of other players. If GTA 6's multiplayer is even half as chaotic as the current GTA Online, every frame is going to count.

As I was booting up GTA 6, I was expecting to be annoyed by the game's paltry frame rate. And I was—at first. Much like Fraser, during the opening scene with Lucia, I saw my fears come to life on the big screen in front of me. Objects juddering and blurring as I swung my camera around in the cramped cell. Did I just waste £80 on GTA 6 on PS5 when I should've held my nerve for the PC version?

Truth is, the frame rate has had nowhere near as much of a negative impact on my experience while I've been traversing the Floridian wilds as Jason. In part, I think that's because there's a lot more going on, as cars, shops, people, and poultry fly past me as I break most state, federal and ethical laws. That reduces the chance of my eyes fixating on any one object and noticing the adverse effects of the game's lower fps, or the ways Rockstar has gone about covering for it.

Low fps is one thing, but motion blur is the devil.

Low fps is one thing, but motion blur is the devil. That's not just my opinion, that's been scientifically verified (sorta) to be the opinion of PC Gamer readers. Admittedly, some motion blur may be required when the frame rate is this low, so I have played around with a few settings in GTA 6 to find the perfect balance. I'd heard through the grapevine that higher-than-default motion blur (default is 3, goes up to 10) may be preferential to reduce the more obvious juddering that is present at low fps, but whoever says that is no friend of mine. I've landed on setting motion blur to 1 on the standard PS5, as it feels the cleanest overall. I wouldn't blame anyone for turning it off entirely, especially on a more powerful console where the frame rate may not dip as much.

Of course, I do agree with the rest of this lot: in an ideal world, playing the PC version, I'd be pushing a much higher frame rate with motion blur banished to the abyss where it belongs, and a full selection of graphics settings to tinker with to my liking. Yet, to my surprise, I'm not as much of a console version curmudgeon as I thought I'd be.

Is the low frame rate noticeable? Yep. Would it be better at a higher frame rate? Also yep. Is the low frame rate a detriment to my overall enjoyment of GTA 6? Only a smidge.

I don't consider myself much of a framerate snob, but I'll admit I was side-eyeing GTA 6's 30fps just a little bit. Well, that was before the game came out. Now that I have my hands on it, with roughly six hours spent lollygagging around Leonida? I'm hardly noticing it, though I'll most definitely be appreciating a smoother experience once the PC version rolls around.

Like Jacob said, motion blur definitely seems to be masking it somewhat—and I am traditionally a motion blur hater, so it's taken a little getting used to—but I'm genuinely pleasantly surprised at the balance Rockstar has managed to strike between a smooth performance and a dense, rich world.

GTA 6 taught me that I don't much care about frame rates as much as I expected. I got to start playing it a few weeks ago and didn't have anyone to compare notes with, and my first thought was: Huh, they got it to run at 60 fps after all! It all felt very smooth, even when I realized it was 30 fps after all. It was only a bit later I started noticing very occasional drops, like while driving during a gradual lighting chance from day to night.

But that's really infrequent. I think it being consistent is key. I'm on a PS5 Slim, by the way, not a Pro. That's not to say I still can't wait to see it at higher frame rates when it finally gets to PC. But 30 fps feels mostly fine for the time being.

It was actually spending a lot of time playing with handheld gaming PCs and laptops with integrated graphics that reminded me I shouldn't give as much of a shit about frame rate as I do. Largely that obsession is born of having high-end hardware sat in my desktop PC, but when crashing around with a range of handhelds I'm reminded of the old days when 30 fps was a target for PC games, too.

It's also reminded me that it really depends on the game engine, as well. Not every 30 fps game looks the same as other 30 fps games. And Rockstar has obviously worked incredibly hard to make sure that 30 fps looks as good as it possible can. Otherwise why would that be a solid target for all four of the console platforms it's released on?

For the most part Rockstar has done a great job of keeping things smooth.

I've been playing GTA 6 on the Xbox Series X, which does suffer from the odd dip in frame rate, too—though oddly I've only really noticed it during canned animations the characters do starting certain actions, such as opening the trunk of a car or pulling something out of it. But for the most part Rockstar has done a great job of keeping things smooth. And not just by using motion blur. For the record, I'm running that at 0 and have not looked back.

I'm less happy about the vignetting that I'm getting around the darkened edges of the screen, but I'm playing it blown up on a 4K projector so I've got plenty of screen real estate to enjoy. And I have been, thoroughly enjoying it, I mean. The frame rate, for me, hasn't got in the way once.

NBA's eight biggest disappointments through one month: Injury bug, same old East and an underwhelming champ

📅 Nov 19, 2026  |  Category: Sports  |  Read Source →

If not listed, please contact your TV provider.

We're roughly one month into the 2026-27 NBA season, and things are going pretty well in certain respects. Luka Dončić and the Lakers are off to a hot start in Los Angeles. Victor Wembanyama and the Spurs have picked up right where they left off after last season's Finals trip. And the Celtics are battling the new-look Sixers at the top of the Eastern Conference.

In other areas, things are going … not so well. Below are the eight biggest disappointments so far this season.

There have been so many players impacted by various injuries that it's almost hard to keep track. Jalen Duren (hamstring) has missed almost the entire season in Detroit. Jimmy Butler and Moses Moody are still recovering from knee injuries and have yet to play for Golden State, while Steph Curry is now out with an abdominal tear and Kristaps Porziņģis stepped away from basketball. After sitting out all of last season with an Achilles tear, Tyrese Haliburton is currently dealing with a hamstring issue. Brandon Ingram (Achilles) hasn't made his debut for the Clippers. Austin Reaves is out with a heel injury. Anthony Edwards underwent knee surgery and will miss a chunk of time for the Wolves, and it seems unlikely that Donte DiVincenzo (Achilles) will play this season, even though he remains hopeful that he will. Paolo Banchero remains sidelined with an ankle sprain. Ja Morant is out with a hamstring strain. Ben Simmons didn't play a single game for the Kings before tearing his Achilles. Kawhi Leonard (knee) is once again on the shelf in Toronto. And the reeling Jazz are without Jaren Jackson Jr (knee), Lauri Markkanen (hand) and Jusuf Nurkic (toe).

It's all a huge bummer for the players, their teams and fans of hoops in general. But no team has been hurt more on the injury front than the Thunder.

The disappointment here is how unkind fate has been to OKC. The Thunder were close to fully healthy when the season started — and then far from it not long after it began. When OKC beat the Spurs in San Antonio in the season opener — after losing four of five regular season games to them last season, as well as falling to the Spurs in the Western Conference Finals — it felt like a statement that the Thunder were back and we were in for an epic battle between the two teams over who would rule the West.

And then all of that was stripped away. Just three games into the season, Jalen Williams — who missed much of last season due to injury — suffered a gruesome fall and had to undergo surgery for a fractured fibula as well as reconstruction of ankle ligaments. In that same fateful game, Cason Wallace tore his labrum and later had shoulder surgery. They haven't been officially ruled out, but if either of them plays basketball again this season, it will be a surprise. Making matters worse, Chet Holmgren tore a tendon in his knee and "underwent a biological treatment" to address it. He'll miss approximately two months, but the Thunder do expect him to return. That's what passes for good news in OKC these days.

The Thunder are — or were — the deepest team in the NBA. That's a credit to what Sam Presti built. But even OKC has its limits. Losing three of five starters is tough to overcome, even when you're led by the reigning back-to-back MVP in Shai Gilgeous-Alexander. That doesn't mean the Thunder (currently 9-6 and eighth in the West) can't still make a deep playoff run, but the odds of them doing so — and of them fighting the Spurs all season for the top spot — got a lot longer.

We were told that this was finally the season that the East returned to its former glory. We were told that the conference would be loaded. We were wrong. (Full disclosure while putting a palm to my face: I might have done some of that telling.)

Through the first month, the East has been mostly underwhelming. Eight of the 15 teams are below .500. The conference has a combined 17-37 record against the West. Only one of the top seven teams in net rating — the Celtics — plays in the East. Five of the top six teams by point differential play in the West.

The defending champion Knicks are 8-6. The Cavaliers, who made it to last season's Eastern Conference Finals and had championship aspirations of their own, are 8-8. The Magic, Raptors and Pacers — who currently occupy the eight, nine and 10 seeds — all have losing records. By contrast, the worst play-in team in the West right now is Golden State at 8-7. That record would be good enough for the seven seed if they played in the East.

But maybe we shouldn't be surprised. Since the 1999-2000 season, the West has had the better overall record in all but three campaigns. Maybe next year the East will finally snap out of its funk, but don't bet on it.

The Jazz just picked up back-to-back road wins in L.A. over the Lakers and Clippers, but that doesn't begin to dig them out of the deep hole they've made for themselves. The Jazz were gearing up to be competitive in the West this season. So much for that. Like the Thunder, injuries have not been kind to them. Nurkic has yet to play a single game this season, while Markkanen and JJJ have missed significant time with maladies of their own. What looked like a formidable frontcourt has been decimated.

Still, it's hard to believe how bad the Jazz have been in pretty much every facet. Their first win came in the second game of the season, a one-point squeaker over the Pelicans. It took until mid-November to end an ugly 12-game losing streak and pick up their second and third wins. They're 25th in net rating and point differential. The season has barely begun, and it's already basically wait until next year for the Jazz.

The NBA panicked and tried to solve for a problem that wasn't actually an issue, except for a small fragment of people who care more about optics than the product itself. And so the league enacted lottery reform and the new 3-2-1 system was put in place. This was ostensibly designed to prevent tanking, incentivize winning and bring greater parity to the league. Oops.

The Hornets, Wizards, Bulls, Nets and Bucks are all well underwater in the East. The Pelicans and Clippers are drowning in the West, while the Kings and Jazz are even worse. What the league failed to account for is that some of these teams are just organically bad — and now they will have decreased odds at getting better.

Under the old system, the Grizzlies would be pretty thrilled to see the Jazz struggling since Memphis has Utah's first-round pick in the upcoming draft as part of the JJJ trade. Alas, after making that move, the league changed the rules, which means the Grizzlies can pick no higher than sixth now as a consequence of the new system preventing a team from picking in the top five in three consecutive drafts. Since the Jazz picked in the top five in each of the last two drafts, that means the Grizzlies are now out of luck with that Utah pick this time around. Womp, womp for them.

And on top of it all, fans of bad teams were robbed of the entertaining-in-its-own-way race to the bottom to maximize lottery odds, giving supporters a small silver lining in the otherwise gray clouds of their season. RIP Tank Watch.

When aren't the Kings disappointing? Aside from their unsightly 1-13 record, the Sacramento front office continues to make baffling decisions. The Kings originally acquired Nique Clifford from the Thunder for a Spurs 2027 first-round pick. Sacramento held onto Clifford for 83 games — choosing to bury him on the bench this season and play him barely six minutes per game — before shipping him back to the Thunder for two second-round picks.

So the Thunder ended up with the player and a first and the Kings ended up with two seconds.

One of these organizations is savvy and opportunistic, and the other is the Kings. It's a miracle they nailed the Darius Acuff pick.

Oof. It has not been a good season to date for Bridges. After going cold to begin their first-round playoff series against the Hawks, Bridges found his form and played well, helping the Knicks go on an epic run that ended with New York's first championship in 53 years.

If that hope was that Bridges would snap out of his early-season funk in similar fashion, the Knicks seem somewhat less patient these days. While head coach Mike Brown has insisted that Bridges is a starter, he has sometimes hedged his bets in crucial situations, going with Landry Shamet or Deuce McBride over Bridges when things get tight and the Knicks need more reliable offense on the floor.

Through 14 games, Bridges is averaging 7.5 points, 3.6 rebounds and 2.1 assists on 40.2/28.9/75 shooting splits. It's the lowest scoring output and the worst shooting numbers of his career. As a result, his minutes have dipped to 25.6 per contest. That's also a career low. Bridges is still a plus defender. And he's only 30. The Knicks obviously hope he'll figure it out, as he has in the past. If not, his new four-year, $150 million extension that kicked in this season will start to look worrisome.

What Every Home Needs for an Extended Power Outage

📅 Nov 19, 2026  |  Category: Business  |  Read Source →

Americans are “pantry-loading” following a cascade of technology outages that have disrupted email, grounded flights, and interrupted banking services—while feuling fears that the failures could be signs of something bigger.

Costco and Walmart have reported increased purchases of emergency supplies, from food and water to batteries and generators. As of Nov. 15, 21 of the 26 high-priced generators offered on Costco's website were sold out, according to Bloomberg.

"Consumer anxiety following the Microsoft Outlook outage, the ground stoppage for all U.S. airline flights and apocalyptic concerns flaring up across social media have all contributed to increased emergency prep sales," David Bellinger, an analyst for Mizuho, a Japanese investment bank, wrote in a recent research note.

Federal officials say they've found no evidence that the outages are connected or resulted from coordinated cyberattacks. But while the fears driving the rush to stockpile may be unfounded, the threat of prolonged disruptions is all-too real.

In August, severe storms knocked out power to nearly 375,000 customers across Northwest Indiana, leaving some without electricity for nearly two weeks.

A historic ice storm in January caused more than 330,000 outages across Mississippi and Louisiana, where some residents faced similarly long waits.

Both emergencies stretched far beyond the familiar three-day supply of food and water—and exposed how quickly households can lose access to basic necessities.

“A three-day emergency kit is still a good starting point, but I’d consider it the minimum rather than the actual goal,” says Brian Armentrout of Chariot Energy. “In Texas, we’ve seen severe weather events where power outages and other disruptions can last well beyond 72 hours.”

But preparing for the next emergency means more than filling a pantry. It means knowing how long your home can function without the systems that keep it safe—and having a plan for when it can't.

The familiar three-day kit is still a vital part of emergency preparedness, but the American Red Cross recommends that amount for an evacuation kit.

For households sheltering at home, the recommendation is much longer: two weeks of food and water.

Daniel Kilburn, an emergency-planning consultant and retired U.S. Army senior infantry drill sergeant, makes a similar distinction.

“The three-day emergency kit is still good if you're actually evacuating. It should not take more than three days to get to where you need to go,” he says. “However, based on current power outages happening around the country, eight to 10 days is not unreasonable."

But most households aren't prepared for that: 69% of respondents said they had assembled emergency supplies, according to the Federal Emergency Management Agency's latest National Household Survey. Among that group, just 34% said their supplies could last more than two weeks.

Kilburn recommends having enough food and water for every member of the household to last a full two weeks. But that doesn't mean buying two weeks of emergency supplies all at once.

Instead, he recommends gradually adding extra shelf-stable foods and water during normal shopping trips, then rotating those foods through the kitchen so the emergency supply functions more like a well-stocked pantry.

“Water is one of the biggest things people tend to overlook,” Armentrout says. “During an extended outage, water service can also be disrupted, so it’s important to have enough bottled water on hand for drinking and basic needs.”

Winter Storm Uri showed how those failures can overlap. The 2021 storm caused power losses for 69% of Texans and water service disruptions for 49%, according to the Texas State Auditor's Office.

The Red Cross recommends one gallon of water per person per day. For a family of four, that works out to 12 gallons for three days—or 56 gallons for two weeks.

And while food may be more intuitive to store than water, it presents a different problem: how to cook it without power.

A closed refrigerator generally keeps food cold for only about four hours without power, according to FoodSafety.gov. A full freezer can maintain its temperature for about 48 hours; a half-full freezer, about 24 hours.

That makes shelf-stable foods a good choice. But Kilburn warns that households should also have a safe way to boil water or prepare food if the power stays out.

A cellphone may become one of the most useful tools in a blackout, and useless within hours without a way to recharge it.

“Backup power is another area people often underestimate,” Armentrout says. “A portable battery bank, generator, or small solar charger can help keep phones and other essential devices operating when the power is out for several days.”

The Red Cross also recommends a battery-powered or hand-crank radio, backup batteries and chargers, and a paper copy of important phone numbers.

But phones are only one electrical dependency inside a modern home. Kilburn recommends mentally walking through an ordinary day and asking what stops working without electricity.

“Let's say you decide it's time to evacuate and your car is in the garage, how are you going to get the garage door open?” he says.

Medical equipment is also a vital consideration. More than 3 million Medicare beneficiaries rely on electricity-dependent durable medical and assistive equipment, according to the Department of Health and Human Services.

For these households, the Red Cross recommends establishing a backup plan in advance.

Generators can provide backup power, but they carry their own risks: carbon-monoxide poisoning from misuse. Nearly 100 Americans die each year in poisonings caused by portable generators, according to the U.S. Consumer Product Safety Commission.

To stay safe, never operate a generator in an enclosed space like your home or garage—even with doors or windows open. Keep them outdoors and more than 20 feet from windows, doors, and vents, and make sure you also have a battery-powered or battery-backup carbon-monoxide detector.

Eventually, the biggest question during a prolonged outage may not be how many supplies remain, but whether the home itself is still safe.

“Before an outage, close blinds or blackout curtains to reduce the amount of heat coming into the house,” Armentrout says. “If the power goes out, stay in the coolest part of the home, which is typically a room on the lower floor away from direct sunlight.”

Battery-powered fans can help, but only to a point. The Centers for Disease Control says fans should be used indoors only when temperatures are below 90 degrees Fahrenheit. Above that, using a fan can actually increase body temperature.

That means one of the most important parts of a blackout plan can't be stored in a closet: somewhere else to go.

Kilburn recommends finding out in advance where cooling centers would operate during a major outage. And Armentrout says households should establish their threshold for leaving before conditions deteriorate.

“It becomes unsafe when your home can no longer meet your basic needs,” he says. “That could be caused by a variety of things, from temperatures becoming too hot or too cold, to running low on water, necessary medications, or having the ability to run critical medical equipment.”

So preparing for two weeks without electricity doesn't mean planning to stay inside a powerless home for two weeks no matter what.

It means having enough food, water, and backup power to preserve your options—and a plan for where to go when staying home is no longer one of them.

Stedelijk Museum appoints Krist Gruijthuijsen as new director

📅 Nov 19, 2026  |  Category: World  |  Read Source →

Krist Gruijthuijsen is currently the director of the Espoo Museum of Modern Art (EMMA) in Finland

Photo: Joel Karppanen. Courtesy Stedelijk Museum, Amsterdam

The Stedelijk Museum in Amsterdam has announced that the Dutchman Krist Gruijthuijsen will take over as its new director from July 2027.

Moving from Finland, where he has since 2025 been director of the Espoo Museum of Modern Art (EMMA), he said he wants to bring an outsider’s perspective and a focus on how the modern city connects with the world.

“The Stedelijk museum’s strength is in bringing large and Modernist exhibitions to the Netherlands,” he tells The Art Newspaper. “People go to the Rijksmuseum for Rembrandt and to the city [Stedelijk] museum to understand how Amsterdam has developed, to understand Modernism through the lens of art and design.”

The Stedelijk Museum enjoyed success last year with an Anselm Kiefer show—organised with the neighbouring Van Gogh Museum—and an exhibition of work by the Dutch photographer Erwin Olaf, increasing annual ticket revenue compared to 2024 by almost a quarter, to €10.8 million. Its current Yayoi Kusama show has been extended to the end of January due to its popularity.

This success stands out against a backdrop of Dutch museums reporting financial pressure and a fall in visits compared to pre-pandemic levels. The Rijksmuseum’s most recent annual report describes internal discussions that took place about the fact visitor numbers fell 4% in 2025 to 2.3 million, while the Rembrandt House Museum recently warned that rising tourist taxes will damage cultural tourism.

But Gruijthuijsen said he is more interested in variety than visitor records. “I’m not a visitor numbers director,” he says. “It is quality rather than quantity: what we need economically, and the rest is a plus. It is very difficult these days because people only choose what they know. My goal is first and foremost to support artists and art.”

Gruijthuijsen takes over from Rein Wolfs, who has been the director since 2019.

A New Fallout Game Leaks, But Dogmeat Won’t Like It

Here’s a fun fact: in the lore of the Fallout series, dogs weren’t allowed into the Vault-Tec Vaults, with all the dogs we see throughout the games being those that survived and evolved into wolf-like forms after the Great War. Cats, on the other hand, were permitted into the Vaults within reason thanks to their applications as therapy pets and efficacy as pest-hunters. In spite of that, cats didn’t start appearing in the Fallout games until Fallout 4 and Fallout 76. Apparently, the reason for that is that they all just sorta got lost somewhere.

A newly-discovered document from the Australian Classification Board has leaked the existence of a new Fallout spin-off game titled A Wasteland Full of Cats. This game is apparently being created in cooperation between Bethesda and Devcats, an indie developer specializing in hidden object games about cats like A Park Full of Cats, A Building Full of Cats, and An Arcade Full of Cats.

Is it the Fallout game we’ve all been waiting for in the near-decade since the launch of Fallout 76? No, definitely not, but, well, it’s something, I suppose. Every bit of Fallout-adjacent content we get can continue to tide us over until Fallout 5, whenever that ends up being, or at least the next season of the Fallout television series.

Tim Cain, the creator of Fallout, initially proposed a much happier ending, but it was ultimately scrapped.

According to the description of the game on the Australian Classification Board website, A Wasteland Full of Cats will cast you in the role of one of the various cats living in one of the Vaults that dot the Wasteland. Following some manner of accident, all of the other cats living in the Vault alongside you are let loose out into the Wasteland, and you’ll need to go out there to round them all up.

In the same vein as Devcats’s other games, this will be a jaunt through 14 locations and 33 rooms where you’ll need to point out the errant cats hidden within the scenery, Where’s Waldo-style.

A Wasteland Full of Cats will cast you in the role of one of the various cats living in one of the Vaults.

You’ll be helped along the way by various NPC characters, hopefully including some familiar faces, with many of the locations you visit apparently being classic spots from across the Fallout franchise. Some of these locations will have mild puzzle elements, allowing you to uncover non-critical secret levels. According to the description, the game will culminate in a final battle against a horde of Radroaches, though I’m admittedly not quite sure how finding cats translates into battling giant mutant roaches, but I guess we’ll find out.

It has been an exceptionally hot minute since the last mainline Fallout game, specifically the release of Fallout 4 in 2015. Since then, it’s been pretty much exclusively spin-offs, such as the mobile title Fallout Shelter, its gacha-added version, Fallout Shelter Online, the MMO stylings of Fallout 76, and I guess Zen Pinball’s Fallout table for the Pinball FX games, if you want to count that.

Back in 2022, Todd Howard said in an interview with IGN that development on Fallout 5 would begin in earnest once The Elder Scrolls VI was done and dusted. Of course, considering we only just learned that game’s full title recently, I have a sinking feeling the timeline has shifted on that a bit. For what it’s worth, Bethesda did say in an X, formerly Twitter post this past July that Fallout 5 has officially begun pre-production, but again, that doesn’t give us much to go on.

As of now, we don’t have any particular release dates or even release windows for upcoming Fallout projects, whether it’s mainline games, the TV show, or even this new cat thing. Just gotta keep wading through the Wasteland patiently, I guess…
