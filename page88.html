Applying for Spousal Social Security Benefits in 2027? 3 Things You Need to Know.

📅 Nov 20, 2026  |  Category: Business  |  Read Source →

Spousal benefits have some unique rules that are worth knowing before you sign up.

By Kailey Hagen, CFP® – Nov 19, 2026 at 7:00PM EST

Spousal Social Security checks tend to be a lot smaller than retirement benefits, averaging just $988 in October 2026 compared to $2,092 for retirement checks. But they can still make a meaningful difference to your household's budget in 2027.

Spousal benefits have a few rules that make them different from retirement benefits, and it's important to understand them if you plan to apply for these checks next year. Here are three of the biggest differences you should be aware of.

You must be at least 62 or older to claim a spousal Social Security benefit. But even at this age, you can’t sign up if your partner is not yet receiving the partner's own retirement benefit. If you're eligible for a retirement benefit on your own work record, you may claim this in the meantime. Otherwise, you have to wait for your partner to apply first.

It's important that you and your spouse are both on the same page about when each of you will apply so that you can maximize your household income. If you haven't already talked through your plans, now is a great time to do so before you try to apply for Social Security benefits in 2027.

Spousal benefits are also available to ex-spouses if you were married to a qualifying worker for at least 10 years before divorcing and you haven’t remarried. Your ex's remarriage will not affect your eligibility to claim benefits on the ex's work record.

Benefits for ex-spouses work almost exactly the same as benefits for current spouses, except you don't need to wait for your ex to sign up for benefits first. As long as you've been divorced for at least two years and are 62 or older, you can claim a spousal benefit on your ex's work record, provided it's worth more than your own retirement benefit.

Social Security retirement benefits continue to grow for every month you delay your application until you qualify for your maximum checks at age 70. But spousal benefits only grow until you reach your full retirement age (FRA). This is 67 if you were born in 1960 or later.

Make sure you sign up by this point at the latest, unless you can’t because your spouse hasn't applied yet. If you fail to sign up by your FRA, contact the Social Security Administration right away to get the process started and to see if you qualify for any retroactive benefits.

Buying a home doesn’t take 20% down. Many loans take as little as 3% or 3.5%, and VA loans for veterans and USDA loans for some rural buyers need no down payment. Set aside your cash for closing costs, moving, and emergencies first, then put down what’s left and weigh the mortgage insurance a smaller down payment adds.

Most first-time buyers put down less than 20%. Their median down payment was 9% in the National Association of Realtors’ 2026 buyer survey.

Twenty percent is only the point where a conventional loan, one without government backing, stops requiring private mortgage insurance (PMI), which protects the lender if you stop paying. A smaller down payment can also mean a higher interest rate. You can ask to drop PMI once you owe 80% of the home’s original value. Government-insured FHA loans keep their mortgage insurance for the life of the loan when you put down less than 10%.

On a $350,000 home, 5% down is $17,500, against $70,000 for 20%. Freddie Mac estimates PMI at $30 to $70 a month per $100,000 borrowed, or roughly $100 to $230 a month on the $332,500 loan, a cost to weigh against the $52,500 you’d keep. Waiting costs something too. Home prices rose 2.4% in the year through August 2026, the Federal Housing Finance Agency reports. At that pace, if saving the other $52,500 takes three years, the same house will cost about $376,000, and 20% of it about $75,000. Buying sooner with less down avoids that rise, as long as your savings stay intact.

Start from the lowest down payment your loan allows, on a home that appraises for at least its price.

Then set aside closing costs, typically 2% to 5% of the price, money for moving and repairs, and an emergency fund covering three to six months of essential bills. Whatever is left goes toward the down payment, and down payment assistance programs can help you reach your loan’s minimum. Ask lenders to quote your monthly payment at 5% and 10% down.

PMI can come off later, but cash you put into the house comes back out only if you borrow against it or sell.

French giants Lyon outclass Arsenal in Women's Champions League

In contrast, Lyon have been flying domestically, winning all nine of their matches, scoring 49 goals, and then not conceding any in their unbeaten Champions League run.

Finnair Celebrates International Men’s Day With All-Male Crew On Flight

📅 Nov 19, 2026  |  Category: World  |  Read Source →

There’s still quite a gender gap in the airline industry, with a disproportionate number of pilots being male, and a disproportionate number of flight attendants being female. Airlines will sometimes have special celebrations with all-female crews, so here’s an interesting twist on that.

Today is (apparently) International Men’s Day. We often see airlines commemorate International Women’s Day, but there aren’t many airlines that celebrate the equivalent for men… well, except Finnair! The airline has today operated its annual flight with an all-male crew. This was the case for Finnair flight AY1331, from Helsinki (HEL) to London (LHR), using an Airbus A350-900.

The crew consisted of 10 people, comprised of two pilots and eight flight attendants. The gate agents were also all men. So, why is this important? Finnair highlights how only around 10% of cabin crew at the airline are male, but the company hopes to see that number grow in the future.

I think this is a fun concept, and it would be nice to see a closer to 50/50 gender ratio in both the cockpit and cabin, as it would show that everyone is given a fair chance at such a career.

I think it’s worth acknowledging that at many airlines, hiring of flight attendants is still primarily or exclusively based on gender. Now, the policies do vary significantly by airline, but perhaps an extreme example is India’s largest airline, IndiGo, which only hires female flight attendants. It’s literally written on the application, that it’s a job for “Female Indian nationals.”

It’s all so strange, because the airline has this super enthusiastic page about the importance of diversity in the workplace, and says “all employment decisions are made solely on the basis of merit and qualifications, without regard to a candidate’s gender…” You mean except the single largest portion of your workforce, which is cabin crew?

What’s interesting is that while those gender restrictions exist in the cabin, roughly 18% of the carrier’s pilots are female, which is way above the global average of 7%. That’s obviously a conscious effort, so I guess the idea is that women can become pilots but men can’t become flight attendants.

In fairness, I suspect part of the motivation here is that IndiGo flight attendants share hotel rooms on layovers, and that is a bit more complicated with mixed genders. Then again, it seems easy enough to just try to pair male flight attendants on trips, so it’s hardly a hurdle that can’t be overcome.

While we’re used to seeing airlines celebrate International Women’s Day, it’s rare to see an airline celebrating International Men’s Day. Finnair has operated an all-male flight today, intended to highlight how only 10% of flight attendants at the airline are male, and the airline wants to change that. While an unconventional initiative nowadays, I think this is still pretty cool.

Enter Chanel Chance Eau Splendide, the purple-hued 2025 addition to the brand’s beloved Chance fragrance collection. After attending the launch event for Eau Splendide last April, I didn’t intend to make it my new go-to... but that’s exactly what happened. Now, after wearing it pretty consistently for a year and a half, here’s my honest review of Chanel Chance Eau Splendide Eau de Parfum, including what it actually smells like on skin, how it wears throughout the day, and whether it’s worth the luxury price tag. But that’s not all: As the fragrance dries down, you’ll experience base notes of iris, cedar, and white musk that make for a soft finish. To me, it results in a scent that feels elegant, feminine, and sophisticated—very Chanel—without being particularly heavy.

Splendide is a fresh and light scent, but don’t think that means it’s not staying put on your skin throughout the day. It usually lasts about four to six hours on me, so while it’s certainly not the longest-lasting fragrance I own, about four spritzes of it gets me through most of the day.

With so many great Chanel fragrances, how does this stand up against the rest? If I had to describe the overall vibe, I’d say that Chance Eau Splendide makes me feel effortlessly put together, but it also has a playful streak to it. I smell the raspberry most prominently on my skin, though, as it gets later in the day, I also notice the iris and musk. Even though I have dozens of fruity floral perfumes, it’s different than any of the others in my fragrance wardrobe.

The first time I spritzed it, I did wonder if it was going to be too sweet for me. Ultimately, I found that the raspberry and rose geranium mix well together and don’t feel too sugary whatsoever. I also love how youthful it is, especially compared to some of the other Chanel fragrances out there. It’s super flirty, vibrant, and fresh, making it the perfect companion for daytime meetings, date nights, and more—I even wore it to Coachella this year. All in all, the one word I’d use to describe it is approachable. Is it the most complex perfume I’ve ever sniffed? No. But the scent, the vibe, and the longevity make it perfect for anyone from a first-time Chanel fragrance wearer (I think it’s a great introduction to the brand) to a fruity floral fanatic. For me, it’s an everyday bottle that takes me from day to night all year long.

On that last point: I can see why someone might call it a spring or summer perfume, given the notes. But there’s no rule that says you have to put away your fruity florals when temperatures drop. This is the kind of fragrance that works whenever I’m craving something bright and fresh.

Okay, so is Chanel Chance Eau Splendide actually worth the splurge? It’ll definitely be a re-purchase for me, and not just because it comes in a pretty purple bottle.

Here’s my case for giving it a chance—yes, pun intended. The juicy raspberry opening is playful without veering into candy territory, while the other notes give it that effortlessly polished Chanel feel. It’s the kind of fruity floral that doesn’t need to be retired the second sweater weather arrives. I say we let this one live its best life all 12 months of the year.

Of course, Chanel prices are Chanel prices (even the smallest bottle is $115), so I’d recommend giving it a test spray before committing to a full bottle—especially if you typically gravitate toward super-sweet gourmands or bold, spicy fragrances. But that being said, I’ve been wearing it regularly for well over a year and I’m nowhere near the end of my bottle, so it’ll last you a while. For anyone who loves a fresh, feminine scent with a little personality, Eau Splendide is definitely worth a sniff. That said, since it’s such an easy-to-wear, clean-meets-powdery-meets-sweet scent, I’d consider it a pretty safe blind buy if that sounds like the type of perfume you generally gravitate toward.

I’ve worn this to weddings, concerts, dinners... You name it, you can wear this perfume there. She’s effortlessly versatile, isn’t overpowering, and isn’t a snob—even though she’s polished, there’s no intimidation factor, and she doesn’t take herself too seriously. She’s a must-have for anyone, whether you’re newly dabbling in fragrance or someone who already swears by a classic fruity floral. Consider this review my formal petition to add it to a list of all-time best year-round scents.
