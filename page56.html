Realty Income Pays Dividends Every Month. Here's What You Can Expect $10,000 Invested to Pay Monthly in 2027.

Realty Income has paid a monthly dividend every month for more than 56 years.

By Jennifer Saibil – Nov 21, 2026 at 6:33PM EST

10 stocks we like better than Realty Income ›

If you're looking for a dividend stock, Realty Income (O +0.64%) is one of the best. It's a real estate investment trust (REIT), a business structure for real estate companies that pays out 90% of income as dividends, which is why REITs are often on best-dividend-stock lists.

Realty Income has an excellent track record, a high yield, and plenty of reasons to be confident about its future success. If you invest $10,000 right now, here's how much you can expect to get in dividends in 2027.

Realty Income has supported its top dividend by running a massive real estate business that leases properties to large essential retailers. These are chains you know and love and likely shop at, like Walmart (WMT +1.51%), Home Depot (HD +0.70%), 7-Eleven, and Dollar General (DG -2.23%).

These are stores with well-established brands and strong cash flow, making them reliable renters. They have multi-year leases, some as long as 20 years, and Realty Income's occupancy rate is a high 98.8%. It has maintained a rate between 97% and 99% since 2000.

Grocery and convenience stores make up almost 20% of the portfolio, providing a resilient revenue stream, but the company has also branched out into other areas as it expands and diversifies. The U.K. is one of its largest markets today, accounting for more than 15% of total properties, and Wynn Resorts (WYNN -2.07%) has become one of its biggest customers. Industrials make up a healthy 17% of the portfolio, and Realty Income is also getting started in data centers, which make up 1%.

The company has more than 15,500 properties in 96 industries, and it's always buying new properties to keep growing. It has a $5.5 billion revolving credit facility, and it's raised more than $2 billion so far this year from various sources. It has a large pipeline of quality properties to choose from, and expanding beyond retail offers it more opportunities.

Realty Income has paid a dividend monthly for the past 677 months, or more than 56 years. It's one of the few dividend stocks that cuts you a check every month.

It also raises its dividend quarterly rather than annually, which is unusual among great dividend stocks. It has raised the dividend for the past 117 quarters, or more than 29 years.

At the current price, the dividend yields 5.6%, an excellent yield for a reliable dividend stock.

Realty Income is a dividend play, and the stock hasn't changed much over the past 10 years. However, now has been a particularly challenging time for the company as interest and mortgage rates remain high.

Since its business model involves borrowing lots of money, higher interest rates mean it has to pay more to service its debt.

While shares are down 7% over the last six months, it doesn’t appear to be in any danger, and it’s flourished across a range of environments for more than 50 years, paying its dividend without missing a monthly payment.

As of its latest raise last week, Realty Income pays $3.288 in annualized dividends, up from $3.258. That's a small change of only 0.9%. If it continues like that for the next four quarters, by the end of 2027, it should be $3.408 in annualized dividends. The average of the two is $3.348, and divided by 12, that's an average monthly payment of $0.279.

If you invest $10,000 today at a share price of $57.66 (at the time of writing), that gets you 173 shares, and next year, you'd get about $48.27 every month.

That may not seem like much, but it's reliable and will continue to rise. It also highlights the need to save for retirement to ensure a livable monthly income.

German e-invoicing for developers: XRechnung and ZUGFeRD via REST API and MCP

📅 Nov 21, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Posted on Nov 21 • Edited on Nov 21 • AI-assisted

From 2027, many German businesses have to send structured e-invoices to other businesses, and from 2028 almost all of them. Receiving them has been mandatory since 1 January 2025. If you build software that creates or processes invoices for German customers, this is now your problem too.

This post covers what counts as an e-invoice, the two formats you will meet, how to validate them, and how we generate both formats from a single invoice object in our own API. Disclosure up front: we build MaxRechnung, a German invoicing app, and the examples in the second half use our API.

Small businesses under § 19 UStG ("Kleinunternehmer") are exempt from sending, but they still have to receive. Small-amount invoices up to €250 and travel tickets are exempt as well, and B2C invoices are not affected.

A PDF is not an e-invoice under German law, even if it is generated by software. An e-invoice is a structured, machine-readable format that complies with the European standard EN 16931. The law also allows other agreed formats such as EDI, as long as the data can be extracted in a way compatible with EN 16931. In practice, though, you will meet one of two formats:

Legally, the XML is what counts. If the PDF and the embedded XML disagree, the XML wins. That also means you have to archive the original file, not a printout or a re-rendered PDF. Strictly, the XML is what must be kept; the PDF part only matters if it carries additional relevant information, but keeping the original file covers both.

Two open validators cover almost everything:

If you build an invoicing feature yourself, run these validators in CI against a set of fixture invoices. The schematron rules change with every XRechnung release, and you want to notice before your customers' accounting software does.

In MaxRechnung, an invoice is created once as a structured object. XRechnung and ZUGFeRD are two renderings of the same data, and once an e-invoice is finalized, its XML is frozen. The flow over the REST API (/api/v1) looks like this.

Setting e_invoice_formats makes finalizing enforce the full EN 16931/XRechnung rules and freezes the XML. Leave it out only for invoices that don't need to be e-invoices (B2C, for example). The sender (name, address, tax ID) is taken from your account settings; pass a sender object to override it. Seller contact name and phone (BT-41/BT-42) always come from your account settings. A draft may be incomplete, so this call succeeds even if those settings are still empty.

The Idempotency-Key makes retries safe: the same key with the same body within 24 hours returns the original response instead of creating a second invoice, while the same key with a different body returns 409 conflict. Use something stable like your order ID.

Finalizing checks the mandatory invoice fields required by § 14 UStG. If something is missing, you get a 422 validation_failed with the affected fields in details (on a fresh account without sender data, for example sender.name, sender.address and sender.tax_id), and nothing is finalized. After that the invoice is immutable; corrections go through a separate corrections endpoint, which is what German bookkeeping rules (GoBD) expect.

This returns XRechnung 3.0.2 (CII). With Accept: application/json you get a JSON wrapper with the result of our pre-check against the XRechnung rules: valid (true/false), an errors array for rule violations and a warnings array for hints, e.g. a missing seller contact (BT-41/BT-42) or buyer e-mail address (BT-49). The raw XML download carries the same verdict in the X-EInvoice-Valid header. The free-text unit Std. from the example ends up as unitCode="HUR" in the XML. If you set e_invoice_formats in step 1, finalizing has already enforced these rules and you get the frozen file. For invoices created without it, check valid/errors or the X-EInvoice-Valid header: the call still returns 200 so that existing integrations don't break.

The full reference, including a cURL import for each endpoint, is at maxrechnung.de/developers/api, and the OpenAPI 3.1 spec is at https://maxrechnung.de/api/openapi.json.

We documented four building blocks (create customer, create and finalize invoice, sum payments, list overdue invoices for a daily dunning list) in the n8n guide.

District 5-2A champ North Star faces big state playoff test in Westinghouse

📅 Nov 21, 2026  |  Category: Sports  |  Read Source →

North Star High School football coach Shane Smith made a straightforward observation about the Westinghouse team his Cougars will face in the PIAA Class 2A playoffs Saturday.

"This is a machine like we haven't seen all year long," Smith said of the undefeated and state sixth-ranked Bulldogs. "They are very big and very fast. On tape, they look like a very physical football team.

"We definitely have to play the best game we've played all year long."

Kickoff is at noon at Bedford High School.

North Star (10-2) won its first District 5 title in 15 years Nov. 13, defeating Berlin Brothersvalley 16-7 in the Class 2A title game at Somerset Area High School.

District 8 Westinghouse (10-0), the Pittsburgh City League champion, has outscored opponents 489-81, an average of 48.9 to 8.1 points a game.

The Bulldogs beat Karns City 50-21 Nov. 13, marking the third straight game Westinghouse scored exactly 50 points. Coach Donta Green's squad tallied a season-high 81 points against Brashear and followed that with 66 points against Taylor Allderdice.

"We're going to go in with the best game plan possible, but we know the test that lies ahead of us," Smith said. "We are going to have to play very disciplined, fundamental football."

Westinghouse quarterback Omar Pack ran for 170 yards and scored three touchdowns against the Gremlins Nov. 13.

Running back Jordan Reid's 131-yard rushing performance in the subregional helped the Bulldogs collect 413 rushing yards.

"We have to tackle better than we have all year long," Smith said. "Their team speed is just off the charts. If we give them a little crease, it's going to go to the house.

"We have to match their physicality. Fundamental, physical football is going to be the approach this week."

North Star is led by senior Kasey Yoder, fourth in the area with 1,413 passing yards, 22 touchdowns and eight interceptions. In the District 5-2A title game, Yoder carried 17 times for 94 yards, including a touchdown. He only threw three passes for a net of two yards.

On defense, Yoder closed the game with a 45-yard interception return to the end zone.

Junior back Will Eshleman ran for 85 yards on 15 carries against Berlin Brothersvalley, as North Star earned its first District 5 championship since a repeat title performance in 2011.

"We had a big win last week – first District 5 (title) in a number of years," Smith said. "We're going to run with the momentum we have. We've had a good week of practice. The attitude is upbeat and positive."

Westinghouse has had success against District 5 opponents, winning in the playoffs five consecutive seasons.

In 2025, the Bulldogs beat Meyersdale (44-0) in the Class 1A playoffs after previously earning 2A wins over Windber (2021, 2024) and Berlin Brothersvalley (2022, 2023).

Westinghouse reached the Class 2A state final in back-to-back seasons, falling each time to Southern Columbia in the 2022 (37-22) and 2023 (21-20) championship games.

Prior to the current stretch, the Bulldogs struggled against District 5, dropping seven straight playoff games from 2013-19 against Bedford (2018, 2019), Berlin Brothersvalley (2016, 2017) and Chestnut Ridge (2013, 2014, 2015).

"We're going to rely on Kasey Yoder, and we're hoping Will Eshleman has a nice game for us," Smith said. "Westinghouse's team fronts are very big. This will be the first time our guys will be tested by somebody bigger than us.

"I'm anxious to see how our big guys perform as the 'little' guys this week."

Johnstown Mill Rats name new general manager

📅 Nov 21, 2026  |  Category: Business  |  Read Source →

JOHNSTOWN, Pa. – The Johnstown Mill Rats announced Megan Semanchik has been named general manager.

Semanchik brings experience in sports sales, corporate partnerships, game-day operations and food and beverage management to the position, including previous roles with both the Mill Rats in the Prospect League and the Johnstown Tomahawks in the North American Hockey League.

"I'm grateful for the opportunity to take on this role and continue growing with the Mill Rats," Semanchik said.

"I've had the opportunity to see the organization from several different perspectives, and I'm excited to build on that experience.

"I'm looking forward to working with our staff, partners and community to continue growing the Mill Rats and creating a great experience at the ballpark."

Semanchik, who replaced 2025 Prospect League general manager of the year Sarah Finlay, began her professional sports career with the Tomahawks as an intern before joining the organization full time in group sales and sponsorships.

Her responsibilities extended during her time with the organization, giving her experience across multiple areas of sports business and operations.

She later joined the Mill Rats as a member of the game-day staff before moving into the role of food and beverage manager.

"We've had the opportunity to watch Megan grow within our organization, and she has consistently shown the initiative, work ethic and leadership we're looking for," said Bart Vickroy, president of Johnstown Family Entertainment, the ownership group of the Mill Rats. "She understands the business side of sports, she knows our organization and she has a strong connection to the Johnstown sports community. We're excited to have Megan take on this role and help lead the Mill Rats into our next chapter."

As general manager, Semanchik will oversee the club's day-to-day business operations, including corporate partnerships, ticket and group sales, marketing, community relations, game-day operations and the overall fan experience.

Semanchik, a Portage Area High School graduate, earned a bachelor's degree in business administration with a minor in sports management from Mount Aloysius College, completing the program in three and a half years. She was a member of the women's volleyball team and served as team manager for the men's volleyball program.

Star Wars' TV Spinoff Connected To Andor Characters Encouraged By Show Creator

📅 Nov 21, 2026  |  Category: Entertainment  |  Read Source →

Andor creator Tony Gilroy hopes that the Star Wars series will receive its own spinoff.

In 2022, six years after Rogue One: A Star Wars Story was released, Disney and Lucasfilm pointed the spotlight on Diego Luna’s Cassian Andor, one of the movie’s main characters. Andor chronicled the five years of Cassian’s life before he died on that fateful mission to steal the plans for the first Death Star. Though fans know how his story ends, Gilroy would like to see characters introduced in the acclaimed Star Wars TV show be explored further.

During an interview with Polygon, Gilroy encouraged the powers-that-be running the Star Wars franchise to move forward with such a project. He pointed out how at the end of the Andor series finale, Adria Arjona’s Bix Caleen was shown holding a baby, with the implication being that this was Cassian’s child. Gilroy explained:

I don't see how it cannot affect it canonically. We built a lot of canon over 24 hours. I mean, we stuck to canon a lot, but we created a lot of IP, a lot of characters, a lot of different things. I left them with a baby. One would think that at some point someone's gonna continue that.

By the time Andor’s two-season run ended in 2025, many of its important characters had been killed off, including Stellan Skarsgård’s Luthen Rael, Kyle Soller’s Syril Karn, and Varada Sethu’s Cinta Kaz. However, along with Bix, there are still a handful of characters from the series who could hypothetically be brought back for post-Rogue One and A New Hope stories. This ranges from Denise Gough’s Dedra Meero languishing in an Imperial prison, to Elizabeth Dulau’s Kleya Marki potentially going on more missions for the Rebellion.

Because Gilroy’s time in the Star Wars franchise is over, any kind of Andor TV spinoff would need to be handled by another creative. Gilroy noted that he’s not “not in constant communication with Lucasfilm” anymore, so he doesn’t know if such a project is being considered, let alone worked on. Nevertheless, from his perspective, this Andor-centric corner of the Star Wars universe is rife with possibilities for expansion, particularly when it comes to to Bix’s baby.

Although Andor ultimately changed from an original five-season plan to a two-season show, showrunner Tony Gilroy says he doesn't feel any absences.

There are currently only two upcoming Star Wars TV shows officially slated. Ahsoka season 2 will arrive in early 2027, reuniting viewers with Rosario Dawson’s Ahsoka Tano in the post-Return of the Jedi era when Lars Mikkelsen’s Grand Admiral Thrawn has resurfaced. Star Wars: Maul — Shadow Lord season 2 is also expected to premiere sometime in 2027, with that show following Darth Sidious’ former apprentice attempting to rebuild his criminal empire following the conclusion of the Clone Wars. Most recently, all eight episodes of Star Wars: Visions Presents — The Ninth Jedi were released on August 5.

Andor can be streamed in its entirety on Disney+ with the rest of the other Star Wars movies and TV shows. Along with Ahsoka season 2 and Shadow Lord season 2’s imminent arrivals, Star Wars: Starfighter, the franchise’s next movie, will be released on May 28, 2027.

Recasting Sex And The City For 2026: All 8 Main Characters

📅 Nov 21, 2026  |  Category: Entertainment  |  Read Source →

Faith Roswell is a Senior Writer on Screen Rant's Classic TV team. Since earning her degree in Creative Writing over a decade ago, Faith has written articles on film and TV from a variety of different angles. Faith now combines her knowledge of psychology with her love of monster movies to give more insight into what makes the best ones.  You may have read her Screen Rant lists and features covering horror, sci-fi, and fantasy, or read her Amazon Top 10 book, "Movie Monsters of the Deep." Faith has had an extensive career as a writer, appearing on BBC live radio, researching true crime for Rotten Mango podcast, and writing for publications including Mental Floss, Atlas Obscura, and The Daily Jaws before beginning here at Screen Rant.

Sex and the City is one of the iconic TV shows that defined the 1990s, changing the way we talk about and show sex and relationships onscreen, with this discourse continuing today in a very different way. The TV series has aged so badly that this has overshadowed just how unique it really was, but it acts as a time capsule to the 1990s, which gives it a place in both TV and cultural history, with its four main characters still remaining discussion topics today.

Sex and the City's main characters, Carrie, Samantha, Miranda, and Charlotte, were revolutionary, talking about sex and love in a way that no other mainstream TV show had put onscreen, and the four have remained distinctive style icons even during the show's ill-fated reboot. And Just Like That tried to undo some of the worst aspects of Sex and the City, including the characters' unintentional bigotry and lack of diversity, but this was far from a successful approach, and a better one may have been to reboot the series from the start, with new actors exploring modern subjects.

Already no stranger to working with HBO, Issa Rae's work on Insecure has established her as an actor more than capable of embodying the stylish mess that Carrie Bradshaw really is. Carrie sacrifices necessary aspects of living, like her kitchen, in favor of buying shoes, has an enormous wardrobe of striking looks, appears impossibly elegant, and yet is a crumbling mess underneath it all, desperate for acknowledgment from Mr. Big.

Rae's Insecure character, who shares her name, thrives on romantic insecurity, channeling heartbreak into creativity through her signature mirror raps, much as Carrie uses her relationship failures as fodder for her column. Even setting Insecure aside, Issa Rae's best movies and TV shows have such a range that it is easy to imagine her in Carrie's Manolo Blahniks, whether she is falling over in the street, at home working on her column, or dealing with truly devastating events.

Samantha Jones has grown more controversial over time for her misogynistic beliefs and willingness to enter taboo relationships, including with teenagers and Charlotte's brother. An updated version of Samantha would be sexually adventurous rather than predatory, with the viewer exploring newer relationship types through her. This would require an actor who could embody her defiance and flexible sexuality while feeling more authentic than Kim Cattrall when delivering Samantha's often-quoted line, "I'm a try-sexual. I'll try anything once."

Tessa Thompson came out as bisexual in an interview with Net-A-Porter, and is a vocal champion for the LGBT+ community. Casting her as Samantha would allow her controversial relationship with artist Maria to feel natural rather than deliberately provoking, but there is more to Thompson than her sexual orientation that makes her perfect for the role. Her characters in Hedda and Westworld show her commanding a room as Samantha would working as a publicist, and she has the dramatic range to make Samantha and Smith's relationship timeline truly magnetic, from the initial meeting to her entire cancer and recovery storyline.

Phoebe Waller-Bridge might be inescapably British, but this could be written into an updated version of Miranda Hobbes, the power-dressing lawyer whose struggles to break the glass ceiling give her some of the best lines in Sex and the City. A rebooted Miranda could be well-traveled and world-weary, bringing the signature wit and dry British humor Waller-Bridge used to make Fleabag into a masterpiece. Fleabag covers some of the same ground as Sex and the City, but is certain to age better.

Any actor taking on a role like Miranda must put their own stamp on the character rather than merely emulating Cynthia Nixon's performance, and Waller-Bridge has an expressive face that would allow Miranda to say exactly what she is thinking even when the focus is on the other characters. Miranda is sometimes her own worst enemy, and needs to remain a sympathetic character even when she is continuing a pregnancy she doesn't want, and taking back a cheating husband, and if anyone can continue to win viewers over even while her character makes seemingly incomprehensible choices, it is Waller-Bridge.

Lily Collins' effortless "Old Hollywood glamour" style and physical resemblance to Charlotte York has resulted in her often being fan-cast for the role. That said, a successful reboot needs more than a lookalike and Collins' previous roles have also resembled aspects of Charlotte, proving her easily capable of playing her. Emily in Paris showed Collins playing a hopeless romantic with a striking fashion sense, without the character feeling forced.

Her Golden Globe-nominated role in the romantic comedy Rules Don't Apply involves her exploring a potential relationship with a person she is not supposed to, and navigating her rigid religious and life rules in much the same way as Charlotte does when she meets Harry. While Rules Don't Apply ends differently, the movie feels like a trial run for Collins as Charlotte, who must decide whether she would rather wait for a hypothetical fairytale or choose the man she fell in love with.

Daniel Radcliffe might be an unconventional choice to play Charlotte's true love, Harry Goldenblatt, but he has a history of playing unconventional characters, which makes him a surprisingly perfect fit. Having worked tirelessly to step out from the shadow of Harry Potter, Radcliffe has shown his acting range in strange movies and TV shows, and while Sex and the City is considerably less weird than movies like Horns, playing Harry would highlight Radcliffe's ability to play a more subtle character too.

The original main Sex and the City characters and their actors

Radcliffe has appeared in sitcoms before, with a cameo in Unbreakable Kimmy Schmitt, and his character in the movie The F Word showed him as a believable romantic hero. Harry is one of the best partners in Sex and the City, being warm, genuine, and true to himself, and these characters fit with Radcliffe's natural demeanor. His Jewish heritage brings essential cultural grounding to the character, as Harry's religion and culture feature in some of his best storylines.

Of all the love interests in Sex and the City, Steve has aged like milk. Originally portrayed as a sweet and enthusiastic man whose confidence and charm allowed him to pursue Miranda, finally winning her heart, rewatching Sex and the City tells a very different story. Steve is immature, pushy, and deeply insecure over Miranda's success, and yet the character is an extremely flawed human rather than being an outright villain, and he must not be portrayed as one. This requires an actor who can balance these aspects of his character, making his emotional vulnerability show through the mask.

Steve Brady is almost a perfect reversal of Joe Keery's best-known character, Stranger Things' Steve Harrington. While a much younger character, Harrington is popular, sporty, and insecure, but has a moment of reckoning that turns his weaknesses into strengths and wins over both viewers and his love interest. Steve Brady would give Keery the chance to play an alternate universe version of Harrington, in which his petulance overrides his charm, while still being central to some of the show's funniest moments.

Smith Jerrod is one of the few men in Sex and the City who got a character arc almost as strong as the central four women. When Samantha meets the struggling actor working in a restaurant, she works her PR magic, transforming him into a genuine movie star, falling for him in the process. Regé-Jean Page has the movie star quality to carry off Jerrod's transformation, and his charisma was central to his romance storyline in Bridgerton, proving him capable of playing a character who could even make Samantha fall in love.

Sex and the City has 70% and 81% scores on Rotten Tomatoes from critics and audiences, respectively.

Since Bridgerton, Page has been somewhat typecast as a romantic hero through roles like Michael in You, Me, & Tuscany, but while Sex and the City's Jerrod gets a fairytale ending, the character would give Page much more to work with. Jerrod is also a recovering alcoholic who finds New York's alcohol-centered party atmosphere and overenthusiastic fans difficult to cope with, and this story could be given a greater focus in a rebooted version of the show.

To a modern viewer, Mr. Big is a sleazy millionaire who has no intention of committing to Carrie, no matter how desperately she asks him to. However, the archetype is a fixture of 1990s culture, making it essential that a modern version of Sex and the City updates the character, not to redeem him but to make him recognizable to new viewers. Kieran Culkin's character in Succession, Roman Roy, is an ideal modern version, masking his insecurities with sarcasm and crudeness.

Carrie and Mr. Big's relationship is the core of the show, with everything she does ending up leading back to him, which is presented as a happy ending in the context of the series. This would almost certainly be the hardest part to sell in a reboot, making Mr. Big's casting one of the most important. Culkin has made a career out of playing unlikable characters without turning them into outright villains, and if he could balance Mr. Big's worst characteristics with his occasional flashes of genuine kindness, the character might have a chance at carrying the series, even now.
