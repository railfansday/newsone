The film is directed by Genki Kawamura, who previously worked as a producer on Your Name, Weathering With You, and Suzume, and stars Kazunari Ninomiya, who previously appeared in Oshi no Ko and Last Samurai Standing.

Dragon Ball's New Anime Proves That Toei Is Holding The Franchise Back

📅 Nov 04, 2026  |  Category: Business  |  Read Source →

People who grew up loving Dragon Ball are currently able to enjoy the weekly releases of the Japanese version of Dragon Ball Super: Beerus series, but the hype for the improved anime could be higher. Revised animations, pacing, and entirely new sequences have made the newest release more than a standard remaster. But that hasn't stopped Toei, the anime's producers, from making the same mistakes it's been repeating for more than 13 years.

The Battle of Gods movie and Dragon Ball Super anime series helped make Akira Toriyama's franchise more popular than ever. Their dramatic success proved the world still had a big appetite for more adventures with Goku and his friends. And while the Dragon Ball Super: Broly and Super Hero movies delivered in many ways, those who control the rights and production schedules of the beloved franchise have failed to capitalize on that success, holding the franchise back from reaching its full potential.

During the premiere of Dragon Ball Super's final episodes, which featured the incredibly epic conclusion of the universal Tournament of Power, fans flooded the streets of major cities around the world to cheer and enjoy the series together. The anime garnered that sort of reception with the release of the Japanese version of the series, but one can't help but wonder if the hype would have been even greater if the series' largest markets released their dubs at the same time in movie theaters.

Judging by how the world's most popular anime streaming platform, Crunchyroll, has experienced crashes during several anticipated Dragon Ball Super moments, it's clear that a lot of fans around the world are more than willing to watch the series with subtitles. However, there is still a large percentage of hardcore Dragon Ball fans who prefer to wait and watch the franchise's newest releases with the iconic voice actors they grew up with. Unfortunately for them, some of the wait times for those dubs have been much longer than others.

The Japanese language version of the Dragon Ball Super anime premiered in July 2015, with the English version not releasing until January 2017. That was a considerable amount of time for arguably the world's most popular anime series to release its English dub, one of the franchise's largest markets/demographics. Because of the separate release schedules, hype for new releases can be hindered, compared to if Toei made sure the largest markets around the world had their dubs ready so that new Dragon Ball content could be released at the same time in multiple languages.

It's taken little time for the newest Dragon Ball anime to prove that it's more than a remaster, it's a drastic improvement in all the best ways.

Since the Dragon Ball Super anime ended, there have been two movies and one completely separate anime series, Dragon Ball: Daima, released. While the movies made hundreds of millions of dollars at the worldwide box office, Daima received a much more lukewarm reception. Unsurprisingly, all of those releases saw the Japanese version released first, with the English versions of the movies releasing only around a month later. And while English fans did not need to wait as long as they did for the Dragon Ball Super dub, Daima's dub took an extra three months to begin streaming.

Dragon Ball’s current fragmented release model holds it back from even greater heights. Toei is repeating that mistake with Dragon Ball Super: Beerus, which began releasing on Crunchyroll on October 11, 2026. The English dub is likely being worked on as I type this article, but its release date has yet to be announced. And unless Toei changes its current production processes, the highly anticipated Dragon Ball Super: Galactic Patrol arc will also suffer from an uncoordinated launch. And that isn't the only way that Toei is hurting the franchise's potential.

Newer anime series like Jujutsu Kaisen and Demon Slayer have dramatically altered the increasingly popular anime medium in several beneficial and lucrative ways. While their incredible quality and pacing have helped them stand out from many other anime productions, their theatrical releases have both earned their distributors millions of dollars in profit and been used to greatly increase the world's excitement for their anime series' returns. Both qualities that Toei is not currently utilizing with Dragon Ball.

The producers of Demon Slayer's anime have created the perfect format for milking a franchise for all it's worth. Before the decision to make the series' final arc into three separate movies, they helped open Pandora's box with the release of the 2020 film Demon Slayer The Movie: Mugen Train. After the first season’s huge success, millions of fans worldwide hotly anticipated its continuation. When the next section of the story was released as a two-hour movie, that large fanbase was more than anxious to pay for a movie ticket to see the next part on the big screen.

Almost six months later, Demon Slayer's second season finally began releasing on Crunchyroll. It also covered the Mugen Train arc but with additional content and scenes that could not fit in the movie. Fans who were able to see that part of the story in the theaters were more than anxious to share their experience online, which generated even more hype for the anime's return for those who were not able to see the movie in theaters. It was a brilliant business tactic, but future theatrical anime releases would not execute this strategy nearly as effectively.

One of my biggest movie theater disappointments in 2025 was Jujutsu Kaisen: Execution. The series' Shibuya arc remains one of the best seasons of anime to ever release, but some of its greatest battles were unfinished. Its physical release was able to add those missing sections, and Jujutsu Kaisen: Execution could have used those as a way to entice fans to the theater to see the fights as they were intended. Instead, it was an underwhelming summary of the Shibuya arc with a few minutes of the anticipated continuation tagged on at the end. Still, that release made over $40 million.

If Toei wanted to, they could use these lessons to make every Dragon Ball anime release a worldwide event that would take box offices by storm and further generate excitement for its new series. While Dragon Ball: Daima ended up being a disappointment for some, I'm sure others would have joined me at the movie theater if Toei had released the first 4–5 episodes of that series or Dragon Ball Super: Beerus in theaters a few months before they started streaming.

Releases like Demon Slayer: Mugen Train both respected audiences and helped build excitement for the anime series' return, and Toei could do the same with Dragon Ball. On the other hand, regurgitating already released content for an easy cash-in, as Jujutsu Kaisen has, would likely disappoint certain fans like myself. If Toei is willing to change their current Dragon Ball production processes, hopefully they can find a happy medium that will allow even more fans to get excited about each new release while also making the already profitable franchise even more so.

Quentin Tarantino's Major New Netflix Movie Officially Releases In IMAX This Month

📅 Nov 04, 2026  |  Category: Entertainment  |  Read Source →

Nearly 35 years after bursting on the scene with Reservoir Dogs, Quentin Tarantino remains one of the most acclaimed and singularly talented modern filmmakers. The mastermind behind classics such as Pulp Fiction, Kill Bill, and Inglorious Basterds has not helmed a feature film for seven years but returns this month for a major release that ranks among 2026’s most talked about movies.

In 2019, Tarantino wrote and directed Once Upon a Time in Hollywood, a witty, intentionally meandering period piece about 1960s Hollywood, told through the perspective of a fading, alcoholic actor and his effortlessly cool stuntman across three fateful days. In addition to grossing over $400 million at the global box office, the film was lauded for its punchy dialogue, love letter to ‘60s L.A., and duo of stellar lead performances, in particular that of Brad Pitt, whose turn as Cliff Booth earned him an Academy Award.

After seven years, Tarantino follows Once Upon a Time in Hollywood with a grand return to cinema -- in the biggest premium format and on the biggest streaming service.

Quentin Tarantino’s eagerly anticipated return to movies comes in the form of The Further Mis-Adventures of Cliff Booth, a sequel to Once Upon a Time in Hollywood that positions Pitt’s former stuntman in the lead role. Interestingly, Tarantino opted against directing the film as he continues to adhere to a self-imposed limit of 10 feature-length directorial films. As he did not want his final project to be a sequel or a retread of covered ground, Tarantino passed the torch to another acclaimed filmmaker. However, he did write the screenplay for the movie.

Stepping into Tarantino’s lofty shoes is David Fincher, who previously collaborated with Brad Pitt on Seven, Fight Club, and The Curious Case of Benjamin Button. Fincher has had a long relationship with Netflix dating back to House of Cards and continuing with Mindhunter and Mank, making him a perfect choice to spearhead this next chapter in Cliff Booth’s story.

The Further Mis-Adventures of Cliff Booth takes place in 1977, roughly eight years after the events of Once Upon a Time in Hollywood and in the seminal year that saw George Lucas bring the original Star Wars to theaters.

The film picks up with Pitt’s Cliff Booth having transitioned away from full-time stunt work, now taking on odd jobs as a bartender and a cinema projectionist. In the shadows, however, he works as a studio fixer, handling murder, blackmail, extortion, and all manner of dirty secrets for the movers and shakers in Hollywood.

A surprise screening of the first 17 minutes of the film at AFI Fest revealed that Cliff gave up being Rick Dalton’s stuntman due to their growing physical dissimilarities. In more blunt terms, Cliff candidly says in the footage, “Rick got too fat for me to double him anymore.” It is currently unclear if Leonardo DiCaprio will reprise his role as Rick Dalton in the sequel.

Despite DiCaprio’s (presumed) absence, The Further Mis-Adventures of Cliff Booth bolsters an ensemble cast to back Pitt up. Timothy Olyphant will reprise his role as Lancer star James Stacy in the film, while no other cast members from Once Upon a Time in Hollywood are confirmed to appear.

Peter Weller, known for his role in RoboCop, will play Cliff’s father in the film. Yahya Abdul-Mateen II will play Roderick ‘Hot Rod’ Harrison, a Blaxploitation star who Pitt’s character crosses paths with. Rounding out the principal members of the cast are Elizabeth Debicki, Scott Caan, Carla Gugino, Matt Groove, JB Tadena, Corey Fogelmanis, and Karren Karagulian.

Plot details are largely under wraps for the film as promotional material has primarily focused on setting the atmosphere for the story.

On December 8, 2026, Tarantino will publish a companion novel titled The Adventures of Cliff Booth.

The Further Mis-Adventures of Cliff Booth will have a two-week limited IMAX theatrical run in select theaters on November 25, 2026. Tickets can be purchased here. The film will make its global streaming debut on Netflix on December 23, 2026.

The Further Mis-Adventures of Cliff Booth follows Cliff Booth, a former stuntman, set eight years after the events of Once Upon a Time... in Hollywood. Booth now works as a fixer within the studio system, navigating the complexities and challenges of Hollywood's entertainment industry.

'It Follows' Star Officially Addresses Her Return to Another Iconic 2010s Thriller Franchise

📅 Nov 04, 2026  |  Category: Entertainment  |  Read Source →

In a new interview, Monroe addressed the possibility of returning for a sequel

Maika Monroe has built a career as a modern scream queen. After a breakout 2014, when she starred in both It Follows and The Guest, she became a key figure in horror, continuing to revisit the genre in everything from Longlegs to Victorian Psycho. This year, both It Follows and The Guest have been a part of the horror conversation: It Follows just wrapped a sequel, wonderfully titled They Follow, while The Guest just received a quasi-sequel with Onslaught, which director Adam Wingard set in the same continuity as his previous 2014 film.

Monroe notably doesn't appear in Onslaught (there are no repeat characters), but will she ever return to The Guest universe? In a recent interview for her upcoming film, Victorian Psycho, Monroe sounded hopeful.

“I love Adam so much,” she tells Polygon. “We grabbed coffee not too long ago, and he's awesome, so I would love to work with him again, so we'll see.”

The Guest remains one of Wingard’s most beloved films (and this is the guy who directed Godzilla vs. Kong). Written by Simon Barrett and starring Monroe and Dan Stevens, the story follows a charming visitor named David (Stevens) who arrives at the Peterson family home. There, he reveals that he is an army veteran and was a close friend to their son, who died while on tour in Afghanistan. David soon integrates himself into the grieving family, coinciding with strange and violent events taking place in the town. Anna (Monroe), the family’s eldest daughter, begins to grow suspicious of his behavior and past. The Guest marked one of Stevens first freaky roles, an edge that has since become a defining aspect of his career.

Onslaught came out earlier this year and marked a reunion between Wingard and Barrett, who’ve built a long partnership throughout their careers, collaborating on films like A Horrible Way to Die, You’re Next, and the V/H/S franchise.

The film stars Adria Arjona as an army vet with PTSD, who’s forced to defend her community and her daughter against three Terminator-like army experiments who’ve escaped a nearby facility.

Onslaught also features Stevens as Dr. Hans Kammler, an aging German scientist with Nazi ties and the mastermind behind the super-soldiers stalking the protagonist. Wingard confirmed that the events of Onslaught take place near the town that’s featured in The Guest. Both films are a part of his and Barrett’s "KPG-verse," named after the shadowy organization that runs the super-soldier experiments and appears in several of their projects.

Even though Stevens plays different characters in both films, Wingard suggested there might be a connection between them. “I think he’s related to the genesis of the David character in some form [or] fashion, and I think that’s something that maybe we’ll more literally explore hopefully in future installments,” he told The Wrap.

Stevens, for his part, is happy to continue to explore that universe. “We’re definitely open to more KPG-verse. I don’t know whether a Guest 2, per se, is on the cards anytime soon, but you never know,” he told The Hollywood Reporter. “We’ve teed everything up for an Onslaught sequel as well, so watch this space.”

Whether we get a sequel to David and Anna’s stories or something new set in the same universe, the door is open. Monroe, Wingard, and Stevens all seem to agree: The world of The Guest is one they’d be happy to return to.

Transfer agents do the often-invisible work of maintaining official shareholder records and supporting issuer functions. Their participation matters because a token representing a security needs clear ownership, corporate-action handling and investor rights as well as a way to change hands. Putting a token on a ledger does not automatically deliver proxy voting, dividends, reconciliations or legal recognition.

A tokenized instrument must connect technical records with enforceable financial rights. Trading software can move a token quickly, but an issuer, custodian, transfer agent or settlement bank may still have to recognise the result. Real settlement infrastructure is judged by whether it works on bad days, not just during presentations. iPad mini OLED vs. iPad mini: What's new?

📅 Nov 04, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Apple's new iPad mini gets a bunch of upgrades, but they come at a cost.

Apple doesn't update its iPad mini every year, which is why each new generation usually gets a huge performance boost. The new iPad mini is powered by the 5-core, 2nm A20 Pro SoC, which Apple says is up to 2.7 times faster in graphics performance and up to 4.5 times faster in AI workloads than the A17 Pro in its predecessor. It comes in the same three storage configurations as last time, and the 512GB model features an extra CPU core and a dual 16-core Neural Engine.

While both models support Apple Intelligence and Siri AI, the A20 Pro should be more suited to demanding on-device AI tasks. The outgoing iPad mini offered 8GB of RAM, and while Apple doesn't officially list how much memory its phones and tablets have, the new iPad mini likely has 12GB of RAM. Battery life claims haven't changed, but the new iPad mini charges faster (capable of hitting 50 percent in 30 minutes) with a compatible adapter.

Unfortunately, color options have been reduced to just Midnight and Starlight. The price has also increased: the new iPad mini costs $200 more, starting at $699 for the 128GB model. If spec'd with cellular connectivity and a nano-texture glass finish, the 512GB iPad mini will set you back a whopping $1,249 — that's iPad Pro money. We'll let you decide if it's worth it; for now, let's look at the changes Apple has made to its compact tablet offering this year.

The big iPad mini upgrade this time is the OLED panel. That makes it the only other iPad in Apple's lineup with an OLED display aside from the iPad Pro. It's roughly the same resolution as last time, but can get much brighter at up to 1,200 nits of peak HDR brightness. OLED also brings other benefits: the new iPad mini is the same height and nearly the same width, but is thinner at 5.4mm and lighter at 255 grams — likely thanks to the thinner OLED panel. In comparison, the A17 Pro iPad mini maxes out at 500 nits of SDR brightness, weighs 293 grams and is almost an entire millimeter thicker at 6.3mm.

Thailand: Two Uyghurs at Risk of Return to China

📅 Nov 04, 2026  |  Category: Travel  |  Read Source →

Click to expand Image Detainees stand behind cell bars at the police immigration detention center in Bangkok, Thailand, January 21, 2019. © 2019 Sakchai Lalit/AP Photo

(Bangkok) – Thai authorities should not forcibly return two Uyghur men to China, where they face grave risks of torture and other serious abuses, Human Rights Watch said today. The two men, whose names are not public for security reasons, were transferred from prison to immigration detention in Bangkok after receiving a royal pardon, apparently in preparation for deportation.

“Previous Thai governments have kept Uyghurs in inhumane detention for years and then complied with Beijing’s demands to send them to China, where they were forcibly disappeared,” said Elaine Pearson, Asia director at Human Rights Watch. “Thai Prime Minister Anutin Charnvirakul should end forced returns to China and allow the two Uyghur men to travel to a safe third country.”

The two Uyghur men have been detained by Thai authorities since 2014, when one of them was only 14 years old. They were serving a prison sentence for offenses related to an escape attempt from immigration detention in January 2020. On October 21, 2026, the authorities transferred them from Bangkok Central Prison to the Bangkok Immigration Detention Center Suan Phlu after they had served most of their seven-year sentence. Thai Foreign Ministry officials told a parliamentary committee that in August the Chinese embassy in Bangkok demanded their return to China.

Under Thai immigration law and regulations, the two Uyghur men will be deported to China unless they object to the deportation and are resettled to a third country. Lawyers representing them told Human Rights Watch that they have already informed Thai authorities that the two do not consent to be sent to China. Thai immigration authorities have repeatedly refused to allow the United Nations refugee agency (UNHCR) access to the men to determine their refugee status.

The Thai government is obligated to respect the international law principle of nonrefoulement, barring anyone’s forcible return to a place where they would face a genuine risk of persecution, torture or other ill-treatment, or a threat to their life or other comparable serious human rights violations. Refoulement is prohibited by the UN Convention Against Torture, to which Thailand is a party, as well as customary international law. Thailand’s Prevention and Suppression of Torture and Enforced Disappearance Act prohibits deportation when there is a substantial risk of torture or ill-treatment.

In March 2014, Thai police in Songkhla province, near the Malaysia border, arrested about 220 Uyghur men, women, and children, charged them with immigration violations, and soon after transferred them to an immigration detention facility in Bangkok. In several separate incidents around the same period, the authorities arrested dozens of other Uyghurs and placed them in immigration detention facilities around the country. In July 2015, about 170 of the Uyghur women and children detained in Songkhla were released to Turkey. A week later, however, the Thai authorities forcibly transferred over 100 Uyghur men to Chinese authorities, who flew them to China.

On February 27, 2025, Thai authorities took 40 Uyghur men from immigration detention and forcibly returned them to China. Upon the men’s return, the Thai authorities participated in a Chinese government-organized propaganda trip to show some of these men with their families. Despite promises by the Thai and Chinese authorities, there has been no credible monitoring of those Uyghurs since their return.

UN human rights experts issued a statement on the one-year anniversary of the deportation and said the families of returnees “have received no communication, no confirmation of the place of detention or whereabouts, and no indication as to whether their loved ones are even alive.”

Following the deportation, UN High Commissioner for Human Rights Volker Türk said in February 2025 that it was “a clear violation of international human rights laws and standards,” and urged Thai authorities to “ensure there are no further deportations and the remaining members of the group, including potential refugees and asylum-seekers, being held in Thailand are fully protected in accordance with their obligations under international law.”

Uyghurs are Turkic-speaking Muslims, most of whom live in the Xinjiang Uyghur Autonomous Region in China’s northwest. Over the past 10 years, China’s repression of Uyghurs has increased significantly as the authorities have intensified a widespread and systematic campaign of human rights abuses against them, several amounting to crimes against humanity.

The authorities have subjected the population to arbitrary detention, unjust imprisonment, mass surveillance, forced labor, cultural and religious persecution, and severe movement restrictions. Uyghurs considered to have left China illegally are, if forcibly returned, viewed with intense suspicion and subject to arbitrary detention, interrogation, torture, and other cruel, inhuman, and degrading treatment.

“Prior to the UN Human Rights Council’s review of Thailand’s human rights record on November 9, governments should press Thailand to immediately end its abusive treatment of Uyghurs,” Pearson said. “The Thai authorities should be protecting Uyghurs fleeing persecution, not jailing them, and certainly not returning them to harm’s way.”

Jon Ossoff wins reelection in Georgia, easily defeating Republican Mike Collins

📅 Nov 04, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Sen. Jon Ossoff, D-Ga., speaks during a campaign event on Oct. 31, 2026, in Atlanta. Ryan Murphy/AP hide caption

Midterms coverage: Live results | What to know | Stories around the countryStay connected: NPR Politics Podcast | NPR Politics newsletter | NPR app

Jon Ossoff, the only incumbent Senate Democrat defending a seat in a state President Trump carried in 2024, has won the U.S. Senate race in Georgia.

Ossoff defeated Republican Rep. Mike Collins, according to a race call by The Associated Press.

A year ago, Democrats and Republicans envisioned the race in Georgia as one of the cycle's marquee Senate showdowns.

But in Collins, the GOP in Georgia nominated a candidate whose personal baggage alienated independents and some Republican-leaning voters.

In Ossoff, Democrats were buoyed by a candidate who won respect at home even from some Republican officials for the strength of his constituent services. And outside Georgia, Ossoff electrified Democrats searching for fresh faces to lead the party out of the political wilderness.

On paper, the race should have been closer. In 2021, Democrats flipped both of Georgia's Senate seats blue for the first time in a generation. In the 2022 midterms, Democratic Sen. Raphael Warnock barely held onto his seat in a runoff, even against a deeply flawed opponent.

Otherwise, Republicans have continued to win nearly every other statewide race in Georgia.

In the Republican primary this spring, Trump endorsed Collins over former football coach Derek Dooley, a first-time candidate backed by Georgia's popular Republican Gov. Brian Kemp.

Collins, the owner of a family trucking company, struggled to dispense with scrutiny over his ties to family and former staffers who have espoused white nationalist and antisemitic views. Collins also continued to embrace Trump's false claims about widespread election fraud in 2020.

Rep. Mike Collins, R-Ga., at a campaign rally on Oct. 24, 2026, in Perry, Ga. Mark Schiefelbein/AP hide caption

Ossoff, a former congressional aide and documentary filmmaker, first appeared on the national stage when he waged a closer-than-expected attempt to flip a suburban House race in a special election in 2017. After Georgia sent Ossoff to the Senate in a 2021 runoff, he gained attention for his methodical grilling of Trump appointees during hearings on the Hill.

As clips from Ossoff excoriating alleged graft in Trump's orbit in rally speeches ricocheted around the internet over the course of the campaign, many Democrats began to mention him as a possible presidential candidate. Ossoff has said he is not running for the White House in 2028.
