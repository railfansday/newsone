Rosalind Eleazar Thanks Chris Rock for Making Misty Green ‘Messy’ and ‘Difficult’ in Her ELLE Women in Hollywood Speech

📅 Nov 17, 2026  |  Category: Other  |  Read Source →

She thanked her co-star and director for “writing a story with a heavy dose of discomfort.”

After delivering standout performances this year in Slow Horses and Misty Green, Rosalind Eleazar was honored at ELLE’s 2026 Women in Hollywood event. During the celebration—which took place at The Four Seasons Beverly Hills on November 16—she used her speech to thank Chris Rock for choosing her to star in Misty Green, and explained that she pulled strength from her mother for the role.

“I want to thank Chris Rock for writing a woman like Misty and writing a story with a heavy dose of discomfort,” she said. “She’s messy. She’s contradictory, she’s difficult, she’s funny, and at times she’s really quite ugly in her behavior. But underneath all of that is someone in tremendous pain. Someone who has become so accustomed to surviving that she’s almost lost touch with herself and become numb.”

Eleazar took a moment to spotlight her mother’s own achievements, and praised her for being self-sufficient, determined, and tenacious. “When I was little, I thought my mother was some sort of superwoman,” she recalled. “She worked in a male-dominated industry. She achieved things that are still beyond my wildest dreams, and somehow managed to raise a child at the same time.”

Such accomplishments led Eleazar to have “a rather naive understanding of what was possible for women.” She explained, “I didn’t really see the barriers, because I had this extraordinary woman in front of me who seemed to walk straight through them.”

She added, “I think what we often perceive as strength can sometimes be a form of armor. A way of protecting something much more vulnerable underneath. And whilst my mum and Misty Green are very different people, I am drawn to characters that are full and have multiple facets, and are human, and are flawed.”

In addition to thanking her mom, Rock, and her fellow nominees, she also thanked Daniel Kaluuya—her Misty Green co-star, who also introduced her for the honor—for offering her a source of support. “He’s been one of my greatest champions and such a wonderful friend. Someone that I can call and have called at any hour of the day with some existential question about this sort of weird journey we’re on,” she concluded her speech. “Daniel, your generosity, your friendship, and your extraordinary talent mean so much to me. And I feel incredibly lucky to have worked alongside you.”

Google's core updates arrive with a start time and a scope. Everything else you've read about them came from somewhere else.

The routine is familiar by now. Google posts a sentence. It has a name, a start time, and a note that the rollout may take up to two weeks. Within the hour, somebody has published a “what we know so far.” By the evening, there’s a list of winners and losers. Before the week is out, with the rollout still running, there’s a recovery guide in seven steps.

I’ve watched this for most of 20 years. The only part that has changed is the sentence. It used to be longer.

When Google launched what the industry later called Panda, in February 2011, they wrote a post about it. The post said what the change was for: sites that add little, copy from others, or just aren’t very useful. It said how big it was: 11.8% of queries. You could argue with the result, and plenty did, but there was a stated target to argue with.

Here is the entry for the November 2026 core update, which started on Nov. 10. It was released. It applies globally and in all languages. It may take up to two weeks. That’s the lot: three facts, none of them about your site.

Google has been open about why. In a Q&A on Search updates, it said it changes Search thousands of times a year and announces the ones it considers notable, about 10 a year when it last counted in public. Its core updates documentation adds that smaller core updates go out continually and aren’t announced at all. So a named update is a ranking change that Google decided to put on a calendar. The calendar entry tells you when. It tells you nothing about what changed, or about what happens in between.

The announcement shrank to a timestamp, and the commentary expanded to fill the space.

The date does have a use. Google’s own guidance uses it: wait a week after the rollout finishes, then compare that week with the week before it started. The date draws a line through your data so you know where to look. Visibility trackers do a similar job across the market; they show which sites moved.

What neither of them shows is why. Which system changed, and why it moved your site in particular, is known inside Google and nowhere else. A rank tracker and a strong feeling won’t close that gap.

That hasn’t slowed anyone down. An obscure tool vendor published a winners-and-losers study of the November update within a day or two of the launch. It gave the wrong start date and called it the sixth core update of the year. Google’s dashboard lists three. I’d say the analysis was rushed, but that implies there was something to analyze.

The closest thing I know to a core update is the annual performance review.

Anyone who has had a job knows the meeting. You walk in and get a rating that was settled long before the meeting was booked. It reflects the year you had. It also reflects the people you were measured against, because plenty of companies calibrate ratings across a team; you can do the same work as last year and drop a grade because three colleagues did better. Search works the same way. A page is ranked against every other page that could answer the query, so a site can lose ground without changing anything.

A core update is review day. It’s when the rating becomes visible. Nobody sensible leaves that meeting and asks what changed about the meeting.

The comparison stops in one place. After a review you get a manager, written feedback and a chance to ask what went wrong. After a core update you get a dashboard entry. That missing conversation is the entire market for update commentary: an industry of people offering to be the manager who explains your rating, none of whom were in the calibration room.

The strongest objection to all this is that the losses are real, and it’s correct. Companies do lose a large share of their organic revenue overnight, and people lose jobs over it. The practitioners who spend their time on recoveries are dealing with real businesses in real trouble.

There’s also evidence that sometimes the reviewer gets it wrong. In October 2024, Google invited a group of independent publishers hit by the September 2023 helpful content update to its campus. According to people who were in the room, they were told their content wasn’t the problem, and that they shouldn’t count on getting their traffic back. That is an awkward fact for anyone, me included, who says most affected sites had it coming.

Look at what it does to the recovery guide, though. If the content wasn’t the issue, “seven steps for improving your content” were never going to work. The publishers with the best case against Google are also the best evidence that nobody outside it could diagnose them.

Now the less sympathetic group, which in my experience is the bigger one.

Most of the affected sites I’ve been asked to look at over 20 years had the same history. Somebody decided what they could get away with before finding out what the market already offered. They hadn’t researched what customers needed or looked at how good the existing answers were. They picked keywords with volume, wrote about them, and launched.

When that didn’t work, they went looking for a shortcut, and social media had plenty. A thread with a promise that was too good to be true, and was. A “hack” from somebody who discovered SEO in the summer and by autumn had a conference slot and a YouTube channel. In office terms, that’s career advice from the colleague who joined in August and already runs a workshop. They copied it without asking why it would work. Some of these tricks do work for a few months, which is the worst outcome available, because the fall then lands inside an update window and gets blamed on the update.

Then they hired SEO to make it work, as if rankings were a delivery service for businesses that forgot to have a product. I’ve written about the wall that approach runs into; it hasn’t moved.

This is the employee who did the minimum all year and arrives at the review certain that the rating system has changed, and has brought printouts. In fairness to them, they have a problem that needs solving. “The algorithm changed” is something you can say to a board. “We built a site with nothing to say and then paid someone to get it found” tends not to make the deck.

So there’s a buyer for an outside cause, and the update coverage supplies one. It comes with a diagnosis nobody outside Google could have made and a retainer that renews before the next update can contradict it. The seller gets a client, and the client gets a story. The site stays exactly as useful as it was, which was the problem.

This trade feels legitimate to the people in it because of how the industry thinks about quality.

Take E-E-A-T. Google coined it for its quality raters, and Google’s own documentation says it isn’t a specific ranking factor. The industry read that and sold E-E-A-T audits anyway, with scores for a thing Google says it has no score for. Somewhere there is an author bio that was added to fix a business model.

The traits themselves are older than the initialism. Peter Morville’s user experience honeycomb had “credible,” “useful,” and “valuable” in it in 2004, as facets of a product, with no ranking attached. The industry needed Google to hand it an initialism before it noticed that people prefer things they can trust.

“JavaScript SEO” has the same shape: it’s progressive enhancement, which web developers were practicing before anyone gave it a conference track. I made that case in Stop Learning SEO and won’t repeat it.

If quality is a list of items to tick, then a core update is a new edition of the list, and you need someone to tell you what’s on it. There is never a shortage of volunteers. Back in the office, that’s working to the KPIs. The person who works to the KPIs has to know the moment they change. The person who is good at the job mostly finds out on review day that they still are.

So people who hold quality as a checklist need each update explained; the announcement explains nothing, and so somebody makes one up.

The first question a business could ask after an update is “What did this update change for my site?” Answering it would take the list of systems that changed, how much each one counts, and what every competing page did in the same fortnight. Nobody outside Google has those. You can still pay for an answer. It’ll arrive as a confident PDF with a traffic-light table, and it will tell you to improve content quality, show more expertise, and prune the thin pages.

Now read the PDF from the update before. It says the same thing. So will the one after the next update, because the advice was never derived from the update. If the remedy is the same whatever changed, the change taught nobody anything.

The second question is “what do the people I want as customers need that they can’t already get?” It’s a worse question to be asked. It has no villain and no deadline; it can’t be handed to a thread, and the answer may be that the business has nothing to add yet. It’s also slower and more expensive to answer than a PDF. But it can be answered by people you can find and talk to, and it could have been answered before the site launched.

The fair objection to the second question is Google. Google is in the business of taking in what other people know and serving it back as its own answer, and AI Overviews and AI Mode have made that the main event. The part of the web where people found you through a search result is being eaten, and Google is the one holding the fork. I’ve covered what that did to publishers’ traffic. So why polish anything for a channel that’s shrinking?

Because the second question was never about the channel. A site is a product, and what people need from it doesn’t change when fewer of them arrive through Google. What does change is how much of what you know you should hand over for free. I’ve argued that publishing everything is a terrible business strategy, and I still think so. Holding back the method is sensible. Shipping a site that’s a bare outline of what you’re supposed to be good at is a different decision, and it fails the customer long before it fails a ranking system.

The line between those two is thin, and it’s meant to be. It sits in a different place for every business, and no update, checklist, or thread will tell you where yours is. A search engine certainly won’t; it looks after its own interests, as it always has. The only people who know how much is enough are the ones you’re trying to sell to, which is one more reason to go and ask them.

In the office, it’s the gap between “what did the calibration committee change this year?” and “what did I do this year?” Only one of those is yours to find out, and it’s the one that doesn’t come with somebody else to blame.

Google will post another sentence soon enough. The recovery guide for it already exists. It’s the one from last time.

Critically, ULA did not provide any specific information about the “low booster performance.” This matters greatly because the core stage of the Vulcan rocket is powered by two BE-4 engines, which are provided by Blue Origin. That company’s own New Glenn rocket uses a similar but slightly more powerful version of the BE-4 engine. And New Glenn experienced its own catastrophic failure in May during a ground test, a failure that later was attributed to a main oxygen valve on one of the BE-4 engines.

Information about the Vulcan failure is being closely held by ULA and Blue Origin, who have only acknowledged that they’re working together to investigate the cause. According to two sources, the investigation is “incredibly complicated” and remains in the early stages.

However, according to preliminary data, two independent sources indicated to Ars that the problem is currently believed to have originated on the launch vehicle side rather than in the engines themselves. The issue may be related to systems that feed methane and liquid oxygen propellant to the engines.

ULA engineers are looking at the possibility that one of the two BE-4 engines started producing lower thrust shortly after liftoff and never ramped back up to full throttle for the remainder of the burn. Video observations from high-zoom tracking cameras appeared to show one engine slightly dimmer than the other after Vulcan jettisoned its solid rocket boosters nearly two minutes into flight. An asymmetric shutdown of the BE-4 engines could also have contributed to the sudden attitude motion indicated by ULA’s telemetry-driven real-time animation near stage separation.

The Federal Aviation Administration is overseeing the investigation, which is being led by ULA. Again, this information is preliminary, and no definitive conclusions can or should be drawn.

This was the fifth flight of the Vulcan rocket since its debut in January 2024. Two of the previous flights have experienced issues with the nozzles on its solid rocket boosters, but in both cases, the rocket was able to compensate for the reduced and asymmetric thrust and reach its intended orbit. The second nozzle issue caused Vulcan to stand down for nine months.

This latest setback is likely to sideline Vulcan for at least half a year, and probably longer. Most impacted will be the US Space Force, which has national security payloads waiting for a ride to space on Vulcan, and Amazon, which had more than a dozen launches of its Amazon Leo constellation on the manifest for 2027. It would exacerbate a worsening launch crisis in the United States.

There are potentially serious implications for Blue Origin as well, which is seeking to return New Glenn to flight as early as January. Last Tuesday, the company said a modified BE-4 main oxygen valve had successfully completed qualification testing.

“Our modified valve has already been incorporated into engines slated for upcoming flights, and we are now rolling it out across the remaining fleet,” the company said. “We expect to complete the anomaly investigation later this month.”

If Vulcan’s failure were ultimately due to a problem with the BE-4 engine, it would certainly delay the New Glenn rocket’s return to flight. This would be a setback not just for Blue Origin but for NASA, which is counting on the super heavy lift rocket to do much of the heavy lifting for its Artemis Program and Moon Base.

Kansas City Current's grit earns them spot in NWSL Championship while Gotham's logistics problems linger

📅 Nov 17, 2026  |  Category: Sports  |  Read Source →

If not listed, please contact your TV provider.

NEW YORK – The awkward logistics of Sunday's NWSL semifinal at Icahn Stadium, a quirky little venue sandwiched on an island in between Manhattan and Queens, were supposed to give way once Gotham FC and the Kansas City Current took to the pitch for one of the league's showpiece matches. When the Current's Croix Bethune opened the scoring in the 20th minute with an incredible looping shot off a free kick, the theme of the day was in motion. The league would make the best of the unideal circumstances with a top-quality soccer game, or at least with a few highlight-reel moments nestled into a match contested by a collection of the world's most talented players.

"I've been working on free kicks all year," Bethune said post-match, the Current justified in their decision to sign her last winter even if the validation was no longer needed, "and a lot more leading into the playoffs so just practice makes perfect."

Icahn Stadium's irregularities, though, seemed to reflect the game it unexpectedly became the host of six days earlier. The Current's 2-1 win came courtesy of a chippy game plan that would have been the case if the match had taken place at a traditional soccer-specific stadium but it felt especially at home at Icahn Stadium and amidst its elements. The teams played against the wind during New York's first properly cold weekend of the season, and Gotham faced a setting sun that impacted their vision during the first half. Bethune's impressive strike was really the only on-field spectacle to be witnessed on Randall's Island.

BEND IT LIKE BETHUNE 💫 KC takes the lead!NWSL Playoffs presented by Google Pixel pic.twitter.com/F4mQePT5QJ

"We knew it was going to be a physical game, without a doubt," Current forward Haley Hopkins said. "Like I said a million times, Gotham's a very, very good team and so we had to be super disciplined in our structure and catch them on their transition and we were just able to put the ball in the back of the net when we had our opportunities."

The introduction of Gotham's Rose Lavelle, who has been dealing with niggling fitness issues over the last few weeks, did not change the smash-and-grab energy of the game. Much as there was a week ago in their quarterfinal win over the Seattle Reign, there was a dull quality to Gotham's attack despite their star power. Over 180 minutes of possession play, the NWSL Shield winners managed zero shots on target and 0.66 expected goals, their midseason marquee signing Sam Kerr managing no shots along the way and mustering just one attempt in her last five matches. To say they were unlucky would be a stretch but their lively start to the second half gave their failed attempts to put a shot on frame a comedic feel.

"I think we were kind of our own worst enemy today," midfielder Savannah McCaskill said. "I think at times we played into what they wanted to do and their style of play. They want a very chippy game. They want to get into tackles. They want to draw fouls. They want to break up the game. They want to play long and that's not our style. We want to keep the ball. We want to control tempo, and we like to play pretty football. That's [what] we pride ourselves on … but I think we need to find a better way to balance that out, especially in this league. How can we meet the demand of a chippy game while also controlling the tempo? I think we did a really good job when we played them at home the last time [a 2-1 win on Aug. 14]. They scored early but then we completely dominated the rest of the game and I think we failed to do that today and so I think that's really the determining factor."

In other words, it was the perfect setup for Gotham to equalize off an own goal from Laney Rouse, a week after benefiting from another own goal from the Reign's Jordyn Bugg. Those two goals would be the only ones in their postseason run, just as one might expect from the NWSL Shield winners and reigning champions who entered the playoffs as the favorites to win another title.

Gotham FC find a way to tie up the semifinal 💪NWSL Playoffs presented by Google Pixel pic.twitter.com/Itbzs4Bhz1

Gotham goalkeeper Ann-Katrin Berger then slipped into game-saving mode with two important blocks while defender Tierna Davidson did what she could to hold the Current at bay, attempting to create a villain arc rivaled only by Argentina's run to the World Cup final. Though the hosts had lucked their way into an even game by the 88th minute, the dark and cold conditions at a downsized stadium in an obscure part of New York meant the game beckoned for extra time. One final smash and grab was undoubtedly in the cards, though where it would come from was hard to guess – the Current had put in the work to win but soccer's chaotic nature had already weaseled its way into the game.

In the end, it was the Current's Haley Hopkins who had the final say in the 89th minute off of – what else – a set piece, a free kick that led the forward to finally evade Berger with a shot off the crossbar. After losing to them twice in three previous meetings this season, the Current earned their spot in next week's NWSL Championship (Saturday, 8 p.m. ET, CBS, Paramount+) on the back of a well-executed gameplan.

"As much as we tried to analyze certain teams in the league that have given them trouble, we really took information from the games we've played against them, all the ways they challenged us," Current head coach Chris Armas said. "There's been games even where they get high [expected goals], too much progression, too much in our box and we just tried to then look at all the scenes and moments when we made it hard for them so we just tried to take from yesterday and apply it to today, [that] type of thing. We gave up zero shots on goal in 90 minutes. The commitment from our players to just will themselves to it to stick to a match plan that was going to be largely about taking away spaces, controlling the middle of the pitch and if we can do that on the day – and and we did that by running after them, too, and pressing in moments of high intensity – we not only might stop what they're trying to do on one end and maybe force the turnovers that could challenge them so a little bit of a chess match there in moments."

The Current were no slouches, finishing fourth in the regular season and now on a  seven-game winning streak en route to next week's championship. Bethune's star quality shone on Sunday, while the team's record with two-time NWSL MVP Temwa Chawinga and U.S. women's national team standout Michelle Cooper is formidable. It did not erase the fact that they slayed a giant along the way, knocking out the Shield winners and a team that had picked up five major titles in four seasons.

"Gotham has just raised the bar around the league and in our country here, so the way they play, the way the coach sets them up, the talent on the team, the type of games they can put you in, you have to come with a plan," Armas said. "They're a team that, again, if iron sharpens iron type of thing, we try to push teams and we try to raise the bar."

Gotham, though, had not looked the part of the NWSL's gold standard team. Their drastic run, while currently headlined by USWNT players Lavelle and Jaedyn Shaw, is built on the back of an effective defensive structure that has carried an inconsistent offense. Lavelle and, in particular, Shaw have picked up much of the slack en route to the NWSL Shield and Kerr was supposed to bolster their attack after Esther Gonzalez's shock departure for Saudi club Al Qadsiah. They finished the regular season sixth in the NWSL for expected goals with 49.01 but had a genuine finishing problem, notching just 39 goals and ranking in the league's bottom six in that category. Head coach Juan Carlos Amoros is not sounding the alarm just yet but as they enter another offseason, the attacking composition of the team deserves yet another look.

"Of course, we want to create more chances, more shots," Amoros said. "We all do but we look at the way we play. We look at the way we create chances. We look at who does that. I think when it comes to these games, could we have [made] some better decisions? Yes, we did. We actually create some good moments. Yes, I wouldn't over-think about it. Something that I'm happy [about]? No but at the same time, we work a lot to make sure that those moments happen. So you know the set pieces, those situations, the open plays, final decisions … Definitely, something that we want to be better [at]. We want to score more goals. I think that we are the best team defending in the league with our aggressive approach to defending but we want to also be able to create and score more goals."

More importantly, though, Gotham were not treated like the bar-raising team they have become over the years. Icahn Stadium has its charms and is a worthy inclusion on the list of wacky and wonderful places that embody American soccer, an enjoyably chaotic slice of the world's most popular game. It was also a back-up plan for a team that has called Sports Illustrated Stadium home for five years, settling in nicely to a state-of-the-art soccer-specific build for MLS' Red Bull New York that can hold 20,000 more fans than the 5,000-seater Icahn Stadium can. The pitch at Sports Illustrated Stadium, managed by RBNY, had fallen into disrepair and with the primary tenants' end-of-season friendly scheduled for the night before, there was not enough time to fix it. The quirks of Icahn Stadium only served as a reminder that Gotham – and the NWSL – should not have actually been there, even if the field at Randall's Island was better suited for the occasion.

"I want to be really clear and direct in saying that in these types of situations, the field is never an excuse," winger Midge Purce said. "We lost today because we weren't the better team. That being said, I would say it's inexcusable. It's not what you want in a professional league. I think it's a bad look for everyone involved and we always want to be at Sports Illustrated [Stadium] in front of our fans when we earned that right throughout the season but I would say it had no effect on performance today."

The Current's trip to Icahn Stadium is not where the NWSL's efforts to make lemonade end, either. Saturday's semifinal, in which the Washington Spirit beat the Utah Royals to book their spot in a championship game at their home of Audi Field, only added to it. One of the Spirit's sponsors underwrote the costs to push the start time of the Secretaries' Cup, a Division III football game, by a few hours but both matches were played on a field with soccer and football lines. "I think we will never know what happened if we would have played [at Sports Illustrated Stadium] on a good pitch," Amoros said. "I think it was the right decision to come here. I think the pitch in the other stadium was unfortunately unplayable under our view. The last couple of games we picked up injuries. Luckily, nothing that has been a season-ending injury but we have to protect our players and guarantee the quality of football so to thank the club, obviously.

"Overall, I think it's been a sad weekend. I think also the situation in Washington yesterday. This league deserves better. The two, arguably, best teams, [most] consistent, having to deal with these situations and then yesterday, you sit down to watch that game around the world and you're seeing those American football lines across today. Credit to the club and the fans. I think they've done an amazing job here. The fans welcomed us. The club has really shifted everything to play here but we couldn't play in our ground where we could have had more people, where we are unbeaten in 13 games at the moment and but I think we took that decision, putting players' safety first, and the outcome is the outcome."
