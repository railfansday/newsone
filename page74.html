Personally, I still haven't wrapped my head around BioWare's closure. The legendary home of Mass Effect, Star Wars: Knights of the Old Republic, and Dragon Age was a standard-bearer in modern RPG design for so long; it seemed like it could weather any storm. Alas, the industry right now is difficult, but even former leadership is struggling to comprehend this turn of events.

On The Critical Path podcast, Casey Hudson, former BioWare general manager and creator of the original Mass Effect, explained where his head's at as the company closes. "Part of the reason I came back and wanted to be GM of the studio, and was interested in that, was because I've just got so many friends there and I love the studio," he says.

And I wanted to do whatever I could in the years I was there to try and set it up for some kind of longevity," he explains. Hudson left BioWare twice: first exiting in 2014 and winding up at Microsoft for a spell, then returning to upper management in 2017 before leaving again in 2020.

"There are things that you think are just going to last forever," he adds. "But I think that even though we all worried that the day would come when it would get shut down, it's still a different feeling to realize that it is actually closed."

I suspect the notion of an unceremonious end lingered in the air quite a few times, especially over the last ten years or so. You had the rocky reception to Mass Effect: Andromeda, and the years it took to make Dragon Age: The Veilguard, only for the game to get a muted response. These are the kinds of things that can make any studio unsustainable.

But it always seemed like BioWare had another banger in it, and what we've heard about Mass Effect 5 indicated the team had the right ideas. Hudson mentions that he's among the chorus wondering if we'll ever get some version of that game. EA could sell the franchise to someone else, but it may hoard the dormant IP, too.

"It's very difficult," he comments. "Especially for folks that I know that are on that team. I think everyone's just hoping there's some way forward, both individually for folks in their careers, but also for the great games that BioWare made."

What Do We Do With Leftover Love?

📅 Nov 16, 2026  |  Category: Other  |  Read Source →

After years or decades, problems and patterns can become deeply entrenched. Be a fly on the wall during a sample couples therapy session and witness the slow, deliberate work of changing those patterns for a more enduring love.

Self Tests are all about you. Are you outgoing or introverted? Are you a narcissist? Does perfectionism hold you back? Find out the answers to these questions and more with Psychology Today.

Posted November 16, 2026 | Reviewed by Ekua Hagan

He was everything you had ever wanted. Kind, gentle, thoughtful. Almost too good to be true. You imagined a future together, a home, a family, a lifetime of ordinary mornings. Then, one day, while making eggs for breakfast, you saw the messages. The pan was still warm. The sun was coming through the window. Nothing around you had changed, yet it's all gone. In a matter of seconds, the man you thought you knew disappeared. The future you had imagined vanished with him. But the love you felt still lingers.

You can throw the eggs into the bin, but the feelings cannot disappear as quickly. Love involves several interconnected brain systems. Dopamine-rich reward pathways contribute to desire and anticipation (Fisher et al., 2010); the brain's natural opioid system helps make closeness comforting and social rejection distressing (Hsu et al., 2013; Nummenmaa et al., 2016); and limbic regions, particularly the amygdala, help connect emotional experiences with memory (Cahill et al., 1996). The truth can change what you think in seconds, but it cannot instantly erase what your brain has learned over months or years, so do not let people tell you that you are "co-dependent" or "weak." You have a normal brain that might play tricks on you.

You have leftover love, and that leftover love can be weaponised against you (Lesiak & Gelsthorpe, 2025). He may use the feelings that remain to persuade you to let him stay, asking you to ignore what you have discovered, silence your judgment, and compromise the very values that define who you are.

He has already betrayed you. Now he is asking you to betray yourself for him.

You are the one expected to swallow your anger, silence your judgment, and call it "forgiveness." Look at the bargain. He gets to keep the woman who loves him. And you are expected to give up the woman who knows the truth.

Decades ago, psychiatrist Judith Herman wrote about it in Trauma and Recovery (1992):

His ultimate goal appears to be the creation of a willing victim. Hostages, political prisoners, battered women, and slaves have all remarked upon the captor’s curious psychological dependence upon his victim. George Orwell gives voice to the totalitarian mind in the novel 1984:

“We are not content with negative obedience, nor even with the most abject submission. When finally you surrender to us, it must be of your own free will. We do not destroy the heretic because he resists us; so long as he resists us we never destroy him. We convert him, we capture his inner mind, we reshape him. We burn all evil and all illusion out of him; we bring him over to our side, not in appearance, but genuinely, heart and soul.”

Read those last words again. Heart and soul.

It is not enough that you stay. You must want to stay. It is not enough that you forgive him. You must learn to see his betrayal as something you can live with. You must look at the evidence of your own eyes and persuade yourself that it no longer matters.

And if keeping him means abandoning everything you believe in, what exactly are you trying to save—and what's left—when you do that?

Some wounds are easier to bear because you know you stood by your values while receiving them. In Kraków, officers Jakub Kulik and Artur Morański were seriously wounded confronting a mass shooter. Poland calls them heroes, and people across the country are grateful for their courage and service.

Then there is another kind of wound. One that no surgeon can stitch. Amy Winehouse sings it in "You Know I'm No Good": “I cheated myself, like I knew I would.” By the end of the song, she is no longer simply confessing what she did. She is passing judgment on herself.

Imagine waking up beside a man you no longer respect, living a life you never wanted, having persuaded yourself to accept things you once knew were unacceptable. You kept him, perhaps. But somewhere along the way, you lost the ability to trust your own judgment.

That is the deeper cruelty of weaponised love (Lesiak & Gelsthorpe, 2025). He does not need you to believe he is innocent. He needs you to stop caring that he isn't.

And if manipulation or coercion brought you to that point, the shame belongs to the person who engineered that surrender, not to you.

Name the man, not the fantasy. The betrayal wasn't simply another woman. It was the months of lies that made you believe you were building a future together while he was living another life. He needed you to trust him because, had you known the truth, you might have walked away. So he made sure you didn't.

That is what a programme of deception does. It robs you of the freedom to make informed choices about your own life. When used to dominate and subordinate, deception becomes a tool of coercive control and psychological abuse. Stop reaching for words that make him sound better. A liar is a liar. A traitor is a traitor. A backstabber is a backstabber. Grieve the man he pretended to be, the image he created to win your love and use it to control you. But don't defend the man behind the mask.

The eggs went into the bin that morning. The relationship ended. The feelings are disappearing. You'll be ok! Probably you are already more ok now than when you were cheated on, lied to, and abused.

Bitcoin staking allows holders to earn rewards on their BTC without selling the underlying asset. In 2026, Stacks, Babylon, Core, Starknet and Stacking DAO offer different approaches, with varying rewards, custody models and staking requirements.

Stacks targets approximately 3% annualized rewards in BTC, while Babylon, Core and Starknet distribute rewards in their native tokens. Stacking DAO offers liquid staking with BTC-linked returns.

Choosing a platform requires looking beyond advertised yields to understand token requirements, custody arrangements and withdrawal conditions.

Rates as of November 10, 2026. Stacks quotes a target annual yield, Babylon quotes APR, and other displayed rates may use different calculation conventions.

The main differences concern reward sources, custody arrangements, additional capital requirements and withdrawal flexibility.

Bitcoin staking rewards vary by source, denomination and additional capital requirements.

Stacks is a Bitcoin layer where BTC holders earn BTC.

Stacks miners commit BTC through Proof of Transfer (PoX) to compete for the right to produce Stacks blocks and earn STX rewards. The committed BTC is distributed to staking participants, providing the source of their BTC rewards. PoX has distributed over 4,200 BTC since January 2021.

Direct Bitcoin staking targets approximately 3% annualized yield, with weekly BTC rewards. Since September 2026, three staking bonds have sold out, bringing total BTC staked to 1,740 BTC.

Babylon distributes BABY rewards funded by newly issued tokens through the protocol’s inflation mechanism. Its displayed APR ranges from 0.04% to 0.52%, with the higher rate requiring participants to co-stake BABY alongside BTC.

Returns depend on reward distribution and BABY’s value relative to Bitcoin.

Core distributes CORE rewards to participants who delegate BTC to validators securing its blockchain. Rewards come from newly issued CORE block rewards and transaction fees.

Its displayed top-tier yield is 0.79%, with higher reward tiers requiring participants to stake CORE alongside BTC.

Starknet allows holders of supported tokenized Bitcoin assets to delegate them to validators securing the network.

Participants earn newly minted STRK tokens, with a displayed yield of 2.91%. Actual returns depend on network participation and STRK’s value relative to BTC.

Stacking DAO provides liquid Bitcoin staking through stBTC, a token representing an sBTC-based staking position. BTC rewards generated through Bitcoin staking on Stacks accumulate in the position, increasing the amount of sBTC backing each stBTC over time.

Coming into the year, there was plenty of anticipation for Ridley Scott’s return to the sci-fi genre with his new movie, The Dog Stars. Scott even enlisted some big-time stars such as Josh Brolin and Jacob Elordi for roles in the film, but it led to nothing more than a disappointing run at the box office.

Scott hadn’t directed a sci-fi movie since Alien: Covenant in 2017 before The Dog Stars, but a near-10-year hiatus wasn’t enough to get fans to head to theaters. Scott has a few exciting projects on the horizon, though, not least of which being his new Treasure Island reboot with Hugh Jackman. The movie, which also stars Hugh Grant, has already been set for release on November 11, 2027, and it’s expected to begin filming early next year.

In this later stage of his career, Ridley Scott may not be directing as many movies as he once was, but he’s still involved in projects everywhere thanks to his Scott Free banner. Even with the Alien franchise, Scott didn’t direct Alien: Earth or Alien: Romulus, but he did produce both projects.

Last year, Scott took his producing talents to Apple TV to work on the electric psychological thriller, Echo Valley, which stars Sydney Sweeney opposite Julianne Moore. The movie premiered on Apple TV last year in June, but it overstayed its welcome on watchlists, where it’s now been in the top 10 this year for over 275 days. It joins the ranks of other Apple TV smash hits like The Gorge and Greyhound that have managed to hit this colossal milestone this year.

Four criminal masterminds. One of them was always going to be yours. Here is who comes for you, why they picked you out of everyone available, and what they want from the encounter.

Your nemesis does not want to beat you. He wants to know you completely, and he is patient enough to wait for it. The frightening part is how good the conversation is.

Your nemesis is not after money, territory or revenge. He is after a demonstration, and you are the person he has chosen to prove his point on.

Your nemesis respects you, which sounds flattering until you work out what it means. He chose you because nobody else was worth his time.

Your nemesis has no personal interest in you whatsoever, and that is precisely what makes him unsurvivable. You are not a rival. You are a stop on the way.

Domhnall Gleeson, who can be seen leading the new Office spin-off show, The Paper, also stars alongside Sweeney and Moore in Echo Valley. The movie was written by Brad Ingelsby, who is also famous for writing two of the best HBO crime shows of the last few years, Mare of Easttown and Task. The film was directed by Michael Pearce. The official synopsis for Echo Valley, which earned scores of 53% from critics and 48% from audiences on Rotten Tomatoes, reads as follows:

“In the edge-of-your-seat thriller Echo Valley, Kate (Julianne Moore) is a mother struggling to make peace with her troubled daughter Claire (Sydney Sweeney), a situation that becomes even more perilous when Claire shows up on Kate's doorstep, hysterical and covered in someone else's blood. As Kate pieces together the shocking truth of what happened, she learns just how far a mother will go to try to save her child in this gripping tale of love, sacrifice and survival from BAFTA-winning director Michael Pearce and Emmy-nominated writer Brad Ingelsby.”
