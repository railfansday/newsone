The changes come as the company focuses on developing the infrastructure for its next phase of multimedia development. As we grow across theater, film and television, we’ve been intentional about building a team with the infrastructure to take on bigger and bolder work. Nobile continued, “I’m also thrilled to promote Anna to Head of Theater. I couldn’t be prouder of our team, or more excited for all that’s ahead — on Broadway and beyond.”

Pardee is Seaview’s most senior producer and has been with the company for three and a half years, rising from Associate Producer to Producer and now to the newly formed role. She works across Seaview’s producing and development slate, partnering with artists and creative teams from early development through production.

How Does The REO Runabout Stack Up Against Slate?

📅 Nov 17, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Browse this and many other websites without banner ads, personalized tracking and video ads.

Like the idea of a Slate but don't want an EV? Well, an upstart from Dallas, Texas has just the thing for you. That is, if it ever becomes a reality. Startup company REO recently unveiled renderings of its new truck, dubbed the Runabout. It's a similar idea to Slate, with just one basic frame, three different body variants to choose from, no frills, a bunch of customization, and a sub-$30,000 price tag. The main difference is that that Runabout uses a four-cylinder gas engine, rather than electric motors. So how does it compare to Slate?

First, let's look at price. The most basic Slate you can get—cheekily called "The Blank Slate"—costs $24,950, and it's a two-door pickup with nothing more than some seats, a steering wheel, a battery, and a single rear electric motor to get you around. There's no radio, power windows, or screens to speak of.

The cheapest REO you can get will be the Runabout T4X, which costs $21,500*. Note the asterisk, as REO says the current listed price is an estimate, and the company has no factory or clear plans for getting it to market. For that shockingly low price, it's estimated that you'll get a four-cylinder engine, a choice of either a six-speed manual or automatic transmission, and mechanical four-wheel drive. It's a two-door, two-seat pickup with drop-down sides for the bed, like a Kei truck.

What four-cylinder engine will the Runabout have and which manufacturer will supply it? REO isn't saying. There are far more questions than answers so far. REO does say that it has a "targeted powertrain life of 500,000 miles." To quote myself reading that in the press release: "lol."

While Slate uses a hybrid body-on-frame style chassis, the REO has an old-school ladder frame, like a classic pickup. It's similar to slate in that it offers modular body styles, but with key differences. There's a two-door pickup and an SUV version, like Slate, but there's also a four-door pickup, which Slate doesn't have. REO claims its truck has solid rear axles, too, to give it more off-road credibility.

The design isn't bad, even if it looks like a Grand Theft Auto version of an Australian-market Land Cruiser, but that's to be expected. REO hired longtime automotive designer Frank Stephenson, whose previous work includes the first BMW-owned Mini Cooper, the Maserati MC12, and the McLaren P1. Overall, it looks handsome enough and utilitarian, even if all the photos are just renders at this point.

Inside, REO's truck is said to be pretty barebones (it hasn't been revealed yet), but not as barebones as Slate's. There's a digital screen for gauges and one for navigation, but everything else has a physical control. You're probably wondering if it has power or hand-crank windows. REO used its press release to throw a little shade: " Each model will feature power windows for convenience: hand-cranks are best left to others."

The entire idea behind REO is to offer the sort of basic pickups customers loved in the '80s and '90s, trucks that were simple utilitarian workhorses and nothing else. It's an admirable goal, especially given how expensive pickups have become. But do customers want to go backward in powertrain technology, especially with affordable mainstream electric trucks like the Ford Fathom coming to market soon?

So for less money, you can get a potentially more capable truck, in a similar configuration as the Slate, with a bit of extra interior comfort. But you lose the electric powertrain, which reduces maintenance and fuel costs. Instead, you'll have to make due with a generic four-cylinder of some sort. There is one big "if," though. You can only buy one if REO ever actually makes it. Sure, having a design by Frank Stephenson helps it feel more real, but just because they found the money to pay him to design something doesn't mean they'll actually get around to building it.

Slate is already here, it's ready to launch production vehicles soon. We've seen them in person, touched them, and tested them. Until REO can actually muster a viable product, it should probably avoid throwing anymore shade in its press releases.

Sotheby's makes last-minute deal to sell $180m Van Gogh painting following dispute

📅 Nov 17, 2026  |  Category: Business  |  Read Source →

The Blaquier family, from Argentina, bought Blossoming Chestnut Trees (Châtaigniers en fleurs) (May 1890) in 1975

Sotheby’s today announced that a settlement has been reached over the sale of Van Gogh’s Blossoming Chestnut Trees (Châtaigniers en fleurs) (May 1890). The work has been poised to achieve a record price for the artist but became the subject of a claim by the heirs of its Jewish former owners, who called for the work to be withdrawn from the 18 November auction in New York.

The Sotheby’s statement records that the auction house is “pleased to confirm that an agreement has been reached between the Blaquier-Arrieta family and all of the legal descendants of Hedwig and Samuel Fischer regarding the sale of Vincent van Gogh’s Châtaigniers en fleurs”. The sale will now proceed as planned.

The work is estimated at around $180m ($207m with fees) and has been guaranteed by Sotheby's to sell. This is far in excess of the previous record of $117m, paid at Christie’s in 2022 for Van Gogh’s Orchard with Cypresses (April 1888).

The Blaquier-Arrieta family, from Argentina, bought Blossoming Chestnut Trees in 1975. As the Sotheby’s statement explains: “In recognition of the painting’s complex Nazi-era history and the suffering endured by the Fischer family during the Holocaust, the two families have reached a mutually satisfactory resolution and the work will be sold pursuant to their agreement.”

The provenance in the Sotheby’s catalogue entry notes, as it has done since last month, that the painting was acquired by the Berlin publisher Samuel Fischer and his wife Hedwig in 1909. Their son-in-law, Gottfried Bermann Fischer, deposited the Van Gogh with the Amsterdam gallery Paul Cassirer in 1932 or 1933—and it was then bought by Paris collectors Henriette and Marcel Kapferer in 1936.

Last Thursday (12 November), in a surprise move, the Berlin-based restitution researcher Gunnar Schnabel sent a letter to Sotheby’s, on behalf of the Fischer heirs. He questioned the 1936 sale—which he says took place three months after the family had left Berlin for Vienna—asking Sotheby’s to prove that the picture had been sold at its market value and that its sale was not forced. In the meantime, Schnabel asked for this week’s sale to be delayed.

In a statement, the Fischer heirs commented: “We are grateful for the trusting and fair negotiation and collaboration with Sotheby’s and the Blaquier-Arrieta family, which make it possible to acknowledge the suffering of the Fischer family during the Nazi era and honour Gisela Berman Fischer's [granddaughter of Samuel and Hedwig] history of seeking justice for her family.”

The Blaquier-Arrieta family commented: “We are pleased to have reached an amicable resolution with the Fischer heirs. We appreciate the constructive dialogue that made this resolution possible and look forward to an extraordinary result and to passing along this masterpiece to a new generation of collectors.”

No details have been released on the nature of the agreement and whether the Fischer family will receive financial compensation.

What impact, if any, the short-lived dispute will have on the sale will only become apparent with the bidding.

Disney Touts Its Streaming Success In Latest Earnings

📅 Nov 17, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

After a lot of internal upheaval and consolidation, the House of Mouse finds itself in a pretty good place going into its fiscal 2027 year.

So says new CEO Josh D’Amaro and CFO Hugh Johnston during an earnings call on Tuesday. They told investors that Disney exceeded its guidance for the previous quarter and is on track to exceed its three-year adjusted earnings-per-share growth guidance going into the new year.

For the full year, revenue not only increased 8% but broke the $100 billion mark, from $94.billion in the fiscal year 2025 to $102.3 billion in 2026.

Meanwhile, for Disney’s Q4 (the company starts its fiscal year a quarter earlier), revenue increased 16% to $25.9 billion for last quarter, compared to $22.5 the year before.

Within entertainment, advertising grew 10% to $1.7 billion. And SVOD revenue increased 19% to $6.1 billion, with 22% subscription revenue growth.

In TV, D’Amaro highlighted the critical buzz around Hulu’s crime thriller “Furious,” high ratings for the new season of “Dancing with the Stars” and excitement over “The Remarried Empress,” a South Korean romantasy that debuted as the biggest premiere for a local original across Disney+ and Hulu globally. The latter is indicative of Disney’s plan to invest more in international content, a strategy that’s been working wonders for competitors like Netflix.

Sports are also a big deal, of course. Subscription and affiliate fee revenue in the sports segment increased 18% in the quarter to $3.4 billion, with advertising revenue growth of 20%.

Disney will continue to grow its sports footprint next year with expanded college football rights, increased “Monday Night Football” ratings and ESPN’s first ever Super Bowl telecast. (Well, technically it’s ABC’s turn to host the Big Game, but it’ll also be cross-broadcast on other Disney channels.)

Where Disney’s overall streaming strategy is concerned, D’Amaro told investors that there will be a larger focus on “connecting services” – which includes other non-entertainment sides of the business like parks, cruise ships and merchandise.

More practically, though, “connecting services” means finally unifying Disney+ and Hulu into one app, which D’Amaro said would start in December. It also means adding more sports content to Disney+, and introducing creator content from TikTok into the Disney+ mobile app next month.  The move might strike some as strange, but given that ESPN’s short-form Verts product broke records in September and October, maybe there’s something to it.

At the same time, Disney is still exploring the idea of a free streaming product, which leadership first discussed during last quarter’s earnings reports.

Despite – or maybe because of – all the streaming success, Disney announced it will stop reporting its SVOD operating income beginning next quarter. At the same time, the company will also provide additional disclosures related to revenue within the entertainment segment.

SVOD revenues will still be reported, Johnston clarified to investors but so will content and IP revenues. Revenue for linear networks will be combined with the virtual MVPD business.

The goal, said Johnston, is to provide investors with “additional information and a better lens into our content monetization,” while allowing Disney’s team more flexibility to “invest appropriately” in growing SVOD.

In other words, Disney’s likely planning to spend a lot more money on its SVOD business – and maybe it doesn’t want investors to worry too much about how the sausage is getting made.

Disney’s remarkable growth this past quarter isn’t surprising, given how much the company has restructured since D’Amaro took on the CEO role in March. At least 1000 members of Disney’s workforce have been laid off since then, with more job cuts and consolidation reportedly on the way.

All that streamlining is expected to translate into $3 billion in run rate savings by 2029, according to D’Amaro.

To highlight the ways Disney is prioritizing efficiency and consolidation, D’Amaro also shouted out the company’s first ever chief technology officer, Karandeep Anand, who came from the (now bankrupt) chatbot startup Character.ai.

Not surprisingly, Disney plans to continue investing in AI tools and technology across its entire business, even parks. Although D’Amaro didn’t bring up the company’s recent purchase of Character.ai’s assets, he did hype up how the tech is being used in the streaming side of the business, both within Disney+’s content recommendation engine and in the advertising products Disney makes available to its marketing clients.

In addition to Disney’s AI creative studio that uses existing brand material to develop CTV-ready ads, the company also launched an agentic ad campaign tool in August. Both were previewed at its tech and data showcase at CES earlier this year.

Now, almost half of all new ad campaigns are flowing through the agentic tool, according to Disney’s ad sales team.

Bitcoin Lightning developers needed two patches to stop a potentially costly attack

📅 Nov 17, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

The initial July update blocked one crash method but missed a persistent vulnerability that could leave affected nodes unable to restart.

Bitcoin's Core Lightning software required two security patches to eliminate a vulnerability that could repeatedly crash payment nodes and potentially expose funds held in other channels.

A Nov. 13 security disclosure revealed that an initial repair released in July failed to address a second attack method that could keep affected nodes offline even after they restarted.

The vulnerability, tracked as LNF-2026-0004, affected Core Lightning, one of the software implementations used to operate nodes on Bitcoin's Lightning Network.

Security researcher Nishant Bansal discovered that attackers could exploit how Core Lightning assigned identifiers to payment channels, creating duplicate records that caused the software to crash.

The weakness originated in the network's channel-opening process, where each payment channel receives an identifier derived from the Bitcoin transaction output that funds it. Core Lightning could accept multiple channels referencing the same funding output, producing identical identifiers that conflicted with assumptions in its internal software.

In the first attack, a malicious peer could create duplicate pending channels and crash a victim's node simply by reconnecting. The attack required no Bitcoin transaction fees because the fraudulent channels did not need on-chain funding. Each subsequent reconnection could trigger another crash, allowing attackers to repeatedly interrupt payment processing.

However, researchers identified a more persistent variation involving previously closed channels.

By reusing an identifier associated with a closed channel and subsequently closing another channel with the same identifier, attackers could trigger a software failure that persisted across restarts.

Unlike the initial attack, this method required on-chain transaction fees but could leave the node unable to recover without a software upgrade.

Core Lightning developers addressed the original vulnerability in version 26.06.6, released on July 22, after researchers privately reported the reconnection attack earlier that month.

The update prevented new channels from reusing funding outputs associated with existing live channels. It also modified connection handling to prevent duplicate records already stored from triggering the original crash.

However, the protection did not extend to identifiers associated with closed channels, leaving the second attack route available.

Researcher Crypt-iQ independently identified the remaining weakness, prompting developers to introduce broader safeguards in version 26.06.8, released Sept. 22.

The second update expanded duplicate-identifier checks across active and closed channels belonging to different peers. It also allowed nodes containing previously stored duplicate records to recover without repeatedly crashing.

The potential financial consequences extended beyond disrupted Lightning payments.

According to the advisory, nodes forced offline could lose the ability to monitor Bitcoin's blockchain for attempts to settle channels using revoked transaction states.

That could allow dishonest counterparties to claim funds using outdated channel balances. Offline nodes could also miss deadlines for collecting time-sensitive payments, placing Bitcoin held in unrelated channels at risk.

The researchers did not report confirmed exploitation or financial losses.

The findings affect Core Lightning versions preceding the complete September repair, with the reconnection vulnerability dating back to version 0.12.0 and the persistent closed-channel crash introduced in version 25.05.

Developers have since released additional security updates, including version 26.06.10, which addresses other vulnerabilities involving channel operations, payment resolution, and remote crashes.

Core Lightning's maintainers have urged operators to upgrade to the latest release to protect their nodes against the disclosed weaknesses.
