A great Tuesday night for the Newcastle United young stars as they beat the Rotherham United first team 1-0 at the New York Stadium.

Now renamed the Jersey Mike’s Trophy, the EFL Trophy is a cup competition for all League One and League Two clubs, plus under 21 sides from 16 Category One Academies (Arsenal, Aston Villa, Brentford, Brighton & Hove Albion, Chelsea, Crystal Palace, Everton, Fulham, Ipswich Town, Leeds United, Liverpool, Manchester City, Newcastle United, Nottingham Forest, Sunderland and Tottenham Hotspur).

After an excellent 3-1 win away (the League One and Two clubs always have home advantage) at League Two club York City and an unlucky 2-1 defeat away at League One Bradford City, the Newcastle United young stars had to beat League Two Rotherham United to ensure progress from Northern Group C.

It was 17-year-old Kyran Thompson (pictured at the top of this report) who was the match winner, a very clever finish in the 34th minute to round off an excellent team move involving Alfie Seldon and Vakhtang Salia.

Thompson chose to move from Arsenal to Newcastle United this summer and looks a real star in the making.

It was a real team effort though and a very youthful NUFC Under 21 side controlled the game, 16-year-old keeper Roman Dowell made a few saves in the first half but didn’t face a single shot on target after the break.

As well as the winning goal from Kyran Thompson, the Newcastle United young stars went close with a header from Miodrag Pivas. Michael Mills saw a free-kick well saved and the home keeper also prevented Kyran Thompson scoring his second goal.

Rotherham United are currently managed by Alex Bruce, son of our old mate Steve.

This 1-0 victory makes it five wins in a row in league and cup for the Newcastle United Under 21s.

It also means a first time in eight years that the Newcastle United young stars have progressed to the last 32 of the EFL Trophy.

That came in the 2018-19 season and ended up with the hilarious last 16 match where the Sunderland first team had the humiliation of having to play the Newcastle United kids in a competitive match. The Mackems won that game and progressed all the way to Wembley in what was then known as the Checkatrade Trophy, only to lose to Portsmouth in the final.

‘People really effing hate data centers’: How the AI buildout became the midterms’ affordability issue

📅 Nov 04, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

“People really effing hate data centers,” Abdul El-Sayed said at a campaign event in July, E&E News reported. On Tuesday, he won Michigan’s Senate race, NBC News projected.

El-Sayed was one of many winners Tuesday who made the AI buildout part of their argument about the cost of living. Voters named the cost of living a top issue in AP exit polls. In much of the Midwest and Mid-Atlantic, the clearest way that issue showed up was the monthly electric bill, and candidates in both parties blamed data centers for it.

The link runs through PJM Interconnection, the grid operator serving 13 states from New Jersey to northern Illinois. Capacity prices on PJM’s grid, which pay power plants to guarantee future supply, rose from $28.92 per megawatt-day in 2024-25 to $329.17 for 2026-27. Monitoring Analytics, PJM’s independent market monitor, estimated that data centers were responsible for 63% of the price increase in the 2025-26 auction, or $9.3 billion recovered from customers through higher rates, according to the Institute for Energy Economics and Financial Analysis. That adds roughly $16 a month to the average residential bill in Ohio and $18 in western Maryland, IEEFA estimated.

The increases may not be over. Consulting firm ICF estimated that residential rates across PJM could climb 30% to 60% by 2030. Consumers have already noticed. In an October Consumer Reports survey, 81% of people who had lived in the same home for at least three years said their electricity bills had risen, and 84% said they’d support their utility creating a separate rate category for large users like data centers.

The candidates who won on this issue made the bill the point. El-Sayed’s plan called for “no rate hikes,” arguing data centers should pay for their own energy demand. Two months after OpenAI and Oracle broke ground on a massive data center campus in Saline Township, he held a rally in front of the site.

In Ohio, Democrat Sherrod Brown defeated Republican Sen. Jon Husted after calling him “the face of data centers,” citing the deals Husted worked on as lieutenant governor. Brown campaigned on eliminating tax breaks for data center companies and requiring them to pay for the utilities their campuses use. Logan Phillips, founder of the election forecasting site Race to the White House, told Fortune that Brown focused on the issue “way before anyone else was focused on it,” while Husted had been “basically at every single ribbon cutting for data centers across the state.”

“Boy, did that not age well,” Phillips said.

By the end of the cycle, Republicans were moving in the same direction. In Michigan, El-Sayed’s opponent, former Rep. Mike Rogers, said in August he supported a one-year moratorium on new data centers. In Ohio’s governor’s race, both nominees proposed moratoriums: Democrat Amy Acton, who won, and Republican Vivek Ramaswamy. In Florida, Republican Byron Donalds, who won the governor’s race, proposed restrictions on data centers.

Where voters could decide the question directly, they mostly said no. At least 14 Ohio municipalities passed bans or other restrictions on data centers Tuesday, including Sunbury, where a ban targets a planned Amazon data center. Nationally, voters in 10 states considered 41 data center measures.

The polling matched. More than 70% of Americans said they would oppose a data center planned for their area, according to a Heatmap poll published last week, and rising electricity prices were the top reason for opposition.

Not everyone accepts the link. The Ohio Manufacturers’ Association has rejected the claim that data centers are driving up energy costs. “Ohio does not have a data center cost crisis,” the group said. Amazon has declined to say whether Tuesday’s ballot measures will change its expansion plans, and said its commitment to the communities where it operates remains unchanged.

The industry also made its case on TV. Build American AI, a nonprofit linked to the AI industry’s Leading the Future super PAC network, spent at least $2.54 million on Ohio ads touting data centers’ economic benefits, according to an investigation by DeSmog.

The new Congress could take up the issue. Goldman Sachs strategists believe a Democratic Congress could curb data center tax incentives, though they see a nationwide moratorium as unlikely. The buildout is still accelerating: data center capacity under construction is up 48% nationwide, according to Reuters.

Other metals seeing notable gains on Wednesday morning included platinum, which jumped 3.3% to trade at $1,462.20, and palladium, which was 3.8% higher at $969.33.

The moves came as vote counting continued in the midterms, with the Democrats projected to have won control of the House and multiple governors' offices also turning blue.

U.S. stock futures broadly fell as the results came in, while Treasury yields also moved lower. Oil prices were marginally higher, while the U.S. dollar index shed around 0.3%.

Rhona O'Connell, head of market analysis for EMEA and Asia at StoneX, said in a note on Tuesday that if the Democrats won dual Congressional control, gold should find support as rates and the dollar would likely come under pressure.
