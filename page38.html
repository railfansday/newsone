Bill Belichick, Robert Kraft advance to final round of Hall of Fame voting

📅 Nov 19, 2026  |  Category: Sports  |  Read Source →

Bill Belichick and Robert Kraft will have another chance at election to the Pro Football Hall of Fame in 2027.

The Hall announced on Thursday that Belichick has been chosen as the nominee in the coach category while Kraft is the nominee in the contributor category. That was also the case last year, but neither of them were elected.

Belichick’s failure to get enough votes for election created a particular outcry given the six Super Bowl titles he won while coaching Kraft’s Patriots from 2000-2023.

The Hall of Fame overhauled selection procedures in the wake of last year’s election. The size of the selection committee was cut in half from 50 to 25 and there is no longer be a separate category for senior players.

Belichick and Kraft will join 13 player nominees for consideration by the committee. That list of candidates will be reduced to seven before a final vote with 80 percent approval needed for election.

Stedelijk Museum appoints Krist Gruijthuijsen as new director

📅 Nov 19, 2026  |  Category: World  |  Read Source →

Krist Gruijthuijsen is currently the director of the Espoo Museum of Modern Art (EMMA) in Finland

Photo: Joel Karppanen. Courtesy Stedelijk Museum, Amsterdam

The Stedelijk Museum in Amsterdam has announced that the Dutchman Krist Gruijthuijsen will take over as its new director from July 2027.

Moving from Finland, where he has since 2025 been director of the Espoo Museum of Modern Art (EMMA), he said he wants to bring an outsider’s perspective and a focus on how the modern city connects with the world.

“The Stedelijk museum’s strength is in bringing large and Modernist exhibitions to the Netherlands,” he tells The Art Newspaper. “People go to the Rijksmuseum for Rembrandt and to the city [Stedelijk] museum to understand how Amsterdam has developed, to understand Modernism through the lens of art and design.”

The Stedelijk Museum enjoyed success last year with an Anselm Kiefer show—organised with the neighbouring Van Gogh Museum—and an exhibition of work by the Dutch photographer Erwin Olaf, increasing annual ticket revenue compared to 2024 by almost a quarter, to €10.8 million. Its current Yayoi Kusama show has been extended to the end of January due to its popularity.

This success stands out against a backdrop of Dutch museums reporting financial pressure and a fall in visits compared to pre-pandemic levels. The Rijksmuseum’s most recent annual report describes internal discussions that took place about the fact visitor numbers fell 4% in 2025 to 2.3 million, while the Rembrandt House Museum recently warned that rising tourist taxes will damage cultural tourism.

But Gruijthuijsen said he is more interested in variety than visitor records. “I’m not a visitor numbers director,” he says. “It is quality rather than quantity: what we need economically, and the rest is a plus. It is very difficult these days because people only choose what they know. My goal is first and foremost to support artists and art.”

Gruijthuijsen takes over from Rein Wolfs, who has been the director since 2019.

From one of the first exhibitions to establish Van Gogh’s work in 1905 to experimental shows of the 1960s, the Stedelijk Museum created an international reputation for contemporary art, which Gruijthuijsen wants to make relevant today. “The DNA of the city museum is a modern day and global perspective—trying to find connections,” he says.

From the Finns, he will also bring an important lesson in terms of improving diversity and tackling under-represented subjects, he says: “Less talking, more doing.”

This game is apparently being created in cooperation between Bethesda and Devcats, an indie developer specializing in hidden object games about cats like A Park Full of Cats, A Building Full of Cats, and An Arcade Full of Cats.

Is it the Fallout game we’ve all been waiting for in the near-decade since the launch of Fallout 76? No, definitely not, but, well, it’s something, I suppose. Every bit of Fallout-adjacent content we get can continue to tide us over until Fallout 5, whenever that ends up being, or at least the next season of the Fallout television series.

Tim Cain, the creator of Fallout, initially proposed a much happier ending, but it was ultimately scrapped.

According to the description of the game on the Australian Classification Board website, A Wasteland Full of Cats will cast you in the role of one of the various cats living in one of the Vaults that dot the Wasteland. Following some manner of accident, all of the other cats living in the Vault alongside you are let loose out into the Wasteland, and you’ll need to go out there to round them all up.

In the same vein as Devcats’s other games, this will be a jaunt through 14 locations and 33 rooms where you’ll need to point out the errant cats hidden within the scenery, Where’s Waldo-style.

A Wasteland Full of Cats will cast you in the role of one of the various cats living in one of the Vaults.

A real estate note lets you lend against someone else’s property and collect the interest. The interest is the appeal. The property is your protection, but only if you can claim it and sell it for at least what you’re owed.

You can buy a performing note, one whose borrower is paying, or pay less than the loan’s balance for a non-performing note, one whose borrower has stopped paying. The real estate bonds some apps sell are loans to the company itself and give you no claim on a particular property.

Ubisoft ‘Celebrates’ GTA 6 Day with Canada Layoffs

📅 Nov 19, 2026  |  Category: Business  |  Read Source →

The release of Grand Theft Auto 6 is finally here, bringing with it an avalanche of rave reviews and jokes about creative excuses to miss work. Unfortunately, for many Ubisoft workers, the drive home today will also be their last one.

According to Jon Hicks of GamesIndustry.biz, French gaming giant Ubisoft has dropped the guillotine on 166 roles in Canada, concentrated in the company’s Québec and Montreal studios. Ubisoft attributes the move to matching its resources with “current production priorities and business needs.”

Ubisoft says workers affected “are being provided with severance, extended health care benefits, career transition services and other resources” to help smooth out the ride.

Since the announcing of a joint venture between Ubisoft and Tencent, and a major restructuring plan that comes with it, the company has gone through a turbulent period to try and align its workforce with the newly-set goals. As has become the norm, this has come at the expense of workers.

Ubisoft moves all-in with new bosses, a new operating model, and a billion-dollar investment.

The terminated roles appear to represent approximately 4% of the affected studios’ workforce, primarily in support and production positions, a testament to just how massive Ubisoft’s Canadian operation has gotten over the years. Earlier this year, Ubisoft closed down its Halifax studio in Nova Scotia.

Ubisoft has not commented on specific projects affected, but Jon Hicks claims some of the workers laid off were part of Vantage Studios. Previously known as Creative House 1, Vantage Studios focuses on the company’s big money titles, namely the Assassin’s Creed, Rainbow Six, and Far Cry franchises.

Layoffs have left a dark stain in the gaming industry for a while now, especially as companies have begun overcorrecting for the post-lockdown hiring spree.
