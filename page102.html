10 cars skipping the 2027 model year, from the Acura RDX to the Ford Escape

📅 Nov 09, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Free daily briefing on global business news.

Ten models missing from 2027 lineups, from the Acura RDX to the Ford Escape, sorted into true endings, pauses and changeovers.

When an automaker drops a model, most people assume buyers had stopped wanting it. Many of this year's exits trace back instead to factories being assigned new work, suppliers that can no longer provide parts, and new platforms replacing old ones. As a result, "discontinued" now covers several different outcomes. Some cars are ending for good. Others will skip a year or two and return, leave one country while staying on sale elsewhere, or have their nameplate folded into a sibling model. For a buyer, the useful question is not whether a car is dying but which of those outcomes applies to the one in front of them.

America's two biggest automakers are betting on electric cars in opposite directions. GM took a $1.6 billion charge for unused EV assets and contracts, according to AutoGuide. The Detroit News reports that Ford $F is developing a new electric platform, called UEV, aimed at Chinese competitors. The industry has no single direction on electric cars, and some of this year's exits result from those two opposing strategies. Ford's bet on electric cars helps explain why it gave up a gas-powered compact crossover to free a factory for its new platform. Other exits have plainer causes, such as parts that suppliers can no longer provide or a vehicle that never found enough buyers.

Car and Driver set out to find the cars and trucks that won't be offered for the 2027 model year, from a Cadillac sport sedan and a BMW roadster to an electric delivery van and a family minivan. Several of those nameplates are expected to return in some form, while others are ending for good with no successor named. Shoppers should treat those expected returns with caution, because a promised comeback can arrive as a different kind of vehicle or not arrive at all. Each departure ahead is sorted by what it really is and by what it means for anyone shopping for one now.

A pause in a model's production usually means an orderly handoff, with the old car going out of production just as its replacement arrives. Acura is skipping that handoff with its compact crossover. The third-generation RDX ends with the 2026 model year, according to The Drive, and Car and Driver expects a redesigned version only as a 2028 model, which leaves a year when buyers can't get a new RDX at all. Acura did not pick that gap on a calendar. "We have several suppliers that are no longer in position to continue supplying key parts for the current RDX," an Acura spokesperson said. Acura can't keep building the car it has, and because the shortage starts with its suppliers, Acura has limited control over how firm any return date can be.

Shoppers who plan to wait for the next RDX should treat 2028 as the earliest it could arrive, not as a scheduled launch. That return year is an expectation rather than a confirmed date, and an Acura spokesperson would commit only to having the next generation ready within roughly two years, according to The Drive. The old car is ending before its replacement is ready, so the length of the gap rests on how quickly Acura finishes the redesign. The RDX passes the test of a nameplate Acura means to bring back. It does not yet pass the test of a car with a firm arrival date.

The RDX that goes out of production after 2026 is a much smaller seller than the one Acura had at its peak. Acura sold a record 63,580 RDXs in 2018 and 30,235 in 2025, according to Good Car Bad Car figures reported by The Drive. Some of those buyers probably chose the ADX instead, a cheaper Acura crossover that arrived for 2025 below the RDX in the lineup. A year or more without an RDX gives more shoppers time to settle on other cars, so the next RDX will have to win buyers back rather than inherit them.

For anyone who wants an RDX soon, the cars already built are the whole supply. Acura is scheduled to end production in spring 2026, according to The Drive, and no 2027 model follows, so the remaining new 2026 cars are the last new RDXs until the redesign arrives.

Buying now means buying the final version of a generation its own suppliers can no longer support. Waiting means betting on a date Acura won't pin down.

Audi will no longer sell any version of the A7 once the RS7 goes after the 2026 model year, because the RS7 is the only one left, according to Car and Driver. The RS7's exit completes a cut Audi had already made to the rest of the family. "We will continue offering the RS6 and RS7 for the 2026 [Model Year]. The A7 and S7 are no longer available for the 2026 [Model Year]," an Audi representative told CarBuzz. That reprieve kept the fastest version on sale through 2026, and no later model year follows. In dropping the RS7, Audi is finishing a retreat it had already begun. Audi made the real decision about the A7 when it dropped the A7 and S7, and nothing about the RS7 itself suddenly failed.

Audi had little volume to protect in the A7 family, which drew just 1,574 U.S. buyers for the A7, S7 and RS7 combined in 2024, according to CarBuzz. For a luxury line, that is a small audience. The RS7 reportedly earns Audi more profit than either the A7 or the S7, which would account for its extra year. Low sales explain why the family is ending, and profit explains why the RS7 got one more year. Audi ended the car as a matter of lineup planning, not because buyers lost interest in it.

Audi looks more likely to replace the RS7 than to abandon it, which could keep the gap short. CarBuzz reports that a successor to the RS6 and RS7 is likely for the 2027 model year, right after the RS7's final year. The engine that defines the car may not carry over. The last RS7 uses a 4.0-liter V8 hybrid, and rumors point to a plug-in hybrid for the successor, possibly with a V6 in place of the V8. Those reports remain rumors, so a buyer should not assume the next fast Audi of this kind will match this one.

Shoppers drawn to the A7's shape still have an Audi to consider, though it runs on batteries rather than a V8. The electric A6 e-tron Sportback has a body style that overlaps with the A7's, according to CarBuzz.

A buyer set on the V8 has to choose now or probably never, and the 2026 RS7 carries a base price of $128,600, according to CarBuzz. Anyone open to a hybrid has reason to wait and see what Audi brings for 2027.

BMW is closing out a generation of its grand-touring line rather than retiring it, so the 8-Series leaves as a changeover. The current car ends with the 2026 model year, according to Car and Driver, and BMWBLOG reports that production of this second generation ended earlier in 2026. Any new 8-Series on a dealer lot today comes from that final run. The exit takes all three versions at once: the two-door coupe, the convertible and the four-door Gran Coupe. A buyer who wants BMW's big luxury coupe in any form, whether open-top or with rear doors, has no sibling left to switch to. The choice is no longer which 8-Series to wait for, but whether to take one of the last ones built.

The 8-Series name is expected to return, but the engineering beneath today's car can't come with it. BMWBLOG reports that the current car sits on BMW's CLAR architecture. A badge can move to a new platform, but the car under it cannot. A BMW executive told Automotive News that "a straightforward transfer into the Neue Klasse world is not feasible." The body styles of a future 8-Series are also undetermined, so the convertible or Gran Coupe a shopper wants may not come back. Even if the name returns, a future 8-Series could share little with today's car beyond the badge.

The ALPINA brand offers no shortcut to a successor either. BMW dismissed the idea of continuing the 8-Series as an ALPINA internally, according to Automotive News findings reported by BMWBLOG. BMW modeled the relaunched ALPINA brand's first production car, due next year, after the 7 Series. Shoppers looking to ALPINA for a ready replacement for the 8-Series will not find one there.

Anyone set on a big BMW coupe or convertible should treat the current car as the last confirmed one. A comeback has no settled date or shape.

BMW is pulling the iX from the U.S. without ending the car. American buyers can get the battery-electric luxury SUV through the 2026 model year and no later, according to Car and Driver. BMWBLOG reports that the iX remains on sale in other markets. A BMW spokesperson told BMWBLOG that "we are concluding U.S. allocation of the BMW iX as we prepare for the next generation of our fully electric vehicles." BMW is choosing where the car goes, and nothing in that statement points to buyers walking away from it. The company has not released an end-of-production date for the iX, even though 2026 is the final model year for U.S. buyers. That survival abroad changes little for an American shopper, because BMW has announced no plan to bring new iXs back to U.S. dealers.

BMW is replacing the iX from within its own lineup rather than giving up on it. The company refreshed the iX's styling for 2026, its last U.S. year, according to Car and Driver. BMWBLOG reports that the SUV also served as the development platform for BMW's Gen6 eDrive system. The iX3 uses that same system, so the technology BMW proved out in the iX stays in its U.S. lineup after the iX leaves. BMW plans for the iX3 to be its lead Neue Klasse model in the U.S., which makes this exit a swap of one battery-electric SUV for another. It is not a retreat from electric cars in America. BMW expects the iX3 in U.S. showrooms in the fall, so the gap should be short, though shoppers will find a different model rather than a new iX.

Anyone who wants an iX in the U.S. has only the 2026 model year left to buy one. Those cars are long-range electric luxury SUVs that get more than 300 miles per charge, depending on the model, according to BMWBLOG. Greg S. Fink, a senior editor at Car and Driver, recommends that shoppers who want one buy it while BMW still sells it here. His advice treats the final model year as a real chance to buy, not as a car to avoid.

A U.S. buyer now chooses between the final American model year of a proven car and the next generation that the iX helped BMW build.

BMW's Z4 roadster is leaving with no successor and no return date announced. The car has been on sale for more than 20 years across three generations, and its 2026 model year will be its last, according to Car and Driver. BMWBLOG reports that the final Z4 has already come off the assembly line, though unsold cars may stay on dealer lots for a while. A shopper should treat a Z4 as a car to catch before dealers run out, not as one more option to compare.

The Z4 lost buyers steadily for years, and that slow decline gave BMW little reason to build another. BMW sold 15,827 cars from the current generation in 2019 and only 9,744 in 2025, according to BMWBLOG. BMW did deliver 4.9% more Z4s in the first quarter of 2026 than a year earlier, during the car's final model year. The rise came after BMW announced the retirement, which likely drew in buyers who wanted one before it was gone. One gain in a farewell quarter does not reverse the long slide in sales between 2019 and 2025, and the buyers who arrived at the end came too late to change BMW's plans.

The Z4's exit also leaves BMW with a single open-top car. Once the Z4 is gone, the 4 Series is BMW's only remaining convertible, BMWBLOG reports, and the company will have no roadster at all. The 4 Series convertible is expected to continue only until around 2029, though BMW has not announced that date. Any new gasoline roadster would also have to fit within European Union rules that call for automakers to cut their fleets' carbon dioxide emissions 90% from 2021 levels by 2035. For now, a return is only a wish. "Hopefully, BMW decides to re-enter the reasonably affordable roadster segment in the coming years," Greg S. Fink, a senior editor at Car and Driver, said.

Until BMW says otherwise, the Z4 on a dealer lot today is the last reasonably affordable BMW roadster.

Cadillac is ending the CT4 for good, with every version of the compact sedan leaving the lineup for 2027. That includes the 472-hp Blackwing performance model, according to Car and Driver. AutoGuide reports that Cadillac has no successor planned and that production is scheduled to end in June 2026. The factory will stop building new CT4s by midyear, before the 2027 model year begins. A buyer who wants a new one in any trim can choose only from cars that have already been built, and once dealers sell those, the CT4 will be available only as a used car.

Cadillac is leaving a segment that American buyers have largely abandoned. Sedans made up almost 40% of the U.S. market in 2015 and 15% in 2026, according to Edmunds figures reported by CNBC. Cadillac intends to keep adjusting its lineup to customer demand, according to a statement by Cadillac Global Vice President John Roth reported by AutoGuide. Industry analysts don't expect demand for sedans to return. "We don't see sedans recovering a decade-old heyday," Stephanie Brinley, associate director of AutoIntelligence at S&P Global $SPGI Mobility, told CNBC.

Many buyers still choose sedans, though. Toyota $TM sold about 316,000 Camrys in 2025, according to CNBC. The CT4's end reflects Cadillac's decision about where to compete in a shrinking segment, not proof that this particular car stopped finding buyers.

Dropping the smaller of its two sedans may also cost Cadillac some future customers. CNBC reports that Rebecca Lindland, managing director at Allison Worldwide, considers affordable models the way a brand reaches younger shoppers and people who have never bought from it. If the CT4 served that role for Cadillac, the lack of a replacement leaves the brand with one fewer way to reach those buyers.

A shopper who wants a compact Cadillac sedan can buy one of the last new CT4s before dealer stock runs out or look at used cars, because Cadillac's lineup will no longer include one.

Cadillac is pausing the CT5 rather than ending it. The sport sedan is not in Cadillac's 2027 lineup, and a next-generation version is expected back before the end of the decade, according to Car and Driver. CNBC reports that General Motors $GM confirmed by email that the nameplate will return. The automaker treats the missing year as a gap rather than an ending. GM plans to keep building the current car until late 2026, according to AutoGuide, and new CT5s will reach dealers until then. After that there will be none, and the CT5 becomes a car with a return expected but no date set.

The CT5's pause comes as GM changes its plans for its factory in Lansing, Mich. GM has put on hold $1.4 billion it planned to spend retooling the plant for electric vehicles and is investing $1.25 billion there in combustion engines instead, according to AutoGuide. Cadillac's plan for a gas-powered CT5 built in Lansing fits that shift.

Cadillac's promise commits to a gas engine and a factory, not to a sedan. "We are thrilled to confirm that the legacy of the CT5 will continue with a next-generation ICE vehicle, proudly manufactured at GM’s Lansing Grand River Assembly plant, of U.S. and globally sourced parts," Cadillac Global Vice President John Roth said, as reported by AutoGuide. The word "sedan" never appears in that pledge. Cadillac has yet to say what shape the next CT5 will take, so buyers can't assume it will have four doors and a trunk. The CT5-V and Blackwing performance versions end with the current sedan and are not part of any announced return. A buyer who wants one has to find it among the cars from this final run.

For anyone shopping for a Cadillac sport sedan, the current CT5 is the last one they can count on. The comeback is a promise about a name, not about the car.

General Motors $GM is dropping its electric delivery van with no successor and no planned return. The battery-electric van lasted less than six years in all and carried a Chevrolet badge only for the 2025 and 2026 model years, according to Car and Driver. Its factory had already stopped building it by spring 2025, CBC News reports, barely into the van's first year as a Chevrolet. GM is retiring the Chevrolet BrightDrop before the name had much time to become familiar to buyers.

This exit looks partly like the case of a van that didn't sell. "There's a lot of vehicles sitting in the lots around CAMI's facility and it doesn't look like they're moving," Kevin Stubbe, owner of Delta Machine and Design, told CBC News. Mike Van Boekel, president of Unifor Local 88, sees politics at work as well, and he points to President Trump's effort to move manufacturing jobs into the U.S. That remains his reading rather than a settled cause. The van appears to have been a weak seller, and if Van Boekel is right, its Canadian factory may also have made it an easier cut, so its end is not purely a judgment on electric vans.

The heaviest cost falls on Ingersoll, Ontario, the town where GM built the van. In a community of 13,000 residents, the cancellation hits 1,200 union members at the CAMI plant, CBC News reports, and a loss on that scale reaches local businesses as well as the factory. Quinn Pow, acting president of the Ingersoll Chamber of Commerce, says residents already notice the loss at the grocery store and the local Tim Hortons.

The plant's future remains undecided. The van's last model year is set at 2026, but GM has not said whether it will close the CAMI plant, and it has released no details of any vehicle it might build there next, according to CBC News. Until GM decides, Ingersoll cannot tell whether it has lost one van or its whole factory.

Shoppers can treat the BrightDrop as finished, while Ingersoll still waits to learn what comes next.

For a family shopping for a minivan, this exit takes away a name more than a vehicle. Chrysler will build its last Voyager as a 2026 model, and the 2027 Pacifica LX takes on the Voyager's styling, according to Car and Driver. Chrysler keeps selling the Pacifica, and the Voyager's look moves onto its LX version, so the change happens inside Chrysler's minivan lineup rather than costing its showrooms a minivan. A shopper who wants the Voyager's look next year will find it on a Pacifica LX.

The name was the weak part from the start, because Chrysler revived it rather than created it. Chrysler dropped the Voyager nameplate altogether in 2003 and brought it back for the 2020 model year, according to CBC News. Jason Stein, publisher of Automotive News, says car companies turn to their own history when they try to get ahead. A name brought back once can be set aside again, and Chrysler is now dropping the Voyager name a second time. Another comeback is possible, but nobody should count on one.

Chrysler could use an old name to win attention, but it could not change which vehicles families were choosing. The Voyager's short second run fits a doubt raised as soon as the name returned for 2020. "I think we're just so in love with SUVs and crossovers that minivans don't stand much of a chance," Kyle Asquith, a professor at the University of Windsor, told CBC News. His doubt was about minivans as a whole, and it applies just as much to the Pacifica that Chrysler keeps selling.

For buyers, the lesson is that a revived badge says little about how long a vehicle will last in a lineup.

Ford $F's compact crossover lineup is down to a single model, the Bronco Sport, now that the Escape is gone, according to Car and Driver. The Detroit News reports that Ford's Louisville Assembly Plant in Kentucky stopped building the Escape in December 2025, well before the model year ran out. Any Escape a shopper finds on a dealer lot comes from the last of the stock, and no running line is left to order from. A shopper who wants a small Ford crossover now has only one model to consider.

The Escape's end came as Ford assigned its factory new work. The Louisville plant ended Escape production for retooling, and it will become the launch site for Ford's new UEV electric vehicle platform, according to The Detroit News. The Lincoln Corsair crossover ended at the same plant in the same month. The downside landed on everyone else. As sites tried to tell agents from people, they stacked up bot checks, and the people got caught in them too. Shoppers who sent nothing but themselves found more puzzles between them and a pair of shoes.

But getting through checkout was only part of the problem. The bigger issue was whether the agent was really working for the shopper. Reader’s Digest shared advice for handling some of the most unsettling situations drivers can face, from losing control on a slippery road to encountering dangerous weather. Here are five scary driving scenarios and what to do if they happen to you.

A deer appearing in the road can trigger an instinctive reaction to jerk the steering wheel out of its way. Resist that urge. Swerving can send your vehicle into another lane, into oncoming traffic or off the road.

Instead, keep both hands firmly on the wheel and apply the brakes firmly. Reader’s Digest recommends maintaining control of the vehicle rather than making a sudden evasive turn. If you need to steer, use a controlled shuffle-steering motion to keep both hands engaged.

It’s a frightening few seconds, especially when an animal appears without warning, but a predictable response is safer than a sharp maneuver. Focus on keeping your vehicle under control and avoid creating a second hazard while trying to avoid the first.

A blowout can make the car pull sharply and leave you feeling as though you've lost control. The instinct may be to slam on the brakes, but that can make the situation worse.

Keep a firm grip on the steering wheel, particularly if a front tire blows, since the vehicle may pull in an unexpected direction.

Avoid jerking the wheel or making abrupt corrections. Once you've reached a safe spot, get out of traffic's way before dealing with the tire or calling for help.

A blowout is unsettling, but the goal isn't to stop immediately. It's to slow down in a controlled way and get the vehicle somewhere safe.

Reader’s Digest recommends trying to get the other driver's attention by honking and flashing your headlights while looking for a way to avoid a direct collision. If you need to move, focus your eyes on the safest available path, whether that's an open lane or the shoulder.

There's no guaranteed way to avoid a collision in this situation, but staying focused on an escape route gives you a better chance of responding deliberately instead of panicking.

Administrative subpoenas are a powerful but unchecked investigative tool. Unlike with a grand jury subpoena or a court order, the government doesn’t have to explain to the courts why it needs the documents or what data it’s requesting from a company.

Since the start of the Trump administration, the federal government has used DHS’s administrative subpoena power to try to unmask anonymous online critics and pursue broad customer records about protesters. Congress originally granted the use of 1509 customs summons to the then stand-alone US Customs Service nearly a century ago, with the idea that it would let customs officers inspect business records related to importing merchandise and figure out whether they owed customs duties. In the past, DHS has maintained that customs summons could be used only for specific purposes. “When we give law enforcement agencies powerful investigative tools, even with the best intentions and purposes to try to go after legitimate investigations and crime, you have to be really careful,” says Mackey. The downside comes in its homebuilding numbers. Iowa accounts for just 0.94% of the country’s issued building permits. For years, Britain’s crypto debate has centred on exchanges and specialist firms. The next phase also concerns the banks that already serve financial markets. The Financial Conduct Authority has put those institutions on notice that the route into regulated crypto services is opening well before the rules themselves take effect.

In its November 2 wholesale-banks supervisory update, the FCA said banks can submit a variation of permission to undertake activities brought inside the new regime. The regulator specifically named custody, operating trading platforms, dealing, arranging transactions, stablecoin issuance and staking as activities within the expanding perimeter.

The timing deserves attention. The application window opened on September 30, 2026. The FCA is encouraging wholesale banks to apply by February 28, 2027, ahead of the new regime’s planned start on October 25, 2027.

That early deadline has a practical purpose. According to the FCA, firms applying before February 28 should either receive a decision before the regime begins or, if their application remains under review, be able to continue relevant crypto activities while the regulator makes its decision. Applications made after February 28 but before the October start may fall under a different transitional arrangement.

It would be a mistake, however, to interpret the guidance as blanket approval for every bank to start running cryptocurrency services. Permission still depends on what an individual institution proposes, the relevant legal requirements and the FCA’s assessment. A variation to an existing permission is not the same as automatic approval for every newly regulated activity.

For established banks, assembling that application can be substantial work. Custody design, operational controls, consumer or wholesale protections, sanctions screening and governance all need to fit the activity. The regulator has not suggested these safeguards are optional because the applicant already has a banking licence.

Banks bring customer relationships, compliance teams, payments systems and balance-sheet expertise. If more of them begin offering regulated digital-asset services, a business may eventually be able to access familiar institutional infrastructure rather than work solely through crypto-native intermediaries.

Yet bank participation can also make the market more selective. A bank may accept only certain assets, counterparties or forms of custody. Its willingness to trade or settle tokenized instruments should not be confused with broad support for every token listed on an exchange.

The policy sits alongside efforts to connect financial assets to blockchain infrastructure. NewsBTC has examined the plans to develop infrastructure for tokenized securities, an example of the operational plumbing institutions assess before moving real transactions onchain.

A credible application must explain more than growth potential. Banks will need to show how responsibilities are divided, which assets are supported, how client holdings are safeguarded, and what happens when systems fail or markets become disorderly.

That creates a useful test for the market. The UK has been working toward a clearer framework, but clarity matters only if firms can implement the requirements and supervisors can process applications. February 28 is therefore an operational deadline as much as a policy marker.

The next developments to watch are the number and quality of applications, eventual decisions and any further guidance from the FCA. Wholesale-bank involvement could become an important part of Britain’s crypto market. At this stage, though, the news is that a formal route is open—not that a wave of new bank crypto products has already arrived.
