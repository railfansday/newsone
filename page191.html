Dak Prescott, CeeDee Lamb refuse to lose as Cowboys top Colts

📅 Nov 09, 2026  |  Category: Other  |  Read Source →

Nothing comes easy for the 2026 Dallas Cowboys, but they managed to get back in the win column on Sunday with a 27-23 win at the Indianapolis Colts. It is their first win scoring less than 30 points since last season's miraculous comeback in Week 12 vs. the Eagles, which gave Brian Schottenheimer's team their first winning streak of that 2025 season.

At the end of another rollercoaster game, this fourth win of the season goes in the same bucket as the other narrow win the Cowboys have against another AFC South opponent – Dak Prescott refusing to lose with the ball in his hands, and making the plays when it mattered most to ensure this. The Texans win was defined by Prescott and CeeDee Lamb making all the plays when it mattered, and without George Pickens this time out, Lamb's big day was even more crucial.

There were plays by the defense in the first half that made this game feel like it wouldn't come close to needing any late game heroics from the offense, but the offense didn't capitalize early. Then, there were breakdowns on defense that helped the Colts storm back, but also more rallying by Christian Parker's side of the ball to ultimately put this in the win column.

Let's get to some notes on the gut-it-out performance in Indy.

For the second straight week, the Cowboys faced 16 third-down attempts. The Cowboys have faced more third downs on offense than their opponents in every game since the Packers loss.

Dak Prescott's third-down fumble in the first quarter was the second straight week the Cowboys had a disastrous negative play in a formation that calls on Javonte Williams to stay in and block. This time around, the Cowboys also had Jake Ferguson in the game. Ferguson was the only pass option running a hot route against the pressure the Colts brought on this play, yet somehow the defense had an answer to bracket him with two defenders while also getting to Prescott.

The strip sack set the Colts offense up on a short field they capitalized on to take a 7-3 lead. The Colts would not get points again until midway through the third quarter. While the Cowboys did get two touchdowns before this, they also punted on all four other possessions in this stretch. The touchdown drive that came in response to being down 10-7 got a rare Javonte Williams receiving play that went for positive yards on a 3rd-and-4, then overcame a 2nd-and-19 after a Brevyn Spann-Ford holding penalty, and was gifted an automatic first down after facing 2nd-and-15 on a Tyler Guyton penalty. Even after all this, it still took a 4th-and-2 attempt in the red zone to get points on the board with Jake Ferguson's touchdown.

The other touchdown the Cowboys got came a little bit easier, rewarding a drive that saw what felt like the first successful scramble drill in a long time. Lamb and Prescott made the play when the Cowboys decided to throw on 3rd-and-1, hooking up on the scramble drill for 22 yards. One play later, Lamb got behind Charvarius Ward for his 44-yard touchdown to go ahead 17-7.

Yet again, the Cowboys felt in control of a game, but were left scrambling and fighting for their life to actually finish by the fourth quarter.

The Cowboys had a perfect 34/34 run-pass split in this game, and Dak Prescott's 9.9 yards per attempt was a season high, but it still felt like the offense majored in wanting to be in third downs, makings things hard on themselves yet again, and not putting the game away on their own terms early. Instead, they left it until late for more QB heroics that paint a clear picture of what this team is really all about.

The number one thing the Cowboys defense needs to fix following these last two wild games is limiting the explosive plays for touchdowns. Two weeks in a row, a game has swung in the snap of a finger because any work the defense has done up to a point is wiped out by a long play for a touchdown. Ashton Dulin's 55-yard touchdown on a screen pass evaporated the Cowboys hopes of taking a two-score lead to the fourth quarter, and led to another game that felt like it could see overtime by the fourth.

Marist Liufau, DaRon Bland, and P.J. Locke were the defenders with the best chance to get Dulin on the ground, and none of them could.

The Cowboys continued to do some interesting things with Shemar James in this game, as he was involved in the play where Caleb Downs had a sack, and Locke made a big stop on third down early in the game as well, but more consistent fundamentals is still something holding back the Dallas D as a unit. They need to tackle better.

After the Cowboys offense squandered their chance by missing a FG with over three minutes remaining in the game, the Cowboys defense needed to come up with a stand. The Colts got into comfortable field goal range, but Josh Downs' third-down drop stopped the clock and the defense had held Indy to a 23-20 lead after a field goal. The defense did not give up a touchdown, and they got the ball back to the offense with enough time to go win the game.

The other defensive resolve point in this cardiac fourth quarter was of course Quinnen Williams forcing a fumble on Jonathan Taylor, recovered by James. We said coming into this matchup that just an arbitrary splash plays from Williams wouldn't be good enough. So Williams stepped up and made multiple big plays, none bigger than this one, and helped the Cowboys overcome penalties on Bland and Dee Winters prior to the takeaway that made a difference in which team had just enough in the tank to win on Sunday.

Kenny Clark even added his name to the splash play list from the defensive interior on the final drive, pressuring Jones into an intentional grounding throw that really wrecked the drive and led to Downs' game-sealing interception. The highly-paid Cowboys defensive interior made the plays in crunch time.

The goal for the Cowboys now resets back to exactly what it was after the Eagles game, don't get too high after a win (against an arguably even worse team this time), and stay focused enough to get a win streak started in front of home fans next Sunday.

To do so, the Cowboys will have to waste no time digesting not only the good things that happened to beat the Colts, but the bad as well. Fourteen penalties for 153 yards is one thing that should be unacceptable even in a win, but the bigger and more long-term concern is the poor play-calling that stamped this game as well.

Even while getting Lamb ten catches on 16 targets for 10 catches and 195 yards, nothing outside of Lamb's long touchdown felt like it came easy for the offense. The protection plans were not sound to give Prescott time to throw when needed. The early down aggressiveness to not face 16 third-down attempts was not there. Worst of all, the static routes, lack of motion, and number of pass plays where all of Prescott's options were blanketed from start to finish, with receivers not even running hard on many of these plays, were abundant.

This week will be telling when it comes to how Lamb's leadership is received at large by this team, as even after his huge day he was more focused on what could have gone better and his costly fumble. This is the type of attitude the entire Cowboys team needs to take into the 49ers matchup, as significant improvements from how they played, mainly on offense, will be needed to pull that upset. Brian Schottenheimer's responsibility for how the offense looks will come up as well, but his postgame locker room approach was quite different in mood than Lamb's.

James Gunn and Peter Safran are out as the co-CEO’s of DC Studios, the main team behind projects like Superman, Clayface, Lanterns, and Peacemaker.

The duo are being replaced by Jeremy Latcham, who made his name at Marvel Studios from the very beginning, working under Kevin Feige. And so, it appears that DC’s new overlords at Skydance after its merger with Paramount are looking for some more money out of this whole situation.

“James and Peter began a new era for DC Studios, guiding it through a major transformation and bringing audiences fresh chapters of beloved stories, including Superman, Lanterns and Creature Commandos,” Skydance CEO David Ellison said. “We’re grateful for the passion and care they brought to this work, and for the lasting mark they’ve left on DC.”

Gunn and Safran’s approach to creating self-contained stories rather than an interconnected MCU-like universe had varying returns, but the duo will end their tenure on Dec. 1 before continuing on in their roles for Superman sequel Man of Tomorrow. Anything beyond that is still a question.

“We’re thrilled to welcome Jeremy to DC Studios,” Ellison continued. “As part of the founding team behind the Marvel Cinematic Universe, he helped define the modern superhero movie and powered some of the biggest, most beloved comic book adaptations of our time. We can’t wait to see the stories he’ll bring to life, and how he’ll help build the future of DC for fans everywhere.”

Latcham’s resume is strong, joining Marvel Studios all the way back in 2004 and working in production on such films as Iron Man, Iron Man 2, The Avengers, Guardians of the Galaxy, Avengers: Age of Ultron, andSpider-Man: Homecoming. He will “will lead DC’s film, television and animation slate, overseeing the creative pipeline, along with production, talent and strategy,” according to The Hollywood Reporter.

“DC’s pantheon of characters is among the most iconic in American popular culture,” said Latcham in a statement. “I’ve had the privilege to help bring some of the most celebrated superhero stories to life on the big screen, and I’m excited and honored for the opportunity to now lead DC Studios into its next era. The chance to steward the incredible cast of DC characters to the screen in a way that excites lifelong fans while introducing their timeless stories to the next generation is a massive responsibility that I do not take lightly.”

And so DC fans are left wondering what’s next, again, after seemingly yet another reset following Gunn taking over from Zack Snyder’s “SnyderVerse” set of films that were extremely hit or miss (mostly miss).

Will Latcham continue on the path that Gunn and Safran began, or will we get yet another do-over? Don’t forget, DC Studios still has The Batman: Part II releasing next year (in theory), and who knows if that’s its own thing or connected or otherwise. Five years between Batman movies should not happen, but, alas.

“Four years ago, we set out to unite DC under one creative vision, Gunn and Safran said in a joint statement. “In that time, we helped restore Superman to the heart of DC, while opening new doors and telling new stories in film and television with Peacemaker, Creature Commandos, Lanterns and Clayface. “We’re immensely proud of the team that turned that ambition into something real, including the actors, crews and executives we had the opportunity to collaborate with.” Gunn had a whole lot more to say on Threads.

I think Gunn and Safran deserved more time to build something special, because Superman was great, Clayface was well-received and turned a profit on a small budget, and fans seem to love Lanterns as well. I always felt like there doesn’t need to be a massive, MCU-like interconnected universe with post-credits scenes in DC, but it appears that Skydance feels differently.

Every occupation can take a toll on workers’ mental health, but some professions are considered more stressful than others due to the nature of work-related exposures. To date, policing remains one of the most stressful professions. Hidden behind every badge is a law enforcement professional trying their very best to navigate a steady, and at times relentless, stream of psychological and emotional stress that makes resilience a key attribute that is essential for survival where suicide rates exceed those of the average citizen. Most, if not all, law enforcement officers will, at some point in their careers, be faced with stressful, traumatic encounters that quite literally become embedded in their psyche, and if not addressed, will likely worsen.

This topic has been extensively studied, but most studies focus exclusively on male police officers; yet, emerging research suggests that female officers, in particular, often experience these challenges in distinct and deeply personal ways that differ from the experiences of male officers. Exploring these differences can help agencies design wellness initiatives that truly support all who serve by recognizing and adjusting for gender differences. Although the percentage of female officers pursuing a career in law enforcement has been steadily increasing, female officers only comprise 13.8% of all US law enforcement officers; that is likely why their unique circumstances have been largely understudied and, for the most part, overlooked within the research and practitioner literature (Statista, 2024).

Law enforcement officers are repeatedly exposed to traumatic incidents, most of which are attributed to operational stress. Operational stress is commonly tied to the trauma that police officers encounter in the course of their work, such as responding to homicide scenes, domestic and sexual assaults, fatal collisions, suicides, and other acts of violence that can have a profound influence on officers, leading to anxiety, depression, sleep disturbances, PTSD, and in some extreme cases, suicide (Bishopp et al., 2019). To compound matters, today’s law enforcement officers are also faced with staff shortages, mandatory overtime, erratic shifts, insufficient pay, public scrutiny, and at times, unsupportive, ineffective leadership. In fact, Violanti et al. (2017) found that female police officers report a roughly 37% higher prevalence of lack of support from supervisors compared to male officers.

The paucity of research to date suggests that female officers are more likely to internalize the emotional impact of stressors that lead to burnout when compared to male officers (Rabichuk, Williamson, & Frankel, 2025). In law enforcement, female officers often place significant pressure on themselves. There is a constant burden to maintain a professional image and avoid appearing vulnerable or weak. In response, female officers tend to suppress emotions rather than seek help because they are persistently compelled to prove their competence in a historically male-dominated field, contributing to the high likelihood of chronic stress and burnout.

Female officers are challenged with unique workplace stressors that extend beyond the basic demands of police work, some of which male officers cannot relate to, and as a result, can adversely affect performance, well-being, retention, and career advancement. Some of these stressors include, but are not limited to, pregnancy, postpartum recovery, menopause, and the demands of shift work. It should come as no surprise that contemporary research suggests that police leaders who understand these realities are better positioned to implement policies that support female officer wellness while strengthening organizational effectiveness.

Pregnancy presents unique challenges for women in law enforcement, particularly when agency policies regarding modified assignments and accommodations vary widely. Research suggests female officers may experience elevated reproductive health risks, including miscarriage, highlighting the need for evidence-based workplace policies, reproductive health education, flexible assignments, and supportive supervision.

Illias, Demou, and Riach (2025) emphasized that returning to work after childbirth presents another set of challenges unique to female law enforcement officers. Recovery from childbirth, sleep disruptions, breastfeeding, and childcare responsibilities often lead to concerns about job security and future career advancement. Female officers who received little to no agency support upon return to work experienced significantly higher rates of depression and anxiety than those who had the support of their agencies (Illias et al., 2025). It is therefore recommended that police leaders create family-friendly policies, comprehensive return-to-work policies and programs, breastfeeding spaces, and other supportive accommodations.

This is undoubtedly one of the least studied issues affecting female police officers. Hofman and Spiegel (2026) conducted a qualitative study in which the study’s participants (female police officers) reported concerns about stigma, perceptions of reduced competence, and a lack of understanding from leadership. For police leadership, menopause should be viewed as a workforce wellness issue as opposed to a personal matter (Hofman & Spiegel, 2026). Educating and training supervisors and fellow officers normalizes conversations about women’s health that are typically shunned.

Understanding the realities that female officers face throughout their policing careers is not simply a gender-equity issue, but rather an organizational issue because agencies that recognize and respond to these challenges are more likely than not to retain female officers, improve wellness outcomes, and strengthen modern-day law enforcement agencies.

Children’s Place files for bankruptcy protection, seeks buyer

📅 2026-11-09  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Children's clothing retailer The Children's Place has filed for chapter 11 bankruptcy protection, it said Sunday.

The company is looking to sell substantially all of its assets and is seeking offers from potential buyers by Dec. 9, with a goal to close a deal by the end of December.

The retailer, whose brands include The Children's Place and Gymboree, says it operates two digital storefronts and 514 North American stores, and has distribution in 13 countries through franchise and wholesale partners. The company's performance has taken a hit in recent years from tariff hikes and slower consumer spending, among other factors.

Its stores and e-commerce sites remain open for now, and the company is planning to continue its ongoing inventory sales with court approval.

"For nearly 60 years, The Children's Place has supported growing families with apparel that customers have come to know and love. We recognize the significance of this development for our employees, customers, vendors, and other partners, and we are committed to conducting an efficient and transparent sale process," said Rudy Morando, co-chief restructuring officer of The Children's Place. "In the meantime, we remain focused on serving the families who have trusted The Children's Place for generations."

This move comes days after interim Children's Place CEO Muhammad Asif Seemab and Chief Strategy Officer Muhammad Umair stepped down from their roles. Seemab and Umair will both remain on the company's board. CFO John Szczepanski took on the role of acting president and principal executive officer.

Seemab is also managing director of Saudi-based Mithaq Capital, which took a majority stake in The Children's Place in 2024 when the company was facing financial trouble.

Even with support from the investors, the company has not been able to significantly improve its financial outlook. In its bankruptcy filing, the company reported about $742 million in assets, compared to about $881 million in debts.

In the second fiscal quarter of 2026, its most recently reported quarter, the company's net sales dropped nearly 19% from the same period the year prior. At the time, Seemab said in a news release that during the quarter the company was "stabilizing our customer file" and "driving traffic to our stores and websites," and saw "lower conversion, combined with a very clearance-heavy mix."

He also pointed to tough comparisons to the prior-year period, due in part to back-to-school shopping demand that "was uniquely elevated ... ahead of anticipated tariff price increases."

The Children's Place stock has plummeted in recent years and was trading around 18 cents per share on Monday.

Apple TV's Murderbot Is Officially Not Returning In 2026

While Murderbot season 1 might have been one of 2025’s most acclaimed new sci-fi shows, the satirical action thriller won’t return in 2026. Even though the early 2000s was home to the iconic space western Firefly, the Battlestar Galactica reboot, multiple Stargate shows, Futurama’s original run, and the underrated James Cameron series Dark Angel, the 2020s still mark a uniquely fecund era for the sci-fi genre on the small screen.

Netflix users can choose between Stranger Things, Black Mirror, the animated anthology series Love, Death & Robots, and the ambitious epic 3 Body Problem. Hulu offers sci-fi fans the Margaret Atwood adaptation The Handmaid’s Tale and its spinoff The Testaments, while audience members who log into Apple TV can choose between Invasion, Severance, the Isaac Asimov adaptation Foundation, the acclaimed dystopian series Silo, and the Monsterverse spinoff Monarch: Legacy of Monsters.

As if this lineup were not enough, Apple TV also introduced the superb satirical sci-fi series Murderbot in 2025. Starring Alexander Skarsgård as the titular sardonic Murderbot, this series focused on a private security robot who is equally fascinated and disgusted by the peculiarities of human society. Based on author Martha Wells’ eight-book series The Murderbot Diaries, Murderbot season 1 adapts the first of the writer’s novels, All Systems Red.

Murderbot was a huge critical hit, with a staggering Rotten Tomatoes critical score of 95% and an almost equally impressive audience score of 82%. Season 1 of the series followed Murderbot on a mission to protect scientists during a dangerous trip off Earth, with the acid-tongued robot offering constant deadpan commentary on the group’s foibles. However, viewers who hoped to see season 2 of the series arrive this year will be disappointed.

Instead, season 2 guest star Steve Buscemi told ScreenRant that Murderbot’s sophomore outing wouldn’t arrive until summer 2027. Buscemi, who is also set to appear in Hulu’s upcoming reboot of The X-Files, let the season 2 release window's timeline slip while walking to ScreenRant's Tatiana Hullender about his role in director Martin McDonagh’s blackly comic crime thriller Wild Horse Nine.

Murderbot fans can expect the show’s sophomore outing to drop sometime in the summer of 2027, which puts the show right in the middle of the standard, typical breaks between seasons of streaming series. At the far end of the scale, some of the biggest streaming shows, such as Netflix’s Stranger Things, have been known to take whopping three-year-plus breaks between seasons.

Although Netflix’s Jenna Ortega vehicle Wednesday took similarly lengthy breaks between its seasons, Murderbot’s two-year gap is a lot more common among streaming shows across the board. Disney’s ambitious fantasy adaptation Percy Jackson & the Olympians had a two-year gap between its first two seasons, although seasons 2 and 3 trimmed this down to a one-year gap by shooting back to back.

This strategy might also be part of the reason that Prime Video’s upcoming historical drama Bloodaxe was renewed for a second season before its first season premiered. Murderbot’s season 1 success could not necessarily have been guaranteed before the sci-fi comedy show debuted in 2025, but the two-year wait for season 2 may be due in part to busy cast schedules.

In contrast, Prime Video’s romance novel series adaptation Off Campus is set to release its second season less than a year after its first since the show shot both outings back to back. After the success of Murderbot season 1, Apple TV may follow this release strategy for seasons 2 and 3, but, in the meantime, the wait for season 2 will sadly extend for another six months or so.

Donnie Dunagan Dies: Voice Of Young Bambi In 1942 Disney Classic Was 92

📅 Nov 09, 2026  |  Category: Sports  |  Read Source →

Donnie Dunagan, who voiced the young Bambi in Disney’s 1942 animated classic during a brief Hollywood career as a child actor, has died. He was 92.

Movie historian Tom Weaver said Dunagan died November 4 but did not provide details.

Born on August 16, 1934, in Texas, Dunagan moved with his family to Memphis, where he tap-danced his way to winning a talent contest in Memphis at age 3. He caught the eye of a talent agent, who had the boy and family come to Hollywood by train.

Dunagan’s screen debut came in the 1939 monster flick Mother Carey’s Chickens, leading to more than a half-dozen screen roles including Son of Frankenstein, The Forgotten Woman and Tower of London. As the United States was entering World War II, the youngster would land his most memorable role, playing the orphaned deer in the 1942 toon classic Bambi.

In a 2011 interview, Dunagan recalled meeting Walt Disney several times on the lot. “He was a leader; all over the place, helping, coaching, laughing, and even a kid like me then could sense that he was keenly respected by the air and production people. That was not always the case in other studios when the ‘boss’ came around. Mr. Disney was a class by himself.’

Dunagan was turning 8 as the film was released, but it would be his final screen credit.
