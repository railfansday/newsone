Stove God Cooks & Swizz Beatz Talk Making ‘All This For Me?’: ‘Album of the Decade’

📅 Nov 11, 2026  |  Category: Business  |  Read Source →

The duo's long-awaited and much-talked-about collab album has found a home — and they're excited for fans to hear what they've put together.

Stove God Cooks is finally ready to follow up his underground classic Reasonable Drought, which he dropped alongside Roc Marciano back in 2020. Since then, he’s been on an impressive feature run (mainly on Grisleda records) and has had to deal with a plethora of leaks that have only sent demand for a sophomore album to a fever pitch, especially after his scene-stealing chorus on the Clipse‘s LET GOD SORT EM OUT standout “F.I.C.O.” Related Hilary Duff Reflects On Her Massive Lucky Me Tour: 'I'm Really Proud of Myself, and That's Hard to Feel As An Adult' Sade Won't Be Attending the Rock Hall Induction Ceremony The Chicks & Kacey Musgraves Complete Historic All-Female State Fayre 2027 Headline Bill Explore Stove God Cooks Swizz Beatz See latest videos, charts and news

Now, after a year of label drama and false starts — including one scrapped project in Goat Stamp, and a March 27 release date for the album we’re here to talk about — his parent label Babygrande has a entered a joint partnership with Warner Records in an effort to finally give fans what they’ve been waiting for in a new full-length album.

Part of the reason for the delay has been because Stove wasn’t really aware of how things worked on the sample-clearing side of the business — because he’s used to working alongside prolific rappers like Griselda Records founder Westside Gunn, who craft albums and drop them in a span of a week.

“I didn’t understand a lot of s—t, you know? So, things that was pissing me off, it was really the way I was seeing s—t,” he explains to me over Zoom. “My references wasn’t the way s—t really go. I’m seeing my people move this way. Why we taking so long to drop albums? I see West drop an album in three days. I know he got samples in his s—t. I’m on the songs. So, it’s that kind of s—t. But once you rip the sheet off and Gunn starts telling me about the lawsuits and s—t that he deals with, I’m like, ‘Oh, no, this s—t is for real.'”

Now, alongside legendary producer Swizz Beatz, he’s finally beginning the rollout for his long-awaited sophomore album All This For Me? They first dropped the warm up single, “Welcome to My Garden,” earlier this year in February (which has been “elevated,” according to Stove and Swizz) and the duo recently released the project’s second single and music video in “Stovie’s Home (Down Down)” this past Friday (Nov. 6). And the Syracuse, N.Y. rapper is excited for his fans to finally hear what he’s been working on and put the rollercoaster that was this past year behind him.

“The s—t is a super blessing, man,” he says. “That’s the reality of this moment for me right now. All the other s—t, the old s—t, none of s—t matter to me right now.”

Check out my conversation with Stove God Cooks & Swizz Beatz below as we talk their creative process, their chemistry and what fans can expect once they finally get to hear what they’ve been cooking up.

So, the word is you signed a deal with Warner. So what can you tell us about that?

You’ve ask Jay-Z for help in regards to your label situation, did he play a part in any of this? Whether that be with the Warner partnership or you and Swizz linking up.

Stove God Cooks: Definitely me and Swizz. Hov planted the seed, but he didn’t have anything to do with Warner.

Swizz Beatz: I always show Hov a lot of the artists that I’m working with, and I was working on — I think Godfather of Harlem season one — and I was just showing him all of these different vibes, and he was just like, “Yo, you and Stove gotta get together immediately. He’s one of my favorites out right now. He got it. And you can compliment his sound. He can compliment your sound, You need to get on top of that.”

And I take what he says serious — because Hov has got great insight, and I love to see him listening to artists like Stove. That’s what he’s playing in the crib, smoking a cigar, vibing — and I just thought that was super cool. Now we here and people are gonna get to see the results. I was already a fan, but Hov definitely put the stamp on making it happen.

I just like the way it organically happened. Because even beyond Hov saying that, Stove was really, very talented, and everything that I thought he was already, even more. So, that was just a blessing. I’m just happy to be a part of the project and just riding it out for him until all of the business got settled. Most people would have just left, but once we connected, it was just like, “Yo, I’m already committed.” So, whatever we need to do to make it happen, the world need to hear this talent, this energy, this sound. It’s just refreshing. I’m happy to be a part of it.

How did the album come together? Did you already have a pack of beats with Stove in mind?

Swizz Beatz: The first, initial vibe of us was stuff I had, but once we started getting into the conversations and the ideas, and me speaking to him and actually knowing him, everything was tailor made after that. Myself and Avenue Beatz, we just wanted to put together the best s—t for him that would complement his style.

And the crazy part is, the album took a little bit long, but even the verses got better. You know what I’m saying? The verses got better, the the beats got better. Everything got better with this little wait period that we had. We used the best out of it and kept elevating the project. Stove would be like, “Yo, wait till you hear the new verse.” I’m like, “Damn.” [Laughs.]

Swizz Beatz: He’s different, man. They ass is grass.

Stove God Cooks: The first initial pack he sent, I was like, “Oh, I gotta show him I’m for real.” So I was like, “Yeah, let me lock in” I sent a few back, like five.

You guys are dropping another single with “Stovie’s Home,” but a while back you released “Welcome to My Garden.” Is that still going to be on the album or is that just like a loosie now?

Swizz Beatz: And it’s elevated, by the way.

Swizz Beatz: We wasn’t really prepared as we thought we was when we dropped that. There were still things that needed to be handled. It was like a start and a stop situation. People love that joint, but the cool part is, I think they’re gonna love it even better now.

You added some bells and whistles in post production.

Swizz Beatz: Bells, whistles, hammers. Saws and s—t.

Were you guys able to link up together in the studio, or was this done over email and text? What was the process in putting this together?

Stove God Cooks: A little bit of both, but the energy is always there.

And you guys went back to the lab, though, and made new records. Is what you’re saying too?

Stove God Cooks: Yeah, we did that too. After the little slowdown period, when we had to figure some things out, we locked back in and made it that Frankenstein moment.

I think the storytelling is great. I think that people are gonna have so much content that they’re gonna appreciate the album more. Give people what they actually been waiting for.

Stove God Cooks: That’s what it deserves.

Swizz Beatz: Yeah and there’s not going to be silence at all. Once we pop this thing off, it’s go time. It ain’t no more stopping. We got so many plans. They ass is grass.

You said that in the trailer. I’m assuming you guys shot the video already too?

Swizz Beatz: They ass is grass. [Laughs.] I’m tellin’ you.

Stove God Cooks: Just the energy. It felt like what we do with with jumper cables on it. It felt like the soulful s—t like the Griselda lane and everything that I’m coming out of times a million. Just with the drums and Swizz’s touch on in our world. That’s what it felt like.

Swizz, what is it about artists like Stove that gets you excited. Because you’ve been in the game for a minute. You’ve done it all, and seen it all. How does he motivate you?

They make a blade cut like butter, and it could also go through a brick.

Like the Hattori Hanzo sword in Kill Bill.

That’s why I like this partnership with Warner. I think it’s a positive addition to what he’s doing, and even for Babygrande to understand that it makes sense. I think that’s a power move for everybody, because the more that we can get behind the his talent, the more he gonna fly, and people want it.

We put up a BTS, and there’s more comments on that joint than a person that got 100 million followers. So, the engagement is there, the people, the audience is there. He got a product that people want. We just gotta give him the right airplane to take off and land with, and the rest is history. He’s very selective of what he does, which I think is great, and I think the consistency from this point on is going to be major for him and for us. I think this is a great celebration. The energy’s right, the visuals are right, these interviews are right. I think that it’s worth the wait.

A lot of people want to hang on to the negative side of what they’ve seen. Now we gotta celebrate the positive side. Like, yo, we not on that timing, we not talking about yesterday, we giving you what you want today. Let’s stop talking about yesterday because today’s a new day for him and for the project, and for the projects, you know what I’m sayin’? We ready to rock and roll. I’m with whatever.

Stove God Cooks: Absolutely, but I’m not just recording just to record. The s—t that Swizz was sending me the first time was so inspiring. That’s what “Garden” came out of, and a couple other joints that made that album. When I’m inspired its nonstop, but I never want to get to the point where I’m just doing it just to do it. It’s when I got something to say. There’s joints that I recorded that Swizz heard and was like, “Nah, bro. I understand you going through this, but nah, bro.” I was talking too crazy.

It’s all inspiration. Whether it’s, “I’m going through something,” or, “We’re gonna step on everybody.”

You mean Swizz was telling you to be a little bit more political with what you say?

Stove God Cooks: Nah, not at all. He’s not telling me anything. He’s just saying…

Swizz Beatz: I can’t tell that man what to say.

Stove God Cooks: I’m just one million percent honest.

The album is amazing, and to have this opportunity to work with the Big Homie and him really putting the jumper cables on my s—t and take it to where I always felt it should be.

Right now, it’s just getting the fans what they want, and I’m standing on this album. This s—t is amazing. The Most High got a funny way of leading me where I gotta get to. Meet the young architects behind your favorite movies, series, streams and stars. THR’s 33rd annual power list of Hollywood’s top players, 35 and under.

By Winston Cho, Aaron Couch, Caitlin Huston, Mia Galuppo, Ryan Gajewski, Katie Kilkenny, Borys Kit, Lacey Rose November 11, 2026

Nobody ever said that Hollywood careers come easy. But right now, with consolidation and layoffs decimating the middle rungs of entertainment’s steep corporate ladder and studio remits changing with each earnings report, dream jobs have never been more difficult to land, much less retain.

Such trends aren’t slowing the ascent of THR’s Next Gen class of 2026, who join such illustrious alumni as Donna Langley, Ari Emanuel, Kevin Feige and Peter Rice. In fact, this group is too busy forging new paths through the chaos — whether that’s bringing Gen Z back to cinemas (as indie producer Christian Vazquez did with both Primetime and You Can See Everything) or fortifying the streaming ecosystem amid endless churn (see Claudia Shin and Louie Hayes, behind the Apple TV Emmy darling Widow’s Bay and the Prime Video breakout Sterling Point, respectively.)

Meet the 35 executives and reps, each of them 35 years old or younger, who should be atop your call list, if they aren’t already.

LOGLINE Championing rom-coms like Anyone But You and Netflix’s upcoming 13 Going on 30 update.

THE ARC Altman got a unique view of the industry when she would join her father, Steve, who rented out lighting, camera and grip equipment: “I grew up doing my homework in grip trucks, and I just loved being on set.” Her dad’s rom-com adoration rubbed off during their weekly trips to the movies, where films like Love Actually made an early impression. Altman studied journalism at Emerson but caught the producing bug while moonlighting on college TV projects. After time at Gersh, she became then-Universal exec Jeff Kirschenbaum’s assistant and followed him to RK Films. While the company focused on bigger IP plays, she endorsed the script for Anyone But You (“That was my baby”), Sony’s Sydney Sweeney-Glen Powell feature that became a rare theatrical comedy hit. Up next: Amazon MGM’s tennis rom-com Love Love and Netflix’s Millie Bobby Brown-led Just Picture It.

The higher-ups took note, which is why Berger now oversees some of the studio’s most ambitious films.

Researchers at Google DeepMind have unveiled a new AI model designed to unravel how changes in our DNA affect different cells, giving scientists a more detailed view of a notoriously difficult problem that could hold important clues to the causes of disease — and point the way to new treatments.

AlphaGenome 2 is the successor to DeepMind’s genome-predicting AI model AlphaGenome, which was released last year. While nearly every cell in the human body carries the same DNA, different types of cells — from heart muscle cells and skin cells to immune cells fighting an infection — switch different genes on and off, and those patterns can change depending on what a cell is doing. As a result, the same genetic variant can have very different effects depending on the type and state of the cell.

The new model is designed to capture those differences, predicting the effects of genetic variants on individual cell types and states. Google said in a blog post published Wednesday that it offers a much finer-grained view than broader models like AlphaGenome, potentially revealing disease-relevant effects in specific cells that might otherwise go unnoticed.

AlphaGenome 2 was trained on more than 100 million individual cell profiles across 68 tissues to give it that more detailed view. DeepMind says the model effectively turns static cell atlases — huge data repositories that map and catalog individual cells in detail — “into a predictive engine for human genetics.” According to the company, AlphaGenome 2 identifies nearly twice as many variants that can change gene expression in cells than its previous state-of-the-art model.

Notably, the researchers found that AlphaGenome 2 could also make predictions about new types of cells and biological states without needing to be retrained. It could also identify gaps in experimental data, potentially helping scientists decide what to investigate next in the lab.

For Adam Gayoso, a research scientist at Google DeepMind involved in the project, the big breakthrough is “tying human genetics to cell biology.” AlphaGenome 2 gives researchers a way to drill down on genetic variants identified in large populations, to see what effects they may be having on a cellular level, and potentially connect those effects to disease, he told The Verge.

Google DeepMind’s genomics lead Žiga Avsec told The Verge that this specificity is important if you have a goal like figuring out how to treat a particular disease. “When you develop drugs, you’re targeting specific proteins and specific cell types,” he explained, which makes having this kind of granularity vital.

Building a model like AlphaGenome 2 has only recently become possible thanks to new technologies that can capture individual cells and their states at high resolution and create the vast datasets needed to train it, Avsec said. DeepMind is now working to improve its genetics models by generating more data, including for cell states that aren’t represented well today. Avsec said future versions could also potentially be trained on data “across multiple species,” giving the models a broader view of biology. Longer term, he said models like AlphaGenome 2 could feed into efforts to create “virtual cells,” digital simulations designed to mimic biological systems.

Although Avsec said he views AlphaGenome 2 as a successor to AlphaGenome, it isn’t replacing it any time soon. He said its more detailed predictions come at a computational cost and are “more expensive” to produce than those of its predecessor, he said. Those demands would have made it impractical to use AlphaGenome 2 to produce the genome-wide Atlas covering “every possible DNA letter change in the human genome” that DeepMind released in September.

Google says it is making AlphaGenome 2 available for non-commercial research via the AlphaGenome API from today, with a limited “private preview” for commercial research.

The model is the latest in a growing array of efforts by Google and rival AI labs to use the technology to tackle major problems in science and medicine. Pushmeet Kohli, who heads DeepMind’s AI for science research, has hinted that more impressive scientific results are on the way, and the organization is expected to open an automated materials science lab in the UK soon. Anthropic, meanwhile, has been experimenting with drug design and given Claude a wet lab, while OpenAI has been mowing down an impressive amount of longstanding problems in mathematics and related fields.

He believes Genie can do for the business side what AI, agents and harnesses (the software that wraps around an AI model and lets it use tools) are doing for programmers. "Of course, I was absolutely thrilled. It means a great, great deal to me. I think everyone dreams of being selected for the national team at some point," Mohya said.

Mohya was at the gym when the call came in. He then shared the good news with his parents over dinner. "They started crying," the teenager said in a video posted on Gladbach's social media channels.

The youngster, who was born in a small town east of Düsseldorf, smiles and laughs a lot in interviews. He is always friendly, and in a good mood. On the field, too, he exudes the lightheartedness and carefree spirit of a 17-year-old.

As a technically skilled and creative player, Mohya often seeks out one-on-one situations and puts his opponents under pressure with quick dribbles. In nine Bundesliga appearances this season, Mohya has scored twice and delivered one assist.

"He's so cool and just plays really well. It's fun to watch him," Borussia coach Alexander Blessin said recently.

"Wael brings a lot to the team with his laid-back attitude on the field. He's not afraid to take risks, no matter how much pressure there is," added Gladbach's sporting director Rouven Schröder on a talk show on Sport1.

Borussia Mönchengladbach has been Mohya's sporting home since he was seven years old. Last season, previous head coach Eugen Polanski promoted him to the first team, and in December 2025, Mohya made his Bundesliga debut. Against Wolfsburg, aged 16 years and 347 days old, he became the youngest Bundesliga player in the club's history.

Mohya also recently completed a voluntary social year at an elementary school in Mönchengladbach. This is a program in Germany for young people who volunteer in social sectors—such as hospitals, schools, or nursing homes—for about six to 18 months. They gain work experience, help others, and earn a little money.

At the school's open all-day care program, Mohya helped serve meals, assisted children with their homework, and played with them. That's also where his nickname comes from. Because a girl hadn't understood his first name correctly or couldn't pronounce it, she called him "Waffel." Now, that name has stuck.

Even before being called up to the Germany team, clubs in the Bundesliga and across Europe's top leagues had taken notice of Mohya and were trying to sign him. His contract with Borussia Mönchengladbach runs through 2028.

With Gladbach currently in a relegation battle, his future appears likely to be elsewhere. If the club wants to generate transfer revenue from its biggest talent, next summer would likely be the opportunity to sell him—unless he extends his contract with Borussia.

The 17-year-old's career is managed by his parents. Whichever club can convince them that their son is in good hands and help him reach his full potential likely has the best chance of securing Mohya's signing.

Mohya himself says he doesn't even know how much he's currently earning at Gladbach.

"My parents take care of all that," he told Bild recently. "I get a monthly allowance, which is more than enough for me."

Germany head coach Jürgen Klopp recently revealed how much he had enjoyed his conversations with the youngster.
