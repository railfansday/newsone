Why can't you play darts in GTA 6 when you could in previous games? Because Rockstar "didn't have the resources" to do it to a standard it'd be happy with

📅 Nov 23, 2026  |  Category: World  |  Read Source →

If you've been a fan of Grand Theft Auto for a while, you might have noticed a strange absence in GTA 6. While the game is packed full of different activities, the recurring pastime of darts is missing, having been present in GTA 5, GTA Online, and GTA 4.

The reason for the lack of darts in Grand Theft Auto 6? Well, according to Rockstar North co-studio lead Rob Nelson, it's a simple case of Rockstar having too little time and resources to make it to a high standard.

Nelson told VGC: "The darts one is a contentious one. Even we... You hear they have unlimited money or unlimited time, even we do not and so sometimes you just have to admit we are not going to be able to make this to the level we feel we need to."

Nelson elaborated that since Red Dead Redemption 2, Rockstar has taken to expanding what were small mini-games into full-on activities. He pointed to basketball in GTA 6 as an example, which you can play in a way that feels like an extension of gameplay with "fluid" movement. Even if it's a relatively small chunk of the game, a lot of time went into making it feel like "a game in its own right", Nelson said. And the same is true of pool.

"A lot of work [is needed] to get it tuned right. Darts is one we just didn't get to," Nelson said. The studio "didn't have the resources" to include it in the game and have it up to par. "It's better not to do it than to do it and have it not be good enough," he said.

Even without darts, there are plenty of side activities in Grand Theft Auto 6 that help expand Vice City. This includes diving and treasure hunting and fishing, both of which you unlock near the start of the game. Canada becomes the 25th ally to back the NATO Innovation Fund, with €64.6m

📅 Nov 23, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Ottawa first said in 2023 that it would join the defence tech fund. The new commitment opens its capital and co-investor network to Canadian startups.

Canada’s Minister of National Defence, David McGuinty, at the Triennial Congress of Ukrainian Canadians in Toronto, November 2025

Canada is joining the NATO Innovation Fund, becoming the 25th NATO ally to back the venture capital fund. The government is committing €64.59m, about CA$100m, the fund said in an announcement on 21 November. The Business Development Bank of Canada (BDC) will hold and manage the stake.

The new money takes total commitments to the fund past €1.1bn. The NATO Innovation Fund invests in deep tech startups working on defence, security and resilience.

“Canada has the talent, the innovation, and the ambition to be a global leader in defence and security,” Canada’s Minister of National Defence, David McGuinty, said in the announcement.

Canada has said since 2023 that it intended to join, as Jesse Snyder reported for The Logic in April 2025. In April 2024, the government said it had set aside the money. It had promised CA$107m over 20 years. A fund spokesperson confirmed to The Logic at the time that Canada was not a limited partner.

In July 2026, Prime Minister Mark Carney’s office said Canada had agreed to open technical negotiations to join the fund’s first sub-fund. Its backgrounder said the talks should finish before the end of the year.

Membership gives Canadian startups access to patient capital and a network of co-investors, the fund said. It also opens routes to governments and businesses across the alliance that could adopt their technology. BDC runs a Defence Platform for defence and dual-use companies.

“Canada’s participation in the NATO Innovation Fund extends that reach across the Alliance,” said Isabelle Hudon, BDC’s president and chief executive.

The fund has more than 30 portfolio companies, and some have already moved into Canada. Isar Aerospace, which reached orbit in September, was picked to build a launch pad and launch Canadian satellites. Finnish airship maker Kelluu recently entered the Canadian market.

Fiona Murray, chair of the fund’s board, called the decision a landmark moment. She said every participating nation gains a window into Canada’s deep tech sector. Ari Jónsson, the fund’s president, said the next step is faster adoption, opening doors to contracts for Canadian startups.

Canadian defence tech is already drawing private money. In July, Ottawa’s Dominion Dynamics raised CA$139M, a record for a Canadian defence startup. In Europe, Helsing raised $1.8bn the same month.

Wall Street Sees 64% SpaceX Rally Even as AI Stocks Crash

📅 Nov 23, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Home » Crypto Market News » Wall Street Sees 64% SpaceX Rally Even as AI Stocks Crash

Jefferies initiates SPCX at Buy with a $210 target, signaling institutional appetite for AI compute infrastructure.

Investment bank Jefferies initiated coverage of SpaceX stock (SPCX) with a Buy rating and a $210 price target, implying roughly 64% upside from Friday’s closing price of $127.79. The bullish call stands out sharply as broader artificial intelligence and semiconductor equities sink into a steep correction.

Rather than valuing SpaceX purely as a rocket manufacturer, Jefferies sees Elon Musk’s company emerging as a dominant global infrastructure platform spanning satellite broadband, orbital launch, and AI computing capacity.

In a research note titled It’s Just Rocket Science, Jefferies analyst Sheila Kahyaoglu identified Starlink as the core engine behind SpaceX’s long-term valuation.

Jefferies projects Starlink could generate $92 billion in annual revenue by 2029—growing at a 70% compound annual rate—with operating margins exceeding 60%, according to Investing.com.

The core launch division is forecast to deliver $9 billion in revenue and $4 billion in EBITDA by 2029 as Starship scales toward 315 flights per year.
