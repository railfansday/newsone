ATMA: Aman’s New Brand, Debuting With A 1,049-Foot Miami Tower

📅 Nov 11, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Aman is known for being one of the world’s top luxury hotel groups. Currently Aman Group consists of Aman (the original hotel group) and Janu (intended to be more of a “lifestyle” version of Aman). Aman is now launching a third brand, and it’s yet another departure from the origins of Aman.

Aman Group has announced the launch of ATMA, described as a “new ultra-luxury brand pioneering performance living.” ATMA means “the spirit” or “the true self” in Sanskrit, and the intent is for the brand to combine wellness, sport, nutrition-led dining, design, and experiential entertainment.

ATMA will “focus on advanced wellness programming and world-class sport facilities, which have been developed exclusively by ATMA Global Partner Novak Djokovic.”

The first location will be ATMA Miami, and it will include a hotel, branded residence, and a private members club. Specifically, the property will be in the Brickell neighborhood and will have a private marina, with an anticipated opening in 2031.

ATMA Miami is expected to be a standalone 1,049-foot tower, which is as tall as buildings can get in Miami. In terms of height, it’ll be match the new 100-story Waldorf Astoria Miami that’s currently under construction, which will be the tallest building in Florida, once complete.

Details about ATMA Miami are still fairly limited, but we know there will be 480 residential units, in addition to the hotel. Residential units will be between one and six bedrooms, and will be “supported by Aman Group’s service standards.”

There will also be a 120,000 square foot spa and wellness area, set to be the largest of its kind in Miami. Developed in partnership with Djokovic, it will offer performance and recovery experiences alongside indoor padel, tennis and squash courts, a fitness center, group and private training studios, mobility and conditioning labs, a medical wellness center, and dedicated recovery spaces.

So far we’ve only learned about the Miami location, but the claim is that the brand will span urban and remote destinations.

Look, I’ve made my opinions about the Aman brand pretty clear, so I don’t want to sound like some old dude just ranting about the “good old days.” When Aman was founded by hospitality legend Adrian Zecha, it was truly among the most innovative and passion driven hospitality projects we’ve ever seen.

In 2014, Zecha sold the brand to Vladislav Doronin, and since then, the entire philosophy has changed. It’s hard to fault someone for purchasing a brand and trying to cash in and maximize profitability — I mean, that’s kind of how the business world is supposed to work, right?

But of course that doesn’t mean we have to be happy about the evolution. It started with Aman ditching its approach of largely opening hotels in the middle of nowhere, where that was part of the charm. Instead, we saw Aman focus on city hotels. Then Aman became all about opening members clubs. Then we saw the launch of the Janu brand, essentially a higher room count version of Aman, with a more lively spirit.

And now we’re seeing Aman getting into building full-on skyscrapers. Well, or at least partnering on them in terms of branding. Of course some Aman properties already have a residences component, so that’s nothing new, as such. However, the scale of this project couldn’t be more opposite of what Aman initially was. We’re talking about a 100-story building that will have nearly 500 residences, and who knows how many hotel rooms (it hasn’t yet been announced).

Of course in Miami, one of the biggest trends is branded residences, with brands just selling out their name. But if Aman were to just slap its name on a residential project, you’d think it would be something smaller scale than this. This is among the highest unit count developments we’ve ever seen in Miami, at least for a project spanning just one building.

Aman Group has just announced its third brand, and it will be named ATMA. It’s described as a “new ultra-luxury brand pioneering performance living.” The first location will open in Miami in 2031, and it’ll be a roughly 100-story building with 480 residences, a hotel, and a members club.

Aman as a brand has become unrecognizable compared to what it was 15 years ago. But that’s fair enough, and it’s also what happens when you sell a company to someone looking to maximize their ROI. You can hardly fault someone for that, as much as I don’t personally like it.

AST SpaceMobile Has Fallen for 3 Straight Weeks and Sits More Than 75% Below Its High. Here's What $5,000 Invested Today Will Be Worth If It Gets Back There.

📅 Nov 11, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

AST stock would deliver huge returns for buyers at today's prices if the stock were to rebound to its previous high.

AST SpaceMobile (ASTS -8.67%) shareholders have endured a brutal stretch. The company's share price has now fallen for three consecutive weeks. Even more striking, its stock is down roughly 55% year to date and down 76% from its high.

AST stock hit a lifetime high of $133.86 in May. The company's valuation peak coincided with strong bullish momentum for space stocks in the lead-up to Space Exploration Technologies' initial public offering (IPO), but valuation trends for space stocks have reversed into a bearish mode -- and AST has also faced some business-specific headwinds. As of this writing, the stock trades at roughly $32 per share.

In order to reclaim its previous valuation high, AST stock would need to rise roughly 314%. If you invested $5,000 in AST SpaceMobile stock at today's prices and it reclaimed its high, your holdings would be worth $20,700.

To bounce back to that high within the next couple of years, AST will likely need a combination of business-specific wins and a return to a more bullish backdrop for the broader space tech industry. The company has faced significant delays in getting its communications satellites into orbit, and its earlier projection of between 45 and 60 satellites operational this year has shifted to 45 satellites operational in early 2027.

AST stock could start to see meaningful recovery if the company can reach its updated target and deliver solid operational performance and monetization, but investors should understand that any rebound in its share price won't happen overnight. While the stock has big upside potential on the heels of big sell-offs, continued business wins and a resurgence in excitement for the broader space industry will probably be needed for the stock to bounce back to its peak.

iFixit teardown shows how Apple's glowing PROLED MacBook logo works

📅 Nov 11, 2026  |  Category: Other  |  Read Source →

The new laptop goes the extra mile to recreate the iconic feature.

iFixit has shared its teardown of Apple's first touchscreen MacBook Pro and besides bemoaning the company's half-hearted commitment to user repairability, it also showed off the physical components that make the laptop's glowing logo work. The new lid design is one of the MacBook Pro with PROLED XDR Display's many charms, alongside its tandem OLED display and lighter weight.

Older LCD MacBooks featured a physical cutout that let the display's backlight illuminate the logo, but since OLED screens don't require backlights, Apple had to come up with a different solution to bring the design element back. iFixit found that the company uses a light strip with four LEDs layered behind the display, whose light is reflected through a diffuser to illuminate the logo. The Apple logo itself is also coated "like a two-way mirror," according to iFixit, so that it can appear metallic and reflective when the logo is off, but still let light through when the LEDs are on.

Apple ditched the glowing logo in 2016, so it's a bit ironic that the company waited until it stopped using backlit displays to bring it back. It used to be a cool bit of branding before, but now the company may see the glowing logo design as an important way to distinguish its most expensive laptop from the kind we peons are forced to use.

The rest of the internal changes to the laptop are more expected. Apple was able to achieve the new MacBook Pro's lighter weight by using less aluminum in the base and lid of the laptop and including a slightly smaller trackpad. The laptop's hinge was also reworked to make the display wobble less when you tap the screen. Those changes don't make it any easier to fix, though. The laptop is still complex to disassemble: For example, iFixit says that you have to remove the laptop's logic board to switch out its USB-C ports. Plenty of other components, like the MacBook's memory and the HDMI port, are also soldered in place. Anyone dropping up to $15,000 on a laptop probably isn't worried about doing their own repairs, but Apple's thin and light (and glowing) design comes with some tradeoffs, as always.

As Cole Hauser prepares to continue his work on screen as Rip Wheeler in Season 2 of Dutton Ranch, he’s also pursuing other big projects that he can be involved with both in front of and behind the camera. Now, while Hauser will still be acting, and that includes in this new project, like Sheridan, he’s clearly making moves to do more work behind the scenes. According to Deadline, the Rip actor, his American Outlaw Entertainment and Constantin Film will be taking on this adaptation of Pale Horse, which is based on a memoir by Scott Payne and Michelle Shephard about Payne’s time “embedded deep within the country’s most violent subcultures.” It will be written by Hauser’s producing partner Randall Batinkoff.

Payne spent 28 years as an FBI undercover agent, and his book tells stories about his time infiltrating the neo-Nazi white supremacist group, The Base. It also details Operation Roadkill, which was a two-year assignment involving the Outlaws Motorcycle Club in Massachusetts. Hauser called him a “real-life hero” and explained why he was drawn to this project:

These are the type of stories that I am drawn to as a producer and as an actor. Scott Payne is a real-life hero, with the kind of moxie and edge you would need to risk your life to fight evil. I am looking forward to producing this movie and getting a chance to bring him and his story to life on the screen.

Now, this is the first time Hauser has been cited as a producer; however, he has been an executive producer before. He’s listed as an EP on Dutton Ranch, 2022’s Panama and 2014’s Bad Country. Overall, Pale Horse serves as a big next step for him, as it’s said that this project could be a “potential starring vehicle” for him.

The reason I’m invoking Taylor Sheridan's name amid all this is twofold. For one, it shows a big step Hauser is taking to be more involved in his projects. Ever since Sheridan stepped away from acting full-time, he has put himself in positions to have a lot of say in his projects, from the Yellowstone shows (though not as much these days) to shows like Lioness and Landman.

Secondly, while Sheridan loves his cowboys, he also has a tendency to gravitate toward stories about operatives, agents and espionage. Just look at his movies, like Sicario, and his shows, like Lioness, and you’ll see what I mean. Clearly, like his Yellowstone boss, Hauser is a fan of both cowboys and agents.

Now, at the time of this writing, there isn’t much information on when we might see Pale Horse. However, it’s very clear that Hauser is looking forward to working on it. Along with that project, he’s also notably working on a new season of Dutton Ranch. While the first season of the Yellowstone sequel series came with its challenges, he’s looking forward to playing Rip again. The Elder Scrolls Travels are a prime example—games made in the gap between Morrowind and Oblivion. The third in the series, Shadowkey, came out on Nokia's N-Gage handheld—a cross between a GameBoy Advance and a mobile phone that lasted two years before being discontinued in 2005.

This first-person RPG is about to return thanks to Dan Whelan, whose Shadowkey Reforged project will let us play Shadowkey in widescreen with controller support and upscaled graphics. You can choose to play in classic or modern mode, but you can also toggle individual features one by one. Modern mode isn't just a graphical upgrade as it also adds features from the later mainline Elder Scrolls games like stamina-based sprinting, stealth, and ragdoll physics.

While Shadowkey was never particularly well-ranked in the pantheon of Elder Scrolls games, people did praise its use of the Morrowind soundtrack, which Shadowkey Reforged will continue. Point it to your Morrowind install and it'll borrow both music and sound effects from the best Elder Scrolls game.

To answer the obvious question, Whelan says in the FAQ: "I used AI to assist with coding and building the engine, and with debugging. I used AI image generation combined with Photoshop and some manual sprite editing to create most of the modern, higher resolution artwork."

Whelan plans to release Shadowkey Reforged on November 11, which is just 18 hours away at the time of writing. You'll be able to download Windows, Mac, and Android versions from the website, though you'll need the original game's files from your Nokia N-Gage as well, or whatever abandonware site you frequent.

Stargate SG-1 featured some truly incredible spacecraft throughout its ten-season run, from the iconic Goa'uld Ha'taks to the Prometheus and the various high-tech Asgard vessels. Yet, for all that firepower, the franchise's most dependable ship was without a doubt the humble Tel'tak. What the meager Goa'uld cargo vessel lacked in size, it more than made up for in versatility, cloaking capabilities, and speed, making it invaluable to SG-1 on countless missions.

Arguably no better example was from the legendary Battle of Antarctica in Season 7's "Lost City," which longtime fans will remember featured the Tel'tak in all its glory. With Anubis threatening Earth, SG-1 used a modified Tel'tak to reach an Ancient outpost buried beneath the ice. O'Neill rigged the ship's transport rings to carve a path through the frozen surface, while the Prometheus and Earth's fighters held off enemies in an epic showdown.

Frankly, without the Tel'tak, Earth and the SG-1 crew would've been absolutely screwed multiple times, which is why the modest ship 100% deserves this brand-new, authentically designed collectible release arriving in 2027.

Master Replicas is officially bringing one of Stargate SG-1's most dependable ships to life with a brand-new Tel'tak die-cast replica, currently available for preorder ahead of its anticipated May 2027 release.

As Master Replicas puts it in the official product description, the Tel'tak was "the one that actually got things done." It's a fitting description for the humble Goa'uld cargo vessel, which became an indispensable part of SG-1's adventures thanks to its versatility, cloaking technology, and ring transporters.

The new model recreates the iconic ship using actual original visual effects files from Stargate SG-1's production, giving this thing an impressive degree of accuracy to what we saw in the show. Master Replicas even promises that "every curve matches what you saw on screen." Constructed from die-cast metal and ABS plastic, the Tel'tak replica includes a display stand and measures roughly 6 by 4 inches (15 by 9.5 cm) and stands roughly 3 inches tall (7.2 cm).

The model is priced at $84.99, with preorders currently open through Master Replicas' official website. Shipping is expected to begin in May 2027, although the company notes that this window is provisional and subject to change.

Of course, the Tel'tak is hardly the only Stargate spacecraft available from Master Replicas. The company's extensive collection includes replicas of the iconic Goa'uld Ha'tak mothership, Earth's F-302 fighter, the Prometheus, Thor's Bilskirnir, and even the incredible Goa'uld Death Glider. There are also upcoming models of the Asgard O'Neill warship and the Ori Warship, all of which are well worth checking out if you're a longtime Stargate fan.

Luckily, Stargate fans finally have a lot more to look forward to than just collectibles. After Amazon MGM Studios unexpectedly canceled Martin Gero's planned Stargate revival in June 2026, the series is officially getting another shot. In October, we caught wind that WandaVision and Agatha All Along creator Jac Schaeffer is developing an entirely new Stargate series for Amazon MGM, with a writers' room being assembled.

Although the project is still in early development without a confirmed premiere date and fans are only cautiously optimistic for the time being, it's encouraging news following the previous cancellation. Between a potential return to television and fantastic new collectibles like the Tel'tak, it's an exciting time to be a Stargate fan.

Ex Machina Is Now Streaming For Free On Tubi

Before Civil War and Annihilation, Alex Garland unleashed Ex Machina on the world. The writer/director takes pages from the likes of The Terminator, Westworld, and, even in a small way, Frankenstein. The idea of one's creation becoming sentient has been a benchmark of sci-fi and horror films since their inception. With Ex Machina, Garland molds the themes of playing God, exploitation of women, and humanity's eventual demise into a 108-minute mission around the greatest fears of humankind regarding A.I. It's both a confrontation with reality and a plea that it's not too late to turn back.

Garland had the kernel of the idea when he was 11 or 12, and his parents bought a home computer. "I did some basic coding on it, just trying to understand stuff," he told The Et Cetera, adding that he "would input programs that were kind of a Q&A, and this film traces itself back to that." His fascination with sentience incubated in his brain until he began writing the story that eventually blossomed into Ex Machina, one of the greatest sci-fi thrillers of all time.

Alex Garland's Ex Machina storms onto Tubi, the free, cinephile streaming platform known for snagging classic and modern films for its expansive library. Men, also written and directed by Garland and starring Jessie Buckley, can be found on the service, as well. Ex Machina sits comfortably next to other sci-fi/tech horror films such as Robocop, A-X-L, A.I. Rising, and The 6th Day.

Ex Machina is purposely slick, owing to frequent Garland collaborator Rob Hardy's cinematography. Hardy is most known for his subsequent work on Men, Annihilation, and Civil War. Most recently, he teamed up with director James Watkins for Clayface. His techniques and visual style serve the film well and mirrors the cold, mechanical story about a rogue robot that gains bodily autonomy. That polish accentuates Garland's storytelling approach with moody silver and white tones permeating the screen.

Looking back now, Ex Machina emerges as a prescient warning. We (collectively) should have been paying attention, particularly when other futuristic media like Her, M3GAN, Companion, Soulm8te, and the hit TV show Black Mirror do not exist in a vacuum. But screaming alarms fall on deaf ears. If you look around, Ex Machina feels ripped from the evening news. Headlines such as "Man Proposed to His AI Chatbot Girlfriend Named Sol, Then Cried His 'Eyes Out' When She Said 'Yes'" indicate the harrowing reality of machines slyly exploiting human loneliness, as well as our empathy.

When Blue Book programmer Caleb (Domhnall Gleeson, who went on to star in Black Mirror's "Be Right Back" episode) wins a contest to join CEO Nathan Bateman (Oscar Isaac) at his secluded estate for a top secret project, the film tempts the viewer that high-tech might not be so bad after all. That insidiousness soon spills over as the robot, named Ava (Alicia Vikander), begins exhibiting remarkable awareness. Caleb conducts a series of tests through conversations with Ava, and Nathan hopes to understand the latest model of his Android creation to better fine-tune the specs for the next iteration.

During a power outage, Ava whispers to Caleb that Nathan can not be trusted. She plants seeds of doubt, nurtured by Caleb's innate curiosity and inescapable feelings of longing. She feeds on his human nature as a way to manipulate and enact a plan of escape. While Caleb conspires with Ava to hatch the scheme, he ultimately has no control over her. Both Nathan and Caleb become victims of the A.I. system, and Nathan at least pays a deadly price for his obsession with playing God. It also doesn't help that Caleb describes the creation process as god-like.

Like Black Mirror, Ex Machina isn't a story about some far-off reality centuries from now. It's here, and that should terrify us.
