James Webb’s Mysteriously Bright Galaxies May Finally Have an Explanation

📅 Nov 11, 2026  |  Category: Other  |  Read Source →

Calculations suggest powerful ultraviolet light from young stars could sweep gas out of early galaxies while leaving dense star-forming regions intact, offering a clue to their mysterious brightness.

The James Webb Space Telescope has revealed something unexpected about the early universe: bright galaxies were far more common just a few hundred million years after the Big Bang than astronomers thought possible. A new analysis suggests these galaxies may not necessarily be producing more light than expected. Instead, powerful ultraviolet radiation from young stars could be clearing away gas and dust, allowing more of their light to reach us.

Researchers at the Simons Foundation’s Flatiron Institute in New York City and the University of Texas at Dallas investigated this possibility by examining how ultraviolet light called Lyman-alpha radiation interacts with material surrounding young stars. Their findings, published November 4 in The Astrophysical Journal, suggest that this radiation can drive gas and dust out of less dense regions of galaxies, potentially explaining why some of the earliest galaxies appear unusually bright.

“Our results show Lyman-alpha radiation can push low-density gas and dust away really, really fast,” says Shyam Menon, a research fellow at the Flatiron Institute’s Center for Computational Astrophysics and the study’s lead author. “It can sort of clear the ‘fog,’ allowing us to see stars in the early universe much more clearly than we would otherwise.”

Lyman-alpha radiation forms when an electron collides with a hydrogen atom or joins a hydrogen ion, and it can be especially abundant in regions where young stars are forming. When this light encounters gas or dust, it transfers momentum to the material, creating pressure that can push it outward.

What makes Lyman-alpha radiation particularly powerful is how hydrogen responds to it. While most absorbed light is released at a different wavelength, hydrogen atoms almost immediately re-emit Lyman-alpha radiation at the same wavelength, allowing nearby atoms to absorb and release it repeatedly. These repeated interactions increase the force exerted by the light, potentially pushing surrounding material away from regions where stars are forming.

Astronomers have long suspected that this pressure could reshape stellar nurseries by removing gas needed for star formation, possibly even stopping the process under extreme conditions. However, the repeated interactions between radiation and hydrogen are difficult to reproduce in computer simulations, making it challenging to determine how much influence the radiation actually has.

“We’ve known for a very long time that Lyman-alpha radiation could be important because it carries a lot of force,” Menon says. “But we’ve never actually been able to estimate how important it is in a realistic environment because of how challenging it is to model.”

To address this problem, Menon and collaborator Aaron Smith of the University of Texas at Dallas analyzed snapshots from an existing simulation of a star-forming region in the early universe. Rather than tracking every interaction between light and matter, they calculated how much pressure Lyman-alpha radiation would exert on the surrounding gas and dust.

Their calculations revealed that the radiation behaves differently depending on the density of the surrounding material. In dense star-forming regions, gravity remained strong enough to hold gas and dust in place despite the outward pressure, suggesting that Lyman-alpha radiation had little effect on star formation there. In less crowded regions between stars, however, the radiation could overcome gravity and drive material outward at high speeds.

Multiple views of a 2.5-million-year simulation visualizing a gas cloud as it is cleared away and the formation of stars. From left to right, the views show the gas density, ionization, and temperature. Credit: Shyam Menon (Flatiron Institute) and Aaron Smith (UT Dallas)

“When the density is low enough, the Lyman-alpha radiation can push the gas to really high speeds, launching explosive winds that can shoot material clear out of the galaxy,” Menon says.

These winds could help explain the unexpectedly bright galaxies detected by the James Webb Space Telescope. By removing loosely distributed gas and dust from between stars, the radiation may create clearer paths for starlight to escape, making distant galaxies appear brighter without requiring them to produce unusually large amounts of light.

Important questions remain, particularly about the properties of dust in the early universe and how radiation interacts with moving gas as stars form. Researchers will need more comprehensive simulations that account for these processes together to better understand their influence on early galaxies.

Recent work by Olof Nebrin of Stockholm University and study co-author Smith has demonstrated that these interactions can be modeled in two dimensions, although more realistic three-dimensional simulations remain a challenge.

“But the hope is that in the coming years, with improvements to computing power and the development of more effective algorithms, we can eventually do these simulations in three dimensions.”

Reference: “Lyα Radiation Pressure in Dense Star Clusters: Implications for Star Formation and Winds at Cosmic Dawn” by Shyam H. Menon and Aaron Smith, 4 November 2026, The Astrophysical Journal. DOI: 10.3847/1538-4357/aeaed6

Alaska, Hawaiian Pilot MECs Approve Tentative Joint Contract

📅 Nov 11, 2026  |  Category: Other  |  Read Source →

Pilots at Alaska Airlines and Hawaiian Airlines are one step closer to a deal that will bring both work groups together under a single labor contract.

ALPA announced Wednesday that its Alaska and Hawaiian master executive councils unanimously approved a tentative agreement on a joint collective bargaining compact. The two councils had been negotiating since February 2025.

The proposed agreement will now go before the combined membership for a ratification vote.

Details of the agreement were not immediately available to the public.

Union officials said they worked to address the priorities of both work groups and have established a “contractual foundation for their future as a combined workforce.”

“This TA is the result of more than 600 days of challenging negotiations, countless hours of work by our volunteers, and an unwavering commitment to the pilots we represent,” Drew Coyle, the Alaska MEC chair, said in a statement. “Our Joint Negotiating Committee remained focused on the priorities of both pilot groups while working through some of the most complex issues facing our combined membership. Nothing about this process was easy. Now it’s our pilots’ turn to review the agreement, ask the tough questions, and decide whether it meets their expectations.”

Ratification voting starts Dec. 1 and closes Dec. 22.

Alaska and Hawaiian merged in 2024 and received a single operating certificate from the FAA in 2025. This effectively transformed Hawaiian from an independent airline to a brand under Alaska Air Group. Alaska has pledged to maintain Hawaiian’s brand identity, and Hawaiian retains a separate corporate leadership based in Honolulu.

The two brands have continued to integrate however, with Alaska now making use of long-haul aircraft originally owned by Hawaiian. Earlier this year, Alaska implemented a single passenger service system.

NBC Goes All In On Its Blue Bloods For Adults Replacement

📅 Nov 11, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Network television has become increasingly cautious about committing to lengthy seasons, particularly when introducing new dramas. With streaming reshaping audience expectations and traditional viewing habits, securing additional episodes during a freshman season is an increasingly meaningful achievement. It also signals a network's confidence in a show's long-term potential.

Blue Bloods spent 14 seasons representing the consistency that broadcast networks strive to achieve. The CBS procedural combined family drama with traditional crime storytelling, developing a devoted audience throughout its run. Its conclusion left a noticeable gap in the television landscape, but its success demonstrated that audiences still appreciate character-driven procedurals.

NBC's Line of Fire has emerged as a promising successor, following the Hollingsworth family as they navigate their complicated relationships and careers in federal law enforcement. Starring Peter Krause and Hope Davis, the series brings a politically charged, morally complex approach to the family procedural, distinguishing itself from its CBS predecessor.

Now, NBC has demonstrated its confidence in Line of Fire by expanding its inaugural season from 13 to 18 episodes. The additional five episodes represent a significant endorsement of the freshman drama, suggesting the network recognizes the potential of a series that builds upon an established formula while introducing something distinctly its own.

NBC's decision to extend Line of Fire season 1 is supported by impressive ratings, establishing the freshman drama as one of the network's biggest successes of the 2026–2027 television season.

Originally ordered for 13 episodes, the series received an additional five, bringing its first season to 18 episodes. Notably, Line of Fire became the first new broadcast series of the season to receive an episode extension, demonstrating NBC's early confidence in its performance.

The numbers help explain the network's enthusiasm. Following its September 21 premiere, Line of Fire accumulated more than 14 million cross-platform viewers, including repeat broadcasts. Its second episode also experienced a 47% increase in the coveted 18 to 49 demographic compared to the premiere. These figures suggest that the show isn't simply benefiting from initial curiosity but is successfully encouraging viewers to return for additional episodes.

Beyond its impressive audience numbers, Line of Fire has helped strengthen NBC's Monday night programming. Airing after The Voice, the series considerably improved the network's 10 p.m. timeslot compared to the previous season's performance of Brilliant Minds. Its success across both traditional television and streaming also demonstrates its ability to reach viewers regardless of how they consume entertainment.

The additional episodes are particularly encouraging because Line of Fire has already established an ambitious overarching mystery that benefits from an extended season. Rather than rushing toward a resolution, the series now has more opportunities to develop its characters, explore the consequences of their discoveries, and build upon the conspiracy threatening the Hollingsworth family.

Although an expanded episode order doesn't guarantee a second season, it represents a substantial step toward establishing Line of Fire as a permanent fixture in NBC's programming lineup.

The similarities between Line of Fire and Blue Bloods are immediately apparent, particularly in their emphasis on multi-generational families working in law enforcement. Both series recognize that their characters' professional responsibilities become considerably more complicated when their personal relationships are involved. However, Line of Fire takes that premise further by making those familial connections a potential liability rather than simply a source of strength.

Throughout its 14 seasons, Blue Bloods centered on the Reagan family's commitment to justice, even when their differing perspectives created disagreements. Frank Reagan's position as NYPD Commissioner frequently complicated his relationships with his children, but the family remained largely united by their shared principles. Their weekly Sunday dinners reinforced that connection, reminding audiences that family came first regardless of their professional conflicts.

The Hollingsworth family operates under considerably different circumstances. Rather than working within different branches of the NYPD, they occupy positions across the FBI, Secret Service, U.S. Marshals, and Department of Justice. Their respective agencies often have competing priorities, creating opportunities for professional disagreements that extend beyond the family's personal struggles.

More importantly, the Hollingsworths aren't nearly as united as the Reagans. Mike and Jane's complicated relationship, their children's personal secrets, and the family's involvement in an increasingly dangerous conspiracy create divisions that threaten their ability to trust one another. Whereas the Reagans typically resolved their disagreements through communication and mutual respect, the Hollingsworths frequently conceal information from each other, even when those secrets could have serious consequences.

As CBS's 2026-2027 schedule goes deep into the fall season, the network finally confronts the painful truth about the Reagans in Blue Bloods.

The series also modernizes the traditional procedural format by incorporating an overarching mystery alongside its weekly investigations. Blue Bloods largely relied on self-contained cases, allowing audiences to enjoy individual episodes without following every previous installment. Line of Fire, meanwhile, uses its continuing conspiracy to give seemingly unrelated investigations greater significance while encouraging viewers to remain invested in the larger story.

Ultimately, Line of Fire doesn't need to replicate Blue Bloods to become its spiritual successor. By retaining the emotional foundation of a family-centered procedural while introducing greater moral ambiguity and serialized storytelling, NBC has created a drama capable of appealing to longtime Blue Bloods fans without feeling like a direct imitation. Its expanded episode order suggests that this updated approach is already paying off.

Festo Features at Automation Fair a Robust Suite of Pneumatic Safety Solutions

📅 Nov 11, 2026  |  Category: World  |  Read Source →

The Festo CIP Safety module and valve terminals help reduce energy consumption and increase machine availability.

MASON, Ohio, Nov. 11, 2026 /PRNewswire/ -- Festo features a suite of pneumatic safety solutions at Automation Fair, including a preview of its CPX-AP-A CIP Safety module. The module, in combination with the safety features of Festo valve terminals, helps prevent unexpected startup (PUS) and lowers engineering and assembly time for OEMs. Festo Automation Fair Booth #861, Thomas M. The Festo CPX-AP-A CIP Safety module controls and monitors safety I/O, supporting safety functions up to Performance Level e (PL e). This can be used to prevent unexpected startups without depressurizing the machine. Retaining working pressure reduces energy consumption and recharge time and improves machine availability.

CIP Safety brings functional safety onto the industrial network

The Common Industrial Protocol (CIP), managed by ODVA, an independent standards-development organization for industrial automation, provides a common communications architecture for industrial automation networks and is the basis for EtherNet/IP and DeviceNet.

CIP Safety extends the CIP architecture to functional safety, allowing safety controllers, I/O, interlocks, light curtains, and drives to communicate over the same network used for machine control without requiring a standalone network.

By eliminating the need for a separate safety network, CIP Safety reduces wiring and network infrastructure while simplifying machine design, commissioning, and diagnostics.

Integrating safety into the valve terminal

The CPX-AP-A CIP Safety module, scheduled for availability in 2027, enables OEMs to integrate safety close to the pneumatic application. The module can be mounted on Festo's three leading valve terminal families: VTUX, MPA-S and VTSA, allowing machine builders to apply the same safety architecture across valve terminals designed for different application and performance requirements.

Each CIP Safety module provides six single-channel or three dual-channel safe inputs and three safe outputs. Machine builders can use the inputs for safety devices such as E-stops and light curtains, while the outputs can safely control valve zones, pilot air, exhaust valves, and external devices such as a servo drive's Safe Torque Off (STO) function. Up to four modules can be installed on a valve terminal, providing up to 24 safe inputs and 12 safe outputs. Three additional safety solutions in the suite

Festo's valve terminal, the VTUX, has been designed with safety in mind. VTUX valves feature negative overlap spools that allow for safe exhausting of compressed air in case a valve spool is in an indeterminate position.

End Of An Era: Hong Kong’s Iconic “Symphony Of Lights” Show Ending

📅 Nov 11, 2026  |  Category: World  |  Read Source →

Hong Kong is one of my favorite cities in the world. When I think of Hong Kong, the beautiful waterfront and skyline on both sides of Victoria Harbour come to mind. Along those lines, a nightly staple of the waterfront is coming to an end, and I’m sad about it…

Since 2004, Hong Kong has had the “Symphony of Lights” show every single night at 8PM, lasting for around 10 minutes. It holds the Guinness World Record for being the world’s largest permanent light and sound show, as it involves dozens of buildings.

It’s one of the things that contributes to Hong Kong’s electric energy, as you have a sea of people lining up on the waterfront (typically on the Tsim Sha Tsui side, though sometimes also on the Central side) every night to watch it.

Well, after 22 years, the Symphony of Lights will be coming to an end. The last show is expected to take place on Wednesday, November 18, 2026. To mark the end of the era, the final show will feature special pyrotechnics and fireworks.

Why has Hong Kong decided to end the nightly Symphony of Lights show? Officially, it’s a strategic shift in the city’s tourism and event strategy, described as being driven by shifting visitor habits, diminishing appeal, high maintenance costs, and a pivot to seasonal attractions. We’re told that we can expect “something new” in the future, but it doesn’t sound like it’ll be a full replacement for the Symphony of Lights.

I must admit, I’m sad to see this change in Hong Kong. No matter how often I visited Hong Kong, I always made a point of watching the Symphony of Lights, and always found it to be so enjoyable. Hong Kong has one of the world’s most gorgeous skylines, and the nightly show just took that to the next level.

I am curious what the backstory here is. Was the cost of operating this really so high, in the scheme of things? While I can in theory appreciate the concept of diminishing appeal, the nightly show always had a crowd in the thousands waiting to enjoy it — that was true 20 years ago when I first visited Hong Kong, and it was also true when I visited earlier this year.

Hong Kongers will soon have to bid farewell to the Symphony of Lights, the iconic nightly show that has been lighting up the city’s skyline since 2004. Officially, this change is described as being due to shifting visitor habits, diminishing appeal, high maintenance costs, and a pivot to seasonal attractions.

I’ve enjoyed this show every time I visited Hong Kong, so I’m sad to see this development. The skyline is still incredible and leaves me in awe every time, but there’s no denying that the nightly show added just a little bit of extra sparkle.
