‘Train to Busan’ Director Yeon Sang-ho on AI Thriller ‘Paradise Lost’: ‘Something as Mysterious and Unknowable to Us as a Black Hole’

📅 Nov 11, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

After taking 'Colony' to Cannes earlier this year, the Korean filmmaker discusses AI, grief and family, and why a tight budget and a short shoot sharpened his latest film.

In Musan City, a decaying small town that exists only on screen, a grieving mother puts on a pair of AR glasses and spends time with her 10-year-old son. He has been gone for nine years, one of the children on a camp bus that vanished. Then the real boy comes home, grown up and a stranger, and the comfort the glasses offered starts to turn into something far more frightening.

That is the setup of “Paradise Lost,” the new psychological thriller from Yeon Sang-ho, the director of “Train to Busan” and the series “Hellbound.” It sits at the opposite end of the scale from “Colony,” which Yeon premiered in the Midnight Screenings section at Cannes earlier this year. “Paradise Lost” went on to Toronto’s Special Presentations section, where Yeon had also taken last year’s micro-budget “The Ugly,” and then screened as a Gala Presentation in Busan. Screenings sold out at both festivals. Related Stories Tom Hanks Returning to Broadway After 14 Years for New Play 'This World of Tomorrow' 'You've Got Mail' Reboot in the Works at Warner Bros.

Kim Hyun-joo, who worked with Yeon on both seasons of “Hellbound” and on “Jung_E,” plays the mother, Ryu So-young. Bae Hyeon-seong (“Hospital Playlist”) takes his first big-screen lead as Sun-woo, the son who returns. Yeon wrote the screenplay with Ryu Yong-jae, drawing on an original idea of theirs that author Oh Seong-eun turned into the novel “Black Inferno.” Wowpoint produced, and CJ ENM, which presents the film with Wowpoint, handles distribution. The film has sold to 118 countries, with Well Go USA handling North America, where it released Nov. 6.

“On one level, AI is something mechanical or technological. But when you look more closely, we can see what is beneath it. I think it is ultimately what we share as human knowledge and also collective intelligence,” Yeon tells Variety.

As he argued around “Colony,” that pooled knowledge is now beginning to threaten our individuality, he says, the very qualities that make each of us human. “The relationship between a mother and her son is perhaps one of the most deeply personal and intimate relationships we can have,” Yeon continues. “We can never truly know what lies in the depth of another person’s psyche.”

The film borrows its title from Milton’s epic poem. “Just like the story where the first of mankind are expelled from paradise, I thought that the loss of paradise is precisely where the human identity begins,” Yeon said in the film’s production notes.

He has used AI as a storytelling device before. In 2023’s “Jung_E,” an AI recreation allowed a daughter to free her mother from an idealized, self-sacrificing image, something he says would not be possible in the real world. “With ‘Paradise Lost,’ I wanted to take a much more grounded approach, using AI as a point of contrast, I would say, to explore something that is uniquely human,” he says.

AI may be built on humanity’s shared patterns, he adds, but it can also behave in very unpredictable ways, beyond our ability to fully control or comprehend. “In that sense, I think AI itself represents an uncharted territory. Something as mysterious and unknowable to us as a black hole.”

Where “The Ugly” followed a son digging into his father’s buried past, “Paradise Lost” swaps in a mother and son.

“I think the family is the very first form of society that we encounter as human beings,” Yeon says. “And because the family is our first experience of society, it plays a fundamental role in shaping much of our sense of self and also our personal identity.”

“The Ugly” was shot for KRW200 million ($150,000). “Paradise Lost” was also made lean, for KRW500 million ($374,000) over just 14 shooting sessions. Yeon leaned on Kim, whom he knew understood his directing style extremely well after several projects together. The schedule meant few takes and limited locations.

“But when you think about it, many of the most legendary films in Asian cinema were made under similar constraints,” Yeon says. “In a way I found that those limitations allowed me, and allowed us, to explore the material more creatively, and they also demanded an extraordinary level of focus and concentration from everyone involved.”

Korea’s theatrical industry is going through a major transformation, Yeon says, and some feel the market has shrunk considerably. “But I don’t necessarily see that as an obstacle to making good films. A smaller industry doesn’t automatically mean fewer great films will be made,” he says. Making “Colony” and “Paradise Lost” in the same year gave him firsthand experience of two radically different scales, which he hopes will help him lead productions of any size with greater confidence.

International sales covered the film’s net production budget ahead of release. Among Korean distributors, CJ ENM is one of the most active in working with overseas partners, Yeon says, and that experience counts. “Whether a Korean film is a box office success or not, whether it is a mainstream commercial production or something with a more niche sensibility, I believe there are audiences somewhere in the world who will appreciate it,” he says.

Making a film available everywhere is not enough on its own, he adds. “Global streaming platforms already release films simultaneously across multiple territories, using algorithms to recommend content based on individual preferences. But I don’t think we have necessarily established just how effective that approach is at helping films find their audiences.”

Yeon has two projects in post-production. The first is a Japanese-language Netflix series, based on the manga “Parasyte: Tamiya,” on which he serves as screenwriter and showrunner. It moves one of the original’s villains, Tamiya Ryoko, to the center of the story. “What I found particularly fascinating is the way Tamiya, who is a parasite rather than a human being, gradually tries to understand humanity,” he says.

The second is “Devotion,” a horror feature due next year. It grew out of the way people now put versions of their identities on display and hide behind the personas they build. Its Korean title, “Yeto,” is a Buddhist term for an imperfect world marked by suffering, the opposite of the realm of ultimate bliss.

For a filmmaker who thinks being human starts with the loss of paradise, it is a fitting next stop.

Maybelline updates ‘Maybe’ tagline as beauty perceptions evolve

📅 Nov 11, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Miley Cyrus is leading a new global brand platform promoting changes meant to be more inclusive and empowering for women.

Maybelline is reworking one of its signature brand assets as it tries to adapt to an evolving landscape for beauty and cosmetics. The L'Oréal-owned marketer’s “Maybe she’s born with it” tagline became a fixture of its advertising beginning in 1991, but consumers in 2026 are applying makeup for different reasons.

The company has attempted to modernize “Maybe” in the past. It was at one point dropped for the largely forgotten “Make It Happen” but eventually brought back. Maybelline revamped the “Maybe” tagline for the TikTok era two years ago. The latest overhaul appears more substantial, changing the underlying meaning of the copy, and is described in the release as permanent.

Over half of surveyed global respondents today see beauty as a way to build confidence compared to 26% who see it as a means of looking more attractive to other people or appearing younger for longer, per McKinsey findings cited by Maybelline. The suggestion is that makeup can be something less superficial and more empowering and inclusive.

Tweaks to the marketer’s tagline try to recognize these trends by adjusting the phrasing to sound less like a question being asked about a single woman’s look. Instead, the new “Maybe” is positioned as a declaration of the possibilities all women can achieve by leveraging makeup as a form of self-expression.

“With 'Maybe.', we're building on our legacy and inviting women everywhere, at every stage of life, to explore the possibilities within them,” said Maybelline Global Brand President Sandrine Jolly in a statement.

Maybelline enlisted Cyrus, an ambassador who’s previously voiced the “Maybe” tagline, to lead its new “The Beauty of Infinite Possibility” platform. Cyrus was selected for the effort because her own career has entailed a constantly shifting identity, moving from child star to hard-partying club song purveyor and then back to her country roots.

Cyrus released her latest album, “Bass Persuades,” in September, which could draw additional interest to the Maybelline partnership. “Bass Persuades” sees Cyrus again dabbling in the dance pop and electronic music genres.

Products featured in the campaign include Maybelline’s Serum Lipstick, Lash Sensational Sky High Mascara and Instant Eraser Concealer. Maybelline will partner with additional women in the worlds of music and elsewhere to share their stories of using makeup as a form of expression and reinvention.

Consumer products, the division of L'Oréal that Maybelline sits under, grew 4.3% on a like-for-like basis in Q3, lagging other segments like dermatological beauty and professional products.

Art Week NYC Kicks off at Metropolitan Museum of Art with New York Pols and Art Leaders

📅 Nov 11, 2026  |  Category: Business  |  Read Source →

Dozens of leaders from the New York art and political worlds rubbed shoulders at the Metropolitan Museum of Art on Tuesday morning at the launch of Art Week NYC, a citywide gathering celebrating New York’s leading galleries, institutions, and cultural spaces, organized by ARTnews, Art in America, and Artforum. The inaugural event takes place from November 11–14, 2026.

The distinguished roster of speakers included Julie Menin, City Council Speaker; City Council Deputy Speaker Nantasha Williams; Manhattan Borough President Brad Hoylman-Sigal; New York State Senator Jose Serrano; and City Council member Virginia Maloney. Related Articles After Galliano Firestorm, Met Plans New Costume Institute Exhibition Unlikely to Offend Anyone The Met Museum and Vacheron Constantin Unveil Artworks Produced Through New Residency

In attendance were VIPs like advisor Augusto Arbizo; MoMA PS1 director Connie Butler; Lisson Gallery international director Alex Logsdail; Arts Council England chairman Nicholas Serota; and Simone Subal, senior director at Paula Cooper Gallery, along with ARTnews/Art in America editor Sarah Douglas and publisher Erica Lubow Necarsulmer as well as Artforum co-editor Rachel Wetzler and publisher Danielle McConnell.

The gathering took place in the light-filled Charles Engelhard Court, anchored by the Neoclassical facade of the Second Branch Bank of the United States (1822–24) and featuring sculptures by American artists such as Daniel Chester French, Hiram Powers, and Augustus Saint-Gaudens.

All were there, said David Breslin, the museum’s curator in charge of the modern and contemporary art department, “to launch what we know will become a defining tradition in our city’s cultural landscape—and that’s saying a lot.”

Kicking off the remarks, City Council Speaker Julie Menin looked to history to set the tone, noting that it was 100 years ago when pioneering New York dealer Edith Halpert launched what would become the Downtown Gallery, which was early to show American modern art and folk art. To signal its spirit of inclusivity, Menin said, it was initially called Our Gallery, adding, “As the very first citywide contemporary art festival, that is precisely the same spirit that Art Week NYC has built here.” The City Council was proud to allocate a record $323 million in its latest budget to the Department of Cultural Affairs, she added.

Manhattan Borough President Brad Hoylman-Sigal recalled bringing together all the city’s local community boards at the museum recently and finding that some had never before visited. “Art Week is a way to invite New Yorkers—just like we invited those community board members to the Met to experience art—to meet New Yorkers, as they say, where they are,” he said, and called New York’s hundreds of galleries, many of them participating in Art Week, “magnificent little crown jewels.”

Citing Public Theater founder Joseph Papp to the effect that art belongs to everyone, Hoylman-Sigal noted, “I’m very proud to have committed 100 percent of our capital dollars to arts and cultural institutions.” That was $50 million in discretionary funds, he noted, adding that the recipient institutions were called upon to leverage the public dollars to raise money from private donors, and the 83 cultural institutions raised an additional $52 million through what he called the “Manhattan Multiplier.”

Wrapping up the event, City Council member Virginia Maloney noted that her namesake grandmother was an artist before it was common for women to have careers. Her brother-in-law is also an artist, she noted, adding, “They both taught me that art changes the way that you view the world. As chair of the Economic Development Committee, I also know that culture and art is not a luxury in New York. It’s an economic engine for tourism, for visitors, for employers, and for businesses that surround the organizations that we’re supporting today. When we invest in the arts, we know that we recoup the benefits tenfold.”

For a full listing of member galleries and institutions and a calendar of events, head to Art Week NYC.

Fantasy Football Updated Rookie Report: Fernando Mendoza gets his shot

📅 Nov 11, 2026  |  Category: Sports  |  Read Source →

Nine weeks into the NFL season, we've seen enough from this rookie class to know who's for real, who's still finding their footing and who could make some noise down the stretch. From Carnell Tate's impressive start in Tennessee to Fernando Mendoza getting his opportunity in Las Vegas, here's my midseason check-in on 15 rookies, and what I'm expecting for the rest of the season.

Carnell Tate has been everything we hoped he would be — and then some. Through seven games, he's hauled in 34 receptions for 413 yards and a touchdown despite playing in one of the more inconsistent passing offenses in football. Cam Ward and the Tennessee Titans haven't exactly made life easy for their rookie receiver, but Tate continues to produce.

The talent is undeniable, the opportunity is there and the chemistry with Ward continues to develop. I expect Tate to build on what's already been a successful rookie campaign. He's a weekly fantasy starter with plenty of room to grow.

Jonah Coleman might be the rookie running back I'm most excited about for the second half of the season. He's rushed for 191 yards and three touchdowns while adding 13 receptions for another 128 yards. More importantly, look at how Denver is using him. He handled 21 touches in Week 9, and the coaching staff clearly trusts him.

With R.J. Harvey on injured reserve and J.K. Dobbins getting older, the opportunity is sitting right in front of Coleman. He's already shown he can contribute in the passing game and handle meaningful carries. I wouldn't be surprised if Coleman finishes as one of the most valuable rookie running backs in fantasy.

Omar Cooper Jr. missed time early in the season, but the moment he got healthy, he made his presence felt. Through four games, Cooper has 20 receptions for 306 yards and two touchdowns, including back-to-back 100-yard performances. In Week 9, he followed those outings with eight receptions for 58 yards while Garrett Wilson was sidelined.

What I've really liked is the rapport he's developing with Geno Smith. Cooper looks comfortable; he's earning opportunities and he's showing he can win in different areas of the field. Wilson's return could impact his volume, but I'm buying what we've seen. Cooper has legitimate weekly flex appeal moving forward.

KC Concepcion Jr. has quietly been one of the most impressive rookie wide receivers in football. He's sitting at 37 receptions for 437 yards and a touchdown, including 251 receiving yards over his last three games. What jumps off the screen is his ability to create after the catch. Get this young man the football in space and let him work.

The Browns haven't exactly provided consistent quarterback play, but Concepcion continues to find ways to produce. He's dynamic, explosive and already showing flashes of a player who could eventually become a Pro Bowl-caliber receiver. For fantasy purposes, he's worked his way into weekly flex consideration.

And how about Denzel Boston? The rookie has 507 receiving yards and three touchdowns on 32 receptions. Boston gives me some young Mike Evans vibes with his size, downfield ability and willingness to go make contested catches. He's already producing explosive plays despite the limitations of this Browns offense.

What's encouraging is that both Boston and Concepcion are earning significant opportunities without cannibalizing each other's production. Boston has legitimate big-play upside every single week and continues to look like one of the steals of the rookie class. I love what we've seen and expect a strong finish.

Germie Bernard's rookie season has been a little up and down, but I've seen enough to remain encouraged. He's caught 32 passes for 284 yards and a touchdown, highlighted by a phenomenal 11-reception performance in Week 6. The following two games weren't nearly as productive, but I wouldn't let that discourage you.

Bernard has established himself as a trusted option for Aaron Rodgers in a Pittsburgh offense that's been better than advertised. His role makes sense. Get him the football quickly, let him operate in space and allow his natural ability to take over. Bernard feels like a buy-low candidate with a solid second-half outlook.

I can't believe I'm putting Fernando Mendoza on this list, but here we are. The No. 1 overall pick is taking over as the Raiders' starting quarterback after Kirk Cousins was traded to Minnesota. Mendoza has attempted just eight regular-season passes, completing six for 48 yards, so we have almost nothing to evaluate.

But here's what we do know: Cousins threw 20 touchdowns in nine games and Mendoza gets to operate in that same Klint Kubiak offense featuring Brock Bowers. Listen, any quarterback throwing to Brock Bowers gets a touchdown bonus in my book. Add Ashton Jeanty, Trey Tucker and a potentially more favorable schedule down the stretch, and there's legitimate streaming appeal here. I'm not expecting Mendoza to be a league winner, but don't be surprised if he becomes fantasy relevant sooner than people think.

Jadarian Price is one of those players where the box score doesn't tell the entire story. He's rushed for 172 yards and added 83 receiving yards on 10 catches without scoring a touchdown. The production hasn't been there consistently, but every time he touches the football, you see the explosiveness. Unfortunately, injuries and ball-security issues have limited his opportunities.

With Zach Charbonnet still working his way back from a knee injury, there's a path for Price to earn a larger role. Seattle's offensive line should also improve as it gets healthier. I'm cautiously optimistic, but I need to see him string together consistent performances before trusting him in fantasy lineups.

Makai Lemon has been better than his rookie numbers might suggest. Through nine games, he's caught 37 passes for 312 yards and one touchdown, including 19 receptions for 185 yards over his last three games. The Eagles offense has been difficult to watch at times, and Lemon has dealt with inconsistent quarterback play while occasionally being asked to operate as the team's top receiver with DeVonta Smith unavailable.

What I've loved is his ability to separate, particularly against zone coverage. He's consistently finding openings and earning targets. The big fantasy performances haven't arrived yet, but the underlying usage is encouraging. There's more here than the box score suggests.

We finally got our first look at Jordyn Tyson, and while it wasn't perfect, there were encouraging signs. In his NFL debut, Tyson caught four passes for 30 yards but also lost a fumble on his first reception. I'm not worried about that. What matters is that New Orleans immediately looked to get him involved.

This Saints offense ranks among the league leaders in neutral-situation pass rate, meaning the opportunity should continue to be there. Getting Calvin Banks back should help Tyler Shough operate more comfortably behind that offensive line. The biggest question remains Tyson's health. If that hamstring holds up, I'm expecting his role to expand significantly.

Mike Washington Jr. gave us one of the more surprising rookie performances of the season when he handled 25 carries for 83 yards. Then he turned around the following week and rushed six times for -3 yards. That's been the problem. Washington has 275 rushing yards and a touchdown this season, but his role remains difficult to project.

Neither Washington nor Ashton Jeanty has consistently found success running behind this Raiders offensive line. Washington clearly has some ability, but can this coaching staff establish a defined role for him? Until that happens, he's difficult to trust. He's an interesting stash with upside if Jeanty misses time.

There's no bigger fantasy disappointment from this rookie class than Jeremiyah Love. And before anybody gets upset, I'm not putting all of that on the player. Love has rushed for 268 yards, added just 36 receiving yards and scored two total touchdowns in six games. We've seen flashes of the talent that made him such a highly regarded prospect, but the situation in Arizona has been a disaster.

The offense has struggled, the quarterback situation has deteriorated and now Love is sitting on injured reserve. If you invested a second-, third- or fourth-round fantasy pick in him, there's no way around it — it's been disappointing. The talent hasn't disappeared, but the fantasy return simply hasn't been there.

Antonio Williams is one of those rookies who could become much more relevant over the second half of the season. He's caught 26 passes for 284 yards and a touchdown, including eight receptions for 79 yards over his last two games. The problem hasn't necessarily been Williams. It's been the Commanders' offense.

Jayden Daniels continues to deal with a significant elbow injury and Washington hasn't been particularly creative offensively. We've also seen Williams have some unfortunate plays wiped away. With Terry McLaurin out, there should be additional opportunities available. He's not a weekly fantasy starter yet, but there's a path for Williams to become a useful flex option.

Emmett Johnson has quietly put together 348 yards from scrimmage and two total touchdowns, including that ridiculous 128-yard receiving performance against Denver in Week 8. We saw a 73-yard touchdown reception and a player who can absolutely make things happen with the football in his hands.

The problem? Kenneth Walker III has been phenomenal since arriving in Kansas City.

Johnson isn't taking that starting job anytime soon. But what if the Chiefs can turn him into a Jerick McKinnon-type receiving weapon? There's potential for standalone fantasy value in that role. And if Walker were to miss time, Johnson immediately becomes one of the most valuable backups in football.

Kaelon Black has 303 rushing yards and a touchdown this season, and I don't think enough people are paying attention to what he's doing. That's more rushing production than Jeremiyah Love, Mike Washington Jr. and Jadarian Price. The biggest obstacle is obvious: Christian McCaffrey.

But we've seen San Francisco utilize multiple backs at times, and keeping McCaffrey fresh has to remain a priority. Black has demonstrated that he can be an effective runner when given opportunities. The receiving game hasn't been a major part of his role, so there are limitations to his standalone fantasy value. Still, this is a player I want rostered heading into the second half of the season.

Telegram is gaining crypto users without having to convince them to download another app. More than 1 million self-custodial wallets appeared on the messaging platform within 48 hours. Founder Pavel Durov says the growth reflects a simple feature: wallets activate automatically when users receive Grams. The milestone shows Telegram’s biggest advantage in crypto: distribution.

Unlike traditional crypto wallets, Telegram’s native wallet operates inside an application millions already use daily. Crypto traders, project founders, airdrop hunters, and blockchain communities have long relied on Telegram. That existing audience gives its wallet a distribution advantage that standalone crypto applications must spend years building.

The latest growth also raises an interesting question about blockchain adoption. Does crypto really need another wave of new users, or does it need easier access for people already participating? Telegram could provide exactly that bridge, provided wallet registrations translate into meaningful economic activity.

Durov reported more than 1million new self-custodial wallets over 48 hours in an X post. The dashboard accompanying his announcement showed more than 1 million registrations across the period.

1M+ self-custodial Gram wallets activated on Telegram in just 48 hours. $GRAM is up 10% in 24h, the #1 gainer in the CMC top 100. pic.twitter.com/VGVTT5AZQk

According to Durov, users do not need to manually create a wallet before receiving crypto. Their wallets activate when someone sends them Grams.

This creates a potentially powerful viral distribution mechanism. One user sends tokens to another, introducing the recipient to an on-chain wallet. The recipient can then hold or transfer assets without leaving Telegram.

This approach removes several familiar barriers to crypto adoption. New users do not necessarily need to understand seed phrases, download separate applications, or navigate an exchange. Telegram already provides the social environment where these transactions can begin.

The platform also has more than one billion monthly users, giving its crypto ambitions an enormous potential audience. Even a small conversion rate could produce substantial wallet growth.

The latest wallet milestone is not the only sign of expanding activity across The Open Network (TON), the blockchain powering Telegram’s wallet. TON averaged 4.06 million daily transactions during October. The network also added approximately 2.73 million wallets throughout the month, its strongest monthly addition since October 2024.

Stablecoin activity offers another indication of growing utility. TON averaged 37,693 daily stablecoin users in October, representing a 23.44% increase from September.

The stablecoin figures are particularly interesting because they point toward activity beyond token speculation. Payments and transfers could give Telegram crypto a more practical role in everyday digital interactions.

TON’s stablecoin supply stood at $585 million today. Its decentralized finance total value locked, however, remained comparatively modest at approximately $44 million.

Payments, stablecoin transfers, digital collectibles, and Telegram’s mini-app ecosystem could provide reasons to return. These services could gradually make blockchain transactions feel like another ordinary messaging feature.

Duke head coach Jon Scheyer did not reach for an excuse after his No. 2 Blue Devils lost to the No. 5 Michigan State Spartans by a final score of 79-73 in the Champions Classic on Tuesday night at the United Center in Chicago. He handed the credit to the Spartans and put the blame on his own team.

Michigan State jumped out to an 11-2 lead at the 17:30 mark of the first half, with guard Jeremy Fears Jr. assisting on three straight baskets, and the Spartans never looked back. It was the first loss of the season for Duke, which fell to 2-1 while Michigan State moved to 3-0.

Nov 10, 2026; Chicago, Illinois, USA; Duke Blue Devils head coach Jon Scheyer gestures to his team against the Michigan State Spartans during the first half at United Center. Mandatory Credit: David Banks-Imagn Images Credit: IMAGN IMAGES via Reuters Connect (Reuters)

Scheyer opened his postgame press conference with the Spartans. "Congrats to Michigan State. They 100% deserved to win. I thought they outcompeted us. They played more together," he said, per Spartans Wire. He added, "Credit all the world to Coach Izzo and Michigan State." Tom Izzo had an admission of his own after the win.

On Fears, Scheyer said, "I think you've got to tip your cap to him. He controlled the whole game." He went on: "He plays with amazing control over the game, and it's hard to stop a guy if you give him 17 free throws."

Scheyer was harder on his own group. "That wasn't Duke basketball. We did not play together," he said. "Bottom line, we weren't connected, and we're gonna fix it, I can promise you that much," he told reporters, per CBS Sports. He also said Duke's fans "deserve better."

It was very hard for the Blue Devils to contain Fears all game long. He scored 18 points, grabbed seven rebounds and recorded 11 assists in 33 minutes. He dictated the game from start to finish for this Spartans basketball team.

He did it without needing his jumper, going 3-of-9 from the field and getting to the free-throw line 17 times, where he made 12. Fears was also a factor on the defensive end, blocking one shot and recording one steal.

They are a deep bunch, and the talk coming in was about how deep Duke was.

From the field, Michigan State was 25-of-56 (45%) and 7-of-20 from three-point land (35%). As for Duke, the Blue Devils shot 20-of-60 (33%) from the field and only 7-of-29 from three (24%). Duke being thrown off in this game was about more than just Fears. The Blue Devils offense was out of rhythm because of how well the Spartans defense played.
