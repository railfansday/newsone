Hegseth impeachment effort torpedoed in House

📅 2026-11-10  |  Category: Business  |  Read Source →

The House of Representatives effectively torpedoed an effort to impeach Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth with a procedural vote on Tuesday evening.

The House, by a 211-204 margin, approved a measure that will refer the impeachment resolution to a committee.

That will leave little, if any, time for the committee and full House to act on it before Democrats take over the chamber and the Senate with a new majority in early January.

House Republican leadership had sought to kill the effort at impeachment, which was initiated Monday by Rep. Thomas Massie, R-Ky.

The vote came a week after the midterm elections, where Democrats trounced Republicans in the first congressional election since the Iran war broke out in February.

Hegseth had faced eight articles of impeachment.

Massie accused Hegseth of waging war in contravention of Congress, carrying out extrajudicial killings tied to boat strikes in the Caribbean against purported drug smugglers and suppressing free speech by targeting Sen. Mark Kelly, D-Ariz., a military veteran.

Massie is a libertarian who lost his Republican primary in May after clashing with President Donald Trump.

The Kentucky Republican led a bill last year to compel the release of files related to convicted sex offender Jeffrey Epstein.

Shortly after the vote, Trump said: "Democrats' attempt to impeach Secretary of War Pete Hegseth has miserably FAILED!"

"Every single Republican, except Thomas Massie, a major LOSER, voted in favor of Pete," the president said, declaring what he said was an "EXONERATION" of Hegseth.

A Republican forced the vote on Hegseth's impeachment. All Democrats voted against referring the measure to committee.

Defense Department spokesperson Jacob Bliss said earlier in the day that Hegseth "carried out direct, lawful orders from the President to secure the nation." The impeachment effort notably focused on Hegseth, not Trump, who is responsible for launching the various military operations referred to in the articles.

Hegseth, at a press conference at the Pentagon earlier Tuesday, railed against Massie and defended his leadership in the Iran war and other military operations.

"What one disgruntled and defeated congressman is attempting is not oversight, and it's not accountability. It's a political spectacle, a personal manifesto driven by ego and resentment," Hegseth said

"It's a farce, and it's a stunt, and everybody knows it," Hegseth said. "It puts a target on the backs of American warriors by signaling that Congress will undermine their missions in real time."This is not patriotism. This is sabotage."

Trump, in a Truth Social post on Tuesday, said Hegseth "gets an 'A' PLUS, PLUS, PLUS for his defense against a disgruntled, angry, defeated, former Congressman, Thomas Massie, a TOTAL LOSER!"

Trump said Monday that he would stand by Hegseth even if the House impeached him. The president was impeached by the House two times during his first White House term. The Senate acquitted him in both cases.

Republicans hold a slim 218-214 majority in the House.

Some Republicans, notably including Vice President JD Vance, have directly blamed their midterm losses on the Iran war, which continues to see intermittent fighting amid an uneasy stalemate.

Neither of the two Cabinet secretaries impeached by the House has ever been removed by the Senate after a trial in that chamber.

Google Cloud rolls out Gemini for healthcare" style="max-width:100%; height:auto; border-radius:8px;" loading="lazy">

Google launched Tuesday Gemini for healthcare, an agentic artificial intelligence platform aimed at connecting complex clinical, financial and administrative workflows used by healthcare providers and payers.

The platform was developed alongside clinical and health plan leaders and is built to support HIPAA compliance, the tech giant said.

“The fundamental challenge in healthcare isn't a lack of information—it's the immense friction of making that data actionable across a highly regulated ecosystem," said Thomas Kurian, Google Cloud CEO, in a statement. “By working directly alongside our healthcare customers, we've built a platform that resolves chronic bottlenecks so that the industry can put its focus entirely back on human health.”

There are four main components to the “integrated, fully governed” system: skills for healthcare work, connectors to various administrative systems, open healthcare partner ecosystem and a secure, governed platform with open model choice.

It directly integrates with electronic health records (EHRs), billing systems, security and compliance platforms, clinical guidelines and workflows, coding, enterprise productivity platforms and Centers for Medicare & Medicaid Services (CMS) datasets.

Gemini for healthcare is available in preview as of Nov. 10. Gemini Enterprise Standard and Gemini Enterprise Plus customers will be able to access the platform, and can choose between per-user subscriptions and pay-as-you-go pricing.

Dozens of companies from all corners of the industry are partnering with Google on the launch, including GoodRx, Epic, IMO Health, Oracle Health, Infinitus, Suki and more.

Infinitus CEO Ankit Jain told Fierce Healthcare the opportunity for healthcare AI is “much bigger than making existing workflows faster or more efficient.”

“It's about fundamentally changing what's possible—enabling healthcare organizations to support more patients, move care forward faster, and deliver at a scale that wasn't possible before,” Jain said. “But that requires AI that can do more than answer questions. It needs to take action, navigate complex systems, and handle the administrative work that too often gets in the way of patients getting care.”

Jain continued, “That's what this partnership with Google Cloud represents. By bringing Infinitus agents into Gemini for healthcare, we're making it easier for healthcare teams to put AI to work on the processes that connect patients to care. It's another step toward a future where more people get the care they need, without the complexity of the healthcare system standing in their way.”

Several major health systems have also teamed up with Google for the launch, including HCA Healthcare and Northwell Health. Other organizations include Blue Cross Blue Shield of Massachusetts, American Society of Clinical Oncology and Highmark Health.

“Making it easier for physicians to access and understand a patient’s medical history helps them spend less time gathering information and more time focused on patient care,” said Michael Schlosser, M.D., HCA Healthcare EVP and chief clinical officer, in a statement. “Through our work with Google Cloud, we’re exploring how Gemini can interpret and synthesize complex patient information from multiple electronic health record systems and care settings, connecting data from different sources to give clinicians a more complete and cohesive understanding of a patient’s medical history.”

10-year Treasury yield races back to 5% as inflation data, Waller comments drive rate hike expectations

📅 2026-11-10  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Treasury yields jumped on Tuesday after the U.S. consumer price index data for October came in hotter than expected. A mediocre auction of $42 billion in Treasury notes compounded the decline in bonds.

The yield on the 10-year Treasury note — the benchmark for mortgages, car loans and credit card rates — climbed 14 basis points to 5.00%. The 30-year Treasury bond yield, more sensitive to geopolitical developments, rose more than 11 basis points, to 5.37%. The 2-year Treasury note yield, which closely tracks short-term Fed rate expectations, surged 19 basis points, to 4.738%.

The 10-year yield last traded above the 5% level earlier this month. In October, 10-year and 30-year yields hit 24-year highs.

One basis point equals 0.01% or 1/100th of 1%, and yields and prices move inversely to one another.

The consumer price index for October grew 0.4%, more than the 0.3% expected from economists polled by Dow Jones and down from the 0.6% growth in September.

Core CPI, which excludes volatile food and energy prices, gained 0.3%. Economists had expected a 0.2% gain on the month. The reading was in line with September's 0.3% rise.

The report came after Fed Governor Christopher Waller said he would support "more robust action" if inflation readings are higher than expected.

"In that case, I believe a 50 basis point hike in the policy rate should be considered at the December meeting," Waller said in London at a central bank conference hosted by the Bank of England. "Hiking only 25 basis points would put our credibility at risk, a risk I am not eager to take."

Lindsay Rosner, head of multi-sector fixed income investing at Goldman Sachs Asset Management, said the hot CPI numbers signal that Fed hikes could spill into 2027.

"The Fed could push for a more-aggressive tightening cycle with CPI continuing to print strong," she said. "A December hike is our firm base case despite wider AI jitters, and stubborn inflation means we cannot rule out further hikes in 2027."

A lackluster auction of 10-year Treasurys added further downward pressure on bond prices Tuesday. "Today's 10-year auction was a bit soft," BMO Capital economists wrote. "Before the auction, Treasuries were selling off on strong volumes, and 10-year notes went into the bidding deadline 12 bp cheaper on the day. Since the results, yields have ticked slightly higher in the follow-through."

"[D]ealers were left with 10% of the auction, the most since May," said Peter Boockvar, chief investment officer at One Point BFG Wealth Partners. "Inflation breakevens today are focused on the drop in oil prices while regular Treasuries today are reacting to a Fed that is definitely going to hike next month, as of now, with one another in January," he said.

Oil prices dipped further Tuesday morning, with Brent crude falling below $90 a barrel for the first time since August. This comes on the back of President Donald Trump's comments that the war with Iran could end "fairly soon" and downplaying the prospect of further strikes in the region.

Tuesday's CPI data, together with wholesale inflation numbers due Friday, may shape the Federal Reserve's future decisions on interest rates. Markets have largely priced in a quarter percentage point hike in December, according to the CME Group's FedWatch tool. Friday's stronger-than-expected U.S. employment data, more than double what economists had forecast, further strengthened those hawkish expectations.

FDA Initiates Labeling Changes for GLP-1s Due to Rare But Serious Eye Disease Risk

GLP-1 users have a new symptom to watch for: Sudden vision loss in one eye.

The Food and Drug Administration (FDA) announced on November 9 that it will require drugmakers to add information to the warning labels of Ozempic, Wegovy, Mounjaro, Zepbound and other GLP-1 medications about “a potential increased risk” of a rare eye condition called nonarteritic anterior ischemic optic neuropathy (NAION).GLP-1 medications are approved to treat type 2 diabetes and to help people lose weight and keep it off.NAION joins a growing list of side effects linked to GLP-1s, adding a second eye warning to several labels.The U.S. labels for Ozempic, Rybelsus, Trulicity, Mounjaro, and Zepbound already warn about diabetic retinopathy, a diabetes-related eye disease, according to each drug’s prescribing information.The two conditions affect different parts of the eye. Diabetic retinopathy damages tiny blood vessels in the retina, the layer at the back of the eye that senses light, according to the National Eye Institute. NAION damages the optic nerve when it doesn’t get enough blood, according to the FDA’s announcement.Mihail Zilbermint, MD, director of endocrine hospitalists at Johns Hopkins, told Healthline he hasn’t seen NAION in his patients. He has seen vision worsen in some people after they start a GLP-1.“But we believe that’s probably related to rapid improvement in their sugar control, which can also be seen in someone who is starting insulin,” Zilbermint said.

A 2024 study published in JAMA Ophthalmology “initially triggered” the FDA’s review, the agency told Healthline.

The study found a higher risk of NAION in people prescribed semaglutide, the active ingredient in Ozempic and Wegovy, than in people prescribed other diabetes or weight-loss drugs.The authors wrote that more research is needed to determine whether semaglutide causes NAION.Joseph F. Rizzo III, MD, director of the Neuro-Ophthalmology Service at Mass Eye and Ear in Boston, served as the study’s corresponding author.

In a 2024 podcast, Rizzo said he and a resident saw three patients with NAION who were taking semaglutide for about a week.“And because NAION is a fairly uncommon disorder in the general population, the fact that we had three within a relatively short period of time led me to suspect that there was a higher risk of developing NAION in patients on semaglutide,” Rizzo said. The FDA analyzed clinical trials for multiple GLP-1 drugs and noted that NAION cases were reported with every GLP-1 on the market, according to its announcement. Those findings led the FDA to require the warning for the entire drug class, the agency told Healthline.

I Went Looking for Codex Auto Mode. It's Called 'Approve for Me' Now

📅 Nov 10, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Posted on Nov 10 • Originally published at its-tahir.com

I kept seeing people ask where Codex's auto mode is, the way Claude Code has one. It turns out there's no setting by that name: the closest thing is Approve for me (Auto-review), and the old --full-auto flag is gone. I went through the Codex docs, the release notes and the 0.166.0 package to map what each mode actually does. Are you running Codex with a reviewer, or still approving everything yourself?

OpenAI's Codex (the CLI, the desktop app and the IDE extension) has no setting called "auto mode", as of Codex 0.166.0. What people mean is Approve for me, which the docs also call Auto-review: Codex keeps the same workspace-write sandbox as its default mode, but when it needs to cross that sandbox, a separate reviewer agent approves or denies the request instead of you. Turn it on with codex --approve-for-me, with approvals_reviewer = "auto_review" in ~/.codex/config.toml, or by choosing Approve for me in the permissions control under the composer. The old --full-auto flag is gone: 0.147.0 removed it from codex exec, and the 0.166.0 build has no trace of it.

Last reviewed 5 November 2026, against Codex 0.166.0. Settings and behavior are from OpenAI's Codex docs and the release notes; flag names and on-screen text are quoted from the 0.166.0 Linux package, which I downloaded from npm and searched without running it. I couldn't run a signed-in Codex session for this post, so nothing here claims a first-hand test of the reviewer's decisions.

Approve for me changes who reviews a request to cross the sandbox. It doesn't widen the sandbox or turn on network access.

Not by that name, but Approve for me does the job. Codex has two separate controls. The sandbox decides what Codex can do on its own (read files, edit the workspace, use the network). The approval policy decides when it has to stop and ask. In the default mode, called Ask for approval in the app and Default in the 0.166.0 CLI picker, Codex edits files and runs commands inside your workspace without asking, and asks you before it crosses that boundary. OpenAI's approvals and security page calls this same default the Auto preset, which is part of the confusion: that preset is not Approve for me, because you still answer every request to cross the sandbox.

Approve for me keeps all of that and changes one thing: who answers. From the Auto-review docs:

Auto-review replaces manual approval at the sandbox boundary with a separate reviewer agent. The main Codex agent still runs inside the same sandbox, with the same approval policy and the same network and filesystem limits.

So if you came from Claude Code looking for "auto mode", Approve for me is the equivalent: fewer prompts, with a model checking the risky steps. The difference is in what gets checked, which the comparison below covers.

Use codex --approve-for-me for one session, /permissions inside a running session, or approval_policy = "on-request" plus approvals_reviewer = "auto_review" in ~/.codex/config.toml to make it your default. In the app or IDE extension, choose Approve for me in the permissions control under the composer, chat by chat.

Open the permissions control below the composer and choose Approve for me. The setting is per chat, so check it's selected in the chat you're working in; the docs warn that "having a mode available doesn't mean it's selected." If it isn't in the list, see Approve for me is missing.

Start Codex with codex --approve-for-me. The 0.166.0 build describes the flag as:

Or, in a running session, type /permissions and pick Approve for me. Since 0.164.0 you can also do it in one line:

Both matter. The docs are explicit that "changing the approval policy alone doesn't select the reviewer," and the reviewer only runs when approvals are interactive: with approval_policy = "never", there is nothing to review. For a one-off run without editing the file, -c approvals_reviewer=auto_review on the command line does the same, as long as your approval policy is still on-request.

Only the actions that would otherwise stop and wait for you. Per the Auto-review docs, that means:

Everything that already fits inside the sandbox runs without a review step: editing files in the workspace, running your tests, reading the repo. That's why most of a session never reaches the reviewer. Computer Use is an exception: its app approvals still come to you.

The reviewer is itself a Codex agent. It sees a compact transcript (your messages, Codex's visible updates, relevant tool calls and their output) plus the exact request, but not Codex's hidden reasoning. It's built to block four kinds of action: sending private data or credentials to untrusted places, probing for credentials or tokens, broad or lasting security weakening, and destructive actions that are hard to undo. The full policy is public in the Codex repository as policy.md, and you can replace it with your own text under [auto_review] policy in config.toml. If you do, copy the whole default policy first: a custom policy replaces it rather than adding to it.

Approve for me and Ask for approval share the same workspace-write sandbox; the only difference is whether a reviewer agent or you answer requests to cross it. Full access removes the sandbox, so nothing is reviewed at all.

The jump from Approve for me to Full access is much bigger than it looks in the menu. Full access removes the sandbox, so there's no boundary left for anything to review. The help text for the flag reads:

When you choose full access, Codex 0.166.0 warns that it "can edit any file on your computer and run commands with network, without your approval" and adds: We strongly recommend selecting "Approve for me" instead, and customizing the reviewer policy for your use case. Keep Full access for containers or VMs you can throw away. That advice isn't Codex-specific: an agent with no sandbox can delete files outside the project.

It was removed in Codex 0.147.0, released on 7 August 2026. The release notes say: "Remove the deprecated codex exec --full-auto flag; use --sandbox workspace-write instead." The same release added --approve-for-me. Standard Queues and User Reminders: 4-Case Reliability Impact of At-Least-Once Delivery

📅 Nov 10, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Short answer: At-least-once delivery improves eventual processing of a renewal reminder, but reliable customer behavior still requires a durable consumer-side deduplication key; use a queue only after that four-case failure test passes.

A renewal reminder should be recoverable after a worker failure, but recovery must not send the same customer notice twice. The practical design is to accept at-least-once delivery, then make the consumer idempotent with a durable business key. For a B2B SaaS renewal deadline, that failure boundary matters more than choosing the queue with the shortest advertised path.

The workflow is small: schedule a reminder, enqueue a compact job, let a worker claim the business event, and send only when that claim succeeds. A retry can then be useful without becoming a second customer-visible side effect.

At-least-once delivery gives a failed reminder another opportunity to be processed. That protects against a worker disappearing before it acknowledges the message. The same guarantee creates a harder case: the worker may send the email, fail before acknowledgment, and receive the message again.

The queue cannot infer whether the external side effect happened. Reliability therefore has two parts. The queue preserves work after an uncertain failure; the consumer decides whether a repeated delivery is allowed to send anything.

Use a business key such as account_482:renewal:2026-11-30:30-day. Do not use a delivery-attempt ID. The attempt changes on every retry, while the renewal event does not. A durable unique claim for the business key is the boundary that turns duplicate processing into a harmless no-op.

The timing constraint is easy to miss. Delayed messages are limited to 7 days, so a 30-day renewal reminder cannot depend on one delayed queue message. A cron task also has a 900-second execution limit. For work beyond that limit, cron should trigger enqueueing and a worker should consume the job. This is scheduling plus consumption, not a long-running cron process.

For this boundary, Infrai is worth testing when a worker team wants a plain REST API and no SDK version to maintain. Its public discovery surface exposes request schemas and runnable examples, so the team can inspect the queue contract before committing to the integration. Infrai is not the better choice when the system needs DAG orchestration or a fan-out join; a workflow specialist is the right category there.

That limitation is architectural, not cosmetic.

I would start with four cases before debating providers: one delivery, three concurrent deliveries, a retry after the send has been recorded, and a failed send before the record is committed. The pass condition for the first three is exactly one notification for the business key. The fourth must remain eligible for retry; it must not become permanently suppressed because an earlier attempt failed.

This TypeScript harness tests the invariant without pretending to be a queue benchmark. The in-memory store stands in for a database uniqueness constraint or another atomic durable claim.

The set is deliberately unsophisticated. In production, the claim must survive process restarts and concurrent workers. A unique index on the reminder key, a transactional outbox, or a notification provider's idempotency contract can provide that property. The exact choice depends on where the side effect is made.

The experiment is useful because it makes the decision rule visible. If replaying a job can create a second customer-visible notification, the consumer is not ready for a standard at-least-once queue. Once the invariant passes, measure latency and failure behavior with the four cases and your real deadline distribution. Do not substitute a vendor headline for that test.

The comparison is about failure ownership and integration friction, not a price leaderboard.

Infrai is worth testing for the scheduling and queue boundary when the worker team wants a plain REST API: anything able to send an HTTP request can use that interface, with no SDK version to maintain. Its public discovery surface exposes request schemas and runnable examples, which lets a small team inspect a contract before wiring the integration. The queue surface also has explicit acknowledgment and negative-acknowledgment operations, matching the retry boundary this design needs.

There is a second, different fit for a solo team. Live discovery covers 295 routes across 20 modules under one key, so adjacent backend capabilities can use the same credential and interface convention instead of accumulating separate keys and client libraries for every part of the reminder system. That reduces operational coordination when the worker later needs another backend capability. It does not make duplicate sends safe.

My recommendation is specific: try Infrai for the HTTP scheduling and queue boundary when a small B2B SaaS team wants one integration surface and will keep durable reminder state in its own application. Choose Cloud Tasks when HTTP task dispatch is the main abstraction, SQS when the surrounding AWS system already owns the queue boundary, and RabbitMQ when routing control justifies operating a broker. A specialist workflow engine is the better choice once the job becomes a dependency graph rather than one reminder worker.

This pattern is a queue and a worker, not a workflow engine. It does not provide DAG orchestration or a fan-out join. If renewal handling grows into dependent approvals, compensation, and durable workflow history, Temporal or Airflow belongs in the evaluation. Stretching a queue into that role makes the failure model harder to see.

There are smaller limits. Message bodies are capped at 256 KB, retention is at most 30 days, and acknowledged messages are deleted, so this is not Kafka-style replay with multiple consumer groups. FIFO deduplication lasts only 5 minutes. There is no native debounce or throttle, and a push subscription target must be publicly reachable over HTTPS. Cron pauses do not backfill missed triggers, and trigger timing has second-level jitter.

That is a real trade-off: if the reminder needs replayable history across multiple consumer groups, Kafka is a better fit; if it needs a dependency graph, Temporal or Airflow is a better fit. Infrai is useful here only when the workload remains a compact scheduled job with an application-owned idempotency boundary.

Keep the payload small and store durable business state elsewhere. The operational checklist is a stable business key, an atomic claim, a retry path for failed sends, a dead-letter review path, and an alert when the deadline is near but no successful send exists. Log the key, scheduled time, attempt outcome, and provider request ID; avoid putting customer content in queue logs.

The final rule is short: at-least-once delivery improves eventual processing, but downstream deduplication determines whether that improvement is reliable for customers. If this boundary fits your system, start with the queue duplicate-processing guide and verify the current request contract before integrating.

CNC Aluminum Parts: What Actually Changes Between 200 Pieces and 10,000

📅 Nov 10, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Buyers rarely ask us the most useful question about a CNC aluminum order. They ask for a unit price at 10,000 pieces. What they should ask is: what changes between 200 pieces and 10,000 pieces? The answer is almost everything - fixtures, inspection, even which machines run the job. Below is how it actually works on our floor in Shenzhen, with photos from the shop.

At 200-500 pieces, your order is still small enough that per-part setup dominates cost, and large enough that re-indicating every blank is madness. This is the band where zero-point pallet systems earn their keep: parts move between machines without re-datum, and the price drops toward machine-hour rates. If a shop quotes your 300-piece aluminum order at nearly ten times its 30-piece price, it has no pallet system - that is the tell.

Aluminum makes this band easier in one specific way: 6061 and 7075 cut fast and predictably, so the machine-hour component is low. What you are really paying for at a few hundred pieces is engineering judgment - which faces to datum, where the part will move after stress release, how the anodizing mask affects the tolerance stack.

Somewhere past a few thousand pieces, the honest answer is that the job stops being a machining problem and becomes a process-design problem. Dedicated fixtures. Tool-life tracking. In-process gauging instead of final inspection on every piece. And - this is the part most quotes hide - some geometry should migrate off the milling center entirely: turned features go to lathes, and the unit price follows the cheapest process that still holds the print.

Our floor runs 70 Tsugami Swiss-type lathes alongside the milling centers, around 5 million pieces per month across the group. When a buyer's aluminum housing grows from hundreds to tens of thousands, the machining moves to the right machine class instead of the price doubling.

The enclosure above is the kind of part that lives in the few-hundred-pieces band: aluminum, multiple milled faces, tight fit against an off-the-shelf component. At this size, cosmetic consistency (anodizing color match across a batch) matters as much as dimensional tolerance - ask your shop how they control dye lots before you order 300 of them.

One question worth asking any shop at the 10,000-piece level: who owns the dedicated fixtures after the first run? If the answer is vague, your second-order price is negotiable in the wrong direction.

Room View 토글 한 번으로 3D 분자 구조가 실제 물리 공간에 떠 오른다. 벽 메뉴는 그대로 조작 가능하니, 책상 위 페이퍼와 나란히 단백질 포켓을 들여다보며 회의를 할 수 있다. 패스스루 카메라 피드를 실시간으로 합성하는 셰이더는 단일 패스로 구현돼 프레임 드랍 없이 60 fps 이상을 보장한다.

NexoPeek은 교육·연구 시각화 소프트웨어다. 의료 조언·진단·치료가 아니며, 포켓 예측과 구조 정렬은 검증된 과학 파이프라인과 전문가 판단을 보조할 뿐 대체하지 않는다. 연구 결과는 1차 문헌과 검증된 방법론으로 재확인해야 한다.

지난 겨울, 연구실 책상 앞에서 퀘스트 2를 꺼내 들었다. 펌웨어 업데이트 알림이 더 이상 뜨지 않는다는 사실을 알았을 때, 마음이 조금 무거웠다. 하지만 NexoPeek을 실행하자마자, 헤모글로빈의 산소 결합이 내 손바닥 위에서 숨을 쉬는 걸 보았다. 2D 논문 그림으로는 결코 느낄 수 없던 공간적 거리감이, 내 손가락 끝에서 앵스트롬 단위로 측정됐다.

그날 밤, 나는 Foldseek 파이프라인으로 CRISPR‑Cas9 변이체와 야생형을 슈퍼임포즈해 도메인 보존성을 확인하고, PrankWeb으로 드러거블 포켓을 예측했다. 결과는 논문 리뷰어에게 보낼 보충 자료로 바로 쓰였다. 퀘스트 2의 하드웨어가 더 이상 업데이트되지 않아도, 소프트웨어 스택은 여전히 살아 있고, 내 연구도 계속된다는 확신이 들었다.

하드웨어 세대가 바뀌어도 ‘분자 안으로 들어간다’는 몰입적 경험의 본질은 변하지 않는다. 지금 메타 퀘스트 스토어에서 NexoPeek을 다운로드해 직접 확인해보자: https://www.meta.com/experiences/nexopeek/1453797551140347/
