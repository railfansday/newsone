Three outside contenders who could replace Michael Carrick as Man United's next manager

📅 Nov 10, 2026  |  Category: Sports  |  Read Source →

By Ben Sully | 10 Nov 2026 23:44, Last updated: 10 Nov 2026 23:48

Manchester United are said to be on the brink of parting ways with head coach Michael Carrick, with the team languishing in the Premier League relegation zone after winning just one of their 10 matches.

While a final decision is yet to be confirmed, rumours are already swirling about who the club could pick as Carrick's replacement should they decide to change head coach over the international break.

USA boss Mauricio Pochettino is reportedly towards the top of the club's managerial shortlist, with his current contract believed to contain a release clause.

Meanwhile, the Red Devils have also been linked with former Newcastle United boss Eddie Howe, ex-Germany head coach Julian Nagelsmann and the vastly experienced Antonio Conte, who previously managed Chelsea and Tottenham Hotspur in the Premier League.

While the aforementioned quartet are believed to be among the main contenders, there may be alternative candidates who are at least on the radar of the Man United hierarchy.

With that in mind, Sports Mole highlights three outside picks the Red Devils could consider if they decide to replace Carrick.

According to the latest odds, Kieran McKenna is among the potential contenders for the Man United job, although he is seen as an outsider compared to the likes of Pochettino, Howe, Nagelsmann and Conte.

There is a temptation to say that McKenna ‘knows the club’, but instead of using that tired old cliche, it is perhaps better to say he is someone who will be well-known by many figures at the club, and with that, there is less uncertainty about the way he works as a coach.

McKenna spent a couple of years working as the Under-18 coach at Man United before he was promoted to the role of assistant manager, where he worked with Ole Gunnar Solskjaer, Jose Mourinho, Ralf Rangnick and Carrick.

The 40-year-old ultimately decided to forge his own path as a manager when he took the top job at Ipswich Town in December 2021, going on to lead the club to back-to-back promotions before suffering Premier League relegation in the 2024-25 campaign.

Ipswich stuck with McKenna and were rewarded with another promotion-winning season, but he ultimately decided against overseeing the club’s latest Premier League campaign, deciding to take a break from football management.

Considering it has been five months since he made that decision, it is unclear whether McKenna is now ready to jump back into football management, although the chance to manage a club of Man United’s stature would surely be a convincing factor.

There may be a tendency to think he would be a similar appointment to Solskjaer and Carrick due to his previous connections with the club, but he has already built a stronger CV than the aforementioned pair, and his willingness to use width as a means of attack would suit a philosophy that has previously been associated with Man United.

With Fabian Hurzeler and Marco Rose currently impressing at Brighton & Hove Albion and Bournemouth, Man United may want to look to Bundesliga’s exciting coaching talent for their next head coach.

Stuttgart boss Sebastian Hoeness is seen as one of the league’s best coaches, even though his side has struggled for consistency this season.

Hoeness got his first taste as a Bundesliga head coach at Hoffenheim, where he oversaw 11th and ninth-place finishes before taking a year out of management.

In April  2023, the 44-year-old got the call from Stuttgart, who were languishing at the bottom of the table at the time of his appointment.

Hoeness lifted the club out of the automatic relegation spots before he guided them to victory in the relegation/promotion playoff against Hamburg.

Stuttgart’s transformation under Hoeness was there for all to see when the club achieved a runners-up finish in 2023-24, their best league finish in 17 years.

While there was a drop-off in the next Bundesliga campaign, any frustration from a ninth-place finish was made to feel insignificant by Stuttgart’s triumphant DFB-Pokal, which ended the club’s 28-year wait for glory in the domestic cup.

Champions League qualification followed in the 2025-26 campaign, an achievement that means Hoeness is yet to experience an unsuccessful season at Stuttgart, the sort of consistency that Man United could certainly benefit from.

Hoeness has shown a willingness throughout his coaching career to select a formation that best suits the player profiles in his squad, while still having a clear philosophy to certain aspects of the game, including the willingness for his teams to push full-backs and wing-backs high in a bid to get in good crossing positions.

Out of the three options, Marcelino is the biggest curveball on this list, although he has much more senior management experience than McKenna and Hoeness.

While he may be relatively unknown to an English audience, he has a strong reputation in Spain as a wily operator who tends to command the respect of his players.

Marcelino has managed some of Spain’s biggest clubs, including Sevilla, Valencia and Athletic Club.

The 61-year-old guided Valencia to their first Copa del Rey triumph in 11 years in the 2018-19 season, while he beat Real Madrid for the Spanish Super Cup during his time in charge of Athletic.

However, he perhaps spent his best years in charge of Villarreal, first for a spell from 2013 to 2016 before spending three more years in charge from 2023 to 2026.

In his first stint in charge, Marcelino guided the Yellow Submarine to promotion before achieving three consecutive top-three finishes in La Liga and a run to the Europa League semi-finals in 2015-16.

After returning to the club in November 2023, the experienced coach steered the club to an eighth-place finish, before leading them to Champions League qualification in back-to-back seasons.

In fact, he guided the club to third spot last season, their best top-flight placing since finishing runners-up in 2007-08, but it proved to be his final campaign in charge after failing to agree terms on a new contract.

Inigo Perez’s struggle to find consistency since taking over the reins at Villarreal has only added to the sense that the club may have made a mistake by allowing him to depart.

However, Villarreal’s potential error may be an opportunity for another club, and while it would be a left-field decision to choose him as Carrick’s replacement, he possesses over 800 games of senior managerial experience, has already coached clubs with demanding fanbases and would not be fazed by the Red Devils’ precarious position in the Premier League relegation zone.

His pragmatism has sometimes been used against him as a criticism, but it may be just what Man United need after shipping 19 goals in 10 Premier League games this season.

Marcelino's keenness to play quickly through the lines and hit teams on the counter may also suit a team that is struggling for confidence in midfield and has players in advanced areas who can race in behind defences.

Can a bird flu vaccine keep up with a virus that keeps changing?

📅 Nov 10, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

A computer-designed bird flu vaccine protected mice against three H5N1 strains, offering early evidence of broader protection before human trials.

Governments hold doses of bird flu vaccine in storage for the day the virus starts spreading between people.

Each of those vaccines was built around a single virus sample. H5 bird flu, meanwhile, keeps splitting into branches that look different to the immune system.

A Cambridge team built a rival vaccine, with active ingredients drawn up by computer instead of copied from one virus.

Researchers gave it to mice, then exposed separate groups to three H5N1 viruses spanning two decades of evolution.

Every one of those mice lived. Half the mice given the stockpile-style vaccine died after exposure to the clade 1 virus.

The study was led by Jonathan Heeney, a professor of research in vaccinology in the Department of Veterinary Medicine at the University of Cambridge.

The vaccine comes out of the university spin-out DIOSynVax, where he and several co-authors are employees or shareholders.

The tests were in mice and ferrets, and no one has given this vaccine to a person.

In an interview with Earth.com, Heeney said vaccine design has spent most of a century looking the wrong way.

“For more than 70 years, vaccines against rapidly evolving viruses have largely been designed by looking backwards at the pathogen that already exists,” he said.

Bird flu now turns up in wild birds, poultry and mammals on nearly every continent. The H5 family has broken into branches that evolve on their own in Asia, Europe, Africa and the Americas.

How dangerous those branches are varies sharply.

The clade 2.3.4.4b viruses spreading in the Americas have caused 97 known human cases and three deaths, and most of those infections were mild.

These viruses have also spread through dairy herds.

The 2.3.2.1e viruses circulating in Southeast Asia have killed more than half the people with reported infections over the past two years.

The bird flu vaccines in national stockpiles are matched to single virus samples, picked through the World Health Organization’s influenza program.

Those vaccines haven’t been widely tested against the other H5 branches now circulating, the authors wrote.

“These vaccines are based on single viral isolates, whereas H5Nx viruses have continued to evolve and diversify into multiple lineages around the world,” Heeney told Earth.com.

“In many cases, viral evolution has outpaced the strains on which those stockpiled vaccines were originally based.”

The part of a vaccine that teaches the immune system has a name: the antigen.

Rather than lift one from a single virus, the team pooled the genetic sequences of H5 viruses collected worldwide by surveillance programs.

Software then drew up antigens carrying the features shared across the whole group.

The vaccine carries mRNA instructions for making three designed antigens.

One is based on the outer protein the virus uses to get into a cell, and a second on the protein it uses to get back out.

The third is a small protein that helps rouse the immune response.

The team screened the candidates in cells, then packaged mRNA encoding the selected antigens inside tiny fat particles, using the same delivery approach as mRNA COVID-19 vaccines.

Heeney said the design looks ahead instead of back.

He noted that his platform uses computational biology to anticipate viral evolution and design broadly protective vaccine antigens before the next outbreak occurs.

The team immunized mice, then exposed them to H5N1 viruses from three branches that have all infected people: clade 1, clade 2.3.2.1e and clade 2.3.4.4b.

All mice given the designed vaccine survived exposure to their assigned H5N1 virus, with no weight loss or signs of illness.

Four days after exposure, no virus could be recovered from their lungs or spleens. The stockpile-style vaccine matched to 2.3.4.4b protected fully against its own matched virus.

Against the 2.3.2.1e virus, 67% of those mice survived, and against clade 1, 50% did.

Blood tests pointed the same way. Antibodies from the designed vaccine blocked lab-made stand-ins for eleven H5 branches.

Each stockpile-style vaccine covered only its own corner of the family.

Ferrets catch and spread flu much as people do, making them an important model for testing influenza vaccines.

The team exposed them to a 2.3.4.4b H5N1 taken from an Italian chicken. In the entry protein, it differs from the stockpile vaccine’s strain by four amino acids.

Every unvaccinated ferret died or reached the humane endpoint by day eight. So did half of those given the stockpile-style vaccine.

All ferrets given the lower dose of the designed vaccine survived without weight loss or fever. One ferret given the higher dose reached the humane endpoint.

The oddity sits in the bloodwork. On the day of exposure, those ferrets scored below the antibody level flu researchers treat as protective against that virus.

Most survived despite those low antibody readings.

Something other than that one measure was carrying the protection, the authors wrote, most likely antibodies against the exit protein working alongside antibodies against the entry protein.

The comparison is not clean, and the authors wrote as much.

Their vaccine is mRNA, while the benchmark is a killed whole virus, so dose, formulation and delivery all differ along with the antigen design.

Comparing both antigen designs using the same mRNA platform would help isolate the effect of antigen design.

The mice also got a single dose of the stockpile-style comparator, a benchmark taken from older work.

And the designed vaccine’s edge over the stockpile shots can’t yet be pinned on the antigen design alone.

Nobody has measured whether any of this holds in people.

The next steps are manufacturing the vaccine to meet regulatory standards and a first trial in humans, Heeney said, and DIOSynVax is raising money to pay for them.

The designed antigen sequences are patented.

What the virus does in the meantime is the part nobody controls. “It’s when bird flu adapts to live in the airways of mammals that we start to get really nervous,” Heeney said.

That is the shift researchers watch for in dairy cows and pigs, and the reason a bird flu vaccine that protects against multiple branches could be valuable.

The full study was published in the journal Cell Reports Medicine.

Is Smith the player that the Rams needed to make up for the losses of Puka Nacua and Davante Adams? Right or wrong it's what the Rams just did, and for now, that's all they did to address their wide receiver room.

Monopoly and Scrabble Get a Scandinavian Modern Redesign

📅 Nov 10, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Monopoly and Scrabble have been fixtures of family game nights for generations, but their colorful boards and familiar pieces rarely stay out once the competition ends. When the last property has been traded or the final word spelled, everything usually disappears back into a cabinet. WS Game Company is rethinking that relationship between play and display.

The new Nordic Collection gives the two classic board games a Scandinavian-inspired redesign, replacing conventional folding boards and cardboard boxes with warm wood, understated graphics, and carefully considered details. Launched last month, the collection draws on the principles of Scandinavian modernism to create games that feel as much like design objects as they do as games.

“The Nordic Collection was inspired by the rich design history of Scandinavian modern design,” explains Kerry Addis, co-owner and COO of WS Game Company. She points to designers such as Hans Wegner and Arne Jacobsen, who combined natural materials and traditional craftsmanship with modern production techniques to create objects that were both innovative and inviting. For the Nordic Collection, the goal was to bring that same balance of familiarity and innovation to the games’ form, construction, and functionality.

The goal was to create something that could fit comfortably into an existing interior while offering meaningful innovations in form, construction, and practicality.

The most immediately noticeable change is the material palette. Both Monopoly Nordic and Scrabble Nordic feature wooden game boards made from individual panels that connect through concealed magnets. Instead of unfolding a conventional board, players snap the pieces together, creating a sturdy playing surface that breaks down for compact storage.

The assembly itself was designed to be as satisfying as the finished product. “The intuitive magnetic connections allow the user to snap the panels easily together, creating a solution that is as elegant and functional as it is satisfying to use,” explains James Fuschetti, WS Game Company’s senior industrial designer. Cork backing adds warmth while helping prevent the boards from sliding during a game.

Working with manufacturing partners, the designers selected woods and stains that would produce a warm tone with a subtle luster. Generously rounded corners on the storage boxes and other components soften their appearance while making them more comfortable to handle.

The result is a collection designed to complement a range of interiors rather than compete with them. Each game comes in a coordinating beechwood box that can sit comfortably on a shelf or coffee table, with the components stored neatly inside.

Each game comes in a coordinating beechwood box that keeps the components neatly organized while allowing the games to remain on display between uses.

Beyond the wooden construction, smaller details reveal how WS Game Company balanced Scandinavian influences with the visual identities of two instantly recognizable games.

In Monopoly Nordic, the traditional houses have become miniature pine trees. Instead of placing four houses side by side on a property, players stack them vertically to create increasingly tall conifers. As the game progresses, the board begins to resemble a Scandinavian forest.

The graphic design draws on the region’s history, too. Monopoly’s Chance and Community Chest cards have been recolored light blue and red, a subtle nod to early Swedish editions of the game. The railroad icon has been reimagined as an electric trolley, while the custom money takes cues from modernist Scandinavian banknotes, pairing crisp graphics with patterns inspired by traditional folk art.

Despite these updates, the game retains its recognizable corner-square icons, Chance question mark, and classic playing tokens, including the top hat and shoe. A wooden banker’s tray, die-cast tokens, and custom property deeds round out the set.

Scrabble Nordic takes a similarly considered approach. Its scorepad features graphics inspired by Scandinavian banknotes and folk art, while wooden letter tiles and racks bring warmth to the familiar game. The set also includes a microsuede tile bag, a pencil, and a sand timer with wooden end caps.

The designers were careful to preserve the details that make Scrabble instantly recognizable, from the size and weight of the letter tiles to the small triangular markings along the edges of premium squares. Those markings help players identify bonus spaces even when tiles cover the board, preserving a small but important functional detail of the original design.

Together, the two editions demonstrate how familiar games can be updated through materials, graphics, and playful details without losing the elements that make them enjoyable in the first place.

The collection builds on an idea WS Game Company has explored for years: Board games can be part of a home’s décor rather than hidden away between uses.

Its Vintage Bookshelf Collection takes a similar approach, packaging classic games in formats intended to blend into a bookshelf. The Nordic Collection extends that thinking through natural materials, a more sculptural presentation, and an emphasis on the physical experience of playing.

“We’ve always believed that good design should have staying power,” Addis says. Rather than chasing fleeting trends, the company aims to create products that can evolve with people’s interiors and remain relevant as their lives change.

As more daily activities migrate to screens, WS Game Company sees an opportunity to bring analog experiences back into everyday life. “Board games are a natural fit, bringing people together for shared experiences,” Addis says.

The Nordic Collection makes a case for finding new possibilities in objects we already know. Monopoly and Scrabble don’t need to be reinvented to feel fresh. Sometimes, a thoughtful change in materials, a more intuitive construction, or a miniature forest of wooden houses is enough to make a familiar game worth leaving out long after everyone has gone home.
