Stephen Colbert Signs With UTA

📅 Nov 19, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

The former ‘Late Show’ host and current ‘Lord of the Rings’ writer will be represented by the Beverly Hills-based talent agency.

Stephen Colbert has signed with UTA as he explores opportunities after The Late Show concluded earlier this year, The Hollywood Reporter has learned.

UTA will be representing the 13-time Emmy winner as well as his production company, Spartina Industries, across film, television, publishing work and more.

“We are thrilled to be working with Stephen Colbert. He is an exceptionally talented artist and creator, a dedicated philanthropist and a terrific person,” UTA president of filmed entertainment Matt Rice and managing partners Jay Gassner and Julien Thuan said in a joint statement. “We look forward to partnering with Stephen and Spartina Industries to support and further expand his creative endeavors.”

Colbert signed off from The Late Show in May after hosting the late-night series, which became a casualty of the Paramount-Skydance merger, for a decade. In the process, the comedian — who also wrote on and executive produced the series — collected several of his many Emmys as well as a Peabody Award.

Prior to his tenure at The Late Show, Colbert hosted, wrote and produced Comedy Central’s satire news program The Colbert Report for nine years and wrote and served as a correspondent on The Daily Show for seven years.

And Colbert is keeping busy post-Late Show. Currently, he’s co-writing the next Lord of the Rings movie with veteran LOTR screenwriter Philippa Boyens and his son Peter McGee, as well as working on his screenwriting software company Scripto. In a recent interview with THR, Colbert said he has been entertaining other opportunities but hasn’t landed on one yet. “There’s some fun stuff to think about. I’m in no hurry,” he said.

His production company, Spartina Industries, is currently developing a TV adaptation of Roger Zelazny’s sci-fi book series The Chronicles of Amber with The Walking Dead co-creator Robert Kirkman’s Skybound Entertainment. The company, headed up by Carrie Byalick, previously produced two seasons of the late-night show After Midnight on CBS.

Colbert continues to be managed by James Baby Doll Dixon at Dixon Talent, Inc. and represented by Tom Hansen and Don Steele at Hansen, Jacobson, Teller, Hoberman.

Amazon debuted its latest Kindle Paperwhite in October, which is now available for $199.99 and replaces the outgoing 2024 model. Some important pricing context: The waterproof e-reader held steady at $159.99 until late August, when its price rose 25 percent due to rising component costs (many other Amazon tech products got more expensive, too). If you want more features — including twice the storage, zero ads, wireless charging, and an auto-adjusting front light — opt instead for the Kindle Paperwhite Signature Edition while it’s down to $179.99 (also $70 off), which is just $10 shy of its best price to date.

For the sake of being comprehensive, the last-gen base Kindle is going for $119.99 ($30 off) at Amazon ahead of Black Friday. While the standard Kindle has improved with each new iteration, it’s tough to recommend when the vastly superior Paperwhite is on sale for just $10 more. That 10 bucks gets you so much: waterproofing, better battery life, faster page-turning speeds, and a higher pixel density screen that’s slightly larger (7 inches vs. 6 inches).

Richard Powell Unsettles Race and Color Theory

📅 Nov 19, 2026  |  Category: Science  |  Read Source →

Drawing on the work of artists and theorists alike, the art historian traces how generations of Black painters have deployed color and how color, in turn, shapes our understanding of Blackness.

Sometime in the 1980s, when I was still a fledgling curator of 20th-century art at the Metropolitan Museum of Art, I was invited to the studio of a rather prominent artist. During our conversation, the work of Jacob Lawrence came up somehow, and the artist mentioned that he was impressed by what he described as Lawrence’s use of “Black” colors. Did I mention this artist was White?

I was gobsmacked by the comment. Not that I wasn’t attuned to stereotypical societal associations of color — most notably, of bright, loud colors with Black people. In the 1950s, a full three decades before Nancy Reagan practically made red her official color, my Aunt Ruth was appalled that my mother made red coats for my sister and me, rather than using more acceptable pastel colors. Then a friend who grew up in Northern Ireland told me that Protestant Irish factions associated Catholic Irish with loud and bright colors.

But how did such racially associative ideas translate to the realm of the so-called “fine” arts? I was aware of the place of color theory in the practice of art and knew the names of important theorists, from Johann Wolfgang von Goethe to Johannes Itten to Josef Albers. But otherwise, I presumed artists’ palettes were the result of personal predilection, technical explorations (i.e., the opening up of brushstrokes and form in the 19th century and the introduction of a wall of color in the 20th century), or cultural orientation. So it was therefore with great interest that I approached reading Richard Powell’s latest publication, Colorstruck!: Painting, Pigment, Affect (2026).

Toyota’s Corolla Cross Deck Is a Forbidden Fruit Rival To the Ford Maverick

📅 Nov 19, 2026  |  Category: Business  |  Read Source →

The four-door unibody pickup, unfortunately, seems only destined for Latin America and the Caribbean for the moment.

Toyota has had incredible success with pickup trucks in the United States of America. The Tacoma has become one of America’s perennial best-selling vehicles, while the Tundra has carved out a sustainable niche in America’s grueling full-size segment. But there’s arguably room for more.

Rumors have long suggested Toyota is working on a compact, Ford Maverick-fighting pickup that would fill the truck lineup’s glaring need for a cheaper and more efficient option. Dealers have reportedly been asking Toyota for a compact pickup for several years. Mark Templin, Toyota’s COO in the United States, confirmed Toyota is “looking at” bringing a smaller pickup to the U.S. back in May 2025. And now, Toyota has just unveiled a new pickup that could fit that bill … for Latin American and Caribbean markets.

Toyota revealed a new compact pickup called the Corolla Cross Deck on Wednesday during its inauguration of a new production facility in Sococaba, Brazil, and it will enter production in the first half of 2027. According to Motor1 Brazil, the Corolla Cross Deck shares a TNGA-C platform with the Corolla sedan and Corolla Cross SUV. Toyota did not provide full specs, but did reveal a hybrid version—likely featuring the 1.8-liter 122-horsepower powertrain Toyota uses for the Corolla Cross in that market.

Sadly, Toyota has not confirmed potential availability for the Corolla Cross Deck in the American market. The automaker did not respond to a Road & Track inquiry about its potential availability by the time of publication, but we’ll update this story if we receive a response.

On paper, a Corolla-based compact pickup would likely be the easiest route for Toyota to bring a small, cheap pickup to the United States in the foreseeable future. Toyota already builds the Corolla and Corolla Cross at its Mazda Toyota Manufacturing Facility in Alabama; a new truck truck could be built on the same line—and even spawn potential rebadged variants for Toyota partners Mazda and Subaru.

In the American market, the Toyota Corolla Cross uses 2.0-liter gas and hybrid engines. That powertrain could give Toyota options for a 169-horsepower base gas model truck—potentially starting below $30,000—and a 196-hp hybrid version that could eclipse 40 miles per gallon.

That said, one could also see Toyota needing a different small truck approach to resonate with an American audience. One of Ford’s great innovations with the Maverick was that it looks more like the F-150 and Ranger than the Escape crossover it shared a platform with. And even if a new Toyota pickup were to share its bones with the Corolla, Toyota may want to brand it as an extension of the pickup line, rather than the Corolla family.

Last of the old school buyers, Publicis Media vet John Muszynski prepares to retire

📅 Nov 19, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Join us Dec. 1-3 in Nashville for the Digiday Programmatic Marketing Summit

He’s equally comfortable at the negotiating table or on skates in an ice rink.

He doesn’t do it himself but he knows principal media is part of the business.

He’s said to have once humbled the CEO of a major cable network group by holding back $200 million of his clients’ media spend when that CEO tried to bully him into big price increases.

He’s the longest-serving head of investment in media. And now, John Muszynski, legendary media investment executive with Publicis Media, plans to retire.

After 45 years at one holding company and negotiating nearly as many upfronts over that span, the chairman of Publicis Media Exchange (PMX) U.S., is stepping down from a full-time role this year, although he plans to continue to consult with the holdco.

Chicago-based Muszynski, who oversees decision-making on what Publicis estimates to be 40% of the U.S. video marketplace inventory, is arguably the last of the old-school heads of investment that have seen the upfronts morph into the byzantine bazaar it is today.

“For nearly five decades, I’ve had a front row seat to the industry’s transformation,” Muszynski told Digiday. “It’s gone from three networks to always-on content spanning the entire digital ecosystem. It’s been fascinating to be a part of it all.”

The avid ice hockey fan, hunter and fisherman (when he’s not negotiating) got his start in 1981 as a planner for Leo Burnett’s burgeoning media unit, handling longtime Publicis clients including Kimberly-Clark, McDonald’s and Kellogg’s. That unit eventually became standalone media agency Starcom, for which he rose to CEO of Starcom USA from 2005-2009. He then helped to build Spark Foundry from a 65-person boutique into a 3,000-person media agency.

In 2016, Publicis formed Publicis Media Exchange, which harnessed the collective buying clout of what was then Starcom, Mediavest, Spark, Zenith, Optimedia and Blue 449 media agencies. He was the lead negotiator when that formed, and became chairman of the group in 2022.

The execs who sat across from him at the negotiating table said they always felt they got a fair shake — in part because Muszynski always came prepared, was knowledgable and true to his word.

“John has been a tremendous influence on generations of marketers, agency team members and publishers,” said Mark Marshall, chairman of global advertising and partnerships at NBCUniversal. “He has always put his clients’ objectives at the forefront and pushed all of us to deliver for those clients. He is the epitome of ‘a handshake is his word’.”

Rita Ferro, president, global advertising, disney, was even more personal in her assessment of working with Muszynski. “John is a legend in this business and from the moment we met, he became a trusted partner and more importantly a dear friend,” she said.

Sean Moran, COO at Evergreen Trading agency, who negotiated with Muszynski during his 24 years as an ad sales exec with Viacom until 2020, thinks of Muszynski as a contemporary of the old-school buyers of the TV business, from Grey Advertising’s Jon Mandel to UM’s Donna Wolfe, and from Omnicom’s John Swift to GroupM’s Rino Scanzoni.

“When I was running MTV and we had a big upfront event, and had to get people there, if I could get John Muszynski to fly in and come to my event, that was a huge win,” said Moran. ”He represented so much gravitas. Obviously he controlled a lot of budgets, but it was also because he was so smart and yet had such empathy for good people. You couldn’t bullshit him. He was a good Midwestern guy who could talk about a great deal over over a steak, but he did his homework and knew his numbers.”

Moran said Muszynski often set the marketplace as “the main controller” of the upfronts. “One of his negotiating tactics was, ‘Yeah, I haven’t seen everything, but boy, it looks down’,” said Moran. “If he respected your inventory, you could do a deal with him fairly quickly and cut through the chaff. He was known to pull that with one or two people that were at the front of the line, and protect his clients. He would therefore set the marketplace for himself. It was very smart.”

Although Muszynski will retire from his full-time position at PMX, he will stay on to consult through 2027, working with Joel Lunenfeld who leads PMX globally and Mark Griffin, who oversees all investment in the U.S. as chief investment officer.

5 Ian Evatt replacements Blackpool must consider ASAP

📅 Nov 19, 2026  |  Category: Sports  |  Read Source →

Blackpool have confirmed the sacking of Ian Evatt following a year in charge at Bloomfield Road, with the Tangerines now intent on reviving their fortunes under fresh management and avoiding a battle against relegation from League One.

Evatt had led the side to a respectable 13th-placed finish during the previous campaign after taking over from Steve Bruce, though the former Bolton Wanderers boss would struggle to get going once again at Bloomfield Road this term.

Last Saturday's 1-0 defeat away to fellow strugglers AFC Wimbledon marked the sixth in just ten games for Blackpool, who now find themselves only positioned above Doncaster Rovers in 21st by virtue of goal difference at this moment in time.

Blackpool's key decision-makers eventually listened to intensified sack calls from supporters by relieving Evatt of his duties on Thursday afternoon, and whilst the relegation-threatened third-tier outfit have confirmed that Steve Thompson will be in charge for this weekend's clash with Wigan Athletic, the search for a replacement has already begun.

With that in mind, FLW has taken a close look at FIVE managers who Blackpool should be seriously considering as potential successors to Evatt.

One manager who should surely feature within Blackpool's thinking is Andy Woodman who, remarkably enough, currently finds himself out of work following a controversial EFL sacking for the ages from League One rivals Bromley.

Woodman had, of course, inspired the plucky Ravens' unprecedented rise into the EFL pyramid for the first time in their history before winning the League Two title against all odds last season, although matters both on and off the field in that particular corner of South London have not been plain-sailing as of late.

Nonetheless, it was a huge surprise when the 52-year-old's historic 289-match reign at Bromley came to an abrupt end last month, and one suspects he won't be sat around unattached for too long.

We rank all 24 League One stadiums by their ground's distance from the city or town, starting with the longest distance.

Woodman's resourceful ability to get the very best out of what was at his disposal with the Ravens certainly reads well, and he frequently elevated and identified talent with great success.

His strong body of work, albeit at a lower level, means that he should be entering the thinking of the Tangerines hierarchy in the coming days.

A previous specialist of the firefighting genre, Gary Rowett's knack in that field has faded in recent times and he may need to re-attempt his hand in a lower division.

As such, Rowett could represent the most outstanding realistic candidate available to Blackpool right now, with the former Derby County, Millwall and Stoke City boss having been linked with the vacancy at promotion hopefuls Bradford City prior to Michael Duff's appointment.

The last few years haven't reflected too kindly on Rowett, who led both Birmingham City and Leicester to relegations while setting Oxford United up for the same fate in the same season that he was in charge of the Foxes' demotion last time out.

Therefore, the wily 52-year-old may not score favourably in the consideration of Championship decision-makers and could instead drop to League One for the first time in his managerial career.

Previously, of course, Rowett has excelled in firing teams towards safety and, though much of his fresher work has been conducted on a short-term basis, there's no reason why he couldn't provide a stable, steady and longer-term presence with the Seasiders.

Matt Bloomfield, meanwhile, having reportedly held talks with Blackpool following the aforementioned Bruce's departure in October of last year, is only likely to re-emerge as a contender for the Tangerines' top job in the near future.

Bloomfield was ultimately unable to prevent Oxford's relegation to League One after succeeding Rowett, yet many believed his subsequent sacking from the Kassam Stadium to represent a surprise and even harsh call - albeit Aaron Ramsey has since vindicated the Yellows' change in direction.

Still aged just 42, Bloomfield's stock has taken a few hits and he's perhaps yet to recover from an ill-fated spell in charge of Luton Town, in which he fell narrowly short of ensuring survival on the 2024/25 second-tier term's final-day before failing to inspire a tangible promotion push in League One.

He has, however, previously excelled at this level, with Wycombe Wanderers having been situated just two points behind eventual-runaway champions Birmingham at the time of his departure to Luton in January 2025.

Bloomfield may now require a sustained spell back in League One leading a side with more modest expectations such as the Chairboys, where he was able to work his magic - and Blackpool can provide just that.

In contrast to Rowett and Bloomfield, Stuart Maynard lacks pedigree above League Two level, but that shouldn't rule the ex-Notts County and York City boss out of contention.

Indeed, at the age of 46 and with an obvious appetite to prove his managerial mettle higher up the pyramid, Maynard could be an ideal candidate for Blackpool in their efforts to not only survive, but begin laying the foundational blueprint for longer-term stability and success.

Much like Woodman at Bromley, Maynard's dismissal from York in the wake of promotion back to League Two following a decade-long exile as National League champions with no fewer than 108 points was scarcely believable. The difference, of course, is that Maynard did not even receive the opportunity to lead the Ministermen into the current campaign, and their loss could turn out to be Blackpool's gain.

Maynard has a solid body of work to boast of from his time at Notts County, where he failed to deliver promotion but did take the Magpies into the fourth-tier play-offs during their second season back at the level and ultimately put the foundations in place for Martin Paterson to go one better with the Meadow Lane outfit.

Blackpool fans, having sat through an overly-patient and slow-paced possession-based style under Evatt, would likely warm to a more dynamic, attack-oriented approach through Maynard, who would tick many boxes for the League One strugglers.

A more left-field option with greater risk but no shortage of potential upside, Ryan Mason remains available following his departure from West Bromwich Albion at the start of the year and represents an interesting candidates for lower-ranked EFL outfits to consider.

Mason was highly-thought-of during his time coaching Tottenham Hotspur and even taking occasional interim charge of the Lilywhites, which meant that many were surprised when the one-cap ex-England international failed to get going at West Brom.

He would take just nine wins from 27 matches across all competitions with the Baggies before being dismissed ahead of a relegation battle in the deeper stages of the previous campaign, with James Morrison going on to prove just how much his two predecessors in Mason and Eric Ramsey had underperformed at the Hawthorns.

It was, however, Mason's first senior, full-time gig, and it would be harsh to judge him entirely from that alone.

Mason showed plenty of signs at West Brom that suggest he'd be a more-than-capable candidate somewhat lower down the ladder, with his progressive style, experience with young talent and connections within the game all representing healthy selling points for a club in Blackpool's position.

It's essentially a case of risk versus reward, and only Blackpool's key boardroom figures can decide whether it's a gamble worth taking amid the threat of relegation to League Two.

They do have a history of appointing older managers with their best years very much in the past, though, and there are worse options than going for a change of tact and rolling the dice on Mason.

Bitcoin fell as low as $62k late Saturday, its sixth straight weekly decline. Meanwhile, tokenized stocks crossed $4 billion in onchain value for the first time on Nov 11. Tokenized stocks are now roughly five times their value on January 1.

Tokenized stocks track equity prices, not crypto, so a falling BTC doesn’t directly pull their prices down. What stands out, however, is that crypto users appear to keep buying them through the selloff. Demand for 24/7 trading and onchain access to US equities appears to have found strong product-market fit as they are holding up even as the rest of the crypto market weakens.

In September, rising share prices drove most of the growth in value of tokenized stocks. Since mid-October, new minting has driven the growth in value even as share prices dropped. Over the eleven weeks to Nov 13, minting added $1.12 billion while price moves took away $339 million. Binance’s bStocks accounted for about four fifths of that minting, led by NVIDIA, SpaceX and Circle.

How to adjust and turn off motion blur in GTA 6 if it makes you feel sick

📅 Nov 19, 2026  |  Category: Other  |  Read Source →

If the blur makes you feel a little nauseous, there are settings to mitigate it.

If you've been waiting years to play Grand Theft Auto 6, nothing is more disappointing than booting it up and finding it doesn't look quite as good as you'd hoped—or worse, that it makes you feel sick. There are a number of things that could be causing that, but the good news is that one of the most common culprits is extremely easy to fix. Motion blur is a standard feature in most AAA games these days, but there are good reasons some players want to avoid it.

For some people, motion blur just doesn't look good. GTA 6 is the most expensive game ever made, and one reason is its incredibly lifelike graphics. If a layer of motion blur on top of that ruins the illusion, that's enough of a reason to turn it off. Even worse, motion blur commonly contributes to motion sickness, causing dizziness and nausea in some players. Motion blur is nearly ubiquitous in modern games, but fortunately, there are usually settings to tone it down or turn it off completely.

It's easy to adjust or turn off motion blur in GTA 6. Just pause the game and select Settings from the bottom of the main menu. Next, scroll over to the Display tab by pressing the right shoulder button. About halfway down the page, under the visual effects heading, you'll see a setting called Motion Blur Strength with a slider. By default, it's set to three out of 10, so you can actually tone down the effect without completely turning it off, or even increase it. You can also just set the strength to zero to turn off motion blur completely.

It's worth playing with this setting a little bit to find the level that works for you. Especially at lower frame rates, motion blur can help smooth out camera movements that might otherwise be distractingly jittery, so even leaving it at its lowest level can help. Including a slider instead of a simple toggle is a welcome feature in GTA 6, letting you dial it in to your exact needs.

Since GTA 6 targets 30 fps on consoles, rather than 60 fps, those jarring camera movements may be a bigger deal than they are in other big releases. The best level of motion blur for you will depend more on your TV and personal preference than anything else.
