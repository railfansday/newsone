CDT Southern Terminus Closure Lifted, Border Monument To Be Relocated

📅 Nov 13, 2026  |  Category: Other  |  Read Source →

The Continental Divide Trail’s southern terminus and southernmost mile have reopened following a temporary construction closure, according to the Continental Divide Trail Coalition (CDTC). This section of the trail had been closed in late October so that construction crews could widen an access road and construct a second border barrier at the terminus.

The first mile of the CDT falls within the Fort Huachuca New Mexico National Defense Area (NDA), a military zone created in spring 2025 when roughly 110,000 acres along the US-Mexico border were transferred from the Bureau of Land Management into Army control. Hikers seeking to access the trail’s southern terminus must submit to a background check and obtain an Army permit. Noncitizens must also have a US citizen escort through the NDA who also has an access permit.

“These events are unprecedented in the history of our trail system: the transfer of a critically significant element of our national scenic trail into the Department of Defense control, the conversion of the CDT Southern Terminus into a military and construction zone of momentous scale, and trail management partnerships that include land management agencies and state governments, but also the US military,” the CDTC said in a press release.

The CDTC received word today from US Army officials at Fort Huachuca that the trail is once again officially open, although construction activities are ongoing and hikers may still encounter traffic, road closures, and delays in the area. “This closure came at a critical time for southbound hikers,” the organization said. “Completing the 3,100-mile trail is a monumental achievement, and the construction activities at the border created dynamic changes to the permitted NDA access in place during the past year.”

The press release also indicates that the construction of the second border wall means the southern terminus monument will need to be relocated to an area outside of the border walls, and that access to the monument may be temporarily prohibited during construction and relocation. “CDTC does not know the exact timing of the relocation but will provide updates as available,” the organization wrote.

What 12 12 12's Title Really Means

📅 Nov 13, 2026  |  Category: Other  |  Read Source →

Apple TV's newest crime thriller is 12 12 12, so what exactly does that unusual title mean? Anthony Mackie and Jamie Dornan star in 12 12 12, an Apple TV heist series that premiered on November 13, 2026. Created by Jim Keeble and Dudi Appleton, the series currently has a 64% on Rotten Tomatoes.

12 12 12 follows Jack Strickland (Mackie), an FBI agent on the tail of a famed thief and the possible murderer of Jack's former partner, Paul Galvin (Dornan). After a Newark, NJ heist went sideways and Jack's agent friend was killed, his despair hardened into resolve and then into obsession.

Now, he is doing everything he can to catch Paul in the act. For his part, Paul hasn't slowed down, and he now has his eyes on 800,000 Swiss francs that a Russian criminal organization has recently placed into a vault in Zurich. As we watch the cat-and-mouse game, it becomes clear that Paul and Jack may be more alike than it appears.

It's a fairly standard setup for a heist story, with a few curveballs thrown in here and there. The biggest of these curveballs is the structure, which fans of a show like 24 will quickly understand. It's this conceit that sets the show apart from other ones in the genre, and it also helps explain the title.

At the top of each episode of 12 12 12, the audience is reminded of something allegedly in the FBI's Principles of Crime. First, a high-asset robbery, like the one Paul has done in Zurich, takes 12 months to plan. The actual execution takes 12 hours. Then, the police have 12 days to solve it before the thieves can erase their tracks.

The 12 12 12 of the title then comes from those principles. Not only are we reminded of these at the start of the episode, but the show is also structured so that we are jumping through each of these frameworks, sometimes viewing an event in the future before we see its build-up in the past.

The 12-month timeline is titled onscreen as "The Planning", the 12-hour one is "The Heist", and the 12-day one is "The Takedown". As we move through each of these different sections, more details are revealed to us that change the way we think about the future events we may have already seen.

The first two episodes of 12 12 12 premiered on November 13, 2026, and the series will release new episodes weekly until December 25, 2026, for a total of 8 episodes. At the moment, there are no plans for 12 12 12 season 2, so it's unclear what kind of ending viewers should expect.

FAA Grounds All US Flights Until Further Notice

📅 Nov 13, 2026  |  Category: Other  |  Read Source →

The Federal Aviation Administration grounded all US flights Friday afternoon, due to “extraordinary circumstances and for reasons of safety,” the agency said in a statement. Flights already in the air will be allowed to land, the agency said, but no new ones are permitted to take off.

In a post on X, US Transportation Secretary Sean Duffy said that the ground stop was related to a communication issue affecting Air Traffic Control Centers, and said that agency technicians are investigating.

The FAA has not provided any additional details, and the agency did not immediately respond to a request for comment.

Nationwide ground stops are rare. The FAA halted all air traffic on September 11, 2001. In January of 2023, a ground stop was issued after an outage to a critical system that the FAA used to distribute real-time data and warnings to pilots, known as NOTAM (Notice to Air Mission).

Warhammer Survivors Puts Your Brain In A Mindless Blender Of Non-Stop Action

📅 Nov 13, 2026  |  Category: World  |  Read Source →

My quest to play every genre of Warhammer 40,000 video game has led me now to Warhammer Survivors. Since I’ve fallen and I can’t get out of the Warhammer brain-rot swamp, this grimdark palette swap of Luca Galante’s make the brain go brrr-like Vampire Survivors should be extremely my shit. But not only does playing it makes me feel like something is being extracted from me, be it time, mental energy, or life force, but the game is also just…not my scene.

I’ve never played Vampire Survivors or its roguelike deckbuilder spinoff, Vampire Crawlers, but I watched my husband surrender his life to the game last year so I have a basic understanding of how it works. Pick a character, kill an ever-growing, nigh-endless horde of enemies, get upgrades to increase your power, rinse and repeat.

What surprised and impressed me the most about WS is, for however much the game’s description makes it seem mindless, there’s some strategy to be had. How you move around structures, knowing when to deploy items like grenades or flamethrowers, and which upgrades to prioritize during a run, all required more brain power than I expected the game would demand. There’s an item called vuln oil which randomly spits out gobs of black sludge at regular intervals. Because it doesn’t proc as often nor do as much damage as my chainsword or bolter, I thought it was worthless and ignored it my first few runs. After being forced into the item from a treasure chest and seeing how it worked after a few upgrades, I’ve now decided it’s a must-have.

I’m not fond of the obvious dark pattern-ification inherent in the game, the way it uses psychological tactics to keep you playing. Getting a treasure chest in Warhammer Survivors and seeing coins spit out of it slot-machine style while jubilant music plays is extremely off-putting. I know why it’s designed like that. I know the emotion it’s trying to evoke. Yuck. Playing Warhammer Survivors also makes me feel like I’m actively losing my mind. Not going crazy, but like my brain is atrophying in real time. It involves a ton of focus, which I generally enjoy in a game, but it’s a passive kind of focus. Like with the awareness you need to drive a car, your brain is on but it’s not really engaged in any kind of problem solving.

But honestly, I can forgive all that. I’m not immune to dark pattern exploitation and want to spend a little more time with it to unlock the Sister of Battle character. I think my biggest, pettiest complaint about Warhammer Survivors is that…it’s just not the flavor of Warhammer I want. Firstly, your starting character is an Ultramarine and I’m sick to the back teeth of fucking Ultramarines in Warhammer video games. Space Marine, Space Marine 2, both Boltgun games and now Warhammer Survivors. Ultramarines are to Warhammer what the Yankees are to Major League Baseball. Yes they put up a bunch of Ws; they have the biggest, loudest fanbase; and they once counted among their number some legendary guys. But there are far more interesting factions out there worth paying attention to.

Secondly, and I really must stress this is a petty complaint, where’s the love for the Horus Heresy, man? At the beginning of the game you can choose to play in the 40,000 universe or in the sword-and-board variant, Age of Sigmar. I would have loved to get one extra option to play in the Horus Heresy universe. Walk with me here. You’re a little Salamander space marine, trudging around Isstvan V fighting off wave after wave of Death Guards, Emperor’s Children, World Eaters, and more. As you get stronger, you progress from regular space marines to space marines in terminator armor, then chaos corrupted space marines, then a first captain, then Horus himself. Do you know how hard that would go?? I was ready to scrap this entire blog because I thought I had unlocked my absolute favorite character from the Horus Heresy, the goodest boy Tarik Torgaddon, but when I realized his name was actually Tor Garadon I was crushed.

I know as a Horus Heresy girlie I’m spoiled for choice when it comes to books, animated series, and audio dramas. I know. But sometimes a girl needs a video game that will let her kill her mortal enemy, Horus Lupercal. Warhammer Survivors, then, isn’t the one she’s looking for.

Before or After the Snow? Here’s The Right Time to Salt Your Driveway This Winter

📅 Nov 13, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Salt your driveway like a pro this winter with these expert-backed tips.

The Centers for Disease Control report a 35 percent increase in outdoor slips and falls during the winter months. Icy sidewalks, stairs and driveways are largely to blame for this seasonal uptick. Icy driveways are not only dangerous to walk on but also make driving and parking difficult. Ice melt salt is one of the best ways to keep driveways clear, but how should you best use it? Should you apply it before or after a storm — or both? Two industry experts weigh in on the best way to salt your driveway for optimal results this winter.

It depends. Salting before a big storm with heavy snow and ice accumulation is a good idea, but timing matters.

Plan to apply salt several hours before a storm hits for best results. It creates a salty coating that prevents the initial snowfall from bonding with the driveway surface. Pre-salting reduces total snow accumulation and makes snow removal easier once the storm is over. Avoid doing it too far in advance, or if rain is in the forecast, as the salt can be washed or blown away before the snow arrives. It’s not necessary to salt before a light snowfall of less than six inches.

It’s always a good idea to salt after it snows, but don’t rely on it to replace snow removal efforts. First remove as much snow as possible with a shovel or snowblower, then use salt to clear away any remaining slush and snow.

The best time to salt is immediately after snow removal, before cold nighttime temperatures set in. “When applied before the temperatures drop, salt prevents ice from forming and ensures the snow does not freeze solid to the ground,” Angelika Klimorowski, a winter maintenance specialist at Online Rock Salt, says.

Apply salt before and after a big storm for best results, especially when heavy snow, sleet or freezing rain is expected.

“Pre-salting helps prevent ice from bonding to the driveway, while a second application can help break down any ice that forms,” Aaron Strickland, owner of Mr. Trim Lawn & Garden Services, says. “For a typical snowfall, both applications usually aren’t necessary.”

If only a few inches of snow is forecast, salting after snow removal is likely sufficient. Applying salt before and after a small storm is not only a waste of money, but can damage driveway surfaces, burn lawn edges and generate harmful storm runoff.

“The exact amount of rock salt that you need will depend on how big the area is that you are planning to de-ice,” Klimorowski says. A little goes a long way, and more is not better. A 12-oz. coffee mug filled with salt is enough to treat a 1,000-sq.-ft. driveway. Spread salt evenly, leaving about 3 inches between salt grains. Avoid overapplication, as it can degrade driveway surfaces and harm the environment.

A handheld spreader makes application easier and more consistent, especially for larger driveways. “We try to follow recommended coverage rates and use a spreader wherever possible,” Strickland says. “Too much salt wastes product and can damage surrounding grass and gardens.” If spreading salt by hand, always wear gloves and cascade it for even coverage.

Rock salt is only effective down to ground temperatures of 15 to 17 degrees. Don’t apply more salt if it’s not working, as it’s likely too cold out for it to work. “If your area tends to experience colder temperatures, you may need to find an alternative de-icing product to keep your surfaces safe,” Klimorowski says. Calcium chloride and magnesium chloride ice melt products are two common alternatives that remain effective at sub-zero temperatures.

Yes, rain will wash away salt rather than dissolve it. It then ends up in storm drains and potentially pollutes local bodies of water.

Rock salt stops working in temperatures below 10 to 15 degrees.

“While it is true that salt will work best if you apply it before the temperatures drop, there is no deadline, and you can always apply it later,” Klimorowski says.

There’s no guarantee salt will damage your driveway, but you should use it sparingly. Concrete driveways are particularly vulnerable to salt damage.

Defamation Counterclaim Arising out Reagan National Helicopter Crash Case Can Go Forward

📅 Nov 13, 2026  |  Category: World  |  Read Source →

Mostly law professors | Sometimes contrarian | Often libertarian | Always independent

[A.] From Ellis v. Wallace, decided today by Chief Magistrate Judge Scott Varholak (D. Colo.):

On January 29, 2025, a Black Hawk Helicopter and American Airlines Flight 5342 collided mid-air near Ronald Reagan Washington National Airport. Hours after the crash, several posts and articles appeared claiming Plaintiff was the helicopter pilot in the midair collision ("the rumor"), including:

Defendant made his first X post addressing the rumor on January 31, 2025, at 1:00 a.m. Eastern Time. And Plaintiff filed the Complaint in this matter on April 9, 2025, alleging that Defendant's posts were defamatory. Plaintiff then proceeded to appear as a guest on several podcasts. On July 18, 2025, she was interviewed on the Kill Tony podcast. During the interview, the hosts asked Plaintiff about the collision rumor and her pending litigation. Plaintiff explained that Defendant was the "[f]irst one to tie my likeness to the rumor. We don't know if he started the rumor, but he had millions of followers. He did the most what we can determine as damage."

Ten days later, Defendant filed the Special Motion to Dismiss Plaintiff's Complaint, attaching copies of X posts and articles linking Plaintiff to the crash hours before Defendant commented on the rumor. Plaintiff then appeared on a second podcast, the RVA Magazine podcast, on September 30, 2025. Plaintiff was again asked about the rumor and her pending litigation. In response, she stated:

[W]ithin 24 hours of the crash, I was finding out that I was being named as as the pilot as, like, the unnamed female pilot that was the supposed DEI hire that Trump, President Trump had alluded to at a press conference that "we know it's DEI because we just know." And so that kind of sent the online world into a frenzy of like, let's figure out who this pilot is. And, from what we can tell, [Defendant] picked up on that and used a picture of me and the [Michael] Smerconish article and, um, kind of put it all together. And with his following, he had, I think, two and half million followers or something like that, we believe he did the most damage. Like it would not have had the legs it had if he hadn't posted about it.

Finally, on January 12, 2026, Kim Congdon interviewed Plaintiff on The Kim Congdon Takeover podcast. Congdon asked Plaintiff if she was suing the individual that started the rumor. Plaintiff responded:

I don't, yeah, I don't know if he started it, but he definitely, like, he pushed it. He's the first one to tie my likeness to it. Yeah. His name's Matt Wallace and, uh, he, uh, millions of followers on Twitter, and he pushed it pretty hard and we believe he did the most damage and so I'm suing him in federal court."

On April 6, 2026, Defendant filed the Counterclaim alleging that these podcast statements were defamatory.

Defendant alleges that Plaintiff made repeated defamatory statements that: (1) Defendant "was the first one to post [Plaintiff's] likeness in connection with the helicopter crash," and (2) that Plaintiff believes Defendant's "statements caused the most damage." {Plaintiff alleges that there is a material difference between claiming that Defendant was the first to name Plaintiff in relation to the tragedy, and that Defendant was the first one to post Plaintiff's likeness in connection with the collision. The distinction is unclear to the Court. Accordingly, the Court assesses Plaintiff's "likeness" statements as conveying Defendant was the first to connect Plaintiff to the collision.}

[B.] The court rejected Plaintiff's arguments that Plaintiff's statements were protected by the "litigation privilege." That privilege bars defamation lawsuits based on statements in court filings and in-court statements, but doesn't apply to statements about the lawsuit made outside the litigation.

(Recall that, though in a typical case it would be a defendant who's arguing the statements were privileged, opinion, true, etc., and plaintiff would be responding, here the roles are flipped because the defendant is counterclaiming against plaintiff based on plaintiff's statements.)

[C.] The court also rejected Plaintiff's arguments that the statements were nonactionable opinion: It concluded that both statements were "susceptible of being proved true or false," and were framed as factual assertions rather than using "loose or hyperbolic language that provided the impression that her statements should not be taken seriously."

[D.] The court concluded that Defendant had introduced sufficient evidence that the statements were false:

First, with respect to Plaintiff's "likeness" statements, Plaintiff repeatedly claimed that Defendant was "the first one to tie [her] likeness to the rumor." However, Defendant provided several pieces of evidence showing that other X users linked Plaintiff to the rumor before Defendant….

Second, Defendant has shown a reasonable likelihood of proving by clear and convincing evidence that Plaintiff's "most damage" statements were false. Defendant declares that "the rumor was spread primarily by much larger voices than [Defendant's,] such as Ann Coulter's, Alex Jones's, and others." He claims that these "prominent individuals and entities" had a "far greater reach than anything [he] posted" about Plaintiff. If true, this declaration could provide clear and convincing evidence that Defendant's "most damage" statements were false. At the very least, it creates a question for the jury since Plaintiff has offered competing evidence. …

[E.] The court also concluded that Defendant had introduced sufficient evidence that Plaintiff knew the "likeness"/"first one to tie" statements were probably false (which is required by Colorado law in such cases):

First, Plaintiff's statements on the RVA Magazine podcast and The Kim Congdon Takeover podcast occurred after Defendant filed his Special Motion to Dismiss and Plaintiff filed her response. In his Special Motion to Dismiss, Defendant included evidence of other X users linking Plaintiff to the rumor before Defendant's first post. And Plaintiff's podcast statements referred to the ongoing litigation. Thus, it is plausible to infer that Plaintiff had a high degree of awareness of the probable falsity of her statements.

Second, Plaintiff's statements reveal inconsistencies in her account of the underlying events. During her interview on the Kill Tony podcast and The Kim Congdon Takeover podcast, Plaintiff repeatedly stated that she did not know whether Defendant started the rumor despite claiming he was "the first one to tie [her] likeness to it." Plaintiff repeated these conflicting statements in her declaration. was the first to blame [her] for this horrific crash because [she] clearly knew that information was incorrect.")] Consequently, such inconsistencies weigh in favor of finding that Defendant has shown a reasonable likelihood of proving actual malice by clear and convincing evidence….

But the court concluded there wasn't enough evidence of knowing or reckless falsehood as to the "most damage" statement:

Defendant declares that at the time Plaintiff made her public statements, she knew "prominent individuals and entities" had discussed her connection with the crash and that those individuals had a "far greater reach than anything [Defendant] posted." However, such evidence does not support an inference that Plaintiff entertained serious doubts as to the truth of the statements or was highly aware of their probable falsity. Further, Defendant provides no other evidence to support his actual malice claim here.

On the other hand, the mere fact that the actor stated that the podcaster did the most harm—even knowing the New York Times had a broader audience—does not necessarily equate to actual malice; the actor could easily believe (even if wrongfully) that the podcaster caused the most harm because the podcaster gave credibility to the false rumor and thus led others to run with the story. Here, Defendant has only presented evidence that those with broader audiences likewise posted the false story. But, as the hypothetical shows, this does not necessarily mean that Plaintiff's statement that Defendant caused the most damage was made with actual malice.}

[F.] Finally, the court concludes that Defendant had sufficiently alleged damages, as required by Colorado law:

Specifically, he alleged that Plaintiff's statements have caused: (1) "significant stress and strain in [his] family relations," and (2) stress from his alleged tarnished reputation. Although Plaintiff claims that that Defendant asserted these damages "in conclusory fashion," the Court finds the presented evidence sufficient to satisfy Defendant's burden at this stage.

The court thus denies Plaintiff's motion to dismiss as to the "likeness"/"first one to tie" statements, which means Defendant's counterclaim can go forward (though of course it doesn't mean the Defendant's counterclaim will ultimately prevail).

Opinion: Arne Slot axed Liverpool star who is perfect for Andoni Iraola

📅 Nov 13, 2026  |  Category: Sports  |  Read Source →

Arne Slot's time as Liverpool's manager was fairly mixed.

He won the Premier League title during his first season in charge but then, just a year later, he was relieved of his duties.

Most fans wanted his sacking to occur months earlier but, Fenway Sports Group chose to stick by him and, luckily, he managed to get his men to qualify for the Champions League.

His fifth-place finish was clearly miles away from where Liverpool actually needed to be which is why Andoni Iraola was brought in.

And, while a lot of people thought his time at Anfield would be smooth-sailing, there are still a lot of changes he needs to make before his team are properly ready to compete for silverware again.

The main issues that need to be rectified are the performances of some players with the January transfer window the best way for them to fix the issues that they're suffering with.

But, while Slot was in charge, he had the answer to one of Iraola's main problems but, instead of keeping him around, he chose to move him on with fans now wondering why that decision was ever made.

Liverpool must figure out what they're doing with their midfield

Liverpool's main problem area among their first-team squad at the moment is their midfield.

They currently have four options to choose from as their number sixes and, while Alexis Mac Allister and Trey Nyoni have put in some solid performances, Dominik Szoboszlai and Ryan Gravenberch have both failed to do the same.

The duo were the two midfielders offered long-term contracts by FSG last season with each of them being handed bumper new deals.

Clearly, Liverpool expected them to be their regular midfield duo but, that has been far from the case.

Mac Allister and Nyoni have both performed far better than their teammates and, it feels like they should have been the ones given new contracts instead.

However, that isn't what happened and now, Iraola and Julian Ward seem to be stuck between a rock and a hard place.

So many positions within Liverpool's squad still need upgrading but, if Slot and Richard Hughes had kept one player they sold in the 2025-26 summer window, they'd have one less position to worry about.

Tyler Morton would have been perfect for Andoni Iraola

As mentioned, Liverpool's clear-out last season was taken differently depending on who you asked.

Some people believed that selling players such as Luis Diaz, Tyler Morton and Jarell Quansah was the best thing to do.

But, looking back now, all three of them had incredible campaigns last season and each of them would have been a huge help to Slot.

And, of the lot, Morton is probably the one that Liverpool wish they didn't let go, especially since he's the perfect defensive midfielder for Iraola's system.

Not only can he win duels, keep his line and add extra stability to a team but, he can also create chances and score them if needed.

Alongside all this, he's also a really hard-working central midfielder so, the fact that each of these skills line up perfectly with Iraola's system is a real kick in the teeth.

Obviously, what's done is done and these things can't be changed now but, in hindsight, selling a Liverpool-born midfielder who wanted nothing else but to play for his childhood club was maybe not the Reds' best idea.

That is, especially when they already knew that their midfield was going to be a problem for them.
