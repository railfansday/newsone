Gap names new Athleta CEO, lowers full-year guidance

📅 2026-11-19  |  Category: Business  |  Read Source →

Gap Inc. on Thursday announced Ashley Kechter will be the next president and CEO of its Athleta brand, effective Dec. 14.

Kechter comes to Gap from activewear retailer Vuori, where she was global president and oversaw the brand's global business strategy, marketing and financials. She was previously global brand president of Fabletics and began her career at Gap before that.

"I'm excited to return to Gap Inc. and partner with [Gap CEO Richard Dickson] and the team to strengthen our connection with customers and shape the brand's next chapter," Kechter said in a statement.

The announcement comes after Lululemon poached former Athleta CEO Maggie Gauger to be its chief product officer last month.

"Clearly, we're disappointed with Athleta's performance, and we recognize there's a lot of work ahead," Dickson told CNBC. "We had been in rebuild mode, but now we're really in turnaround mode."

Dickson said the company is focused on getting back to its fundamentals: fabrications, feature, form, function and fit. Dickson added that Kechter will be conducting a "comprehensive review" of the brand to reposition Athleta for future success.

While the execution of its strategy has thus far "fallen short," Dickson said this new approach will allow Athleta to fully realize its potential.

Gap also reported mixed fiscal third-quarter earnings on Thursday and posted flat overall comparable sales. Wall Street had been expecting a comparable sales increase of 1.3%, according to StreetAccount.

Net sales grew 2%, though demand softened in October and early November due to warmer weather, the company said. Store sales increased 1%, while online sales increased 3%.

Gap also said it's recorded a $420 million adjustment to cost of goods sold and a $21 million adjustment to interest income from tariff refunds so far this year.

The company lowered its full-year net sales outlook to be "roughly flat," down from previous guidance of up between 1% and 1.5%. Gap is also now expecting adjusted earnings per share of between $2.29 and $2.34, down from an earlier expectation of between $2.35 and $2.45 per share.

Dickson said in an interview that much of the lowered guidance was due to warmer weather in the first two weeks of the quarter, but he said Gap is expecting "meaningful acceleration" in shopping ahead of the peak holiday season.

"We're monitoring the trends, and we're going to remain agile as we respond to the changing business trends," Dickson said.

For the quarter, Gap reported net income of $311 million, or 86 cents per share, compared with $236 million, or 62 cents per share, the year prior. Sales rose slightly from $3.94 billion the year prior.

At Athleta, net sales were down 14% compared with last year, coming in at $220 million. Comparable sales for the banner sank 15%, which the company attributed to "limited traction" from efforts to modernize its merchandise and marketing.

The company's namesake brand saw 9% comparable sales growth for the quarter, with net sales of $1.1 billion, marking an 11% increase compared with last year. Dickson said it was the brand's 12th consecutive quarter of positive comparable sales.

"Gap is really firing on all cylinders, and the consistency of the brand is really giving us confidence in the brand's ability to continue its momentum," Dickson said.

The Old Navy banner saw flat comparable sales for the quarter and net sales of $2.3 billion, up 1% compared with last year. It's the first earnings report since the brand, which has been experiencing sluggish sales, named Michael Francis as its new CEO last quarter. Francis began his role at Old Navy on Nov. 2.

Banana Republic saw its net sales sink 3% compared with last year, which the company attributed to delayed demand for cold-weather categories and a women's fall assortment that fell flat. Wall Street had been expecting growth of 1.5%, according to StreetAccount.

Dickson said the brand has since introduced new products and seen demand "rebound" as cooler weather has set in across the country.

"We've been encouraged by the early customer response, and we should see sequential improvement in the fourth quarter," he said. "There's some work to do here, but with peak selling still ahead, I'm confident that the team is focused on the right areas and [will] ultimately position Banana well for growth."

Fear of God Fall 2027 Ready-to-Wear

📅 Nov 19, 2026  |  Category: Other  |  Read Source →

After spending almost a year with his design team in Paris, Jerry Lorenzo moved it back to Los Angeles, while continuing to develop Fear of God’s collections in Milan: “I just felt like—creatively, emotionally—I needed to be rooted in my city,” he said. The decision influenced TEN, his latest collection and the tenth numbered offering in thirteen years. “It doesn't have the cleanliness of Paris,” he acknowledged. The designer hadn't planned for the tenth collection to mark any particular milestone, but described its timing as “providential,” considering the spiritual significance of the number. Bringing together everything from tailoring to vintage-inspired sportswear, he left nothing out: Or, as he put it: “To say something that's unequivocally yours, you have to put all of you into it.”

There was no particular theme this season. Lorenzo cited everything from Keanu Reeves in flannel to Michael Jordan in a suit to Allen Iverson walking into a basketball game among the references that have long informed his work. Instead, the inspiration came from “knowing who we are,” he said. Having navigated what he described as a difficult year, he considers TEN one of his most emotional collections, shaped as much by setbacks as by successes. As a young brand, Fear of God had sometimes tried to “serve an industry,” rather than its own audience: “It's easy to get lost there,” Lorenzo said.

The tailoring was generous yet controlled: long black overcoats layered over grey waistcoats and wide trousers, cropped leather jackets paired with fluid suiting, and softly structured blazers with exaggerated shoulders. A belted black leather coat came with bright red gloves, while quilted leather jackets, faded denim, and washed workwear introduced more rugged textures. The palette moved between black, charcoal, olive, earthy browns and washed-out blue, punctuated by flashes of red. Alongside familiar wool-cashmere fabrics and American jersey, Lorenzo introduced velour, new leathers and “a lot of Japanese fabrics,” including denim and canvas. Bonded details and articulated sleeves added structure to the oversized silhouettes.

The American influence was particularly evident in Athletics, the category rooted in Lorenzo's personal approach to sportswear: “I don't ever put anything technical on,” he said of his gym wardrobe. The collection also introduces the Fear of God Trainer, the brand's first athletic shoe developed entirely in-house, with supercritical foam cushioning. Rather than relying on conspicuous branding, Lorenzo believes its identity is immediately recognizable through its design: “Shape and materials are undeniably ours.” After years of collaborations with sportswear partners, the experience brought less a lesson than a realization: “You kind of realize you could have done it on your own the whole time.”

Commerce approved sales of more than 879,000 AI chips to Chinese companies, Rep. Mast says

📅 2026-11-19  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

The subagency of the Commerce Department that regulates and enforces exports approved the sale of more than 879,000 AI chips to Chinese companies including Alibaba and Tencent as of May 2026, according to documents released Thursday by the House Foreign Affairs Committee.

Some of those approvals, made by Commerce's Bureau of Industry and Security, included sales to companies designated by the Department of Defense as "Chinese Military Companies," according to the documents. From December through May, there had been 3.4 million requested sales of U.S. AI chips to China, the committee reported, citing those made by Nvidia and Advanced Micro Devices.

"Over the past month, the administration's handling of chip export controls has put our national security and technology edge at risk. The Bureau of Industry and Security has failed to enforce the rules, and Congress must now conduct rigorous oversight of the administration's policies," Rep. Gregory Meeks, D-N.Y., the top Democrat on the House Foreign Affairs Committee, said at a meeting of the panel Thursday at which members voted to release the documents.

Spokespersons for Nvidia and AMD said their companies comply with U.S. export control laws.

Nvidia argued it was following the Commerce Department regulations that were in effect at the time.

"The review and licensing process spanned more than half a year, with approvals subject to strict conditions and limited volumes. Despite receiving some licenses, we have since stopped shipping H200s to China," Nvidia spokesperson John Rizzo told CNBC.

On Tuesday, Nvidia reported $2.4 billion in quarterly revenue from H200 and related chip sales in China. In total, customers based in China and Hong Kong made up about 9.5% of all revenue.

"AMD complies with applicable U.S. export control laws and regulations and maintains rigorous processes to ensure compliance across our global operations," AMD said through a spokesperson.

The approval of 879,000 AI chip is "not that many," Bernstein Senior Analyst Stacy Rasgon told CNBC, adding that this news is not surprising.

"It doesn't sound to me like they're approving everything they got willy nilly," Rasgon said of the Commerce Department. With 879,000 approvals out of 3.4 million requested AI chip sales, he added, "There was a process in place, and it sounds like they approved about one out of every four."

Despite a rapid succession of China export controls on AI chips that started under the Biden administration in 2022, Nvidia and AMD both re-engineered some chips to comply and be sold legally in China. And in December, Trump said Nvidia could ship H200s, its older generation Hopper chips, to "approved customers in China" if the U.S. got a 25% cut of sales.

The U.S. then licensed Nvidia H200 sales to Alibaba, Tencent, ByteDance and other Chinese companies, although sales were limited because Beijing didn't approve large-scale imports, chip analyst Patrick Moorhead of Moor Insights & Strategy told CNBC last week.

H200 sales numbers remained low due to those restrictions on China's end. Nvidia said H200 shipments to China made up less than 1% of data center revenue for the quarter that ended in July.

"In the grand context of the size of this market and how much they're shipping, it's a rounding error. It's tiny," Rasgon told CNBC.

The committee cited the Export Control Reform Act as justification for releasing the data. The panel said the law allows it to declassify and release export data if it "determines that the withholding of the following information is contrary to the national interest."

The Commerce Department did not respond to a request for comment.The license requests released by the Foreign Affairs Committee include requests to sell to major firms including Bytedance, Tesla (Shanghai) and Baidu.

The release of documents comes in the wake of Jensen Huang's resignation from the chip supplier Nvidia after news surfaced that he had several extramarital affairs, including one with a suspected Chinese spy that led to concerns about Nvidia chips being diverted to China.

In the weeks since, Republicans and Democrats in Congress have called for legislation to be passed that would impose stricter export controls on the sale of advanced AI chips to China and other adversaries.

"The license information we are disclosing today will help inform the American public about how BIS has handled licenses for Nvidia H200 and equivalent AMD chips to China. The public has the right to know how these decisions were made," Meeks said.

Mast said two of the firms listed in Nvidia license applications to BIS — Maginfra and New Matrix — are suspected "smuggling fronts." The New York Times reported in November that both companies had empty offices and had ties to companies under national security sanctions.

"Both are fake companies. If BIS approved the sale of these chips to these firms, obviously that is of tremendous concern to us — especially given that President Trump made clear last year that the sale of any AI chips to China will have very real national security conditions," Mast said at the Thursday meeting.

Maginfra and New Matrix did not reply to requests for comment.

Shares of AMD fell Thursday after the investment firm Hunterbrook Media issued a report alleging the company "has its own China problem." The report claims that "after the U.S. blacklisted Chinese server maker IEIT Systems, the company kept getting newer AMD firmware, which enables AMD chips to run in servers."

CNBC did not independently verify this information.

Fallout from the Huang scandal on Capitol Hill has intensified in the last week, as lawmakers question the degree to which the former head of Nvidia's relationship with the suspected Chinese spy could have jeopardized national security and American AI dominance.

Huang is due for a closed-door transcribed interview with the House Foreign Affairs panel and two other committees on Dec. 1. Nvidia general counsel Tim Teter is scheduled to appear before the panels Dec. 2.

Senate banking committee Chair Tim Scott, R-S.C., announced on CNBC's "Squawk Box" on Wednesday that Huang and Commerce Secretary Howard Lutnick had also agreed to testify.

Lutnick has come under fire over his relationship to Huang and for his failure to take action on Megaspeed, the Singaporean company where Alice Huang, with whom the former Nvidia CEO had an affair, was an executive until 2025. U.S. investigators suspect Megaspeed diverted Nvidia chips to China.

Amid that criticism, the Commerce Department on Monday issued an interim final rule to close a loophole that has allowed Chinese companies lease Nvidia's advancing computing power from data centers in third controls.

The rule was "written by and at the direction of" the Treasury Department and the White House National Security Council, BIS said in a press release.

Lumivero Opens SharpCloud POLI Beta for Project Portfolio Decisions

📅 Nov 19, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

SharpCloud’s POLI beta uses portfolio data to answer questions and model changes, but keeps approval with the people running the projects.

Lumivero has opened a beta of POLI, a set of AI agents built into its SharpCloud portfolio management software. The company introduced POLI on November 16 and says beta access began November 17 for customers and prospects. The agents can answer questions about projects and risks or explore the consequences of a proposed change. A person has to confirm a change before it is applied to the portfolio.

The design targets project portfolios, not investment trading. A company running several factory upgrades, for example, might use it to ask which work depends on a delayed delivery. The answer depends on connections the team has already recorded in SharpCloud. If the shared supplier or affected milestone is missing, the model cannot be relied on to know about it.

Teams typically keep schedules, risks and dependencies in separate systems. A project may appear on track in its own schedule even though a prerequisite is slipping elsewhere. SharpCloud gives teams a way to represent those relationships explicitly. In January, Lumivero announced its acquisition of SharpCloud, identifying portfolio visualization and the ability to model how one change might affect multiple projects as reasons for the deal.

POLI works on that connected portfolio model. Its five default agents are Getting Started, Comparison, What-If Impact, Story Summary and Product Knowledge. They cover different jobs, from helping a new user get oriented to comparing items or summarizing a portfolio view. Comparison can make the differences between projects easier to see. What-If Impact asks a different question: if a project assumption changes, what other parts of the modeled portfolio could be affected?

The distinction matters because dependencies are often more useful than a ranking. If a delayed equipment installation pushes back the training associated with it, the important result is not just that a milestone moved. It is which people need to adjust their plans and which assumptions they need to verify. Scenario analysis helps identify those questions without asking the system to decide what the business should do.

Lumivero says What-If Impact is read-only and does not change the data it evaluates. The company also says POLI respects existing user permissions, shows the basis for its answers and leaves proposed changes for people to approve. AI is opt-in per SharpCloud instance. These are vendor-described controls, not independent findings about how accurately the agents work on messy or incomplete data.

Human approval is most useful when an explanation can be checked against the original records. A plausible answer may use a valid but outdated estimate, or a dependency that no longer applies. A reviewer needs to see which records the assistant used and know who maintains them. An approval step provides a point of control, but it does not fix stale inputs or make every recommendation correct.

Lumivero’s own capital project decision-making report says few surveyed respondents would act on an AI recommendation without significant review. The survey comes from the vendor and does not verify POLI’s performance. It does, however, illustrate the adoption problem the product is meant to address: teams want help pulling together scattered information while retaining responsibility for any decision based on it.

Early users can test the agents on familiar examples where the project team already knows the outcome, then compare the answers with the underlying records and repeat the test with a different access level. That would show where POLI helps reduce the time spent cross-checking schedules, and where missing records still require manual investigation. The initial beta is best understood as a way to query and test a portfolio model, not a replacement for the people accountable for it. Lumivero lists separate analysis and controls modules on its roadmap for the first half of 2027; those modules are not part of the current beta.

Anthropic IPO Draws Protests Over AI Safety and Healthcare

📅 Nov 19, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

ACT UP NY and Ban RSI organized separate demonstrations in New York and San Francisco on November 19, 2026, as Anthropic entered public markets. The actions challenged AI in healthcare and recursive self-improvement, while a separate campaign pressed financial institutions over AI investment and the use of retirement savings.

ACT UP NY called supporters to 4 Times Square at 8:30 a.m. ET, where Anthropic was holding its Nasdaq IPO event. Its action notice asked members to hold an AI Out Of Our Healthcare banner, distribute flyers and potentially speak at the rally and to reporters.

ACT UP’s notice said some of the practices it opposes have already begun in hospitals and doctors’ offices. Its concerns include AI replacing live medical screening or hospital staff, influencing treatment and outcomes, training on health data, and racial, gender and anti-LGBTQ bias.

Ban RSI’s rally page scheduled a separate gathering outside Anthropic’s office at 500 Howard Street in San Francisco. The event listing set it for 11 a.m. to noon Pacific.

The listing subsequently showed 164 accounts in its Went category, a host-platform RSVP status rather than a verified crowd count. Ban RSI listed former AI researchers Jacob Coxon and Daniel Kokotajlo and AI safety educator Rob Miles as scheduled speakers.

Ban RSI defines recursive self-improvement as AI doing the work of building more powerful AI. It wants large AI companies prohibited from using systems more powerful than those available at the rally for AI research, ideally through an international treaty. Its proposed enforcement mechanism would require more powerful systems to have safeguards blocking such research even internally, red-team testing of those safeguards, and exceptions for monitoring and safety research that clearly do not increase AI capability.

The group is not demanding a unilateral pause. It wants Anthropic to announce concrete, verifiable steps toward avoiding recursive self-improvement, potentially including urging other AI companies to follow suit or lobbying for legislation.

Anthropic’s final prospectus describes closing the loop as a structural advantage: successive Claude versions assist in building the next through experiments, training-data generation and processing, evaluations, safety audits, and code writing and review.

An Anthropic Institute article says full recursive self-improvement, meaning an AI system autonomously designing and developing its own successor, has not arrived and is not inevitable. It also says such a capability could increase the risk of humans losing control. The article identifies a remaining gap in Claude’s judgment about which goals and experiments to pursue, even as the system takes on more engineering and research work.

The company says it would expect to slow down or temporarily pause if other developers at or near the frontier did so verifiably. Its proposed conditions include multiple well-resourced labs in multiple countries and systems that can confirm compliance.

Anthropic argues that a unilateral pause would accomplish less and could change the industry front-runner. That creates a narrow point of agreement with Ban RSI, which also rejects a unilateral-pause demand, but a difference over the immediate obligation: the protesters want Anthropic to announce verifiable steps itself, while the company’s stated expectation to pause depends on reciprocal action.

A separate joint petition sponsored by Action Center on Race and the Economy, Stop Funding Billionaires, Climate Defenders and other organizations calls for a boycott of Anthropic’s IPO, a pause on all AI and data-center investments, and a pledge not to use taxpayer or retirement money for an AI bailout. It is addressed to bank executives underwriting or facilitating the offering, state and local financial officers, asset managers and institutional investors, including university endowments and foundations.

The financial objections also turn attention to who can control Anthropic following its initial public offering. The final prospectus priced 980 million Class A shares at $83 each, an $81.34 billion offering. It provides for a single Class F share held by the founders’ Founder LLC to carry votes equal to 50.1% of general voting power, while the Long-Term Benefit Trust generally can elect and remove the Class T directors who constitute a majority of the board. Class A shares carry one vote each.

Those arrangements concentrate general voting power with the founders’ vehicle and board-selection power with the trust. The IPO therefore leaves a paired accountability question: whether Anthropic’s development practices can be verifiably constrained, and whether that governance structure will uphold the company’s public-benefit purpose of responsible AI development for humanity’s long-term benefit.

Form & Function Launches Lever to Give AI Assistants Shared Business Context

📅 Nov 19, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Form & Function Consulting has launched Lever, an AI platform designed to give professional services firms a consistent view of their operations across disconnected systems. Announced on November 12, the product combines operational data with company-specific business definitions so leaders can ask questions about staffing, revenue forecasts and project profitability through conversational interfaces.

The interesting part is not another chatbot answering financial questions. It is the work required before those answers become useful. A sales system can record a promising deal while a delivery team sees insufficient capacity to fulfill it. An AI assistant needs more than access to both databases to explain what that combination means for the business.

According to the launch announcement, Form & Function starts with information from customer relationship management, professional services automation and finance systems. It also gathers documents and the knowledge employees use to interpret those records. Its Lev chatbot supports stakeholder interviews that capture how a firm actually operates.

That onboarding feeds a context layer describing how work is sold, staffed, delivered and measured. The goal is to connect questions that otherwise fall between departments: whether the sales pipeline supports a hiring decision, which engagements offer the best margins, or how a delayed investment would affect projected cash flow.

This distinction matters in services businesses because similarly named metrics can conceal different assumptions. Available capacity might exclude time spent selling, while a revenue forecast might depend on both a deal’s likelihood of closing and when the resulting work can be delivered. A plausible answer based on the wrong definition can be more misleading than an obvious error.

The company’s Lever product page demonstrates Lev answering questions, generating visual output and working inside Slack. It also shows access through other AI clients, including Claude and ChatGPT, using Model Context Protocol, or MCP. Rather than requiring every interface to reconstruct the firm’s business logic, the approach supplies a shared foundation for those interactions.

The launch positioned Lever within operating-partner advisory engagements. The current product page also describes standalone availability. Pricing and detailed deployment requirements are not disclosed there, so the announcement should not be read as a self-service rollout with a published subscription price.

The broader move toward shared business meaning is also visible in Collate’s Ontology Studio announcement. Lever takes a narrower approach centered on professional services operations and consultant-assisted onboarding, rather than treating business context as a generic layer that every organization can adopt unchanged.

Form & Function reports that one client identified additional delivery capacity sufficient to support approximately $1.8 million in incremental annual revenue without adding employees. The release also describes an improvement in projected gross margins. Those are potential operating gains identified through analysis, not evidence that the client has already collected that additional revenue.

The firm’s technical discussion of semantic layers provides more context. In a company-run comparison, it tested five business questions five times each, contrasting access to raw BigQuery data with a semantic model built using Malloy. The structured approach performed better on questions requiring business definitions and relationships between tables.

That is a useful illustration of the mechanism, but it is a small vendor-run test. It does not establish perfect accuracy across arbitrary questions, changing datasets or every customer’s deployment. Defining metrics in advance reduces ambiguity; it does not eliminate the need to maintain those definitions and review consequential decisions.

Lever’s launch highlights a practical shift in enterprise AI: connecting a model to data is only the beginning. Someone must decide what the data means, resolve conflicting definitions and keep that interpretation current as contracts, staffing policies and business processes change.

For services firms, the potential benefit is a shorter path from operational records to a decision about capacity or profitability. The lasting test will be whether that shared context stays reliable as the business evolves, and whether identified opportunities turn into measurable results rather than simply more persuasive answers.

Tanium AgentSight Brings Shadow AI Into the Endpoint Inventory

📅 Nov 19, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Tanium’s AgentSight launch gives security teams a view of AI tools installed on employee devices, while agent activity tracking and broader enforcement remain on the roadmap.

Tanium introduced AgentSight at its Converge conference in Las Vegas this week, giving security teams a way to inventory AI software running on employee devices. The company says the first capabilities are available now in the Tanium console. They identify local models, coding agents, AI extensions and Model Context Protocol (MCP) servers across Windows, macOS and Linux, then associate each item with a device and the person who installed it.

That launch is narrower than the long-term product Tanium described. The first release answers what is installed and where. The company is still developing parts of the system that would explain what an agent did, flag unusual behavior or apply a policy across many devices. For organizations choosing how to govern AI, the distinction matters: a complete inventory is valuable, but it does not establish that an agent’s actions are authorized.

An enterprise can govern a company-wide AI subscription and still have limited visibility into the assistants staff install on their own workstations. An approved coding tool might sit alongside several extensions, a local model and a connector to another service. A software inventory lets IT see which devices are running those components and gives security staff a starting point for tracking down who installed them. It also helps explain why approving one service does not automatically account for every tool that ends up in a workflow.

Tanium says its initial visibility is available in on-premises and air-gapped environments as well. That matters for teams whose devices cannot send telemetry to a cloud service. The company also says cost tracking in Tanium Asset shows where AI software is deployed and what it costs. An endpoint view is useful for these questions, but it is not a substitute for records from browser-based services, cloud systems or the applications an agent uses remotely.

In the launch announcement, Tanium described a Converge demonstration of a Shadow AI page in Atlas where security teams can see changes in the inventory and ask questions in plain language. The company described additional plans to rank findings, provide explanations, and tie agent activity to the originating session and prompt. The demonstration and roadmap should not be mistaken for proof that those features are generally available or that they cover every agent or service.

Tanium also plans to let security teams define policies centrally and detect violations before they turn on enforcement. That sequence matters operationally. A local tool that looks unusual in the inventory might be part of a legitimate engineering or accessibility workflow. Reviewing those cases with the people who own the affected systems can help organizations distinguish authorized exceptions from unmanaged software without disrupting routine work.

The voluntary NIST AI Risk Management Framework puts AI risk in the context of how systems are used, not simply what is installed. For an enterprise, that means linking a device inventory to the people, permissions and business processes behind the entries. It also means deciding what information should be collected and who can review it, especially if future activity records include prompts that may contain confidential work.

The near-term test for AgentSight is whether security teams can keep an accurate inventory as employees adopt and change tools. The more demanding test is whether businesses can connect that visibility to useful decisions: which tools are approved, where an exception is justified, and who should review behavior that falls outside policy. Tanium has shipped the first part of that work. The rest will depend on both the product roadmap and how companies put it to use.

AWS Lets AgentCore Agents Buy Marketplace Products Through AWS Billing

📅 Nov 19, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Amazon Web Services announced on November 19, 2026 that Amazon Bedrock AgentCore payments can now settle purchases from x402-enabled AWS Marketplace sellers through AWS Billing. AI agents can buy software, APIs, data and content per request in US dollars, with charges added to the agent developer’s AWS bill.

For those purchases, the integration removes the need for stablecoin wallets or API keys. An optional AWS Marketplace connector for AgentCore Gateway also lets agents discover paid products and endpoints, while developers keep spending limits, seller permissions and audit records within their AWS accounts.

A purchase starts when an agent calls a seller endpoint without payment. The seller responds with HTTP 402 Payment Required and places a signed quote in the PAYMENT-REQUIRED header. The agent forwards the quote to AgentCore payments through the ProcessPayment API using the AWS_BILLING_X402 payment type. If the session has enough remaining budget, the service returns a payment token; the agent retries the request with that token in the PAYMENT-SIGNATURE header and receives the content.

At launch, AWS Billing supports the x402 exact scheme, under which the seller quotes a fixed price for each request. AWS Marketplace verifies the quote’s signature against the seller’s registered key, rejecting a quote whose price has been altered. The post says payment tokens remain valid for about five minutes, so the quote, payment and redemption steps occur in a single pass.

The first purchase from a seller creates an AWS Marketplace agreement for the developer’s AWS account, and later purchases are tracked against it. Products appear as Marketplace line items, with settlement running asynchronously before charges appear on the monthly AWS bill. Purchases are also recorded in AWS CloudTrail and AWS Cost Explorer.

Setup requires a Payment Manager with an AWS Billing connector and an AWS Marketplace service-linked role. For each task, developers can create a payment session with a spending limit and give the agent its session ID. If the remaining budget is insufficient, the payment call fails and the developer is not charged.

AgentCore policy supports additional spending limits and approved-seller lists. Organizations using Private Marketplace restrict agents to the products that private catalog allows. The open-source sample also separates permissions: its session role can set budgets but cannot pay, while its agent role can pay but cannot create sessions, preventing the agent from increasing its own budget.

The optional gateway connector exposes three read-only tools: search listings, get a listing and list fulfillment options. Listing search returns only endpoints that accept x402, while the fulfillment call supplies the seller’s endpoint URL and accepted payment protocols. Developers can bypass discovery by setting a seller URL directly; the agent then pays only that endpoint.

AWS’s walkthrough uses a Strands research agent answering a financial analyst’s question about agentic-payment announcements outside the model’s training data. The agent finds Tavily’s live-search listing, requests its x402 endpoints, obtains a quote and pays for one request. Its answer includes sources and identifies what it purchased and how much it cost.

The sample defaults to a $0.05 session budget and a $0.02 cap per request. In AWS’s documented test, the agent compared two listings before completing one $0.01 payment.

For AWS customers, the launch connects product discovery and per-request payment to established cloud billing and governance controls. An agent’s purchasing autonomy is therefore bounded by the specific session budget, approved sellers, role permissions and Marketplace catalog access its developers configure.

GPS, Timezone and IP Are Three Different Things: Testing Geo Behaviour on Real Phones

📅 Nov 19, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Posted on Nov 19 • Originally published at robotactions.com

Short answer: you set your test phone's GPS to Paris, and your checkout still shows prices in Canadian dollars. That is because GPS is only one of the signals that say where a phone is. The server it talks to reads its IP address. The app reads its clock. The app store reads the account. If you want to see what a user in Paris sees, those signals have to agree on one city, and each one is set in a different place. This post goes through each signal, what it controls, how to set it on a real Android phone or iPhone, and how to check that nothing leaked.

Two more signals are worth knowing because they cannot be changed by any of the above:

Most "it works in testing but not for users in Brazil" bugs come from testing one signal and assuming the others followed.

GPS and API responses can be faked from inside your own test setup: you mock the location, or you intercept the response from your geo-lookup service and return a different country. That covers anything your app decides.

It does not cover decisions made before your code runs:

To test those, the traffic has to actually leave from that country. That means a proxy.

Use this whether you test on your own phones or in a device cloud.

In both cases the proxy applies to that Wi-Fi network only. Mobile data still goes out directly.

If your provider gives you a SOCKS5 proxy, prefer the socks5h:// form where your tool accepts it. With socks5h, DNS lookups are done by the proxy, in the target country. With plain socks5, the phone or your tool resolves hostnames locally first, which can send it to a CDN node near you, not near the exit IP, and quietly undo the test.

A phone's proxy setting is a request, not a rule. Apps and system services that open their own connections can ignore it:

So "the browser shows a New York IP" is not proof that your app's traffic left from New York. If the behaviour you are testing comes from one of these paths, check that path specifically: watch the connections the phone opens while you use the app, and look for any that went straight to the internet.

RobotActions runs real Android and iOS devices in our cloud, and we added proxy routing for this kind of test. You bring your own HTTP(S) or SOCKS5 proxy from any provider and pay them directly. We do not sell proxies. Paste it once in Settings, Proxies (in any of the usual formats, including a provider's host:port:user:pass line or a whole curl -x command), click Test to see the exit IP and city, and save it. The password is stored encrypted and never shown again. On a device, Location & Network applies it, checks it before the phone changes, and can move the GPS and timezone to the proxy's exit city in one click. On Android the exit IP is also read back from inside the phone. Releasing the device removes the proxy and puts GPS and timezone back. Proxy routing is on the paid plans.

AI agents get the same thing as MCP tools:

On iPhone, fullDevice: true is opt-in and sends every app and system service through your proxy, not just the ones that honour the proxy setting. While it is on, UDP traffic such as calls and some games stops. ios_proxy_leak_check watches for 5 to 60 seconds while you use the app and lists the connections that went around the proxy. Both proxy and timezone are cleared automatically when the device is released.
