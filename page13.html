Michael Burry Says He's Looking at Netflix Stock in the High $30s. I'd Consider It Now, Well Above That.

📅 Nov 21, 2026  |  Category: Business  |  Read Source →

The famous investor is eyeing a price around a third lower. Is it worth waiting for?

By Daniel Sparks – Nov 21, 2026 at 6:22PM EST

Netflix (NFLX -2.96%) has Michael Burry's attention. Just not at today's price.

That's a long way down. Netflix shares closed at $58.46 on Thursday, Nov. 19, their lowest close since May 2024 (using split-adjusted prices, because Netflix did a 10-for-1 stock split in November 2025).

With the stock trading near $58 as of this writing, a price in the high $30s would cut about another third off the shares.

I don't think investors have to wait for that. At today's price, Netflix already looks cheap for a company whose profits are still climbing.

Michael Burry. Image source: Getty Images.

Burry has been circling Netflix for months. In July, he figured that Netflix at $67.75 per share traded at about 1.6 times its intrinsic value, which comes out to a value of roughly $42 a share by his math. He also said then that the stock would have to drop to the low $50s to get closer to an ideal buying opportunity.

So, the high $30s isn't a random number. It would let him buy under his own estimate of what the company is worth.

His concern is how long Netflix's content keeps its value. In July, he compared the streaming giant to Walt Disney and its Pixar studio, whose content he called evergreen. Much of what Netflix makes, he suggested, is OK for now but doesn't improve with age.

Sure, that's a fair worry about any business built on hit shows. But it's a long-term debate. In the meantime, Netflix's profits keep growing, making the high $30s look like a really pessimistic price.

Call the high $30s $38 a share. On average, analysts expect Netflix to earn about $3.80 per share in 2027, so a $38 stock would cost about 10 times next year's expected earnings. At around $58, shares trade at about 15 times next year's expected earnings.

By comparison, the S&P 500 (^GSPC -3.70%) traded at 17.7 times its expected earnings for the next 12 months on Nov. 5, according to FactSet.

Said another way, Netflix stock already costs less than the average stock relative to its earnings. Burry's price would put it at a discount of over 40% to the market's price-to-earnings ratio.

For $38 to be a market-average price, Netflix would have to earn only about $2.15 per share. It earned $2.53 per share in 2025 (split-adjusted too), and its earnings per share have grown year over year in every quarter of 2026 so far.

The high $30s would only make sense, then, if Netflix's profits were about to shrink.

They don't look close to shrinking. Netflix's operating margin was 26.7% in 2024 and 29.5% in 2025, and the business is targeting 31.5% for 2026. Its third-quarter operating margin hit 34.6%. And management is guiding for a 26% margin in the fourth quarter, above the 24.5% Netflix reported a year before, even with costs from newly announced job cuts. All told, Netflix says its outlook implies operating income growth of over 20% this year.

Also, Netflix keeps returning cash to shareholders. It spent $4.8 billion buying back its own stock in the third quarter alone, and it still had $22.3 billion in repurchase authorizations left.

Sales growth, though, keeps slowing. Revenue growth has fallen every quarter this year, to 11.8% year over year in the third quarter from 16.2% in the first. And management expects around 11.5% for the fourth quarter, while it has lowered its 2026 ad revenue outlook to about $2.7 billion from about $3 billion.

But a business still growing sales by double digits while raising its operating margin looks unlikely to wind up earning less than it did in 2025.

Could Burry still get his price? He might. Netflix stock is already down over half from its June 2025 record, and a stock that's fallen this far can keep falling.

But waiting for the high $30s is arguably a bet that Netflix's earnings stall. At around 15 times next year's expected earnings, Netflix stock already prices in a lot of caution, I think. I'd consider buying shares now rather than waiting for Burry's number.

AppLovin vs. Reddit: Which Technology Stock Is a Better Buy for 2027?

📅 Nov 21, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

AppLovin is growing fast and is highly profitable, but its stock has been volatile. Reddit is profitable and still growing, but Google's AI search is sending it fewer visitors.

By Sara Appino – Updated Nov 21, 2026 at 6:19PM EST

The digital advertising landscape is shifting as data-driven platforms fight for marketing budgets. Choosing between AppLovin (APP -8.11%) and Reddit (RDDT -5.06%) requires weighing a seasoned software powerhouse against a rapidly growing social platform.

AppLovin specializes in ad-tech software that helps developers grow their apps, while Reddit operates a massive network of user-generated communities. Both companies are successfully capitalizing on specialized data to attract advertisers, but they operate with different business models and cost structures.

AppLovin provides a software-based marketing platform and advertising solutions for consumer brands and gaming apps. The company notes that some of its customers are also competitors, such as Meta Platforms, Alphabet, Amazon (AMZN -4.10%), and Unity Software (U -4.18%). Following the June 2025 divestiture of its Apps business, the company focuses entirely on its high-margin software platform.

In the 12 months ended Sept. 30, 2026, revenue reached nearly $7.6 billion, representing growth of approximately 46.5% compared to the prior period. Net income was roughly $5.0 billion during this time frame, supported by a strong net margin of 65.9%. This performance highlights the company's ability to scale its ad-tech infrastructure efficiently while maintaining significant profitability.

As of its December 2025 balance sheet, the debt-to-equity ratio was 1.7x, which compares total debt to shareholder equity. The current ratio was 3.3x, a measure of the ability to pay short-term bills using current assets. In the fiscal year ended Dec. 31, 2025, free cash flow, which is cash from operations minus capital expenditures, was nearly $3.9 billion.

Reddit is establishing itself as a unique player among communication stocks by leveraging its vast array of user-generated communities. The company generates roughly 94% of its revenue from third-party advertisers, though it is expanding into content licensing for AI model training. While it serves a global audience, Reddit faces some customer concentration, with its top ten advertisers accounting for approximately 21% of total revenue.

In the 12 months ended Sept. 30, 2026, revenue reached nearly $3.1 billion, fueled by growth of approximately 62.2%. Net income was close to $991.7 million, marking a significant transition to profitability compared to previous years. The platform continues to focus on increasing user engagement and expanding its data-licensing partnerships to diversify its income streams.

As of its December 2025 balance sheet, the debt-to-equity ratio was 0.0x, while the current ratio was 11.6x, measuring the company's ability to cover short-term debts with current assets. In the fiscal year ended Dec. 31, 2025, free cash flow was close to $684.2 million. Note that stock-based compensation represented roughly 49.7% of operating cash flow, which inflates reported cash generation since SBC is a non-cash expense added back in the cash flow statement.

AppLovin faces significant risks related to its reliance on third-party platforms like Apple and Alphabet, which can alter access to user data through privacy changes. The company also deals with intense competition from large technology firms, including Amazon and Unity Software, in the mobile app ecosystem. Furthermore, revenue is heavily concentrated in the gaming sector, leaving the business vulnerable to shifts in that specific market.

Reddit relies heavily on advertising revenue, making the company vulnerable to volatility in marketing budgets and user engagement levels. The platform depends on volunteer moderators to maintain its communities and relies on cloud service providers like Amazon and Alphabet for its core infrastructure. Changes in search engine algorithms or a decline in user growth could significantly impact its site traffic and overall business results.

Reddit appears to trade at a more attractive valuation based on its Forward P/E and P/S ratio, which measures market capitalization against sales over the past twelve months.

Valuation metrics include sourcing from Financial Modeling Prep (FMP) and may differ from other data providers.

I'd go with AppLovin. It has built a leading, highly profitable ad business in mobile games, and it is just getting started in online shopping. Advertisers pay it for results like installs and purchases, so it grows when its customers do. Last quarter, sales jumped 55% year over year, and management guided for another strong quarter, exceeding analyst expectations.

The stock has been volatile this year as the company faced short-seller attacks and investor lawsuits it disputes. But the latest quarter beat the company's own targets by a wide margin, and the stock is still down for the year, which gives long-term investors a lower entry point.

To give Reddit its due, it is very profitable and its sales are still growing fast. But Google's AI search is sending it fewer visitors, its U.S. users fell last quarter, and it warned of more pressure ahead, which sent the stock plunging. AppLovin faces no such traffic problem, since its ads run inside other companies' mobile apps, and advertisers are spending more with it. With fast growth, big profits, and a whole new market opening up in online shopping, AppLovin is my pick for 2027.

Forget Butter And Oil: Cook Steak In This Rich Ingredient For A Better Crust & Beefier Flavor

📅 Nov 21, 2026  |  Category: Other  |  Read Source →

When preparing steak, cooks often focus on selecting the best cut, then preparing it with a specific method to an ideal doneness. Less emphasis is placed on the fat employed for the task — although what you pour into the pan also impacts both the flavor and texture of your meal. So skip standard pantry staples like butter and oil, and try a version with beef tallow instead.

So — what is beef tallow? It's essentially rendered beef fat, an ingredient that lends numerous advantages to steak night. Foremost is the ultra-savory profile: Tallow is made by slowly cooking down beef trimmings, resulting in a rich, meaty taste. This staple works as a flavor enhancer in addition to a cooking medium, complementing the deep, savory character of steak. And if you buy a higher-quality grass-fed variety, you may impart even more complexity, introducing earthy and grassy notes to your dining experience.

In parallel, beef tallow helps create a mouthwatering crust. The ingredient boasts a higher smoke point than butter and some other fats. So whether you're searing on high heat or finishing the steak in the oven, the fat can withstand high temperatures, enabling browning and Maillard reactions that create a terrific crust. That adds up to an elevated steak experience, all from swapping out the fat that goes into the pan.

Preparing a steak with beef tallow doesn't require any special accommodations. The fat is solid at room temperature and can be shelf-stable when properly stored, making it an easy pantry addition. You'll only need about a tablespoon per portion: Simply spoon the tallow into the skillet, where it'll melt and cover the cooking surface. It's a cooking medium that's especially effective on cast iron — whether a griddle or a pan — as it can also be used to season the cookware. You can also use tallow when grilling by coating both the cut itself and the grill grates with a thin layer of beef fat.

Any steak cut can be prepared in tallow; just note that it lends a rich flavor. So if you're making a mild-tasting filet mignon, for example, the addition could be overpowering. It's better to pair tallow with robust cuts like a ribeye, porterhouse, or New York strip, thereby producing an especially beef-forward meal. Otherwise, there's no need to worry about the details. Simply add a spoonful to your pan and sear away; this staple makes for a dependably delicious steak experience.

New Research Finds Common ADHD Classroom Accommodations May Not Actually Help

📅 Nov 21, 2026  |  Category: Health  |  Read Source →

For children with ADHD, the support they receive at school may not always make the difference parents and teachers hope for.

Giving children with attention deficit hyperactivity disorder (ADHD) more time to finish assignments, frequent breaks, or a quieter place to work might seem like straightforward ways to help them succeed in school. These adjustments are widely used in classrooms, yet new research suggests they may do little to improve academic performance or behavior.

A study from Florida International University (FIU), published in JAMA Pediatrics, found that four common accommodations did not produce significant improvements in children with ADHD. The results raise questions about how schools support students whose difficulties with attention, behavior, and completing schoolwork can interfere with their education.

“We were surprised that none of the accommodations we tested had a significant effect, even when combined with behavioral interventions or medication,” said Gregory Fabiano, a psychology professor at FIU and the study’s lead author.

Researchers at FIU’s Center for Children and Families conducted a randomized clinical trial involving 255 children with ADHD, ranging from kindergarten through sixth grade. The children entered the study between 2022 and 2025, and 235 completed it.

The research took place through FIU’s Summer Treatment Program, which provides structured classroom and recreational activities for children with ADHD.

Researchers tested four accommodations commonly offered in schools: extended time on assignments, frequent breaks, standing while working at a desk, and completing work in a low-distraction environment. They measured how these adjustments affected classroom rule violations, academic productivity, and quiz scores under different treatment conditions.

The results revealed clear differences between the commonly used accommodations and treatments designed to address ADHD symptoms:

The distinction matters because schools frequently include accommodations in Individualized Education Programs (IEPs) and Section 504 plans, which are designed to meet students’ individual educational needs. Although these adjustments may make classroom tasks more manageable for some children, the findings suggest that routinely providing them does not necessarily improve measurable academic outcomes or behavior.

“These approaches fail to build skills or address motivation,” Fabiano said.

Rather than relying heavily on accommodations, researchers recommend placing greater emphasis on behavioral strategies supported by evidence. These include setting clear expectations, providing consistent feedback, praising positive behavior, and rewarding students for meeting personalized goals.

One approach is the use of daily report cards, which allow teachers to monitor specific behaviors and give students regular feedback on their progress.

The findings do not mean accommodations are never useful. Individual students may benefit from particular adjustments, even if those approaches did not produce significant improvements across the children studied.

“There may be times when accommodations may be helpful, but a ‘one size fits all’ approach to ADHD doesn’t seem to be effective,” he said.

The research builds on decades of work by the late William E. Pelham Jr., the founding director of FIU’s Center for Children and Families, who helped design and conduct the study.

A pioneer in ADHD treatment, Pelham also led landmark research showing that stimulant medication improved children’s classroom productivity without significantly improving how much they learned. That earlier finding highlights an important distinction between helping students complete schoolwork and helping them understand and retain information.

The latest study also has limitations. Researchers conducted the trial in structured summer treatment classrooms rather than ordinary schools, where classroom conditions, teaching practices, and available support may differ. Additional research is needed to determine whether the results hold in everyday educational settings.

For Fabiano, the findings underscore the need to reconsider which forms of support schools prioritize for children with ADHD.

“We need to rethink how we’re supporting these students, with greater emphasis on effective treatments in everyday classrooms rather than relying so heavily on accommodations. Every child with ADHD deserves the opportunity to succeed and feel proud of what they’ve accomplished in the classroom, and it is our job to make sure we give them the best chance for that,” Fabiano said.

If a bank turned your business down, apply for an SBA microloan of up to $50,000 from a nonprofit lender on the SBA’s list of microlenders. These lenders also coach borrowers on marketing and management. The money can buy equipment, inventory, or supplies, but it can’t pay off debt or buy real estate.

The SBA funds these lenders but doesn’t review your credit. Each lender decides who qualifies and sets your rate and terms.

A microloan tops out at $50,000, carries a fixed rate, and must be repaid within seven years. The SBA says rates usually run 8% to 13%. The business can be brand new, but it must be a for-profit small business in the U.S. Since April 2026, an SBA policy also requires every owner to be a U.S. citizen or national whose main home is in the U.S., which rules out green card holders.

SBA rules let a lender go past $20,000 only after you show that the business can’t get credit elsewhere at a comparable rate and that it’s likely to succeed. A bank’s rejection in writing helps you show the first.

The money can also serve as working capital, the cash that runs the business day to day, or pay for furniture, fixtures, and machinery.

Say a mobile dog groomer turned down by her bank borrows $18,000 at a fixed 9% over five years to outfit a van. Her payment would be about $374 a month, and she’d pay about $4,420 in interest over the loan.

The coaching can start before you borrow. Lenders can spend up to half of their SBA coaching grants on people who haven’t borrowed yet.

Find lenders on the SBA’s list of microlenders, filtered to your state, and call two or three for help with your application. Ask each for its rate, what property it would hold as collateral, and whether you’d sign a personal guarantee, which makes you personally responsible for the loan.

7 Classic Fantasy Books That Are Even Bigger Today

📅 Nov 21, 2026  |  Category: Business  |  Read Source →

Beatriz (Bea) Valero de Urquia is a Books & Literature author at Collider, where she focuses on Lists surrounding recent bestsellers, classical novels, and everything in between. She is a historian by training and a bilingual reader, moving between English and Spanish literature.   Beatriz began her career as a technology writer at a startup in the UK. Almost a decade later, journalism has taken her to five cities across three continents and into business reporting, cultural criticism, and live TV commentary. She joined Collider in 2026.  Beatriz has a passion for travelling and loves keeping an eye on the books headed to the screen. One of her favorite pastimes is critiquing the historical accuracy of period movies. She also once volunteered for the Charles Dickens Museum. Beatriz also has a core belief that a book is always better than the movie, but she has been happy to have been proven wrong in a couple of occasions.

When she isn’t reading, Beatriz is watching musicals, stage managing Shakespeare plays with a local theater group or doing historical research. She enjoys tracing family trees for her history and genealogy TikTok, creating databases for historical analysis and sifting through 17th-century stories for the novel she is currently in the process of writing.

Just like our favorite characters, some books need time to find their place in the world. The author might not be well known, or perhaps they were born in a non-English-speaking country. Some books are so long they can be intimidating, particularly when they introduce readers to a complex magical system. But when given the chance — through word of mouth, a movie adaptation, or just the passing of time — great books bloom.

The books on this list are far greater today than they were when they were first published. Most were never truly obscure, but they are or have been cultural phenomena in a way that few fantasy stories are able to achieve. They are classics that have stood the test of time, as new generations grow into them. If you are a fan of fantasy novels, it is likely that the reason lies in one of these series.

Terry Pratchett was the top-selling and highest-earning author in the UK in 1996. It is therefore hard to imagine a time when his novels didn’t sell all that well. When the author’s publisher, Colin Smythe Ltd, released the first novel in what would become the Discworld series, it only printed 506 copies, the majority going to libraries. The US printed about 4,000 copies, and the author suffered poorer sales in North America until the early 2000s.

The first Discworld book follows the adventures of the hopeless wizard Rincewind, who is forced to be a guide for Twoflower and show him a flat planet balanced on the backs of four elephants standing on a giant turtle. From that description alone, one grasps the type of humor Pratchett uses throughout, and what makes it so brilliant. The main characters’ contrasting personalities also carry the story on their backs. "Twoflower was a tourist, the first ever seen on the discworld,” the book says. “Tourist, Rincewind had decided, meant 'idiot'."

What happens when an author dies before finishing his best-selling, fan-adored fantasy book series? In this case, another would-be best-selling, fan-adored fantasy book author picks up the pen and does the job. The writer chosen to complete Robert Jordan’s The Wheel of Time series, which began with The Eye of the World, was Brandon Sanderson. Prime Video’s TV show adaptation also helped propel its popularity and introduced it to a new generation of readers, 30 years after the book’s release.

Rand, Perrin, and Matrim are just three young men living in the small village of Emond’s Field. The possibility of being the Dragon Reborn is the furthest thing they could ever imagine. And yet, when a mysterious woman, a member of the powerful Aes Sedai, arrives at the village, and shortly after it is attacked by Trollocs, the possibility starts to become more likely. The three of them then agree to leave alongside their friend Egwene. Their travels will see them separate and reunite, as the four characters struggle to learn who they are and how to fight the Dark One.

If there is a living titan of the fantasy genre, that would be Brandon Sanderson. In addition to completing The Wheel of Time series, Sanderson has written over 60 books, including roughly 30 novels, alongside numerous novellas, short stories, and graphic novels. Most of them are set in the Cosmere universe. More impressively, he has become an undisputed best-selling author in the US and abroad, and it all started with Mistborn.

The premise for Mistborn: The Final Empire was simple yet innovative: What happens if the villain wins the war? The book is set in the world of Scadrial, within the Cosmere universe. At the start of the story, the Final Empire is ruled by the Lord Ruler. It is a society where humans are divided into nobility and the oppressed skaa, and certain people called Allomancers are able to use magic by ingesting certain metals. When the half-skaa Kelsier and his crew find Vin, a street urchin with Allomantic powers, and develop a plan to rob the empire, the regime’s foundations start to shake.

Before it was Studio Ghibli’s beloved 2004 animated movie, Howl's Moving Castle was an 18-year-old children’s fantasy book, written by the British author Diana Wynne Jones. In a note at the front of the 1986 novel, Jones explains that it was a child at a school she was visiting who asked her to write a book about a moving castle. The Oscar-nominated movie pushed the story into the limelight and sent a wave of readers searching for more information about their lives and adventures.

The story follows Sophie, a young woman who is turned into a crone by the powerful Witch of the Waste. Embarrassed, she leaves her home and gets employment as the cleaning lady for the famous wizard Howl, who lives in a mechanical moving castle. It is the same premise as that of the Japanese adaptation. However, in the novel, everything is turned up. Book Howl is more of a coward, while Sophie is wittier. Moreover, the plot takes a wild detour into modern-day Wales that Miyazaki dropped entirely.

Meet Geralt of Rivia. If you don’t know him already, he is a witcher, a magical monster hunter who travels across the world killing dangerous creatures for money. In The Last Wish, Geralt is recovering from battle wounds in a temple, remembering several recent events from his life, such as the time he acted as a bodyguard at a royal banquet that ended up in a surprise engagement, or when he was asked to lift a king’s daughter’s curse, which turned her into a vicious striga.

The Last Wish is a collection of fantasy short stories written by Andrzej Sapkowski and published in the early 1990s. For the first decade after its publication, Geralt of Rivia’s fame was contained within Central and Eastern Europe. The short story collection was only translated into English in 2007. It became a worldwide phenomenon after it was adapted into a video game and, later, became a hit Netflix series originally starring Henry Cavill. Today, CD Projekt Red's The Witcher 3: Wild Hunt has sold more than 60 million copies, making Geralt one of gaming's most recognizable faces.

"Are people born wicked? Or do they have wickedness thrust upon them?" This is the question that Glinda the Good Witch poses at the start of the Tony Award-winning musical Wicked, which tells the story of Elphaba and how she became the Wicked Witch of the West. The musical is a loose (and very sanitized) adaptation of Gregory Maguire's novel Wicked, itself a prequel to L. Frank Baum's 1900 children's novel The Wonderful Wizard of Oz. The book was published to mixed reviews and modest sales.

However, the musical and, later, Jon M. Chu’s two-part movie adaptation starring Cynthia Erivo and Ariana Grande made the novel a bestseller. Gregory Maguire’s version of Elphaba’s story is dark and very adult. While the musical and Baum’s original story are beloved by children, Maguire’s take is densely detailed, intensely political, very cynical, and much more sexual. Glinda has a much smaller role to play, and there is little of the whimsy of Baum’s world. For those ready to delve into a story about the nature of evil and political radicalization set in a fantasy world, it is a fascinating and worthwhile read.

The first book in the series that created phrases like “winter is coming” or “sweet summer child” barely sold a few thousand copies upon release. It seems unthinkable today, but for 15 years, A Song of Ice and Fire was known mostly within fantasy book circles. George R.R. Martin's book signings drew few fans of the story that follows Ned Stark’s family and the power struggle for the Iron Throne in a history-inspired fantasy realm. It was not until HBO turned it into a TV show that it became a worldwide hit.

Fans are still waiting for The Winds of Winter and the conclusion to the book series, but the story that started it all has never been bigger. Despite its divisive final season, the show is still considered one of the best of all time, earning 59 Primetime Emmy Awards, spurring two prequel shows (House of the Dragon and A Knight of the Seven Kingdoms), and drawing millions of fans across the world to pick up a fantasy book for perhaps the first time.
