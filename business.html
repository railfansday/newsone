‘The Incomer’ Attracted Domhnall Gleeson and Moby and Got Its Creator on the BAFTA Breakthrough List. Now, He’s Working on a Plan B Film.

📅 Nov 04, 2026  |  Category: Business  |  Read Source →

Scottish writer-director Louis Paxton takes THR through his feature debut, how much it reflects real-life experiences and what’s next for him.

Scottish writer-director Louis Paxton’s debut feature film, the dark comedy-drama The Incomer, has star power – in the form of Domhnall Gleeson (Star Wars sequel trilogy), Gayle Rankin (House of the Dragon), John Hannah (The Mummy franchise) and others. It also has an oddball, quirky vibe. And it has the backing of Focus Features, Universal Pictures International and Moby.

What else can you ask for!? Well, on Wednesday, the up-and-coming creative was unveiled as being among the names featured on the 2026 U.K. BAFTA Breakthrough list, supported by Netflix. The Incomer is set on a remote Scottish island and follows siblings who have lived in peaceful isolation for decades, hunting seabirds, chatting to mythic creatures, and defending their isle from dreaded Incomers. Their world is upended with the arrival of Daniel (Gleeson), an awkward council worker, tasked with moving them to the mainland.

Paxton talked to THR about the star-studded offbeat movie, how Moby got involved, his hopes for the BAFTA Breakthrough experience and what else is next for him.

“I’m thrilled to be part of BAFTA Breakthrough, and I’m really excited to meet the cohort, particularly all the other creatives from all of these different mediums, including games,” the filmmaker tells THR. “It will also be great to have the chance just to sit with people who are more experienced than me and have been working for a while, just ask them about their career and how it works.”

The Incomer is infused with a feel and stories he knows. “My mother’s family is from the Northern Isles of Scotland. They’re from Orkney,” Paxton tells THR. “I grew up in Edinburgh, but my mother moved back there about 10 years ago. We grew up going to these islands, which are unique. They are a very beautiful, quite remote part of Scotland, but have a very specific identity and culture.”

For example? “I went to other islands where people genuinely survived by eating birds,” he shares. “And at some point, these people had to leave, and I was just fascinated by this. What a big decision that would be if that was where your family had lived their entire lives and your heritage was that. Even if it was going to make your life better, how hard would it be to leave that behind?”

Was his youth as surreal as some of the scenes in the film? “I would say about me and my sister that there are parts of the sibling relationship that made it into the film,” Paxton explains. “When we were kids, we really knew how to push each other’s buttons and annoy each other, and so things could escalate from zero to 100 really quickly. And we definitely had some terrible fights when we were young.”

The creative credits casting director Kharmel Cochrane with bringing together a dream ensemble. “We wanted to work with as many Scottish actors as we could, and if they weren’t Scottish, that they were very good at doing a Scottish accent,” he recalls. “And thankfully Domhnall does an exceptional Scottish accent.”

His key to attracting all this talent was the script. “I spent a long time working on the script,” Paxton highlights. “And it was really reassuring in this age of IP and big projects and really humbling that a complete nobody, who’d only made short films, could write a script and that actors would want to be part of it just on the strength of the script.”

So, how did Moby’s production company Little Walnut Productions end up backing The Incomer? “They had been funding films and making films that were largely activist films,” Paxton says. “And then they’d widened their remit to include films that embodied the values that they had as people. They read The Incomer and just really liked it.”

Did Daniel being vegan have to do with that? “It’s funny because people have asked if the veganism in the film came after. Did I add that after Moby came on board?” Paxton says with a laugh. “But it wasn’t. It was already a big part of the film, and I think maybe he appreciated that. But Moby and [creative partner] Lindsay [Hicks] liked the film generally. And they were the best partners to work with – just excellent and really supportive.”

Can fans of The Incomer expect more from Paxton ahead? “It’s all about film for me, film and TV,” he tells THR. “I’ve always wanted to be a filmmaker. I’ve signed with CAA in the U.S., and I’ve been having really exciting conversations about my next projects. I’m writing and directing two projects which are U.K.-set.”

And he can share this much: “One’s a horror comedy, and the other is more of a straight comedy. But I’m also attached to direct a film called The Waffle House Index, which is a Plan B project,” meaning from Brad Pitt’s production company, in which French company Mediawan owns a majority stake.“We’ve got Inde Navarrette as the lead, and Apple are the studio we’re making it with, and we’re really excited about that,” Paxton says. “That’ll be shooting next year. I think that’s most likely to be my next project. I didn’t write it; Andrew Nunnelly wrote the script. It was on the Black List and is a terrific script – really original, really different. It feels totally in line with the kind of projects or films that people are really responding to at the moment. I’m so excited to be a part of that.”

US midterm election results: How Americans voted and what the data tells us

📅 Nov 04, 2026  |  Category: Business  |  Read Source →

Results are starting to come in from across the US, where people voted on their next Congress. DW takes a look at who voted Republican, who voted Democrat and what it all means.

We will update this article throughout the night as more data becomes available, so keep checking back.

In the 2026 midterms, Americans voted on all 435 members of the House of Representatives and 35 members of the Senate. Before election night, Republicans held the majority in both Congress chambers, if only by a hair. In 36 US states, voters also decided on who will become the next governor.

Midterm elections are held every four years, at the exact halfway point between presidential elections. The results will influence US politics for the next few years. Despite the significant executive power delegated to the presidency according to the US Constitution, the majority of federal political decisions have to be approved by Congress before they can be implemented. If the Democrats win the majority in one or both chambers of Congress, it will be harder for President Donald Trump to govern as he chooses.

Because the 50 US states lie across six time zones in total, results will trickle in over time, starting with the US East Coast and going west from there. DW follows the Associated Press (AP) for when a seat is called for Republicans or Democrats.

Polling stations in several eastern states closed at 1 a.m. local time. For more updates, go to our US midterms live blog.

In the US Senate, Democrats retained several of their seats on the East Coast, with Virginia being the first to be called. North Carolina was the first Republican Senate seat, which the Democrats were able to flip.

In the House of Representatives, all 435 seats are up for election.

The data on voter demographics and attitudes we use is based on a representative survey of more than 75,000 verified voters conducted by survey and market research firm SSRS on behalf of AP and other news organizations. It builds on interviews of both early and mail-in voters as well as exit polls of in-person voters on Election Day, November 3. Where percentages of Republican and Democratic voters don't add up to 100%, the remaining percent were voters who voted for third-party candidates.

One of the most important factors in this year's midterms is the economy. Prices for gas have increased significantly since the start of the Iran war, and everyday goods have become more and more expensive across the country as well. Almost half of all Americans say their family's financial situation is worse than two years ago.

That sentiment is not the same across the board though. Among those saying their family is better off than two years ago, 75% voted Republican and 23% Democrat. Among those saying they are worse off, only 24% voted Republican and 72% Democrat. President Trump has been saying that the country is on the upswing.

While not on the ballot, Trump is still a strong presence in the midterms: The midterms are considered a referendum on how he and his Republicans governed the country for the past two years. Most recently, Trump's approval ratings have fallen to the lowest he has ever had, with just under 30% of Americans saying they think he is doing a good job.

In recent history, only George W. Bush has fared worse, with 25% at the end of his second term. During the 2006 midterm election, Bush's Republican Party lost control of both chambers of Congress.

Exit polls showed that half of voters saw the 2026 midterm elections as an opportunity to rebuke the president. Three-quarters of US voters said they are "angry" or "dissatisfied" with the way the country is headed.

According to the AP Voter Poll, dissatisfaction was very high in Democratic-leaning states such as California and Massachusetts. However, even in Republican-leaning states, a majority of voters were at least "dissatisfied."

Before Tuesday's election, Republicans (and independents who caucus with them) held 53 of 100 seats in the Senate and 220 of 435 seats in the House. That means they only held two more seats than the 218 required for a majority. In recent years, such razor-thin majorities have been the lay of the land, especially in the House of Representatives.

Dragon Ball's New Anime Proves That Toei Is Holding The Franchise Back

📅 Nov 04, 2026  |  Category: Business  |  Read Source →

People who grew up loving Dragon Ball are currently able to enjoy the weekly releases of the Japanese version of Dragon Ball Super: Beerus series, but the hype for the improved anime could be higher. Revised animations, pacing, and entirely new sequences have made the newest release more than a standard remaster. But that hasn't stopped Toei, the anime's producers, from making the same mistakes it's been repeating for more than 13 years.

The Battle of Gods movie and Dragon Ball Super anime series helped make Akira Toriyama's franchise more popular than ever. Their dramatic success proved the world still had a big appetite for more adventures with Goku and his friends. And while the Dragon Ball Super: Broly and Super Hero movies delivered in many ways, those who control the rights and production schedules of the beloved franchise have failed to capitalize on that success, holding the franchise back from reaching its full potential.

During the premiere of Dragon Ball Super's final episodes, which featured the incredibly epic conclusion of the universal Tournament of Power, fans flooded the streets of major cities around the world to cheer and enjoy the series together. The anime garnered that sort of reception with the release of the Japanese version of the series, but one can't help but wonder if the hype would have been even greater if the series' largest markets released their dubs at the same time in movie theaters.

Judging by how the world's most popular anime streaming platform, Crunchyroll, has experienced crashes during several anticipated Dragon Ball Super moments, it's clear that a lot of fans around the world are more than willing to watch the series with subtitles. However, there is still a large percentage of hardcore Dragon Ball fans who prefer to wait and watch the franchise's newest releases with the iconic voice actors they grew up with. Unfortunately for them, some of the wait times for those dubs have been much longer than others.

The Japanese language version of the Dragon Ball Super anime premiered in July 2015, with the English version not releasing until January 2017. That was a considerable amount of time for arguably the world's most popular anime series to release its English dub, one of the franchise's largest markets/demographics. Because of the separate release schedules, hype for new releases can be hindered, compared to if Toei made sure the largest markets around the world had their dubs ready so that new Dragon Ball content could be released at the same time in multiple languages.

It's taken little time for the newest Dragon Ball anime to prove that it's more than a remaster, it's a drastic improvement in all the best ways.

Since the Dragon Ball Super anime ended, there have been two movies and one completely separate anime series, Dragon Ball: Daima, released. While the movies made hundreds of millions of dollars at the worldwide box office, Daima received a much more lukewarm reception. Unsurprisingly, all of those releases saw the Japanese version released first, with the English versions of the movies releasing only around a month later. And while English fans did not need to wait as long as they did for the Dragon Ball Super dub, Daima's dub took an extra three months to begin streaming.

Dragon Ball’s current fragmented release model holds it back from even greater heights. Toei is repeating that mistake with Dragon Ball Super: Beerus, which began releasing on Crunchyroll on October 11, 2026. The English dub is likely being worked on as I type this article, but its release date has yet to be announced. And unless Toei changes its current production processes, the highly anticipated Dragon Ball Super: Galactic Patrol arc will also suffer from an uncoordinated launch. And that isn't the only way that Toei is hurting the franchise's potential.

Newer anime series like Jujutsu Kaisen and Demon Slayer have dramatically altered the increasingly popular anime medium in several beneficial and lucrative ways. While their incredible quality and pacing have helped them stand out from many other anime productions, their theatrical releases have both earned their distributors millions of dollars in profit and been used to greatly increase the world's excitement for their anime series' returns. Both qualities that Toei is not currently utilizing with Dragon Ball.

The producers of Demon Slayer's anime have created the perfect format for milking a franchise for all it's worth. Before the decision to make the series' final arc into three separate movies, they helped open Pandora's box with the release of the 2020 film Demon Slayer The Movie: Mugen Train. After the first season’s huge success, millions of fans worldwide hotly anticipated its continuation. When the next section of the story was released as a two-hour movie, that large fanbase was more than anxious to pay for a movie ticket to see the next part on the big screen.

Almost six months later, Demon Slayer's second season finally began releasing on Crunchyroll. It also covered the Mugen Train arc but with additional content and scenes that could not fit in the movie. Fans who were able to see that part of the story in the theaters were more than anxious to share their experience online, which generated even more hype for the anime's return for those who were not able to see the movie in theaters. It was a brilliant business tactic, but future theatrical anime releases would not execute this strategy nearly as effectively.

One of my biggest movie theater disappointments in 2025 was Jujutsu Kaisen: Execution. The series' Shibuya arc remains one of the best seasons of anime to ever release, but some of its greatest battles were unfinished. Its physical release was able to add those missing sections, and Jujutsu Kaisen: Execution could have used those as a way to entice fans to the theater to see the fights as they were intended. Instead, it was an underwhelming summary of the Shibuya arc with a few minutes of the anticipated continuation tagged on at the end. Still, that release made over $40 million.

If Toei wanted to, they could use these lessons to make every Dragon Ball anime release a worldwide event that would take box offices by storm and further generate excitement for its new series. While Dragon Ball: Daima ended up being a disappointment for some, I'm sure others would have joined me at the movie theater if Toei had released the first 4–5 episodes of that series or Dragon Ball Super: Beerus in theaters a few months before they started streaming.

Releases like Demon Slayer: Mugen Train both respected audiences and helped build excitement for the anime series' return, and Toei could do the same with Dragon Ball. On the other hand, regurgitating already released content for an easy cash-in, as Jujutsu Kaisen has, would likely disappoint certain fans like myself. If Toei is willing to change their current Dragon Ball production processes, hopefully they can find a happy medium that will allow even more fans to get excited about each new release while also making the already profitable franchise even more so.
