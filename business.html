Reaching Full Retirement Age for Social Security in 2027? Here's Why You May Not Want to File Just Yet

📅 Nov 04, 2026  |  Category: Business  |  Read Source →

Taking benefits right away isn't your only option.

If you'll be turning 67 in 2027, you should know that you'll be hitting a big milestone in the context of Social Security. Age 67 is Social Security's full retirement age. That's when you can collect your monthly benefits based on your earnings history without a reduction.

The earliest age to claim Social Security is actually 62. But for each month you take benefits before reaching full retirement age, those monthly checks are reduced. So if you were born in 1960 or later, waiting until 67 to claim Social Security can be a smart move.

But that doesn't mean you should rush to claim Social Security next year if you'll be turning 67. You may find that waiting makes even more sense.

While full retirement age is when you get your Social Security checks without a reduction, delaying your claim past that point boosts your monthly benefits by 8% a year for life. And while this incentive does run out at age 70, if you hold off on filing for three years, you can boost your Social Security benefits by 24%.

Imagine your full retirement age benefit is $2,000 a month. Waiting until age 70 to file gives you $2,480 per month instead. And that's not the only perk.

Each year, Social Security benefits are eligible for a cost-of-living adjustment, or COLA. If you boost your benefits by waiting past full retirement age to file, each COLA that comes through will be worth more money to you by virtue of having a larger monthly baseline check.

Also, if you're the higher earner in your household and your spouse outlives you, they'll typically be entitled to survivor benefits from Social Security after you pass away. The larger your benefits are, the more money your spouse stands to collect.

If 2027 is the year you'll reach full retirement age for Social Security, you may be inclined to take benefits at that time. But there's a lot to be gained by waiting longer to file.

Even if delaying your claim until age 70 isn't feasible, holding off for a single year results in an 8% boost. And a smaller increase could help your retirement finances, too.

Remember, the more Social Security pays you each month, the less pressure there is on your savings. Keep that and the points above in mind when your 67th birthday arrives so you don't make a filing decision you regret.

What Trump losing power over Congress means for Iran, Ukraine and foreign policy

📅 2026-11-04  |  Category: Business  |  Read Source →

With Democrats taking control of Congress, U.S. President Donald Trump could intensify his focus on foreign policy in his final two years in office, while facing greater scrutiny over the wars in Iran and Ukraine, experts told CNBC.

The Associated Press is projecting Democrats will have a Senate majority, flipping seats from Republicans in Iowa, Kansas, North Carolina, Ohio, South Carolina, and Texas.

Democrats also won control of the House, giving the party greater scope to scrutinize the administration, shape spending and challenge Trump's foreign-policy agenda.

"We are going to see a president that is somewhat constrained on matters of oversight and investigation, but on foreign policy, he does still retain a lot of latitude," Laurel Rapp, director of Chatham House's U.S. and North America Programme, told CNBC's "Squawk Box Europe."

Rapp said the Iran war was a "major issue" in this election cycle amid soaring fuel prices, food costs, and inflation. The war began when Trump launched joint strikes with Israel on Feb. 28.

"Republicans don't really have an off-ramp here at this stage. We know the Democrats will seek to take votes in the House and the Senate to constrain the Republicans, to restrain the administration on Iran war powers. But whether that will bind the administration is another matter," Rapp added.

She said that Democrats could exercise the "power of the purse" to limit spending on U.S. military and other Iran-related costs.

With control of both chambers, Democrats could pass legislation seeking to direct Trump to end U.S. involvement in the conflict. However, making such a measure binding over a presidential veto would require enough votes in Congress to override it.

J.C. Lintzenich, visiting senior fellow at the German Marshall Fund, said that the president is not a "lame duck."

"Remember that this election changes the Congress; it does not change the president," Lintzenich told CNBC's "Squawk Box Europe" on Wednesday.

"The President of the United States still has lots of power, lots of authority. We still have pending action in Iran. We still have an unresolved situation with Russia and Ukraine."

He added that while Congress has changed, foreign policy is still executed by the president and his appointed officials.

"What we'll now see is a little bit more congressional oversight, the application of funding, for example, Ukraine, that's required by statute and law," Lintzenich said.

Lintzenich said the outgoing Congress was "more willing to let the White House slide," but Democratic control of both chambers would mean a more assertive Congress, using its Article I powers over legislation, spending, and oversight to hold the White House more closely to existing law and congressional requirements.

West Ham took too long to sack Nuno and now must trust Parker to do what he does best | Jacob Steinberg

📅 Nov 04, 2026  |  Category: Business  |  Read Source →

Forget speculation over how a Parker-led side would fare in the Premier League and let the man who has taken three clubs up do his work again

It has taken West Ham six months to come to their senses and sack Nuno Espírito Santo, but at least the decision has arrived early enough to revive their hopes of automatic promotion.

Dithering was no longer an option after the dismal performance against West Brom last Saturday.

West Ham, eighth in the Championship and four points off the top two, had to act after a second consecutive home defeat. They are in danger of slipping into a battle for a playoff spot and needed to move on from Nuno after just over a year of his confused, passive football.

Nuno did a terrible job after replacing Graham Potter as manager in September 2025. The 52-year-old had 33 games to save West Ham from relegation and failed. He has the most expensive squad in the Championship but his side have not lived up to their billing as title favourites and their stuttering form has exposed the folly of not sacking Nuno at the end of last season.

David Sullivan, then the club’s largest shareholder, wanted a change. But Daniel Kretinsky favoured continuity and looked to capitalise on the shifting power dynamics in the boardroom. Karren Brady had stepped down as vice-chair in April – Karim Virani replaced her in an interim role – and there was further upheaval when Sullivan resigned as joint-chair and a director of the club to fight what he described as “false allegations” regarding his personal conduct.

Sullivan bears most culpability for West Ham’s relegation. He still owns a 40% stake but is no longer on the board and West Ham are better off without him dictating key decisions. Equally, though, where Sullivan was not mistaken was in wanting Nuno gone. Kretinsky got his first big call wrong. At least the Czech billionaire has given Nuno’s replacement time to get the team back on track. The task falls to Scott Parker, who has been on the market since leaving Burnley last April. Parker is regarded as a “safe” appointment and has been on West Ham’s radar for a while.

Some wanted the former England midfielder in the summer and his name was mentioned again when West Ham failed to win their first three league games. Patience was wearing thin. A subsequent run of four wins in a row swiftly proved a false dawn.

West Ham have not had the right mindset. They have two wins from their past six games and have often started playing only after going a goal down. A 6-0 win against Wrexham was a highlight but victory at Lincoln by the same scoreline came with the caveat that the Imps received two red cards before half-time.

There was no identity under Nuno. West Ham are unbeaten on the road and won 1-0 at the then table-topping Swansea last month but it has been hard to tell Nuno broke the Football League transfer record with the £22m signing of the Belgium midfielder Arne Engels from Celtic. Even Jarrod Bowen, who was convinced to sign a new deal in the summer, has been off colour. Nuno’s tactics mean the winger, West Ham’s top scorer, is often crowded out.

West Ham were lopsided against West Brom. Enough was enough. Kretinsky, who is not at every game, was unimpressed when he attended West Ham’s 3-2 defeat by Southampton a week earlier. He pays attention to the views of Bowen, Tomas Soucek and Mark Noble, the technical director and former club captain.

Nuno, whose substitutions were almost as weird as his starting lineups, had lost the players. The mood darkened after the West Brom game. West Ham were easy to play through because of Nuno’s reluctance to use a holding midfielder, an issue exacerbated by his refusal to use Edson Álvarez or Soungoutou Magassa after both sought moves during the summer.

Magassa’s exile has caused disquiet. Not only is a young asset being devalued, a fragile defence is going without enough protection. West Ham have a lot of No 8s but they need a No 6. It is an area for Parker to address. He will also need to work out whether to stick with Mads Hermansen in goal, and the jury is out on summer signings.

One source questions whether West Ham targeted players equipped to handle the intensity of the Championship. Divine Mukasa, the 19-year-old midfielder, is talented but inconsistent. Manor Solomon and Edvin Austbø have flattered to deceive on the left wing. Joël Veltman, the 34-year-old Dutch right-back, has had fitness issues. Adama Boiro, a 24-year-old left‑back on loan from Athletic Bilbao, has not been seen since a cameo against Derby in early September. The three strikers signed by Nuno across two windows have toiled; Pablo Felipe, Taty Castellanos and Joël Piroe have five league goals between them this season.

Nils Koppen, the head of recruitment appointed in the summer, will have to be busy in January, but Parker will hope to have made his impact before the window opens. He is not known for expansive football but he is proven at this level. Parker won promotion with Bournemouth, Burnley and Fulham. The 46-year-old’s track record is clear and it helps that he has an association with West Ham.

Parker, whose all-action playing style made him a fan favourite, knows how difficult it can be in east London. He played in some pretty bad West Ham sides, experienced relegation in 2011 and arrives with the club in a tricky spot again.

It is not the moment to start worrying about Parker’s questionable record as a Premier League manager. What do West Ham do if they go up? Do they stick with Parker? Do they make an instant change? Well, how about they get promoted first? The rest can wait.

Gold, silver and precious metals prices jump, dollar dips as Democrats take control of Congress

📅 2026-11-04  |  Category: Business  |  Read Source →

Precious metals saw strong gains on Tuesday morning, while the value of the dollar fell, following the U.S. midterm elections.

Spot gold was 2% higher at $4,005.40 an ounce by 1:30 a.m. ET, while spot silver jumped 4% to trade at $51.68 per ounce.

Front-month gold futures added 2.2% to trade at $4,020.60, while silver futures were up 3.8% at $51.53.

Other metals seeing notable gains on Wednesday morning included platinum, which jumped 3.3% to trade at $1,462.20, and palladium, which was 3.8% higher at $969.33.

The moves came as vote counting continued in the midterms, with the Democrats projected to have won control of the House and multiple governors' offices also turning blue.

U.S. stock futures broadly fell as the results came in, while Treasury yields also moved lower. Oil prices were marginally higher, while the U.S. dollar index shed around 0.3%.

Rhona O'Connell, head of market analysis for EMEA and Asia at StoneX, said in a note on Tuesday that if the Democrats won dual Congressional control, gold should find support as rates and the dollar would likely come under pressure.

She said it would result in "fiscal brinkmanship, institutional friction, both leading to uncertainty," adding that "the Iran war and a weaker dollar would compete with [the] impact of [a] hawkish Fed."

"Less fiscal stimulus argues for lower long yields and a softer dollar, offset by debt-limit premia in bills and a Fed leaning towards another hike," she said.

Both gold and silver enjoyed record-breaking rallies in 2025, surging 66% and 135% respectively over the course of the year as investors sought safe haven assets amid geopolitical uncertainty and broader market volatility.

But the momentum has come undone this year, with rising inflation, surging bond yields and hawkish central banks removing support from the metals. Gold is down more than 7% year-to-date, while silver has tumbled almost 28%.

Spot gold rose on Monday as oil prices retreated, and is now on course for a weekly rise of around 3.7%.

Daniela Hathorn, senior market analyst at Capital.com, said in a Tuesday note that the recovery remains fragile.

"The challenge for precious metals is that geopolitical uncertainty is not automatically translating into safe-haven demand when investors are also concerned about restrictive monetary policy," she said. "They also face the risk of liquidation during sharp equity selloffs, as investors sell profitable or liquid positions to raise cash."

In the 2026 midterms, Americans voted on all 435 members of the House of Representatives and 35 members of the Senate. Before election night, Republicans held the majority in both Congress chambers, if only by a hair. In 36 US states, voters also decided on who will become the next governor.

Midterm elections are held every four years, at the exact halfway point between presidential elections. The results will influence US politics for the next few years. Despite the significant executive power delegated to the presidency according to the US Constitution, the majority of federal political decisions have to be approved by Congress before they can be implemented. If the Democrats win the majority in one or both chambers of Congress, it will be harder for President Donald Trump to govern as he chooses.

Because the 50 US states lie across six time zones in total, results will trickle in over time, starting with the US East Coast and going west from there. DW follows the Associated Press (AP) for when a seat is called for Republicans or Democrats.

Polling stations in several eastern states closed at 1 a.m. local time. For more updates, go to our US midterms live blog.

In the US Senate, Democrats retained several of their seats on the East Coast, with Virginia being the first to be called. North Carolina was the first Republican Senate seat, which the Democrats were able to flip.

In the House of Representatives, all 435 seats are up for election.

The data on voter demographics and attitudes we use is based on a representative survey of more than 75,000 verified voters conducted by survey and market research firm SSRS on behalf of AP and other news organizations. It builds on interviews of both early and mail-in voters as well as exit polls of in-person voters on Election Day, November 3. Exit polls showed that half of voters saw the 2026 midterm elections as an opportunity to rebuke the president. Three-quarters of US voters said they are "angry" or "dissatisfied" with the way the country is headed.

According to the AP Voter Poll, dissatisfaction was very high in Democratic-leaning states such as California and Massachusetts. However, even in Republican-leaning states, a majority of voters were at least "dissatisfied."

Before Tuesday's election, Republicans (and independents who caucus with them) held 53 of 100 seats in the Senate and 220 of 435 seats in the House. Dragon Ball's New Anime Proves That Toei Is Holding The Franchise Back

📅 Nov 04, 2026  |  Category: Business  |  Read Source →

People who grew up loving Dragon Ball are currently able to enjoy the weekly releases of the Japanese version of Dragon Ball Super: Beerus series, but the hype for the improved anime could be higher. Revised animations, pacing, and entirely new sequences have made the newest release more than a standard remaster. But that hasn't stopped Toei, the anime's producers, from making the same mistakes it's been repeating for more than 13 years.

The Battle of Gods movie and Dragon Ball Super anime series helped make Akira Toriyama's franchise more popular than ever. Their dramatic success proved the world still had a big appetite for more adventures with Goku and his friends. And while the Dragon Ball Super: Broly and Super Hero movies delivered in many ways, those who control the rights and production schedules of the beloved franchise have failed to capitalize on that success, holding the franchise back from reaching its full potential.

During the premiere of Dragon Ball Super's final episodes, which featured the incredibly epic conclusion of the universal Tournament of Power, fans flooded the streets of major cities around the world to cheer and enjoy the series together. The anime garnered that sort of reception with the release of the Japanese version of the series, but one can't help but wonder if the hype would have been even greater if the series' largest markets released their dubs at the same time in movie theaters.

Judging by how the world's most popular anime streaming platform, Crunchyroll, has experienced crashes during several anticipated Dragon Ball Super moments, it's clear that a lot of fans around the world are more than willing to watch the series with subtitles. However, there is still a large percentage of hardcore Dragon Ball fans who prefer to wait and watch the franchise's newest releases with the iconic voice actors they grew up with. Unfortunately for them, some of the wait times for those dubs have been much longer than others.

The Japanese language version of the Dragon Ball Super anime premiered in July 2015, with the English version not releasing until January 2017. That was a considerable amount of time for arguably the world's most popular anime series to release its English dub, one of the franchise's largest markets/demographics. Because of the separate release schedules, hype for new releases can be hindered, compared to if Toei made sure the largest markets around the world had their dubs ready so that new Dragon Ball content could be released at the same time in multiple languages.

It's taken little time for the newest Dragon Ball anime to prove that it's more than a remaster, it's a drastic improvement in all the best ways.

Since the Dragon Ball Super anime ended, there have been two movies and one completely separate anime series, Dragon Ball: Daima, released. While the movies made hundreds of millions of dollars at the worldwide box office, Daima received a much more lukewarm reception. Unsurprisingly, all of those releases saw the Japanese version released first, with the English versions of the movies releasing only around a month later. And while English fans did not need to wait as long as they did for the Dragon Ball Super dub, Daima's dub took an extra three months to begin streaming.

Dragon Ball’s current fragmented release model holds it back from even greater heights. Toei is repeating that mistake with Dragon Ball Super: Beerus, which began releasing on Crunchyroll on October 11, 2026. The English dub is likely being worked on as I type this article, but its release date has yet to be announced. And unless Toei changes its current production processes, the highly anticipated Dragon Ball Super: Galactic Patrol arc will also suffer from an uncoordinated launch. And that isn't the only way that Toei is hurting the franchise's potential.

Newer anime series like Jujutsu Kaisen and Demon Slayer have dramatically altered the increasingly popular anime medium in several beneficial and lucrative ways. While their incredible quality and pacing have helped them stand out from many other anime productions, their theatrical releases have both earned their distributors millions of dollars in profit and been used to greatly increase the world's excitement for their anime series' returns. Both qualities that Toei is not currently utilizing with Dragon Ball.

The producers of Demon Slayer's anime have created the perfect format for milking a franchise for all it's worth. Before the decision to make the series' final arc into three separate movies, they helped open Pandora's box with the release of the 2020 film Demon Slayer The Movie: Mugen Train. After the first season’s huge success, millions of fans worldwide hotly anticipated its continuation. When the next section of the story was released as a two-hour movie, that large fanbase was more than anxious to pay for a movie ticket to see the next part on the big screen.

Almost six months later, Demon Slayer's second season finally began releasing on Crunchyroll. It also covered the Mugen Train arc but with additional content and scenes that could not fit in the movie. Fans who were able to see that part of the story in the theaters were more than anxious to share their experience online, which generated even more hype for the anime's return for those who were not able to see the movie in theaters. It was a brilliant business tactic, but future theatrical anime releases would not execute this strategy nearly as effectively.

One of my biggest movie theater disappointments in 2025 was Jujutsu Kaisen: Execution. The series' Shibuya arc remains one of the best seasons of anime to ever release, but some of its greatest battles were unfinished. Its physical release was able to add those missing sections, and Jujutsu Kaisen: Execution could have used those as a way to entice fans to the theater to see the fights as they were intended. Instead, it was an underwhelming summary of the Shibuya arc with a few minutes of the anticipated continuation tagged on at the end. Still, that release made over $40 million.

Beyond the credit report: How lenders can navigate the next era of mortgage credit

Advantage Partners Solutions’ Patrick Tynan explains why new scoring options, rising costs and evolving credit requirements are changing how lenders approach mortgage credit strategy.

New scoring models, evolving credit requirements and persistent cost pressures are forcing mortgage lenders to rethink their mortgage credit strategy. As the industry moves toward more flexible credit scoring, lenders must balance potential savings with risk, operational efficiency and borrower access.

In this conversation, Patrick Tynan, Chief Sales Officer at Advantage Partners Solutions (APS), discusses the impact of VantageScore 4.0, why cost per funded loan matters more than individual credit report pricing and how strategic CRA partnerships can help lenders prepare for what comes next.

HousingWire: Mortgage credit is entering a period of significant change, with new scoring models and continued cost pressure. Which shifts do you believe will have the greatest impact on lenders, and where should they be paying closer attention?

Patrick Tynan: One of the biggest changes I have seen in my career is happening right now, and it is VantageScore 4.0. For decades, lenders had one option. Now they have a real choice. Lenders can use VantageScore 4.0 today on most loans sold to Fannie Mae and Freddie Mac, and FHA begins accepting it on January 1.

I am a big believer in it. VantageScore 4.0 builds trended data into its scoring algorithm, so it can score borrowers who have thin files or who have worked hard to improve their credit. That means more people getting into homes without anyone lowering the bar, and it delivers significant savings compared with what lenders pay today.

Where I would tell lenders to slow down is the push to collect less information on the borrower. There is a lot of talk about pulling fewer bureaus, and I understand the appeal. But saving a few dollars by seeing less of the borrower is not a good trade. My advice is to take advantage of the new score option and keep the tri-merge credit report.

HW: As lenders evaluate adopting VantageScore 4.0 and other changes to the credit landscape, how should they balance potential cost savings with adapting workflows, implementation and borrower experience?

PT: Here is what I tell every lender I sit down with: When it comes to credit, there is no one-size-fits-all. A shop doing heavy purchase business with a lot of preapprovals runs completely differently than one with strong pull-through. Some lenders come out ahead paying per report. Others save a lot more with a bundled approach.

That is why we do not just hand someone a price sheet. We are a partner, not just a company that sells credit reports. We learn how a lender does business and build the pricing structure that saves them the most money and runs the most efficiently. The number I care about is cost per funded loan, not cost per pull, because only about one in five pulls ever closes. From there, make sure your systems, investors and MI partners are ready for VantageScore 4.0 and for FICO 10T down the road.

HW: As credit becomes more complex, how is the relationship between lenders and their credit providers evolving beyond a transactional service? What should lenders expect from a strategic CRA partner today?

PT: I came up on the lending side, managing teams at Wells Fargo and Countrywide, before I ever worked for a vendor. So, I know what it feels like to be the lender waiting on a report with a closing on the line. What has changed is that lenders need a lot more than a fast, accurate report now. They are deciding which score to use, how many bureaus to pull and when to pull them, and the rules keep moving under their feet. That is where a real partner earns their keep.

Tell them what a new rule means for their shop. Deliver the scores they need from all three bureaus without making them rip out their technology. Show them where every credit dollar is going. And answer the phone when a rescore is holding up a closing. If your credit provider only shows up when the invoice does, you do not have a partner. You have a vendor.

HW: Advantage Partners Solutions combines scale with a privately held structure and capabilities beyond credit reporting. How does that position APS to support lenders differently as market and vendor dynamics continue to shift?

PT: Advantage Partners Solutions is one of the top five credit resellers in the country by revenue, but we are one of the rare companies at that level that is privately held rather than owned by private equity or publicly traded. Our job is to bring the three bureaus together for our clients. Because we are not a bureau ourselves, we are not tied to any one source of data, so we can give lenders the complete tri-merge picture with whatever scoring model fits their business best – that independence matters to us. We make decisions based on what our clients need, not on an investor’s timeline or a quarterly earnings call, and that lets us move early.

VantageScore 4.0 is a good example of how we move early and in the best interests of our clients. We believed lenders and borrowers should have options, so we moved quickly to offer it.

Also, bringing Advantage Credit and Partners Credit & Verifications together gave us the scale to keep investing in technology and people, so our clients are not rebuilding workflows every time the rules shift. It also means we are a fully stocked provider of credit, verification services, flood certifications, and a full suite of QC products with the same breadth lenders expect from the largest companies in our industry. Lenders can rely on one partner across more of the loan file instead of managing a long list of vendors.

HW: Looking ahead, what changes do you expect to reshape mortgage credit over the next several years, and what should lenders be doing now to make sure their credit strategy can adapt?

PT: A few years from now, I think having multiple score options will simply be how business gets done. More data, such as rent payments and other nontraditional tradelines, will feed into scoring, but the cost pressure is not going away. The rules are still moving, too.

FHFA has said it is reviewing both the credit report requirement and loan-level pricing, so lenders should expect more change, and likely soon. With that said, our positions is simple: keep the tri-merge. It is the safest and fairest way to evaluate a borrower, and there are smarter ways to save money than pulling less data.

My advice is not to wait for a final rule. We are actively working with our lenders to create a mortgage credit strategy. We are making sure our technology is ready for VantageScore 4.0. The lenders who do that work now will be the ones ahead when the next announcement comes.
