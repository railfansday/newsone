If you do a price comparison (after adjusting for known inflation and seasonality) of individual TIPS to T-bills, TIPS almost always sell at a small liquidity price discount (aka a liquidity premium) in the last year of their life. If you really have no plan to sell before maturity, it is close to free alpha. The premium is pretty consistent, but it works both ways, get better return than a T-Bill, but if you have to sell it early you lose on price.

Note, for folks buying/selling these, be careful in the day before/of/after each CPI release. The price adjusts over the trading day, not instantly, but the newly known inflation is always priced in within a trading day or so. For some reason, yields are often lower on TIPS maturing in April as well. One of the market's mysteries.....

Assuming ICPI holds to maturity, it is a good case for holding that period. VTIP also holds to maturity, but as noted term risk is higher. I've been using VTIP for years as the core to my TIPS holdings along with a longer-duration TIPS ladder. I don't really sell from VTIP though, I just spend the dividends for the most part so I have little if any stress about term risk.

I will note that any TIPS with less than 6 mos. to maturity has almost no unknown inflation coming since the known inflation is always between 15-45 days depending on the time of the month. Really, in the last year, TIPS provide very little inflation protection on an absolute $ basis.
