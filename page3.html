The Minnesota Vikings (6-2) aren't providing their old friend Kirk Cousins any ramp up before throwing him back in the fire. After trading for the 38-year-old quarterback at the NFL trade deadline on Tuesday in a deal with the Las Vegas Raiders, Cousins will start against the Green Bay Packers (6-3) on Sunday at Lambeau Field.

That means he will join Cam Newton with the Carolina Panthers and Boomer Esiason with the Cincinnati Bengals as the only quarterbacks since 1950 to start for a team in a second stint after they previously won 50-plus games with said team in their first tour of duty.

Head Coach Kevin O'Connell has announced that QB Kirk Cousins will start Sunday at Green Bay. pic.twitter.com/zSMqWo0pHQ

"Cannot give him enough credit for coming in here, how he's gone about the week, both him and the coaching staff getting him ready to go, using all the time we possibly can," O'Connell said during Friday's media availability. "It's been really impressive what he's been able to do and just the command and just getting right back in the huddle with a lot of guys he's played with, but certainly some guys he hasn't. It's been something that everybody's been very, very impressed by."

Minnesota is desperate for the jolt the four-time Pro Bowl quarterback can provide for their sagging offense. Cousins has 22 total touchdowns in 2026, and the Vikings have scored just 11 offensive touchdowns, tied for the fewest in the NFL with the 0-8 Los Angeles Chargers. His 20 passing touchdowns rank as the second-most in the league this season, just three behind Joe Burrow's league-leading 23 and one ahead of Jordan Love's 19.

This news certainly makes four-time Pro Bowl wide receiver Justin Jefferson's Friday a happy one. Jefferson won NFL Offensive Player of the Year honors in 2022, his last season with a fully healthy Cousins, after leading the league in targets (184), catches (128), receiving yards (1,809), and receiving first downs (83).  The 98.9 passing yards per game that Cousins averages to Jefferson is the most by a quarterback-receiver duo in NFL history (min. 50 games together).

Coach Kevin O'Connell also hopes Cousins' presence can halt the quarterback carousel Minnesota has gone through since Cousins tore his Achilles in his last Vikings start at Lambeau Field against the Packers in Week 8 of the 2023 season. They have made an NFL-high 17 starting quarterback changes since then.

Should the Vikings hang on and make the playoffs in the 2026 season, Cousins could become the first quarterback in NFL history to start a playoff game in the same season he started a game for multiple teams.

Friday Five With Shaina Tabak: Art, Architecture, and the Spaces Between

📅 Nov 14, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Though she’s still pursuing her MFA in Visual Arts at Columbia University, artist Shaina Tabak is hardly a newcomer to the art world. The RISD graduate, who earned her BFA in furniture design, already has more than 20 exhibitions under her belt. Now she’s adding curator to her résumé with 260 Madison, an artist-run exhibition bringing together more than 50 artists across a former Duane Reade storefront and a 23,000-square-foot office floor above it in Midtown Manhattan.

Tabak’s own work is equally expansive. Drawing on history, technology, and materiality, she explores the relationship between the digital and physical worlds, often starting with digitally modeled forms that she translates into sculptures through hands-on processes such as cutting, perforation, lamination, and extrusion.

‘On Certainties (streetlight)’ (2026) by Anthony Discenza and ‘Arbor Armor’ (2015–2026) by Yasue Maetake on display at ‘260 Madison.’ Photography courtesy of Shaina Tabak.

But her interest in materials extends beyond their physical properties. She also sees them as records of labor, manufacturing, extraction, and use. Her work examines contemporary material culture through a historical lens, imagining how the things made and valued today might be understood generations from now. As she puts it, her sculptures are “less concerned with imagining the future than with creating historical distance from the present.”

That perspective comes through in her recent work, which examines the increasingly blurred line between looking and shopping. In her Brooklyn studio, the Queens-born artist has been embedding collected industrial objects in three-ply cardboard, referencing the packaging and shipping systems that support consumer culture. She’s also exploring the influence of visual search and algorithmic commerce, where objects spotted in an image can lead to an endless stream of purchasable alternatives. It’s a commentary on a culture in which images don’t just inspire desire; they generate transactions.

‘First Water (deer)’ and ‘First Water (banner)’ (2026) by Dexter Barker-Glenn on view at ‘260 Madison.’ Photography courtesy of Shaina Tabak.

For today’s Friday Five, we spent five minutes with Shaina Tabak to explore the ideas behind her curatorial debut at 260 Madison and the influences shaping her own work in the exhibition.

‘Ghosts’ (2025) by Alisa Berger and ‘Big Red Piece’ (2026) by Kari Cholnoky on view at ‘260 Madison.’ Photography courtesy of Shaina Tabak.

For her curatorial debut, Tabak drew on Columbia University’s MFA community and her own network of artists, bringing together emerging talents and established names. Co-curated by artistic duo Dayana Matasheva and Edson Niebla Rodríguez of Demon Lovers, Inc., 260 Madison includes artists such as Simon Denny, Jon Kessler, and Eva and Franco Mattes. The exhibition was organized in collaboration with the building’s owner, AmTrustRE, which gave the curators full creative control.

‘Longcat’ (2026) by Eva & Franco Mattes on view at ‘260 Madison.’ Photography courtesy of Shaina Tabak.

On view through Nov. 22, 2026, the expansive exhibition brings together familiar materials, objects, and technologies that are dismantled, reconstructed, or made strange. Exposed HVAC, ripped-out toilets, and corporate-logo wallpaper create the backdrop for works spanning sculpture, new media, and video. Tabak’s goal for 260 Madison is to “examine the systems organizing contemporary life—from control, surveillance, AI, and extraction to the material and architectural structures that shape how we work, move, and live.”

Below, in her own words, Tabak shares the influences that shaped her approach to 260 Madison, from artist-run exhibitions that challenged traditional institutions to architectural theories and sculptures that informed her own work.

Shaina Tabak’s works ‘Localized Collapse / Frozen Zone’ (2026) and ‘Sweep Towards Skylight’ (2026) on view at ‘260 Madison.’ Photography courtesy of the artist.

“The first one, Damien Hirst’s Freeze (1988), was suggested to me by my professor Jon Kessler. Freeze was particularly compelling because it took place amid the redevelopment of London’s Docklands and was itself supported by real-estate developers, revealing the uneasy relationship between artistic independence and the production of urban capital. That history made me particularly conscious of a contradiction in our own project: While 260 Madison temporarily interrupts the building’s commercial function, the exhibition also generates cultural value for a property undergoing modernization.”

“A more recent exhibition, Christopher Wool’s See Stop Run (2024), took place in New York City’s Financial District and demonstrated how artists could operate outside established institutions by appropriating vacant commercial spaces. Wool’s exhibition was also installed on a gutted office floor and offered a more immediate architectural reference, allowing the building’s exposed infrastructure and traces of demolition to enter into dialogue with the work. With 260 Madison, we wanted to push these precedents even further, treating the vacant corporate office as a material record of changing relationships between labor, capital, and the built environment.”

“Rem Koolhaas’ book Delirious New York and his essay “Junkspace” were really helpful in connecting the architecture of the space to the exhibition. My partner has worked on the 17th floor of 260 Madison for the past three years—a complete coincidence—and his 9-to-5 job as a paralegal brought to mind Koolhaas’ concepts of the vertical schism, architectural lobotomy, and definitive instability as I considered the exhibition’s function.”

“All three of Koolhaas’ concepts, in their own ways, describe the Manhattan skyscraper as a structure in which the exterior remains independent of the interior, allowing different floors to accommodate radically different and constantly changing activities without altering the building’s outward appearance.”

“I was thinking about House, a temporary public sculpture by Rachel Whiteread, when creating my sculpture Swept Towards Skylight (2026) for 260 Madison. I embedded an earlier work in insulation foam and coated it with cement to give the illusion that the negative space around it had been cast solid. Whiteread’s work transformed the interior of a house slated for demolition into a concrete monument to its former inhabitants and the lives that had occupied it.”

“I kept returning to this idea while curating 260 Madison, where the vacant office floor still bears physical traces of the labor and routines that once animated it. I wanted the building to function not simply as a container for artworks, but as an artifact in its own right, with its exposed infrastructure and signs of abandonment becoming part of the exhibition. What interests me about House, and what I hoped to capture at 260 Madison, was how architecture outlives its function and becomes a monument to the systems that once gave it meaning.”

260 Madison is on view through Nov. 22, 2026, at 260 Madison Avenue in New York City.

Shares of SBC Medical Group (SBC +37.64%) soared more than 37% on Friday after the health clinic management leader reported strong quarterly growth figures and announced a new artificial intelligence (AI) alliance.

SBC's revenue jumped 35% year over year to $59 million in the third quarter. The gains were driven by new clinic openings and service fee increases.

SBC's network grew by 36 clinics over the past year, bringing its total to 293 locations as of Sept. 30. In turn, patient visits over the trailing 12 months increased 9% to 7.06 million.
