McCord Needs Run Game Help Against Jets On Sunday

📅 Nov 26, 2026  |  Category: Sports  |  Read Source →

Kyle McCord has a chance to reset his rookie season, but the path forward is narrow. The Miami Dolphins face the New York Jets on Sunday, Nov. 29, at Hard Rock Stadium. McCord just endured a brutal outing against the Bills, completing only 12 of 33 passes for 102 yards. He also threw an interception and took eight hits while getting sacked twice. That performance highlighted a team that has lost four straight games since its lone win. The Dolphins need a better plan than just hoping the quarterback improves on his own.

The offense has stalled because the run game has disappeared. Since beating the Jets on Oct. 25, Miami has failed to rush for 100 yards in any single game. This offensive drought lines up perfectly with the current four-game losing streak. McCord does not need to be a hero to give the team a chance. He simply needs protection and a running back who can move the pile. If the Dolphins cannot run the football, the rookie could face another long afternoon against a defense that will be ready.

The New York Jets remember their first meeting well, even though they lost that day. In Week 7, Miami built a 21-7 lead and held on for a 28-25 victory. In the first half, the Jets recorded 114 yards while the Dolphins amassed 231 total net yards. They also owned the clock with 21:09 of possession compared to just 8:51 for New York. Jets head coach Aaron Glenn admitted the difficulty of that start. "They punched us in the mouth in the first quarter," HC Aaron Glenn said. That lesson sticks with a team that now sits at 3-8.

Geno Smith and his offense are trying to fix their issues after a solid win against the Chargers. During his most recent start, Smith completed 17-of-21 passes for 176 yards and 3 TDs. He led long scoring drives and scored points on 5 of 6 possessions. The Jets want to start fast this time, especially since Smith grew up near the stadium. According to "We want to start fast first and foremost,", Smith, a Miramar, FL native, grew up roughly 20 minutes away from Hard Rock Stadium. The Dolphins cannot afford to let the Jets control the game early.

Dolphins coach Jeff Hafley believes the extra time in practice is helping McCord. The rookie has now started two games and faced different defenses in Indianapolis and Buffalo. Hafley sees the value in these reps as the team prepares for Sunday. "I think the reps that he's getting in practice," Hafley said. "The chemistry he's developing with the guys, whether it's the offensive line and the huddle, the wide receivers and the timing. I think that's all going to be continued improvement." The coach added that McCord is the type of guy who grows from both success and failure.

The Dolphins are not asking McCord to reinvent the wheel or make impossible plays. They just need competent quarterback play and the ball to stay in his hands. The Jets have been wildly inconsistent this season, which gives Miami a real opening. But the offense must execute better than it did against the Bills. The next game is Sunday, Nov. 29, at 1 p.m.

Real Madrid staged an excellent second-half comeback to secure a 5-1 win over PSV Eindhoven in the UEFA Champions League this midweek.

Kylian Mbappe scored a brace and was subsequently nominated for the UCL Player of the Week. But the Frenchman had actually gifted his MOTM award to Vinicius Jr.

It was an important game for the Brazilian international, who scored for Real Madrid for the first time in three months. In doing so, he has achieved a key milestone.

Thanks to his late goal against PSV, Vinicius has now netted his 35th goal for Real Madrid in the Champions League, drawing him level with club legend Ferenc Puskas, reports Defensa Central.

It was a special night for Vinicius. (Photo by Angel Martinez/Getty Images)

Vinicius Jr. now stands fifth in the all-time list of goalscorers for Real Madrid in the competition, behind only an iconic quartet of Alfredo Di Stefano (49), Raul Gonzalez (66), Karim Benzema (78) and Cristiano Ronaldo (105).

This is quite the achievement for a player who has been central to Real Madrid's recent success and has already bagged two Champions League trophies.

In the overall scheme of things, Vinicius currently sits 24th, just one goal behind Puskas. His teammate Kylian Mbappe is further ahead in fifth spot with 76 goals, but a majority of them came in PSG colours.

Apart from the milestone, it was a special goal for Vinicius in many ways, as the forward has been struggling with form throughout the entirety of the season so far.

Vinicius Jr. has shown early signs of promise. (Photo by Denis Doyle/Getty Images)

That is why the Real Madrid team showed exceptional unity when the Brazilian international did eventually find the back of the net, with Mbappe being at the heart of it.

Despite being named the MOTM, the Frenchman handed the award to Vinicius, in a gesture of recognition for his efforts on the night.

Vinicius Jr. himself was moved by the gesture and thanked his teammates after the game.

"Very happy for how my teammates have celebrated a goal that I needed. We are a family. Thank you very much, really. I love you all, let's go for more!" he wrote after the game.

This chimes in well with reports that the entire Real Madrid team are now all in on helping Vini get back to his best.

Liverpool v Man Utd referee made ‘obvious error’ sending off Leny Yoro as written reasons published

The Man Utd defender’s suspension has been overturned, with an independent regulatory commission unanimously concluding that Referee Sam Barrott’s decision was wrong

Want to bookmark your favourite articles and stories to read or reference later? Start your Independent Membership today.

Referee Sam Barrott made an “obvious error” by giving Manchester United defender Leny Yoro a red card against Liverpool, an independent regulatory commission unanimously concluded.

Yoro was given his marching orders when he hauled down Swedish striker Alexander Isak in the opening stages of last Sunday’s fiery match at Anfield.

Debate raged over Isak’s touch and whether goalkeeper Senne Lammens would have collected the ball had Yoro not made contact.

Even referees’ chief Howard Webb concluded that the call was “certainly not clearly wrong”, but then Manchester United appealed to the Football Association for wrongful dismissal and successfully removed his one-match ban on Tuesday.

The three-person panel’s written reasons were published on Thursday and stated: “The commission members were unanimous that, taking the foul committed by LY (Yoro) out of the scenario, AI (Isak) would not have had an obvious goalscoring opportunity.

“Despite the fact that AI had reached the ball initially, his touch was heavy to the extent that he had in fact lost control of the ball – that control had, in effect, passed to the advancing MUFC goalkeeper who in the view of the commission would have been able to gather or smother the ball before AI could get to it and shoot at goal.

“To the mind of the commission, the referee had made an ‘obvious error’ in deciding that AI (Isak) would have had an obvious goalscoring opportunity.”

Webb, the chief refereeing officer at Pro Ref, told the Match Officials: Mic’d Up programme which aired on Tuesday, shortly after confirmation the ban had been removed: “There’s a question mark I guess around whether or not he (Isak) would get onto that ball before Lammens. Now, in the moment, the referee looked at it and felt he would.

“The VAR checks to see whether that decision made on the field was clearly and obviously incorrect, and formed the opinion that it wasn’t.

“I think by and large, most people would see that as a correct decision, with Isak moving quickly on towards that ball. The alternative view is that Lammens might get there first, but I think it’s a little bit debatable. (It’s) certainly not clearly wrong.”

The FA’s independent panel, which was made up of former Blackburn and Middlesbrough winger Stuart Ripley, former Sheffield United and Notts County forward Tony Agana and ex-Walsall goalkeeper Mick Kearns, disagreed.

The reasons confirm that United submitted technical information to the panel which concluded Isak would have been, at best, 1.8 metres short of the ball even if Yoro had not made the challenge.

The reasons state that the commission “had no way of testing the validity of this analysis undertaken by the club and as such the evidence was viewed with caution and allocated weight accordingly”.

To add to the confusion, the Key Match Incidents (KMI) Panel is reported by the Daily Mail to have backed Barrott’s call and the decision of the VAR John Brooks not to recommend a review.

The five-person KMI Panel is comprised of three former players and coaches, one Premier League representative and one from Pro Ref, and its findings for the match week where the incident occurred are due to be published on December 2.

Pro Ref declined to comment on the Mail story, or on the commission’s findings. Manchester United have been approached for comment.

The reasons revealed that United also submitted a video of a challenge by Brighton’s Pascal Struijk on Wilson Isidor of Sunderland last month. Struijk was shown a red card after the VAR recommended a review for denial of an obvious goalscoring opportunity. Struijk’s suspension was removed after an appeal to the FA by Brighton.

The removal of the ban means Yoro is free to face Brentford in the Premier League on Saturday.

United encountered further refereeing controversy in their 2-2 Champions League draw away to Sporting in Lisbon on Wednesday night.

The Red Devils had a first-half goal disallowed after Polish referee Szymon Marciniak blew for what looked at best a very soft-looking foul by Youri Tielemans before Amad Diallo had put the ball in the net. They then had a winner ruled out when VAR recommended a review for what was deemed to be a foul by United’s Patrick Dorgu on Sporting goalkeeper Kaique Pereira, though the Brazilian appeared to have fumbled into his own net before the Dorgu challenge came in.

To rub salt into their wounds, Tielemans – who was booked for the challenge that led to the Diallo goal being disallowed – was then shown a second yellow in injury time.
