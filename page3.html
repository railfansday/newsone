The advantages of using a USB-C docking station

📅 Nov 22, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Plus, there are differences between a USB-C hub and a docking station.

Laptops have been getting thinner and lighter from year to year, and they have become so powerful that many people choose a laptop as their main computer. But a thin body means fewer ports, and a desktop setup with a screen, mouse, webcam and keyboard requires a lot of them. If you have a laptop and still want the full desktop experience, a USB-C docking station can be the solution you're looking for.

A USB-C docking station is a box that connects to your laptop through a single USB-C cable and gives it a full set of ports. USB-C docking stations reduce cord clutter, add more ports to your setup and can keep your laptop's own ports from wearing down.

The biggest advantage of a dock is simplicity. One single cable connects the laptop to its peripherals. That means you can come home and plug in all your devices with one plug; you can also grab your laptop on your way out just as quickly. Monitors, a keyboard, a webcam and a charger no longer need to be reconnected every time the laptop comes and goes, and your desk stays tidier because the cables run to the dock instead of the laptop.

A dock can also add ports your laptop doesn't have. Depending on the model, you can get HDMI or DisplayPort outputs for external monitors, more USB ports, an SD card reader and an Ethernet port for a stable wired internet connection. A thin laptop that comes with three or four ports can end up with a dozen.

The third advantage is less wear on your laptop. Plugging and unplugging several cables over and over puts strain on the ports, and a dock takes that job over. Instead of plugging several cables into the laptop every day, you plug in just one.

If you only need a few basic connections, a USB-C hub is the cheaper option. Hubs have roughly four to 10 ports and cost anywhere between $30 and $150. Docking stations generally have more ports, come with their own DC power adapter, and cost more, with some higher-end models costing more than $400. A dock can give you a full desktop setup with multiple monitors, plus more ports of every kind, and the higher-end Thunderbolt 4 and USB4 models can run multiple displays, Ethernet, audio and laptop charging through that one cable. If that sounds like what you need, here are the best docking stations for laptops.

Before buying, it's worth checking a few things, because not every dock works with every laptop. A USB-C port only outputs video if it supports DisplayPort Alt Mode or is a Thunderbolt or USB4 port, and the dock needs to support Power Delivery pass-through at your laptop's required wattage. If you plan to work with the lid closed, keep the power plugged in, since the laptop draws more power with an external monitor attached.

Get those details right and a dock quietly does its job, providing a laptop that goes back to being portable by unplugging just one cable.

Before you pay for unlimited phone data, check how much cellular data you actually use. Your carrier’s app or past bills show it month by month, and if your heaviest recent month, hotspot included, fits under a cheaper plan with a data cap, switch to that plan and keep the difference.

Data your phone uses on Wi-Fi at home and at work never counts against your plan. If you’re on Wi-Fi most of the day, your phone may use only a few gigabytes of cellular data a month.

Start with your carrier’s count, since a data cap is measured against it. Its app or online account shows each line’s use by billing cycle, as do past bills, so look back three to six months to catch any heavy month.

Your phone’s settings show which apps used the data. On an iPhone, the Current Period total in Settings > Cellular counts all data since the last time anyone tapped Reset Statistics on that screen, so tap it on the day your billing cycle starts. On an Android phone, you can set the data usage cycle to start on your billing date.

Size the plan to your heaviest month, not your average, and make sure that month includes any hotspot use, which some carriers list separately. Then check whether a capped plan counts hotspot data against its monthly allowance or leaves hotspot out, as some cheap plans do.

Take a commuter whose carrier’s app shows about 6 GB in a typical month and 9 GB in her heaviest. She pays $65 a month for an unlimited line whose extras she wouldn’t miss. Switching to a prepaid plan with 15 GB on the same network, at $25 a month, covers her heaviest month and saves her $480 a year.

Going past a prepaid plan’s allowance usually slows your data or pauses it until you buy more, rather than adding a charge. The plan’s broadband label, a fact sheet the FCC requires for every plan, has to say which. Unlimited is worth paying for if you stream video away from Wi-Fi most days, often use your phone as a hotspot, take video calls on the road, or have a family whose data use swings from month to month.

Choose a plan whose allowance clears your heaviest month by a few gigabytes.

Opinion: Liverpool eye move for perfect forward, they need him

📅 Nov 22, 2026  |  Category: Other  |  Read Source →

Liverpool have been linked with a lot of players recently.

They have several areas among their ranks that need upgrading despite them spending well over £600 million on new players already.

This is obviously a major cause for concern but, right now, there isn't a lot that the Reds can do.

However, with the January transfer window now being just over a month away, some decisions are going to have to be made quickly.

Because, even though every section of Liverpool's first-team squad could do with improving, they don't have an endless amount of money to dip into.

So, that's why this latest move that they've been linked with could be perfect.

Gustavo Varela is constantly being scouted by Liverpool

One of Liverpool's main areas of focus that they've had over the last few years is their attack.

Everyone involved with the club knew that change was coming with Sadio Mane and Roberto Firmino already being shown the exit door.

However, the exit of Mohamed Salah was something that nobody could have anticipated, not after his performances the season prior, anyway.

But, the Egyptian King did end up leaving which has forced the Reds to spend well over £400 million on new attackers.

Some deals have worked out while others still have a lot to figure out.

But, right now, as per a fresh update from Corriere dello Sport, Gustavo Varela could be one of the players on top of Liverpool's shortlist.

"Napoli themselves have conducted a survey though not directly with Monza to try to anticipate the competition, which, in addition to Juventus, is fierce," they wrote.

"In the Premier League, Tottenham, Brentford, and Sunderland have inquired , while Liverpool sends scouts every week to monitor Varela's performance.

"The Serie A top scorer is also a hot topic abroad, but there's another factor: Benfica has a 20% interest in the future resale and the ability to match any offer that comes to the Monza club, having a preferential route. In other words, if a €40 million offer were to arrive, the Portuguese club could match it (in that case, the outlay would be € 32 million ) and bring home Gustavo Varela.

"It would still be a huge capital gain for the red and whites, given that they paid €2.5 million for him a few months ago, after he was close to joining Frosinone, who then preferred to invest in Tomas Bobcek, a mystery item in the Ciociaria's extraordinary start to the season."

Gustavo Varela would be a perfect back-up option for Liverpool

Right now, Liverpool only really have one proper number nine available to them.

Alexander Isak is their record signing and, while he's performed well, a lot more could be coming from the Sweden international.

Of course, Hugo Ekitike is going to return from injury in 2027 so he'll be able to take some pressure off Isak's shoulders but, since nobody knows how good the Frenchman is going to be upon his return, there could be a gap about to open up in Liverpool's squad.

Varela is a relatively unknown attacker but, right now, he's the Serie A's top goal scorer and he's easily Monza's best player.

Indeed, they may be sitting in the relegation zone at the moment but, with how well Varela is performing, they could find themselves out of the bottom three in no time.

Obviously, the transfer links with Liverpool are all just rumours for now but, if the club are sending scouts to watch him every week, they must be serious about wanting to invest in the Portuguese striker.

Life at Manchester United got off to a nightmare start for Mauricio Pochettino as his side was reduced to 10 men inside eight minutes in his managerial bow against Liverpool.

Liverpool started brightly and fashioned a chance out of nothing when Ronald Araujo’s hooked clearance left Matthijs de Ligt - making his first league start in a year - in no man’s land.

Alexander Isak latched onto the ball but was clattered into last man Leny Yoro just outside the box, prompting referee Sam Barrett to reach into his pocket and show the Frenchman a red card.

"It doesn't matter if you're playing with a back four, or back five, you've got to do the basics right. De Ligt - I'm amazed with him at this level,” Roy Keane said on Sky Sports.

"I know he's come back from an injury, he's just not reading the flight of the ball. If you do get it wrong, you run back, you sprint back, and then Yoro has got to be covering. If you're a defender, you always expect the worst."

There was a debate over whether VAR should have overturned the decision due to Isak’s heavy touch, which put into question he would have kept control of the ball - a key consideration in determining if a player has denied an obvious goalscoring opportunity (DOGSO).

"Isak's not getting the ball. There's no way he's getting the ball,” Keane added.

"It's clumsy from United. Obviously it's one straight ball over the top. You shouldn't be in so much trouble.

"But there's no way he's getting that ball. Absolutely not. I know Isak is quick and he's sharp, but we've looked at it again.

"It's really poor defending. Players not smelling danger. But Isak is not getting that ball. Absolutely not."

But VAR John Brooks did not call for a review, with the Premier League Match Centre writing in a statement: “The referee’s call of red card to Yoro for denial of an obvious goal-scoring opportunity (DOGSO) was checked and confirmed by VAR.

“REF: ‘It's a red card for DOGSO. On-field decision is outside [the box], red card, DOGSO’.

“VAR: ‘Defender bundles into him into the back, gets too tight, I think it's a foul’.”

Things then went from bad to worse for United as Dominik Szoboszlai stepped up to the free-kick and fired past Senne Lammens into the bottom-left corner, ensuring Yoro’s blunder was fully punished.

Liverpool doubled their lead just three minutes later, with Florian Wirtz volleying high into the goal as Pochettino’s side rapidly unravelled.

Liverpool’s outright domination of the opening stages, combined with losing a defender, sparked Pochettino into making an early change, replacing forward Bryan Mbeumo with centre-back Harry Maguire in the 22nd minute.

Augsburg’s Manuel Baum questions rulebook’s double standard after seeing red in Bundesliga win

📅 Nov 22, 2026  |  Category: Other  |  Read Source →

FC Augsburg head coach Manuel Baum has questioned the severity of the straight red card he received during Sunday’s 3-1 Bundesliga victory over Mainz 05, while acknowledging that referee Martin Petersen acted in accordance with the rules. The FCA trainer was dismissed in the 43rd minute following a heated sequence of events toward the end of the first half.

Augsburg, already leading 2-0, thought they had added a third goal when Yannik Keitel finished from close range following a throw-in. Petersen nevertheless disallowed the goal for a foul on Mainz defender Kacper Potulski in the build-up. Baum’s frustration with the decision ultimately led to his dismissal. The 47-year-old held onto the ball as Mainz midfielder Nadiem Amiri attempted to execute a quick throw-in.

“The 3-0 should have been legitimate and in accordance with the rules,” Baum remarked at the post-match press conference. “We’ve been informed afterwards that it should have counted. The ball came to me and I held onto it because I wanted to have a word with the ref and, I only noticed on the replay that I moved it from my left to right hand [while Nadiem Amiri was trying to execute a quick throw in].”

Under IFAB Law 12, a team official who delays the opposition’s restart by holding onto the ball commits a sending-off offence. Players, by contrast, are generally cautioned for delaying a restart. Baum acknowledged that Petersen’s decision was technically correct but suggested that the rules impose an unnecessarily harsh punishment on coaches.

“According to the rules, the coach must be punished draconically in such a case,” Baum continued. “A player would be given a yellow. A coach must automatically be given a red for such a thing. What? I find that a little strange. I think a yellow card would have been sufficient. But, okay. That’s the letter of the law. The team, I think did a great job of disengaging themselves from the emotions in the game and seeing it through.”

Despite losing their head coach before halftime, Augsburg successfully protected their advantage and ultimately ended a seven-match winless Bundesliga streak. Michael Gregoritsch completed his brace shortly after the restart, and the hosts held firm after Mainz substitute Ransford Königsdörffer pulled a goal back.
