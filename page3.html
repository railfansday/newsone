Modern Westerns are experiencing a major boom thanks in no small part to the brilliance of Taylor Sheridan. Dinosaur movies, on the other hand, not so much. The Jurassic Park franchise, the singular dominant force in the field, seems to have reached a point of diminishing returns, creating a golden opportunity for another major player to step in. Notable efforts made to launch its competition have included Adam Driver's 65, Anne Hathaway's The End of Oak Street, and Primitive War. Of the trio, Primitive War, directed by Luke Sparke, drew the best reception. Sparke is now officially set to return with another ambitious project that can truly rival Jurassic Park — a sci-fi Western mashup that is the stuff dreams are made of.

The news of Sparke's dino-Western project first broke in the summer. It was described as a sci-fi Western set in an alternate universe where dinosaurs and humans co-exist together in a symbiotic relationship. The bonkers idea immediately drew widespread appeal, leading to the release of a concept art and a teaser trailer, which went viral, generating over 5.5 million views. The project, titled Dinosaurs of the Wild West, however, had a financing problem. This led to the creation of a Kickstarter campaign, which has, fortunately, earned enough support to get things officially moving. An exclusive ScreenRant report reveals that Dinosaurs of the Wild West has begun filming as a TV series in New Zealand and Australia. An official synopsis also released reads as follows:

"Set in an alternate 1879, Dinosaurs of the Wild West imagines a frontier where humans and dinosaurs have built civilization together. Massive Brontosaurs haul entire towns across the plains. Raptors are ridden into battle. Pterosaurs rule the skies. Against this extraordinary backdrop, pioneers, outlaws and adventurers navigate a dangerous frontier where prehistoric creatures have reshaped the course of human history."

Jensen Huang's Return Could Make a Tesla/SpaceX Merger More Likely

📅 Nov 25, 2026  |  Category: Business  |  Read Source →

The regulatory environment looks more forgiving after Huang's return.

By Jeremy Bowman – Nov 24, 2026 at 9:30PM EST

Tesla (TSLA -0.20%) and Space Exploration Technologies (SPCX -5.65%), Elon Musk's two trillion-dollar companies, took a step closer to merging on Tuesday.

Both companies have appointed a total of five directors, three from Tesla and two from SpaceX, to guide two special committees to handle merger negotiations.

The committees have also hired investment banks to develop financial models for the all-stock merger, and a deal could be reached before year's end. If the merger were timely enough, it would also allow SpaceX to be included in the S&P 500 earlier than it would as a stand-alone company.

Naturally, merging two companies that have the same CEO is easier than merging two unrelated companies. Musk has a majority of SpaceX's voting power and 20% of Tesla's voting power, and could own a majority of the combined company.

The full details of Huang's return to Nvidia may never be clear, but President Trump says he played an active role in getting Huang reinstated, telling CNBC that he "convinced" the board to do so.

That move may have shifted the Overton window significantly in corporate governance, or what's considered acceptable, and also shows that Trump is willing to put his thumb on the scale for his corporate friends, at least when he believes it's in the country's interest, one of which is Musk.

The dynamics of getting the merger approved versus reinstating Huang are much different, however. Trump just needed to get the board's approval to get Huang back as Nvidia CEO. The Tesla/SpaceX merger, on the other hand, will face a battery of regulatory hurdles to get cleared.

China would be an issue in the merger, as Tesla has a significant business there, and SpaceX's technology would likely raise national security concerns. It would have to pass antitrust muster in both the U.S. and Europe, where there could be resistance to creating a company with a leading position across so many disparate industries. Additionally, Texas Attorney General-elect Nathan Johnson has expressed skepticism about corporate concentration and could challenge the combination.

Still, expectations of corporate governance and President Trump's own ability to influence such decisions have shifted with Huang's reinstatement, which could help the merger go through.

While the two companies haven't formally declared an intention to merge, they're likely to go through with it, as all of the interested parties, including Elon Musk, appear to be incentivized by a merger. The deal will serve as a major test for the regulatory apparatus, as antitrust regulators have blocked much smaller deals in the past.

Bayern: the FIFPro union steps up for Jamal Musiala

📅 Nov 25, 2026  |  Category: Sports  |  Read Source →

FIFPRO is stepping up to defend Jamal Musiala. In a statement released this Saturday, the global players’ union said it “deeply concerned” by the repeated public statements of several Bayern Munich managers regarding the contract of the German international, currently recovering from his serious injury. FIFPRO denounces a “concerted strategy” aimed at putting pressure on the player and his representatives before possible negotiations. She also recalls that the clubs have “a duty of protection» towards their players, particularly during a recovery period, and that potential contractual concerns should be addressed privately.

This position comes after several media outings from Bavarian leaders on the Musiala situation. Honorary president Uli Hoeness, general manager Jan-Christian Dreesen and former general manager Oliver Kahn successively spoke publicly about his contract and the questions surrounding his future. The 23-year-old attacking midfielder is linked to Bayern until June 2030, after having extended his lease in February 2025. For FIFPRO, an injury or drop in form cannot justify calling into question contractual commitments. The union thus supports the player and calls on Bayern to respect the terms of his contract.

“FIFPRO is deeply concerned by repeated public statements from the management of FC Bayern Munich questioning the contract of Jamal Musiala upon his return from injury. Bayern’s honorary president, general manager and former general manager have all, in quick succession, publicly questioned Musiala’s contract. According to FIFPRO, this is a concerted strategy aimed at putting pressure on Musiala and his representatives before any negotiations. A club of Bayern’s caliber should show more discernment. Bayern have a duty of care towards Musiala. In professional football, uncertainty about a player’s future can take a toll on their mental health, especially during recovery. Making Musiala’s health and future a subject of public debate only increases this pressure. Any concerns regarding his contract should be addressed privately with him and his representatives. Contracts are meant to be respected. An injury or drop in form does not entitle a club to renege on the terms of a contract, just as a string of exceptional performances does not automatically entitle a player to a pay rise. Clubs, leagues and players’ unions have agreed at FIFA level that contractual stability is a fundamental principle of football. It protects both the clubs and the players. FIFPRO supports Jamal Musiala and all players who honor their commitments. We expect their employers to do the same.»

How to Watch F1 Like a Pro, According to McLaren Drivers Lando Norris and Oscar Piastri

📅 Nov 25, 2026  |  Category: Sports  |  Read Source →

If you purchase an independently reviewed product or service through a link on our website, Rolling Stone may receive an affiliate commission.

As the 2026 F1 season made its final U.S. stop of the year, Las Vegas once again turned its neon-lit streets into an impressive, 17-turn race that wound its way through The Strip, with drivers whizzing past the city’s most iconic landmarks. On Sunday, Max Verstappen took home the checkered flag at the Formula 1 Las Vegas Grand Prix after a nail-biting finish, with McLaren’s Lando Norris and Oscar Piastri posting a double-podium finish to complete the top three.

With F1’s growing prominence in the U.S., new fans continue to enter the high-speed world of racing. But while the rules of Formula 1 seem simple enough — the first car to pass the checkered flag after 50-odd laps, wins — it does take some time to truly understand the complexities of the sport.

Rolling Stone sat down with McLaren drivers Oscar Piastri and Lando Norris ahead of the Las Vegas Grand Prix to glean some tips on how new fans can optimize their viewing experience.

Norris advises the audience to watch onboard videos and listen to the commentators who make the race more understandable. “When you then see the onboard cameras in the driver’s eye [line], I think it looks a lot more realistic [since] that is what I’m seeing with my head,” he says, “So I think that gives a much better experience. The more that they can do that, and get the sensation of speed, the easier it will be for people to understand.”

And while new viewers might only focus on the leaderboard, Piastri begs to differ. “I think the first thing about Formula 1 is not everyone’s car is the same, so I think just because some people aren’t winning, it doesn’t necessarily mean that they’re worse than the people that are winning,” he says. “Oftentimes the racing in the midfield can sometimes be more entertaining than the racing at the front.”

The drivers both say that strategy shifts within varying climate conditions, tire compounds and their impact to performance and longevity, and the car’s on-track behavior are just a few of the factors being considered on the sidelines during every race.

As part of their preparation, the team reviews prior races of the current season, as well as footage of the same track last year to see any learnings that could apply. Norris emphasizes how the addition of new cameras showing extra angles on the track can help the driver’s pre-race POV while adding to the immersive fan experience.

T-Mobile, F1’s regional 5G innovation partner, supports this by (literally) taking connectivity to new heights, with more than 20 5G-connected cameras that supply additional angles and track views. With its partnership with Apple TV, more angles are made available to the customer, in addition to a private 5G network to ensure reliable connectivity for broadcast feeds.

“We saw an opportunity with the technology-forward nature of the sport to be able to come in and look at how could we provide network solutions that are actually making the experience better,” offers Amy Azzi, VP of Sponsorships and Events at T-Mobile.

Azzi highlights the “additional broadcast views and angles, using our connectivity, and devices that are able to capture those angles and bring them into a more functional broadcast or into the app, which allow more opportunities for fans to engage.”

For fans who can’t watch the races live, the network giant recently launched T-Mix, where customers can mix and match benefits and entertainment options every three months, including streamers like Apple TV, home of the F1 U.S. broadcast, alongside other premier streamers, travel partners, and music providers.

At the end of the day, the McLaren duo’s most important connections are to their supporters. Piastri says he’s collected his fair share of fan gifts this year, shouting out a glitter-covered cowboy hat in his helmet design, to a hardcover book with special fan messages. “Hearing some some people’s stories and how coming to an F1 race is literally their life dream, and being able to to help them kind of fulfill that dream is is really cool,” he says.

Norris emphasizes that fans are the most important thing in their sport. To wit: the driver kicked off race week by doing an exclusive meet-and-greet at T-Mobile’s Las Vegas Signature Store, where fans got to experience an F1 simulator and other racing-inspired activations while getting up close and personal to one of the sports biggest stars.

“The fans are the reason our sport is what it is, and you don’t just say it because you have to,” he says. “What I love to do is the driving [but] the bonus I get out of it is I have people traveling around the world, supporting me, cheering me on, wanting me to do well. I feel like I need to go out and perform well for them to make it feel like their trip was worth it.”

T-Mobile has been part of the F1 Las Vegas Grand Prix since its inaugural race in 2023, and the mobile carrier once again brought its members-only Club Magenta viewing experience to the track this year, while also sponsoring performances from artists like Ja Rule, Gorgon City and Natasha Bedingfield on the T-Mobile Stage. Saturday culminated with the first-ever official post-race show with the Backstreet Boys at Sphere.

Arista Networks vs. SentinelOne: Which Technology Stock Is a Better Buy for 2027?

📅 Nov 25, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Arista's 40% net margin meets SentinelOne's AI-driven growth, but only one of these stocks offers the steadier path to 2027.

By Seena Hassouna – Updated Nov 24, 2026 at 9:23PM EST

10 stocks we like better than Arista Networks ›

Should you prioritize the hardware infrastructure that powers the cloud or the software that protects it? Choosing between Arista Networks (ANET -5.47%) and SentinelOne (S -2.00%) requires balancing established profitability with high-growth potential.

Arista designs the high-speed networking equipment essential for modern data centers, while SentinelOne provides an autonomous security platform. Both companies are central to the expansion of artificial intelligence, making them notable contenders for investors who want exposure to the future of computing.

Arista Networks designs and sells cloud networking hardware and software for massive data centers, campus, and routing environments. With over 10,000 global customers, it serves cloud titans and specialized providers, including a strategic reliance on Broadcom (AVGO -3.98%) for merchant silicon. Customer concentration adds a layer of risk to the business, as sales to two specific end customers represented nearly 16% and 26% of total revenue in 2025.

In the 12 months ended Sept. 30, 2026, revenue reached close to $11.9 billion, representing growth of approximately 40.7% compared to the prior year. This expansion was accompanied by a net income of nearly $4.8 billion and a net margin of 40%. The company maintains a strong competitive position among tech stocks by providing the plumbing that powers the world's largest internet companies.

As of its December 2025 balance sheet, the current ratio is roughly 3.0x, which measures a company's ability to cover short-term debts with short-term assets. The debt-to-equity ratio is approximately 0.0x, indicating a very low level of total debt relative to shareholder equity. In the fiscal year ended Dec. 31, 2025, free cash flow was nearly $4.3 billion, which is the cash remaining after paying for operations and equipment.

SentinelOne provides AI-powered cybersecurity through its Singularity Platform, which detects and responds to attacks across endpoints, cloud workloads, and containers. The company targets organizations of all sizes and works with strategic partners such as N-able (NABL -0.99%) and Cloudflare (NET -1.32%) to reach its customers. Much of its growth is driven by its multi-tenant model, which allows security providers to manage multiple distinct clients through a single interface.

In the 12 months ended July 31, 2026, revenue reached nearly $1.1 billion, representing growth of nearly 21.1%. Despite this top-line expansion, the company reported a net loss of approximately $340.1 million and a negative net margin of 31%. This reflects the early stage of its growth cycle as it prioritizes market share and platform development over immediate bottom-line profits.

Based on its January 2026 balance sheet, a current ratio of 1.4x indicates the company has sufficient current assets to cover its short-term liabilities. The debt-to-equity ratio is close to 0.0x. In the fiscal year ended Jan. 31, 2026, free cash flow was nearly $75.9 million, though stock-based compensation represented roughly 388.4% of operating cash flow, meaning reported cash generation is heavily inflated by this non-cash add-back.

Arista Networks faces significant risks from high revenue concentration among a small number of massive cloud customers. One of the biggest complaints that has plagued Escape From Tarkov, especially after the 1.0 launch, was loading times. When playing online, loading the map and assets and actually connecting to a server were done in tandem. The practical outcome was that you'd often spend 5, sometimes even 10 minutes or more staring at a screen.

Escape From Tarkov developer Nikita Buyanov reveals details about BSG's next game.

Starting with patch 1.3.5.0, Escape From Tarkov now does the asset buffering and server matchmaking in parallel, shaving some precious minutes off of the loading screen.

This will have a particularly tangible impact for users who aren't hogging up 32GB of RAM instead of sharing it with poor Sam Altman. On top of that, the issues where uncontrolled RAM usage could happen during long gaming sessions have finally been fixed.

Game server performance has also undergone an optimization pass. The practical implication here is that you'll have to deal with less hitreg and packet loss issues. Seasonal transitions will now work with preloading landscape materials to significantly reduce RAM usage.

In the future, the change to RenderStack will allow Tarkov to use DLSS Frame Generation and FSR 4.0.

It will allow Tarkov to use NVIDIA DLSS technology and FSR 2 and FSR 3

If a player happens to lose connection during a raid due to network issues, it will now be easier and faster to join back up. With the new addition, the game won’t need to reload the entire map, resources, or world state, as it will only require the connection to come back, and you’ll be joined back up with everything else stored where it was left off.

These changes won’t be implemented when the game client closes unexpectedly, such as OS failure, PC restart, and similar changes.

Your First Look at 7th Avenue's First NYC Showroom

📅 Nov 25, 2026  |  Category: Business  |  Read Source →

You don't need much of an imagination to picture yourself curled up on one of 7th Avenue's sectionals. One look at the extra deep seats and plush cushions is enough to have you planning your movie marathon—pass the popcorn, please. But even so, a couch is the sort of thing you want to try before you buy. With the opening of a new flagship showroom in New York City's SoHo neighborhood, the brand is hoping you'll make a day of it; get comfortable, stay a while.

This month, 7th Avenue cut the ribbon on a 3,240-square-foot storefront in a historic cast-iron building at 476 Broome Street. It's the 30th brick-and-mortar space in the US and first permanent location in NYC to open since the brand's founding as a direct-to-consumer business in 2022. Co-founder and CEO Billy Shaw says that he and co-founder Josh Stinson decided to put down roots in the Big Apple after seeing the way New Yorkers took to their hero product—the "World's Greatest Modular Sofa," they call it—during the years they operated a pop-up shop in the city.

“The main showroom celebrates volume and openness, with soaring ceilings, dramatic columns, and refined architectural detailing,” says Stinson.

Co-founders Stinson and Shaw sourced design details for the showroom from around the world. “[These items] have an Old-World patina that balances the modern, contemporary aesthetics of our sofas,” says Shaw.

"We were inspired to create 7th Avenue to solve for a frustration we kept hearing from customers: Finding a sofa that was both beautifully designed and genuinely practical for everyday living was surprisingly difficult," Shaw says. (The brand famously uses water-repellent and stain-resistant performance fabrics, and all sofa covers can be removed and thrown in the washing machine for cleaning.) New Yorkers appreciated the modular designs, which can fit into petite elevators and through the narrow doors and hallways ubiquitous in city homes (gotta love a pre-war building!). “Our sofas arrive in individual boxes, making those tight spaces easier to navigate,” he adds.

A store in New York was a no-brainer, but that doesn't mean finding a location was easy. Shaw and Stinson, who is COO, considered addresses in the Flatiron District, where shoppers can bop in and out of home retailers—like Herman Miller, Design Within Reach, ABC Carpet & Home, Crate & Barrel, the list goes on—without leaving a three-block radius. But ultimately they couldn't let go of SoHo's "unmatched" energy. "We wanted to find a place that is grand and historic, paying homage to NYC’s architectural heritage," says Stinson. With 14-foot ceilings and a skylight, "the space on Broome Street creates a sense of openness and grandeur from the moment customers walk through the door."

Just how durable are 7th Avenue's furniture pieces? During the SoHo store's opening party, guests were encouraged to drop their drinks on the sofas in a “spill lab.”

Shaw and Stinson, who designed the space themselves, say they used the building's architecture to create different vignettes and experiences for guests. The main showroom, for instance, with its dramatic columns and open space, is perfect for showing off 7th Avenue's larger sectionals. Elsewhere, a daybed is nestled into an alcove for a more intimate scene. "We wanted to demonstrate that great furniture design isn't simply about size," says Stinson. "It's about proportion and the relationship between a piece and the space surrounding it."

The two also wanted to show that "beautiful design should work for real life." "That meant balancing the stateliness of the cast-iron architecture and high ceilings with warm materials, organic textures, and comfortable places to sit," says Shaw.

A light and bright backdrop of European oak and Turkish travertine is dotted with treasures gathered from around the world: coral stone coffee tables quarried from volcanic islands in Indonesia, century-old olive oil jars from Turkey and Morocco, sculptural plants like a Hawaiian dragon tree. "A Queensland Bottle Tree Bonsai, native to Australia and sourced from Los Angeles plant specialists The Plant Daddies, was shaped over two years to achieve its distinctive curved form," says Stinson. These details, Shaw adds, have "an Old-World patina that balances the modern, contemporary aesthetics of our sofas."

Guests readily settled into 7th Avenue's plush sofas at the flagship's opening party.

"We want customers to feel comfortable settling into this beautiful sofa, yet imagining it in their own home and being used in their day-to-day lives," says Stinson. At the opening party, props scattered about the furniture brought this idea to life.

For all the finery, 476 Broome Street feels more like someone's home than an art gallery—and that's very on purpose. "We want customers to feel comfortable settling into this beautiful sofa, yet imagining it in their own home and being used in their day-to-day lives," says Stinson. The co-founders want visitors to interact with the furniture. Indeed, a cheeky neon sign that reads "Come Spill on Me" hangs over an emerald velvet couch; at the store's opening party, red wine was passed with nonchalance and guests were invited to plop onto a double-wide sofa and pose for photos with an artful disarray of newspapers, a pizza box, and an overturned gag martini glass—no need to take their shoes off.

According to Shaw and Stinson, great design should accommodate the realities of modern life, messy things like kids, pets, and parties. "Our clients appreciate beautiful, thoughtfully designed spaces, but they also want to live in them without hesitation," says Shaw. "Ultimately, we feel that luxury should be lived in, not simply admired."
