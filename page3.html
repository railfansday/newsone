Building your own mini PC is harder than you would think

📅 Nov 21, 2026  |  Category: Business  |  Read Source →

Building a custom PC is a wonderfully satisfying feeling. Even if you've never built one for yourself, it's ingrained itself into the world of content creation and social media so fiercely that everyone has most likely at least watched someone partially build a PC at some point.

As you may have noticed, there are so many different form factors for cases and unique enclosures. One of the more interesting ones that has been carving out its own funky niche in the market is the mini PC build. Because of the unique form factors and limited space these cases allow, they come with challenges that make a build of this type a bit more difficult than normal.

Unlike larger mid- and full-range towers, the mini PC builds tend to focus on a straight-ahead approach with very little wiggle room for frills. Things that we take for granted in larger builds, like room for maximum airflow or a larger GPU, become pertinent struggles in the mini PC world.

One of the core things to understand upfront about mini PC builds is the case itself. What we know as mini PCs tend to be built within Mini-ITX or microATX cases. For as small as they are, you might think they'd cost significantly less than their larger siblings. In a lot of situations, that's true, but Mini-ITX cases also come in a lot of different types. Some of them offer fun bells and whistles, which can run your bill up before you know it.

A lot of the mainstream mini computers that you can buy from companies, like Apple's Mac mini or certain models of the ASUS NUC, are built with an SoC — a system-on-a-chip that combines a CPU with a GPU — and RAM sometimes soldered into the motherboard itself. Doing something like that isn't really feasible, but you can get fairly close with some commercial cases.

No matter which one you're looking at, it may be reassuring to know that despite their small stature, a lot of them are surprisingly roomy. You're still going to be limited by what the case allows in terms of dimensions and compatibility, but those are all easy to contrast and compare with the official specs or using a site like PCPartPicker to determine if a component will fit in your case.

The unorthodox shape and size of a lot of mini PC cases means that while it's entirely possible to fill them with a lot of cool stuff, your approach is going to have to be a bit more economical and predetermined. For most of your situations, a mini ITX case will only accommodate a mini-ITX motherboard, while microATX cases will be able to fit in both Mini-ITX and microATX motherboards due to the micro cases being a bit bigger on average.

Airflow also becomes something of a problem, as the smaller size of these cases means your internal components have a lot less room to breathe. There's usually very little space once all the guts are mashed inside, and that's especially true if you go for a discrete GPU as opposed to an APU that combines the CPU and the GPU. The prices of most small form factor, or SFF, PC components are also a bit steep.

SFF power supplies, motherboards, CPU coolers and GPUs can all contribute a surprising amount to the final price of a machine, which may make going for a standard ATX build more appealing. Finding all these unique-sized parts isn't even the most challenging issue when building a mini PC, as the physical build itself can be far more difficult than that of a standard ATX machine.

There's a lot less room, but you also need to be more careful with the actual installation of parts. A standard ATX build can largely be put together in any order, but mini PC builds need to be put together in a specific order due to the part sizes and clearance concerns.

John Wayne Almost Played Clint Eastwood's Dirty Harry

📅 Nov 21, 2026  |  Category: Entertainment  |  Read Source →

​​August is a freelance film writer. She studied journalism and cinema at American University. She is a member of the journalism honor society, Kappa Tau Alpha. August has written for various networks and publications, including PBS, Gargoyle Magazine, TheThings, and Identity Theory Magazine. Before returning to entertainment writing, August worked in the Automotive section of U.S. News & World Report. In her free time, August enjoys watching movies in theaters, watching movies at home, listening to podcasts about movies, reading books about movies, watching video essays on movies, talking about movies, thinking about movies, and doing crosswords.

Hollywood film experienced a major shift in the 1970s, following the fall of American idealism and Golden Age of Hollywood. Traditionally optimistic genres such as westerns and musicals waned in popularity while gritty crime-dramas and conspiracy flicks took center stage. This left traditional Hollywood stars in a state of flux, perhaps having to transition into more ambiguous roles. Such changes were deemed necessary even for the likes of John Wayne, who nearly stepped into a major role that now feels tailor-made for Clint Eastwood.

John Wayne was on top of Hollywood for an incredible length of time. Shortly after his leading role in Stagecoach, Wayne gradually evolved into the face of the Western movie genre. He starred in a multitude of high-quality Westerns in the 1940s, and his career exploded further in the 1950s, with the actor making some of the best movies of his entire career, including The Searchers and The Quiet Man.

While still a period of success, the 1960s were a bit quieter for John Wayne, whose specific brand of Westerns gave way to the grittier films made popular by Spaghetti Westerns like Clint Eastwood and Sergio Leone's Dollars Trilogy. In the 1970s, Wayne adjusted to the changing demands of Hollywood, and that approach could very well have landed him in the role of a now-iconic crime thriller.

While Clint Eastwood famously held the titular role, he was not the first name in the running. Western legend John Wayne was reportedly considered for the role of Detective Callahan in Dirty Harry. There are conflicting reports concerning his reasons for turning down the role, or whether he was actually considered for the part at all. Despite some mixed information, it is undeniable that Dirty Harry would be a completely different film with Wayne in the lead.

Several Hollywood icons were reportedly in the running to lead Dirty Harry, including George C. Scott, Paul Newman, Frank Sinatra, and John Wayne (via Far Out Magazine). Sinatra was said to have signed on until a hand injury forced him to withdraw. Reports suggest that Wayne was next on the list, but turned down the role. Warner Bros. released a press release on December 17, 1970, announcing Eastwood as Harry Callahan in the coming drama (via AFI).

There are several reasons as to why Wayne would decline such an opportunity. Michael Munn's 2001 biography, John Wayne: The Man Behind The Myth, quotes Wayne as admitting that the decision came down to pride and dissatisfaction with the character. He was reluctant to accept what he considered “Sinatra’s rejections.” He was also reluctant to inhibit a crooked cop character, which went against his morals and Hollywood persona. However, as the book notes, he later came to realize that he had played rogue heroes before, in Westerns such as The Searchers.

"I turned it down for what seemed to me to be three very good reasons. The first is that they offered it to Frank Sinatra first, but he’d hurt his hand and couldn’t do it. I don’t like being offered Sinatra’s rejections. Put that one down to pride. The second reason is that I thought Harry was a rogue cop. Put that down to narrow-mindedness because when I saw the picture I realized that Harry was the kind of part I’d played often enough; a guy who lives within the law but breaks the rules when he really has to in order to save others.”

Outside of Wayne's own feelings about the role, both he and Sinatra were also considered too old to play Harry. Wayne was a good 15 years older than Eastwood. For this reason, some sources suggest that Wayne was never officially offered the titular lead.

Dirty Harry may not have achieved such astronomic success without Eastwood helming the cast. The actor possessed the intensity and edge that Wayne lacked.

While both Wayne and Eastwood came from a Western background, Eastwood was more versed in playing lawless anti-heroes. Wayne gained popularity in traditional Westerns of the 1940s and ‘50s, portraying generally altruistic figures. Meanwhile, Eastwood entered the genre during the Spaghetti Western period in which morally grey protagonists were in vogue. He played anti-heroic leads in hits such as 1966’s The Good, the Bad and the Ugly.

There is little question about how Wayne would have played Harry Callahan, as he later starred in two Dirty Harry imitations—McQ and Brannigan. In both instances, he seemed ill-suited to the rogue-cop archetype. Wayne read as a man entrenched in a traditional American idealism, clearly uncomfortable with the crime genre's random violence. In contrast to Clint Eastwood, he was ultimately stiff and unconvincing, contrary to the commanding presence he exudes in his Westerns.

Put To The Test, Those Special Engine Cleaning Oils Do Some Lifting

📅 Nov 21, 2026  |  Category: Automotive  |  Read Source →

Motor oil naturally helps to flush out common contaminants, and there's a whole supply of products specifically made to keep the mechanical parts of a vehicle clean and running. From Top Tier gasoline complete with detergents to some types of engine motor oil — these treatments and additives are designed and marketed to you, the consumer, to keep things clean internally so your car lasts. Do they really do what they claim to do, though? The Motor Oil Geek on YouTube put those "cleaning" oils to the test to see if they do make a difference.

The Motor Oil Geek is Lake Speed Jr., of SPEEDiagnostix, known for its used oil analysis services. Speed himself is a Certified Lubrication Specialist and OIl Analyst who used that expertise to help Joe Gibbs Racing for 12 years — a testing ground sometimes more brutal than the testing cars go through in-house.

Using those skills, he put three "cleaning" motor oils up to the test: Mobil 1 Advanced Clean, Valvoline Restore & Protect, and Pennzoil Ultra Platinum. There's actually a fourth oil, Valvoline Premium Blue Restore Gen2 — but that's a separate, yet important test, also included in the video. The results didn't match what real in-engine testing produced, but even in this comparable bench testing scenario, these oils managed to clean up expectations.

The Motor Oil Geek put the three oils through what's called an ASTM sequence IIIH test, which is a standard test used to evaluate engine oils at high temperatures and high speeds. Typically, these tests are conducted in an actual running engine for 90 total hours, broken down into four 20-hour test segments and one 10-hour segment. For bench test purposes, Speed ran the oils for 168 hours with varying temperatures from room temperature to 150 degrees Celsius, to better emulate real-time conditions of an engine from cold runs to high-speed performance.

He used pistons with considerable deposits from a turbocharged Mini Cooper engine, each marked with a letter code to track which one was tested in what oil. Those pistons were sent off to be professionally rated before the test, as well as after, to measure improved "cleanliness."

Three of those pistons were then hung separately from a device so they could soak in an oil in 1000 mL beakers over a magnetic stirring hot plate. It's a way to control the important aspects of the test concerning time, temperature, and agitation (movement), so Speed could focus on the concentration and cleaning. The fourth piston was soaked in the Valvoline Premium Blue Restore Gen2, which claimed it could clean parts just soaked in the oil.

In the end, all four oils cleaned some of the deposits on the pistons. The most improved was the fourth piston that soaked in the oil, which The Motor Oil Geek explained contains an agent called Ester, also used in two-stroke oils to keep things clean. And keep in mind, they perform even better in a running engine. So, if you're at all worried about buildup slowing you down, these cleaning oils might actually do the trick.

Michael Burry Says He's Looking at Netflix Stock in the High $30s. I'd Consider It Now, Well Above That.

📅 Nov 21, 2026  |  Category: Business  |  Read Source →

The famous investor is eyeing a price around a third lower. Is it worth waiting for?

By Daniel Sparks – Nov 21, 2026 at 6:22PM EST

Netflix (NFLX -2.96%) has Michael Burry's attention. Just not at today's price.

That's a long way down. Netflix shares closed at $58.46 on Thursday, Nov. 19, their lowest close since May 2024 (using split-adjusted prices, because Netflix did a 10-for-1 stock split in November 2025).

With the stock trading near $58 as of this writing, a price in the high $30s would cut about another third off the shares.

I don't think investors have to wait for that. At today's price, Netflix already looks cheap for a company whose profits are still climbing.

Michael Burry. Image source: Getty Images.

Burry has been circling Netflix for months. In July, he figured that Netflix at $67.75 per share traded at about 1.6 times its intrinsic value, which comes out to a value of roughly $42 a share by his math. He also said then that the stock would have to drop to the low $50s to get closer to an ideal buying opportunity.

So, the high $30s isn't a random number. It would let him buy under his own estimate of what the company is worth.

His concern is how long Netflix's content keeps its value. In July, he compared the streaming giant to Walt Disney and its Pixar studio, whose content he called evergreen. Much of what Netflix makes, he suggested, is OK for now but doesn't improve with age.

Sure, that's a fair worry about any business built on hit shows. But it's a long-term debate. In the meantime, Netflix's profits keep growing, making the high $30s look like a really pessimistic price.

Call the high $30s $38 a share. On average, analysts expect Netflix to earn about $3.80 per share in 2027, so a $38 stock would cost about 10 times next year's expected earnings. At around $58, shares trade at about 15 times next year's expected earnings.

By comparison, the S&P 500 (^GSPC -3.70%) traded at 17.7 times its expected earnings for the next 12 months on Nov. 5, according to FactSet.

Said another way, Netflix stock already costs less than the average stock relative to its earnings. Burry's price would put it at a discount of over 40% to the market's price-to-earnings ratio.

For $38 to be a market-average price, Netflix would have to earn only about $2.15 per share. It earned $2.53 per share in 2025 (split-adjusted too), and its earnings per share have grown year over year in every quarter of 2026 so far.

The high $30s would only make sense, then, if Netflix's profits were about to shrink.

They don't look close to shrinking. Netflix's operating margin was 26.7% in 2024 and 29.5% in 2025, and the business is targeting 31.5% for 2026. Its third-quarter operating margin hit 34.6%. And management is guiding for a 26% margin in the fourth quarter, above the 24.5% Netflix reported a year before, even with costs from newly announced job cuts. All told, Netflix says its outlook implies operating income growth of over 20% this year.

Also, Netflix keeps returning cash to shareholders. It spent $4.8 billion buying back its own stock in the third quarter alone, and it still had $22.3 billion in repurchase authorizations left.

Sales growth, though, keeps slowing. Revenue growth has fallen every quarter this year, to 11.8% year over year in the third quarter from 16.2% in the first. And management expects around 11.5% for the fourth quarter, while it has lowered its 2026 ad revenue outlook to about $2.7 billion from about $3 billion.

But a business still growing sales by double digits while raising its operating margin looks unlikely to wind up earning less than it did in 2025.

Could Burry still get his price? He might. Netflix stock is already down over half from its June 2025 record, and a stock that's fallen this far can keep falling.

But waiting for the high $30s is arguably a bet that Netflix's earnings stall. At around 15 times next year's expected earnings, Netflix stock already prices in a lot of caution, I think. I'd consider buying shares now rather than waiting for Burry's number.

AppLovin vs. Reddit: Which Technology Stock Is a Better Buy for 2027?

📅 Nov 21, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

AppLovin is growing fast and is highly profitable, but its stock has been volatile. Reddit is profitable and still growing, but Google's AI search is sending it fewer visitors.

By Sara Appino – Updated Nov 21, 2026 at 6:19PM EST

The digital advertising landscape is shifting as data-driven platforms fight for marketing budgets. Choosing between AppLovin (APP -8.11%) and Reddit (RDDT -5.06%) requires weighing a seasoned software powerhouse against a rapidly growing social platform.

AppLovin specializes in ad-tech software that helps developers grow their apps, while Reddit operates a massive network of user-generated communities. Both companies are successfully capitalizing on specialized data to attract advertisers, but they operate with different business models and cost structures.

AppLovin provides a software-based marketing platform and advertising solutions for consumer brands and gaming apps. The company notes that some of its customers are also competitors, such as Meta Platforms, Alphabet, Amazon (AMZN -4.10%), and Unity Software (U -4.18%). Following the June 2025 divestiture of its Apps business, the company focuses entirely on its high-margin software platform.

In the 12 months ended Sept. 30, 2026, revenue reached nearly $7.6 billion, representing growth of approximately 46.5% compared to the prior period. Net income was roughly $5.0 billion during this time frame, supported by a strong net margin of 65.9%. This performance highlights the company's ability to scale its ad-tech infrastructure efficiently while maintaining significant profitability.

As of its December 2025 balance sheet, the debt-to-equity ratio was 1.7x, which compares total debt to shareholder equity. The current ratio was 3.3x, a measure of the ability to pay short-term bills using current assets. In the fiscal year ended Dec. 31, 2025, free cash flow, which is cash from operations minus capital expenditures, was nearly $3.9 billion.

Reddit is establishing itself as a unique player among communication stocks by leveraging its vast array of user-generated communities. The company generates roughly 94% of its revenue from third-party advertisers, though it is expanding into content licensing for AI model training. While it serves a global audience, Reddit faces some customer concentration, with its top ten advertisers accounting for approximately 21% of total revenue.

In the 12 months ended Sept. 30, 2026, revenue reached nearly $3.1 billion, fueled by growth of approximately 62.2%. Net income was close to $991.7 million, marking a significant transition to profitability compared to previous years. The platform continues to focus on increasing user engagement and expanding its data-licensing partnerships to diversify its income streams.

As of its December 2025 balance sheet, the debt-to-equity ratio was 0.0x, while the current ratio was 11.6x, measuring the company's ability to cover short-term debts with current assets. In the fiscal year ended Dec. 31, 2025, free cash flow was close to $684.2 million. Note that stock-based compensation represented roughly 49.7% of operating cash flow, which inflates reported cash generation since SBC is a non-cash expense added back in the cash flow statement.

AppLovin faces significant risks related to its reliance on third-party platforms like Apple and Alphabet, which can alter access to user data through privacy changes. The company also deals with intense competition from large technology firms, including Amazon and Unity Software, in the mobile app ecosystem. Furthermore, revenue is heavily concentrated in the gaming sector, leaving the business vulnerable to shifts in that specific market.

Reddit relies heavily on advertising revenue, making the company vulnerable to volatility in marketing budgets and user engagement levels. The platform depends on volunteer moderators to maintain its communities and relies on cloud service providers like Amazon and Alphabet for its core infrastructure. Changes in search engine algorithms or a decline in user growth could significantly impact its site traffic and overall business results.

Reddit appears to trade at a more attractive valuation based on its Forward P/E and P/S ratio, which measures market capitalization against sales over the past twelve months.

Valuation metrics include sourcing from Financial Modeling Prep (FMP) and may differ from other data providers.

I'd go with AppLovin. It has built a leading, highly profitable ad business in mobile games, and it is just getting started in online shopping. Advertisers pay it for results like installs and purchases, so it grows when its customers do. Last quarter, sales jumped 55% year over year, and management guided for another strong quarter, exceeding analyst expectations.

The stock has been volatile this year as the company faced short-seller attacks and investor lawsuits it disputes. But the latest quarter beat the company's own targets by a wide margin, and the stock is still down for the year, which gives long-term investors a lower entry point.

To give Reddit its due, it is very profitable and its sales are still growing fast. But Google's AI search is sending it fewer visitors, its U.S. users fell last quarter, and it warned of more pressure ahead, which sent the stock plunging. AppLovin faces no such traffic problem, since its ads run inside other companies' mobile apps, and advertisers are spending more with it. With fast growth, big profits, and a whole new market opening up in online shopping, AppLovin is my pick for 2027.

Forget Butter And Oil: Cook Steak In This Rich Ingredient For A Better Crust & Beefier Flavor

📅 Nov 21, 2026  |  Category: Other  |  Read Source →

When preparing steak, cooks often focus on selecting the best cut, then preparing it with a specific method to an ideal doneness. Less emphasis is placed on the fat employed for the task — although what you pour into the pan also impacts both the flavor and texture of your meal. So skip standard pantry staples like butter and oil, and try a version with beef tallow instead.

So — what is beef tallow? It's essentially rendered beef fat, an ingredient that lends numerous advantages to steak night. Foremost is the ultra-savory profile: Tallow is made by slowly cooking down beef trimmings, resulting in a rich, meaty taste. This staple works as a flavor enhancer in addition to a cooking medium, complementing the deep, savory character of steak. And if you buy a higher-quality grass-fed variety, you may impart even more complexity, introducing earthy and grassy notes to your dining experience.

In parallel, beef tallow helps create a mouthwatering crust. The ingredient boasts a higher smoke point than butter and some other fats. So whether you're searing on high heat or finishing the steak in the oven, the fat can withstand high temperatures, enabling browning and Maillard reactions that create a terrific crust. That adds up to an elevated steak experience, all from swapping out the fat that goes into the pan.

Preparing a steak with beef tallow doesn't require any special accommodations. The fat is solid at room temperature and can be shelf-stable when properly stored, making it an easy pantry addition. You'll only need about a tablespoon per portion: Simply spoon the tallow into the skillet, where it'll melt and cover the cooking surface. It's a cooking medium that's especially effective on cast iron — whether a griddle or a pan — as it can also be used to season the cookware. You can also use tallow when grilling by coating both the cut itself and the grill grates with a thin layer of beef fat.

Any steak cut can be prepared in tallow; just note that it lends a rich flavor. So if you're making a mild-tasting filet mignon, for example, the addition could be overpowering. It's better to pair tallow with robust cuts like a ribeye, porterhouse, or New York strip, thereby producing an especially beef-forward meal. Otherwise, there's no need to worry about the details. Simply add a spoonful to your pan and sear away; this staple makes for a dependably delicious steak experience.

It's been a while since Tarantino had anything new to direct, but he is set to return to the screen later this month with a new release, The Further Mis-Adventures of Cliff Booth, which stars Brad Pitt and Yahya Abdul-Mateen II. Tarantino wrote the script for the film, but he did not direct, relying on David Fincher (Se7en, The Social Network) to helm the film. Earlier this month, Fincher confirmed that he had to cut dozens of pages from the script Tarantino first delivered him, citing that the material he wrote was closer to two movies than just one.

The Further Mis-Adventures of Cliff Booth is a direct sequel to the 2019 film, Once Upon a Time... in Hollywood, which stars Leonardo DiCaprio opposite Brad Pitt. Tarantino even helped Brad Pitt win his first acting Oscar for his performance in the film, which is partially why his return to the role later this month has so much motion behind it. Seven years after leaving theaters, Once Upon a Time... in Hollywood has found a streaming home on Prime Video, where it's bounced in and out of the top 10 for over 100 days this year. For those without a subscription to Prime Video, the film is also available to stream for free courtesy of the Roku Channel, which doesn't even require a subscription or account for its users. There are no shortage of ways to watch Tarantino's original Hollywood masterpiece.
