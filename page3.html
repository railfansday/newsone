Josh Allen has run for 5,000 yards and only 4 other QBs can say that

📅 Nov 10, 2026  |  Category: Sports  |  Read Source →

Josh Allen has run for 5,000 yards in the NFL. For a guy whose primary job is throwing the football, that's a lot of ground covered.

The Buffalo Bills quarterback became just the fifth quarterback in NFL history to reach that total during Buffalo's Week 9 game against the Minnesota Vikings. Allen crossed the 5k mark in the third quarter on a series of designed runs.

He entered the game at 4991 then had a 7-yard run in the first quarter to get to 4998. He lost one yard on the kneel down to end the first half to fall back to 4997. A 5-yard scramble from the Minnesota 21 was followed by a 3-yard run to make sure he was over. And counting…

Allen joins Lamar Jackson, Michael Vick, Cam Newton, and Russell Wilson in the 5,000-yard group. That's the entire list, and Allen has put himself on it in his ninth NFL season.

Bills fans have watched those yards come a few different ways. There have been scrambles after the protection breaks down, designed runs, and more than a few plays where Allen decides the best way to get a first down is to go through whoever is standing in front of him. They all count. Of course, if he slid more often, like we all keep screaming for him to do, it would have taken a few more carries to get there.

Allen already owns the NFL record for career rushing touchdowns by a quarterback. Now he has another number to go with it, and another reminder of how much of Buffalo's offense has come from his legs.

Casting Brandon Sanderson's Live-Action Stormlight Archive Series: All 9 Main Roles

📅 Nov 10, 2026  |  Category: Entertainment  |  Read Source →

Faith Roswell is a Senior Writer on Screen Rant's Classic TV team. Since earning her degree in Creative Writing over a decade ago, Faith has written articles on film and TV from a variety of different angles. Faith now combines her knowledge of psychology with her love of monster movies to give more insight into what makes the best ones.  You may have read her Screen Rant lists and features covering horror, sci-fi, and fantasy, or read her Amazon Top 10 book, "Movie Monsters of the Deep." Faith has had an extensive career as a writer, appearing on BBC live radio, researching true crime for Rotten Mango podcast, and writing for publications including Mental Floss, Atlas Obscura, and The Daily Jaws before beginning here at Screen Rant.

The upcoming adaptation of Brandon Sanderson's Stormlight Archive looks set to be the most exciting upcoming fantasy TV series currently in development. While original TV series like Stranger Things have made their mark on the fantasy landscape, most adaptations, with the exception of Dungeons & Dragons projects, have not fared so well. The Wheel of Time and The Witcher looked set to be groundbreaking achievements for the genre, but where they failed to live up to their potential, The Stormlight Archive may be the fantasy TV show franchise we need.

One main reason is due to Brandon Sanderson's unprecedented creative control over his projects. The author has been more involved than virtually any other, which has allowed him to oversee the creative process, including the casting, which he has been passionate about. The best fantasy TV show ensemble casts have excellent chemistry and acting ability, but bringing an already beloved character to life is a challenge for any actor, and while Sanderson has a very specific vision, these actors could do the characters justice.

Of all the actors who would be perfect for Brandon Sanderson's Stormlight Archive, Aramis Knight has repeatedly drawn the attention of fans, who have seen him as a potentially perfect choice for Kaladin Stormblessed. Kaladin's formidable martial arts and healing skills make him one of the series' most conflicted characters, suffering from depression and survivors' guilt, and torn between the desire to heal and the need to kill in order to protect people.

This internal struggle makes Kaladin one of fantasy's most interesting heroes, and as he also needs to inspire loyalty from the people he commands, this adds an extra layer of nuance. Knight's martial arts-heavy work in Into the Badlands and Ms. Marvel make him an ideal actor to handle the action scenes, while he often plays characters with difficult backstories, proving him capable of taking on the role of a man with the world on his shoulders.

Shallan Davar is one of the central characters in The Stormlight Archive, and a fan favorite who has inspired considerable debate over her potential casting. She is intelligent, witty, artistic, and complicated in a very human way, as most of Sanderson's characters are. Coming from a troubled family background, she uses humor as a shield, often projecting a confident and charming exterior as she struggles beneath the surface.

This description could easily apply to Stranger Things' Max, Sadie Sink's breakout role, which won her the Hollywood Critics Association TV Award for Best Supporting Actress in a Streaming Series, Drama. Sadie Sink's best movies and TV shows all showcase her ability to play a multifaceted character with emotional depth that shows in her face without the need for extensive dialogue, and Shallan feels like a continuation of these characters, but at a more mature level.

Practically unstoppable on the battlefield, the highprince of Alethkar was once a ruthless warrior whose fearsome reputation as "the Blackthorn" preceded him. The fantasy genre is full of archetypes like this, but Brandon Sanderson's Cosmere books stand out for the way they turn these tropes upside-down, and the character we meet is no longer a warlord. Dalinar Kholin is now a very introspective man, and casting him requires an actor with more than just an imposing physique.

At first glance, Dalinar is similar to Khal Drogo, Jason Momoa's character in Game of Thrones, but the role needs elements of Momoa's other characters to be a successful casting. While best known as an action star with a commanding screen presence, Momoa's performances in Dune and See showcase more of the range that he is not always assumed to have, and playing out Dalinar's redemption arc would be a good opportunity to highlight his more subtle acting skills.

The friendly and charming prince is the opposite of what a reader would expect the son of the Blackthorn to be, but Adolin Kholin's fan-described "golden retriever energy" has made him one of the most popular characters in the Cosmere universe. Adolin is so important that he is one of the few characters that Brandon Sanderson has personally fan-cast. However, while Sanderson suggested Alex Landi for the role, he has since aged beyond the character.

Dallas Liu already resembles a younger Landi and did his own stunts in Avatar: the Last Airbender, where he played Prince Zuko, also a fan favorite character. Zuko's journey from villain to hero is one of the best storylines in the fantasy genre, and Liu has both the charm, humor, and physical fighting training to convincingly play Adolin, including his complicated relationship with his father.

With a former warlord as a father and a celebrated swordsman as a brother, Renarin Kholin has a lot to live up to, and the responsibility weighs on him, despite his growing knowledge that he has skills of his own. Renarin's story is one of the most unsettling in the Cosmere universe, with the younger prince becoming a Truthwatcher, which drastically changes his demeanor from a gentle, reserved, and studious boy into one who can see elements of the future.

Any actor playing Renarin must be able to capture the shyness and anxiety that Sanderson has confirmed he has, before also portraying deep and complicated wisdom far beyond that of most other characters. Sean Kaufman's performance in The Summer I Turned Pretty showcases a seemingly outgoing character experiencing moments of vulnerability, and he could expand on this role while being believable as Dallas Liu's younger brother.

The scholarly Jasnah is one of the most intelligent and politically influential women in the Cosmere universe, and her radical honesty makes her polarizing among fans. Any actor playing Jasnah must be able to straddle the line between her admirable insistence on speaking her mind and her willingness to suggest the unthinkable, as she must remain likable even while suggesting options that frighten career politicians.

Florence Faivre was captivating during her short scenes as The Expanse's Julie Mao, even while being seen through cameras and the eyes of other characters. Mao's rebellious streak as she breaks from her highly influential family hits similar notes to Jasnah's arc, despite the stories being drastically different genres and with different final fates. Faivre can convey intelligence and conviction without needing to overact, and this is likely to be the key to keeping Jasnah fascinating, even to viewers who do not like the character.

Michelle Yeoh's career-defining movies began by showcasing her work as a talented martial artist, but her nuanced character acting has taken center stage in recent years, with Everything Everywhere All At Once and Wicked both focusing on her ability to make even an exaggerated character feel real. Yeoh may still be defined by her stunts and action work, but playing the Cosmere universe's Navani Kholin would give her even more to work with, as the character has one of Roshar's leading scientific minds.

Everything Everywhere All At Once won Michelle Yeoh an Academy Award for Best Actress.

Even in book form, Navani feels vivid and three-dimensional, negotiating with kings in a political firestorm one moment and being absorbed in an experiment the next. Her dedication to her family sets her as the Kholin matriarch, but to put her on the sidelines as merely a wife and mother does not do her justice. Formidably intelligent, her curiosity and stubbornness allow her to break the rules, which often works in her favor, and this combination of grit and joy is the perfect opportunity for Michelle Yeoh to continue her streak of playing compelling and unpredictable characters.

Szeth-Son-Neturo is one of the most frightening characters in Brandon Sanderson's books. Known as "The Assassin in White," his supernatural abilities allow him to manipulate the forces of the Earth, yet he believes he is being forced to kill against his will. Obeying the possessor of his Oathstone, Szeth's mind has fractured, with the belief that he has no choice forming the basis for his every action, including murder.

Bill Skarsgård's best movies and TV shows see him playing some fantastically unhinged characters, including IT's Pennywise and Boy Kills World's nameless "Boy," but playing Szeth could redeem his attempt at The Crow's Eric Draven. His performance was one of the few factors praised by critics, and it proves that he can carry the combination of deep sorrow, eerie stillness, and lethal fighting ability that is needed to embody the horror and tragedy driving Szeth.

Hoid is not strictly a main character in the Cosmere universe, but he is one of Brandon Sanderson's best antihero characters and an important recurring figure. Showing up in various guises, Hoid is not recognized until he makes himself known. This requires an established character actor who can blend into the background until he is needed, and William Fichtner is more than capable of taking on Hoid's many guises and personas.

As a master of Lightweaving, Hoid's appearance often shifts, but Fichtner shares this chameleon-like ability to transform himself, and he has made his career playing characters who are often difficult to read. Prison Break's Alex Mahone is a more grounded example, with the character being a villain, dangerous enemy, and loyal ally over time, while always remaining somewhat mysterious. This slightly remote quality makes Fichtner perfect casting for the enigmatic Hoid.

10 Funniest Far Side Comics About Cows That Only Get Better With Age

📅 Nov 10, 2026  |  Category: World  |  Read Source →

The Far Side has never seemed to lose its appeal even decades after the end of its original run in 1995. The series is deliciously absurd, darkly hilarious in mocking a surprising array of topics, and showed a clear tendency to amp up its surreality by putting animals into mundane, humanlike situations. Many creatures have frequently graced the pages of Gary Larson's work, but his obsession with cows as seen in The Far Side is impressively well-documented. They're given just as much of a humanizing treatment as other creatures, but something about them just lands so well much of the time, with many cow comics standing the test of time.

Naturally, not all The Far Side's cow comic strips are as timeless as the rest. Carmen Miranda references feel significantly dated even during this 1980-1995 run, so gags featuring cows with fruit-basket turbans just don't connect as quickly with readers born closer to this century. But even then, there are plenty of relatable, hilarious, and delightfully weird cow comics that have aged perfectly beyond The Far Side's original run, sampled below.

Taking a panoramic perspective unlike many other of The Far Side's largely one-panel strips, this particular moment is perfectly relatable while poking fun at tourists who make it far too obvious they're not familiar with the area. Touting a camera and a cow/buffalo dictionary, they're clearly in uncharted territory while trying to make the most of it, sporting loud colorful outfits, walking on two hooves while the buffalo intently stare, bewildered by these oddball outsiders. It's often relatable to be on both sides of this experience, but as these cows may find, the grass isn't always greener on The Far Side.

Despite often sharing peaceful interactions and being commonly associated with one another on a farm setting, cows and chickens are two wildly different animals. But while cows will eagerly eat chicken feed, the same can't be said about chickens sampling cow diets, so each having their own safe havens to gather was clearly the answer for Larson. But in this case, he decided to turn this fish-out-of-water situation up a notch by throwing in a killer "gay bar" pun, turning this predominantly cow establishment into a "hay bar", turning a classic trope into a rather clever spin.

This particular Far Side comic has hardly gone unnoticed here or elsewhere, and it's no wonder why: the cows are out for revenge, and just as Hammurabi would have it, the punishment matched the crime. They set out to unleash the Auto Milker on Farmer MacDougal and his wife, which is already an upsetting and deeply weird scenario, but the dark humor of police investigating the crime scene and finding it admittedly hilarious adds to the absurdity.

Putting aside the logistics of what the Auto Milker would do to humans and the mechanical weirdness of the implicit death by suction entrapment, this comic isn't necessarily outright about human cruelty toward animals. It's arguably poking fun just at the utter (or udder) madness of human ingenuity, while leaning into the notions of how cows would react if they achieved greater cognitive consciousness.

This particular comic from the early days of The Far Side is perfectly timeless in its execution, speaking even now to memes anybody can relate to, particularly the idea of reaching cognitive consciousness around the age of 5. Described nowadays with flashbang sound effects with adults reenacting childhood as they turn off "autopilot", this strip implies the cows are not only aware of the existence of grass, but noticeably disappointed by it the moment their consciousness kicks in. But as seen in Larson's other work, these cows are otherwise standing on all fours, grazing as usual, implying this to be the early stages of the featured cow's origin story.

Maintaining the illusion of being mundane cows is apparently tricky in The Far Side, as this 1984 strip shows they regularly watch for when they can be seen by humans, actively concealing their ability to stand on two legs. It begs the question of why they would go to such lengths to trick humans, other than the believable notion that people aren't ready for creatures which, on their hind legs, would regularly tower above virtually everyone.

This particular Far Side comic is at times funny while also deeply relatable, blending Epicurean-style philosphy about appreciating the simpler things in life. The parody here is of the classic idiom, "stop and smell the roses", that is, to take in the natural beauty of what one might otherwise pass by. It's a reminder to practice mindfulness toward the goal of achieving serenity. But cows are also perfectly content eating just about anything as it is, so perhaps they're further along than humans may ever be.

Kids can be a lot for any species to handle, especially when trying to manage the household all the while. In the case of cows, their young calves could easily trample furniture and cause all sorts of property damage. But Larson's solution in this comic for The Far Side is surprisingly clever improvised playpen made from an overturned table, but with the added twist of electrified cables running between the legs across multiple levels.

But while everybody laughs at the idea of cavemen bouncing a cow around, the bigger technological achievement is right in front of them, as they appear to have made a crude, primitive version of the life net, a far more helpful invention. The Nasdaq Fell Into Correction Territory Recently. Should You Buy the Dip?

📅 Nov 10, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

The decline in the Nasdaq is being led by what took it up: AI investment.

Recent sharp volatility in the Nasdaq Composite (^IXIC +2.74%) index shouldn't surprise experienced investors, because the stock market doesn't move in a perfectly straight line over the long term. Markets will always be volatile.

The key question is whether downside moves, such as a recent one-day dip of 5.5% that sent the Nasdaq briefly into correction territory, signal an extended period of weakness or a buying opportunity. I think the answer is the latter, but with qualifications.

First, try to discern the reasons for the dip. As ever, multiple causes are likely, but history and human nature suggest a consistent theme. Simply put, rapidly rising prices push investors to misallocate resources, leading to unproductive investment, which then triggers a correction when reality kicks in.

These "rising prices" can be a general increase in the price level (inflation), or they can be in asset classes (equities in the dot-com boom of the late 1990s, or commodities in the 2000s, or subprime mortgages in the great financial crisis).

And now, we are in an artificial intelligence infrastructure boom.

It's no secret that hyperscaler investment in AI data center infrastructure has soared, as has investment in AI applications. In other words, the "rapidly rising prices" I referred to have induced me-too entrants into markets that they might not otherwise be comfortable in.

But don't take my word as gospel; here's what Warren Buffett said when asked about hyperscalers and Alphabet's (GOOG +0.73%) (GOOGL +1.18%) competitors. "They're now playing a game, in many cases, or in some cases, where they're playing a game they don't want to play," he said. For reference, Buffett initiated Berkshire Hathaway's position in Google's owner, Alphabet.

The me-too game doesn't just extend to companies overextending themselves with AI investment; it also comes from capital markets issuing credit to companies with questionable business models, and some that are struggling to cover their investments with cash flow. As you can see below, Alphabet can cover its capital investments with cash from operations; Oracle (ORCL +3.18%) and CoreWeave (CRWV +5.56%) can not.

CRWV Capital Expenditures (TTM) data by YCharts

The recent news is arguably both bad and good. As Elon Musk recently argued, "debt markets have almost entirely shut off access to money for any company that doesn't have substantial revenue independent of AI data centers." And now equity markets are punishing overextended AI participants.

These conditions represent exactly the kind of risk pricing that will curtail the excesses of investment in AI by the me-too players and the new game players Buffett alluded to.

That's bad news in the near term, as it will shake up the market. However, it's good news over the long term because it will likely lead to the market consolidation Musk referenced and ensure AI investment can continue on a more secure basis. In other words, the market needs this correction to flush out weaker AI players and allow it to move higher on a solid basis.

While it's impossible to know when to pick the bottom, it is possible to look at the lessons of history and stay invested in companies with sound balance sheets that can ride through volatility, and that means Alphabet, not Oracle or CoreWeave. In other words, selectively buy shares of strong companies during corrections, and avoid those facing challenging financing conditions. History suggests the market will reward the former over the long term.

Applied Materials vs. Dell Technologies: Comparing Stable vs. Accelerating Revenue Between These Artificial Intelligence Companies

📅 Nov 10, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Applied Materials has stabilized near $7 billion in quarterly sales, while Dell's revenue has surged past $47 billion -- a widening gap that raises questions about diverging growth trajectories.

Applied Materials (AMAT +11.37%) primarily generates its revenue by designing, developing, manufacturing, and selling the highly specialized engineering materials, technical services, and precision fabrication tools utilized by global electronics customers to manufacture complex integrated circuits.

It recently expanded its institutional research network by entering into multiple collaborative technology agreements with major university engineering programs, while additionally formalizing an arrangement to increase its strategic financial equity investment in BE Semiconductor Industries.

Dell Technologies (DELL +5.41%) primarily generates its operational revenue by designing, producing, and selling a broad assortment of enterprise-grade infrastructure hardware, client computing devices, complex data storage equipment, and complementary software applications alongside extensive global technical support services.

It introduced a newly updated portfolio of specialized server hardware platforms designed specifically to handle intensive local workloads, and it simultaneously rolled out an entirely new line of advanced personal computing workstations while expanding its cybersecurity software integrations.

Revenue is a foundational baseline figure that helps everyday investors understand exactly how much total unadjusted capital a business brings in through its core commercial activities before any internal operational costs or taxes are subtracted. This metric reveals whether an organization is successfully attracting customers and growing its overall business volume over time.

Comparing the revenue trajectories of Applied Materials and Dell Technologies quickly helps investors identify the faster growing company amid the artificial intelligence boom. From the end of 2025 into 2026, both have experienced rapid quarter-over-quarter sales growth, indicating customer demand has accelerated.

This acceleration is structurally driven by the explosion in enterprise artificial intelligence infrastructure spending, moving from steady linear growth to aggressive exponential scaling in 2026. Examining these two companies offers investors a masterclass in how the AI supercycle impacts different layers of the technology stack.
