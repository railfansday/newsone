Sam Barrott was a busy man in the middle of the park as he oversaw Liverpool's win against Manchester United.

The 34-year-old took charge of the fiery derby clash at Anfield in what proved to be a difficult day for the officials live on Sky Sports.

Barrott was immediately given a big call to make as Leny Yoro was sent off after just eight minutes after crashing into Alexander Isak when he was through on goal.

The resulting free kick was slammed home by Dominik Szoboszlai, and just three minutes later, Florian Wirtz volleyed the ball in to give Andoni Iraola's side a 2-0 lead.

Goals from Rio Ngumoha and Victor Munoz made it 4-0 in the second half as Liverpool eased away from their rivals, sparking further concerns about the Red Devils' form.

Barrott, however, could find himself at the centre of many of the discussions and while the referee was far from the one to blame, some fans are not happy.

As previously mentioned, the biggest flashpoint in the game came after just eight minutes, as Yoro was shown a straight red card for bringing down Isak on the edge of the box.

While it seemed a relatively straightforward decision at the time, replays quickly showed it was more subjective, as it may not have been a clear opportunity.

The striker's touch was well out of his feet, and questions have been asked about whether he would have met it ahead of the on-rushing Senne Lammens.

A free kick was given in favour of the Liverpool defender, launching Fernandes into an explosion of frustration towards the referee after he felt he was hard-done-by.

The attacking midfielder insisted the contact was shoulder-to-shoulder, while also suggesting he had his shirt pulled, but his explosive cries fell on deaf ears.

Despite winning the ball, the Argentina midfielder's trailing leg caught the back foot of Cunha, leaving him in a heap, but Barrott was not moved to brandish his card.

It was another of multiple decisions which went against the Red Devils, and while they cannot be used as excuses, they will feel harshly treated by the match officials.

6 Iconic Movie Roles Nicholas Hoult Auditioned For But Didn't Get, Including James Bond

📅 Nov 22, 2026  |  Category: Entertainment  |  Read Source →

Nicholas Hoult has spent more than two decades establishing himself as one of his generation's most versatile performers, building an impressive career that has seen him move effortlessly between independent cinema, prestige television, blockbuster franchises, and awards-worthy dramas. However, despite his considerable success, there are several occasions when Hollywood's biggest opportunities have slipped through his fingers, and that extends to James Bond 26.

From his early days as a child performer to becoming a genuine leading actor, Nicholas Hoult has demonstrated an extraordinary ability to reinvent himself. Audiences have watched him transform into everything from a troubled teenager to a post-apocalyptic warrior, a blue-skinned Marvel superhero, and a chilling historical monarch. His performances in About a Boy, Skins, Mad Max: Fury Road, The Favourite, The Great, and Nosferatu have showcased an actor with remarkable range, while his involvement in major franchises has made him an increasingly recognizable face around the world.

Hoult has frequently found himself in contention for some of Hollywood's most coveted characters. This has been most recently evidenced with reports that he was among the actors who screen-tested for James Bond, only for Robert Pattinson to ultimately secure the coveted 007 role. With Pattinson officially announced as the new Bond, Hoult has once again found himself on the wrong side of a major casting decision. But it's certainly not the first time. Here are six iconic roles Nicholas Hoult auditioned for but didn't get.

Before he was battling mutants or surviving the wasteland, Hoult could have been attending Hogwarts School of Witchcraft and Wizardry. Indeed, Hoult was among the hopefuls considered for the lead role in Warner Bros.' adaptation of J.K. Rowling's beloved novel Harry Potter and the Philosopher's Stone.

During an appearance on Hot Ones Versus alongside Nosferatu co-star Lily-Rose Depp in 2024, Hoult opened up about his childhood auditions for the franchise. He recalled attending several auditions and eventually meeting director Chris Columbus, even remembering wearing fake glasses during the process.

Hoult apparently got far enough into the casting process to audition directly with Columbus, although the director didn't remember their encounter when the subject came up years later. Ultimately, the role went to Daniel Radcliffe, who would become synonymous with the boy wizard across eight films.

Missing out on Harry Potter wasn't the end of Hoult's Hogwarts ambitions. In fact, the actor revealed that he was subsequently invited to audition for another major character in the franchise, Draco Malfoy.

The blonde-haired Slytherin antagonist ultimately became one of the series' most memorable characters, thanks to Tom Felton's performance. Initially introduced as Harry's schoolyard nemesis, Draco gradually developed into a more complicated character, particularly as the films explored his family's connection to Lord Voldemort.

Hoult recalled that, after failing to secure the lead role, he was asked whether he would audition for Malfoy. Unfortunately, that opportunity didn't work out either.

Interestingly, his recollection of the experience suggests that he kept auditioning for different parts throughout the casting process. As he jokingly explained, he was gradually auditioning for practically every role in the franchise until the filmmakers eventually decided against casting him.

Felton, meanwhile, would go on to become one of the franchise's breakout performers, with his portrayal of Draco remaining a fan favorite decades later.

One of Hoult's more recent casting disappointments involved another character with an enormous cultural footprint. Before becoming one of the most recognizable villains in the DC Universe, Hoult actually had his sights set on playing Superman himself.

When James Gunn began assembling the cast for his DC Universe reboot, Hoult emerged as one of the serious contenders for the Man of Steel. He was reportedly among the finalists for the coveted role, competing against David Corenswet, who ultimately secured the part.

The casting process even produced an amusing anecdote that Hoult later shared while appearing alongside Corenswet on Jimmy Kimmel Live! in June 2025. Hoult revealed that the pair had auditioned for Superman on the same day, with the actor recalling an encounter that made the eventual casting decision particularly memorable.

He described walking out of one of his audition scenes feeling reasonably confident, only to encounter Corenswet nearby. Recalling the moment, Hoult painted a rather cinematic picture of his future Superman co-star sitting in a ray of sunshine on the studio lot.

However, Gunn clearly wasn't ready to let Hoult leave the DC Universe altogether. Rather than losing the actor entirely, the filmmaker offered him the role of Superman’s bald-headed arch-nemesis, Lex Luthor.

If missing out on Superman wasn't enough, Hoult also came remarkably close to playing another of DC's most iconic characters.

When Matt Reeves began developing The Batman, Hoult emerged as one of the leading contenders to portray Gotham City's legendary caped crusader. The competition was particularly interesting because Robert Pattinson was also being considered for the role, setting up a showdown between two hugely talented British actors.

However, Hoult's experience with this particular casting process was somewhat unusual. During an appearance on the Happy Sad Confused podcast, he revealed that he actually learned Pattinson was expected to play Batman while listening to the radio in his car, approximately a week before his own screen test.

Understandably, the news was something of an emotional blow. Hoult recalled thinking that the casting hadn't been officially confirmed and that he still had an opportunity to prove himself.

Ultimately, Pattinson was selected, a decision Hoult subsequently acknowledged that he thought the decision was the right one, praising his fellow actor's performance.

Long before Game of Thrones became a global television phenomenon, Nicholas Hoult had an opportunity to join the fantasy epic as one of its most important characters.

During a 2019 interview with Page Six, the actor revealed that he had auditioned to play Jon Snow, the brooding member of House Stark who would eventually become one of the show's central figures.

At the time, Hoult was filming Clash of the Titans (2010), and his appearance for the audition was apparently rather unconventional. He recalled having long hair extensions, a ponytail, and a particularly patchy fake tan. In his own words, he remembered looking at himself and thinking that the appearance probably wasn't what the casting team had in mind.

Eventually, and unfortunately for Hoult, the role of Jon Snow went to Kit Harington, whose portrayal of the honorable, yet troubled warrior became one of the defining performances of the entire series.

For an actor who has spent years demonstrating his ability to lead major franchises, becoming James Bond would have represented perhaps the biggest opportunity of his career. Following Daniel Craig's departure from the franchise after No Time to Die, speculation surrounding the next 007 reached fever pitch. Countless names were thrown into the conversation, with Hoult among the British actors considered capable of inheriting the iconic role.

In November 2026, reports emerged that both Hoult and Robert Pattinson had been screen-tested for the part under director Denis Villeneuve. The two actors were among the later contenders in a competitive casting process, which also reportedly involved Callum Turner, Josh O'Connor and Jack Lowden.

Unfortunately for Hoult, history repeated itself. On November 16, 2026, Amazon MGM Studios officially announced that Robert Pattinson would become the next James Bond, succeeding Craig as the seventh actor to portray the cinematic spy. Pattinson's casting marks a major new chapter for the franchise, with Villeneuve directing and Steven Knight writing the screenplay.

Despite the inevitable blows missing out on so many iconic roles took on him, particularly James Bond 26, Hoult’s accomplished career demonstrates that missing out on iconic roles doesn’t necessarily mean missing out on success. Across a variety of roles, Hoult has cemented his status as an accomplished headlining star and formidable character actor.

The victories helped steady the ship after Galatasaray had suffered defeats against Sporting CP and Barcelona in their opening two Champions League fixtures, leaving them with little margin for error in their pursuit of a place in the knockout stages.

They have also continued their impressive run domestically, winning their last six league matches, a run that includes a win over Fenerbahce, and most recently a 2-1 win over Samsunspor 2-1 at the weekend.

Galatasaray have also been particularly impressive at home this season, winning seven of their nine matches at Rams Park while drawing one and losing one, with Barcelona the only side to defeat them on their own turf.

They will now have the opportunity to equal their best-ever run of consecutive victories in the European Cup or Champions League by recording a third straight success.

However, their defensive record remains a concern, having conceded nine goals in their opening four Champions League matches, including four in their home defeat against Barcelona and two against Lille.

Meanwhile, Villa arrive in Istanbul having experienced a significant dip in form, with their five-game winless run following a period during which they were without defeat in eight straight games, winning seven of those matches.

10 Greatest Film Noir Movies of the '80s, Ranked

📅 Nov 22, 2026  |  Category: Entertainment  |  Read Source →

Film noir has been the go-to for compelling entertainment since the Golden Age of Hollywood — back when this genre was first coined in the 1940s and '50s, when stylish black-and-white classics like Double Indemnity, Sunset Boulevard, and The Maltese Falcon captivated audiences with their blend of crime and drama, becoming timeless staples of the genre for decades. Today, it's still a current filmmaking style that has a huge impact on pop culture.

There's no shortage of compelling film noir classics throughout the decade. Many, as mentioned before, are from the early years of its inception. But one decade has stood out spectacularly for reinventing the genre and incorporating its thrilling potential into the creative mix of the time period. That was the glorious 1980s, a decade marked by cinematic excellence, where blockbusters ruled, and it was just an absolute blast to be a movie buff. The 10 below define the 1980s with their unique use of film noir mixed with their equally complex stories, proving that this was a time marked by this genre.

Best described as a rock-and-roll action roller-coaster ride of 1980s awesomeness, Walter Hill's cult classic Streets of Fire takes neo-noir filmmaking to new extremes. This kick-ass flick just screams the '80s, featuring a thrilling soundtrack and a unique, rebellious style that perfectly encapsulates the time period. Michael Paré, Diane Lane, and Willem Dafoe lead in this memorable tale of a dangerous ex-soldier on a mission to rescue his singer girlfriend kidnapped by a biker gang and taken somewhere in a dystopian city.

Though a box office flop and critically mixed upon initial reception, Streets of Fire has garnered a cult following that many other '80s films could only dream of. It's stylish, one-of-a-kind, and above all, very entertaining, featuring a perfect mix of action, sci-fi, and film noir to provide viewers with an unforgettable experience. This embodies the youthful spirit and angst of '80s culture and tries to be nothing else.

Hitting all the right notes to make one of the more fascinating and memorable neo-noir classics of the decade, Peter Wier's 1985 film Witness is a must-watch for audiences due to how motivating and emotional its story beats are. Acting icon Harrison Ford steals the show as always, this time in, to date, his only Oscar-nominated performance as a Philadelphia police detective who infiltrates an Amish community to watch over a young boy who witnessed an undercover cop's murder.

The film is perfectly shot, written, and notably acted, as well as tightly paced, providing decent thrills, a compelling mystery, and enough drama and heart to satisfy just about anyone. Witness was a sleeper hit at the box office, racking in an impressive number, and, of course, was a hit with critics, citing its excellent blend of film noir and drama genres, and for its mature storytelling.

A darkly erotic, one-of-a-kind nailbiter, Brian De Palma's cult classic Body Double became one of the most original entries in the film noir genre to come out of the '80s. Though there's a heavy emphasis on shocking and risqué storytelling, it's much in line with a classic Alfred Hitchcock film, following a voyeuristic young man who's pulled into a bizarre murder conspiracy after spying on a beautiful neighbor one night outside an apartment window.

This has the makings of a classic suspenseful set-up, much like Hitchcock's Rear Window, which further explores the themes of voyeuristic obsession as this film does. Except, here the suspense is much more tense, the shock and violence really crank things up a notch, and the unique '80s style really sets it apart and gives it a charm. Body Double offers an edge-of-your-seat experience with great thrills and mysterious presence, making it one of the more fascinating film noir classics that perfectly represent this decade.

Featuring the triumphant feature-film debut of Academy Award-winning filmmakers Joel and Ethan Coen, as well as cinematographer and future director Barry Sonnenfeld and Oscar-winning actress Francis McDormand, the 1984 neo-noir crime thriller Blood Simple is quite simply one of the most underappreciated but masterful cult favorites to enter the decade. McDormand shone, showcasing what she would later give to more prominent roles in a twisted story of romance and murder as a scorned saloon owner hires a private investigator to kill his wife and the bartender she's been cheating with.

This unique, stylish thriller not only showcased the talent of its up-and-coming stars but also became a slow-growing fan favorite among movie buffs over the years for its tense pacing and great concept. It perfectly fits into the genre, complete with a decent mix of crime and drama to grip viewers and disturb them with its creeping suspense. Not to mention, it's a Coen film, which, of course, features their signature filmmaking style, which is visually stellar and hard not to appreciate. Overall, it's a must-watch that would delight any film noir fan.

Based on the legendary Thomas Harris novel Red Dragon, 1986's Manhunter was the first compelling look into the exciting yet terrifying world of the Hannibal Lecter series before becoming more mainstream when Jonathan Demme's The Silence of the Lambs shocked the world. Directed by Michael Mann (yes, the man behind Heat), it delivers a tightly paced, slow-building thriller which sees an ace FBI profiler, Will Graham (William Petersen), as he asks the infamous Dr. Lecter (Brian Cox) to assist in his hunt to catch the elusive Tooth Fairy Killer (Tom Noonan).

Though it's a whole different beast compared to The Silence of the Lambs, Manhunter is a perfectly made and epically gripping classic in its own right. The performances are near flawless, especially the late Tom Noonan as the killer, who can easily give audiences the creeps even now. Not to mention, its mystery and suspense are well-paced and build to a satisfying payoff, which has steadily made this film such a rewatchable and adored cult classic that's gotten more appreciation over the years.

Packing a powerful mix of neo-noir storytelling and tense suspense, William Friedkin's 1985 classic To Live and Die in L.A. provides cinematic excitement, which was what the '80s were all about. William Petersen brings his services again to the genre in this action spectacular, which sees him as a vengeful U.S. Secret Service Agent who crosses the line to eliminate the counterfeit artist (Willem Dafoe) who killed his partner.

From its uniquely '80s style, the kick-ass Wang Chung soundtrack to the heavy, awesome fight sequences and tense chases, To Live and Die in L.A. is an absolute blast of entertainment, one that's not just one of the best of the film noir genre, but just one of the most exciting features of the 1980s, period. It instantly hooks you in, never letting go for even a moment, and fascinates you with its tale of revenge and justice.

Truly one of the most remarkable, bold, and sexiest noir thrillers to come out of the '80s was 1981's Body Heat, a film directed by Lawrence Kasdan and starring the late William Hurt and Kathleen Turner. A neat, twisted little spin on the genre, which in itself is an homage to 1944's Double Indemnity, it follows a naive lawyer manipulated into a murder plot by a seductive femme fatale who has a few tricks up her sleeves.

It's tensely paced, perfectly acted, competently shot, and doesn't lose your attention for a second. Body Heat is a highly rewatchable masterwork that pays tribute to the classic film noir genre, as well as standing in its own right, telling a dark and alluring crime tale that has to be experienced. Just a few minutes of this classic can instantly hook you in.

Michael Mann returns, this time with arguably one of his greatest, most captivating works, Thief, a 1981 neo-noir thriller starring the late James Caan. It follows Caan as a professional thief who is on the path to turning his life around, but his pursuit of this dream becomes far more difficult as he desperately tries to get one last big score and deals with his old associates who are unwilling to let him leave his life of crime.

A critical and commercial success, Thief was instrumental in keeping film noir hot going into the 1980s. Today, it's regarded as one of the genre's most recognizable cult favorites. From Caan's nuanced lead performance as Frank to Mann's remarkable filmmaking talents, to the overall thrilling '80s feel of the film, it's an absolute delight that keeps getting better with age.

The one and only David Lynch fascinated countless fans with his unusual charm and one-of-a-kind storytelling. The film that arguably showed the greatest potential of his talents outside of Twin Peaks was 1986's Blue Velvet, a favorite among many Lynch enthusiasts for its captivating mystery and bizarre tone. Kyle MacLachlan stars as a curious college boy who returns to his old town and is quickly dragged into the town's dark criminal underworld after finding a severed human ear in a field.

It's certainly strange, off, and borderline nonsensical at a few points, but its mystery plot is straight to the point and undeniably fascinating. That's the best way to describe Lynch's masterpiece. Blue Velvet incorporates all his best attributes, and his story is a complement as well as something new to the film noir genre, leading it to become one of the best films of the year.

But truly, at the end of it all, there was no other place for Ridley Scott's 1982 sci-fi and noir masterpiece Blade Runner than at number one. It's honestly hailed as one of the most significant movies in cinematic history, a near-perfect film with flawless visuals, competent performances, and thought-provoking themes. Harrison Ford shines once again as a former cop turned detective-for-hire who is tasked with finding and killing a group of rogue androids who have rebelled against their creator.

This is a film that's still widely talked about and appreciated today for its brilliance and complex storytelling. There's no denying Blade Runner's impact on film noir. It not only breathed new life into the genre but redefined it in every way. This continues to influence the current genre, and rightfully takes this final spot as the film that most defines the film noir genre in the 1980s.
