The US Ambassador to the United Nations Linda Thomas-Greenfield on Monday described Russia assuming the United Nations Security Council (UNSC) presidency this month as “an April Fool’s joke.”

Ukraine has received the first tranche of $2.7 billion from a new International Monetary Fund (IMF) program, Ukrainian Finance Minister Sergii Marchenko said in a tweet Monday.

The IMF, which regularly makes emergency loans to countries in crisis, on Friday approved a new four-year extended arrangement under the Extended Fund Facility (EFF) of around $15.6 billion as part of a $115 billion total support package for Ukraine.

The program aims to “anchor policies that sustain fiscal, external, price and financial stability and support economic recovery, while enhancing governance and strengthening institutions to promote long-term growth in the context of post-war reconstruction and Ukraine’s path to EU accession,” the IMF said in a statement.

The program also helps Ukraine to carry out “more ambitious structural reforms,” it said.

The Extended Fund Facility (EFF) loan is the first major conventional financing program approved by the IMF for a country involved in a large-scale war, Reuters reported.

The risks to the arrangement are “exceptionally high,” said Gita Gopinath, first deputy managing director of the global lender.

“The success of the program depends on the size, composition, and timing of external financing on concessional terms to help close fiscal and external financing gaps and restore debt sustainability on a forward-looking basis under the baseline and downside scenarios,” she added.

NATO Secretary General Jens Stoltenberg said Monday that the alliance has not seen any changes in Russia’s nuclear posture since Russian President Vladimir Putin announced late last month Moscow’s plans to station tactical nuclear weapons in neighboring Belarus.

“We monitor very closely what Russia does. But so far, we haven’t seen any changes in their nuclear posture that requires any change in our nuclear posture,” the NATO chief said, while answering questions from reporters on the topic at a news conference in Brussels.

Stoltenberg described Putin’s announcement as a “pattern of dangerous, reckless nuclear rhetoric.”

“President Putin tries to use nuclear weapons as a way to prevent us from supporting Ukraine… intimidation, coercion to stop NATO Allies and partners for supporting Ukraine in their right to defend their own country,” he said.

“We will not be intimidated. We will continue to support Ukraine,” Stoltenberg continued.

NATO’s chief Jens Stoltenberg said on Monday that Western allies have delivered more than $70 billion in military aid to Ukraine over the last year since the Russian invasion started.

“There are no signs that President Putin is preparing for peace. He is preparing for more war,” Stoltenberg warned, speaking at a news conference in Brussels.

“We do not know when this war will end. But when it does, we will need to put in place arrangements so that Ukraine can deter future aggression. And history does not repeat itself. We cannot allow Russia to continue to chip away at European security,” he said.

Stoltenberg added that allies have delivered 65 billion euros (around $70.7 billion) in military aid, and said he welcomed the recent arrival of modern battle tanks and other armored vehicles in Ukraine.

“This can make a real difference on the front lines and allow the Ukrainian forces to liberate more territory,” he said.

The NATO head affirmed that the alliance’s support is for the “long haul” and said, “we will discuss how we can step up our support, including by continuing to strengthen Ukraine’s armed forces and supporting the transition from Soviet-era to NATO equipment and doctrine.”

Stoltenberg also said allies must address other threats and challenges, citing instability, terrorism, and the growing influence of Iran, Russia, and China. He urged allies to invest more in defense to tackle all these wide range of issues.

The war in Ukraine has cost an estimated $2.6 billion of damage to heritage and cultural sites in the country, the UN cultural body UNESCO said in a recently published report.

The report, which covers one year of war in Ukraine between February 24, 2022, and February 24, 2023, was a joint assessment conducted by the Ukrainian government, the World Bank, the European Commission, and the United Nations.

UNESCO was in charge of the part of the report dedicated to culture and tourism.

“As of February 24, 2023, the total damage cost from identified assets is estimated at $2.6 billion,” the report said in its assessment of damage related to culture and tourism.

Some $1.7 billion of that cost was distributed to “historic cities, buildings, and sites imbued with recognized cultural/social values,” $650 million to tourism facilities, $143 million to “movable cultural properties and collections, repositories of culture,” and $150 million to “buildings/workshops/ateliers dedicated to cultural and creative industries,” according to UNESCO.

The most impacted region in Ukraine was Kharkivska, which has suffered 30% of the total damage to its cultural assets, the report added.

UNESCO said that losses are estimated at around $15.2 billion, which includes revenue losses from tourism, art, sports, entertainment, recreation, cultural education, and creative and cultural industries.

“The war has significantly impacted the diversity and richness of culture and cultural heritage in Ukraine, causing damage to cultural infrastructure and assets, reducing livelihoods for cultural creators, bearers and practitioners, limiting access to culture, and impeding the exercise of cultural rights,” the report said.

UNESCO’s head Audrey Azoulay is currently in Ukraine, the organization told CNN on Monday.

“IAEA Director General Rafael Mariano Grossi will visit Kaliningrad on Wednesday as part of his ongoing consultations aimed at ensuring the protection of Ukraine’s Zaporizhzhia Nuclear Power Plant during the military conflict,” the spokesperson said Monday.

Mocha Brown Hair-Color Season Is Here—and It’s Universally Flattering

📅 Nov 18, 2021  |  Category: Business  |  Read Source →

There’s something about this time of year that makes everybody want to go darker. From their nail polish to their wardrobe, it’s a turn toward rich, darker shades. So it’s only natural that hair color would follow suit, going deeper and darker too. This winter in particular, it’s all about mocha brown.

Also called mocha chocolate, the rich, glossy shade was first popularized by celebrity colorist Jenna Perry, whose multidimensional brunette wizardry has fans like Bella Hadid and Kaia Gerber. Since then, the smooth and chocolaty hue—which combines darker espresso tones with lighter, milkier brunette shades—has become popular with a much wider section of the A-list, with Hailey Bieber and Daisy Edgar-Jones among the latest devotees.

“It reminds me of a chocolate mousse or a rich chocolate cake,” Perry tells Vogue. “That’s where the name came from. I thought mocha chocolate resembled the decadence of this shade of brown.” In line with sweater weather, Perry has churned out individualized takes on what she calls the “universally flattering brunette hair color” for a slew of stars, including longtime client Emily Ratajkowski and Amelia Gray. “A deep, rich brunette is chic,” Perry says. “The color reflects warmly in the sun, so it doesn’t ever look drab.”

According to Stephen Buller and Anita Rice, founders of Buller and Rice salon, these cacao tones feel luxurious for winter, particularly when the color isn’t completely flat. The key? Having a varied tone. “This is important throughout the lengths and ends so it reflects light and feels expensive rather than looking like one solid block of dark color,” they say. “This also gives clients who have had years of balayage an opportunity to go darker without feeling as though they’re simply covering everything up.”

Celebrity hairstylist Lorraine Dublin says that these indulgent, chocolaty shades look especially beautiful on textured and curly hair: “Think deep espresso brown with subtle ribbons of mocha, creating a rich, multidimensional finish,” she previously told Vogue. “It works beautifully on textured hair because the tonal variations catch the light, highlighting the shape and movement of curls, coils, and kinks.” She recommends asking your colorist for a “rich espresso base” with “delicate mocha or cinnamon brown accents,” which will impart a soft, seamless color melt.

Another important part of mastering the mocha chocolate look is keeping hair strong and shiny. “What makes this hair color so incredible from the start is having healthy hair,” explains Perry, who adds that preserving pigments with a color-safe shampoo and conditioner is key. Try Matrix Total Results Brass Off Shampoo to nix brassy or red tones. You can also use an at-home gloss or color conditioner, like Glaze Supergloss Color Conditioning Hair Gloss Chocolate Gleam or John Frieda Brilliant Brunette Color Vibrancy Conditioner, to prolong your hair’s vibrancy.

A cordless stick vacuum has the potential to be one of the most convenient cleaning tools in your home. These lightweight machines make it easy to clean up pet hair from area rugs and hidden dust along baseboards, without having to haul out an upright vacuum or wrestling with a power cord. Most models also convert into handheld vacuums, giving you more flexibility to clean furniture, stairs, and other hard-to-reach spots.

Suction power, battery life, maneuverability, and dustbin capacities vary between models, so the right cordless stick vacuum ultimately depends on your home’s flooring, how much debris you’re dealing with, and the features that will actually make your cleaning routine easier.

We’ve rounded up the best cordless stick vacuums worth considering based on our hands-on experience, with options for everything from everyday maintenance to deeper carpet cleaning.

The main advantages of owning a cordless stick vacuum are easy portability and storage. But what you trade for the convenience is battery life and runtime. Most models clean from 30 to 60 minutes, but that’s also dependent on what setting you’re using. If you’re running the vacuum on its highest speed the entire time, it might die faster. Be wary of manufacturers’ advertised total runtime because it may represent performance on the lowest setting or using two fully charged batteries, for instance. Battery charging should also be factored in, so plan your cleaning session accordingly so you don’t lose power in the middle of a floor swipe.

Like we mentioned earlier, the dustbins on cordless stick vacuums are significantly smaller than those of an upright or canister unit. You should consider your cleaning habits and the spaces you usually vacuum to find a model that aligns with your needs. If you live in an apartment or are using the cordless vac to supplement your other cleaning supplies, then a dustbin with at least half a liter capacity should do. Some of the models we recommend hold up to one liter of debris, which helps limit the number of trips to the trash can.

Although not a necessity, having a good amount of attachments with your vacuum is especially helpful for cleaning hard-to-reach places or targeting specific issues. Crevice tools pick up dirt and dander from corners and couch cushions, while bristled brushes and motorized pet tools agitate and lift hair and fur that’s been embedded into upholstery.

A good filtration system helps combat allergens such as dust and pet dander that are being kicked up by a vacuum. The best option is a HEPA filter, or “high-efficiency particulate air” filter, which traps 99.97 percent of particles that are 0.3 microns or larger in size. A fully sealed or multi-step filtration system also has the potential to trap a wide variety of airborne allergens without releasing them back into your home.

Cordless vacuums are very effective at picking up pet hair, especially models designed with high suction power and specialized brush rolls. Some of the models we recommend include motorized attachments that lift embedded hair from upholstery and carpets. Anti-tangle vacuum heads are also a key feature that helps prevent hair from wrapping around the brush roll. While not all models perform equally, a high-quality cordless stick vacuum is able to handle pet hair just as well as traditional versions.

The lifespan of these vacuums typically lasts from three to five years, depending on usage, build quality, and maintenance. Battery performance plays a major role, since lithium-ion batteries start to lose capacity over time.

Avoiding overcharging and storing the vacuum properly could help extend the battery life significantly. Running regular maintenance like cleaning the vacuum filters and emptying the dustbin completely preserves suction and functionality. Higher-end models often last longer due to better materials and replaceable parts, but either price point has potential to last for years with proper care.

Modern designs are increasingly more effective at cleaning many surface types, including rugs and carpets. Advances in battery tech and general efficiency have allowed for strong suction performance that is comparable to corded models. Adjustable power modes allow for deeper cleaning on thicker carpets, and multi-roller and motorized brush heads agitate fibers to loosen dirt and debris.

Performance may vary depending on the type of flooring. Some vacuums are designed to work on low-to-medium pile carpets, while others are able to handle high-pile. It’s best to consult the listed features and specs of the model you’re considering to ensure it aligns with the flooring types in your home.
