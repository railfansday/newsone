Reds' Sal Stewart 'just getting started' with NL Rookie of Year award

📅 Nov 10, 2026  |  Category: Other  |  Read Source →

LAS VEGAS — Sal Stewart had no sooner accepted the National League Rookie of the Year award than he embarked on the task of getting the Cincinnati Reds back to the playoffs next year.

Well, he planned to embark on that once the party in Miami with dozens of friends and family concludes at some point this week.

"Just a kid from Miami living his dream!" he said at the end of a video conference with Cincinnati media after becoming the ninth player in franchise history to win the award.

"You might wake up at 8. a.m., and I'm still gonna be out partying. I'll be enjoying it."

No matter how long that party might last, no matter how long a labor lockout might last once MLB's labor agreement expires Dec. 1, Stewart has already fixed his sights firmly on 2027 and helping lead the Reds from last place back to the postseason he experienced a month into his big-league career in 2025.

"My career is just getting started. I promise you that," he said. "I promise you this is just a steppingstone, and there's more to come.

"This season didn't go the way we wanted," he added, "but I feel like if I want to be the player that I want to be for this organization, (this award) is a good steppingstone to show everybody (coming up), 'Hey, I'm willing to do it. Everyone that comes up here better be willing to do it, to put your life on the line for your team.'

"I feel that will go a long way, and I feel our season will turn out better next year."

Stewart, 22, hit 32 home runs and drove in 112 runs in 2026, earning an All-Star selection and the Reds' first Rookie of the Year award since Jonathan India in 2021 – winning 20 of 30 first-place votes and beating second-place Carson Benge (seven first-place votes) of the Mets 170 points to 107. JJ Wetherholt (three) of the Cardinals was third with 99 points.

The award was a goal Stewart set for himself long before getting to spring training, even with a loaded field of impact rookies who figured to be in play across the league.

"Because so many people doubted me. So many people didn't think I could," he said. "I love that. My whole life I've been doubted. Even when I got drafted, I had people on MLB Network saying I was a bad decision. 'He didn't have a hit ability.' 'His power needed to come.' 'His defense was bad.'

"I don't forget those things," he said. "I keep all the receipts for all the people that talk. I just wanted to prove those people wrong."

The ultra competitive Stewart played all season with a level of confidence and apparent chip on his shoulder that belied his age and experience level. It especially showed up in high-leverage moments, with men in scoring position and in close-and-late game situations.

It's what led to RBI totals that led the league much of the season before Stewart eventually finished four behind the MLB leader.

Beyond the significance of being one of just seven players to play all 162 games this season, he preaches the value of an RBI stat that has fallen out of favor in this era of advanced stats and analytics – an appreciation instilled by his baseball mentors growing up and that boils down to situational hitting to try to win games.

"Whether it's a ground ball to shortstop with the infield back or whether it's a sac fly," he said. "You can't win a game if you don't drive in runs at the end of the day."

The Reds didn't have enough runs or enough wins regardless in this case. Which already seems to be Stewart's focus with the prestigious award in his back pocket.

He says he wants to get with team officials to find out what their plan is for him positionally next year – back to his original third base or staying primarily at first – then working this winter on getting better there.

And performing again, and helping lead others, to get back to the playoffs.

"I really want to win for this city," he said. "This award's great, but at the end of the day I don't like sitting down and watching the playoffs. I want to be playing in them. And that's something I've got to figure out a way to help my team do that.

"I know what we're capable of," he added. "We have the right guy at the helm (in manager Terry Francona). Tito knows what the expectation is. And he's the first one to make sure I'm living up to it, and if I'm not he's the first one calling me into his office making sure I'm living up to it. We know what it takes. We know what the expectation is. Losing's not fun, man. We do this to win."

In Photos: Asadula, Black Panther, And PTT Give Fans Glimpse Of Their Firepower At The Inner Circle Open Workout

📅 Nov 10, 2026  |  Category: World  |  Read Source →

Reigning ONE Flyweight Muay Thai World Champion Asadula "The Dagestan Ninja" Imangazaliev and Thai warriors Black Panther and PTT Apichart Farm showed their skills at an open workout last week at Venum Training Camp in Pattaya, Thailand.

The three stars of Venum Training Camp and Team Mehdi Zatout also spoke to media ahead of their upcoming fights at The Inner Circle 38 on Friday, November 13, and The Inner Circle 39 on Friday, November 20. Both events take place inside the fabled walls of Lumpinee Stadium in Bangkok, Thailand, streaming exclusively for members via live.onefc.com.

The 23-year-old Russian headlines The Inner Circle 39, where he defends his crown for the first time against Aslamjon "El Pantera" Ortikov in a rematch. The clash also serves as the last quarterfinal bout of the 2026-2027 ONE Flyweight Muay Thai World Grand Prix, with the winner advancing to the semifinals.

Imangazaliev is 13-0 and has built his reputation as an incredibly strong and unpredictable fighter who can finish a bout with any technique at his disposal. He became the first Russian athlete to capture Muay Thai gold in ONE Championship when he beat Ortikov in June, and now he wants to prove it was no fluke.

That first meeting at The Inner Circle 20 was the first time two undefeated stars from the weekly event series collided for a ONE World Title, and Imangazaliev edged it by split decision. This time, he is aiming for a more convincing win that leaves no room for debate, and one that sends him into the semifinal with his perfect record intact.

Before the main event, the penultimate quarterfinal of the tournament sees 26-year-old Black Panther take on undefeated Ubaid "Bad" Hussain at The Inner Circle 39.

The Thai striker is 82-17 overall and 6-1 in ONE. A technical fighter with a strong kicking game and powerful punches that come in bunches, he recovered from a losing start in the promotion and now rides a six-fight winning streak against world-class opposition. A win over Hussain would book his place in the semifinals.

That streak has included victories over the likes of Suriyanlek Por Yenying, Diego Paez and Johan Estupinan, and it has turned a promising newcomer into one of the most in-form strikers in the division. The Grand Prix gives him the biggest stage of his career and, with a semifinal spot on the line, he plans to keep his momentum rolling.

A week earlier, the 29-year-old PTT Apichart Farm meets Moroccan finisher Hamza Rachid in a bantamweight Muay Thai matchup at The Inner Circle 38.

The experienced Thai veteran owns a 114-25 career record and three wins in ONE, thanks to explosive power in his right hand and body kicks. After three straight victories, he suffered a loss in his most recent outing in June, and he is determined to get back on track against Rachid.

PTT has rebounded before. After a rough debut loss in the promotion, he turned things around in style and strung together impressive wins to establish himself as a name to watch in the division. Now he looks to show the same resilience against a dangerous finisher in Rachid and start a new run.

Finally, Purdue basketball finds its offense late to run off Illinois State

📅 Nov 10, 2026  |  Category: Sports  |  Read Source →

WEST LAFAYETTE − At last, Purdue basketball fans can breathe a sigh of relief.

It took huge late run to blow the game open, but the Boilermakers left Mackey Arena with their first victory.

A lineup change that included Jack Benter and Antione West Jr. among the starting helped pilot Purdue's 76-59 win over Illinois State.

Purdue (1-2) led 50-48 midway through the second half before landing the knockout blow it needed after losses to Gonzaga and Valparaiso to open the season while also snapping out of its 3-point shooting funk.

After combining to shoot just 20.4% from 3 in the first two games, the Boilers made 8 of 19 against the Redbirds (1-2).

Despite a hot shooting first half, Purdue couldn't separate itself from the Redbirds.

Illinois State had a chance to cut the lead to two Louie Semona II's 3-pointer was off and Enzo Shahrvin pulled down the rebound. Purdue called timeout to set up a final play. Antione West Jr. ended up making a stepback 3-pointer from the right wing to give the Boilermakers a 36-28 halftime lead.

That shot was Purdue's saving grace for a long time, but it was just enough to keep Illinois State from ever pulling even or going ahead. Eventually, Purdue found its footing and blew the game open with a 24-2 run relying on a lineup of Antione West Jr., Luke Ertel, CJ Cox, Jack Benter and Daniel Jacobsen.

Jack Benter received his first two career starts out of necessity when Trey Kaufman-Renn was sidelined to open last season. On Monday, the junior was in the starting rotation for the first time in a year. He showed what he's capable of from the get go, sinking a 3-pointer for the game's first points, then drawing a charge on the defensive end.

It set the stage for a productive night across the board for Benter, who finished with 14 points, 5 rebounds, 3 assists and a steal.

Daniel Jacobsen: Three games in a row is a trend. The 7-foot-4 center is Purdue's best offensive piece. It was another stellar night for the Boilermaker big man, contributing his third straight game with at least 20 points, hitting that number on the nose. Jacobsen also had 7 rebounds and 3 assists.

Antione West Jr.: In his first career start after sitting out last season, West had a coming out party. West scored 17 points on 7 of 11 shooting and added 2 rebounds, 2 assists and a steal.

CJ Cox: The junior guard made three early midrange jumpers. He didn't fill up the stat sheet, but he didn't hurt the Boilermakers either. Cox had 10 points, 3 assists and, in typical Cox fashion, no turnovers.

Next Delhi Capitals captain after Axar Patel exit: R Ashwin sends warning to DC regarding Rishabh Pant

📅 Nov 10, 2026  |  Category: Business  |  Read Source →

Next Delhi Capitals captain after Axar Patel exit: R Ashwin sends warning to DC regarding Rishabh Pant originally appeared on Cricket News. Add Cricket News as a Preferred Source by clicking here.

Delhi Capitals are reportedly in talks with Chennai Super Kings over a player trade.

A cryptic social media photograph from Chennai has set the cricketing rumour mill spinning.

Ravichandran Ashwin has shared a clear view on Delhi's trade targets and captaincy choices.

Delhi Capitals may soon be hunting for a new captain, and Ravichandran Ashwin has already cautioned the franchise to be careful about how it handles Rishabh Pant. His warning comes amid reports that DC have opened trade discussions with Chennai Super Kings, and that their current skipper, Axar Patel, could be the player who changes hands.

The left-arm spinning all-rounder is reportedly at the centre of the talks, which could take him to the five-time IPL champions. It is not yet clear, however, which player Delhi would ask for in return, so the deal remains very much a matter of speculation for now.

Chennai themselves did little to cool things down. The franchise shared a photograph on its official X account that shows its own captain, Ruturaj Gaikwad, deep in a relaxed chat with Patel. The picture was taken in New Zealand once India had wrapped up a win in the second ODI on Saturday.

MORE: CSK tease fans with trade rumour as they post photograph of Ruturaj and Axar

Supporters were quick to treat the post as a hint, and talk of a possible IPL trade between the two sides gained momentum.

Delhi brought Pant back to the franchise from Lucknow Super Giants straight after the IPL 2026 season ended, which means the experienced wicketkeeper-batter is home again after two years away.

KL Rahul is also part of the squad, so DC now have two senior names who could lead the side on the field. That depth gives the management some freedom, and it is easy to see why they might feel tempted to let Patel go.

Ashwin accepts that the deal could happen, but he believes Delhi must be wary of several things if they let their captain walk out. In his view, the franchise should ask for a player such as Dewald Brevis or Noor Ahmed in return. Kuldeep Yadav has already moved to Lucknow in the deal that brought Pant back, so Delhi are short of spin options at the moment.

On his YouTube channel 'Ash ki Baat', the former India spinner started by questioning the very logic of the trade. He also pointed out what Axar's arrival would mean for the Chennai squad, as it would leave one of their overseas spinners without a role.

"Why is this trade even possible? Think about why this trade could even make sense. A player of this commodity can give them balance. If Axar comes, they will not need Akeal Hosein at all. Why is Delhi even thinking, if at all they are?" he said.

Ashwin then moved on to the players he would want Delhi to chase, and he leaned on Brevis's recent record with a Sourav Ganguly-coached side as the main argument.

"Where Ganguly was the mentor at Pretoria Capitals, Brevis did well single-handedly, and he will rate him. They might go after him. And Brevis is with CSK. They let Kuldeep go, so Noor Ahmed could be enticing too," he added.

MORE: 'Duniya Hila Denge Hum': Axar Patel trades cheeky banter with Arshdeep Singh amid Hardik Pandya PBKS-MI trade buzz

An Axar exit would leave Delhi with the big job of finding a new leader, and Pant is the name that comes to mind first because he has captained the franchise before.

Ashwin, however, does not want the left-hander to be handed the role again. He thinks veteran opener KL Rahul is a far better fit to fill the gap if the trade goes through, and instead Pant should be left free to express himself as a player, without the weight of leadership on his shoulders.

"It might be possible. It can happen. KL Rahul is a good and experienced option. Pant needs to play freely. If someone can convince him not to take captaincy and play freely as a player, it is Dada (Ganguly). No one else can. It may not be a bad call," Ashwin said.

From a purely cricketing point of view, Delhi should hold on to Axar Patel rather than hand him over to Chennai. Indian all-rounders who can bowl four tight overs of left-arm spin and then bat anywhere between No. 5 and No. 7 are among the rarest assets in the IPL, and that makes Axar close to irreplaceable in the way DC build their side.

The destination makes the idea even harder to defend. Chepauk has long suited spinners, so Axar would solve what is arguably Chennai's biggest tactical gap, and Delhi would be strengthening a direct rival in the process. It is difficult to see the benefit in that, however attractive the cash or the swap might look on paper.

Unless Chennai offer something extraordinary, such as a proven Indian middle-order powerhouse or a marquee fast bowler, the numbers simply do not add up for Delhi. Losing their captain and their most dependable utility player would also leave them hunting for both a leader and a spinner at the same time, and that is a lot to ask of any franchise in one window.

The presence of Pant and Rahul gives the management comfort on the leadership front, but comfort is not a reason to sell. Ashwin's own list of targets shows how thin the market is for a like-for-like replacement. DC would be wise to keep Axar and let the trade talk fade away.
