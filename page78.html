In my new book, The Future Begins With Z—I challenge business leaders who report that young employees are the toughest age group to manage. I suggest we reframe the issue: Instead of complaining about their attitudes, unprofessional conduct, and disrespect, we should look for any kernels of truth and foresight into the future. It’s easy for older generations to assume the world is getting worse.

No doubt, there’s a tension at play—but I wonder if we’re solving the wrong problem.

Is Generation Z genuinely difficult to manage, or are they simply less willing to quietly accept workplace norms that previous generations were taught never to question?

It is easy to describe a generation as demanding or entitled when it asks for more feedback, greater flexibility, meaningful work, attention to mental health, and for leaders to listen. But a more revealing question is why we interpret these expectations as unreasonable rather than as signals that the traditional relationship between employee and employer is shifting.

Caleb would agree. After he graduated college, he went to work for a Fortune 500 company in New York, serving on the business development team. Caleb was a talented 22-year-old who quickly saw ways the company could improve their use of AI, as well as its HR and operational systems. His supervisor, however, wasn’t interested in his ideas and told Caleb to “pay his dues” before he offered any advice. In less than a year, Caleb quit his job and has since launched three businesses of his own. He’s now leading one that leverages AI to get work done more efficiently. I believe his first boss missed out on an incredible talent.

Nicki would agree. She was 21 years old when her supervisor exposed how little he knew about his young team member. He accused her of having a poor work ethic, since she never worked a minute past 5:00 pm. She had to explain that she left at 5:00 to rush over to a side hustle, since her salary wasn’t enough to pay the bills. (She worked another side hustle on weekends as well.) Like Caleb, Nicki left the company. This turnover will continue to happen until older managers stop stereotyping and get acquainted with their young employees.

Ashton would agree. He started working as a sixth grader but saw jobs differently than his much older boss. This 11-year-old left behind a structured lawn company to start his own business after noticing his neighbors struggling with dirty trash bins. He wondered: Would someone pay to have them cleaned? He discovered they would. Within months, Ashton’s new company was earning him a high income to put in savings, and his work went viral on TikTok, showing how a young leader solving a local problem can inspire everyone.

Reframing this issue has helped so many organizations I have served. Rather than treating Gen Z as a workplace problem to solve, I ask leaders to consider what this generation might be revealing about the future of work.

Let me offer six questions to ask yourself if you’ve experienced clashes with younger teammates and have assumed they need to do all the changing:

Perhaps it’s time we seek growth over comfort and familiarity. If we do, however, it will require intentionality. I’m in Orlando today, spending time with my new grandson, Max. He’s 9 weeks old. When he’s awake, he constantly fidgets and groans, making sweet little noises and movements. Doctors say babies often do this in search of comfort. Each move is a search for comfort for their bodies or food if they’re hungry. This is a natural urge humans have all our lives—to find comfort and satisfaction. Call it "the comfort zone."

Leaders, however, must resist this natural quest for comfort and seek growth and progress. In our day, I believe this means learning from our young instead of just lecturing them. Let’s reframe how we perceive our youngest teammates. Maybe, just maybe, they’ll lead us into a better future.

France accuses Iran of supermarket fridge hacking

📅 Nov 09, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Fridges and freezers in hundreds of supermarkets across France were affected last month

France has accused Iran's Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps (IRGC) of being behind cyber-attacks that raised the temperatures of supermarket fridges and freezers across the country last month.

Foreign Minister Jean-NoÃ«l Barrot said the attack was "unprecedented in both its scale and its level of aggression", adding those who were responsible would be hit with sanctions.

Iran has yet to respond officially, but some Iranian media outlets have dismissed the allegations as baseless.

The French hacking, which led to the destruction of tons of frozen anc chilled food in France was followed by a similar attack against supermarkets across several other European countries. It is not known if the instances are linked.

Barrot said the attack on France's supermarkets "marks the first large-scale cyber-attack aimed at sabotage on our national territory, and it constitutes a major attack on the nation's interests and the security of the French people".

He said the government had summoned the Iranian ambassador to Paris to "lodge a very strong protest against this extremely grave act".

France will take "any other necessary and proportionate measures" to respond to the incident and "deter anyone who might seek to repeat it", Barrot added.

The Paris prosecutor said an investigation had been opened into the head of an offensive cyber unit associated with the IRGC as well as his two deputies.

Laure Beccuau named the unit as Shahid Kaveh, which she said had an affiliation with the electronic and cyber centre of the IRGC.

Shahid Kaveh is the name given to a cluster of hackers thought to be directly employed by the Iranian regime.

In recent weeks, a number of commentators and politicians in Iran have argued that the country should extend any retaliation beyond the Middle East if the US and Israel resume the war they started earlier this year.

If the IRGC carried out the cyber-attacks, it remains unclear whether they were part of Iran's deterrence strategy.

On 11 October, hackers gained control of the software that regulates the temperatures of the refrigeration units used in many of France's supermarkets.

Other major food retailers including Carrefour, Monoprix and E.Leclerc were also hit by the attack, which targeted software provided by Danish engineering firm Danfoss.

The software allows retailers to manage their cold units remotely.

Earlier this month a second wave of hacks took place, hitting more than 100 stores across Europe.

Supermarkets in Belgium and Luxembourg were affected, while a smaller number of shops were hit in countries including Sweden, Ireland, the Netherlands, Estonia and Finland.

Separately in August a small number of US military bases had fridges mysteriously malfunction, spoiling food. This incident is also being investigated as deliberate cyber-attacks, Reuters news agency has reported, citing sources familiar with the matter.

Since the waves of attacks on freezers, experts have been speculating which hacking groups could be responsible, with Iran being on a list of potential suspects.

In spite of a pair of Russian-aligned hacktivist groups claiming they were responsible on Wednesday, few believed them.

The French government has not said how it reached the conclusion that Iran was responsible, and attribution of cyber-attacks is extremely hard as hackers are able to hide their locations.

BMW Sets 2 New Nürburgring Records With Help from Pirelli

📅 Nov 09, 2026  |  Category: Automotive  |  Read Source →

A revised semi-slick tire helped the carmaker knock down the compact car and touring car lap records at the Nordschleife.

Pirelli’s P Zero Trofeo RS is one of its most extreme performance tires, one capable of helping performance cars extract the most out of their potential. And that’s especially true when the company works with automakers directly—as the Italian rubbermaker did with BMW, developed a bespoke version specifically for the BMW M range. The new semi-slick tire remains street legal, but doesn’t sacrifice at-the-limit grip. And it helped BMW set two new ‘Ring lap records with its BMW M2 with the M Performance Track Kit and BMW M3 CS Touring.

The BMW M2 with the M Performance Track Kit managed a lap time of 7:25.068minutes. This lowered the Nürburgring Nordschleife compact car record, previously held by the M2 CS, by 0.5 seconds. The long-roof BMW M3, however, made more significant history: BMW’s M3 CS Touring lapped the 12.9-mile racetrack in 7:29.49 minutes, setting a new record for the fastest touring car ever by shaving off more than five seconds. The previous touring car lap record of 7:35.06 was held by the standard BMW M3 Touring. A combination of increased horsepower, stronger brakes, and custom rubber—as well as BMW M Development engineer Jörg Weidinger behind the wheel—pushed the wagon to success.

“The bespoke development program is the result of close collaboration between Pirelli and BMW M’s R&D teams. Thanks to advanced virtual development techniques, the two teams were able to adapt the tire’s characteristics to the different requirements from the earliest stages of the project, in line with Pirelli’s Perfect Fit approach, which aims to create products tailored to each specific model or application,” the tire maker said in a statement.

Pirelli initially tested the tires alongside BMW at the Nürburgring Nordschleife, and the tires were also later tested at the Nardò test circuit in Italy. BMW and Pirelli have collaborated on over 30 bespoke tire projects across the P Zero family over the years, and like many others, a keen eye can tell which ones are made for Bimmers; the manufacturer-specific tires feature a BMW star marking, as a nod to the integrated development process.

Arsenal are still monitoring Bayer Leverkusen striker Christian Kofane as they weigh up options to strengthen their attack, according to BBC Sport.

The Gunners already know the 20-year-old well.

Kofane featured for Leverkusen when the sides met in last season’s Champions League round of 16, when Mikel Arteta’s team progressed 3-1 on aggregate.

The renewed interest follows heavy speculation during the summer.

In April, Kofane’s agent Eric Depolo said Arsenal’s interest was genuine and confirmed he was in contact with the club, claiming Kofane would give them a top striker for the next decade.

Leverkusen were reported to value him at around €70m, though Depolo had talked up a €100m figure. The Gunners were said to be ‘in pole position’, but no move materialised.

Arsenal wanted a forward in the last window but missed out on Vinicius Jr and Julian Alvarez, while Morgan Rogers joined Chelsea instead.

Kofane’s rise has been rapid. He moved from AS Nylon in Cameroon to Albacete in 2024, scoring eight goals in 20 Segunda Division games, before joining Leverkusen in July 2025.

He made 44 appearances in his first season in Germany, including 11 in the Champions League, and has three goals in six games this term. The frontman is currently sidelined with a hamstring injury.

If and when, the striker leaves North Rhine-Westphalia, Arsenal face competition.

Manchester United, Real Madrid and Bayern Munich are all reported to be tracking him, amid reports his contract talks with Leverkusen have stalled.

The striker situation at the Emirates adds context. Viktor Gyokeres scored 21 goals last season but has been second choice behind Kai Havertz and has started only once in the league.

A January deal looks unlikely unless Arsenal sell one of their current forwards first.

For now, Kofane remains a long-term target rather than an imminent signing, but Arsenal’s interest has clearly not faded.

Tottenham Hotspur saw their winning streak in the Premier League come to an end at Elland Road on Saturday afternoon as they fell to a 1-0 defeat against Leeds United.

Spurs headed into the game on the back of three consecutive victories, while Leeds had won just one of their last nine, but Noah Okafor's late strike was the difference between the two sides as the hosts took all three points to ease some of the pressure on Daniel Farke from the fans.

Tottenham, meanwhile, must now head back to the drawing board after a disappointing display which saw the manager apologise after the full-time whistle, with the north London side now sitting 11th in the table heading into the international break.

The game saw De Zerbi recall World Cup-winning full-back Pedro Porro to the starting lineup for the first time since the 3-2 defeat against Aston Villa back in September, with the Spaniard having recovered from a hamstring injury he suffered in that match.

But his performance against Leeds was disappointing to say the least. He completed just 67% of his passes in the game, was given a yellow card, didn't win a single aerial duel, committed three fouls, and won just half of his tackle attempts in the 65 minutes he was on the pitch.

It's worrying that his return coincided with a poor performance, especially when there has been speculation in recent weeks that he is keen to leave the club amid interest from Barcelona and Real Madrid, despite signing a new long-term contract before the World Cup.

Reports last month suggested that the player has lost faith in the project under De Zerbi and is seriously considering an exit in 2027. He has also since confirmed publicly that he wishes to return to Spain at some point in his career, admitting it was a "big dream".

“Obviously, I’d love to return to Spain someday. It’s my country, and I like the league. Right now, it would be very unfair to talk about anything other than my club because I’m under contract, but obviously, I’d love to go back to Spain; it’s a big dream of mine.”

Losing such an important member of the squad would be a blow, but Spurs got rid of Cristian Romero in the summer and haven't looked back, with Jan Paul van Hecke and Marcos Senesi brought in to partner Micky Van de Ven instead.

Porro's departure would leave a hole in the squad that needs filling, but Spurs have already got a right-back option in the squad who fits the needs of De Zerbi's system even better than Porro does, and that is Archie Gray.

It's clear that Archie Gray is a key part of the Spurs squad under De Zerbi, even being included in the leadership group and wearing the captain's armband on occasion despite his tender age.

Playing either in central midfield or at right-back, the former Leeds sensation has established himself as an undroppable member of the squad this season, and the manager must find a way to continue giving him regular game time to help him develop.

While he may not be a better right-back than Porro in the traditional sense, he seems to fit the needs of De Zerbi's system better at this stage, and there would be no need to panic-buy a new right-back to replace Porro should he be sold. With the Spaniard having just signed a new contract, they could demand a huge fee to let him leave, and then put that towards addressing other areas of the squad that are in more urgent need, like the forward line.

It's unlikely to be a popular decision immediately, but if the board get the aftermath right then it will prove to be the perfect solution and something that can be looked back on fondly.

Exclusive: Mikael Silvestre Names 'Very Special' England Star as Most Underrated Teammate

📅 Nov 09, 2026  |  Category: Business  |  Read Source →

Former Manchester United, Arsenal, and France defender Mikael Silvestre has named the most underrated teammate from his playing career, while speaking exclusively to GIVEMESPORT.

The talented defender had an illustrious career in English football, making 275 appearances in the Premier League over 11 seasons, lifting the title five times, and also winning the Champions League, FA Cup and League Cup once.

He also had an excellent international career, winning the Confederations Cup twice and reaching the World Cup final in 2006, racking up 40 caps for Les Bleus between 2001 and 2006.

During those prime years of his career, Silvestre played alongside some legendary figures including Cristiano Ronaldo, Wayne Rooney, Carlos Tevez, Ryan Giggs, Roy Keane, Zinedine Zidane, Thierry Henry, and Patrick Vieira.

At different points in his time at Old Trafford, Silvestre was the first-choice left-back and also the first-choice centre-back, showing his incredible versatility and quality, with Sir Alex Ferguson a huge fan of him after signing him from Inter Milan in 1999.

His experience and quality convinced Arsene Wenger to take him to the Emirates Stadium when his time at Man Utd came to an end, which allowed him to continue his career in England and play alongside some more top stars such as Cesc Fabregas, Samir Nasri, and Robin van Persie.

But while speaking to GIVEMESPORT, via Action Network, a prediction market publisher covering Polymarket offers, Silvestre was asked to name the most underrated teammate of his playing career, and he didn't hesitate when providing his answer.

“Paul Scholes was underrated. Obviously, he got his flowers at Man Utd but for England and outside of Man Utd he wasn't rated as high as he should be in my opinion.

“He was a top, top player, and being his teammate in training, he was a very special talent.”

Scholes was part of the Man Utd squad for the entirety of Silvestre's time at Old Trafford, and is widely regarded as one of the club's best ever players. The midfield playmaker made 718 appearances for Man Utd during his career, winning 11 Premier League titles, three FA Cups, two League Cups, and two Champions League titles.

But outside of Manchester, his reputation is not as esteemed as you would think, with his quiet nature in the public eye during his playing days likely going against him.

Considering some of the talent that Silvestre played alongside, it's no surprise that the former England midfielder, one of the greatest midfielders of the Premier League era, is likely seen as underrated.

But Scholes is often heralded by his peers, and was recently described as the best player in Premier League history by Thierry Henry, who also felt the iconic midfielder was underrated.

"I don't think people have given him the credit he deserves. (He's) one of the best, if not the best, I've played against, he could do anything. Unfortunately for him, when he was playing for the national team, for England, sometimes people didn't see it like that."

The England star made an interesting choice.
