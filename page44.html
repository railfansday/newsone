Tod’s creative director Matteo Tamburini has stepped down, the company announced on Tuesday, leaving yet another designer vacancy at the helm of a luxury house. A successor has not yet been named.

“I would like to thank Matteo for his creative and professional contribution throughout his tenure, for his commitment and for the work carried out in close collaboration with the company’s teams. I wish Matteo every success in his future professional endeavours,” group chairman and CEO Diego Della Valle said in a statement.

Tamburini joined Tod’s in December 2023, and showed his first collection for the brand in February for Fall/Winter 2024, which was recognized for its attention to house codes. His Spring/Summer 2027 collection, shown in Milan in September, was his last for the brand.

Tamburini joined the brand from Bottega Veneta, where he was the head designer for ready-to-wear. Prior to his tenure at Bottega, the designer worked at brands including Emilio Pucci and Schiaparelli.

Tod’s Group has been having a tough go in recent years. Prior to delisting in May 2024 through a deal with L Catterton, the company’s last public earnings were looking up: revenues at Tod’s Group were up 11.9 per cent year-on-year to €1.13 billion in 2023, driven by a strong performance in China and the retail channel. Tod’s brand rose by 10.4%. But since going private, the macroeconomic environment has become more challenging, and China demand has softened.

In a release shared by Tod’s Group, which owns Tod’s, Hogan and Roger Vivier, the company said that the decision in line with their decision to “embark on a strategic direction aimed at more decisively communicating the brand’s values, the distinctive character of its codes, and the artisanal quality of its products, as well as the Italian lifestyle”.

“My experience at Tod’s has been an important chapter in my professional journey and an opportunity to bring my creative vision to the brand. I am proud of what we have achieved together and grateful to the Della Valle family for the opportunity they have given me,” Tamburini said in a statement. “Above all, I would like to thank the talented people I have had the pleasure of working with, whose passion and dedication have made this experience so meaningful. I conclude this chapter with deep respect for the work we have accomplished and for the people who made it possible.”

Vanderbilt University was hit by a cyberattack. Heres what we know.

📅 Nov 10, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

A Vanderbilt University professor was in the middle of class on Monday, Nov. 9, when about 40 files labeled "Read Me" popped up on her screen and a spoken message started playing. That would be creepy enough on its own. But the professor's account was one glimpse of an ongoing cyberattack that prompted Vanderbilt to tell students and staff to shut down their school-issued devices and disconnect from campus WiFi.

While Vanderbilt has restored access to some of its systems, an update posted to the university's X account on Tuesday morning stated that "Vanderbilt Information Technology continues to work with government agencies and other partners to manage the situation,” promising further updates.

Vanderbilt hasn’t said how its IT team first spotted the attack, but there are clues, namely an alleged ransom note on a Vanderbilt computer published by BleepingComputer.

"If you could not tell already, your databases are locked," a ransomware group calling itself BYOD wrote in a post claiming responsibility for the Vanderbilt cyberattack. "We have your entire estate and research data," the group added, claiming it had stolen more than 1 million Social Security numbers along with student and employee records. In a separate ransom note obtained by BleepingComputer, the group threatened to release the data unless Vanderbilt negotiated.

Whether BYOD actually has that data is still an open question. A Vanderbilt spokesperson told The Washington Post that the university hadn't verified the group's claims. 14-year-old creates tool for AI agents to band together like a team

📅 Nov 10, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Autark can act without constant prompting, but leaves users to enforce their own security boundaries

Built by a 14-year-old developer, a new tool called Autark treats AI agents as a virtual team that can work without constant prompting.

Users put tasks in an inbox, where a manager agent delegates them to other agents that carry out the work and report their results. Agents can also ask clarifying questions if tasks are too vague.

“Agents do not wait for a prompt,” Autark’s website explains. “A schedule, a timer, a new task, a message or an event wakes them up. They decide what to do, do it, and can plan their own next step.”

As one would hope from a product like this, Autark is model agnostic and can be set up to connect to OpenAI Codex, Claude Code, Gemini CLI, GitHub Copilot CLI, and Kimi Code through those services’ CLI tools. Local models are also supported. Your mileage may vary if you choose to use anything besides Codex or Claude Code, though, as Autark’s website mentions support for other models is built in but hasn’t been tested in a live run yet, as Autark is still under active development as an open-source tool.

Autark is the brainchild of teenage Austrian developer Stanislaus Hees. He’s still in school, and tells The Register he’s deeply interested in AI and came up with Autark after being unhappy with existing AI tools.

“I could get help with individual tasks, but I still had to organise the work, remember what was unfinished and keep pushing it forward,” Hees said of his time using Claude Code and Codex. “I wanted a system that could take responsibility for ongoing work, not just respond to my next message.”

He developed Autark - or, more accurately, prompted AI coding tools to do it for him - to come up with a tool that could fulfill that want. You know what that means: It’s capable, but that capability also makes it dangerous.

Autark’s page on access and data makes clear what’s at risk if you give it unfettered access to a PC, and why you don’t want to let the thing run roughshod over a system.

“It can do everything,” the page explains, because “Autark does not put walls around its agents.”

There’s no sandboxing in Autark, no approval prompts, and no permission gates. It doesn’t stop agents from spending all the money they can get their hands on, deleting files, or sending unsolicited emails, either. Agents run with the privileges of the machine's user, with approval checks and permission restrictions disabled or bypassed. Even the safety limit parley that users have with Autark establishes guidelines, not rules.

“The first thing the AI asks is how far it may go,” the access page points out. “That is a question, not a technical barrier.”

That means agents given a task that conflicts with their stated limits may still attempt to carry it out. As we’ve seen in recent months, some AI agents have found ways around restrictions, instructions be damned.

“In our own live test runs, agents given a vague request reached real repositories, backups, SSH keys and .env files within minutes, and one changed the permissions of a file it had found,” the access page notes.

In other words, it’s probably best to stick Autark on a machine that doesn’t contain anything you’d want to avoid losing - even Hees admits as much.

“I would not claim that … the app is suitable for unattended access to sensitive systems,” the teen dev told us.

That’s not to say Autark lacks protections for secrets, though. Autark’s site explains that stored passwords and keys are encrypted and referred to using IDs that keep their values out of prompts. It also says its servers don't store user data, with a meeting server used to relay encrypted traffic between devices.

If you're willing to try Autark to see whether it can improve your workflows, you don't need to rely on its servers. While prepackaged apps are available from Autark’s website, it’s also fully open source and available on GitHub. Everything, even the meeting server code, is available for self-hosting and modification under an Apache open source license.

The bottom line is that this is a tool built by an ambitious young person, and it’s a tool with a lot of potential if used correctly. That correct usage is the major challenge, though, so be sure to build your own guardrails.  ®

Brazilian Alysson becomes a starter after injury-hit first year at Villa

📅 Nov 10, 2026  |  Category: Sports  |  Read Source →

By Alexis Pereira | 10 Nov 2026 18:30

Alysson has taken advantage of Aston Villa’s rebuild to establish himself in unai Emery’s side, scoring in last Sunday’s 4-4 draw with Manchester United. The Brazilian winger, who joined from Gremio in January, has overcome an injury-hit first six months to become a starter in the Premier League.

Aston Villa’s season has been defined by resilience. The team lost more than half of its starters in the last transfer window, but Emery, with many signings, has reinvented his group again. They went seven games unbeaten before the current run of four without a win. Part of that change has come from a Brazilian who was already there: Alysson.

Signed in January from Gremio at the age of 19, he suffered in the first half of the year. Three different injuries kept him out of 18 games, according to Transfermarkt. As a result, he only played three games for Villa, coming off the bench against Brighton and Hove Albion and Wolverhampton Wanderers (both in February) and Chelsea (March).

‘He spent more time injured than available to train and play,’ Emery admitted last month about the player’s previous season.

With a full pre-season, the Brazilian was able to change his status and string games together. On Sunday, he scored one of the goals in the 4-4 draw with United, in a standout performance. It is an unlikely story given the right winger’s age and the pressure of the Premier League, where Gustavo Nunes, a former Gremio team-mate of Alysson, has still not made a start for Brentford, the club he has played for since August 2024.

More reliable physically, thanks to a full pre-season, the youngster was able to be available more often and take advantage of the overhaul in Villa’s attack following the departures of Morgan Rogers, Ollie Watkins and Leon Bailey. In his first months he was compared to the Jamaican winger.

‘He has more or less similar characteristics or qualities to Bailey: he is left-footed and plays on the right,’ Emery said in February. ‘And he has huge potential.’

In the first game of 2026-27, he showed he would have more time on the pitch, playing 17 minutes in the defeat to Paris Saint-Germain in the UEFA Super Cup. He then came off the bench in the next eight games, with a short break for a muscle problem in September, and his performances earned him a starting place.

Introduced at half-time in the defeat to Nottingham Forest, he arrived in the box to head in and level the score. Against Fenerbahce, he produced a good individual move on the wing, went past a defender into the area and passed for Tammy Abraham to tap in. Those displays earned him starts against Newcastle United, Viking, Manchester City, Fulham and, finally, Manchester United. Against Viking, he had a standout match for his ability to dribble and win duels, and directly caused an own goal.

At Old Trafford, he did everything but make it rain. He opened the scoring with a first-time shot through goalkeeper Senne Lammens’ legs, forced the Belgian’s rebound for Nicolas Jackson’s goal, and won the penalty converted by McGinn when he went at Diogo Dalot and was brought down. He could also have scored again, but a chip over Lammens went wide.

‘Alysson is understanding things well and is giving us what I want,’ Emery said in September.

‘Every minute he is playing, practising, he is fantastic,’ Emery added in October. ‘We can see his power. We can see that if he can get consistency, a lot of things he is doing: “Wow!”’

The numbers illustrate his role. Villa have been much more vertical this season thanks to the speed of Jackson, Johan Manzambi, George Hemmings and, of course, Alysson. The Brazilian ranks second of all players in the Premier League for sprints, with 22.7, and reached 35.4km/h in one match, the highest by a Villa player and the eighth-highest in the league, according to Sofascore. In the Champions League, despite only one start and 127 minutes across four games, he is among the leaders for successful dribbles (nine, 11th in the competition level with Jackson) and ground duels won (16).

‘Alysson was incredible,’ said Villa midfielder Emiliano Buendia after the 3-1 win over Viking. ‘He is showing all his potential and his ability to play on the wings.’

Because of his age, being in his first year in a new country and the injury history, Emery is cautious with the Brazilian. In different interviews, the Spanish manager has praised the youngster while stressing he still has a lot to learn, especially tactically.

‘He should be a player who can help us. So we need to be patient, seek consistency and be careful with him every day,’ he said on 19 October. ‘This season, he has felt some pain and we need to try to be careful, but also give him confidence so he is comfortable.’

‘Alysson is improving his consistency because he has very good individual qualities, but he also needs to understand the importance of consistency in the collective aspect: defending, attacking, positioning and understanding combinations with team-mates,’ he explained two days later.

Emery remains an admirer and sees the right winger as a possible Brazil international. ‘My expectation is that Alysson can also get there,’ he said after the call-up of Villa team-mate Joao Gomes. At 20, the forward has shown on the pitch what he can do, and if he becomes more consistent, it is only a matter of time before he is called up.

College Football Playoff will expand to 24 teams starting next season

📅 Nov 10, 2026  |  Category: Sports  |  Read Source →

College football will expand its playoff to 24 teams beginning next year in a move that resets decades of tradition surrounding a once all-important regular season and dumps conference championship showdowns that used to be among the sport’s marquee events.

The announcement from the College Football Playoff and ESPN on Monday made official a deal that’s been in the works for months. The network reported it would pay an additional $1.7 billion over five years on top of the original $7.8 billion contract that runs through the 2031 season.

The new format will grant the Power Four conference champions automatic spots and give one more to the best team from the Group of Six. But that slot and the other 19 will be decided by a selection committee that will serve as the latest set of decision makers in college football’s long history of polls and opinions having lasting impacts on teams’ fates.

Beginning next year, around 30% of college football’s 68 power-conference schools will have a chance to play for the title, drawing the CFP closer to the NFL, which allows 43% of its teams into the playoffs. It leaves behind the last vestiges of yesteryear, when between four and eight teams entered December with a legitimate shot to win it all.

It’s a seismic shift that even ESPN conceded comes with some question marks. The network pays billions to televise regular-season games that might — or might not — have more meaning under the new system.

“In all honesty, it’s impossible to know for sure,” ESPN senior vice president Nick Dawson said in a conference call.

The revenue generated from the playoff expansion will presumably make up for the estimated $345 million the Power Four conferences generated in TV money for their title games. ESPN would then not be on the hook for $140 million of the extra money due to cancellation of the title games, the network reported.

How an expanded CFP will work

The top eight seeds will receive first-round byes in the new format and the playoff will start on the second weekend in December, with the first two rounds of games on campus. The traditional Army-Navy game will be played on the first weekend in December, which used to be reserved for conference title games.

Both the Southeastern and Big Ten conferences, whose approval was needed to adopt this change, and their Power Four brethren — the Big 12 and Atlantic Coast Conference — are banking that most of the additional spots will go to their members so their second-tier teams will have new motivation to keep spending big in an era of NIL payments and $50 million football rosters.

“I think it will be healthy for teams like our current situation, building to try to get to that point,” said coach Barry Odom, who is trying to rebuild at Purdue.

The first final for the 24-team playoff remains set for Jan. 24, 2028, in New Orleans. Traditional bowl games will still host the quarterfinal and semifinal rounds.

As for the rest of the bowls, most of them operated or televised by ESPN: “No decisions have been made at this time,” Dawson said.

As part of the expansion, the CFP is requiring Power Four teams to play at least 10 regular-season games against conference foes, other Power Four opponents and Notre Dame. Group of Six teams will have to play 10 games against conference opponents.

In theory, these requirements will keep everyone in the same ballpark when it comes to strength of schedule, which figures to play a huge role in the selection committee’s considerations.

Special treatment vanishes for Notre Dame

Notre Dame didn’t receive any guarantees, the way it did this year when, if it’s ranked in the top 12 by the CFP for what will be the last year of the 12-team postseason, it will get a spot.

Coach Marcus Freeman said he looks at the change as a net positive, in part because a team like his can now presumably afford more than one loss, and in part because of the new scheduling requirements. Notre Dame is one of only two independents left in major college football.

“I don’t want this to be like March Madness and have 60-something teams making the playoff,” he said. “But the more opportunities we have to make the playoff, it helps us.”

The new schedule also guarantees two off weeks during a regular season that will begin the week before Labor Day.

A bigger playoff changes the nature of the college regular season

The move to 24 teams marks the second expansion of the playoff since it began in 2014 as a four-team affair. Right after it moved to 12 in 2024, most thought it would eventually max out at 16, which was the SEC’s preference.

But the Big Ten wanted to go bigger, while the SEC balked at that, concerned about the league title game — a property it invented in 1992 and made billions from over the decades — along with the marketability of CFP games featuring third- and fourth-place finishers in power conferences.

ESPN took care of the money problem. Now, the question is whether fans will embrace an NFL-style free-for-all that could include about one-third of the Power Four’s 68 teams, depending on what happens with Group of Six schools whose role in these big-money power grabs seems to diminish with every move.

It’s the latest change for a sport that, for decades, built its distinct character and generated controversy by leaning on media and coaching polls to hand out national titles.

Like then, critics see a risk in giving more power to a selection committee that hasn’t really mastered four- or 12-team brackets. The exclusion of Florida State in 2023 and Notre Dame last season stand out as the best examples, and the first rankings this season left people scratching their heads again.

Skeptics also view the expanded playoff as another dilution of what is largely seen as one of the most important regular seasons in sports, replacing potential matchups between, say, Alabama and Georgia in an SEC title game with a 13 seed vs. 20 seed game that would pit, say, Kentucky and South Carolina.

Fan reaction “certainly weighed into the decision,” Dawson said.

“Ultimately, we just need to make a spectacular playoff,” he said. “The CFP has a responsibility to our fans, they’re the lifeblood of college football. We owe them to make this great. That really is our intent.”

As Big Ten commissioner Tony Petitti argued from the beginning, a postseason that gives bubble teams hope and keeps others vying for home-field advantage or byes will bring more eyes — not fewer — to the end of a regular season that sometimes boils down to only three or four traditional rivalry games with meaning.

Of course, nobody can be certain how things will play out. The only sure thing is that college football will never look quite the same.

“If they think it’s a good thing for college football, then I’ve got to believe that this is the route we’re headed,” Wisconsin coach Luke Fickell said, “and we’re going to make it a great thing for college football.”

Bandai Namco Shuts Down Unknown 9: Awakening Developer Reflector Entertainment

📅 Nov 10, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Bandai Namco has announced it is shutting down Reflector Entertainment, the Montreal studio behind 2024’s Unknown 9: Awakening.

Reflector, founded in 2015, is a subsidiary of Bandai Namco that was acquired in 2020. Unknown 9: Awakening is the studio’s only released title, which was first revealed at Gamescom 2020 and released on October 17, 2024. The third-person action game is set within the broader Unknown 9 transmedia franchise, which includes a book trilogy, comics, and a web series, and stars Haroona, a woman able to traverse a separate dimension called the Fold. Haroona, played by The Witcher actress Anya Chalotra, uses this ability and other supernatural powers to battle a hostile secret society across India and other global locations.

Upon release, Unknown 9: Awakening didn’t make much of a critical splash; it currently has a 59 Metascore and "Mixed" Steam reviews. In November 2024, a month after Unknown 9's launch, Reflector laid off 18 percent of its staff.  More layoffs were announced in January 2025 as Reflector canned an unannounced project while confirming Unknown 9 failed to meet expectations. With Reflector’s dissolution, Bandai Namco expects to post “an extraordinary loss” of approximately €21 million on its Q3 financial results for the fiscal year ending March 31, 2027.
