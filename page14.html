Anthropic is trying to kill traditional desktop apps with Claude-generated desktop apps

📅 Nov 25, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Rarely a day goes by that Anthropic PBC doesn’t announce some kind of new capability, feature or model, and today wasn’t any different. Today came the ability to use Claude to create fully functional desktop applications for Windows and Mac computers with a single prompt.

It promises to be one of its more impactful updates, fanning the flames of those who argue that artificial intelligence is ultimately destined to eat the software world.

The company said today that users can now transform what it calls “artifacts” into standalone applications that run independently of Claude and can be accessed from the desktop. Artifacts have been around for a while, and they can be almost anything, such as a simple order tracker or a meeting dashboard that pulls information from the user’s calendar and email inbox.

However, until today, artifacts were a bit tricky to access. They could be opened up only from inside Claude itself, which meant users first had to open up the chatbot, login and then find the old conversation where they originally created the artifact they want to use.

Anthropic said people are using Claude artifacts all the time for various everyday tasks, and so it makes sense to mature the concept. “If you rely on a tool for work, it should open like the rest of your apps and remind you when something needs your attention,” the company said.

That’s why Anthropic is making it possible for people to use artifacts to create, run and share desktop applications with Claude’s new “Install as App” feature, which is available in beta now on Windows and macOS.

In a video posted on X, Anthropic showed how straightforward it is to create a functional desktop app from a simple prompt. The user simply describes the tool they want Claude to create, click confirm and then sit back and wait for a few seconds as it slaps together the required code to bring that app to life.

You can now install Claude artifacts as desktop apps on Mac and Windows.

Ask Claude to build an artifact, click "Install as app", and open it like any other app. It can use your connectors and, if you allow it, send notifications or add a menu bar item. pic.twitter.com/8CBgqe5OKq

— ClaudeDevs (@ClaudeDevs) November 24, 2026

Because these are ‘real’ apps, they come with their own app icons and they open in a dedicated Window, rather than running within the Claude interface. They also get the ability to send notifications to users, Anthropic said.

As an added benefit, Claude artifacts that are transformed into desktop apps will be able to use Claude and various third-party connectors, meaning that AI agents will be able to interact with them autonomously, Anthropic said. However, the company did warn that any requests made by an app to Claude will count towards their monthly credit usage, which means it could become expensive if an app is used excessively.

In a blog post Anthropic showcased a number of examples of what its internal teams have built, including a fully-featured image editing app called Linnet, a file manager with a cinematic design called Phosphor, and a terminal application that integrates with Claude, which is known as Tern.

There is one major caveat to Claude artifacts-as-apps that sets them apart from traditional desktop applications. According to Anthropic, “you need to be online and signed in to Claude to open an app.”

That could be a pretty big downside, for many desktop applications are designed to work even if the computer is not connected to the internet. But if someone lacks a Wi-Fi connection, they will be unable to access their Claude-generated apps, even if they’re already built and configured.

Houthi attack on Saudi Arabian sovereign wealth fund PIF's tower injures 11

📅 2026-11-25  |  Category: Business  |  Read Source →

Yemen's Iran-backed Houthis hit Saudi Arabian sovereign wealth fund Public Investment Fund Tower on Tuesday, injuring 11.

The kingdom's civil defense directorate did not name the tower, but posted an image showing damage to the PIF tower. Eyewitness reports by Al Jazeera also indicated that the building that was hit was the PIF Tower in Riyadh.

The injured included two Saudi citizens, two Indians, three Nepalis, and four Egyptians.

The attack comes as tensions have flared up again between Saudi Arabia, which is leading an international coalition to restore the internationally recognized government of Yemen after it was forced to flee in 2015, and the Houthis.

Turki al-Maliki, a spokesperson for the coalition, said one of the towers of the financial center was targeted in a "cowardly terrorist attack," referring to the financial center in the Saudi capital of Riyadh.

He added that authorities were conducting an on-site investigation at the scene of the attack.

This has been the second attack by the Iran-backed group in six days on the tower, although the previous attack did not cause any casualties.

On Wednesday, Houthi brigadier general Yahya Saree warned on X that the port of Jeddah and all Saudi ports along the Red Sea coast will become "targets for our forces," if Riyadh attacks the Yemeni Red Sea port of Hodeidah.

The coalition had asked civilians to evacuate the port "for their safety," on Nov. 20.

Attacks by the rebel Houthis have been escalating on Saudi Arabia, with the group targeting one of Saudi Arabia's oil refineries on Sunday, according to Reuters. The Houthis also launched drone and ballistic missiles against the country's airports, most recently targeting the King Khalid International Airport on Oct. 25, according to reports.

The new attacks come amid wider strikes by Tehran and Iran-linked forces in the Gulf, with Bahrain also reporting that it was attacked by Iran over the weekend. Bahrain is home to the U.S. Navy's Fifth Fleet.

"As a result of the heinous Iranian aggression, a civilian industrial facility was targeted, causing a fire at the site," Bahrain's Ministry of Interior said on X.

The ministry later said two people, a Bahraini citizen and a Pakistani resident, were injured in the attack.

Jensen Huang's Return Could Make a Tesla/SpaceX Merger More Likely

📅 Nov 25, 2026  |  Category: Business  |  Read Source →

The regulatory environment looks more forgiving after Huang's return.

By Jeremy Bowman – Nov 24, 2026 at 9:30PM EST

Tesla (TSLA -0.20%) and Space Exploration Technologies (SPCX -5.65%), Elon Musk's two trillion-dollar companies, took a step closer to merging on Tuesday.

Both companies have appointed a total of five directors, three from Tesla and two from SpaceX, to guide two special committees to handle merger negotiations.

The committees have also hired investment banks to develop financial models for the all-stock merger, and a deal could be reached before year's end. If the merger were timely enough, it would also allow SpaceX to be included in the S&P 500 earlier than it would as a stand-alone company.

Naturally, merging two companies that have the same CEO is easier than merging two unrelated companies. Musk has a majority of SpaceX's voting power and 20% of Tesla's voting power, and could own a majority of the combined company.

The full details of Huang's return to Nvidia may never be clear, but President Trump says he played an active role in getting Huang reinstated, telling CNBC that he "convinced" the board to do so.

That move may have shifted the Overton window significantly in corporate governance, or what's considered acceptable, and also shows that Trump is willing to put his thumb on the scale for his corporate friends, at least when he believes it's in the country's interest, one of which is Musk.

The dynamics of getting the merger approved versus reinstating Huang are much different, however. Trump just needed to get the board's approval to get Huang back as Nvidia CEO. The Tesla/SpaceX merger, on the other hand, will face a battery of regulatory hurdles to get cleared.

China would be an issue in the merger, as Tesla has a significant business there, and SpaceX's technology would likely raise national security concerns. It would have to pass antitrust muster in both the U.S. and Europe, where there could be resistance to creating a company with a leading position across so many disparate industries. Additionally, Texas Attorney General-elect Nathan Johnson has expressed skepticism about corporate concentration and could challenge the combination.

Still, expectations of corporate governance and President Trump's own ability to influence such decisions have shifted with Huang's reinstatement, which could help the merger go through.

While the two companies haven't formally declared an intention to merge, they're likely to go through with it, as all of the interested parties, including Elon Musk, appear to be incentivized by a merger. The deal will serve as a major test for the regulatory apparatus, as antitrust regulators have blocked much smaller deals in the past.

How to Watch F1 Like a Pro, According to McLaren Drivers Lando Norris and Oscar Piastri

📅 Nov 25, 2026  |  Category: Sports  |  Read Source →

If you purchase an independently reviewed product or service through a link on our website, Rolling Stone may receive an affiliate commission.

As the 2026 F1 season made its final U.S. stop of the year, Las Vegas once again turned its neon-lit streets into an impressive, 17-turn race that wound its way through The Strip, with drivers whizzing past the city’s most iconic landmarks. On Sunday, Max Verstappen took home the checkered flag at the Formula 1 Las Vegas Grand Prix after a nail-biting finish, with McLaren’s Lando Norris and Oscar Piastri posting a double-podium finish to complete the top three.

With F1’s growing prominence in the U.S., new fans continue to enter the high-speed world of racing. But while the rules of Formula 1 seem simple enough — the first car to pass the checkered flag after 50-odd laps, wins — it does take some time to truly understand the complexities of the sport.

Rolling Stone sat down with McLaren drivers Oscar Piastri and Lando Norris ahead of the Las Vegas Grand Prix to glean some tips on how new fans can optimize their viewing experience.

Norris advises the audience to watch onboard videos and listen to the commentators who make the race more understandable. “When you then see the onboard cameras in the driver’s eye [line], I think it looks a lot more realistic [since] that is what I’m seeing with my head,” he says, “So I think that gives a much better experience. The more that they can do that, and get the sensation of speed, the easier it will be for people to understand.”

And while new viewers might only focus on the leaderboard, Piastri begs to differ. “I think the first thing about Formula 1 is not everyone’s car is the same, so I think just because some people aren’t winning, it doesn’t necessarily mean that they’re worse than the people that are winning,” he says. “Oftentimes the racing in the midfield can sometimes be more entertaining than the racing at the front.”

The drivers both say that strategy shifts within varying climate conditions, tire compounds and their impact to performance and longevity, and the car’s on-track behavior are just a few of the factors being considered on the sidelines during every race.

As part of their preparation, the team reviews prior races of the current season, as well as footage of the same track last year to see any learnings that could apply. Norris emphasizes how the addition of new cameras showing extra angles on the track can help the driver’s pre-race POV while adding to the immersive fan experience.

T-Mobile, F1’s regional 5G innovation partner, supports this by (literally) taking connectivity to new heights, with more than 20 5G-connected cameras that supply additional angles and track views. With its partnership with Apple TV, more angles are made available to the customer, in addition to a private 5G network to ensure reliable connectivity for broadcast feeds.

“We saw an opportunity with the technology-forward nature of the sport to be able to come in and look at how could we provide network solutions that are actually making the experience better,” offers Amy Azzi, VP of Sponsorships and Events at T-Mobile.

Azzi highlights the “additional broadcast views and angles, using our connectivity, and devices that are able to capture those angles and bring them into a more functional broadcast or into the app, which allow more opportunities for fans to engage.”

For fans who can’t watch the races live, the network giant recently launched T-Mix, where customers can mix and match benefits and entertainment options every three months, including streamers like Apple TV, home of the F1 U.S. broadcast, alongside other premier streamers, travel partners, and music providers.

At the end of the day, the McLaren duo’s most important connections are to their supporters. Piastri says he’s collected his fair share of fan gifts this year, shouting out a glitter-covered cowboy hat in his helmet design, to a hardcover book with special fan messages. “Hearing some some people’s stories and how coming to an F1 race is literally their life dream, and being able to to help them kind of fulfill that dream is is really cool,” he says.

Norris emphasizes that fans are the most important thing in their sport. To wit: the driver kicked off race week by doing an exclusive meet-and-greet at T-Mobile’s Las Vegas Signature Store, where fans got to experience an F1 simulator and other racing-inspired activations while getting up close and personal to one of the sports biggest stars.

“The fans are the reason our sport is what it is, and you don’t just say it because you have to,” he says. “What I love to do is the driving [but] the bonus I get out of it is I have people traveling around the world, supporting me, cheering me on, wanting me to do well. I feel like I need to go out and perform well for them to make it feel like their trip was worth it.”

T-Mobile has been part of the F1 Las Vegas Grand Prix since its inaugural race in 2023, and the mobile carrier once again brought its members-only Club Magenta viewing experience to the track this year, while also sponsoring performances from artists like Ja Rule, Gorgon City and Natasha Bedingfield on the T-Mobile Stage. Saturday culminated with the first-ever official post-race show with the Backstreet Boys at Sphere.
