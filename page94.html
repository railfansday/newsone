Advocate Health to increase total employee compensation by $846M for 2027" style="max-width:100%; height:auto; border-radius:8px;" loading="lazy">

Nonprofit health system Advocate Health will invest an additional $1.24 billion in its workforce beginning in 2027, with approximately $846 million dedicated towards compensation and $390 million towards incremental benefits.

Advocate will increase its minimum wage to $20 an hour, building on its $18.85 per hour minimum wage established this year.

For a 40-hour workweek, that wage would pay over $26,520 more per year than the federal minimum wage, it said.

Compensation investments will also include merit increases, incentives, pay-range updates and other investments in pay, executives said in the Nov. 5 announcement.

“The most important investment we make isn’t in buildings, technology or equipment. It’s in our people,” said Eugene A. Woods, Advocate Health CEO, in a statement. “Healthcare is a 24/7 calling and every single day our 170,000 teammates bring extraordinary skill, compassion and commitment to the patients and communities we serve. Their dedication is what brings our purpose to life for the millions of people who count on us each year.”

The country’s third-largest nonprofit health system currently covers approximately 83% of employee medical costs, and plans to invest an additional $138 million for what it says will absorb “more than 90% of the projected $149 million increase in medical plan costs.”

Moreover, the health system’s 2027 benefits package extends fully paid parental bonding leave to four weeks enterprise-wide; expands paid bereavement leave; continues coverage for GLP-1 medications for weight loss; provides no-cost mental health support for employees and family members; continues sustained retirement contributions of up to 6% of eligible pay and offers education assistance across more than 1,300 eligible programs.

The package also offers student loan repayment assistance, with with lifetime support of up to $21,000 for eligible full-time employees and $10,500 for eligible part-time employees.

“The benefits that matter most are the ones that make a real difference in people’s lives,” said Nakesha Lopez, Advocate Health senior executive vice president and chief people and culture officer, in a statement. “We want our benefits to reflect what teammates need at different stages of their lives, while keeping affordability at the forefront and providing meaningful support for their health, families and financial futures.”

Infant Bleeding Cases Surge as More Parents Refuse Vitamin K Shots for Newborns

📅 Nov 09, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Follow Healthline on our socials for all things health.

Two large studies published on November 5 link declining newborn vitamin K shot rates to a sharp increase in infant bleeding cases, including potentially fatal brain hemorrhages.

A research letter, published in JAMA Pediatrics, analyzed data on approximately 11 million infants from 2017 to 2025 and found that vitamin K administration declined from 97.7% to 95%, while cases of vitamin K deficiency bleeding (VKDB) nearly quadrupled.

A second study published in JAMA, based on data from 5.2 million U.S. infants, found that refusal rates more than doubled, rising from 3.1% in 2017 to 6.8% in 2025. Infants who did not receive the shot had a 47% higher risk of bleeding and more than twice the risk of intracranial bleeding.

Additionally, new data from Truveta suggest the trend is continuing: refusals of vitamin K shots increased by 57% between January and June 2026, rising from 5.2% to 8.1% of newborns.

Vitamin K is essential for blood clotting, and newborns naturally have very low levels. Without the preventive injection, infants are at risk for VKDB, which can cause sudden bleeding in the brain, gastrointestinal tract, and other organs. Late-onset VKDB can occur up to six months after birth, often without warning signs, and may be fatal.

The Centers for Disease Control and Prevention (CDC) and American Academy of Pediatrics (AAP) continue to recommend an intramuscular vitamin K injection for all newborns, ideally within 6 hours of birth.

The shot is not a vaccine; it is a nutrient injection that helps prevent dangerous bleeding. A single dose reduces the risk of late-onset VKDB by approximately 98%.

Experts not involved in the studies attribute the rise in refusals partly to broader vaccine hesitancy and mixed public health messaging.

“The decision to vaccinate a newborn or allow a vitamin K injection at birth is complex, with multiple factors playing a role in the ultimate decision,” said Graham Tse, MD, a pediatrician and chief medical officer of MemorialCare Miller Children’s & Women’s Hospital in Long Beach, CA.

Some of these factors, Tse said, include conflicting messages from federal health agencies such as the Department of Health and Human Services (HHS) and the Centers for Disease Control and Prevention (CDC), both overseen by HHS Secretary Robert F. Kennedy, Jr., an outspoken critic of the childhood immunization schedule.

The vitamin K shot is not a vaccine, yet RFK Jr. has declined to reassure parents about its safety, despite the CDC’s current recommendation to administer it at birth.

Additionally, growing medical distrust among parents has led to refusal of routine newborn care, such as the vitamin K shot, Reuters reports.

Healthline spoke with Tse to learn more about the risks of refusing the vitamin K shot and what new or expectant parents should know about this routine newborn treatment.

This interview has been lightly edited and condensed for clarity and length.

Tse: The decision to vaccinate a newborn or allow a vitamin K injection at birth is complex, with multiple factors playing a role in the ultimate decision.

A contributing factor to the decline in vitamin K injection at birth, as well as in newborn hepatitis B vaccination, is the conflicting messages from the Department of Health and Human Services (HHS), and the Centers for Disease Control and Prevention (CDC).

Recommendations from these entities and others are conflicting or do not align with established, research-based, and proven recommendations from organizations such as the American Academy of Pediatrics (AAP) and the Infectious Diseases Society of America.

When there are conflicting recommendations, it creates confusion and doubt among parents about what the best or right choice is for their child.

As well, there is so much misinformation on the internet, among influencers, and on social media that it is difficult to know what information is accurate, well-researched, and proven, versus what may be just someone’s opinion or belief.

Some are mistaking the vitamin K injection at birth for a vaccination, which it is not. Ultimately, all these factors are contributing to parents declining or delaying vitamin K injections at birth.

Tse: Declining the newborn vitamin K injection puts the newborn at significant risk of serious and life threatening complications from vitamin K-deficient bleeding (VKDB). This was previously referred to as hemorrhagic disease of the newborn.

VKDB can be as mild as bruising of the skin but can be as serious as bleeding into the brain, and newborns and infants can die from VKDB.

Declining vitamin K injection at birth is significant because of the serious and potentially life threatening consequences of deficiency of Vitamin K and not being able to clot the blood.

The vitamin K injection at birth is highly effective at preventing VKDB.

Tse: If a neonate or infant has vitamin K deficiency, the ability to clot their blood and stop bleeding is highly reduced.

All newborns are at risk of low vitamin K due to low body stores, low vitamin K content of breast milk, reduced ability of the gut to mobilize vitamin K, and poor transfer of vitamin K across the placenta.

When the blood cannot clot, bleeding from minor or minimal trauma can occur, and even bleeding without injury or trauma may occur. This can include bruises, bleeding from the mouth or gastrointestinal tract, bleeding from cuts or circumcision.

Bleeding can occur anywhere in the body, including in the brain, which can be catastrophic.

VKDB can occur from birth and through several months of life. Bleeding in the brain from VKDB, one of the most serious consequences, can occur at any time from birth to up to 6 months or so and can occur unexpectedly and suddenly. Vitamin K given at birth can prevent this risk of bleeding.

Tse: Vitamin K is a necessary vitamin that all humans need. Vitamin K is not a vaccination.

Newborns need vitamin K to prevent serious, significant, and often catastrophic bleeding during their first several months of life.

Vitamin K has been given to newborns in the United States for close to 70 years, is safe, is studied, and is proven to help prevent vitamin K-deficient bleeding.

I urge new parents to allow their newborns to receive the vitamin K injection at birth — it protects them, keeps them healthy, is proven safe, and is recommended.

U15 : de la joie contre les Caladois

📅 Nov 09, 2026  |  Category: Other  |  Read Source →

Les U15 de Benjamin Petit pouvaient être heureux à la fin de leur match contre les Caladois du FC Villefranche Beaujolais. A l'issue d'une rencontre très disputée, comme souvent dans cet exigeant championnat, ils n'ont rien lâché face à un adversaire de valeur et ont su trouver les ressources pour l'emporter. Cela récompense leur volonté d'aller de l'avant, leur ténacité et leur concentration dans l'aspect défensif. Sans quelques joueurs retenus avec les U16 de Kevin De Jesus (Ilan Clapeyron, Riad Moutae, Kaïs Boukerma et Hugo Durin) tous ont su se mettre au diapason, et cette victoire est celle d'un collectif très solidaire.

L'entame du match a été stéphanoise, les Verts s'installant dans le camp caladois. Dès la 9ème minute, sur un centre venu de la gauche, Nolan Lebreton remettait pour Charly Davignon qui était devancé de peu par un défenseur. Deux minutes plus tard, un coup-franc de ce même Nolan Lebreton était capté par le gardien. Les Verts monopolisaient le ballon mais le dernier geste, souvent dans les petits espaces, manquait de justesse pour mettre de conclure. Peu à peu, Villefranche déserrait l'étreinte stéphanoise et se montrait dangereux sur coup de pied arrêté, mais Manoa Frison faisait bonne garde, notamment, à la 26ème, où il réalisait une double parade avant que Victor Hamouma ne s'interpose pour stopper la tentative adverse. Quelques minutes plus tard, Charly Davignon, qui repiquait au centre, était victime d'une faute laquelle donnait lieu à un coup-franc. Nolhan Fernandes le frappait et mettait le gardien à contribution. Peu après sur un long centre de Nolhan Fernandes, Hugo Bastide obtenait un corner. Ce corner, joué à deux, permettait à Nolan Lebreton d'adresser un long centre au deuxième poteau que le portier caladois dégageait. Les contres de Villefranche butaient sur une solide défense stéphanoise, concentrée et rigoureuse qui gérait bien les espaces et s'attachait à ressortir proprement les ballons. Le score était donc nul et vierge au repos.

La deuxième mi-temps repartait sur les mêmes bases, avec une maitrîse verte retrouvée et des situations intéressantes, sans parvenir toutefois à faire la différence.  Mais les Verts, suite à un carton blanc à la 54ème minute, se retrouvaient réduits à 10 pendant une dizaine de minutes, et ce fait de jeu freinait un peu leur dynamique. Ils allaient trouver la faille suite à un cafouillage dans la surface, avec une main adverse un accrochage sur Mathis La Rosa. Toujours est-il que l'arbitre sanctionnait cette action d'un penalty que Charly Davignon transformait, donnnt l'avantage aux Verts (68ème). La fin du match allait être plutôt hâchée avec plusieurs coups-de-pied arrêtés que Villefranche jouait directement. Les Verts se créaient cependant quelques opportunités mais un manque de justesse ne leur permettait pas de les concrétiser. Le match se terminait donc sur cette victoire stéphanoise somme toute logique, tant elle avait été bien construite.

La composition : Frison - Bergougnoux (Uneau, 50ème), L. La Rosa, Hamouma, Bastide - Wojcik (Davignon, 50ème), M.La Rosa, Lebreton - Fernandes (Madjidi, 50ème), Maganga Verrier, Davignon (Linganzi, 41ème).

La réaction de Benjamin Petit : "Le match a été serré, très disputé face à une bonne équipe de Villefranche, comme souvent dans ce championnat. Il n'y a pas de match facile et c'est un bon apprentissage pour les jeunes. Nous avons fait vingt premières minutes plutôt intéressantes, avec de la maîtrise, une volonté de défendre en avançant, un point que travaillons. Nous avons baissé en fin de première mi-temps, avec plus de difficulté à maintenir notre pression, ce qui nous a mené à défendre plus bas. En conclusion, je dirais que globalement, nous avons eu la maîtrise, mais mon regret est le manque de justesse, de percussion et d'efficacité pour créer du danger sur le but adverse et se mettre à l'abri. Je retiens notre solidité défensive, notre rigueur dans les prises en charge, notre détermination à ne rien encaisser, notamment sur les coups-de-pied arrêtés, un domaine dans lequel nous avons progressé. Il faudra néanmoins éviter certaines fautes, évitables, qui offrent des situations dangereuses à l'adversaire. C'était un match intéressant dans l'apprentissage, la gestion des temps forts et des temps faibles. Il faut toutefois  essayer d'être plus efficace dans nos temps forts tout en restant solides dans les temps faibles."

The Trump administration announced Monday funding for the second year of the Rural Health Transformation Program (RHTP), with $10 billion in awards slated for all 50 states.

The U.S. Department of Health and Human Services (HHS) announced awards through the Centers for Medicare and Medicaid Services.

“President Trump made a historic investment in rural America, and HHS is putting those resources to work in every state,” said Robert F. Kennedy, Jr., HHS secretary, in a statement. “This next $10 billion will expand access to care, strengthen the rural health workforce, improve prevention, and help states build solutions that fit the needs of their own communities. We are giving rural America the resources and flexibility to build stronger health systems that deliver better care and better outcomes.”

Under the statute, half of the $10 billion-per-year distribution is split between the states evenly. The remaining half is determined by the CMS, with a portion of second-year funding being determined by states’ progress in implementing approved RHTP plans.

Every state submitted applications to the federal government last November outlining plans to improve rural healthcare through their share of $50 billion in funds from the program, with CMS announcing first year awards in December.

Plant-Based Milk Alternatives May Not Give You Enough Iodine. Here’s Why That Matters

📅 Nov 09, 2026  |  Category: Health  |  Read Source →

Follow Healthline on our socials for all things health.

Replacing dairy with plant-based alternatives (PBAs), even partially, could substantially lower iodine intake, especially if those alternatives aren’t fortified, according to a recent study published in the Lancet Planetary Health.

The United Kingdom-based researchers found that only 12% of UK plant-based milk, yogurt, and cheese products analyzed were fortified with iodine. This compares with 61% for calcium and 46% for vitamin B12.

In the United States, the Food and Drug Administration (FDA) provides guidance on the nutritional content of PBAs, but fortification with iodine is voluntary.

The study found that replacing as little as 20% of dairy intake with plant-based alternatives could increase the risk of inadequate iodine intake, particularly among adolescent girls and women of reproductive age.

“People often worry about a decline in calcium intake when they replace some of their dairy with plant-based alternatives. As a result, iodine is frequently overlooked. This study serves as a valuable reminder of iodine’s significance as a nutrient,” said Victoria Finn, MD, board certified endocrinologist at Medical Offices of Manhattan, who wasn’t involved in the study.

The study included analysis of UK plant-based milk, cheese, and yogurt products, as well as dietary data from 3,500 people.

The researchers used statistical modeling to predict how swapping dairy for PBAs might affect iodine and other nutrients.

The people who are most vulnerable to iodine inadequacy are adolescent females and people at reproductive age, especially those who are pregnant or who plan to become pregnant.

The researchers estimated that replacing 20% to 35% of dairy intake with PBAs would increase the proportion of people with inadequate iodine intake:

When the researchers modeled the replacement of all dairy products with PBAs, the proportion of adolescent females with inadequate iodine intake rose to 80%. Among females ages 19 to 49, that proportion remained relatively stable as replacement rates increased.

It is important to keep in mind that the study used dietary modeling rather than direct measurement of iodine status.

Iodine is important for thyroid hormone production, metabolism, and both fetal and child brain development.

“Your thyroid gland requires iodine to produce thyroid hormones, which play a crucial role in regulating metabolism and supporting normal growth and development,” said Finn.

She added that if you consistently consume too little iodine over an extended period, the thyroid gland may enlarge and eventually struggle to produce enough hormones. This condition is known as hypothyroidism, which can lead to various symptoms such as lethargy, sleepiness, weight gain, dry skin, and more.

“Hypothyroidism can be particularly dangerous during pregnancy, as the baby’s brain, nervous system, and organs rely heavily on the mother’s thyroid hormone during the early stages of development,” Finn added.

In the United Kingdom, dairy is a major iodine source due to cattle feed supplementation, not because dairy is naturally high in iodine.

In the United States, dairy is also an important source of iodine for the same reasons.

However, the risk profile for iodine deficiency in the U.S. may differ from the U.K., as iodized salt is also a significant source of iodine and is widely available in the U.S., but not in the U.K.

According to the National Institutes of Health (NIH), the recommended daily intake of iodine depends on your age.

Plant-based or plant-forward diets have grown in popularity, in part due to the health benefits they offer. For example, consuming less meat can reduce your risk of heart disease, type 2 diabetes, obesity, and high cholesterol.

However, as this study suggests, it may be possible to reduce your iodine intake when transitioning to a plant-based diet. There are ways you can protect your iodine intake when choosing a more plant-forward diet, though.

Check the labels on plant-based dairy alternatives to find those fortified with iodine. You can also read the nutrition label. Iodine may be listed as potassium iodide or potassium iodate.

Seafood, eggs, and iodized salt are all good sources of iodine.

You can also discuss iodine supplements with your healthcare professional.

“Iodine supplements should be taken with caution, especially without checking your thyroid levels,” said Finn. “Additionally, be cautious with kelp supplements, as their iodine content can vary, and excessive iodine intake can disrupt thyroid function.”

Rotate a leaked API key without stopping your services: dotvault hands out the new value first

📅 Nov 09, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

This is part 11 of the series "dotvault, Start to Finish: One Command from Your Laptop to CI, Your Team, and Your AI Agents." dotvault will be released free of charge when this series ends.

In the demo, a script kept calling with the key at 15-second intervals during the whole rotation, and in all 111 calls that got a response, the caller's value was accepted. The caller moved to the new value on its own, and the leaked value was rejected from then on.

That matters because rotating a key between two of your services has an ordering problem. Switch the service that checks the key first, and the caller still holding the old value gets rejected. Switch the caller first, and the checker rejects a value it has never seen. Either way, calls fail until the other side catches up, and the usual way around it is to stop both and switch them together. This part shows how dotvault rotate --reason leak avoids that.

This part also covers a second feature: rotating a key automatically at an interval you choose.

Rotation is not only for leaks. It is part of routine key management. Set an interval when you register a key, and dotvault keeps rotating it on its own, even when nothing has leaked. Nobody has to rotate keys by hand any more, so you can keep each key's lifetime short.

This follows part 4 (overwriting and rolling back values) and part 7 (creating service tokens). You can follow along without them.

The demo: order-worker processes orders and calls payment-api, the payment API. It sends PAYMENT_API_KEY, and payment-api checks that the value is right. Assume PAYMENT_API_KEY has leaked.

Rotation works for keys that both sides receive from dotvault, which means keys between your own services. dotvault generates the new value. An API key issued by another company's service has to be replaced in that service's dashboard.

In the demo project shop-backend, the first value of PAYMENT_API_KEY went into production with dotvault set. order-worker and payment-api each got a Read only service token. A service token is the string, starting with est_, that CI or a service uses instead of logging in. Part 7 shows how to create one.

Registering takes a token of the Credential rotation type. In Service Tokens, click Create token and pick "Credential rotation" as the Role.

As the dialog says, this token can issue tokens and register keys only inside one project and the environments you pick. It is not needed once the key is registered, so it expires in 1 day.

With that token, send one HTTP PUT, the request type that stores data at a URL. The body is JSON naming the holder and verifier tokens by Token ID.

HTTP 201 (created) and "created":true mean the key is registered. The fields:

key= in the URL is the key name, and project=, env= and file= say where it lives. $DOTVAULT_TOKEN in the Authorization header holds the Credential rotation token.

A rotation cannot start in the first 5 minutes after registering. Register service-to-service keys when you store them, not after a leak.

After registering, pull the same project as a person, with the holder token and with the verifier token.

Only the holder, order-worker, gets the value. This screen was captured after the rotation in section 4; a registered key is handed out the same way before and after a rotation. The person (an org admin) and the verifier see the key name in a "withheld 1 rotation-managed key(s)" warning, with no value. If you need a key with that name for local development, store a development value under a different key name.

The verifier does not pull. It reads the set of values it should accept from GET /v1/env/key-set. Each entry has the value, an ID for the value (keyId) and a state (state). The verifier accepts a value that is in this set.

When nothing has leaked, a routine rotation runs without --reason (the same as --reason manual).

waiting on verifier payment-api means payment-api has not read the new value yet. last ok is when it last read the set. The phase deadline of a routine rotation (24 hours after the start) is how long it waits for a side that has not read the new value. Past it, the rotation does not move on; it stops (STALLED).

Even when nothing has leaked, you may not want to keep one key for a long time. Add interval_seconds (the rotation interval, in seconds) when you register the key, and dotvault starts a routine rotation on its own at that interval. Rotation stops being a person's job.

In the demo, a new key, WEBHOOK_SECRET, was registered with the same holder and verifier tokens as in section 1, with interval_seconds set to 600 (10 minutes). Ten minutes is only for the recording; in real use you would pick something like 30 days (2592000).

Right after registering, --status says managed but has never run: the key is registered but has never been rotated. Nobody typed a rotate command after that (the only thing running was the script that reads values). Once the 10-minute interval had passed, --status showed reason scheduled (dotvault started this rotation itself because the interval came round), and the rotation had already added the new value to the verifier's accepted set (STAGED).

The audit log agrees. After the registration (rotation_provisioner.config_created), both secret.rotation.started and secret.rotation.staged have the Actor system:rotation-driver. In the leak rotation in section 6, a person was the Actor of started; here dotvault started the rotation too.

A scheduled rotation is the same as the routine rotation in section 7: the old and new values both stay valid for at least 24 hours, and the new value goes to the verifier first, then to the holder. The next rotation starts once the interval has passed after the previous one finished. Right after you register a key, or shorten its interval, nothing starts for 5 minutes.

A rotation only reaches the end when the holders and verifiers read from dotvault as part of their normal work. If a service never reads again, the rotation waits on it with the waiting on … line from section 7, and past the deadline it stops (STALLED).

A rotation accepts the leaked value until the new one is everywhere. Even after the holder has read the new value, there is a 15-minute wait and the 60-second residual. To make the leaked value stop working sooner, skip the remaining waits with --revoke-now.

This was recorded after PAYMENT_API_KEY had been retired (later in this section), on a second key, REFUND_API_KEY, registered the same way. One of the script's calls already carried the new value (PROMOTED). Then --revoke-now: in the audit log, revoke_now, revoked and completed share the same second, and from the next call that got a response, the leaked value was REJECTED. The holder's value stayed accepted. The script ran 74 times during this recording, and in all 57 calls that got a response, the holder's value was accepted (the other 17 failed on the script's side before reaching dotvault).

Run --revoke-now once every holder has the new value, that is, after PROMOTED. It skips every wait from that point on, so if you run it earlier, dotvault switches values and revokes the old one without waiting for the verifier or the holder to read the new one, and a holder still on the old value is rejected.

The waiting on holder … line in --status is per token. When several services share one token, the line disappears as soon as one of them reads. Any other service still holding the old value is rejected after --revoke-now; that is what 10 of the 15 minutes of waiting are for. Run it only when you know every service has pulled again, for example after restarting them. In the demo the holder's value stayed accepted because there was one holder and it pulled on every call.

One more thing: revoking takes the value out of dotvault's accepted set. While a verifier keeps its copy of the set (up to 5 minutes), it may still accept the leaked value. The demo's verifier read the set on every call, so the leaked value was REJECTED from the next call.

--confirm repeats the key name; one wrong character stops it with VALIDATION_ERROR and nothing is sent. Only a signed-in person can run it; a service token cannot.

If you cannot even wait for the holder to move to the new value, retire the key.

The retire ran on the same key after the PAYMENT_API_KEY rotation was recorded. revoked version(s): 3 means it revoked v3, the current value from the rotation; v1 and v2 were already revoked. The verifier can no longer read the set (HTTP 403, NOT_BOUND), and writing the leaked value back with set fails with VALUE_REVOKED: a revoked value can never be written again.

Retire hands out no new value, so the holder and the verifier both stop at once. --confirm repeats the key name, and one wrong character means nothing happens. Only a signed-in person can retire; a service token cannot. Use it only when the leaked value has to stop working right now, even at the cost of stopping your services.

For a routine rotation when nothing has leaked, use dotvault rotate (--reason manual); the old value stays usable for at least 24 hours. Add interval_seconds to the registration, and dotvault starts that routine rotation on its own at the interval you set.

Rotating a key between two services fails calls whichever side you switch first, until the other side catches up. Register service-to-service keys for rotation management when you store them, and when one leaks, run dotvault rotate --reason leak. dotvault gives the new value to the verifier, then to the holder, and revokes the leaked value only after both have read the new one, so the leaked value stops working without stopping your services. Set an interval when you register the key, and routine rotation before any leak stops being a person's job too.

Because the beacon tag carries a sha512 hash, I hashed the 31,853 bytes Cloudflare served and compared the digest with the one in the tag. They are the same string, so a browser that checks the hash finds a match.

Both edge scripts come after the closing footer tag and before the closing body tag on all 56 pages. The rest of the document is ours: 155 script tags across the fleet, and not one of them carries an integrity attribute.

Fortnite Officially Reveals Pirate Shop Access Token Odds

📅 Nov 09, 2026  |  Category: Other  |  Read Source →

How much are you willing to grind for each treasure?

Since Fortnite Treasure Hunters began on Nov. 1, Epic Games has been flooded with disgruntled fans wanting to know the odds of treasures that can be found in chests this season, namely the Pirate Shop Access token. Epic has finally relented, revealing the odds for each item and how much Treasure Hours influence them.

This season, the Pirate Shop Access token was introduced in Fortnite. The token is an item that, upon activation, makes every item sold in the Item Shop with in-game currency free for two weeks. It can be used twice: once in 2026 and again in 2027. This item has created an unpredictable frenzy among the community. Even though it's against Epic Games' terms of service, players have been selling accounts and offering token-hunting services at hourly rates.

The Pirate Shop Access token led fans to focus on opening chests rather than enjoying the highly-anticipated One Piece-themed season. After opening thousands of chests with no luck, fans wanted to know their chances of finding it. On Saturday, before Treasure Hours began, they spammed the hashtag ReleaseTheOdds, demanding Epic Games share the odds of each treasure in Fortnite. It finally complied.

According to Epic Games, the Pirate Shop Access token has a drop chance of 1 per 137,500 chests, which is the equivalent of 0.000007273%. Odds have not been tweaked throughout the season except for Treasure Hours timed-event — they were boosted 1.5x for the first Treasure Hour on Saturday, and 2x the second time around. The golden skin, which will rotate each week, is the rarest treasure players can find by far (1 per 1,562,500 chests), but it's safe to say that the token is still more desirable because it's worth the equivalent of thousands of dollars.

It's good that Epic Games was finally transparent with fans, but one question remains. The Solid Gold A$AP Rocky outfit has now rotated out of the weekly treasures available, replaced by Solid Gold Lil Uzi Vert. These skins are free to find during Treasure Hunters, but it is unknown whether either will be available for purchase in the future. If not, they would become the rarest cosmetics in Fortnite history.

Nov. 14 marks the last set of Treasure Hours before the launch of Grand Theft Auto 6, so if you're still grinding for the token, this is the best time to try to find it. The season ends on Dec. 5, so gamers can still search for it on Nov. 21 and 28 if they're willing to put GTA 6 aside for a couple of hours.
