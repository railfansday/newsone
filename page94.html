GM is recalling more than 67,000 Cadillac and Chevy EVs over a brake software defect

📅 Nov 04, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

General Motors (GM) issued a recall affecting more than 67,000 Cadillac and Chevy EVs because a software glitch can cause a temporary loss of braking.

The recall comes after a 2024 Cadillac Lyriq ran into a garage (at a low speed). On April 10, 2026, a GM tech expert submitted a report to GM’s Speak Up for Safety (SUFS) program for review.

After internal testing, GM was able to replicate the glitch, similar to a recall it issued in 2024. GM said the issue stems from a software glitch with the brake system control module (BSCM) that can cause a temporary loss of braking while driving under certain conditions.

When drivers lightly tap the brakes during a sharp turn and the vehicle hits a bump, the software’s slip calculation may misinterpret wheel movement.

In some instances, it can lead to a temporary loss of braking. The glitch only affects pAWD vehicles, including certain Cadillac Lyriq, Celestiq, Vistiq, and Chevy Blazer EV.

According to documents filed with the NHTSA, GM found 24 field complaints related to the issue. Of these, 20 involved minor accidents, and two involved allegations of minor injuries.

The complaints were received between February 24, 2024, and June 11, 2026. GM said it has implemented software corrections in production over the past few months.

GM will update the BSCM software via an over-the-air (OTA) update or through a dealer, free of charge.

Owner notification letters are expected to be mailed out on December 14, 2026. In the meantime, you can contact Cadillac’s customer service at 1-800-333-4223 or Chevrolet’s customer service at 1-800-222-1020. GM’s recall number is N262570900.

You can also contact the National Highway Traffic Safety Administration Hotline at 1-888-327-4236 or visit the nhtsa.gov website for more information. The NHTSA campaign number is 26V717.

Crypto Market Today, Nov. 4: Bitcoin Stages Sharp Recovery as Wall Street Rallies and Regulatory Hopes Return

📅 Nov 04, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Bitcoin reclaimed $80,000 as cryptocurrencies rallied alongside Wall Street following the midterm elections, with Ethereum leading gains among major digital assets.

As of early evening on Nov. 4, Bitcoin (BTC +3.35%) rose 3.64% to $80,156.15, Ethereum (ETH +5.44%) rose 6.58% to $2,421.50, and Solana (SOL +1.42%) rose 2.19% to $94.94.

Cryptocurrency markets staged a broad recovery on Wednesday as the conclusion of the U.S. midterm elections helped ease political uncertainty that had weighed on risk assets in recent weeks. Bitcoin, which had fallen as low as $75,700 amid sustained selling pressure, rebounded above $80,000, while Ethereum led gains among major cryptocurrencies, climbing more than 6% to a two-week high above $2,400.

The recovery coincided with a broader rally across U.S. equities, as investors responded positively to the election results and the prospect of greater policy stability. Declining oil prices and easing Treasury yields provided additional support for risk appetite, helping lift cryptocurrencies alongside traditional financial markets.

Among key crypto funds, iShares Bitcoin Trust ETF (IBIT +2.91%) rose 2.91%, Fidelity Wise Origin Bitcoin Fund (FBTC +2.84%) rose 2.87%, and iShares Ethereum Trust ETF (ETHA +5.72%) rose 5.72%.

The rebound follows an extended period of weakness that resulted in more than $1 billion of outflows from spot Bitcoin ETFs. Whether Wednesday's gains mark the beginning of a sustained recovery will likely depend on improving ETF flows.

In the wake of the Democratic sweep of Congress, crypto investors should be watching for a potential shift in the regulatory landscape. Democratic lawmakers are reportedly considering revisions to the stalled CLARITY Act that could expand the U.S. Securities and Exchange Commission's (SEC) authority, strengthen investor protections, and impose additional restrictions on government officials' cryptocurrency activities. Although discussions remain preliminary, renewed legislative momentum could help resolve regulatory uncertainty that has weighed on the industry for months and establish a clearer framework heading into 2027.

In the near term, investors will be looking for confirmation that Wednesday's rebound has staying power. Bitcoin holding above the psychologically important $80,000 level and reclaiming $82,000 would be encouraging signs of renewed buying momentum.

Ultimately, the combination of regulatory developments and reclamation of key levels could provide a foundation for a more sustained recovery. However, macroeconomic uncertainty, shifting interest rate expectations, and lingering weakness across the broader cryptocurrency market suggest investors should remain prepared for continued volatility.

TKO reports $1.36 billion in Q3 revenue, with UFC alone pulling in $519.2 million from July to September

📅 Nov 04, 2026  |  Category: Business  |  Read Source →

TKO Group Holdings LLC, the parent company to UFC and WWE, reported revenues of $1.36 billion in its 2026 third-quarter earnings report on Wednesday, citing the substantial financial growth of the UFC as the main driver.

According to the earnings report released by the company, UFC revenues increased by $194 million in the third quarter of 2026 — spanning July 1 through Sept. 30 — bringing total UFC revenues for the quarter to $519.2 million. It also reported third-quarter revenues of $399.3 million for WWE, which represents a decrease of $2.8 million.

"Our third quarter performance reflects the secular tailwinds powering live events and experiences around the world, and TKO's assets sit front and center of that premium ecosystem," TKO President and COO Mark Shapiro said in the release. "As we close out what we've called our year of execution, our decision to raise full-year guidance for the second time this year is the clearest expression of our conviction across the entire portfolio.

"Our business is durable and our fundamentals are solid, with the vast majority of our existing revenues locked in for the next five to seven years," Shapiro continued. "Macro trends are firmly in our favor, every facet of sports consumption is pointing north, and we see substantial runway ahead."

According to Wednesday's earnings report, the bulk of the increase in UFC revenue was due to a $132.3 million increase in the category of media rights, production and content. The report noted that this increase was "primarily related to higher media rights fees, which reflected the impact of the new distribution agreement" with Skydance, which brought UFC events to Paramount+ at the beginning of the year.

UFC revenues also benefited from "an increase in ticket sales revenue, which reflected the record-setting performance of UFC 329 and two additional events." UFC 329 saw the return of former UFC two-division champion Conor McGregor, which helped propel the event to a $26,430,566 live gate — a new UFC record.

On the WWE side, the slight dip in revenue was attributed to "a $24.8 million decrease in live events and hospitality revenue," driven in part by fewer premium live events, as well as decreased ticket sales for the company's SummerSlam event in August.

Overall, TKO again increased its target revenue for the year, raising the expected mark from $5.79 billion to $5.83 billion for the fiscal year.

Yearly total revenue for the UFC currently sits at more than $1.45 billion, per the report, while yearly total revenue for the WWE currently sits at more than $1.49 billion.

Welcome to Foreign Policy’s South Asia Brief.

The highlights this week: A proposed liquefied natural gas project in Pakistan underscores close ties with the Trump administration, garment workers in Bangladesh protest for pay increases, and India’s opposition alliance rallies to call for the resignation of the chief election commissioner.

Welcome to Foreign Policy’s South Asia Brief.

The highlights this week: A proposed liquefied natural gas project in Pakistan underscores close ties with the Trump administration, garment workers in Bangladesh protest for pay increases, and India’s opposition alliance rallies to call for the resignation of the chief election commissioner.

Since U.S. President Donald Trump returned to the White House last year, Pakistan has banked heavily on doing business with the United States. With the Democrats flipping both houses of Congress in the U.S. midterm elections on Tuesday, though, one has to wonder if Islamabad could come out as another loser.

For one thing, scrutiny of Trump’s business dealings will certainly increase, constraining his administration’s capacity to pursue deals abroad.

A big topic of discussion in Pakistan this week—along with the midterms—is the news that a consortium of U.S. companies has proposed a $4.4 billion liquefied natural gas (LNG) project in Port Qasim, near Karachi. It would be Pakistan’s first LNG storage facility, helping to shield the country from global energy shocks.

The proposal entails a large-scale storage terminal and eventually a 680-mile delivery pipeline. The plan is backed by the U.S. Trade and Development Agency, U.S. International Development Finance Corp., and the Export-Import Bank of the United States.

Washington’s support for the proposed LNG project underscores just how much the U.S.-Pakistan relationship has changed in Trump’s second term. Bilateral ties were downgraded after the U.S. withdrawal from Afghanistan in 2021, in large part because Washington had long viewed relations with Islamabad through a security lens.

The Trump administration turned this approach on its head. The United States now prioritizes ties with Pakistan, and Trump often praises the country’s leadership. Though counterterrorism still figures in the relationship, Washington has emphasized economic and commercial issues in its engagement with Islamabad.

In particular, the United States has taken a strong interest in Pakistan’s critical minerals and cryptocurrency resources. It has pushed for the country to import more U.S. goods, including energy supplies. In October 2025, Pakistan received its first-ever shipment of U.S. crude oil. Washington has pushed to remove trade obstacles with Islamabad, including ending a two-year ban on U.S. soybean exports.

Pakistan, which had long pitched its economic offerings to the United States unsuccessfully, has capitalized on the Trump administration’s newfound interest in its assets. The Financial Times reported last year that Islamabad went as far as to offer Washington the opportunity to build and manage a port on the Arabian Sea. (Pakistan publicly denies this.)

The proposed LNG project would be one of the Trump administration’s most ambitious commercial ventures in Pakistan, perhaps putting an exclamation point on the shift in the U.S. approach to the country. Yet that shift might not even be sustainable—especially given the midterm results.

The Trump administration’s commercial focus is largely driven by the president’s whims and by the economic interests of his family members and close associates. But this arrangement won’t outlast Trump’s term, which ends in two years. Further, with Congress in the hands of the Democrats starting in January, it could also become more difficult to pull off.

Conversations with Pakistani interlocutors this week revealed a combination of curiosity and concern about ties with the United States in the Trump era. There seems to be a desire to understand how the relationship improved so quickly alongside a sense that it might not last for long, given Trump’s unpredictability. The Democrats’ victory will only sharpen these concerns.

These worries reflect an evergreen challenge for the U.S.-Pakistan relationship: how to craft a strong partnership that can withstand political change in both countries and weather external shocks.

The last time the White House officially announced a policy on Pakistan was during Trump’s first term, in August 2017, when a new South Asia strategy included a hard-line approach toward Islamabad. It’s clear that the United States is overdue in conducting a formal review of its Pakistan policy, even as the relationship is being informally reevaluated and refashioned.

Garment protests in Bangladesh. Garment workers have taken to the streets in industrial areas of Dhaka, Bangladesh’s capital, in recent days to call for pay increases. Clashes with security forces have prompted the closure of dozens of garment factories and raised concerns about shipment delays.

Garment workers are a critical constituency in Bangladesh, numbering around 4 million, and the sector accounts for 80 percent of the country’s export earnings. Bangladesh’s ready-made garment industry is a global success story; it made the country one of South Asia’s best-performing economies until a slump in the last few years.

With the downturn, Bangladesh seems to be paying the price for relying so heavily on a single export—as globally competitive as it was—for so long. The protests are just the latest policy challenge for Dhaka, which is already struggling with economic stress, energy shortages, and the world’s most serious measles crisis.

These issues risk distracting Bangladesh from pursuing economic and governance reforms—something the government, which took office in February, was given a mandate to prioritize.

India’s opposition alliance rallies. On Sunday, the main opposition alliance in India staged protests in New Delhi to call for the resignation of India’s chief election commissioner, Gyanesh Kumar. The rally drew tens of thousands of people and marked the latest mobilization against a voter roll revision that critics say favored the ruling Bharatiya Janata Party.

The youth-led Cockroach Janta Party has spearheaded the protests calling for Kumar to step down. This summer, the Cockroach movement galvanized protests over leaked exam papers that ultimately resulted in the resignation of the country’s education minister.

Now, India’s opposition is riding on the coattails of this activism. The success of the Cockroach movement is a gift to the opposition, which has long been hobbled by the absence of a popular leader and disagreements within its alliance.

Heat stress becomes serious vulnerability. The International Labor Organization (ILO) released studies last week warning that by 2040, workplace heat stress spawned by rising temperatures could lead to reduced working time equivalent to around 145,000 full-time jobs in Sri Lanka and 2,400 in the Maldives.

The reports estimate that these losses will be valued at around $26 billion in Sri Lanka and $576 million in the Maldives. The hardest-hit sector in Sri Lanka will be agriculture, along with services, manufacturing, and construction. In the Maldives, services and construction will be the most vulnerable industries.

The ILO findings are a reminder of the high economic cost of climate change in a region where economies are fragile—and where some of the most labor-dependent sectors are especially vulnerable to climate effects.

Last Sunday, around 200,000 people converged on the Bhutanese town of Gelephu to watch volunteers construct 108 stupas. (The number 108 is sacred in Buddhism.) The initiative is part of a campaign led by Bhutan’s king to transform Gelephu, near Bhutan’s border with India, into a “mindfulness city.”

Bhutan hopes to use the initiative, which emphasizes ecological sustainability and happiness, as an antidote to the country’s deepening economic stress. The stupa project is certainly an unorthodox way to tackle these problems. Critics will likely say it does little to reverse rising unemployment and a rapid outflow of highly skilled workers.

However, the project aligns with a long-standing Bhutanese principle known as “gross national happiness,” which measures national well-being not by income or other economic indicators but instead by the level of contentment among citizens.

When indie film distributor Black Bear released the first trailer for Wicker, it hit social media timelines with a bang. Based on the short story The Wicker Husband by Ursula Wills-Jones, the film follows a small village fisherwoman (Olivia Colman) who convinces a local basket weaver (Peter Dinklage) to build her a husband out of wicker (Alexander Skarsgård). Fans, naturally, went a bit feral over the scenes of Colman and a basket-ified Skarsgård consummating their marriage so rapturously they break the bed. Eventually, a common joke emerged: “Will this be available in 4DX?”

Sometimes, for the pure of heart, prayers are answered. PSV Eindhoven endured a frustrating night in the Champions League as their defensive frailties were exposed in a 2-1 defeat by Club Brugge.

PSV started full of attacking intent and should have taken an early lead when Ivan Perisic put Sergiño Dest clean through on goal, but the defender’s shot was too close to goalkeeper Yann Sommer.

Instead it was the visitors who took the lead with their first real attack when Nicolo Tresoldi headed in a teasing cross from Joaquin Seys.

PSV equalised early in the second half as an outstanding pass by Sven Mijnans into the path Lutsharel Geertruida allowed the defender to charge to the byline. His pullback found its way to Kodai Sano, whose shot took a deflection off Seys into the net.

But five minutes later PSV were caught out as another razor-sharp pass by Seys released Carlos Forbs in space to net the winner.

The home side refused to give up, but were unable to find a way past Sommer in the closing stages as the Belgian side closed out the win.

Team captain Mauro Junior admitted that PSV had been undone by shortcomings at the back. “We had a lot of opportunities in the first half but we didn’t score,” he told Ziggo Sport.

“We want to attack and score goals, but we weren’ well organised today in defence. We need to keep our heads up and look forward to the next game.”

The result leaves PSV in 24th place, just above the elimination line, ahead of their next match away to Real Madrid on November 24, with four points from their opening four matches.

Slavia Prague 1-2 Arsenal: The Swedish striker provided Noni Madueke with an assist and then scored a stylish goal as the Gunners continued their impressive form

Viktor Gyokeres grasped his opportunity as Arsenal beat Slavia Prague 2-1 in the Champions League.

Gyokeres made the most of a rare start, his sixth of the season, with an assist and a stylish goal as the Gunners climbed to third in the Champions League table.

Noni Madueke scored the opener and Arsenal were cruising until the closing stages, when Wiktor Nowak pulled one back to leave Mikel Arteta’s men hanging on in stoppage time.

Gyokeres, whose only previous goal this season was in the Carabao Cup defeat at Fleetwood, was one of six changes Arteta made to the side which beat Liverpool 1-0 at the weekend.

Ben White and Piero Hincapie started in defence in the Czech capital with Martin Zubimendi and Mikel Merino slotting into midfield and Eberechi Eze a lively presence behind Gyokeres.

There was also a late run-out for youngster Max Dowman, who is suspended domestically but eligible for Europe following his red card at Fleetwood.

Madueke put them ahead in the ninth minute after Gyokeres muscled his marker off the ball on the halfway line and hooked the ball forward into space.

The England winger raced past the defence and slid his angled shot through the legs of Nazar Domchak.

The Slavia goalkeeper then had to come out and block from Gyokeres, tip Merino’s drive over the crossbar and make a fine reflex save from the Spain international.

Arsenal had a mild scare eight minutes before half-time when Tomas Holes got free in the area at a long throw and poked his shot wide.

But the Premier League champions immediately went up the other end and doubled their lead through Gyokeres.

The Sweden striker exchanged passes with Merino as he made his way into the area before opening up his body and clipping the ball across Domchak and in off the far post.

It was the 28-year-old’s first Champions League goal from open play since he scored against Kairat Almaty in January.

Arsenal were almost caught cold seconds after the break when Oskar Kubiak got forward and forced David Raya into his first save of the match.

Madueke almost had a second after more good play from Gyokeres, who held up Declan Rice’s pass before laying the ball off to Christos Tzolis.

Fit-again Tzolis fed Madueke out on the right and his fierce angled drive was blocked by the chest of Domchak.

Arsenal retreated for the remainder of the match and Gabriel had to clear a header from Slavia substitute Ange N’Guessan off the goal-line.

The VAR checked for a potential penalty after Merino knocked over Nowak as they challenged for the rebound, but it was deemed an accidental clash of heads.

In an unneccesarily nervous ending Raya had to make a full-stretch save to push Kubiak’s header against a post.

And the Gunners lost their clean sheet when Nowak converted David Jurasek’s cross for a deserved consolation for the hosts.
