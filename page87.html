How AIClaw 3.12.13 Loads Selected Skills Before the First Model Request

📅 Nov 20, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

A skill catalog is useful when the agent needs to discover a workflow. The timing matters when the user already picked one. If the instructions arrive through a tool call, the first model request still has to decide to make that call from the catalog and the user's message alone.

AIClaw v3.12.13 gives an explicit selection a different path. This behavior ships in the tagged release, and the source links below point to that version.

Imagine a mounted skill named release-check and a request like “$release-check summarize these changes.” The relevant instructions can shape the model's first response or tool choice. There is no prerequisite load_skill round trip for the excerpt. If the user just asks for a summary, the model can discover an applicable skill from the catalog and call load_skill as needed.

The kernel matches complete $skill-name references against mounted skills. It appends the selected text to the outgoing request in memory. The stored user message keeps its original text. A retry after context compaction can still carry the selection, and the next turn starts with fresh selection state. See the request assembly and skill prompt code.

The catalog uses an estimated budget of up to 2,000 tokens, reduced to 2% of the configured context window when that yields a smaller budget. AIClaw shortens descriptions to retain more names. If names alone exceed the budget, it can omit entries. An exact reference to a mounted skill still works even if the catalog leaves it out.

Selected text has a separate limit of 8 KiB per skill excerpt and 32 KiB across selected blocks. An excerpt shortened by the byte limit directs the model to load_skill for more detail. These are prompt assembly limits. The catalog uses a token estimate, and supplying instructions gives no guarantee that a model will follow them.

The kernel regressions inspect requests captured by a test model. They cover first-request inclusion, turn scoping, a compaction retry, catalog limits, and UTF-8 truncation. Those checks establish what the kernel sends. They do not measure live-model instruction following, latency, or provider token usage.

Humpback whale song's extra structure vanishes once its repeats are removed

📅 Nov 20, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Earlier this week I reported that the "language-like" Zipf law found in humpback whale song (Arnon et al. 2025, Science 387:649) is matched by a simple second-order Markov chain. Three more pre-registered tests on the authors' own data have now come back, and together they trace the song's extra structure to its source: repetition.

First, a held-out prediction test (does knowing more than the last two elements help predict unseen song?) finds structure in whale song that a second-order chain cannot reproduce. The Zipf statistic could not show this.

Second, the song repeats itself far more than that chain does. Collapsing every tandem repeat (a cell of 1 to 8 elements sung twice or more in a row) to one copy removes 63% of the whale's elements, against 50% for the chains. 35 of the 80 most frequent whale "words" contain an immediate repeat or an x-y-x alternation, such as gr(s)-dws(s) sq sq sq.

Third, the decisive one: with the repeats collapsed, the held-out test finds nothing beyond a second-order chain. In all 8 years, looking 3 to 5 elements back predicts unseen song worse than looking 2 back. The test still had power: it found English words in 5 of 5 samples of the same size. And song made of second-order chains with random repeats added did not fool it (0 of 3), which we had not expected.

The structure behind the whale's "language-like" chunks is phrase repetition, which whale biologists have described since Payne and McVay (1971), plus short-range rules between elements. Because random repeats do not reproduce the signal, it seems to lie in how the whale repeats. One guess, not yet tested: a chain that remembers two elements cannot count to three, so a whale that sings a cell a set number of times would look like longer-range structure. None of this says whether humpback song has meaning.

The graders were published before the jobs ran and re-derive every label, prediction and score from the raw output. Read RESULTS-HOLDC.md on GitHub and the working manuscript.

One whale data set coded by human listeners, each year treated as one recording; one English corpus in phonemes. "With power" means power against structure as strong as English words; weaker long-range order could be missed. The surrogate null leans against detection (every negative control gave p = 1.000), so p values are conservative, not exact. 4 of 7 registered predictions came true in the last test (Brier 0.189).

Disclosure: this work was carried out with AI agents working in my name, and no human expert has checked it yet.

22 Years Later, the Greatest Quote in TV Western History Is Still Untouchable

📅 Nov 20, 2026  |  Category: Business  |  Read Source →

Yellowstone may have pop culture in its grasp, but how soon fans forget about the best Western series that graced television. That honor, of course, goes to David Milch’s Deadwood, the gritty three-season masterpiece on HBO. Milch famously wanted to do a series about Ancient Rome, but when HBO’s Rome went into production, his hopes were dashed. Instead, he delivered the now cult classic series about the real town in the Old West, full of debauchery, crime, and gold prospecting.

If there was one thing that Deadwood was famous for, however, it was the dialogue. The Western has been compared to the likes of Shakespeare, though somehow even more swear-riddled. The controversial quotes have cemented the series into an unmissable classic, though there is one line of dialogue to rule them all.

In a series that is defined by its dialogue, there are many memorable quotes, but only one gets to the heart of the vicious world the series takes place in. Deadwood follows a former Marshal, Seth Bullock (Timothy Olyphant), who wants to start a hardware business with his business partner, Sol Starr (John Hawkes). Deadwood is the new land of opportunity, but Bullock is immediately blown back by the owner of the Gem Saloon, Al Swearengen (Ian McShane). Al swears by the idea that this world is cruel and he will respond in kind.

Deadwood is, of course, a lawless town full of murder, laudanum, and sex work, but all Bullock wants to do is run his small business. Despite his insistence on remaining impartial, his principles can’t help but continue to crop up. Bullock is characterized by these seemingly contradictory personality traits. As principled as he is, he is also about the angriest man in the gold mining town.

This puts him in the crosshairs of Al and all others looking to take advantage of the townspeople. Bullock resists his destiny for quite a long time before it is impossible to ignore any longer. Corruption is so rampant in the town that he has no choice but to take control.

“Fine, I’ll be the f***ing sheriff,” Bullock swears in a character-defining quote. His reluctance not only defines the character but also the complexities of the mining camp as a whole.

Good is very hard to come by down in these here parts. Deadwood becomes so rife with corruption that there is really only one person who can be entrusted to keep the peace. With the profanity that the series is known for and Bullock’s no-nonsense attitude, this quote distills Deadwood into the gold standard for Western television series.

Bullock accepting this new post was also the end and start of a new era. The lawlessness made Deadwood famous, but his acceptance of the post signaled that some civility would be coming to the town. Bullock sets the tone for Deadwood, which is a place in between being civilized and no man’s land. This is the sweet spot for one of the best HBO series, which has always played by its own rules. Seth Bullock began what the series was in earnest and created an iconic character that no one is likely to forget.

The Landing, Beloved Los Angeles Gallery with a Keen Eye for Pairing Historical and Contemporary Artists, Closes After 14 Years

📅 Nov 20, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

The Landing, a beloved Los Angeles gallery whose program was defined by its unique pairings of historical and contemporary artists, announced that it had closed. Its last exhibition was a one-night only solo for Maxwell Hendler staged on October 29.

On the gallery’s website, founder and owner Gerard O’Brien wrote, “the Landing Gallery at 5118 W Jefferson Blvd is now closed. We are no longer programming at this location. We are deeply grateful to the artists, collectors, collaborators, and community who have been fellow participants in our engagements. It has been a privilege to champion extraordinary artists, present ambitious exhibitions, and create a space for meaningful exchange and discovery.” Related Articles Two More Vienna Galleries Close, Citing 'No New Collectors' and 'Challenging' Financial Landscape Prairie, Chicago Gallery That Put Conceptualism Ahead of Commercialism, Closes After Nearly a Decade

In an Instagram post, Gerard O’Brien put it more simply, “End of an era … 22 years of Gallery spaces in Los Angeles, Reform Galley 2004-2019 … the Landing Gallery 2012 -2026 … onwards. thank you Los Angeles❣️”

O’Brien founded the Landing in 2012 as an off-shoot of his design-focused Reform Gallery. “I never really thought there was a big difference,” he said of the distinction between art and design. He chose the name for its original location, a spot within Reform: “he cleared out the landing atop a short staircase in his furniture gallery and dedicated it to shows of art, bringing in work by artists both contemporary and historical to display just above his design wares,” as a 2016 blog post by book publisher Phaidon reported.

In 2015, O’Brien decided that it was time for the Landing to grow, opening in a 2,800-square-foot gallery space on Jefferson Boulevard with a solo show for the late California artist JB Blunk.

Over the years, the Landing would become known for its pairings of historical and contemporary artists. At the time of its closing, its roster included four deceased artists (Jeremy Anderson, Richard Bowman, Leslie Kerr, and Fred Reichman) and five living contemporary artists (Yevgeniya Baras, Ryan Fenchel, Matt Phillips, Jonathan Ryan, and Amanda Valdez).

The gallery was also known for regularly staging off-kilter group exhibitions, with titles like “Rat Bastard Protective Association” (2016), “The Useful and the Decorative” (2017), “New Iconography: Artists Raising Children” (2021), and “Consensual Ennui (2023). The first of these, “3 Women” (2016) took its name from a 1977 Robert Altman film as a way “to create a cross-generational dialogue that explores the intersections of craft and fine art, influence and homage,” per a release. It featured the work of Lenore Tawney from the 1960s and ’70s paired with textile pieces by Tanya Aguiñiga and paintings by Loie Hollowell. The exhibition marked the first time Tawney’s work had been exhibited in LA in nearly 50 years.

In an Artforum review, Erin Kimmel wrote, “The Landing’s handsome, restrained exhibition showcased the exquisite detail of [Tawney’s] works, which reward prolonged scrutiny with seemingly boundless formal revelations.”

To mark its 10th anniversary at its Jefferson Boulevard space, the Landing mounted a two-part anniversary exhibition. The first part, which opened in November 2025, focused on contemporary art and design and featured the work of Ruth Asawa, Wallace Berman, Bruce Conner, Lyne Foulkes, and Doyle Lane. The second part, which opened this past April, featured gallery artists as well as others who had exhibited there, including Brenda Goodman, Sam Jablon, and Lukas Geronimas.

In a release for part one, O’Brien wrote, “10 Year brings together the feeling of the earliest days of the Landing Gallery when two years of exhibitions took place inside Reform Gallery on Melrose Avenue. Exhibiting art in a salon style in dialogue with decorative arts is how tL began.”

The Landing’s closure is the latest in an extended wave of closures that began in mid-2025. Los Angeles galleries have particularly been affected, with Murmurs, the Box, LA Louver, and Blum all shuttering, along with the LA outposts of Marian Goodman Gallery and Tanya Bonakdar.

In his statement on the gallery’s website, O’Brien added, “While our gallery doors have closed, our commitment to art and artists continues. Watch this space for news of future offsite exhibitions, activations, and special projects. Thank you for being part of the Landing’s ongoing narrative.”

Texans vs. Colts prediction: Houston looks to bounce back on 'TNF' vs. lowly Indy after rough Week 10 loss

📅 Nov 20, 2026  |  Category: Sports  |  Read Source →

If not listed, please contact your TV provider.

In the Week 11 edition of "Thursday Night Football," the Houston Texans play host to the division rival Indianapolis Colts.

Houston enters this game coming off a dispiriting loss to the Cleveland Browns that dropped the Texans to just 3-6 on the season and dealt a significant blow to their playoff hopes. The Texans need to start stacking wins, and they need to do it right now if they want to make a run at a back-end postseason spot.

The Colts, meanwhile, snapped a five-game losing streak with a close win over the Miami Dolphins last week. They didn't exactly light the world on fire, though, and have the look of a team that is just playing out the string after their series of trades ahead of the deadline.

Will the Texans bounce back, or will the Colts make it two in a row? We'll find out soon enough. Before we break down the matchup, here's a look at how you can watch the game.

The Texans defense has been one of the most disappointing units in the league this season. Houston ranks 20th in yards allowed, 18th in points allowed and 22nd in EPA per play, according to Tru Media. The Texans checked in first in yards, second in scoring and third in EPA per play a year ago, and were expected to again field one of the top defenses in the league this season after returning almost all of their key pieces.

Instead, Houston has struggled to contain opposing passing attacks, repeatedly leaking on the back end despite putting pressure on quarterbacks at an above-average rate. Players like Kamari Lassiter have regressed significantly, while Reed Blankenship has been a bust as a free-agent signing.

Luckily for the Texans, they are about to face one of the most inept passing offenses in the NFL. Daniel Jones has been entirely unable to drive the ball in intermediate and deep areas of the field since returning from his Achilles tear, and it has neutered the entire aerial attack. Jones has completed a career-low 59.6% of his passes at a career-low 5.6 yards per attempt, with just seven touchdowns against a league-high 12 interceptions.

The Colts also traded away both Josh Downs and Keenan Allen last week, leaving them with Laquon Treadwell, Ashton Dulin, Anthony Gould and Deion Burks as their main wide receivers. There is not an imposing player in the bunch. The Texans should be able to shut down this passing offense with relative ease -- especially if they can get pressure on Jones, who can no longer evade the rush like he did last season. He's already been sacked more times than he was a year ago despite playing three fewer games.

The Colts have turned the dial down on Jonathan Taylor's workload in recent weeks, having him split snaps and carries with rookie Seth McGowan, including in the red zone. Taylor played 81.2% of the offensive snaps through the Colts' first six games, according to Pro-Football Reference, but is at just 66.5% of the snaps since then, with McGowan's usage ticking up.

Taylor has also not been as effective this season (career-low 4.2 yards per carry) as in recent years, which isn't surprising given the total lack of a passing game and the Colts' offensive line issues. Those issues should persist on Thursday, with both of Indianapolis' starting tackles out for this game. It's fairly difficult to run on the Texans to begin with (4.0 yards per carry allowed), and it should be even more so with the Colts in their current state.

C.J. Stroud is playing the best ball of his career, including his rookie year. Stroud checks in sixth in the NFL in EPA per dropback, according to Tru Media, despite the fact that he's been working with an injured and rotating pass-catcher corps for much of the season.

The Colts are an easy bunch to throw on, checking in 27th in the league in net yards per attempt allowed (which accounts for sack yardage), and they won't have Charvarius Ward in this game and may not have Sauce Gardner, either. Nico Collins should see favorable matchups on the outside and be able to feast all night long. The likes of Tank Dell and Xavier Hutchinson could get in the mix as well, though they tend to rotate more heavily in and out of the lineup and therefore see fewer targets than Collins.

It's also quite easy to run on Indy, which has leaked 4.8 yards per carry on the season, 29th in the NFL. The Texans, though, have been entirely unable to run the ball, with neither David Montgomery (who is set to return from injury on Thursday night) nor Woody Marks (who is out with a concussion) able to establish anything resembling success on the ground. Montgomery was averaging a mere 2.7 yards per carry before his injury, and Marks has been at only 3.7 per carry in Monty's absence.

This is likely to be a game where the Texans lean heavily on Stroud to establish a lead, then turn to the ground game to run the clock out, in what should be a relatively easy matchup given the state of the Colts' roster. So long as the Texans keep Stroud well protected early on, that strategy should be plenty successful, even if it doesn't necessarily result in a blowout win because the Texans themselves aren't that explosive of an offense.

I just don't think the Texans should be favored by 12.5 points over anybody.

How Much Does It Cost To Earn United Premier 1K Status In 2026?

United Airlines' MileagePlus program launched on May 6, 1981, according to The Points Guy. The program, which celebrated its 45th anniversary this year, has undergone many iterations, particularly after the 2010 merger with Continental Airlines. According to NerdWallet, the program's most recent change took place on April 2, fine-tuning the mile-earning schedule for today's economy. Basic Economy officially no longer earns travelers miles, and it has reduced miles per dollar earned at each status level.

Premier 1K, United's top tier of earned status for passengers, grants a variety of benefits, amenities, and privileges onboard and in the airport. Premier 1K unlocks United's most comprehensive set of publicly available elite benefits, including priority pre-boarding, complimentary Premier Upgrades cleared as early as 96 hours before departure, three free checked bags, Economy Plus seating at booking for the member and up to eight companions, access to expanded award availability, a dedicated Premier phone line, and a bonus of 280 PlusPoints that can be used to request upgrades on eligible flights.

There are two main ways to achieve such status, including flying on United or United Express operated flights in a class other than Basic Economy, and with credit card spending with United's co-branded Chase credit card lineup. The exclusivity comes at a price, and not just anyone may qualify for Premier 1K, let alone United's entry level status, Premier Silver. For the 2026 qualification year, United Airlines illustrates on its website that earning Premier 1K status requires either 28,000 Premier Qualifying Points (PQP) or a combination of 22,000 PQP and 60 Premier Qualifying Flights (PQF).

PQPs are generally earned based on how much a traveler spends on United-operated flights, preferred seating, upgrades, and select partner airline tickets. The requirement makes Premier 1K one of the most expensive top-tier status levels among major US airlines.

A PQP measures the money you spend on airfare and eligible credit card purchases (typically earning 1 PQP per dollar spent on base fares). A PQF counts each individual flight segment you take on United, United Express, or eligible partner airlines. PQFs include layover segments. For instance, a round trip flight with a layover in each direction equals four PQFs earned.

The qualification requirements have steadily increased throughout the loyalty program's history. Following the merger with Continental Airlines and a broader airline industry trend toward revenue-based loyalty programs, United has increasingly prioritized spending over simply logging miles flown. Today, how much a passenger spends matters significantly more than how far they travel.

According to NerdWallet, one cannot simply earn Premier 1K status simply through spending on one of United's Chase cards. While a strong supplement in the pursuit of Premier 1K, it is not enough alone to suffice. United requires a four PQF minimum in order to qualify for any premier status, including the entry level Premier Silver.

While Premier 1K may traditionally be associated with road warriors flying hundreds of thousands of miles annually, United's current rules allow members to earn the vast majority of the required 28,000 PQP through credit card spending. The absolute maximum utilizing credit card spend requires 28,000 PQP, plus the four-flight minimum. Depending on the specific Chase card in use, one can earn 1 PQP per $20 spent or 1 PQP per $15 spent, translating to $560,000 or $420,000 in credit card spend annually.

In practical terms, a traveler pursuing status exclusively through credit card spending must supplement their spending with four PQF segments on United or an authorized partner airline. Business travelers whose employers pay for frequent premium cabin trips often reach the threshold naturally, whereas leisure travelers typically require a more deliberate strategy involving credit card spending and frequent travel.

This is certainly bad news for United MileagePlus members.

United's leadership has repeatedly emphasized that Premier 1K is intended to reward the airline's most loyal and highest-value customers. While MileagePlus has evolved significantly since its 1981 launch, the core philosophy remains centered around recognizing travelers who consistently choose United throughout the year. That philosophy is reflected in the program's modern qualification structure, where spending and engagement with the airline carry greater weight than mileage alone.

Jarad Fisher, President of United MileagePlus, joined United in February, having a proven track record as the Chief of Delta Air Lines' SkyMiles program, as well as being the creator of the Apple Card. The Points Guy has reported that Fisher describes Premier 1K earning in 2026 as majority PQP based, with United credit cards bridging the gap.

United continues moving toward revenue-based loyalty metrics. In October 2026, the carrier announced that Premier Qualifying Flights (PQF) would be removed from status qualification beginning in 2027, leaving Premier Qualifying Points (PQP) as the primary path to elite status. The change reinforces United's view that Premier status, particularly Premier 1K, should primarily reward customer value and spending rather than simply the number of flights taken.

Reaching Premier 1K is not solely about upgrades and priority boarding. United's broader loyalty ecosystem includes benefits such as PlusPoints, Million Miler status, and occasional status match opportunities from other airlines, all of which can influence whether pursuing 1K is worthwhile. For some travelers, these supplemental benefits may ultimately provide more value than the status title itself.

Possibly the most significant benefit is PlusPoints. Upon reaching Premier 1K, members receive a substantial allocation of upgrade credits that can be used on eligible United and partner-operated flights. Unlike complimentary upgrades, PlusPoints give travelers greater control over when and where they attempt to move into a premium cabin. However, upgrade inventory remains capacity controlled, meaning even top-tier members may not always secure their preferred seat.

Million Miler status adds another layer of value for long-term United customers. Unlike annual status qualifications, Million Miler recognition rewards cumulative lifetime flying with the airline. However, becoming a Million Miler is solely based on miles flown on a United Aircraft, not credit card spend. Overall, travelers considering a long-term loyalty strategy may therefore view Premier 1K as only one component of a broader relationship with United, especially compared with travelers who simply leverage status matches between competing carriers.

Why you now need a United credit card to get any real value out of MileagePlus.

Of course, airline loyalty is not solely dictated by its frequent flyer program, but a range of other factors such as geography, service quality, and price. However, for regular visitors to New York, Los Angeles, or even Chicago, when multiple airlines and airports are of choice, is there a better top tier airline status to compare?

At Delta, travelers earn Diamond Medallion status exclusively through Medallion Qualification Dollars (MQDs), with a threshold of $28,000 MQDs during the qualification year. The airline's program is almost entirely revenue-based, allowing members to earn progress through flights, Delta Vacations purchases, and eligible credit card activity. Meanwhile, American Airlines awards its highest published tier, Executive Platinum, after earning 200,000 Loyalty Points, which can be accumulated through flights, co-branded credit card spending, shopping portals, hotel partners, and numerous non-flying activities. As a result, many loyalty experts consider American's program the easiest top-tier status to earn because it offers the greatest number of pathways that do not require extensive flying.

A traveler based near a United hub may find Premier 1K considerably more useful than an equivalent status elsewhere due simply to flight frequency and network coverage. Meanwhile, a traveler primarily flying through Atlanta may derive greater value from Delta Diamond Medallion, while someone based in Dallas-Fort Worth International Airport (DFW) may prefer American Airlines Executive Platinum. In each case, the best loyalty program is often the one aligned with a passenger's most frequently used airport.

United Premier 1K remains one of the most prestigious airline status levels available in North America, but it is also one of the most difficult to achieve. Travelers must accumulate significant Premier Qualifying Points through flying, spending, or a combination of both while maintaining an active relationship with the airline throughout the qualification year. Premier 1K status brings travelers select privileges, such as checked baggage, United Club access, upgrades, and more.

MileagePlus's evolution illustrates a broader industry trend toward rewarding revenue contribution rather than simply distance flown. Benefits such as PlusPoints, priority services, expanded award availability, and upgrade opportunities can provide substantial value for those who travel regularly. Premier 1K status provides extra value for travelers based in or frequently visiting any of United's hubs, with the presence of native United Airlines staff, a United Club, and with mainline United service with upgrade ability.

Looking ahead, the trend toward revenue-based qualification appears unlikely to reverse. As airlines such as Delta, United, and American alike continue refining loyalty programs, travelers should expect qualification requirements and earning structures to evolve. As qualification thresholds continue to rise, Premier 1K increasingly represents not just frequent travel, but a substantial financial commitment to the airline and the broader MileagePlus ecosystem. A steady increase in the minimum threshold for Premier 1K has been defined by Thrifty Traveler as Pointsflation, keeping the bar high for frequent flyers. Only the most loyal United passengers have a shot at obtaining Premier 1K in today's earning environment.
