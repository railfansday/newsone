Mike Flanagan’s 10-Episode Netflix Thriller Masterpiece Is Everything ‘Carrie’ Should Have Been

📅 Nov 09, 2026  |  Category: Other  |  Read Source →

Carrie is missing something this show has in spades

Mike Flanagan knows how to make horror. He also knows how to make serialized TV. His most recent show is a mix of most of the things we’ve come to love about him: his bond with Stephen King, his skills as an adapter, his great eye for casting, and his ability to deliver pathos. But one key thing is missing: fear.

Carrie came out in October of this year, sparking much conversation, with many praising how it brought King’s ‘70s novel to 2026. One of its major criticisms, though, is that it’s not scary, especially for Flanagan, a guy who’s known for making some truly spooky stuff. Case in point: The Haunting of Hill House.

Released on Netflix in 2018, The Haunting of Hill House was Flanagan’s first TV show. Based on Shirley Jackson’s landmark novel of the same name, Flanagan takes her characters and reimagines them, at times scrapping their identities completely. While Jackson collected a group of strangers in a haunted house to explore their deepest traumas, Flanagan turned them into siblings, transforming Hill House into a story about their childhood home.

It’s an adaptational choice that many hardcore Jackson fans don’t forgive. Still, if Flanagan does one thing, it’s commit fully to the haunted house concept, taking the psychological fear that permeates the novel and literalizing it. Hill House is a true house of horrors.

Rather than leaning on jump scares, Hill House runs on dread, the type that has your eyes scanning through every inch of the screen so you’re not caught off guard by something that looks out of place. “If you go back and watch the series again, in every episode there’s probably somewhere between eight and 10 ghosts that are just hidden in plain sight that we called no attention to but they’re there,” Flanagan told Bloody Disgusting.

Flanagan also revealed that the ghosts had personal origins. The tall man with the bowler hat was added because one of the writers is afraid of tall people. “People on stilts freak her out,” he said. “It’s one of the most specific and weird phobias I’ve heard. We all thought it was so funny that we decided to make the tall man.”

And while Hill House delivers plenty of scary ghosts for texture and disquiet, some pull double duty, driving the story too. Flanagan splits Jackson’s Eleanor in two characters, giving her instability to the Crain siblings’ mother, Olivia (Carla Gugino), and her frailty and imagination to Nell (Victoria Pedretti). He turns the latter into the perfect example of a house cannibalizing its weakest. The series’ standout episode, “The Bent-Neck Lady,” relies on the twist that (spoiler alert) the ghost that has haunted Nell her entire life is herself, driven mad and killed by the house. Hill House’s biggest twist makes the horror intimate. Carrie’s twist does the opposite, removing the horror from her completely.

Warning: Spoilers follow for Carrie’s final episodes.

While Flanagan’s Carrie sticks closer to the book, keeping its mythology, sequence of events, and its characters' identities, it veers off in the one place it shouldn’t: the prom. We spend an entire season building up to one of the most famous moments in horror history, only for the series to unnecessarily complicate it. Instead of making the prom the place where Carrie breaks and enacts her revenge, it’s Emaline (Thalia Dudek), Carrie’s friend, who bears the majority of the blame, taking over Carrie’s body for most of the massacre.

In an interview with Polygon, Flanagan discussed the prom scene at length, clarifying what had happened. He shared that he and the writers wanted Carrie to be "100% solely responsible for" the deaths of key characters, innocent and guilty alike, including Tommy Ross (Joel Oulette), Billy Nolan (Arthur Conti) and Chris Hargensen (Alison Thornton).

They drew the line at the prom massacre. "None of us believed the Carrie White we created would consciously choose to kill Principal Grayle (Matthew Lillard), would choose to kill Ms. D (Amber Midthunder), and would choose to ignite innocent people around her,” he said.

Carrie has always been more than a story about a girl setting her prom on fire. It’s about the cruelty of high schoolers who use their weaker peers to feel better about themselves. That comes through in Flanagan’s show; as with Hill House, he has the ability to make us care deeply about his characters. But the prom is the moment where all that cruelty infects Carrie, where she transcends the page and becomes a horror icon. It’s there the show flinches. There’s no Bent-Neck Lady moment, just a massacre with no one clearly to blame. Is the show called Emaline?

The Haunting of Hill House is streaming on Netflix.

The Elder Scrolls: Return To Skyrim Arrives On November 9

📅 Nov 09, 2026  |  Category: World  |  Read Source →

One might argue that Skyrim is a tad overplayed in the world of The Elder Scrolls, if only because the fifth game in the series has been re-released ad nauseam, and the setting, as beautiful as it may be, has been so dutifully ingrained into our respective consciousness that one would be forgiven for mistaking it for one's second home. Still, Skyrim is as crucial to Tamriel as TES V is to Bethesda's bottom line, so it's understandable why it continues to crop back up every few years.

Additionally, now that we know the setting of The Elder Scrolls 6, it makes sense for Bethesda to make a return to Skyrim one more time, if only to help differentiate the series a little from what TES VI aims to offer. Fortunately, those who've missed Skyrim or simply wish to return there can do so in the brand-new The Elder Scrolls: Return to Skyrim event. This offers fans the opportunity to see the region like never before, unlock a whole host of goodies, and, one hopes, have a good time in the process.

The Elder Scrolls Online has received a major update centered around the region of Skyrim. Titled Echoes of Skyrim, it aims to bring the vast majority of content in the region to all ESO players. This already includes the Greymoor Chapter, Wolfhunter DLC, and Moon Hunter Keep and March of Sacrifices solo dungeons, but will also include the Markarth DLC, several Golden Pursuits campaigns, gameplay improvements, and a new PvP season at a later date. It's a huge amount of content that was previously locked behind a paywall and an obvious win for long-term fans and newcomers alike.

One of the aforementioned Golden Pursuits campaigns is Return to Skyrim. This event is running from November 9 to December 2, and, as the name suggests, will see players need to head to the region and complete a series of objectives. As with previous Golden Pursuits, these objectives will likely be tasks and activities you would have completed anyway in Skyrim, such as running dungeons and crafting items. Once you've completed the Golden Pursuit, you'll then be given a series of rewards.

The rewards for completing the Return to Skyrim Golden Pursuit in The Elder Scrolls Online will be a new pet, mount, and Antiquities leads. You can track the Golden Pursuit campaign in the Activity Finder, and it shouldn't take you too long to complete, especially if you're a seasoned player already. In addition to the Return to Skyrim Golden Pursuit, the Markarth DLC will also be given to all ESO players for free on November 9, making it a day you'll absolutely want to log in for.

It's great to see so much content being given to Elder Scrolls Online fans for free, especially surrounding Skyrim. With numerous events running alongside all the DLC and Chapters being handed out for free, players should be kept extremely busy while awaiting the upcoming Elder Scrolls 6. However, should you burn through all of this ESO content quickly, fear not, as even more expansions and updates are on their way, ensuring there will be absolutely no shortage of Elder Scrolls content to enjoy.

When Cupertino premiered last month, Stiller’s Christy Palladino was introduced as the assistant of Mike Colter’s Michael Price, an attorney and equity co-founder of S-Talk. After Michael was fired from the rising technology company, Christy met the same fate shortly thereafter for maintaining communication with him, forcing her to go back to work at TGI Fridays. However, after Michael and Rachel Keller’s Olivia Siffre, another freshly-fired S-Talk attorney, started their own law firm, Christy came aboard as its office manager.

Since then, Christy has held down the fort at Price and Siffre and become the firm’s informal investigator, as well as made first contact with their AI client, Bo. Cupertino’s newest episode, “Harper,” delivered its deepest look into Christy’s personal life yet. Her roommate Harper was initially denied a liver transplant due to what initially looked like an AI algorithm switching her place in the queue with an older, wealthy man. It was later revealed that it was a doctor who had actually gone against the judge’s order for the liver to go to Harper.

In an interview with ScreenRant’s Adam Holmes ahead of the airing of “Harper,” Stiller talked about getting into the mindset of playing Christy’s emotional scenes in this episode, which ended with Harper getting the liver just in the nick of time thanks to Christy, Michael, and Olivia’s coordinated efforts from different locations. The actor explained that it was important for her not to just draw from her professional training, but also envision what she would do if she was placed in the same kind of position with one of her friends:

It's interesting because you're right. It was the most emotional and the most I had gotten to do in terms of the high stakes for me thus far on the show. You just have to put yourself in the position. And for me, I draw from a lot of different methods of acting. In drama school, they say you build your little toolbox. It's living truthfully under imaginary circumstances, that's what I was taught.

Also, imagine it's your best friend in that position, and then you picture it, and that's helpful for me, always connecting things to my real life and connecting to people I love. I also feel like by that point, I was so invested in Christy. I was from the beginning, but by Episode 6, I believe I think this girl is really special.

Stiller also highlighted the guest stars in “Harper” as another reason why she enjoyed working on this Cupertino episode, as she admired several of them before the cameras started rolling. In her words:

I think that Christy has a real way with people and a real sense of being in touch with her feelings and knowing when to put that aside and when to play into it. It was also really easy with these incredible actors that I got to work with on that episode. Aaron Serotsky, who played the doctor, I was a huge fan of his before like I saw him from Marcel on the Train. When I saw his name on the call sheet, I was like, ‘Oh, this is huge for me!’

These theater actors, Vicky Clark and Katrina Lane. It was so special, and they were all in it, and we just had this little separate world in that hospital for a few days. We also shot all my stuff for it in two days, actually. So it was intense because I think that made the emotion easier, too. Because we're just buckled up for the ride, and it's all happening and feeling all of it and the running.

The legal drama has quickly become one of fall 2026’s biggest TV hits, with the show boasting a perfect 100% Tomatometer on Rotten Tomatoes, pulling from 30 reviews. Along with Colter and Keller, Stiller’s co-stars include Nik Domain as Vic Malhotra, Busy Philipps as Natalia Boutella, and Renée Elise Goldsberry as Nichole Ferebee.

10 Strongest X-Men Teams in Marvel History, Ranked

📅 Nov 09, 2026  |  Category: World  |  Read Source →

The X-Men are one of the most iconic superhero teams in comic book history. Over the years, the team has had different versions with new, diverse lineups. Individual teams usually have seven to ten members, but many X-Men have joined over time.

Many heroes and villains have joined the team, with new X-Men regularly appearing and becoming fan favourites. The X-Men evolve and adapt to face different challenges, constantly changing their roster to suit the task at hand. However, not all X-Men teams are created equal, and some are noticeably stronger than others.

This article ranks 10 of the strongest X-Men teams from six decades of history. The X-Men have no shortage of powerful members, and some of these characters appear on multiple teams while consistently serving as heavy hitters. Nevertheless, certain line-ups bring together a unique mix of top-tier mutants that sets them apart from the rest.

Professor Xavier initially enrolled five students into the Xavier School for Gifted Youngsters. Under the guidance of arguably the franchise’s most powerful telepath, Professor Xavier, each member was a formidable mutant with incredible potential. The class, composed of Scott Summers, Hank McCoy, Bobby Drake, Jean Grey and Warren Worthington III, would become the very foundation of the X-Men.

Although the original five were inherently formidable mutants, their full potential remained dormant early on. They were still learning to master their powers, with Jean and Bobby’s abilities actively developing. The team grew stronger after Cyclops’ brother, Havok, joined alongside Polaris; however, the series was cancelled before this squad could truly reach its peak.

Events such as the Mutant Massacre and Inferno dealt a heavy blow to the X-Men, fracturing the team. In the Fall of the Mutants, the X-Men were killed and resurrected, inadvertently settling as the Outback for a time.

After faking their deaths in the Fall of Mutants event, the X-Men set up a base in an abandoned subterranean town in the Australian desert. The Outback team consisted of Storm, Havok, Colossus, Wolverine, Rogue, Psylocke, Longshot, and Dazzler, who operated in absolute secrecy and launched covert strikes against global threats.

This team is criminally underrated, with some of the strongest X-Men, including Storm and Colossus. Although the team never handled large-scale threats, it wasn’t for lack of firepower. The Outback team was a force to be reckoned with, no matter how you slice it.

Len Wein and Dave Cockrum’s Giant-Size X-Men #1 introduced a team that changed the course of X-Men comics. Commonly known as the Chris Claremont/John Byrne team, it went on to tell some of the most iconic stories in X-Men history and introduced fans to characters who would become fan favourites and staples of pop culture.

The team introduced fans to classic characters who became powerful additions to the X-Men. Nightcrawler, Banshee, Thunderbird, and Wolverine are all devastating in combat, while characters like Storm, Colossus, and Sunfire are powerhouses who instantly made the X-Men a force of nature.

Storm is an Omega-level mutant and a master of the elements, while Sunfire is essentially the X-Men’s version of the Human Torch, able to create similarly intense flames. These new X-Men, along with the original seven X-Men, teamed up to fight on Krakoa and became one of the X-Men’s largest and most powerful rosters to date.

The Dark Phoenix Saga may be one of the greatest and most iconic X-Men events, but the Phoenix Saga that led up to it is not only a great storyline; it also features the X-Men at their strongest yet. This team included classic members such as Cyclops, Storm, Nightcrawler, Banshee, Colossus, Wolverine, Beast, and the powerful Phoenix.

With the help of the Phoenix Force, Jean was at her strongest ever, showing greater mastery and power than ever before. She was strong enough to go toe-to-toe with Galactus’s Herald, Firelord, and save the universe by containing the M’Kraan Crystal’s power, the frozen birth of a neutron galaxy.

The Age of Apocalypse team fields one of the strongest X-Men teams in Marvel history, largely because of its size. While the individual members are powerful, the X-Men team was effectively a rebellion, uniting mutants behind a single cause against Apocalypse’s genocidal rule.

Magneto led a strike force consisting of Weapon X (Wolverine), Jean Grey, Colossus, Rogue, Iceman, Storm, Gambit, Scarlet Witch, and Quicksilver. Most notably, Quicksilver and his twin sister were powerful additions to the team. While not at their full potential, Quicksilver is a powerful speedster, and Scarlet Witch can manipulate probability to bring ruin to everything around her.

Phoenix Resurrection saw Jean Grey return to life, only to find a world still rife with fear and hatred for mutants. Jean was at the peak of her abilities when not possessed by the Phoenix Force, and she set out to gather a team of mutants to build a new mutant nation.

X-Men Red included a few classic members, such as Nightcrawler and Gambit, along with heavy hitters like Namor and Gentle. Wolverine (X-23) and Honey Badger ensured the team was never short on lethality, while the new mutant Trinity further cements Jean’s team as one of the strongest in history with her ability to control technology.

Grant Morrison and Frank Quitely revamped the X-Men after Claremont’s Revolution event amounted to a failure. The new team, sporting leather, immediately set itself apart from previous iterations. The team featured an impressive line-up of powerful mutants, giving fans one of the most stacked X-Men teams yet.

Alongside classics such as Beast, Cyclops, Wolverine, Jean Grey, Emma Frost, and Professor X, the team also introduced a newcomer, Xorn, who drastically increased the team’s power level. This team boasted 3 of the strongest telepaths in Marvel history, whose combined might could control every mind on the planet and topple entire armies in seconds.

Nonetheless, the real standout is Xorn, who brings with him an array of powerful abilities. He controls gravity and magnetism, among other things. Combined with Cyclops, whose powerful optic blasts can level mountains, and Wolverine’s fierce lethality, this team is no joke.

In recent years, X-Men fans have seen one of the strongest X-Men teams during the From the Ashes era. These days, X-Men often split into separate squads, each led by a competent leader, because of the sheer number of mutant heroes scattered throughout the Marvel Universe.

After the fall of Krakoa, From the Ashes had Cyclops assemble a team of powerful mutants, including Magik, Psylocke, Kid Omega, and a newly reformed Juggernaut, alongside powerhouses like Magneto, Temper, Xorn, and Blob. The group has incredible potential, with each member a top-tier mutant who can cause devastating damage on the battlefield on their own.

Though Magneto has lost his powers, that does not make this team any less dangerous. Psylocke is a formidable combatant and telepath, while Temper can alter entire landscapes by redirecting temperature, creating intense flames and crippling cold in the form of ice. Kid Omega is Marvel’s fourth most powerful telepath, making this lineup one of the most dangerous in history.

Chris Claremont and Jim Lee are responsible for one of the most iconic eras in X-Men history that came in the 90s. After the “Muir Island Saga”, the X-Men and X-Factor came together and split into two earth-shattering strike forces known as the Blue and Gold teams.

The Blue team, led by Cyclops, featured Wolverine, Gambit, Rogue, Beast, and Psylocke. However, the more impressive showing was Jean Grey’s team, which comprised herself, Storm, Colossus, Archangel, and Iceman. This five-person team has enough power to level cities in seconds.

Furthermore, not only does the Gold team have Jean Grey, one of, if not the most powerful X-Men around, with world-class telekinesis, Omega-level telepathy and Phoenix Force powers, but it also has two other Omega-level mutants, Iceman and Storm. The team also has Colossus, one of the physically strongest mutants in the X-Men.

The Extinction team is the most powerful in X-Men history, and for good reason. The “Utopia” era saw the mutant race on the brink of extinction, with most mutants gathered on the island refuge of Utopia, under Cyclops’ leadership. There, Cyclops assembled his “Extinction team,” bringing together the most powerful mutants to protect Utopia.

Led by Cyclops, the team included Emma Frost, Magneto, Storm, Namor, Colossus, Magik, Danger, and Hope Summers. At this point, Colossus had Juggernaut’s powers, giving him unstoppable strength on top of his already powerful body. Namor is no slouch either, as he is strong enough to trade blows with the likes of Thor and Hulk.

Danger, the living Danger Room, is also a powerhouse capable of creating hard-light holograms of anything she can imagine. She’s not only extremely versatile, but also strong enough to lift over 100 tons, making her one of the X-Men’s more powerful members.

What Are The Best Seats To Book On A Widebody Long-Haul Flight?

📅 Nov 09, 2026  |  Category: Business  |  Read Source →

There is no single answer for where you should try to sit on a long-haul widebody flight because it all depends on what sort of traveler you are and what you want to prioritize. The best seat depends on whether you care most about sleep, legroom, getting off quickly, or relaxing. Whatever you prioritize, you'll have to trade off other aspects, such as convenient access to the toilet or the best window views.

Undoubtedly, the best place to sit in a widebody flight is in first or business class. Economy class varies a lot depending on where you sit, so this article focuses on that.

The first step in choosing the right seat for your economy class widebody flight is understanding how the cabin works. This is because an economy class cabin is so large on a widebody aircraft that it acts more like a set of mini-cabins, which are broken up by exit rows, galleys, toilets, and more. Some superjumbos also have an economy class enclave on the upper deck. Each mini-section of the economy class cabin is suited to different passenger archetypes.

The table below shows what you can expect in each part of the economy class cabin, making use of insights from Seat Compare:

Proximity to the galley and toilets. You may get meal service last

Stairs that can lead to separate boarding, and it takes longer to get off the aircraft.

In modern aviation, most airlines now expect you to pay extra to choose a specific seat. Some of the most in-demand seats, such as the exit row, cost more, though still well below premium economy or business class. As a result, an additional option opens up, particularly for solo travelers. That is to refuse to pay for seat selection and gamble that you won't get put right next to the toilet.

The most well-known path to extra legroom in economy class is to book a seat in the exit row. The extra few inches of legroom makes the seat feel closer to premium economy. It is ideal for taller passengers and often means you won't need to get up to let fellow passengers out.

Please note that these seats come with responsibility in the event of an accident. Each airline has a different minimum age to sit here, usually between 15 and 18 years old, and you also need to be physically able to operate the emergency exit.

This isn't the only reason that the exit row can be a poisoned chalice. First, aviation rules mean you can't keep any bags under the seat in front of you, as these could cause an obstruction if the aircraft must be evacuated. You'll need to be organized if you want a specific device, book, or magazine during takeoff and landing. Exit-row seats can also frustrate some passengers. These seats are fixed, often with no recline and armrests that can't be moved. The armrests also make the seat a little narrower than normal. Robustly built passengers may find this cramped. The tray tables are built into the armrest rather than on the back of the seat, which can make for an odd eating experience. For some passengers, these downsides will be irrelevant, but for others, they make exit row seats hellish.

Finding the best seat in the house can be tricky.

A poor night's sleep on a red-eye long-haul flight can steal a day from your hard-earned vacation time, making it a priority for many economy class passengers. Yet, given the inherent discomfort and crowding of economy class cabins, this can be impossible. Some seats within the class will still give you the best chance of sleep.

The first step is to secure a window seat. You'll have a wall to lean on or wedge a pillow or sweater against for additional comfort. It also means you won't be woken up when the other passengers in your row want to use the bathroom. The window seat allows you to control the blinds, though an eye mask is always a good idea.

The best spots are toward the front of the economy class cabin, in front of the wings due to the engine noise, although not far forward enough to be disturbed by cabin crew clattering around in the galley. This spot, near the plane's center of gravity, also helps you avoid turbulence that can jolt you awake. Another great spot is a mini-cabin, a smaller economy class enclave formed by galleys, exit rows, and other objects that divide the cabin. These mini-cabins have fewer people, so they have less activity that could disturb your sleep.

Another popular seat that is available throughout the aircraft is the aisle seat. It has numerous benefits. Most obviously, it is more spacious than the average economy class seat, as you can enjoy some of the room in the aisle when passengers and cabin crew aren't coming past. That gives the impression of more width than the basic amount that the seat offers.

You'll find deplaning much easier, as you won't have to wait for the passenger next to you to finish before you can move. That makes the seat ideal if you have a connecting flight or need to get to your accommodation quickly. The seat also lets you access the bathroom easily, although you will also have to move whenever the other passengers in your row need to get up. Likewise, you can easily access the overhead bins, but will be disturbed when the passengers next to you need their items.

Yet, the aisle seat usually isn't ideal for passengers who want to be undisturbed or enjoy a good night's sleep. Due to the size of long-haul economy class cabins, there will constantly be other passengers and flight attendants traveling down the aisle. They could bump into you and wake you from your slumber.

Which seat should you choose for your next long-haul flight?

No seat combines everything you want to avoid on long-haul widebody flights like the final row of economy class. As you board, you may struggle to find space for your bags as the overhead bins may be partially filled with airline emergency equipment. During the flight, you'll often have no or limited recline because the seat is directly against a wall. You'll also often be close to noises and smells from the lavatory and galley. If there is a queue for the lavatory, it will always be directly next to you.

It is also common for meal services to work from the front to the back of the aircraft. Along with the wait, the best options may already be taken by the time the trolley gets to you. Finally, the rear of the aircraft is set back from its center of gravity, so you'll get the worst turbulence. The problems continue when it's time to get off the aircraft. You will often be one of the last people off the aircraft. That can cause problems if you have a tight connection. You also won't be able to get ahead of the rush to deal with immigration controls, which can cause even more delays.

The middle seat anywhere on the aircraft is also a must-avoid. You'll feel cramped between two other passengers and have no window view. This seat can become a disaster if the passengers on either side of you are large individuals. As with the window seat, you'll have to disturb the passenger next to you whenever you want to get up.
