Stargate fans have been on a roller-coaster ride over the past year or so, first learning that the highly anticipated Amazon MGM revival from Martin Gero had been cancelled, only to find out just months later that an entirely new take on the franchise was in development from WandaVision creator Jac Schaeffer. As you might expect, the fanbase is tempering their expectations for the franchise's on-screen future, despite being as eager as ever to see how this new project takes shape.

While we still don't know much about the new series' story or placement in the timeline, fans won't have to rely on a live-action adaptation for their next trip through the gate. BlueBrixx is bringing the beloved Stargate SG-1 back in an entirely different form, with officially licensed building sets that let fans recreate some of its most recognizable elements.

While fans wait patiently for concrete news about Stargate's television future, the franchise is experiencing something of a renaissance in the merchandise/collectible department. BlueBrixx, which is a LEGO-style alternative, has been steadily expanding its officially licensed Stargate collection, and one of its newest additions is bringing back one of the most iconic alien species from Stargate SG-1 in an entirely new kit.

The Goa'uld Symbiont set recreates the infamous snake-like parasites responsible for some of the franchise's weirdest and best encounters. Throughout SG-1, the Goa'uld were one of humanity's most formidable enemies, taking control of human hosts and masquerading as gods while building an empire spanning countless worlds.

BlueBrixx's new 400-piece set recreates the creature in what the company describes as a "raised and threatening pose," complete with its segmented body, distinctive red eyes, and terrifying open-mouth fangs. The finished model measures roughly 9.5 inches tall, features a completely sticker-free design, and rests on a sand-colored display base.

The Goa'uld Symbiont is currently listed for €19.95 (~$23), although an official release date has not been announced. BlueBrixx is accepting email signups to notify fans when the set becomes available.

Why factory settings aren’t enough to keep your personal data safe

Securing your digital life can feel overwhelming, but the most effective protections are the ones you can activate in just a few minutes. Your iPhone comes equipped with built-in features engineered to safeguard your identity, shield your finances, and keep your personal information private.

You do not need technical expertise to take advantage of them either, you just need to know where to look. Here are five essential settings that will help protect you and your iPhone from day-to-day threats.

If a thief memorizes your passcode before snatching your phone, that six-digit number used to be enough to reset your master Apple account password and lock you out permanently. Stolen Device Protection eliminates this flaw.

When enabled, your iPhone demands Face ID or Touch ID authenticationfor sensitive actions whenever you are away from familiar locations like home or work. Crucially, it imposes a mandatory one-hour delay before anyone can alter account credentials outside those trusted zones, giving you time to mark the hardware lost via iCloud.

To enable it, go to Settings, tap Face ID & Passcode, and turn on Stolen Device Protection.

Scammers frequently rely on high-pressure calls and spoofed texts to trick you into wiring funds or surrendering credentials. Introduced in iOS 27, Impersonation Risk Detection serves as Apple’s built-in defense against this social engineering.

The system monitors on-device interaction patterns and behavioral cues during high-stakes actions without reading your personal messages, emails, or photos. If you attempt a large transfer or sensitive account change under signs of coercion, your iPhone flags the event and directs supported banking apps to require secondary verification before funds can leave your account.

To activate it, open Settings, tap Privacy & Security, scroll down and select Impersonation Risk Detection, then toggle it on.

Most financial fraud starts with an unsolicited call from someone posing as a bank representative, courier, or tax official. Silence Unknown Callers stops these intrusions before your phone even rings.

To set this up, open Settings, Apps, tap Phone, and check Silence Unknown Callers. Then go to Settings, tap Messages, and toggle on Filter Unknown Senders.

Standard location tracking fails if a thief turns your phone off or the battery drains completely. Expanding your configuration to include the entire Find My network solves this problem.

To turn both on, open Settings, tap your name at the top, select Find My, tap Find My iPhone, and toggle on both Find My network and Send Last Location.

This lets nearby Apple devices locate your iPhone even when it is powered down or offline, while Send Last Location sends one final location ping right before the battery dies.

Leaving your lock screen on its default settings makes it easy for anyone nearby to see private texts, calendar alerts, and one-time bank codes. Tightening what shows up and what works while your phone is locked keeps your personal business to yourself.

To hide your alerts, open Settings, tap Notifications, select Show Previews, and choose When Unlocked.

Next, head to Face ID & Passcode, scroll down to Allow Access When Locked, and switch off Control Center, Wallet, and USB Accessories.

Is XRP the Best Cryptocurrency You Can Buy Right Now?

📅 Nov 11, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

XRP's price may be lower than before, but its upside bids are starting to look improbable.

With its price down by 70% from its all-time high set in mid-2025, it's natural for investors to wonder if XRP (XRP -4.33%) is priced at a bargain, and perhaps even one so deep as to make it the best cryptocurrency that it's possible to buy. But price isn't the factor that determines whether an asset is a bargain; the fundamentals are the more important part.

On that front, Ripple, XRP's issuer, has spent a handful of years and considerable effort courting banks and other financial institutions around the globe. Ripple has also worked extensively on expanding the capabilities of XRP's blockchain, the XRP Ledger (XRPL). Has any of that work actually made XRP a more appealing coin to buy?

Financial institutions are evaluating blockchain technology to manage tokenized assets.

Tokenization is the process of recording the ownership data of an asset, such as a Treasury bill or a stock, on a crypto token that's held and exchanged on a blockchain. The XRP Ledger was home to $642.9 million in tradeable tokenized assets as of Nov. 9, making it the ninth-largest among blockchain networks. Ethereum, the destination for the biggest collection of those assets, hosted $15.1 billion, so the XRPL is very far from being a leader despite developing its own tech explicitly to make its tokenized asset features more appealing to banks.

What's more, just $22.5 million worth of tokenized assets changed hands on the network in the 30 days ending Nov. 9. And that volume was incredibly lumpy, too; in the last week of October, only $4,620 in tokenized assets were traded. That's a strong sign that there isn't really an active ecosystem of tokenized assets, as the overwhelming majority of the tradeable assets on the chain are sitting inert.

The data on another potential growth area for XRP, AI agents, largely tell the same story. The XRPL's AI Hub recorded a cumulative total of $58,683 in payments performed autonomously by AI agents on the ledger as of early November. Other networks saw $42.6 million in cumulative x402 stablecoin settlements as of Nov. 9.

In other words, in crypto's two hottest growth areas, XRP is a niche player at best.

Last year, Ripple made a spate of acquisitions to build out its capabilities in crypto custody, trade settlement, and liquidity provision for institutional players. But that's not something likely to make XRP a better buy at this juncture.

In theory, if institutions actually respond to those efforts by working with Ripple, onboarding their capital to the XRPL, and tapping its various services, it probably still wouldn't boost XRP's price much, since XRP isn't even needed to use most of what Ripple offers. And even if Ripple's services did use XRP in some way, its transaction fees are so low that the return to holders wouldn't be meaningful.

Thus, XRP is probably not the best cryptocurrency to buy right now. If the XRPL's performance metrics change, it still might not be enough to change that.

Does Pickford finally face serious competition for his England place?

📅 Nov 11, 2026  |  Category: World  |  Read Source →

England head coach Thomas Tuchel used James Trafford (left) and long-time first choice Jordan Pickford (right) in the previous international break

When England head coach Thomas Tuchel decided to pick Leeds United goalkeeper James Trafford to face World Cup holders Spain at Wembley in the last international break, it sparked the first stirrings about preparations for life without long-time first choice Jordan Pickford.

Tuchel made it clear Pickford was still England's number one pick, but the simple fact he decided to alternate keepers - playing Trafford in the 3-2 loss to Spain then victory against Czech Republic, before Everton's 32-year-old returned for the Wembley win against the Czechs and the 7-0 rout in Croatia - created intrigue and debate.

England meet Croatia at Wembley on Thursday before facing the biggest test of their Uefa Nations League campaign when the play Spain in Madrid on Sunday.

Tuchel maintains strong faith in Pickford, an outstanding performer for England, but Trafford has at least had the chance to stake his claim.

Tuchel has suggested he may use a rotation system for the pair once more in the upcoming internationals, which will only increase scrutiny on England's goalkeeping position.

Pickford's record is outstanding since he made his debut in a 0-0 draw against Germany at Wembley in 2017.

In his time as first choice, England have once more emerged as major players on global stage, reaching the World Cup semi-finals in 2018 and 2026, as well as successive Euros finals at the delayed 2020 tournament and Euro 2024.

Measured by Opta stats, Pickford has made only two errors leading to goals in 93 appearances, with 46 clean sheets and a 74% save ratio.

Trafford's international career is in its infancy and, while former England goalkeeper Paul Robinson says Pickford remains Tuchel's undisputed first choice, he believes the head coach's recent strategy is a sensible one.

Robinson told BBC Sport: "It obviously remains to be seen what Thomas Tuchel does in these two England games, but I could see exactly what he was doing by giving James Trafford game time in the last round of matches.

"We are so guilty as a nation of taking one goalkeeper to a tournament who has 70, 80, 90 caps, then the two of three below him could have less than double figures combined."

Robinson's point is proved by the statistic that 80 of Pickford's appearances have been competitive games, with 33 at major tournaments, made up of 19 at the World Cup and 14 at European Championships.

"Competitive and tournament caps give you the type of experience you can't get as a number two or three keeper," said Robinson.

"James Trafford is only 24 and has four caps - two of them competitive - so you can see exactly what Tuchel is doing, looking ahead to a situation where England's recognised number one is not available to him, or there is a drop off in form.

"We saw the last selections and all thought Jordan Pickford's place is under threat and James Trafford is going to be the next number one. I don't think Thomas Tuchel saw it as starkly or simply as that.

"It's a conversation that will definitely take place down the road, but not now. Jordan is England's first choice, in my opinion, rightfully so given his consistency and performances over such a long period.

"This is a debate and conversation for the future definitely because Father Time catches up with everyone eventually and the manager needs to have a contingency plan, but Jordan Pickford very much remains number one and Thomas Tuchel has said as much.

"I don't think Jordan Pickford is under threat for his jersey on a permanent basis any time soon, irrespective of the next selection, but what Tuchel is doing reinforces the impression that he has to be at the top of his game and maintain high standards, which he certainly has been doing when I've watched him this season.

"The biggest compliment I can pay Jordan Pickford is that when you have got that England goalkeeper's shirt, everybody thinks somebody else should be doing it. I know that from personal experience.

"You're there to be shot at, but there has been no noise around Jordan Pickford for a very long time now.

"It has worked both ways for Jordan, James and Thomas Tuchel, and worked very well. Jordan knows he can't rest on his laurels and he will just play regardless. He has to be playing well.

"Is James Trafford challenging Jordan Pickford right now? Yes he is - if Jordan Pickford isn't playing very well and Jordan Pickford is making errors.

"None of the above are true right now because he's playing well for Everton and has done nothing wrong for England."

Robinson also drew on club form to support Pickford's case. Since the start of the 2024-25 season, only Arsenal's title-winning keeper David Raya has more clean sheets in the league than Pickford at Everton.

Raya has 39 clean sheets in that time, while Pickford has 27, with Robinson saying: "That's a superb record and all logic tells you Pickford will have to work a lot harder for his clean sheets than David Raya."

Trafford staked his claim in those Nations League games against Spain and the Czech Republic, putting him at the forefront of Tuchel's thoughts as the main understudy to Pickford.

Crystal Palace's Dean Henderson is now back in contention after injury and hoping to add to his six caps, although he has not been included in Tuchel's latest squad.

TIER IV and the Technical University of Munich (TUM) announced on November 11, 2026 that their Autoware research center will support development of an autonomous-driving system for a full-size city bus in Munich’s RISE project, which has up to €20 million in German federal funding.

Through the center, TUM will draw on experience from Autoware-based autonomous bus deployments in Japan to adapt the open-source software stack to a new vehicle platform and Munich’s urban operating environment. The announcement concerns development work rather than an already-operational driverless bus: real-world operations on MVG service routes remain planned and will have safety personnel on board.

TUM is providing the sensor setup and developing the Autoware-based autonomous-driving system. MAN Truck & Bus is equipping the bus with autonomous-driving actuation and low-level software, placing responsibility for the vehicle’s underlying control systems with the manufacturer.

The bus work forms part of RISE, a TUM-led consortium project intended to lay the scientific, technical, organizational and regulatory groundwork for autonomous buses and shuttles to enter regular public-transport operation in the long run. The project also covers control centers, operational processes and digital infrastructure alongside vehicles, extending its scope beyond the driving software to the wider transit operating environment.

Germany’s Federal Ministry of Transport approved the funding. TUM’s November 9, 2026 announcement of the opening of the German Center for Future Mobility’s Munich project site sets the RISE funding period from October 1, 2026 through September 30, 2029.

Testing is planned to begin with controlled trials at a closed site in Ottobrunn, followed by real-world laboratory operations on two MVG routes. That sequence will move the project from a controlled environment into the operating conditions where its vehicle technology and transit processes can be examined together.

Beyond TUM and MAN, RISE’s core partners include the City of Munich, SWM, MVG, the Munich Transport and Fare Association, the University of the German Armed Forces in Munich, Ludwig Maximilian University of Munich and UnternehmerTUM. The consortium connects research and vehicle development with municipal and public-transport organizations involved in preparing autonomous mobility for routine use.

The bus initiative is an early application of the TUM Autoware Research Center by TIER IV, established through a multi-year strategic research collaboration announced on October 15, 2026. Supported by strategic research funding from TIER IV, the center brings together TUM’s Chair of Automotive Technology and its professorships for Cyber-Physical Systems and Autonomous Vehicle Systems. Its agenda covers safety, evaluation and scalability, including combining learning-based driving models with rule-based supervision and developing tests across simulation, offline analysis and real-world operation.

“The research center was established to help TUM connect open research with real-world deployment,” said Shinpei Kato, founder and CEO of TIER IV.

Findings from the bus system’s integration and real-world testing are intended to inform Autoware and related evaluation methods, with outcomes contributed to the open-source ecosystem where appropriate. The partners aim to establish a repeatable path for adapting Autoware to European vehicle platforms and practical mobility applications.

The central question is whether the center can turn experience from Japanese bus deployments into repeatable methods for European vehicle platforms, while the planned Munich trials examine how Autoware fits into supervised public-transport operations.

📅 Nov 11, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

On November 11, 2026, The Hong Kong University of Science and Technology (HKUST) announced ASD Super Agent, an AI platform for autism screening and family support, with a preliminary area under the curve score of 0.92, a measure of discriminative precision. Further clinical studies and real-world validation are planned before deployment.

The platform combines early risk screening, personalized intervention recommendations and continuous follow-up for parents and caregivers. HKUST describes it as Asia’s first integrated AI platform for autism support.

HKUST says autism spectrum disorder affects approximately one in 100 children worldwide, while lengthy assessment waits, high treatment costs and fragmented intervention information can prevent families from obtaining timely support. The platform is intended to assist families from initial risk assessment through home-based training recommendations and longer-term follow-up.

Kani Chen, a professor in HKUST’s Department of Mathematics and Department of Industrial Engineering and Decision Analytics, leads the project. During development, the team visited special education institutions, child development centers and psychiatric hospitals in Hong Kong, the Greater Bay Area and Beijing, and engaged with nearly 500 parents of children with autism. The researchers used that work to shape a practical support tool around the difficulties families reported.

Parents can record short videos of a child’s everyday activities with a smartphone or another smart device, complete standardized questionnaires and provide basic developmental information. ASD Super Agent uses those inputs to generate a risk assessment and analytical report. The report is intended as a reference when families seek professional evaluation, not as a replacement for that evaluation.

The privacy design follows a data-minimization approach. It prioritizes extracting posture features on the user’s device and processes only information described as essential for analysis, an important consideration because the screening inputs include home videos and children’s developmental information.

Parents can describe challenges including emotional regulation difficulties, limited eye contact, communication difficulties or repetitive behaviors and receive customized support suggestions. Recommendations can include academic literature and background references to inform discussions with healthcare or educational professionals.

The platform’s 20+1 framework incorporates more than 20 autism intervention models and allows therapists, special education providers and organizations to add their own methods and practical experience to its knowledge base. Named approaches include Applied Behaviour Analysis, Naturalistic Developmental Behavioural Interventions, TEACCH Structured Teaching and the Localized BSR Model.

This structure combines research-based methodologies with frontline professional experience. It also gives professionals a route to contribute their own intervention knowledge rather than limiting the system to its initial set of models.

For continuing support, the system combines text records, video assessments and developmental information supplied by families and professionals into a dynamic developmental profile for each child. It can divide long-term goals into manageable daily actions, track progress over time and support discussions between families and professionals. Voice interactions are currently supported in Cantonese, Putonghua and English.

The team has also built a multimodal autism database containing more than 1.1 million video, posture and behavioral records. HKUST says the database provides a foundation for AI model development and autism research, with continued data collection and analysis intended to improve risk-assessment accuracy, refine intervention recommendations and deepen understanding of autism development and support strategies.

The researchers plan to refine the user experience, expand clinical validation studies and strengthen system performance, with the goal of making autism support more accessible in Hong Kong, the Greater Bay Area and beyond. The central question for the next stage is whether clinical and real-world testing can substantiate the preliminary screening result and the platform’s integrated approach to family support.

Devia Emerges From Stealth With AI Agents for Pharmaceutical Quality Investigations

📅 Nov 11, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

A pharmaceutical quality investigation has to do more than close a ticket. It must explain what went wrong, establish the cause, document the response, and show whether the same problem has happened before. Devia, a Zurich-based Visium spin-out emerging from stealth, is applying AI agents to that chain of work while keeping quality professionals responsible for the decisions.

The company says it spent a year piloting the system with quality teams in live GxP environments, including work with a major contract development and manufacturing organization. GxP is the umbrella term for regulated good-practice requirements across areas such as manufacturing, laboratories, and clinical research.

Devia’s launch targets biopharma manufacturers, contract development and manufacturing organizations (CDMOs), and contract research organizations (CROs). Its premise is that an agent can assemble evidence and draft investigative work without replacing the people accountable for approving an investigation.

In pharmaceutical operations, a deviation is a departure from an expected procedure, process, or requirement. Investigating it can require staff to reconstruct an event from production records, standard operating procedures, attachments, and earlier cases scattered across systems.

Devia describes an agent that structures the initial report, brings relevant evidence together, suggests possible root causes, and drafts corrective and preventive actions, or CAPA. Operators, investigators, and quality assurance staff review and approve the work as it progresses.

The company’s platform documentation adds detail about that process. Intake questions follow the organization’s own procedures, and the system can read attached photographs and PDFs. For root-cause analysis, it drafts Ishikawa diagrams and 5-Why investigations using the event report, procedures, and relevant historical deviations.

These methods provide structure, rather than proof by themselves. An Ishikawa analysis organizes potential contributing factors; a 5-Why investigation follows a chain of explanations toward an underlying cause. The important question is whether each proposed explanation is supported by evidence that a qualified investigator can examine.

“Quality teams don’t need another system of record. They need an agent that does the groundwork, so their experts can focus on the root causes that matter,” said Pascal Rodriguez, Devia’s CTO.

Historical investigations become useful when they help staff recognize a shared cause, rather than simply find a report with similar wording. Devia says it compares new root-cause proposals with previous cases and keeps its own experts’ reviewed decisions available for future investigations.

The website distinguishes a cause that keeps appearing without an effective intervention from one that returns after a corrective action was verified as effective. That distinction can change the investigation: an organization may need to address a previously untreated systemic issue, or explain why a supposedly effective fix failed.

Devia presents these comparisons for human review. Staff accept or reject the suggested relationships, and the resulting decisions form part of the record. This makes the review process central to the product’s usefulness: an unsupported AI suggestion should not silently become the precedent for the next case.

According to the launch release, users in customer pilots spent half as much time investigating deviations. Devia has not disclosed the pilot sample size or evaluation methodology, so the figure represents a company-reported result rather than an independently established performance benchmark.

The launch names established quality platforms such as TrackWise, Veeva Vault QMS, and MasterControl as systems customers already use. Devia’s integration documentation makes the current implementation more specific: historical deviations and CAPAs are imported from QMS exports, procedures are brought in as documents, and completed investigations produce closure reports matching the customer’s QMS format.

That is different from a fully synchronized connection to every operational system. Direct connectors for quality management, manufacturing execution, laboratory information management, and enterprise resource planning are listed as upcoming capabilities. Broader trend monitoring and CAPA-effectiveness features also appear on the platform roadmap, alongside inspection-support tools.

For deployment, Devia says the platform can run in the customer’s Azure or AWS account, in a chosen region, using Azure OpenAI or Amazon Bedrock. Its documentation states that customer records are not used to train models for other organizations. It does not identify a proprietary foundation model; the product’s described value lies in applying AI to company procedures, historical cases, and controlled review workflows.

The need for rigorous investigations is well established. The FDA’s review of drug inspection observations identifies failures to investigate discrepancies and product failures among recurrent manufacturing-quality problems. Faster drafting alone does not resolve that issue if the evidence and conclusions remain weak.

Devia’s security documentation describes individually identified users, role-based access, single sign-on, encryption, and time-stamped records of changes and approvals. It also says the company supplies validation documentation to support customers’ computerized-system validation or assurance work. These are capabilities intended to support regulated use, rather than a substitute for the customer’s own validation and quality responsibilities.

The launch also references EN 18286:2026. The European Commission confirms that the harmonized standard addresses quality-management requirements for providers of high-risk AI under Article 17 of the AI Act. Devia says its approach follows those principles; the announcement does not establish that the product has been independently certified to that standard.

Devia’s opportunity is to reduce the time investigators spend collecting and organizing information while making the reasoning easier to inspect. Its more consequential test will be whether quality teams can consistently turn that assistance into stronger investigations—and corrective actions that prevent the same manufacturing problem from returning.

A separate cybersecurity school notice dated September 4, 2026 lists two more H200 cards for 796,000 yuan. The university also obtained computing without buying the underlying hardware. Two January 28, 2026 awards from its cybersecurity school covered separate NVIDIA HGX H200 eight-GPU systems: a 441,000-yuan reinforcement-learning fine-tuning service running from February 16, 2026 through September 16, 2026, and a 441,700-yuan multitask fine-tuning service running from February 1, 2026 through August 31, 2026. Both specified 141GB of memory per GPU. These contracts supplied server capacity rather than additional H200 cards.

Two other January 28, 2026 awards covered a server with eight Nvidia A800 80GB GPUs for training-data processing, costing 240,000 yuan for 12 months, and a server with eight A100 SXM4 80GB GPUs for model inference, costing 471,600 yuan for 12 months. The A100 and A800 are barred from export to all Chinese entities. In April 2026, another university institute agreed to pay 375,000 yuan for computing services requiring at least 24 A100 chips for structural and fluid-dynamics simulations.

A January 2026 licensing change for some H200 exports did not remove the university’s separate restrictions. Northwestern Polytechnical University was added to the Entity List, the Commerce Department’s trade blacklist, on May 14, 2001; since September 20, 2016, a license has been required to export, reexport or transfer any item subject to the Export Administration Regulations to the university, according to a Bureau of Industry and Security enforcement order. Restrictions were tightened in 2019, making licenses for controlled U.S. technology exports to the university subject to denial in most circumstances.

The January 15, 2026 rule moved qualifying exports from the United States of H200 and equivalent chips to end users in China or Macau from a presumption of denial to case-by-case review. Applicants must certify sufficient U.S. supply and no diversion of global foundry capacity that would otherwise serve U.S. customers, arrange independent third-party testing in the United States, and document customer screening and physical security. They must also confirm that the commodities are not for military or military-intelligence end uses or users, that no transaction party is covered by the Entity List provision, and that restricted parties will not receive remote access. The revised route therefore does not override the university’s Entity List restrictions.

The review also identified purchases at other institutions. Shenzhen University bought three H100 processors for 769,800 yuan in September 2026, and Beihang University bought four A800 processors for 489,000 yuan in April 2026. Beihang also belongs to the Seven Sons group and has close ties to China’s aerospace and defense industries.

Chinese public-procurement rules require agencies to verify that suppliers can fulfill orders before announcing winning bidders and signing contracts. The records nevertheless offered little information about where the processors originated or where the rented servers operated. 📅 Nov 11, 2026  |  Category: Business  |  Read Source →

I gift stock shares to my 2 sons from my Schwab account to each of their Schwab accounts. There's a form that you can find on their site, but you'll want to send them a letter (a picture is fine) to them to create a path directly to the person you want to gift. Once this is done, in your accounts, there will be an account labeled SLOA-Schwab One Acct ### with ### to identify this account. I can very easily and quickly gift cash or shares by doing this. You can set your account default so that shares you want to gift specifically can be designated. I do FIFO (first in first out) so the oldest shares, which are long term get gifted. Remember the default if you sell shares through regular trade and if needed, during the trade process, change the default share designation to what you want to. This does not change the default.

The receiver gets the shares and just like any Schwab account, when you see the stock or fund name, the cost basis, in blue is clickable and that brings up a list of each lot with cost basis, date of purchase, long/short term, gain, etc. I find that Schwab's site has everything I could possibly want to know.

When Schwab generates its consolidated 1099, it will show the sale of any of these shares they have sold and the information can be used for tax preparation.
