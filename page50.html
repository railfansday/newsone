Voyager 1 went silent in deep space right before reaching a historic milestone of one ‘light day’ away from Earth

📅 Nov 11, 2026  |  Category: Science  |  Read Source →

NASA briefly lost contact with Voyager 1 in October 2026. The signal returned just weeks before the spacecraft reaches a historic milestone.

On Oct. 18, 2026, the antennas of NASA’s Deep Space Network tried to pick up Voyager 1 during a scheduled contact and found nothing. A second attempt 24 hours later also failed.

The signal came back on Oct. 20, and the team at NASA’s Jet Propulsion Laboratory (JPL) in Southern California has kept in touch with the probe since then.

NASA schedules Nov. 18 as the day Voyager 1, already the farthest human-made object, reaches one light-day from Earth, according to JPL. They had not previously said what caused the October problems.

The Deep Space Network has large antennas at Goldstone in California, near Madrid in Spain and near Canberra in Australia. The largest dish at each site measures about 230 feet (70 meters) across.

Those antennas handled the two contact attempts in October. Mission controllers logged no detectable signal on Oct. 18 or Oct. 19.

Kareem Badaruddin, the Voyager mission manager at JPL, said the episode was new to his team. “This is the first time we’ve seen the spacecraft do something like this,” he said in the JPL statement.

He also said the mission has reached a stage where the team has “to expect the unexpected.” The probe has surprised its operators before, including the time it began talking through a radio system not used since 1981.

After contact returned, the team worked out that one group of thrusters on the spacecraft had gone offline and that a backup group had turned on.

The onboard computers carry instructions to make that swap by themselves when they detect a problem with the active thrusters.

JPL calls this kind of automatic reaction fault protection. It puts the probe in a safe, stable state and pauses normal operations so that engineers on the ground can study the problem.

In March 2025, engineers revived a set of roll thrusters that had stopped working in 2004.

Early in September, about six weeks before the probe dropped out of contact, JPL engineers sent the commands that turned off three devices on Voyager 1 and turned on three lower-power substitutes.

Inside the team, the job goes by the name Big Bang. The devices double as a source of warmth for the spacecraft, so JPL could not do them one at a time and all three changed at once.

Voyager 2 got the treatment first, and NASA reported on Aug. 4, 2026 that the swap there should keep three instruments powered for at least one more year. An instrument on Voyager 2 would otherwise have gone dark before the end of 2026.

Nothing in the Oct. 29 note ties the September commands to the thrusters.

Both probes run on radioisotope generators, which turn the heat from decaying plutonium into electricity. The output on each probe falls by about 4 watts a year and, with margins now thin, JPL has been shutting off whatever is not essential.

Ten science instruments went up on Voyager 1 in 1977. A NASA status page updated in April lists only two still running on it, the magnetometer and the plasma wave subsystem.

Controllers switched off the cosmic ray subsystem on Feb. 25, 2025, in one of a series of shutdowns meant to stretch the remaining power. The low-energy charged particle instrument was next, on April 17, 2026.

Without the Big Bang swap, another Voyager 1 instrument would likely have been turned off as early as summer 2027, JPL said.

Voyager 1 launched on Sept. 5, 1977, two weeks after its twin, passed Jupiter and Saturn, and entered interstellar space in August 2012. It is moving at about 38,000 miles per hour (61,155 kilometers per hour).

NASA’s light-day page sets the crossing on Nov. 18, when the probe will sit about 16.09 billion miles (25.9 billion kilometers) from Earth.

Light covers the distance to the Moon in 1.3 seconds. Artemis II astronauts needed about three days for the same trip.

NASA’s Jet Propulsion Laboratory explains how far away Voyager 1 is when it reaches one light-day from Earth. Credit: NASA Jet Propulsion Laboratory.

Voyager 2 took a different route so it could visit Uranus and Neptune, and NASA expects it to reach the same distance in November 2035. The nearest star system, Proxima Centauri, lies about 4.24 light-years away.

Voyager project manager Suzanne Dodd described what the distance does to a conversation. “If you want to say ‘hello’ to Voyager, you send your message and then you go home and you wait two days before you finally get a response,” she said in the JPL statement.

JPL’s Oct. 29 note does not say what knocked the thrusters offline, and engineers are still investigating. It also does not confirm that the thruster switch was what interrupted the signal.

The note does not name the three devices replaced in September or give the amount of power saved. It also says nothing about when the mission might end, although the team expects more surprises before summer 2027.

The light-day crossing itself will change nothing on board or around the spacecraft, JPL said. Its value lies in showing the length of the mission and the size of the distances involved.

At one light-day, a command sent from Goldstone, Madrid or Canberra needs a full day to reach Voyager 1. The answer needs another.

The information was obtained from NASA’s Jet Propulsion Laboratory.

My IEnumerable Parameter Boxed 80 MB. PGO Clawed Back 85%.

📅 Nov 11, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

I had a helper that summed a column of ints. Tiny method, the kind you write without thinking. I'd typed its parameter as IEnumerable out of pure habit, the "accept the most general thing you can" reflex that every C# tutorial drills into you. Months later it turned up near the top of an allocation trace, and I stared at it for a while because there's no new anywhere in it. It's a foreach and a +=. What could it possibly be allocating?

The allocation wasn't in my code. It was hiding in the foreach.

Same body, character for character. The only thing that differs is the parameter type, and that one difference decides whether the enumerator the foreach grabs lives on the stack or on the heap.

Here's why. List.GetEnumerator() returns List.Enumerator, which is a struct. When you foreach over a concrete List, the compiler binds straight to that struct method and the enumerator never leaves the stack. But IEnumerable.GetEnumerator() returns IEnumerator, an interface. To pass a struct back through an interface reference, the runtime has to box it. So every call into the interface version allocates one boxed enumerator before the loop even starts.

I measured it the way I measure most of these: GC.GetAllocatedBytesForCurrentThread() wrapped around two million calls on an eight-element list. Allocation counts don't jitter the way timings do, so the number actually means something.

Zero versus twelve megabytes for identical arithmetic. Then I ran it again, expecting the same twelve.

A different number. Allocation counts are supposed to be deterministic. Same work in, same bytes out. A figure that wobbles between runs means something non-deterministic is deciding how much to allocate, and that's the clue that pointed me at the JIT.

.NET compiles your hot methods twice. The first pass, tier-0, is fast and dumb and boxes the enumerator on every single call. While that version runs, the JIT keeps notes: which concrete type actually shows up at this call site? Every time, it's List. So when the method gets hot enough to recompile at tier-1, the JIT bakes in a guard that says "if it's a List, run the inlined struct enumerator, skip the box," and the common path stops allocating. The ~12 MB I measured is just the boxes from the warmup window, before tier-1 took over. How many that is depends on how quickly the method tiered up on that particular run, which is exactly why the number wobbles.

There's the honest number. Forty bytes a call, two million calls, exactly 80 MB, flat across runs. That's the real weight of a boxed List.Enumerator, and PGO had been eating about 85% of it on my behalf without telling me.

The rescue comes with one condition: the call site has to be monomorphic. The JIT devirtualizes to one type precisely because it only ever sees one type. Hand the same method a mix and it can't pick a winner.

Back to boxing on nearly every call. Which means the methods most likely to pay full freight are the flexible, reusable ones that cheerfully accept whatever you throw at them. The exact spots where IEnumerable felt like the grown-up choice.

None of this touched gen0. Eighty megabytes didn't even trigger a collection on this box, because the container has a roomy ephemeral budget and best-of-a-few-runs is the lens I care about, not a lab-grade millisecond. For a method you call a few hundred times, 40 bytes each is noise. Type it however reads cleanest and move on. This only earns a second look on a genuinely hot inner loop, and on those, modern .NET already handles the monomorphic case for you.

My rule now, for whatever it's worth: when I own both sides of the call and the loop is hot, I type the parameter as the concrete collection, or I take a ReadOnlySpan and stop thinking about enumerators altogether. I keep IEnumerable for public APIs, where accepting anything is the whole point. "Accept the most general type" is great advice for a library and a quiet tax on a hot internal helper.

The thing I keep relearning is that the allocation wasn't in the code I wrote. It was in the contract I picked for one parameter, and whether the JIT argues me back out of it depends entirely on how predictable my call sites are.

For grasses in any stage of growth or dormancy, leaves can trap moisture, restrict airflow, and encourage disease. Left unchecked, smothering leaves will suffocate entire sections of turf, leaving bare, dead zones by spring.

Your removal strategy should match the volume of fallen leaf cover. For light to moderate leaf scatter, use a mulching mower and let the nutrients fall naturally into the ground. However, if your yard is buried under dense, wet blankets from mature oaks or maples, rake or blow them off immediately. Collect the leaves for rich leaf mold or vegetable garden mulch. Focus extra attention on perimeter fence lines and low dips in the lawn, where fall winds can quickly create suffocating drifts.

Timing your final mow comes down to knowing your grass type, and considering the weather. In northern regions, cool-season grasses (fescues, bluegrass, ryegrass) continue growing until ambient soil temperatures consistently sit below 40°F (4°C). So they could still be growing in November if the weather is mild, but plan for a last cut soon. In the south, warm-season lawns (Bermuda, St. Augustine, Zoysia) have ceased upward growth and could be dormant already, so your last cut may already have happened.

When making a final cut, aim for a medium length. Cutting grass too short will leave the lawn more vulnerable to stress over winter, exposing tender root crowns to frost heaving and biting winds. Conversely, leaving turf overly tall causes long blades to fold over under heavy snow or winter rain, forming dense mats that trap moisture and increase the risk of disease. Aim for a final height of 2-2.5 inches (5-6cm) for cool-season lawns, and 2 inches (5cm) for warm-season lawns.

Once complete, hose down the underside of the mower deck, empty the fuel tank or run the carburetor dry, and store your mower away clean. Late-fall lawn fertilization can be either beneficial or destructive, depending on the species of grass you cultivate. For cool-season turf, late fall is often considered the most important feeding of the year. Cool-season roots remain active, absorbing nitrogen and potassium. So this last infusion of nutrients supports strong root development and helps encourage early spring green-up and drought resistance. Timing depends on location. In some areas, November may be too late for a last application. Apply fertilizer only while the grass is still green and before the ground freezes solid. If you’re unsure, contact your local extension office for guidance. Meanwhile, warm-season grasses should not be fed in November. They are entering dormancy or are already dormant. Nutrients added now could trigger new growth that have zero cold tolerance, leaving the turf vulnerable to winterkill during sudden freezes. Wait until spring to feed your warm-season turf.

Cooler weather and shorter daylight hours reduce your lawn’s evaporation rates. Continuing to run summer lawn watering schedules through November wastes water and runs the risk of waterlogging. If your cool-season turf is green and receiving less than an inch of natural rainfall per week, you only need to provide occasional deep waterings. Irrigate only when the top two inches (2cm) of soil feel dry to the touch, and irrigate during morning hours so grass blades dry before nightfall.

Do think about winterization with watering, too. Trapped water in underground pipes, sprinkler bodies, and backflow preventers expands upon freezing, cracking brass valves and rupturing poly lines. Turn off the main water shutoff valve, deactivate the controller, and blow out the lines using a commercial air compressor, or open manual drain valves. Disconnect, drain, and coil all garden hoses, storing them indoors. Leaving a pressurized hose attached to an outdoor spigot traps water inside the sillcock, leading to burst interior pipes during winter freezes.

Spotting thin, bare patches in your lawn during November is frustrating, but rushing out with a bag of standard seed and fertilizer is usually a waste of time and money. Across most northern and transitional zones, soil temperatures have dropped below the 50-55°F (10-13°C) threshold required for grass seed germination. Seed spread now will absorb moisture, fail to sprout, rot in cold mud, or be devoured by birds. Similarly, warm-season varieties cannot establish from seed in late fall.

Use November to diagnose and prep. Determine why the patch developed: is it compacted soil from a high-traffic shortcut, poor drainage, pet urine, or heavy tree shade? Aerate the bald soil with a hand cultivator, incorporate a light dusting of compost, and make a plan for early spring overseeding. If you live in an area with reliable, long-lasting winter snow cover, you can experiment with dormant seeding. Spread cool-season seed over the prepped soil just before the first big snowfall. The seed will remain dormant over winter, and the freeze-thaw cycles work the seeds into the dirt for uniform germination during the first warm days of March.

Grass enters a vulnerable state in dormancy, and plants lose their natural elasticity. Walking, driving, or playing on frosted or frozen turf fractures brittle leaf blades and crushes delicate crowns. This creates winter lawn damage like marks and ruts that will not heal until active growth resumes in spring. Plan to keep people and pets off the grass for the next few months. Finally, avoid late-season core aeration or power dethatching, which can do more harm than good as grass prepares for winter.

VPS Hardening: 10 Practices That Reduce Risk

📅 Nov 11, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

A virtual private server (VPS) is easy to deploy and surprisingly easy to leave exposed. A developer provisions a Linux instance, installs Docker, configures a reverse proxy, and deploys an application. Everything works. Six months later, the server has accumulated open ports, unused accounts, outdated containers, and configuration changes nobody remembers making.

The problem is rarely one catastrophic mistake. It is the gradual loss of control over what runs on the server, who can access it, and how it can be recovered.

Effective VPS security comes down to three priorities: reduce what an attacker can reach, limit what compromised software can do, and make recovery predictable.

These practices focus on Ubuntu and similar Linux distributions hosting web applications, APIs, and containers.

A common VPS issue is configuration drift. Developers install packages, modify firewall rules, and adjust services directly on the server. Eventually, nobody can reproduce the original configuration.

Treat the VPS as something you should be able to replace.

Keep the initial configuration in Ansible, cloud-init, or another provisioning system. Record required packages, firewall rules, application users, and service configurations.

Separate persistent data from server configuration. Databases, uploaded files, and encryption keys need explicit backup and recovery procedures.

A practical test is to provision a second VPS and deploy the application without copying the original machine's filesystem. If that requires recovering undocumented commands from shell history, the infrastructure is not sufficiently reproducible.

This also improves incident response. When compromise is suspected, rebuilding from trusted images and restoring verified data is generally safer than attempting to remove every possible persistence mechanism.

2. Keep SSH Off the Public Internet When Possible

SSH keys are essential, but authentication alone does not eliminate the risks of exposing an administrative service.

For a VPS administered by a small team, consider making SSH accessible through a private network such as WireGuard or Tailscale. Alternatively, restrict inbound connections to known administrative IP addresses through the hosting provider's firewall.

If SSH must remain public, disable password authentication and direct root login after confirming that a separate administrator account works.

On Ubuntu, check both /etc/ssh/sshd_config and any included files under /etc/ssh/sshd_config.d/. OpenSSH generally uses the first obtained value for each configuration keyword, so adding a setting at the end of the main file may not override an earlier one.

Validate the configuration with sshd -t and inspect effective settings with sshd -T. For conditional Match rules, use sshd -T -C with the relevant connection parameters.

Keep an existing SSH session open until a new administrative connection succeeds.

Protect developers' private SSH keys, preferably with hardware backing, and revoke credentials when people leave the team. Changing SSH from port 22 may reduce automated scanning noise, but it does not replace access restrictions.

Developers often configure a firewall once and assume the server remains protected. Meanwhile, a debugging service or database may begin listening on every network interface.

This displays listening TCP and UDP sockets and their associated processes. It helps you find unexpected listeners, but it does not show which services are externally reachable, especially when containers or network address translation are involved.

For a typical web application, public access may only be necessary on ports 80 and 443. Databases, message brokers, metrics endpoints, and administration interfaces should generally remain private.

Use two levels of enforcement: the provider's network firewall and a host firewall such as UFW or nftables. Remember that container networking may introduce additional firewall rules.

Also check IPv6. Restricting access through IPv4 rules does not guarantee that the same service is inaccessible over IPv6.

After modifying firewall rules, test connectivity from an external network. Checking what remote clients can actually reach is more reliable than inspecting firewall configuration alone.

5. Restrict What Compromised Applications Can Do

Even a well-configured firewall cannot prevent exploitation of vulnerabilities in applications that must remain publicly accessible.

The next question is what happens after an attacker controls an application process.

Run services under dedicated accounts without unnecessary permissions. A Node.js API, for example, generally should not need root privileges, write access to application code, or access to another application's configuration files.

For system services managed by systemd, inspect the available hardening restrictions:

These controls restrict privilege escalation and filesystem access. They do not provide complete process isolation. In particular, ProtectSystem=strict does not make the API filesystems /dev, /proc, and /sys read-only, and filesystem restrictions do not prevent communication through accessible Unix sockets.

Test the configuration against normal application behavior before deploying it to production. Some applications require additional writable directories or access to system resources.

For containers, follow the same principle: run processes as nonroot where practical, remove unnecessary Linux capabilities, and avoid privileged mode.

The goal is to give each service only the access required to perform its job.

Automatic Linux updates are necessary, but they do not cover every component running on the VPS.

Ubuntu's unattended-upgrades can install distribution security updates, but third-party repositories require appropriate configuration. Application dependencies, downloaded binaries, and container images need separate update processes.

Track three maintenance areas: the host operating system, supporting infrastructure such as Docker Engine, and application dependencies.

Rebuild container images regularly using maintained base images. Scan dependencies during development and deploy updated images through the normal release process.

Also plan restarts. Installing a patched library does not necessarily replace vulnerable code already loaded into memory.

On Ubuntu 24.04 LTS, the needrestart package, when installed and invoked through APT, automatically restarts affected services by default, subject to configured exclusions. This also applies to unattended upgrades and can unexpectedly interrupt production workloads.

Review its behavior and configure exclusions for critical services when necessary. Schedule their restarts during maintenance windows rather than leaving vulnerable code running indefinitely.

Kernel updates may require a reboot unless an appropriate live patching mechanism covers the vulnerability.

7. Stop Treating Environment Files as Secret Storage Solutions

Secrets frequently leak through deployment shortcuts. Developers copy .env files between servers, embed tokens in scripts, or accidentally expose credentials through logs.

Environment files can be an acceptable configuration mechanism, but they are not a dedicated security boundary.

Use a secrets manager where practical. For smaller deployments, restricted configuration files or systemd credentials can be reasonable alternatives.

Avoid placing secrets in command arguments, where they may appear in process information or operational logs. Environment variables are convenient, but they can also be exposed through application diagnostics, process inspection under appropriate privileges, or a compromised application.

Also check whether the hosting platform provides instance credentials or sensitive information through a metadata service. On applicable cloud platforms, a server-side request forgery (SSRF) vulnerability may allow attackers to access metadata that the application can reach.

Use the provider's metadata protections, restrict unnecessary access, and assign instance credentials only the permissions required.

Finally, make credential rotation routine. Replacing a leaked database password should not require rediscovering the entire deployment procedure.

8. Collect Logs That Help Investigate Incidents

A VPS may record authentication attempts and application errors, but local logs have limited value if attackers can erase them after gaining privileged access.

Forward important events to a separate logging destination. Prioritize authentication activity, privilege escalation, application errors, reverse proxy requests, and changes to important configurations.

Build alerts around events requiring attention. Repeated failed SSH logins are common on publicly accessible servers. A successful administrative login from an unexpected source is usually more useful to investigate.

Monitor disk consumption too. Container logs without appropriate rotation can exhaust available storage and disrupt healthy applications.

Configure log retention, protect sensitive information in log records, and make sure the logging system does not become another source of outages.

9. Make Backups Survive a Server Compromise

Provider snapshots are convenient, but they should not be the only recovery mechanism.

If an attacker compromises the hosting account, snapshots controlled by that account may also be exposed or deleted.

Maintain separate backups of databases, uploaded files, and essential configuration. Encrypt them and use storage with independent credentials. Where supported, enable retention controls that prevent backups from being immediately modified or deleted.

For databases, use backup methods that ensure consistency rather than assuming a simple filesystem copy is always sufficient.

Provision a clean VPS, restore recent data, start the application, and verify that it works. Check more than whether the restore command completed. Confirm that the application can read its data and perform important operations.
