Will Ebenezer Be The Box Office Hit Johnny Depp's Hollywood Comeback Needs?

📅 Nov 04, 2026  |  Category: Entertainment  |  Read Source →

Johnny Depp is attempting to mount a Hollywood comeback. After years of being embroiled in controversy and starring in films you probably haven't heard of (like the colonial drama "Waiting for the Barbarians"), he's back in his first major studio movie in a long time. "Ebenezer" is a new take on Charles Dickens' "A Christmas Carol," with Depp playing Scrooge. The question is, can this be the box office hit that Depp needs?

Directed by Ti West ("X," "The Inkeepers"), "Ebenezer" is expected to pull in between $17 and $24 million domestically when it hits theaters next weekend, per Box Office Theory. Against a reported $38 million production budget, that would be a solid start for the Paramount release, especially since this one should be able to hang around through the holiday season. That is, assuming audiences like what West and Depp have cooked up. If it's eviscerated by critics and audiences alike? That's another conversation.

Competition shouldn't be a big issue, at least not right away. "Godzilla Minus Zero" could make box office history for the franchise topping the charts this weekend, but it shouldn't get in the way come its second frame. "The Cat in the Hat" is unlikely to be a major, direct threat, either. Rather, the biggest competition will come when "The Hunger Games: Sunrise on the Reaping" arrives on November 20.

The "Ebenezer" trailer positions Depp's "Christmas Carol" re-telling as being closer to a horror movie. Now, horror has been extremely hot at the box office lately, so that makes sense. Paramount is also selling the film with the tagline, "The name you know, the story you don't," and it has a star-studded supporting cast that includes Rupert Grint ("Harry Potter"), Andrea Riseborough ("Mandy"), Daisy Ridley ("Star Wars"), Trammell Tillman ("Severence"), and Ian McKellen ("X-Men").

There are obvious risks here. As mentioned, if "Ebenezer" is met with a bad response, all of these numbers could be meaningless, as word of mouth could wreck its ability to have legs. Still, it's likely to enjoy big returns on VOD and streaming, if for no other reason than curiosity from viewers.

The other issue is Johnny Depp himself. Depp dropped out of the "Fantastic Beasts" franchise after a court case verdict tied to tabloid The Sun in 2018. That, coupled with his highly-publicized courtroom battle with his ex-wife Amber Heard over allegations of abuse, derailed his career. Even before that, though, it wasn't going as well as it once had been.

Depp proved his best days were already behind him with the big box office bomb that was "Mortdecai" in 2015. He hasn't starred in a meaningful hit since 2018. Yes, the "Pirates of the Caribbean" movies made billions of dollars, but that was a long time ago. Do audiences still care about Depp? That's the big question Paramount seeks to answer. It's betting the answer is "yes."

Beyond that, Ti West has never directed a high-profile movie of this size. He helmed the "X" films rather successfully, with "MaXXXine" sending off the trilogy on a box office high note, but we're still talking about a movie that made $22 million in total. This is a big step up for West, and in light of the Depp of it all, there's a lot of pressure. Was the director able to tune out the noise and deliver a crowd-pleaser? We'll know soon enough.

"Ebenzer" hits theaters on November 13, 2026.

AMD beats expectations as agentic AI sparks its data center revenue growth engines

📅 Nov 04, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Shares of the chipmaker Advanced Micro Devices Inc. were up more than 5% in late trading today after it delivered solid third-quarter earnings and revenue today, with data center sales almost doubling from a year ago.

The company reported earnings before certain costs such as stock compensation of $2.09 per share, easily beating Wall Street’s forecast of $1.95 per share. Revenue for the period jumped 45% from a year earlier to $13.38 billion, ahead of the $13.09 billion analyst consensus estimate. All told, the company’s net income for the quarter came to $3.47 billion, up 77% from the previous year when it posted a profit of just $1.96 billion.

The star performer in the quarter was AMD’s data center business unit, which accounted for 64% of its overall revenue, up from just 47% one year ago. After spending years trying to compete with Nvidia Corp.’s graphics processing units for artificial intelligence market share, the company’s traditional central processing units or CPUs are suddenly back in vogue thanks to the emergence of agentic AI applications such as Meta Platforms Inc.’s Muse. Autonomous AI agents can run much more efficiently on CPUs than they can on GPUs, boosting demand for the chips.

Looking ahead, AMD expects its server revenue to double year-over-year in fiscal 2027, with AI growth likely to accelerate even faster than the company projected just three months ago. “Adoption is just starting and we expect demand to grow sharply as agents are deployed more broadly and take on more complex work,” said AMD Chief Executive Lisa Su (pictured) on a conference call with analysts.

AMD’s data center story isn’t all about CPUs, though. The company also sells GPUs, including bundles of them shipped inside data center-ready rack-scale systems called Helios, and that system’s popularity is growing fast. Su said Anthropic PBC, OpenAI Group PBC, Microsoft Corp., Oracle Corp. and Meta are all using AMD Helios systems in their data centers. Last week, Meta said its first Helion racks are already up and running as it revealed that AMD invested $1 billion in its latest equity offering.

The chipmaker has also committed to a $5 billion investment in Anthropic, which last week revealed the long-awaited prospectus for its upcoming initial public offering.

AMD delivered its latest earnings report just days after its rival Intel Corp. did the same. Intel also posted strong data center revenue growth, but its stock fell more than 30% on Friday due to weak guidance and concerns over its ability to meet demand for its products. But Su said AMD had no such problems. She made a high-profile visit to Taiwan last month where she held meetings with Taiwan Semiconductor Manufacturing Co. and a number of supply chain partners, and said after the trip that the company plans to substantially increase its supply in the coming year. “We have worked across our supply chain to secure the wafers, substrates, advanced packaging, memory and other key components needed to support much higher growth next year,” Su added.

AMD’s results will go some way towards reassuring investors who have become increasingly nervous about the direction of the AI industry and whether or not the hundreds of billions of dollars being spent on it each year is actually going to generate a return on their investments, said Emarketer analyst Jacob Bourne. “The results are a test of AMD’s own performance and the broader AI trade, and its data center and revenue beats will encourage investors after the recent semiconductor selloff,” he added.

While AMD is far from immune to the recent volatility that has afflicted AI-related stocks, its future still looks increasingly rosy for now. “Demand for AI computing is not going to fall off a cliff, and AMD has the competitive products and major customer relationships to capitalize on that demand,” Bourne said.

Strength in the data center unit is helping AMD to ride out difficulties in its traditional market for personal computers, which is the reason for Intel’s poor outlook. During the second quarter, global PC shipments were down 4% from a year earlier, with buyers dissuaded by the rising cost of components such as memory chips, according to data from Counterpoint Research. Along with Intel, AMD is a leading manufacturer of the x86 CPUs that power most of the world’s computers and laptops. The company reports PC chip revenue within a segment that also includes its chips for games consoles, and that unit saw its revenue decline 8% from a year earlier to $3.7 billion. Gaming revenue was down 40% from the previous year.

For the current quarter, AMD is targeting revenue of around $17 billion at the midpoint of its guidance range, ahead of the Street’s $16.2 billion consensus estimate. The company also sees its gross margins coming in at 55%, just below the Street’s forecast of 55.3%.

AMD was busy on the acquisition front during the quarter. In September it revealed it’s going to buy World Labs Inc. for $8.2 billion in an all-stock deal. The San Francisco-based AI lab was founded by the AI industry luminary Fei-Fei Li, and has developed world models that can simulate 3D environments to support the training of intelligent robots and other physical AI applications.

The company also acquired a startup called Taalas Inc. that makes specialized model-specific integrated circuits, which are essentially chips that can be customized to run a specific AI model more efficiently. That acquisition should help to boost AMD’s position in AI inference. AMD also invested in the AI image generation model maker Stability AI Ltd., which raised $76 million in funding in August.

Alabama Lands at No. 5 in First CFP Rankings Despite Two Losses

📅 Nov 04, 2026  |  Category: Sports  |  Read Source →

The Alabama Crimson Tide have officially earned a spot inside the top five of the first College Football Playoff rankings of the 2026 season. Despite already suffering two losses, the selection committee placed Alabama at No. 5, giving Kalen DeBoer's team an early opportunity to compete for a national championship.

At 6-2 overall and 4-2 in SEC play, Alabama has experienced an interesting season. The Crimson Tide started strong but suffered consecutive losses against Georgia and Tennessee, raising questions about their consistency and championship expectations.

However, the committee clearly believes Alabama has one of the strongest resumes in the country.

One of the biggest reasons Alabama landed at No. 5 is its impressive collection of victories. The Crimson Tide have defeated Kentucky, Florida State, South Carolina, Mississippi State and Texas A&M.

Their 45-17 victory over Kentucky was particularly important. With Kentucky sitting at No. 6 in the first CFP rankings, Alabama holds a valuable head-to-head victory over another highly ranked SEC program.

DeBoer has also helped Alabama remain competitive despite the difficult stretch in October. The Crimson Tide responded to their losses against Georgia and Tennessee with a 31-20 victory over Texas A&M.

That bounce back performance helped Alabama regain some momentum heading into November.

Alabama's next challenge will be another important SEC matchup against LSU on November 7.

The Tigers currently sit at No. 14 in the first CFP rankings, making this another opportunity for Alabama to add a quality victory to its playoff resume.

With games against Vanderbilt and Auburn still remaining, the Crimson Tide have opportunities to strengthen their position.

Can Alabama Make Another Championship Run?

Landing at No. 5 is a significant accomplishment for Alabama, especially considering its two losses.

However, the Crimson Tide cannot afford to become comfortable. The expanded 12 team playoff gives Alabama some room for error, but another loss could make its path much more difficult.

The committee has shown that it still believes in Alabama's potential. Now, DeBoer and his players must prove that ranking is deserved.

The road to the playoff is far from over, but Alabama has officially put itself in the conversation.

Michigan CB Jyaire Hill calls Michigan State rivalry a 'violent game'

📅 Nov 04, 2026  |  Category: Sports  |  Read Source →

ANN ARBOR, Mich. -- The in-state rivalry pitting Michigan football vs. Michigan State is not for the faint of heart. It's more physical than most, and more chippy. In fact, there almost always seems to be some in-game (or postgame) shenanigans between the Wolverines and Spartans.

But it's something that Michigan cornerback Jyaire Hill can't wait to be a part of.

Hill has played in three iterations of the battle for the Paul Bunyan Trophy, having not been a part of the travel party in 2023, but having played in the past three. He says that this game stands apart, because there's a different intensity to it than some others.

"Man, violent game, all four quarters," Hill said. "I'll be ready to go. See it right there, violent. Brotherhood, ready to play for your brothers, all four quarters."

What makes it violent in Hill's eyes? He says that there's an honest hatred between the two teams, and that because of that, there can be some -- extracurriculars.

"Man, I feel like everybody's just mad at each other," Hill said. "You got some players might do something after the play, some try to get in your head. You've got to be real poised into the game, real dialed in. You can't get out your zone."

Michigan is preparing for that aspect, and is not looking to retaliate, but also wants to ensure that they have their brothers' backs.

But there have been questions about head coach Kyle Whittingham, given this is his introduction to the rivalry. It had seemed initially as if he was downplaying it -- much like he appeared to do with Ohio State. But Hill -- who's played under Jim Harbaugh and Sherrone Moore before Hill -- says that he has liked Whittingham's approach thus far.

"He knows what he's doing," Hill said. "Like I said, he's real. It's like he's been here. So he did some history. He did some work on his game. So he knows what time it is."

For now, Hill is just excited to get out there. He's looking forward to the enhanced physicality, noting that it does happen for receivers and cornerbacks as well, and he is ready to take his game up a notch.

"Oh, yeah. I'm ready for that," Hill said. "Yeah, yeah, yeah. Ready to twist some helmets. Yeah, I'm ready."

Kickoff is at 7:40 p.m. EST on Saturday, with the game set to be streamed on Peacock.

CORVALLIS — Two years ago, Jarrel Comer was a freshman at Tallahassee Community College and had not played competitive football since middle school. Now he's a starting outside linebacker at Oregon State.

Comer played basketball at Eastside High School in Gainesville, Florida, but began college as a regular student. He looked for opportunities to play sports again and football became the opportunity at Citrus College in California.

He boarded a plane for the first time to take a cross-country flight to play football for the first time in more than four years.

"I was willing to take that chance to get to where I am today," Comer said.

He had 58 tackles (8.5 for loss), including six sacks at the California junior college and got on the radar of linebackers coach Cort Dennison, then at Idaho. Dennison kept pursuing Comer at OSU, where he signed in January and arrived during the spring.

At 6-foot-4 and 245 pounds, Comer was dominating in junior college from his athleticism alone. He didn't know the finer points of playing football. Hand placement, setting the edge in the run game and pass coverage were not in his repertoire. Comer was there to pin his ears back and attack the quarterback, regardless of the down and distance.

"They didn't ask much of me," Comer said. "They told me just do what I do, use my athleticism, get to the quarterback."

What Comer lacks in experience, he makes up for in ability. He consistently ranks among OSU's fastest players in practice, which was invaluable to a team seeking more pass rush.

"His athleticism is off the charts," defensive coordinator Mike MacIntyre said. "We see him grow leaps and bounds. Playing a lot more (against Montana), he will grow leaps and bounds going into this Saturday because now he's seen it, it slows down, he understands the different things."

Comer has 10 tackles thus far, including four in each of the past two games, and is already more than a situational pass rusher for the Beavers. He credits MacIntyre and edge coaches Joe Seumalo and George Frazier with helping him learn the game, particularly pass coverage.

Ideally, pairing Comer with Takari Hickle will allow OSU to generate more pressure as teams won't be able to focus all their offensive line on one side.

"Teams have to respect his speed," Hickle said. "If they don't, then he'll run right by them. He is definitely a threat in coverage and in the pass game."

The Mystery of Hair Growth Patterns May Finally Have a Simple Explanation

📅 Nov 04, 2026  |  Category: Other  |  Read Source →

A new study reveals how subtle interactions between cells and chemical signals may shape the intricate patterns of mammalian hair.

The precise arrangement of hair follicles across an animal’s skin may arise from a surprisingly simple process. Researchers at the University of Geneva (UNIGE) have developed a mathematical model showing how cells responding to chemical signals can generate distinct hair patterns in two mammalian species, without requiring a complex system to determine where each follicle forms.

Published in PNAS, the research challenges a long-standing explanation for hair follicle development and suggests that variations in the same underlying cellular process could produce different patterns across species.

Hair follicles begin forming before birth in specialized embryonic structures called placodes. These structures also give rise to feathers and most types of scales. Their arrangement varies between species, but scientists have long debated how developing tissues establish such organized patterns.

For laboratory mice (Mus musculus), the prevailing explanation has been the expansion-induction model. According to this theory, each newly formed placode releases an inhibitory molecule that temporarily prevents others from developing nearby. As the skin expands, new areas move beyond the molecule’s influence, allowing additional placodes to form in the spaces between existing ones.

Although this model can account for the developmental patterns observed in laboratory mice, researchers have not experimentally established that it explains hair follicle organization more broadly across mammals.

A research team co-led by Athanasia Tzika, Senior Lecturer, and Michel Milinkovitch, Professor in UNIGE’s Department of Genetics and Evolution, investigated a different mechanism called chemotaxis. This process allows cells to move in response to chemical gradients. It also helps white blood cells navigate toward areas of inflammation.

The researchers created a mathematical model in which mobile cells in the dermis respond to an attractive chemical signal produced by the epidermis, the skin’s outer layer. As the simulated embryonic skin grows, these interactions spontaneously produce placodes in patterns that closely match the developmental sequence observed in laboratory mice.

“Our findings show that the observed patterns do not require a complex system telling each placode where to form,” explain Muhamet Ibrahimi and Ebrahim Jahanbakhsh, researchers in the Department of Genetics and Evolution. “Instead, placodes emerge spontaneously from local interactions between cells and chemical signals.”

The researchers next examined the spiny mouse (Acomys dimidiatus), a rodent whose hair follicles form a highly regular, directionally organized pattern. Unlike the arrangement found in laboratory mice, this distinctive configuration cannot be explained by the traditional expansion-induction model.

Using experimental data obtained through three-dimensional imaging of embryonic skin, the team adapted its mathematical model to reflect the spiny mouse’s particular biochemical characteristics and skin growth patterns. The same chemotactic mechanism successfully reproduced the species’ characteristic follicle arrangement.

These simulations suggest that different mammalian hair patterns could emerge from a shared process governed by cell movement and chemical signaling, with species-specific differences in how those interactions unfold. The results provide a potential alternative to the conventional model, although mathematical agreement with observed patterns does not establish that chemotaxis is the mechanism operating in living skin.

“Our work suggests that simple cellular interactions can generate the remarkable diversity of tissue architectures observed throughout evolution,” concludes Athanasia Tzika. “The differences observed between species therefore result from the same self-organization process, but with variations in the interactions between cells and chemical signals.”

Reference: “Chemotactic self-organization captures the dynamics of mammalian hair follicle patterning” by Muhamet Ibrahimi, Ebrahim Jahanbakhsh, Athanasia C. Tzika and Michel C. Milinkovitch, 2 July 2026, Proceedings of the National Academy of Sciences. DOI: 10.1073/pnas.2530407123

BigBear.ai vs. Nebius: Which AI Stock Is the Better Buy for 2027?

📅 Nov 04, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

One of these AI stocks is shrinking and losing money, while the other is growing at triple digits and just turned profitable.

As the artificial intelligence (AI) race intensifies, investors are looking beyond the biggest names for hidden opportunities. Deciding between BigBear.ai (BBAI -1.94%) and Nebius Group (NBIS +7.25%) requires weighing government contracts against cloud infrastructure.

BigBear.ai focuses on decision-intelligence software for high-stakes defense environments. Nebius builds the actual full-stack infrastructure needed to train and deploy these complex models. While both companies operate in the fast-moving AI sector, their business models, financial health, and customer bases offer investors starkly different paths.

BigBear.ai provides specialized decision-intelligence and autonomy software tailored for government and commercial sectors. It provides biometric and data analysis solutions, with a major concentration of contracts adding a layer of risk.

On a trailing-12-month (TTM) basis through the June quarter, revenue reached nearly $142 million, which represents a decline of approximately 1.5% compared to the prior year.

The company reported a net loss of close to $180 million. This performance indicates that expenses significantly exceeded total sales.

As of its June 2026 balance sheet, the current ratio is approximately 4.9x, measuring the ability to cover short-term debts with current assets. The debt-to-equity ratio was 0, as the company carries no debt.

TTM free cash flow, which is cash from operations minus capital expenditures, was negative $73 million, indicating the business consumes more cash than it generates.

Nebius Group positions itself as an AI cloud company, building the full-stack infrastructure required for complex machine learning tasks. Its platform supports AI builders across healthcare and financial services by offering tools for model training and deployment. The company operates a global footprint with data centers and roughly 400 AI engineers to maintain its competitive position.

As of the June quarter, TTM revenue reached nearly $1.4 billion, representing a significant 444% year-over-year increase. Net income improved to $62 million, up from under $10 million for the full year 2025. This performance shows a swing to profitability following significant net losses in previous years, driven by high demand for cloud services.

Based on its June 2026 balance sheet, the current ratio is approximately 4.0x, while the debt-to-equity ratio is roughly 1.0x. The company's total debt was approximately $8.6 billion, with an additional $1.5 billion in lease liabilities. This was partially offset by $8 billion of cash and equivalents.

TTM free cash flow was negative $5.9 billion, reflecting the capital-intensive nature of the company's AI infrastructure. Note that stock-based compensation accounted for roughly 21.6% of operating cash flow, inflating reported cash generation because it is a non-cash expense.

BigBear.ai faces significant risks from revenue concentration, as a large portion of its business depends on a limited number of government contracts. These agreements are subject to budgetary fluctuations and may be terminated at any time for convenience.

Nebius is funding massive capital spending needs. Heavy capital investment for data centers has resulted in negative free cash flow, and a slowdown in AI demand remains the primary risk.

BigBear.ai appears cheaper based on sales over the past twelve months, while Nebius is the only one with a positive forward P/E using future earnings estimates.

Valuation metrics include those sourced from Financial Modeling Prep (FMP) and may differ from those of other data providers.

I would buy Nebius over BigBear right now. BigBear has a multi-year history of flat revenue. It reported $146 million in revenue in 2021, and $128 million in 2025. It has reported a net loss across that period. The company has to compete for government contracts, while Nebius has seen its revenue explode due to a shortage of data center capacity and high demand.

Nebius is seeing immediate demand for its infrastructure as soon as it is brought online. Additionally, Nebius benefits from pricing power, with recent bids for compute capacity fetching higher prices than before.

Analysts expect BigBear to continue reporting losses over the next few years, while expecting Nebius to continue growing earnings over the long term as it brings more capacity online.

The risk for Nebius is that the stock trades at high multiples of sales and earnings. A lot of growth is priced in, so anything that stalls the AI build-out could hurt the stock. The stock has already fallen 38% in the last month over concerns about AI spending.

Still, Nebius has a scarce asset that is in high demand, and that scarcity is being validated by explosive revenue growth and improving profitability. That's the stock I would rather hold for 2027 and beyond.
