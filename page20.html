Top 9 friendliest EFL Championship fanbases named and ranked by AI

It's always nice to have a good reputation. In the Championship, there are some places that fans enjoy going to a little more than others.

That could be due to the size of the away end or where it's situated in the ground itself, the city or town you get to visit, or even the ability to have a beer or two with someone who supports the home team without feeling threatened in any way.

It doesn't just stop at an away-day experience, though. A community can be created within the home section, and the growth of a fanbase can often be put down to how welcoming and friendly the current supporters are, to the point where even neutrals can attend games and feel that they are part of something.

Earlier this month, Football League World asked AI to rank the nine toughest Championship fanbases. While this isn't the complete opposite, these fans are, per AI's admission, the friendliest in the second tier.

Evidently, it's not the opposite, as Derby County feature in both the toughest Championship fanbases and, indeed, open up the ranking on the friendliest list, too.

The Rams have a loyal following, but they've experienced heartache in the recent past, which helped bring everyone together. Derby owner David Clowes, a lifelong supporter who saved the club from administration after Mel Morris departed, stated that they were a week away from going bust, which must be incredibly sobering to hear as fans.

That's created a sense of community among the supporter base at Pride Park, and it's led to away fans being accepted a little more in the city than they perhaps were beforehand.

Plus, their recent win over Bolton Wanderers aside, away days have been pretty fun for travelling fans this season, so they've enjoyed their visit to Derby for that reason, too.

There's a theme brewing already in the infancy of this ranking, as Charlton Athletic have also undergone some ownership troubles which have united the fanbase and created even more of a community amongst them.

The Addicks weren't close to going bust by any means, but they've had five different owners in six years, and that's made even more alarming by the fact that the GFP group have been in charge since 2023.

Additionally, Charlton are one of the few clubs in this division that know not to expect much in terms of a promotion or play-off charge this season. That can make their fanbase a little less reactionary after a defeat and a bit less bitter toward opposition fans after a game.

That being said, they're still passionate, and that creates a mutual respect between certain fanbases and a subsequent place on the list.

You can often find that supporter groups of London-based clubs who aren't the traditional giants of the game have a bit more of a community-based following.

QPR, like others, don't attract loads of tourists each game, and with the tight atmosphere at Loftus Road, you get the feeling that almost everyone in each row knows each other and connects with them, no matter what walk of life they're from.

They, too, have experienced the highest highs and the more frequent lows that a Championship side has to offer, and despite not being in the Premier League for over a decade now, attendances and support for the club haven't waned.

The community is there to see, and it creates a friendly atmosphere that some other London clubs simply don't have.

Lincoln City fans are enjoying the ride, with this their first second-tier campaign since the 1960/61 term. It's been made even better given the fact that they were in the National League just a decade ago.

The Imps are heading to grounds and welcoming clubs to Sincil Bank that they could only dream of competing with a lifetime ago, and that rise from obscurity to the Championship and the underdog mentality surrounding them have given the club plenty of neutral support.

And it's that mentality which makes them a relatively popular fanbase. Like Charlton, their primary goal is survival, so even if they lose 6-0 against West Ham United at home with nine men, things don't sour, and the back-and-forth banter with Hammers fans during that game just showcases why they're on this list.

Portsmouth have a passionate fanbase; there's no doubt about that, but AI believes that they're among the friendliest, too.

The South Coast side has to welcome away fans who have travelled plenty far to get there, and they make sure that the experience is as hospitable as it can be, albeit hopefully with a result which sends them home far from happy.

The away end at Fratton Park is one of the more fun ones to watch on TV, with the entire Milton End available for away supporters, and when that's packed, alongside a close-to-capacity home section, it makes for an entertaining game.

Pompey have also gone through the wringer financially and are working their way back to their former glory. That history of the fanbase banding together to help the club through difficult times is enough for AI to put them in the top five of this list.

As of writing, the atmosphere around Bristol City right now is incredibly toxic, with head coach Michael Skubala under some pressure after a 5-0 loss at Ashton Gate against Norwich City to lead into the international break.

The Canaries more than enjoyed their trip to Bristol because of that, but the Robins fans are well-renowned for being hospitable, while also providing passion and a desire to see the team improve.

Perhaps that run to the play-offs under Liam Manning in 2024/25 has created some extra expectation, and Bristol City fans may not be as welcoming to an opposition fan trying to engage with them after a defeat, but for now, they rank highly in AI's estimation.

Per the fan engagement index from last season, Swansea City were the highest-ranked Championship side, meaning that the fanbase in South Wales is one of the most content with the state of the club.

Ultimately, that's always going to create a friendly atmosphere, as their outlook on the club is as positive as anyone else's in the division. There are plenty of reasons for that, but the current ownership group allowing for celebrity co-owner involvement and investment will be the main catalyst.

Glen Kamara scored the winner for QPR against Lincoln City on Saturday, a sentence that has very rarely been said throughout the Finnish midfielder's career.

His lovely left-footed effort was his first club goal since January 2023, when he netted against St Johnstone for Rangers. QPR is the fourth club Kamara has played for since that goal, and his second in England.

Indeed, the 31-year-old spent a solitary year at Leeds United during their 2023/24 run to the play-off final under Daniel Farke.

He quickly became an important player for the Whites, starting 33 times and starting in all three play-off games, too, but was sold the following summer to Stade Rennais, his current parent club, during this loan spell at Loftus Road.

Kamara is quickly becoming a well-liked character in the R's fold, but his goalscoring ventures at the weekend was something that not even they could see coming when they secured his services for the year.

Kamara isn't exactly a midfielder famed for his ability to crash the box. The 31-year-old is a cultured veteran who is very good at keeping the play ticking and winning duels at a high rate.

And, ultimately, in a Leeds side that possessed the attacking talents of Crysencio Summerville, Georginio Rutter, Wilfried Gnonto, Dan James and Joel Piroe, he didn't need to be relied on to score goals as the number eight in Daniel Farke's side back in 2023/24.

So much so that, in 2,709 minutes of Championship football during his sole campaign at Elland Road, Kamara took just five shots. That's an average of one shot every 541 minutes, or just over six games per shot.

Even with all that quality ahead of him, that's still quite the record, and it became almost a running joke amongst Leeds fans that Kamara was almost allergic to letting one fly from the edge of the area, given how often he liked to move the play on.

For those Leeds fans who still keep an eye on what's happening in the Championship while they currently sit pretty in the top half of the Premier League, seeing Kamara's name on the scoresheet for QPR would have been quite a surprise.

What would have caught them even more off-guard, however, was the ability he showed to take the ball down with his back to goal, turn with his first touch, and curl one into the far corner with his weaker left foot.

The Finnish international has shown a little more attacking intent in a Hoops shirt, albeit not enough to see him top the goalscoring charts this year. His eight shots and three on target are already more than he managed in the entire 2023/24 campaign.

With finishes like that, he might be urged to take more shots as the season progresses.

It's taken a little while for Julien Stephan to settle on his starting midfield pivot this year, but it seems like Kamara is becoming the first-choice option there, whether that be next to Nicolas Madsen or Jonathan Varane.

The Dane's injury is making things a little easier at the moment, but as and when he returns, you get the feeling that it'll be Varane who makes way, rather than the 31-year-old.

However, they currently occupy that final position in the top eight, and if the Hoops can stay relatively injury-free over the next six months, they could well maintain that.

Banks don't always have the most exciting branding, but Brazil's Caixa Econômica Federal, a huge state-owned financial institution with 165 years of history, has a good go. The bank's rebranded for the first time in almost three decades, and the new identity ticks all the boxes.

The new identity is a fine example of a redesign done right. It feels more modern, more human and approachable, and it remains instantly recognisable, maintaining a clever hidden symbolism.

The Caixa rebrand was the work of the Brazilian agency Tátil. It looked to the bank's history and its existing identity, kept the elements that people most recognised, and turned them up.

The previous logo design had lasted 29 years, so it was doing something right, but it was starting to look drab and dated with the old corporate feel of those muted colours and slightly italicised all-caps letters.

The rebrand turns the colours up with more modern, vibrant hues. Tátil suggests the blue represents enduring solidity while the orange reflects the diversity of Brazil.

The wordmark gets a cleaner lowercase design, and the core X motif, which features in the wordmark and also stands alone, gets a significant refresh.

Jack Grealish's Everton travails could signal a death knell to the 'Gazza generation'

When you purchase through links on our site, we may earn an affiliate commission. Here’s how it works.

Deep down, Jack Grealish will know that his fate extends beyond Everton's season.

English football’s first £100m player has won multiple Premier League titles and a Champions League yet still faces a fight to stave off the death knell to an increasingly lost generation as much as his own elite-level career.

Grealish again finds himself out in the cold after a ‘breach of club discipline’ in the run-up to Everton’s Carabao Cup win over Newcastle and was subsequently deemed unfit for the league rematch at St James’ Park on Monday night. On both occasions, David Moyes’s side were hardly poorer without the winger’s presence.

The Toffees are yet to decide on a definitive course of action over Grealish’s reported unauthorised absence from training, but patience is now in increasingly short supply from the club and its supporters alike. At a time when Moyes is dealing with crippling squad dearth - just 18 senior players were left after a disastrous summer window - the England international's latest misdemeanour threw him another needless curveball.

There was always a risk that Grealish's propensity to hit the headlines for the wrong reasons would again rear its head on Merseyside, where he claimed that feeling ‘loved’ in his initial loan spell was a driving factor in embarking on a second. Such was a fondness for his current surroundings that he undertook the bulk of his rehabilitation from a season-ending foot injury sustained in January at their Finch Farm training ground instead of returning to parent club Manchester City.

But even then, indiscretion was still lurking round the corner. When Everton slipped to a late April defeat at relegation candidates West Ham, Grealish was being snapped slumped against the arm of a chair in a Manchester rooftop bar surrounded by bottles and glasses. Now 31, he is in danger of completing an unholy trinity that was once touted for greatness as successors to Paul Gascoigne’s throne but allowed their careers to crash and burn.

Just 16 months separate the respective birth dates of the Birmingham-born talent, Dele Alli and Raheem Sterling. All three were alive when English football’s flawed genius enjoyed one last hurrah at Euro 96 but none will have any memories of it. That has still done little to stop their affection for ‘Gazzamania’ running deep.

Upon his return to Everton this summer, Grealish revealed he was partly inspired to take the No.18 shirt worn by Gascoigne during a forgettable and fleeting spell in the blue half of Merseyside while Sterling also cited the man that personified the 1990 World Cup as his all-time favourite Three Lions player. Even Dele, who himself previously sought redemption at Everton, made an embarrassing attempt at recreating the infamous ‘dentist’s chair’ celebration during a training session at Euro 2016.

They also appeared destined to follow in his footsteps by earning moves to high-profile leagues overseas with Real Madrid heavily touted as a future destination for each player. Beyond personal homages, Gascoigne has become intertwined with the trio as a quickfire buzzword for their respective rises to prominence. Individual billings as the ‘new Gazza’ also proved to be a self-fulfilling prophecy for a penchant for self-destruction mirroring the downfall of the former Rangers and Lazio playmaker. Dele is now 14 months into unofficial retirement and resorting to calling up former teammates live on Twitch streams in a desperate ploy to stay in the public consciousness. An awkward attempted catch-up with Harry Kane, just weeks before his old Tottenham sparring partner claimed the Ballon d’Or, lasted barely 60 seconds.

The depth of Dele's continental ambitions was short-lived spells with Besiktas and Como, making a solitary appearance for the latter before being released barely midway through an 18-month contract. Free agent Sterling fared far worse with a three-month spell at Feyenoord which was terminated last season. Grealish's former City teammate and fellow Euro 2020 standout faces the real prospect of ending this month behind bars as well as without a club after pleading guilty to dangerous driving while inhaling nitrous oxide and crashing his Lamborghini on the A327 in May.

Without sufficient intervention on his own part, Grealish may soon follow his former contemporaries in falling by the footballing wayside. More than most, he has been the poster boy for Gascoigne's heirs, as the man himself confirmed to FourFourTwo last year by singling out the former Aston Villa graduate as his closest embodiment in the current crop of players. His exploits in City’s 2023 treble-winning campaign hinted that England’s first Ballon d’Or winner since Michael Owen was closer than Kane’s recent triumph but never came to pass. What happens next will be era-defining.

Grealish is still capable of technical excellency, as demonstrated in a truncated first loan spell with Everton, if he can stave off personal indiscipline issues. He would not be the first homegrown talent to do so. Ross Barkley, another ex-teammate born in the mid-90s, was also festooned with the Gascoigne moniker yet managed to overcome his own off-field issues to earn a second chance at Villa. Persistent injuries prevented the playmaker from scaling even a fraction of the heights he was originally tipped to reach but used stints at Nice and Luton Town to rebuild his reputation and earn a bit-part role with Unai Emery’s side.

By personal admission, Grealish has been his own worst enemy when it comes to a sense of timing off the pitch which is at odds with an often impeccable nature on it. Unless he can conquer those social demons which ultimately brought down his idol as well as those in the same talent pool, the epitaph of the 'Gazza generation' will be one of frustratingly unfulfilled potential.

TL;DR: NEAR AI has published a security incident report involving an SGLang vulnerability in legacy deployments. The company says it revoked exposed credentials and addressed the issue, while acknowledging that some requests could have been observed. Its findings distinguish confirmed exposure from data it has no evidence was accessed.

A security disclosure from NEAR AI highlights a familiar danger in the expanding AI-agent industry: sophisticated applications can inherit serious problems from the infrastructure they run on.

In a November 9 incident report, the organization described an SGLang remote-code-execution vulnerability affecting legacy deployment infrastructure. The investigation identified roughly 100 user details and 18 agents in the affected context, with two Cloud API keys exposed and subsequently revoked.

The company said there was no evidence that stored Private Chat content had been compromised. It also cautioned that some requests made on November 8 could potentially have been observed. Those are not contradictory statements: investigators may find no evidence of one category of access while being unable to rule out another.

AI applications are assembled from multiple components: model servers, request routers, authentication systems, logging and the user-facing product. SGLang is part of the infrastructure used to serve language models. A flaw at that level can create opportunities for attackers before a user ever interacts with an onchain wallet or signs a transaction.

Remote-code-execution issues can be particularly serious because they may allow an attacker to run unauthorized instructions in the affected environment. The scope of what follows depends on the privileges, network access and data available to that environment.

For readers, this means the incident should not be conflated with a vulnerability in the NEAR blockchain consensus rules or a general compromise of all NEAR wallets. The report concerns particular AI-serving infrastructure and its potential exposures.

According to the published account, the team investigated affected deployments, revoked the two exposed API keys, remediated the vulnerability and notified impacted parties. Those are necessary containment steps, though the effectiveness of remediation depends on whether other pathways remain open and whether credentials are rotated wherever they were used.

The report’s numerical counts also need careful interpretation. Approximately 100 user details and 18 agents do not mean 100 users lost funds or that every associated request was read. Exposure, confirmed access and financial loss are separate findings that should not be collapsed into one sensational claim.

Crypto firms are integrating AI into development, support, trading and operational workflows. An AI tool may eventually handle private business information even when it cannot directly sign onchain transactions. That raises the value of conventional safeguards: isolating services, limiting credentials, monitoring access and maintaining incident-response plans.

The broader institutional push toward blockchain infrastructure, including the NYSE’s tokenized-securities plans, depends on more than smart-contract security. Offchain services must also be robust. A well-audited ledger cannot compensate for a compromised application server.

Likewise, rising institutional attention to crypto assets, which NewsBTC tracks through developments such as Ether ETF flows, makes operational controls increasingly consequential. These links are contextual rather than a claim that those products were involved in this incident.

Affected users should rely on NEAR AI’s direct notifications and published guidance. Where they have used credentials in related services, reviewing access permissions and rotating keys is prudent. Users should not assume that ordinary NEAR wallet keys have been exposed simply because the company reported an infrastructure flaw.

The most important follow-up is a clear technical postmortem explaining affected versions, root cause and validation of the fixes. For companies building AI agents around finance, the episode is a reminder that infrastructure security and candid disclosure are both part of earning trust.

Ocarina Of Time Remake Sells 5.2 Million Copies In 4 Days

📅 Nov 11, 2026  |  Category: World  |  Read Source →

It feels a little like everyone has been playing nothing but The Legend of Zelda: Ocarina of Time for the last few days, and a first look at its sales figures might explain why it feels that way. 5.2 million copies sold in its first four days, putting it way up there in the Zelda sales rankings before the end of its first week.

In a statement shared by Nintendo (thanks, Wario64), the studio confirmed the Ocarina of Time Remake's massive launch sales, noting that 810,000 of those sales were in Japan. It also confirms that this is the fastest-selling Switch 2 exclusive so far, outpacing Donkey Kong Bananza, Pokemon Pokopia, and even Mario Kart World, which launched with the console.

And came bundled along with it for a lot of people.

You might see this news and say to yourself, "Well, of course a Zelda game has sold this well this fast," but Zelda games selling this well is still a fairly new phenomenon. So new that the Ocarina remake selling 5.2 million copies makes it the eighth best-selling game in the series after just four days, right above Phantom Hourglass, which sold 4.76 million, and right behind the Nintendo 3DS version of Ocarina, which sold 6.44 million copies.

Some of the bigger Zelda games the Ocarina of Time remake has managed to outsell in less than a week include A Link to the Past (4.61 million), The Wind Waker (4.43 million), A Link Between Worlds (4.26 million), and Skyward Sword (4.15 million). Now all that remains to be seen is just how high up the Zelda rankings the new Ocarina can climb.

Another 1.31 million copies, which it will likely hit within the next few days, and it will have outsold the original The Legend of Zelda on the NES. Hard to believe considering its status among video games as a whole, but the remake is already within touching distance of the original Ocarina of Time, which sold 7.6 million copies. The Nintendo 64's limited sales are largely to blame for that. The N64 finished its run having sold 32 million units. The Switch 2 will likely move past that during this coming holiday season since more than 26 million of them have been sold already.

That means around one in five Switch 2 owners bought Ocarina of Time within four days of its release.

Twilight Princess is the third best-selling Zelda game having sold 8.93 million copies across both its GameCube and Wii versions. It's whether the Ocarina remake can sell enough to breathe the same rarified air as this new era's Zelda games, Breath of the Wild and Tears of the Kingdom, and based on its launch numbers, it almost certainly can.

Breath of the Wild was the game that took the series to new heights and set a new standard for Zelda, launching with the Switch and selling almost 35 million copies. Tears of the Kingdom comfortably became the second best-selling Zelda game at 21.73 million copies. Ocarina might be big enough to hit those numbers too, especially as more Switch 2s are sold and along with the likes of Mario Kart World and Pokopia, it just becomes one of the games everyone feels like they have to buy when they get the console.

Endrick is expected to leave Real Madrid again in the January transfer window. A Champions League club has been installed as favourites to land him.

After a successful loan at Lyon in the second half of last season, the Brazilian striker returned to Real Madrid, but the competition in attack is too great. A departure seems increasingly likely.

Asked about his future on Tuesday, Endrick made no secret of his intention to play more. ‘I don’t know what will happen in January’ - he conceded, before adding: ‘I just want to play, I want to be happy, help my teammates, score goals, provide assists. According to AS, the former Palmeiras player feels good at Real Madrid and gets on well with Jose Mourinho, but he needs to leave to continue his progress. ‘The situation would have to change radically for Endrick not to leave in January’, says the Spanish newspaper.

Currently, less than two months before the January transfer window opens, the Real Madrid player is preparing his departure and looking for a new destination.

A rebound like the one offered by Lyon last winter is being sought, where he would have guaranteed playing time and the chance to play European matches.

Roma are interested in Endrick’s profile, but Sporting represent a formidable competitor in this race. The Portuguese club have already positioned themselves to sign the Brazilian in the second half of the season.
