Gritt and MasTec bring bolt-on construction robots to solar sites

📅 Nov 10, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

MasTec will put Gritt's robot arms on its US solar farms, months after Burns & McDonnell signed up.

Infrastructure builder MasTec will deploy Gritt’s AI robotic attachments on its utility-scale solar projects across several US states, after testing them on live sites. It follows a similar deal with Burns & McDonnell in August. No financial terms were disclosed.

MasTec, one of North America’s largest infrastructure builders, will use Gritt’s AI-powered robots on its solar farms, the two companies said on Tuesday. Neither disclosed financial terms.

Gritt makes robotic arms that bolt onto equipment crews already use, such as skid steers and forklifts, to lift and place solar panels. MasTec tested the systems on live sites, where they helped install “megawatts” of panels. The companies now plan a pipeline of projects across several US states.

“We’ve evaluated Gritt systems on active construction sites, and we see an opportunity to pair our teams’ expertise with technology that supports them in the field,” said Rob Turner, vice president of construction at MasTec Renewables.

It is Gritt’s second named partner in three months. Burns & McDonnell signed a similar deal in August after a year of trials, Solar Power World reported.

When Gritt raised $32M in July, it said it worked with three of the top 10 US power construction firms but did not name them. It said its robots let crews place 3,000 to 4,000 panels a day instead of about 800.

At the time, Gritt had only two systems in the field. Its website now lists more than seven job sites.

Construction firms are turning to robots as skilled workers retire. Procore agreed to buy drone maker DroneDeploy for $845M in July, and welding startup Path Robotics raised $300M on Tuesday.

Trump's first Iran war escalation post-midterm losses seeks to choke off Tehran’s trade

📅 Nov 10, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Farzin Nadimi explains how the U.S. could target Iran’s remaining land routes — and why sealing them off will be a "mid- to long-term project."

President Donald Trump isn't backing off his pressure campaign against Iran despite claims the conflict contributed to sweeping Republican losses in last week's midterm elections.

In fact, Trump is doubling down by expanding U.S. efforts to choke off the trade routes Tehran increasingly relies on as it tries to rebuild its military in the midst of war.

The new effort, dubbed Task Force Iron Gate, takes aim at a vulnerability that has persisted despite the U.S. naval blockade: the land and Caspian Sea routes Iran can use to bring in goods, dual-use technology and military supplies beyond the reach of American warships.

That approach, one expert claims, fits into a broader administration strategy of tightening the squeeze on Tehran while stopping short of restarting full-scale military operations. Meanwhile, others caution that it could produce escalation rather than an immediate diplomatic breakthrough.

An Iranian cargo truck crosses into the Pakistan-Iran border as an Iranian national flag (L) flies at half-mast at Taftan, Balochistan province on March 1, 2026 after the death of Iran's supreme leader Ayatollah Ali Khamenei amid US-Israel strikes. (Banaras KHAN / AFP via Getty Images)

"We have Operation Iron Gate that just went into effect. That means nothing comes in by land," Trump said Sunday.

But the roughly 20-person task force established at U.S. Central Command headquarters in Tampa is taking a quieter approach than Trump’s sweeping description might suggest.

"This is not a deployable special operation task force," Farzin Nadimi, a senior fellow at the Washington Institute for Near East Policy, told Fox News Digital, describing Iron Gate instead as a "fusion cell" coordinating military, civilian and intelligence efforts.

Nadimi said its immediate role is likely to center on gathering intelligence about activity along Iran’s borders and building cooperation in the region that could support sanctions and other efforts to disrupt the flow of goods.

Secretary of State Marco Rubio, who heads the department responsible for spearheading the administration's diplomatic foreign agenda, supports taking action that could decrease military escalation.

"We don't need to conduct military strikes if we're achieving the same objective through economic means and economic pressure," Rubio told reporters on Tuesday.

He rejected the suggestion that the U.S. and Iran were at a stalemate, arguing that sanctions, the naval blockade and other measures were steadily weakening Tehran.

"They’re bleeding. They’re bleeding economically," Rubio said. "It is an increased pressure on Iran that continues to grow every day."

The push comes a week after an election in which the war proved a political liability for Republicans.

The Iran war was a crucial factor in the House vote for 34% of voters, according to the Fox News Voter Poll, while about six in 10 said the U.S. had made the wrong decision to take military action against Iran.

An Iranian oil tanker is seen floating on the Caspian Sea, as Iranian President Mohammad Khatami attends the opening ceremony of the Neka oil terminal on the Caspian Sea, 340 kms northwest of Tehran in Mazandaran province. (HENGHAMEH FAHIMI/AFP via Getty Images)

A separate Fox News/NORC survey conducted around Election Day found 68% said avoiding a prolonged military conflict in Iran was very important. When forced to choose, 61% said ending U.S. military involvement to lower gas prices was more important than restarting military action to stop Iran from developing nuclear weapons, while 35% chose restarting military action.

Yet four days after the election, CENTCOM announced Iron Gate, extending the administration’s pressure campaign to the land routes Tehran could use to blunt the effects of the naval blockade.

CENTCOM said the task force would coordinate efforts to disrupt advanced weapons, dual-use technologies, sanctioned materials and illicit revenue moving across Iran’s land borders and the Caspian Sea.

"We will not allow the Iranian regime to rebuild its weapons factories or replenish its arsenal with materials smuggled across its borders," CENTCOM commander Adm. Brad Cooper said in announcing the task force.

CENTCOM declined to provide additional details about how Iron Gate would operate beyond its announcement.

The challenge is the sheer scale of the territory involved.

Iran shares land borders with Afghanistan, Armenia, Azerbaijan, Iraq, Pakistan, Turkey and Turkmenistan, spanning roughly 6,000 kilometers, according to Nadimi. While it has dozens of official border crossings, goods can also be smuggled through mountainous and desert terrain.

"It will be extremely difficult to know where and how things get in or out of Iran," Nadimi said.

Much of Iron Gate’s effectiveness will also depend on governments outside Iran.

Nadimi said the task force could build relationships with military and intelligence services in neighboring countries to identify and disrupt suspicious shipments. Turkey and Pakistan have already placed some limits on goods moving across their borders with Iran, he said, contributing to long truck lines and higher shipping costs.

But even if neighboring governments cooperate, Iran and the Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps are likely to look for ways around the restrictions.

Behnam Ben Taleblu, senior director of the Iran program at the Foundation for Defense of Democracies, said the effort should not be judged by whether Washington can completely eliminate Iran’s overland trade.

"That is not going to mean zeroing out this trade overnight," Taleblu told Fox News Digital. Instead, he said the goal is to increase the cost of moving goods, pressure the foreign companies and individuals facilitating Iranian trade and give Washington more leverage over Tehran.

"This is literally akin to boxing in the Islamic Republic," he said.

The routes have taken on added importance as Iran’s other avenues to the outside world have narrowed.

Taleblu said Iran’s missile program remains dependent on foreign inputs including fuel precursors and high-strength steel. China and Russia are important to those flows, he said, but Iraq, Turkey and Pakistan also represent important regional routes.

The U.S. military has been enforcing a naval blockade in the Strait of Hormuz, even during the ceasefire. (U.S. Central Command)

Nadimi described effectively restricting Iran’s land trade as a "mid- to long-term project" and predicted Tehran would initially "become more aggressive" as the pressure mounts.

"It is entirely plausible that you squeeze them hard enough that they feel like the only way to throw off the strangulation is to throw a punch," he said.

That leaves the administration trying to increase its leverage over Iran without reigniting the full-scale fighting voters strongly signaled they wanted to avoid — while Trump has publicly described the objective in much starker terms.

D.C. Defends Ticket Resale Cap Amid Lawsuit from SeatGeek, Vivid Seats, TickPick & Gametime

📅 Nov 10, 2026  |  Category: Business  |  Read Source →

Secondary ticketing marketplaces and their trade group, Ticket Policy Forum, are seeking to block the district's newly enacted RESALE Act.

The District of Columbia says it should be allowed to cap concert ticket resale prices in the face of a legal challenge brought by a coalition of secondary marketplace operators.

Resale trade group Ticket Policy Forum, along with its members SeatGeek, Vivid Seats, TickPick and Gametime, sued last month to block D.C.’s newly enacted Restricting Egregious Scalping Against Live Entertainment (RESALE) Act as unconstitutional. Scheduled to become enforceable in 2027, the RESALE Act limits secondary ticket price markups at 10% above face value.

The resellers argue that a 10% cap would force them to “operate at a loss” and actually raise consumer prices by driving ticket sales to the black market. They want a judge to issue an immediate injunction barring enforcement of the law. But in a response brief on Monday (Nov. 9), the D.C. Attorney General’s Office says that is both unnecessary and unfair.

“The will of the people plus their interests in protecting consumers and a vital, cultural industry far outweigh the pecuniary interests of a handful of corporations,” write the government lawyers. “Plaintiffs include multi-billion-dollar companies that can easily absorb any hypothesized losses from complying with one city’s law while litigation proceeds. Plaintiffs may later be able to recover those losses, but the harms to the district and its people from a preliminary injunction are truly irreparable.”

D.C.’s attorneys say their resale cap was properly enacted after residents “became fed up with soaring ticket prices,” and that the bill “sailed through” both local government and congressional approval even as ticket resellers lobbied hard to kill it. “Striking out with the democratic process, Plaintiffs dressed their policy objections up in constitutional garb and turned to this court for a judicial veto,” reads Monday’s response brief.

According to D.C., there’s simply no merit to the ticketing companies’ claims that the RESALE Act is unconstitutional. Among other arguments, the resellers have said there’s a First Amendment issue with a portion of the law that requires them to list a ticket’s original, face value cost next to the resale rate. The companies claim this is unlawful “compelled speech” — but D.C. says that argument “strains credulity.”

“The original ticket price is hardly irrelevant to a consumer — without it, the consumer cannot fully understand the value of the resold ticket and make an informed decision about whether to buy it,” write the district’s attorneys.

A hearing on the injunction motion is scheduled for Nov. 19 in D.C. federal court. In a statement to Billboard on Tuesday (Nov. 10), the Ticket Policy Forum said, “The court should bar enforcement of the RESALE Act to prevent the significant and lasting damage it would cause.”

“The law violates the dormant Commerce Clause and D.C. Home Rule Act by regulating sales outside of D.C.; the price and fee caps in the law and arbitrary, confiscatory and violate the Due Process Clause of the U.S. Constitution; and the law compels speech that marketplaces cannot verify in violation of the First Amendment,” added the trade group. “Enforcement of the act also will drive ticket sales into the shadows, subjecting fans to greater risks from street-side scams and increased fraud.”

D.C. is among more than a dozen local governments that have made moves in recent years to cap ticket resale prices. Such laws have passed in Maine and Vermont, and similar bills are under consideration by statehouses in Massachusetts, New York and Tennessee. A proposed resale cap failed in the California legislature this summer after resellers spent millions of dollars lobbying against it.

The new BMW i3 just proved how efficient an EV can be after a nearly 3,700-mile road trip

📅 Nov 10, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Who says long EV road trips mean hours at charging stations? BMW drove the new i3 for over 3 days, covering nearly 3,700 miles, and it took less than 3 hours of charging.

BMW put the i3 to the ultimate test of efficiency, charging speed, and long-distance EV capability on a nearly 3,700-mile Pan-European journey.

The Paneurpeana, as it’s called, is considered the benchmark for range and charging speed. The route begins at the North Cape in Norway, the northernmost point of continental Europe you can reach by car, and ends in Tarifa, Spain, the southernmost point of mainland Europe.

BMW drove the new i3 50 xDrive for a total of 3,658 miles (5,886.7 km) across eight countries and spent just 2 hours, 45 minutes, and 54 seconds charging at an average of 275 kW.

The performance beat the previous record by more than seven hours, according to Mike Reichelt, head of BMW’s Neue Klasse

BMW said the charging points were not predetermined and that it had to account for external factors such as weather and traffic jams.

The new i3 was impressively efficient during the trip, consuming an average of just 12.5 kWh per 100 km, beating its official WLTP combined energy consumption of 18-13.4 kWh/100 km.

BMW said the trip took 3 days, 2 hours, and 41 minutes, with an average speed of 79.7 km/h, and that the i3 was charged exclusively at public charging points across national charging networks.

The vehicle was driven at all times by a single driver and was not modified in any way, in terms of hardware or software, from the standard i3 available for purchase.

BMW’s new i3 uses a 108.7 kWh battery and Gen6 eDrive system that provides a WLTP range of up to 566 miles (912 km).

With a drag coefficient of just 0.21, the new i3 is the most aerodynamically efficient BMW vehicle to date.

In the US, BMW said the new 2027 i3 50 xDrive will offer an EPA-estimated driving range of up to 468 miles.

With an 800V architecture, the electric sedan can add nearly 262 miles (423 km) of range (WLTP) in about 10 minutes at DC fast-charging speeds up to 400 kW. In the US, the i3 has a built-in NACS port.

Like the new iX3, the i3 features BMW’s Neue Klasse interior design. The setup consists of BMW’s massive 43″ Panoramic Vision display that spans the base of the windshield and a 17.9″ floating central infotainment display.

The new BMW i3 50 xDrive is on sale in Germany starting at €65,900 with a WLTP range of up to 566 miles (912 km).

In the US, the 2027 BMW i3 50 xDrive and 2027 iX3 50 xDrive are available to order, both starting at $61,500.

United Airlines Executive Says Iconic Dulles Mobile Lounges Will Retire, But “Won’t Be Forgotten.” What Comes Next?

📅 Nov 10, 2026  |  Category: World  |  Read Source →

United Airlines wants Washington Dulles to move beyond its mobile lounges. But when I asked Andrew Nocella whether he actually likes them, his response suggested that retirement may not mean disappearance. Could one become a bar or café? Could a limited transportation role survive? His answer left room for some speculation.

During a press gaggle at Washington Dulles yesterday, I asked United Airlines Executive Vice President and Chief Commercial Officer Andrew Nocella a simple question: does he like the people movers? (it was in the context of their future place at an evolving IAD).

“I love it,” he responded, before explaining:

“The people movers, of course, is part of my history. I can remember, back in the day, coming out to the aircraft through the people mover, so it’s part of my history. But I do understand the need to evolve and change and innovate. And the people movers need to be innovated out. There are better ways to do it. But it will be an important part of the history of Dulles Airport and I can assure you that People Moves, while they will be retired, they won’t be forgotten.”

These vehicles are technically known as mobile lounges, though people movers is a fitting description too and I’ve called them “moon buggies” for years. Whatever you call them, I found that last assurance intriguing, even if we should be careful not to read too much into it.

Nocella did not announce a preservation project or describe a future use. But promising they will not be forgotten invites the question: how might Dulles preserve something so closely associated with the airport while finally moving beyond the need to use it?

My favorite possibility is turning one into a bar or café.

Imagine a retired mobile lounge parked in a suitable location, with its distinctive shape preserved and the interior converted into somewhere to have a drink while watching aircraft. Keep some of the original seating,  add photographs explaining its history, and suddenly an airport frustration becomes an attraction.

I would happily stop for a cappuccino. I suspect plenty of aviation enthusiasts would make a point of visiting too.

There would obviously be practical questions about access, utilities, and where to put it…maybe the wheels would have to be removed. A vehicle designed to transport passengers is not automatically suitable for operating a restaurant. But as a concept, it strikes me as a fun way to preserve a piece of Dulles without requiring anyone to ride it to immigration.

Nostalgia becomes much easier to appreciate when you can choose whether to participate!

Think how cool it would be to take one of these and put it inside the new Polaris Lounge in Concourse E that will be coming in the years ahead.

Another possibility is that the mobile lounges disappear from most journeys but continue in a narrower role.

I wonder, for example, whether there might be a useful way to run passengers between the D and E gates during the airport’s lengthy transition. Could a mobile lounge provide a convenient connection while the rest of the concourse development catches up? It woudl certainly save a lot of walking.

That is a question, not something Nocella proposed and I do realize that keeping an aging fleet operating for a limited purpose could undermine the savings and simplicity that retirement is supposed to deliver.

Still, “retired” can mean different things. Retired from routine passenger service would not necessarily preclude an occasional special ride or some other carefully limited use.

Of course, “won’t be forgotten” may mean something much less elaborate.

A preserved vehicle, a display in the terminal, or a design reference inside a future lounge could honor the mobile lounges without retaining their transportation function.

I would prefer preserving at least one actual vehicle rather than relying exclusively on photographs. These machines are unusual enough that future travelers should have an opportunity to see what generations of Dulles passengers experienced. I still love them and will always love them.

Nocella’s answer was affectionate, but clear about the need for change. He did not offer a retirement date or reveal what remembering the mobile lounges will look like.

Free video-streaming service Tubi lets users share curated watchlists now

📅 Nov 10, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

User-based recommendations could make it easier to find stuff to watch on Tubi.

Tubi users can now create and share playlists of movies and shows available on the streaming service.

Tubi is a free ad-supported streaming television (FAST) service that has both live channels and on-demand content.

Today, the Fox-owned company announced “Mixtapes” as a way for users to make watchlists from Tubi’s “nearly 400,000 movies and TV episodes,” Tubi’s announcement said, adding:

They can give each mixtape a title and description and arrange the entertainment inside in whatever order feels right to them. Viewers can build one entirely on their own, or describe what they’re looking for and let Tubi offer a starting place that they can edit, rearrange, or rebuild entirely. When their mixtape is finished, they can publish and share it with other people or keep it for themselves.

Tubi’s Mixtapes website already features numerous playlists, including: “Millennial Memory Lane”—with movies like Cruel Intentions (1999) and The Fifth Element (1997) and shows such as Everybody Hates Chris (2005–2009) and Degrassi: The Next Generation (2001–2015); and “Bald Baddies”—which includes Fast & Furious Presents: Hobbs & Shaw (2019) and Central Intelligence (2016).

Watching a Mixtape doesn’t require a Tubi account, but making one does.

Tubi’s Mixtapes comes at a time when streaming service providers are seeking a way to address the content fragmentation that’s frequently cited as a frustration among streaming users, as well as users struggling to find something to watch. Gracenote’s “2025 State of Play” report, for instance, surveyed 3,000 consumers in the US, Brazil, France, Germany, Mexico, and the UK and found that “nearly 33 percent of streaming users feel content and service fragmentation negatively impacts their TV experience.”

Users are also likely to stop using a streaming service if they struggle to find something they want to watch, per the survey. Forty-nine percent of users said they “are willing to cancel a service based on difficulty finding what to watch.” Additionally, 19 percent of respondents said they would “abandon a viewing session if their content search is not successful.”

Although Tubi users don’t have paid subscriptions to cancel, making it easier to find relevant content could help Tubi grow its user base and increase the time people spend watching, which supports the advertising business Tubi relies on.

Sharable watchlists are rare among streaming services. Mubi has a similar feature where users can make lists of movies and shows that people can follow, but the lists can feature content that’s not available on Mubi.

Plex and JustWatch also let people create and share curated watchlists with other users.

Still, it’s nice to see a streaming service, especially a free one, add new capabilities at a time when many other streaming services are consistently increasing subscription prices. And while some streaming service companies are turning to AI to help with content recommendations, a human connection may appeal more to certain users, while keeping costs lower for Tubi.

Phishing Threats Totaled 7.3 Billion in Q3 2026

📅 Nov 10, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

E-mail phishing threats reached 7.3 billion in Q3 2026, with September activity increasing 41% from August, according to Microsoft Threat Intelligence.

According to a report from Microsoft Threat Intelligence and the Microsoft Defender Security Research Team, phishing detections reached nearly 3 billion in September alone.

The findings also highlighted changes in attacker techniques, including business-service impersonation, credential theft, and social engineering through Microsoft Teams.

The Redmond-based firm recognized approximately 129 million malicious messages spoofing the sender domains of tracked business services during Q3.

DocuSign was the most frequently spoofed brand in September, accounting for 29% of the month's tracked spoofing activity. Adobe, SurveyMonkey, and Barracuda also experienced increases toward the end of the quarter.

The research found that some malicious messages were delivered despite failing e-mail authentication checks and being identified as threats.

In September, tenant- or user-level security overrides applied to 63% of these messages with a recorded security outcome — most involved tenant-level exceptions.

Microsoft warned that overly broad exceptions intended to support legitimate business communications can weaken protection against impersonation attacks.

The company emphasized that these findings do not establish whether recipients opened the messages or whether accounts were compromised.

Microsoft also recorded approximately 141,000 malicious Teams call attempts during Q3.

Attackers increasingly used ordinary personal names instead of explicitly impersonating IT support personnel. Such display names accounted for 46% of an observed sample in July, rising to 88% in September.

Meanwhile, QR code phishing declined 67% compared to Q2, totaling roughly 12.1 million attacks.

CAPTCHA-gated phishing activity increased toward the end of the quarter, reaching 3.1 million detected attacks in September. SVG attachments played a significant role in the increase.

Credential theft remained the primary objective of malicious attachment campaigns, with approximately 94% to 96% of payload-based attacks directing users to phishing sites or fraudulent sign-in pages.

Microsoft recommended reviewing Exchange Online Protection and Defender for Office 365 configurations, enabling Safe Links and Safe Attachments, and strengthening authentication protections.

The company also advised organizations to review security exceptions and verify the identities of external Teams contacts before responding to suspicious requests.

For more information, go to the Microsoft blog post.

Organizations are running into problems as they give artificial intelligence more control over critical business applications, according to a new survey from identity security firm Delinea.

AI agents are moving from experiments into everyday enterprise environments, but the security controls around them are not keeping pace, according to enterprise identity security company SailPoint.

Cloud Security Alliance's 2026 Top Threats report ranks identity as the leading cloud security concern, followed by two new AI-related risks.
