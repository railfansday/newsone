Warren Buffett's Successor, Greg Abel, Just Plowed $27 Million Into a Virtual Monopoly (No, Not Alphabet!)

📅 Nov 09, 2026  |  Category: Business  |  Read Source →

Berkshire Hathaway’s new boss has found value amid a historically expensive stock market.

It's been a year of significant change for trillion-dollar conglomerate Berkshire Hathaway (BRKA -0.01%)(BRKB -0.25%). Longtime leader Warren Buffett retired as CEO at the end of last year, handing the reins to the company's day-to-day operations and its $364 billion investment portfolio to his understudy, Greg Abel.

Abel wasted little time making his mark. He completely overhauled Berkshire's investment portfolio in the first quarter and built up a significant position in Google parent Alphabet (GOOGL +0.78%)(GOOG +0.78%). But Alphabet isn't the only virtual monopoly that the Oracle of Omaha's successor is piling into. Abel is also adding to Berkshire's already sizable stake in satellite-radio operator Sirius XM Holdings (SIRI -1.67%).

Berkshire's new boss, Greg Abel, has found value amid a historically pricey stock market. Image source: Getty Images.

Usually, investors have to wait until money managers file Form 13F (due Nov. 16) to get an under-the-hood look at what they've been up to. But when beneficial owners (those who hold 10% or more of a public company's outstanding shares) make a trade, they're required to report it via a Form 4 filing within two business days.

Between Oct. 30 and Nov. 3, Abel and his team purchased 924,281 shares of Sirius XM stock for about $27 million. This increased Berkshire Hathaway's stake in the company to more than 125.7 million shares (over 37% of Sirius XM's outstanding shares are held by Berkshire).

Been a while. $BRK.B once again a buyer of $SIRI after 3Q results. 35% owner and significant repurchases coming next year and beyond going to create a $DVA situation where Berkshire quickly heads towards 50% ownership.

The obvious lure of buying Sirius XM stock is its status as a legal monopoly. Although it faces plenty of competition for listeners among terrestrial and online radio providers, Sirius XM is the only licensed satellite-radio operator in the country. This affords it a healthy degree of pricing power over its subscription services.

But it's not just Sirius XM's virtual monopoly status that has Berkshire's current and former bosses intrigued.

A strong argument can be made that Sirius XM's revenue mix is far more important than its monopoly status. Whereas most traditional radio companies rely almost exclusively on advertising revenue to keep the lights on, Sirius XM has generated 21% of its net sales from ads through the first nine months of 2026 and 76% from subscriptions.

Lastly, Sirius XM offers a value proposition that Warren Buffett's protégé has struggled to find amid a historically expensive stock market. Even though Sirius XM's supercharged growth days are in the rearview mirror, mindful spending, coupled with low-single-digit growth and premier subscription pricing power, has the company trading at just 10 times 2027's forecast earnings.

Virtual monopolies are quickly becoming a staple in Berkshire Hathaway's portfolio under Greg Abel.

Solana Targets Durable Nonces After $270M Drift Hack as Pump.fun Backlash Grows

📅 Nov 09, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Solana developers are preparing to remove durable nonces, the transaction feature exploited in April’s $270 million Drift Protocol hack. The proposed change marks a major shift in how the network handles offline approvals, with implications for exchanges and institutional custodians.

The proposal, known as SIMD-0723, would retire a mechanism that allows signed transactions to remain valid indefinitely. Attackers reportedly exploited this functionality to execute pre-approved transactions more than a week after obtaining signatures from Drift’s security council.

Solana wants to kill the feature behind April's $270M+ Drift hack.SIMD-0723, filed Nov 5 by Anza, would retire "durable nonces": signed transactions that never expire. Normal Solana transactions go stale in ~30 seconds. Earliest switch-off: May 5, 2027. pic.twitter.com/VYYttCsvJk

The move comes as Solana faces another challenge closer to its retail ecosystem. Pump.fun is facing growing community criticism over platform fees, influencer promotions, and allegations of hidden-wallet trading. Together, the developments put renewed attention on security, transparency and trust across Solana’s ecosystem.

Normal Solana transactions use recent blockhashes that expire after 30 seconds. Durable nonces replace those temporary references with stored values, allowing transactions to remain executable until the nonce advances.

The feature was designed for legitimate operational needs. Exchanges, custodians, and multisig organizations often require offline signing procedures that take longer than Solana’s normal transaction window.

However, attackers turned that convenience into an attack vector against Drift on April 1, 2026. They reportedly secured administrative approvals through air-gapped signing systems before allowing the transactions to sit for more than a week.

Once submitted, the approvals helped attackers take control of the protocol and drain at least $270 million. Drift subsequently attributed the attack to a North Korean-linked group.

Anza contributor Gabe Rodriguez filed SIMD-0723 on November 5. The proposal would require transactions to use recent blockhashes after activation. May 5, 2027, represents the earliest proposed mainnet activation date. The change still requires technical approval and migration planning.

Solana is also exploring replacement programs that could support offline signing without relying on durable nonces. These alternatives would introduce different authorization and transaction-failure considerations for custodians.

The proposal forces institutional users to reconsider how they authorize transactions across Solana. Meanwhile, questions about trust are becoming harder to ignore elsewhere in the ecosystem.

Pump.fun has attracted fresh criticism following several developments involving its business model and influencer ecosystem.

On November 6, blockchain tracker Lookonchain reported that Pump.fun transferred 76,111 SOL, worth approximately $6.85 million, to Kraken. The movement reignited criticism over platform fees and whether its incentives adequately serve ordinary traders.

Bubblemaps also accused prominent influencers of using undisclosed wallets to purchase tokens before publicly recommending them. Its investigations estimated $125,000 in combined trading profits and promotional rewards involving influencer Ethan.

Separate allegations involving paid streamer collaborations surfaced on November 7. The revelations have intensified questions about transparency within Pump.fun’s promotional ecosystem.

These controversies involve different problems from Drift’s exploit, but they expose a similar challenge: maintaining confidence among participants.

That challenge also creates an interesting opportunity for Bitcoin-focused projects exploring alternative execution environments.

Bitcoin Hyper is developing a Bitcoin Layer 2 using Solana Virtual Machine technology. Its architecture aims to combine SVM’s high-speed execution with Bitcoin-based settlement, bringing smart contracts, decentralized exchanges, and staking functionality to BTC.

Bitcoin Hyper is not simply another Solana application. Its design uses SVM execution while anchoring Layer 2 state to Bitcoin through periodic commitments and zero-knowledge proofs.

This combination gives it a different proposition from Solana-native applications. Developers can work with an execution environment associated with Solana’s performance, while the project targets Bitcoin’s established settlement infrastructure.

Bitcoin Hyper has also reached several development milestones. Its testnet is live, with a public explorer allowing users to inspect transactions and network activity.

Ashton Jeanty had 5 catches for -5 yards on another awful day for Raiders' running backs

📅 Nov 09, 2026  |  Category: Entertainment  |  Read Source →

The Raiders’ running back situation has been awful all season, but it hit a new low in Sunday’s loss to the 49ers.

Ashton Jeanty, the running back the Raiders drafted with the sixth overall pick last year, had a historically bad game catching passes out of the back field, finishing with five catches for negative-five yards.

Yes, that’s negative yardage for the game. Raiders quarterback Kirk Cousins threw seven passes to Jeanty, and these were the results: incomplete, 3 yards, 3 yards, incomplete, -3 yards, -7 yards, -1 yard.

That’s the second-worst performance ever for a player with at least five catches in a game. The only player ever to finish with fewer yards was Jamie Caleb of the 1960 Browns, who somehow finished a game with five catches for -18 yards. It’s unclear how Caleb managed that feat, but Caleb finished his career with seven catches for -26 yards, so he was doing something spectacularly wrong when the ball was thrown to him.

And “spectacularly wrong” describes everything about the Raiders’ running backs this season. In Sunday’s loss, Jeanty was their leading rusher, with 25 yards on six carries. The Raiders’ other running back, Mike Washington, had six carries for negative-three yards. Between the two of them, Jeanty and Washington had 17 touches for 17 yards. One yard per touch is pathetic.

For the season, the Raiders have a total of 222 rushing attempts for 642 yards, a horrendous average of 2.89 yards per carry. They’re the only team in the NFL averaging less than three yards per carry. Nothing is going right for the Raiders when the ball is in the running backs’ hands.
