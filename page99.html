E.l.f. Beauty posts 51% sales increase, raises full-year guidance

📅 2026-11-04  |  Category: Business  |  Read Source →

E.l.f. Beauty on Wednesday blew past Wall Street expectations as it posted a 51% increase in net sales in its second fiscal quarter.

The beauty company said it saw its 31st consecutive quarter of net sales growth, attributing the streak to strong performance across its portfolio. The revenue spike was driven by both retail and e-commerce channels across the U.S. and internationally, the company added.

"The thing I'm most pleased about is we saw strength across our entire portfolio," CEO Tarang Amin told CNBC in an interview. "Every one of our brands contributed strong growth this quarter."

Amin added that E.l.f. is the most-searched brand on TikTok Shop and the No. 2 brand in cosmetics on the platform. The company also said it saw strong results for Rhode, the skincare brand it acquired last year.

The company raised its full fiscal-year guidance following the better-than-anticipated quarter. It now expects net sales in the range of $2 billion to $2.03 billion, compared with a previous range of between $1.94 billion and $1.97 billion. It also upped its adjusted EBITDA projection to a range of $421 million to $427 million from between $401 million and $407 million.

E.l.f. said it now expects adjusted earnings per share for the year to come in between $3.82 and $3.86, exceeding prior guidance of between $3.50 and $3.55 per share. It's also expecting a net income range of $231 million to $233 million, up from a previous range of $212 million to $215 million.

It had already raised its guidance last quarter after outperforming its expectations.

Amin said the company is "very bullish" on the holiday season, expecting high sales through the end of the year.

Here's how the company performed in its second fiscal quarter compared with what Wall Street was expecting, based on a survey of analysts by LSEG:

For the period ended Sept. 30, E.l.f. reported net income of $30.03 million, or 50 cents per share, up significantly from roughly $3 million, or 5 cents per share, the year prior.

E.l.f. also saw significant growth in its international channels, with business up 165%. Amin told CNBC that only 25% of the company's business is outside of the U.S. and that it has a "tremendous opportunity" to build on that momentum.

The company also said its gross margin increased 1.6% during the quarter, including a 0.3% benefit from tariff refunds. Amin previously said the retailer planned to fully reinvest that money into pricing and marketing to continue driving growth.

Though E.l.f. received most of its refunds in its first fiscal quarter, some of those funds flowed through the second quarter, and the company expects to receive a yearly total of around $55 million. Amin told CNBC on Wednesday that the company lowered the price of about 10% of its units by $1 because of the tariff benefits.

"We're seeing great unit momentum based on that," he said.

Palmer Harding Spring 2027 Ready-to-Wear

📅 Nov 04, 2026  |  Category: Business  |  Read Source →

As much as anniversaries are cause for celebration, they naturally also prompt introspection. For Palmer Harding, this season marks 15 years in operation—and their 60th full collection. These are milestones well worth raising a glass to for any independent brand, not least one that has weathered the market instabilities and headwinds that we’ll take your understanding of as a given.

The past year has prompted particularly significant operational developments for the brand – a noteworthy one being the opening of a retail space within its East London studio. In part prompted by the widely attested decline of global wholesale retail, this direct-to-consumer pivot has allowed for an intimate atmosphere that, Matthew Harding says, “feels almost like when we first started; there’s more interaction between us, the clothes and the customer.”

This, in itself, became something of a prompt for their latest collection; less a nostalgic ‘greatest hits’ offering, more a zeroing in on the pieces they’ve seen resonate. “It’s very much a collection about refining and celebrating our key pieces, translating them into new silhouettes, new categories,” Harding adds.

Contemplating what exactly those pieces were didn’t take them too long. “When we actually hunkered down and said, ‘Okay, what are we about?’ The answer was cotton shirts and shirt dresses. When we find a shape, let's just really hammer it home," Palmer says, holding up a hefty poplin button-down with articulated pleating along its oversized sleeve, creating an easy, boomerang bend. First introduced two seasons ago, here, the aptly-named “Intention” shirt returns across a gamut of plain and striped cottons, and as a full-length dress imbued with a satisfying, fanning swish.

Elsewhere, the asymmetrically-hemmed “Super” shirt—“one of our hero pieces that really launched us way back when,” Palmer recalls, “but that we haven't had in the collection for a long time”—showcases the duo’s knack for sculptural cut and drape with its tie-front and cape-like back. Though the piece that showcases that most intriguingly is what Harding dubs the “Precision” shirt, which features origami-like criss-cross pleating at the waist to create a line that skims the form.

There are, granted, more than crisp cotton shirts and shirt dresses on offer here. A denim riff on the “Intention” silhouette stands out, as does a ruby satin dress with swooping poet sleeves. Their insistent focus on their chosen fabric and garment, however, is in itself somewhat allegorical of the alchemical formula that’s spurred their trajectory over the past 15 years. “There's something really wonderful about taking what’s seen as a boring, everyday fabric and elevating it in a really sculptural way,” Palmer says. “The result is something this blend of something that's really understandable, but also very special.” A decade and a half on from setting out with that mission, the eclectic array of women willing to journey far beyond London’s well-trodden luxury shopping lanes to try and buy their wares in person proves that still holds true.

Every stock that moved because of the election

📅 2026-11-04  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

The blue wave across the country has rippled through the stock market too, driving healthcare and clean energy names higher and data center plays and defense companies tumbling.

The broader market soared after Democrats won control of both the House of Representatives and the Senate, and key gubernatorial races in Texas and Georgia. In Washington, the Democratic Congress will now serve as a counterweight to President Donald Trump's agenda, which has included tax cuts and policies that sent inflation, like tariffs and the Iran war.

Local and state elections will also have an impact on data center stocks, with construction limits passed in some states and a Democratic governor-elect in Texas who promised a moratorium.

"Democratic control could slow the push for deregulation in financials, energy and digital assets," Wells Fargo Investment Institute said in a note Wednesday. "We still believe both parties have an incentive to compromise on data center location and AI generally."

Here are some of the biggest movers as a result of the election outcome.

Nextpower jumped more than 11.2 % and Canadian Solar rallied 6.7%, while Plug Power gained 3.6%, First Solar climbed 4.6% and Sunrun ticked 1.3% higher. Nextera Energy added 4.9%. Investors are hoping that Democrats will push back against the Trump administration's rejection of clean energy policies.

Healthcare companies rallied on investor optimism over a slowdown or reversal in healthcare spending cuts, and the potential that enhanced subsidies will extended in Affordable Care Act plans.

Molina Healthcare and Centene gained 8.9% and 12.6%, respectively. HCA Healthcare soared 10.3% and Universal Health Services jumped 10.9%, while Tenet Healthcare rallied 9.6% and Elevance Health added 4.9%.

Elon Musk's SpaceX and Tesla shed 3.8% and 3.3%, respectively, after Democrats won the gubernatorial, Senate, and attorney general races in Texas, as well as wresting control of the Texas House of Representatives . Musk, CEO of both companies, spent at least $474 million backing Republican candidates in the midterms, including $224 million in the Lone Star State, where both Tesla and SpaceX are headquartered.

Defense companies could face headwinds if Democrats challenge military spending. Shares of RTX slipped 2.4%, Lockheed Martin tumbled 6.9%, Northrop Grumman sank 4.5% and Howmet Aerospace fell 4.3%. Palantir Technologies shed 2.1%.

The Trump administration has requested $1.5 trillion for national defense in fiscal 2027. It is also separately asking for an $87.6 billion supplemental appropriation from Congress for fiscal 2026, largely due to the Iran conflict.

Drone stocks also fell. Kratos Defense & Security Solutions dropped 12.3%, while AeroVironment lost 8.3% and Red Cat fell 10.4%. Ondas shed 7% and Unusual Machines tumbled 9%.

Names connected with data centers moved lower amid concern about the potential for restrictions or moratoriums being placed on AI infrastructure buildouts.

Neocloud provider CoreWeave, the poster child for fears over massive AI-related debt, slumped 9.9%. Peers Nebius and Iren dropped 6.7% and 11.2%, respectively.

AI infrastructure stocks also tumbled, with Comfort Systems losing 15.1% and Vistra sliding 13.8%. Micron Technology fell 7%.

That said, five of the Magnificent Seven rallied. Apple rose 5.3% and Microsoft jumped 6.7%, with Apple touching an all-time high. Alphabet gained 3.2% and Meta added 1.4%.

A consumer relief trade also took hold, sending housing, travel and consumer staples stocks higher. Affordability was a big theme in the election, with Americans feeling the pressure of high prices.

Housing stocks like Pulte, D.R. Horton and Lennar gained roughly 7.2%, 8.1% and 7.1%, respectively. Airlines also moved higher, with United Airlines rising 8% and American Airlines climbing 6.3%. Southwest Airlines jumped 6.8% and Delta Air Lines climbed 7.2%. Cruise operator Carnival increased 7.3%, while Royal Caribbean and Norwegian Cruise Line added 6.4% and 6.8%, respectively. Booking Holdings rallied 9.8%.

Consumer staple names also caught a bid. Dollar Tree soared 9.6%, Walmart gained 3.5% and Constellation Brands climbed 5.3%. Dollar General advanced 8.5%, while Conagra Brands increased 7%.

The GOP's wide losses also extended dragged down President Donald Trump's social media and tech company, which plunged 24.5% and hit a 52-week low.

— CNBC's Luke Fountain, Dominic Chu and Nicholas Wells contributed reporting.

Amazon, Microsoft, Google, Meta, and Apple were on pace to gain close to $1 trillion in market camp in a day

Amazon $AMZN stock climbed as much as 8.3% on Wednesday, with investors appearing to wager that a Democratic-controlled Congress will rein in AI spending that is draining free cash flow.

Microsoft $MSFT stock gained as much as 7%, Alphabet $GOOGL stock rose as much as 5.9% and Meta $META Platforms stock advanced as much as 3.6%. Apple $AAPL stock rose more than 6%. Apple has steered clear of big outlays on AI,. Together, the five companies are on pace to gain close to $1 trillion in market capitalization on Wednesday.

Nvidia $NVDA stock and Tesla $TSLA stock declined. Nvidia has profited more than any other company from the data center buildout.

Democrats won majorities in the House and the Senate in Tuesday's midterm elections. Their candidates campaigned on curbing AI development, especially the large-scale building of data centers that power the technology.

"The market is acting like this represents the end of the AI buildout," Robert Pavlik, a senior portfolio manager at Dakota Wealth Management, said, according to Bloomberg. He added that "the brakes are being applied to their spending, not by the companies, but by the government."

Exit polls found that voters are firmly against having new data centers near where they live, Keith Parker, head of global macro equity strategy and U.S. equity strategy research at UBS, wrote in a Wednesday client note, according to Bloomberg.

Shares of Amazon, Microsoft, Alphabet and Meta had been sliding because investors doubted that the companies' enormous computing outlays, running to hundreds of billions, would pay off adequately. Free cash flow has turned negative at Amazon, Alphabet and Meta, and the companies are turning to borrowing and share sales to finance the outlays.

A Bloomberg gauge of the Magnificent Seven group of tech companies had dropped 5.9% since Oct. 21, when Meta opened the hyperscalers' earnings season. On Wednesday the group climbed by as much as 3.6%, putting it on track for its strongest session since Sept. 21.

Texas is a focus for technology investors. Gina Hinojosa, who defeated Republican Gov. Greg Abbott $ABT, has called for an end to data center development in the state. Wells Fargo $WFC estimates Texas holds 27% of planned U.S. data center gigawatts. Republicans hold the majority in the Texas Legislature, which limits how much she can change state policy.

Chip stocks slipped, and shares of AI cloud providers CoreWeave and IREN each fell 4%. Broader U.S. stocks rose after the opening bell, with the Dow Jones Industrial Average up 2%, the S&P 500 up 1.25% and the Nasdaq $NDAQ Composite up 1.3%.

These Are the 13 Tactics Expert Whitetail Hunters Actually Use to Tag Mature Bucks

📅 Nov 04, 2026  |  Category: Travel  |  Read Source →

Published Nov 4, 2026 5:13 PM EST

Get everything from whitetail news and opinion pieces, to advanced hunting tactics and cutting-edge gear reviews.

The best deer hunters in the nation (known and unknown) all share a common thread. They are students of deer and the woods, and they never stop learning. As such, they continually experiment with new and advanced deer hunting tactics.

In order to learn some of these secrets (and to share them with you), I called a handful of the best deer hunters I know. Here’s how they find success season after season.

Mark Drury of Drury Outdoors has bagged more big deer than most. His approach is proven, and the process he uses includes saving and analyzing a lot of trail camera data, especially historical information. He has kept every single trail cam pic of a racked buck since 2007.

“Save trail monitoring pictures in an organized fashion for easy review in future years,” Drury says. “Bucks are incredibly habitual from year to year. Study your pics year-round to formulate patterns with individual bucks or areas. [Over time you’ll build] a database that helps lead to patterns in future years, as crop rotations, weather, and moon in future seasons mirror conditions that occurred in the past. It takes a lot of time to assemble and study but saves time in future seasons.”

Grant Woods of Growing Deer TV is one of the most renowned deer scientists in the country. He seems to get inside the mind of whitetails and understands what they do and why they do it. To that point, he stresses making hunting decisions based on what deer do, and not what we think they should do.

“An issue I’ve seen is hunters making setup and tactical decisions based on human thoughts,” Woods says. “The hunters I know that have repeated success filling tags in all types of conditions and habitats always think like a deer. They consider what deer need that day, and what will deer avoid.”

As Paul Beasley, co-host of “Canada in the Rough” TV, says, hunters are creatures of habit. “The longer we hunt a piece of ground, the more established our hunting setups and entry/access/travel points become,” he says. “We tend to rely more on what has worked before, and while deer in general also tend to gravitate to the same trails, food sources, and pinch points, it has been my experience that mature bucks need to be treated as individuals and hunted accordingly.

“The buck I shot this year helps illustrate my point. We have hunted this same 100-acre property for over 10 years, and have always taken deer off of it in the same 50-yard-by-80-yard area. Throughout the spring and into summer, this buck was working that area perfectly, but as hunting season approached, his behavior changed. His appearances on camera began to fall off. So, I scrambled to put up more cameras and found that he hadn’t gone anywhere. He was just using different parts of the property.”

Beasley killed that buck on the second afternoon of the season. He arrowed from a setup that his crew had never harvested a buck from before.

“It was through paying attention to this individual buck’s behavior, and not just doing the same old thing that has always worked, that allowed me to put my tag on him,” Beasley says.

Oftentimes deer hunters do things without thinking. We react, rather than making decisions based on logic. HeadHunters TV’s Nate Hosie agrees, and says we should hunt smarter and not harder.

“Growing up, I used to think the more I was out there the better my chances,” he says. “It’s not completely wrong, but as I’ve grown as a hunter, I’ve learned to pick my moments for when to strike. Wind, temperature, time of year, trail cam intel, [and more] now go into my hunt. The less I’m in and out, and the higher probability my attempts are, the more success I find.”

Rick White is a longtime staple in the outdoor industry and he suggests that hunters go all or nothing with scent management.

“One of the biggest things I try to instill in people is always going to the woods as scent-free as possible,” he says. “Always play the wind. Never intentionally hunt a stand on a bad wind. But also know that deer don’t always come in from where we want them to. So, if you’re not going to follow every step with scent elimination, then save your money.”

It sounds like rudimentary advice, but even experienced hunters make blunders that lead to being seen, heard, and smelled by deer. It’s something we must continually keep at the forefront of our mind. Will this entry route cause bedded deer to see me? Will this exit route blow scent into a bedding area?

Bone Collector Co-Host Nick Mundt knows deer hunting as well as anyone. As a former deer hunting guide and current big deer killer, he stresses the importance of going undetected when getting in and out of your hunting spots.

“Never think the wind will just work,” he says. “Always know that they’ll bust you if the wind is wrong. It dictates every sit.”

Aaron Gaines of Ani-Logics advises hunters to kill a target buck as early as possible. The early season and pre-rut offer opportunities to hunt consistent bucks.

“This is when he’s on a pattern and most predictable,” Gaines says. “To do this, I utilize most recent information from cameras. I’m very disciplined on hunting the right wind. If he’s daylighting, and I’ve got the right wind, things usually work out well.”

During the rut, whitetail bucks seem to travel via loops. That’s not hearsay or some hokey hack from your grandaddy’s hunting journal. That’s straight from a deer biologist. More specifically, former CEO of the Quality Deer Management Association and current HuntStand biologist, Brian Murphy.

“The biggest piece of advice I’ve learned over the years is to develop a plan and then stick with it long enough for it to have a reasonable chance of success,” Murphy says. “In other words, informed optimism. The hunters who have done their homework and keep a positive attitude, often after multiple dismal outings, are ultimately the most successful. Knowing from research that bucks often take a few days to complete loops within their home range, it’s not surprising that it takes persistence to increase the odds you will get an opportunity.”

Nathan Mrnak, COO of Base Camp Leasing, is a diehard deer hunter. He spends many days in the field each year. He stresses that it’s OK to move off your original plan and make a move.

“Picking out a stand location can be one of the toughest decisions while setting up on a mature whitetail,” he says. “I have had many first sits that ended with my target buck just out of bow range. Seeing his exact route provided the last bit of intel to moving a set just 50 yards to capitalize the next sit. This has worked time and time again in big timber.”

The most basic element of deer hunting is hunting deer where they are. To do that, you need current information.

Own the Season’s Art Helin knows this well. “Hunt where the bucks want to be during the correct phase of the season,” he says. “Food sources during early season, transition areas during the pre-rut (between field and bedding and staging areas), funnels and ridge edges during the rut (all day or mid-day sits especially), and back to food sources late season.”

During the rut, a lot of deer hunters choose to do all-day sits. Whitetail Properties Land Specialist Ben Harshyne does, too. However, he changes things up.

“I grew up hunting all-day sits in one stand,” he says. “Over time, I learned that deep timber and thick cover are action-packed in the morning and afternoon, yet often go cold by evening. Judge Lets Stock Perpetual Futures Keep Trading but Says CFTC Likely Overstepped Its Authority

📅 Nov 04, 2026  |  Category: Business  |  Read Source →

A federal judge refused to halt stock perpetual futures on Kalshi, Coinbase Derivatives and Bitnomial but found a Cboe- and Citadel Securities-backed group likely to prove the CFTC exceeded its authority.

Stock perpetual futures can keep trading on Kalshi, Coinbase Derivatives and Bitnomial for now after a federal judge denied an emergency bid to halt them Tuesday, while finding that the Wall Street-backed challengers are likely to prove the CFTC overstepped its authority.

U.S. District Judge Valerie Caproni denied the temporary restraining order on Nov. 3 after a teleconference with the parties, hours after the CFTC said the products could begin trading on U.S.-regulated exchanges.

The plaintiff is the Capital Markets Alliance Association, a New York nonprofit whose members include Cboe Global Markets, Citadel Securities and the Healthy Markets Association, according to a declaration from its secretary, Adam Ford.

The group sued the CFTC and Chairman Michael S. Selig in the Southern District of New York, case No. 1:26-cv-09608, seeking to undo the approvals of filings by all three exchanges.

Caproni wrote that the group “is likely to succeed on the merits” of its claim that the agency lacks statutory authority to allow perpetual derivatives on individual stocks to be offered to retail investors on CFTC-regulated platforms.

“Because the perpetual contracts, by definition, do not provide for a ‘sale for future delivery of a single security,’ they cannot be a security future over which the CFTC has statutory and regulatory authority,” she wrote.

Instead, she said, the contracts “appear to satisfy the definition of a swap,” which falls to the Securities and Exchange Commission.

She also found that the balance of equities and the public interest favor the alliance, writing that the agency’s actions “seem to represent a major disruption to the equities market.”

The request failed on one prong. Caproni said the declarations from Cboe, Citadel Securities and Healthy Markets were “conjectural” about the harm members would suffer and did not show irreparable harm that was “actual and imminent.”

The order leaves the approvals in place. It does not decide the case or the alliance’s separate motion for a preliminary injunction.

The CFTC’s opposition is due Friday, Nov. 6, and the alliance’s reply is due Tuesday, Nov. 10. The order says that schedule is subject to change by the assigned judge. The docket lists Judge Lorna G. Schofield as assigned to the case.

Kalshi was already listing four single-stock perps on Qualcomm, McDonald’s, Amgen and Costco by Wednesday morning, with maximum leverage of 4.9 times.

Bloomberg Law reported that the judge signaled she might ultimately side with the Wall Street firms opposed to the products.

The fight is over a label. Selig’s Nov. 3 statement said the contracts were deemed approved under Commission Regulation 40.3 as security futures products, a category the CFTC and SEC oversee jointly. The exchanges filed them Sept. 18, and the agency let its 45-day review period lapse without a written order.

The alliance argues that a contract with no expiration date and no delivery obligation is a security-based swap. Congress limited retail trading in those to national securities exchanges under full SEC oversight.

Its complaint says the CFTC “invited a parallel, synthetic market in U.S. stocks to grow outside the exclusive jurisdiction” of the SEC.

A CFTC spokesperson called the lawsuit “troubling” and told Reuters: “Our markets should be governed by law, not litigation campaigns intended to block innovation.”

For crypto-native venues, the stakes reach past stocks. Perpetuals are the core product of crypto derivatives trading, and Kalshi and Coinbase have used the CFTC’s framework to bring them onshore.

The agency approved the first U.S.-listed bitcoin perpetual on May 29, and Kalshi went live with the product soon after.

That approval already faces a challenge. CME Group sued the CFTC in June, arguing that a contract built on exchanged funding payments is a swap, not a future. That case is pending in Washington.

The New York order covers security futures, not commodities like bitcoin. But a federal judge has now said, at least preliminarily, that a contract with no expiration does not look like a future.
