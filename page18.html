Todd Cantwell reacts as Blackburn Rovers fire West Ham, Scott Parker dig

📅 Nov 09, 2026  |  Category: Sports  |  Read Source →

Scott Parker is already making headlines for all the wrong reasons in charge of West Ham, with the ex-England international having endured a challenging start to proceedings at the London Stadium by failing to win any of his opening two games - and it's causing reaction from both Hammers supporters and opposition players alike.

West Ham only parted company with Nuno Espirito Santo less than a week ago in the wake of successive home defeats against two promotion rivals in West Bromwich Albion and Southampton, which saw the Championship outfit swiftly relieve the long-under-fire Portuguese boss of his duties in favour of sealing a return for Parker.

The 46-year-old had been out of work since leaving Burnley towards the end of the previous campaign, but has wasted no time in getting his feet back under the table and experienced an action-packed debut in the Hammers dugout by claiming a 3-3 draw at Cardiff City last Wednesday after his side had stunningly fallen to a three-goal defecit before the break in South Wales.

West Ham were hot favourites heading into Saturday afternoon's second consecutive away encounter against Blackburn Rovers, although Parker's men would once again flatter to deceive and went two goals down courtesy of strikes from Andri Gudjohnsen and ex-Hammers winger Dapo Afolayan before Joel Piroe's late finish halved the scoreline without setting up yet another dramatic comeback on the road.

Parker's cagey post-match interview at Ewood Park has sparked quite the reaction, too, and after Clarets supporters pointed out just how fatigued and frustrated the former Fulham and Bournemouth boss appeared so early in his return to the club, Blackburn have taken something different from his comments.

Ewood Park isn't exactly renowned for providing an intense and aggressive atmosphere, though Blackburn did not need anything extra to get over the line at home to the pre-season title favourites in what will be remembered as a memorable victory against the odds for Tony Mowbray's side.

That didn't stop Parker from aiming a clear dig towards the East Lancashire outfit, though, remarking in his interview how, on the coach journey to Ewood Park, he "didn't see a Blackburn fan".

Blackburn, however, have been very quick to remind the ex-Burnley figure - hardly a popular presence in this corner of Lancashire in any case, of course - that there were indeed fans present to see his side's winless run continue with a cheeky dig.

The club posted a clip of his quote in a video uploaded to Instagram featuring footage of the Rovers home crowd buoyantly celebrating the hosts' two goals, which was accompanied by a provocative caption reading: "Found some for ya, Scott 😉."

FLW's West Ham fan pundit believes the Hammers should look to offload goalkeeper Mads Hermansen in the January transfer window.

Blackburn are certainly having the last laugh right now and that wasn't limited to the social media admins, with supporters joining the pile-on and some players even getting involved in the antics for good measure.

Namely, club captain Todd Cantwell earned the right to poke fun at the Hammers after enjoying a standout individual display against Parker's side, and the talented playmaker kept it short and sweet by commenting "😂."

His one-emoji dig was followed up by Sam Morsy, who was also in stitches and commented 🤣🤣👏."

Cantwell's comment, unlike that from Morsy, appeared to ignite reaction as some West Ham fans responded to the divisive ex-Norwich City and Rangers starlet, yet he's likely to simply sit back and laugh as his side continue upsetting the odds while ruffling a few feathers in the process.

Blackburn's swipe at Parker was shared via X alongside Instagram, where it gathered an interesting and mixed reaction from both sets of supporters.

West Ham fans, for example, were naturally unimpressed, and this one punter quipped how Rovers' footage failed to capture the high volume of empty seats that we invariably see at Ewood Park.

This fan, meanwhile, is not keen on just how much Blackburn have posted about the victory, suggesting that West Ham's trip up north represented a "cup final" occasion for their more modest Championship counterparts.

Another Hammer banged the drum for humility among those at Ewood, claiming that Parker's quotes had been taken out of context in an effort to gain a cheeky one-up in less-than-humble fashion.

Blackburn fans, by contrast, have been lapping it all up - and who can blame them?

Before the season started, Rovers were widely tipped for relegation whereas Saturday's visitors, fresh off the back of 14 successive seasons in the Premier League with heavy spending prior to an unprecedented relegation last time out, have been backed by the masses to complete a swift top-flight return.

It's not exactly going to plan, though, and Blackburn supporters didn't hold back after their club's jibe, which was described as "unbelievable content" by one fan.

Another was equally happy to have a laugh at Parker's expense, and that can only come as little surprise in light of the manager's aforementioned unpopularity among the Blackburn faithful following his previous exploits with bitter rivals Burnley.

Some have been even more cynical towards Parker, though, and this Blackburn fan labelled the ex-Clarets promotion-winner as "the most annoying manager for about six years now" ahead of cited polarising figures such as Leicester City's Russell Martin and Nathan Jones of Charlton Athletic.

The 7,000-strong West Ham contingent that travelled up to Ewood Park on Saturday returned down south with yet more disappointment as Parker fell short of clinching his first set of three points for the second occasion in under a week.

It's nowhere near good enough for a side with designs on lifting the title with numerous Premier League-quality performers to call upon and, in the last week, the stewardship of a manager who enjoyed a strong playing career with the club before achieving three second-tier promotions in charge of just as many clubs, and he'll be shaking off the jibes and jokes to go back to the drawing board and assess precisely what went wrong.

The encounter itself saw Blackburn score two of their three shots on target, which is hardly a ringing endorsement for goalkeeper Mads Hermansen amid ever-intensifying supporter scrutiny towards the Dane.

West Ham, by contrast, would manage only five shots on target from 21 in total, but with the likes of Jarrod Bowen, Mohamadou Kante and goalscorer Piroe all spurning some glorious opportunities as their side dominated both the possession and chance-creating stakes, the hapless Hammers will scarcely believe their luck riding some of it to overturn a three-goal blitz at Cardiff days earlier.

The two results are hardly representative of an outfit capable of arresting the slide, making up ground and eventually finishing in the top-two, though, and having now fallen out of the play-offs entirely heading into this month's upcoming international break, Parker will need to get it right sooner rather than later at the London Stadium.

The European giants facing a relegation battle 😨

📅 Nov 09, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

The second major stretch of fixtures in the 2026-2027 season came to an end this weekend, just before the final international break of the year. When the internationals return, there will still be a few matches to play before the winter break and, more importantly, the halfway point of the season. But for some big clubs in Europe's top five leagues, European ambitions are already off the agenda.

The matchdays roll by, the points fail to add up, and their league positions are becoming seriously worrying. So much so that they may have to lower their sights: rather than looking towards the European places, some big clubs may well have to focus on an entirely different mission—survival. Here is a look at the clubs already playing with fire.

We naturally begin in the Premier League, where Manchester United's situation is becoming particularly concerning. The Red Devils currently sit 18th in the table, with just eight points after ten matches. That puts them on course for the Championship and is starting to seriously worry United supporters.

Most worrying, though, is the club's form. Manchester United have not won a league match since August 30, when they claimed a spectacular 5-2 victory over Ipswich. Since then, Michael Carrick's men have stumbled from one disappointing result to the next, struggling to find any consistency. This Sunday against Aston Villa, they even came close to another defeat before miraculously snatching a 4-4 draw with a stoppage-time equaliser.

Ultimately, that point does little to change United's position, with the club still stuck in the relegation zone. With tensions already running particularly high amid disappointing results, worrying performances and growing supporter anger towards the club's leadership, it is hard to imagine a peaceful season at Old Trafford. At this rate, Manchester United could well endure a campaign similar to Tottenham's last season, with a relegation battle that could go right down to the wire...

Over in Spain, things are hardly any brighter for Valencia CF. Spanish champions in 2002 and 2004, Los Che appear trapped in a downward spiral they are struggling to escape. For several seasons now, Valencia have regularly been forced to look over their shoulders rather than up the table. And the 2026-2027 campaign does not look set to change that.

After twelve matches, Valencia sit 19th in La Liga with just nine points. A mere two wins, three draws and already seven defeats make for a particularly worrying record. At Mestalla, European ambitions are now a thing of the past. The priority lies elsewhere and, once again, they will probably have to fight to the very end to preserve their place in Spain's top flight.

In Serie A, two sides accustomed to competing near the top in recent years are also beginning to look nervously down the table. Starting with Fiorentina. Having already narrowly avoided disaster last season, the Viola hoped to make a fresh start, helped by some ambitious recruitment. But so far, things still are not clicking.

Heading into the international break, Fiorentina sit 15th with nine points from eleven matches. Just two wins since the start of the league season is far too little for a club that had ambitions of returning to the European places. And while the situation is not yet desperate, Fiorentina will need to find solutions quickly to avoid spending another season battling near the bottom of the table.

A little higher up, Atalanta are not yet in quite such an alarming position, but the signs are hardly reassuring either. The Bergamo club sit 12th with 14 points after eleven matches. With four wins, two draws and five defeats, they are struggling to find consistency and string good results together. A long way short of the standards they have set in recent seasons, Atalanta are currently in no position to look towards the European places. If this inconsistency continues, the club could well find themselves in a much less comfortable position come spring.

Finally, we head to Germany, where Borussia Mönchengladbach are also enduring a particularly difficult start to the season. Long accustomed to European competition and the upper reaches of the Bundesliga, the German club now appear a long way from their best years.

After nine matches, the Foals sit rock bottom of the league with a meagre four points and just one win. With seven defeats already, their record is particularly worrying and points to a very difficult season ahead. While Mönchengladbach have endured several tough campaigns in recent years, this could well be one too many. The club will need to turn things around quickly or risk finding themselves embroiled in a full-blown battle for survival.

3 biggest takeaways for Sixers following trip finale win over Bulls

📅 Nov 09, 2026  |  Category: Business  |  Read Source →

CHICAGO -- The Philadelphia 76ers finished their 3-game road trip with a 127-121 win over the Chicago Bulls on Sunday afternoon to go 2-1 on the trip. The Sixers are now 9-2 on the season after handling business against a rebuilding, but scrappy Bulls team in a hostile United Center.

LeBron James had 22 points, five rebounds, and five assists for Philadelphia while Tyrese Maxey had 27 points and seven assists, and Jaylen Brown had 29 points, seven rebounds, and four assists. Joel Embiid had 18 points, 10 rebounds, and five assists, and VJ Edgecombe had 13 and six rebounds. Jabari Walker was fantastic off the bench with nine and 11 boards and Kentavious Caldwell-Pope added six, six rebounds, and three assists.

Here are the three biggest takeaways following the win:

Of Walker's 11 rebounds, five of them were on the offensive glass. He was fantastic in a number of ways for the Sixers and he gave them a big spark. One could argue Philadelphia doesn't win this game unless Walker steps in and provides the lift he gave on the glass. He created so many extra possessions for the Sixers, and even if he didn't grab the offensive rebound himself, he tapped it away in order to keep it away from the Bulls and allow his teammates to crash and come up with the ball. This is the perfect night for a backup center and his team certainly appreciated his efforts against Chicago.

"He's continuing to do kind of the same thing," said coach Nick Nurse. "He's playing hard. He had five offensive rebounds and probably another five near misses. When he's in there, it's such a hard defensive rebound for them, it slows transition. So those are like, kind of, impactful plays too. And again, finishing at the basket, and throwing in a 3. They are very very good minutes."

After such a miserable game in Cleveland on Friday, James responded in a big way in this one with the season-high in points. The future Hall of Famer helped Philadelphia seize control of the game in the second when he scored 13 points on 5-for-9 shooting, including knocking down two triples, and just imposing his will on the game the way James has done so many times in his career. He has taken a step back with his scoring a bit as he looks to fit in with his new team, but James gave a big lift against the Bulls on Sunday.

"I'm happy he started the game that way," Nurse stated. "He got the jump ball and went down down there and took it to the rim. He didn't score, but, he made an aggressive play. But, I think his mindset was to impact the game, not let it come to him, and kind of take the game, take himself to the game right away. He kind of kept it going, as the game went on he had a few really tough drives, as the game went on, he had a couple of awesome drives for pass outs that we hit shots on too."

On paper, Embiid had a good night. A stat line of 18 points, 10 rebounds, and five assists is always helpful, but the big fella shot 0-for-2 from deep as he has fallen to 3-for-30 on 3-pointers to begin the season. He also shot 7-for-19 on the night and a majority of those makes were on putbacks. He even passed up an open 3-pointer, which is not very Embiid-like. It was a solid Embiid night and he certainly played well, but the 2023 MVP winner needs to find his groove and his rhythm again as he continues on with the season. He is a big key to the team's success.

"I think that his perimeter shooting, his confidence is a little low there, I thought he really battled on the glass," Nurse said on Embiid. "He got a few in the pocket, which seemed that's where most of his damage is being done right now. He did get a couple in at the basket too, but they were pretty big and long and athletic around him a few times too. But, I think he had some good effort plays as well."

Spy x Family Officially Announces New Season and Movie Coming Soon

📅 Nov 09, 2026  |  Category: Entertainment  |  Read Source →

One of the best anime series in recent years is coming with a new season and a feature film. Spy x Family, the comedy-action manga created by Tatsuya Endo and published by Shueisha, is getting a fourth anime season and a new movie, both coming in 2027.

The announcement, shared on Oct. 8 at the Spy x Family Anime Extra Mission II special event and on the official Spy x Family website, came as a pleasant surprise for fans. Season 3 of the anime aired from October to December 2025, exactly two years after season 2 ended. If the timeframe remains consistent, fans can expect season 4 to air at the end of 2027. The franchise's second movie will come out in the same year, following the success of Spy x Family's first outing in cinemas, Spy x Family Code: White, released in 2023.

Spy x Family is a deceptively lighthearted story set in a fictional country resembling Cold War-era East Germany. The two countries of Ostania and Westalis maintain a fragile peace after years of conflict. Westalis' intelligence agency, WISE, sends its top agent, codename Twilight, to Ostania's capital, Berlint, to spy on Donovan Desmond, the country's top politician and former prime minister during the war years. To do so, Twilight has to build a fake family and use his "daughter" to approach Desmond's son in the prestigious Eden Academy. However, Twilight, now posing as esteemed doctor Loid Forger, doesn't know that the child he adopts from an orphanage, Anya, is secretly a telepath, and the woman he convinces to take the role of his wife is Ostania's best assassin, the "Thorn Princess" Yor Briar.

The series' comedy stems from three people living together while hiding their true identities. Airheaded Anya is one of the most adorable characters in anime, while Loid and Yor's marriage of convenience is slowly (too slowly!) developing into a real romance. Seeing this dynamic built on lies grow into a real found family is truly heartwarming, but Spy x Family hides a much more serious side. The themes of war and violence are never treated dismissively, and the series has delivered some of the best reflections on the topic seen in the medium.

One of the most important shonen series ever could return soon

An anime series coproduced by Wit Studio and Cloverworks debuted in 2022 to critical acclaim. The two animation companies also produced the movie Spy x Family Code: White, an original story not adapted from the manga, which sees the Forger family going on an unexpected adventure in a northern town. The movie grossed $95.7 million worldwide, which isn't surprising, considering the series' popularity. The Spy x Family manga has sold over 42 million copies.

Spy x Family is the rare series that can provoke a whole range of emotions in its fans. Wit and Cloverworks have done a great job with the animated adaptations, and it's great to see that they will be coming back for season 4 and, most likely, for the new movie, too. Yasuhiro Akamatsu will replace Yukiko Imai as the season's director, while Aya Takaha will handle the series' composition, and Kazuaki Shimada and will return for character designs.

13 Years Later, the Greatest Studio Ghibli Movie Not From Ghibli Is Free on Streaming

📅 Nov 09, 2026  |  Category: Business  |  Read Source →

When Studio Ghibli co-founder Hayao Miyazaki announced his retirement from filmmaking in 2013, much of the world let out a wail of despair. Miyazaki and Studio Ghibli captivated audiences worldwide with breathtaking animation, imaginative worlds, and whimsical stories for over three decades. Beloved films such as My Neighbor Totoro (1988) and Kiki’s Delivery Service (1989) showcased the studio’s distinctive charm and Miyazaki’s remarkable creative vision.

The thought that audiences might never again experience a new story from such a gifted filmmaker was almost unbearable. Fortunately, that prospect proved to be premature: Miyazaki returned to filmmaking with The Boy and the Heron (2023), giving audiences another chance to be swept away by his storytelling.

However, when Miyazaki departed, and many of Ghibli’s other workers went on to form their own studios and create their own stories, a major gap opened in the market. A gap that Travis Knight, CEO and lead animator of the stop-motion animation studio Laika, took full advantage of with the 2016 Japan-set fantasy epic, Kubo and the Two Strings.

Laika's latest film, Wildwood, is in theaters now and definitely worth checking out. But if you're looking for a cozy night in, Kubo and the Two Strings is now streaming for free on Tubi as of November 2026.

Set in a fantasy version of early feudal Japan, Kubo and the Two Strings follows Kubo (Art Parkinson), a young boy living in feudal Japan whose eye was stolen by his tyrannical grandfather, the Moon King (Ralph Fiennes), when he was an infant. Raised on the outskirts of town, Kubo’s true talent lies in wielding a magical instrument capable of bringing paper to life.

However, one big issue remains: Kubo can never be outside after dark; otherwise, the Moon King will find him and take his other eye.

After he’s caught outside at night, Kubo's demonic aunts (Rooney Mara) and the Moon King pursue him. Kubo escapes, but has little choice but to set out on a quest to defeat these dark forces. Fortunately, he isn’t alone: he’s joined by an anthropomorphic macaque (Charlize Theron) and a humanized stag beetle (Matthew McConaughey).

Like earlier Laika films such as Coraline and ParaNorman, Kubo and the Two Strings emphasizes Kubo’s portrayal as an outcast, weighed down by his circumstances. Only when he embraces who he really is and his talents can hey find the strength to overcome innumerable obstacles.

What makes a Studio Ghibli film? The answer differs from person to person, but for me, it’s a specific blend of the mundane and magical, the beauty and hardship of growing up, and personal, character-driven stories. Even a grand fantasy adventure like Princess Mononoke, while more epic in scale, still makes room for quiet, introspective moments between characters without rushing from one plot point to the next.

These features are perfectly captured in Kubo and the Two Strings. What is more commonplace than paper? But with Laika’s prestigious command of stop-motion animation and writers Shannon Tindle and Marc Haimes at the helm, accompanied by the ethnic instruments from Japan of composer Dario Marianelli, Kubo and the Two Strings made something so banal feel like pure magic to behold.

Although Kubo and the Two Strings is packed with action (the giant skeleton sequence is jaw-dropping), its most extraordinary moments come when the fighting stops and the characters interact. Despite being accompanied by anthropomorphic creatures and paper beings, Kubo finds wisdom in each character, humanizing them just as Miyazaki did with the ineffable Totoro.

Yet the strongest connection between Kubo and the Two Strings and Studio Ghibli is their visual style. Studio Ghibli has such a distinct look that you can recognize its films even without knowing their titles. Laika, meanwhile, blends its sculpted, hand-painted style with elements of ukiyo-e woodblock prints and origami. The result is a visual aesthetic deeply rooted in Japanese art, making Kubo and the Two Strings feel just as noteworthy as a Studio Ghibli film.

Ultimately, Kubo and the Two Strings is not a Studio Ghibli film — but it could easily be mistaken for one. It is stylish, beautiful to behold, and ethereal in its story of adolescence. If you’ve watched all you need to from Ghibli and are looking for a spiritual successor, you can’t go wrong with what Kubo and the Two Strings — and Laika in general — has to offer.

These two aerospace and defense ETFs invest in many of the same companies, but their different approaches could lead to surprising outcomes as military spending and commercial aviation trends evolve.

The iShares U.S. Aerospace & Defense ETF (ITA +1.28%) and State Street SPDR S&P Aerospace & Defense ETF (XAR +4.57%) offer exposure to two important industries: military defense and commercial aviation. With defense spending facing political uncertainty and commercial aerospace benefiting from continued demand, the distinction between these funds is increasingly relevant.

While the iShares fund concentrates heavily on a few household names along with substantial commercial aviation businesses, the State Street fund spreads its investments more evenly across aerospace and defense companies. These different approaches create distinct risk profiles that could influence performance heading into 2027.

These Robotic Arms Are Powered by Algae. Watch How They Move

📅 Nov 09, 2026  |  Category: Other  |  Read Source →

What if a robot could be squishy? Soft, flexible robots could be useful for a wide variety of applications, including artificial muscles and wearable devices—or really any application where a robot must be especially delicate.

This is what has fueled development of “soft actuators” that can flex like a bicep and have a fine-tuned sense of touch as in the human hand. Like your stereotypical robot, soft actuators convert energy—such as electricity, heat, or light—into mechanical motion, but they look nothing like the metal and wire constructs that the word “robot” most often evokes.

One variety of these soft robots, known as bioactuators, utilizes the functions of living cells to generate motion. These biological machines have attracted particular attention in recent years, and now a research team at Delft University of Technology in the Netherlands has developed a soft robotic arm colonized by algae on its surface, and when the microbes photosynthesize, the arm moves.

“We didn’t start with a specific application. Our goal has always been to bring the research of living materials to new levels,” Marie-Eve Aubin-Tam, who led the research, explained in a press release. “We wanted to explore what becomes possible when living cells are integrated into functional materials.”

The tiny robot arms—about three-quarters of an inch long and about as thick as a credit card—are constructed of two layers of hydrogel that the algal cells live on. One of these layers responds to changes in pH, which are mediated by the metabolism of the algae. When light strikes the algae, they begin to photosynthesize. By absorbing carbon dioxide and fixing nitrogen in this process, the algae make the local pH more basic, making the responsive hydrogel swell. Conversely, when it becomes dark, the algae release carbon dioxide through respiration, returning the pH to the acidic side and the hydrogel shrinks.

The difference in the volumes of the two hydrogel layers controls the bending. According to the researchers, the pH-responsive hydrogel layer changed significantly with the algae’s activity. In acidic conditions, such as between pH 3 and 7, the layer was approximately 37 percent of its original weight, but it had quadruple its original weight at pH 11.

Other researchers have constructed soft robots that respond to pH, but typically the chemistry of all the surrounding liquid has to be altered. This is not the case for the algal bots. The photosynthetic activity has an effect that’s much more local, meaning it would be better suited to applications where minimal environmental impact is essential.

These aren’t, however, the speediest of robots. The chemical shift begins quickly, about 20 seconds after light is applied and about 10 seconds after it becomes dark, but it takes several hours for the hydrogel to bend significantly.

After their first proof of concept, the research team constructed several more algal devices. One was a four-fingered gripper that closes when exposed to light. It successfully grasped and lifted a hollow metal sphere, releasing it when the light was turned off. Another hook-shaped gripper could pick up and put down a rubber ring. These items were extremely light, weighing far less than a small paperclip, but the arms were able to grasp them with great dexterity.

These soft robots also require no external energy source other than light and no complex mechanical parts. “Microalgae are plantlike organisms that are largely self-sustaining,” Aubin-Tam said. This “gives the actuator potential for long-term operation with minimal external input. In theory, it wouldn’t require a lot of energy to run a system based on this technology.”

In the long term, the research team hopes this will contribute to the development of more autonomous, environment-responsive soft robots that could be used as automated agricultural equipment or for fluid control in lab-on-a-chip devices or really as part of any underwater robotics system.

Since the algae do not need to be genetically modified, there are none of the biosafety concerns often associated with microbe-based technologies. If anything, these are exceptionally environmentally friendly machines, since these organisms can absorb carbon dioxide from the atmosphere while deriving energy from sunlight.
