The Moment I Saw This Gorgeous Lake Como Airbnb, I Knew I Had to Plan a Trip to Perledo

📅 Nov 13, 2026  |  Category: Business  |  Read Source →

Maybe it’s because I’m a dreamer, or maybe it's because I’m slightly impulsive, but whenever I'm planning a trip, I start by browsing Airbnb. I find it the easiest way to envision my time away, and I dropped the notion a long time ago that you don’t actually spend time in your accommodations. That's how I ended up finding myself in a teeny town called Perledo this past summer—my mom had always dreamed of visiting Lake Como for her birthday, so I decided to see what great accommodations I could find in the area for a visit in August, her birth month.

For a stay in Italy, if it’s sleek, minimalist, or modern, keep it away from me. I’m drawn to homes that feel like a reflection of the country I’m in—something uncommon with character and a story. So when I found this unbelievably charming rental in Perledo—a quiet village in the hills above Varenna, overlooking Lake Como—on the Airbnb platform, I booked it for my mom, sister, and I before we even had flights. I mean, look at it!

My family and I arrived in Lake Como on a scorching August day after spending seven days in Sardinia. After a flight, bus, train, and short taxi ride up and around the mountain, we were thrilled to land in our new home. A few days before our arrival, our host, Giulia (having the same name as her felt like a good omen to me!), helped us navigate getting to the apartment from the train, offering us multiple numbers for local taxis, as we hadn't wanted to rent a car. (I'm glad we went that route, because I wouldn't have wanted to deal with parking.)

Upon entering the home, after several trips up and down the stairs, we threw down our bags and immediately made our way onto the balcony. The sunlight beamed through the French doors with the mountains in the background, and the smell of crisp Alpine air made the three of us pause. Staring at the boats as they glided through the water, we all immediately forgot about the long journey it took to get there.

We discovered a bottle of red wine, taralli, and a bunch of Nespresso pods waiting for us on the kitchen table, and I was delighted to find the pantry filled with essentials—salt, pepper, olive oil, and vinegar. You know, those items you might need to complete a meal or a snack, but are annoying to buy on vacation.

The apartment felt warm from the sunlight, but also by design. The pearly walls, with green cabinets and warm wood accents, felt very grounding, like an extension of the atmosphere outside. The tile flooring in the living area reminded me of my cousin's house in Rome, and the pistachio-green-framed window in the bathroom opened wide for a clear picture of the mountains and lake.

After already spending a week with my mom and sister, I was ready for some space, so I opted for the sofa bed with a memory foam topper in the living room. The bed was already made, so it was easy to slip into the soft sheets for a nap after my shower. Throughout the stay, we were mindful not to let the hot water meter fall below a certain level, per Giulia's advice, but between the three of us, we rarely came close.

Giulia also gave us tons of information about the area, including ferry schedules, walking paths, and numbers for hairstylists (I'm always looking for a good blowout). Being able to communicate with her as a local resource felt like we had a personal tour guide with us at all times.

When we left the apartment on our first evening, we followed Giulia’s instructions to catch the complimentary Perledo Night Bus, a service provided by the town from 7 p.m. till midnight, to get to nearby Varenna, where we could grab the ferry to take us to other towns. Walking up and down the mountain, especially at night, would have been challenging and slightly dangerous, so we were happy to grab the shuttle, which was a 10-minute walk from our place.

Unlike Como town or Bellagio, Varenna is quieter, with restaurants that shut down around 9 p.m. Luckily, there are some places to eat and drink in Perledo too. For example, Bar Milano is where locals congregate for their morning espressos or evening nightcaps. For us, it was where we would grab our bus rides up and down the mountain, as well as breakfast (the apricot croissants are incredible).

The walk to Bar Milano was a deeply scenic experience for us—the sunlight glistening on the lake to our right; the lush green mountains dotted with homes to our left. We often noticed fountains and shrines to pray at on our journeys up and down, something consistent on most streets in the village. After arriving home with our breakfast every day, we would start our mornings slow, sitting on the balcony with our espressos and cornetti, taking in the postcard-perfect views of the lake. We often said hi to our neighbors just below us, families who were keen on enjoying the morning air too.

We made few plans for our days, except a reservation for my mom’s birthday lunch. We spent four hours indulging at Passalacqua, the boutique hotel in Moltrasio, and between the beauty of the space, the unhurried pace of the meal, and the kindness of the staff, it’s a memory we will all keep forever. Other afternoons were spent ferry hopping, exploring different towns. In Bellagio, it was imperative we walked down the most famous corridor—Salita Serbelloni. Filled with small artisanal shops and restaurants, it was here I bought a rosary necklace from a man named Filippo, the owner of Bellagio New Wave, a family business that has been in operation for over 100 years. He told me that he grew up above the building.

After days spent on our feet, ears buzzing from the large crowds, nothing was more serene than returning to the quiet of our apartment. Stepping off the shuttle and onto the street for our walk back to the Airbnb brought a sense of tranquility over our three days there, like we were truly going home. Being able to stare into the night sky, overlooking the stillness of the water while being hit with a slight breeze, offered a respite from the adventures of the day.

When I think of Lake Como, I think of two things: romance and luxury. Away from the noise but still close enough to the action, this Airbnb gave us a slice of that fantasy. I now recommend Perdolo to everyone. Safe to say, I’ll continue building my trips around great Airbnbs. The rest always figures itself out later.

Araceli raises $4.5M for drug discovery tools aimed at reducing animal testing

📅 Nov 13, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

by Lisa Stiffler on Nov 13, 2026 at 12:51 pmNovember 13, 2026 at 12:58 pm

Araceli Biosciences, a Portland-area biotech company, has secured $4.5 million in funding to help commercialize technology that supports using human cells instead of animal studies to test new drugs.

The Tigard, Ore., startup offers instruments and software for creating images of cells and automating analysis. Its technologies fall under the umbrella term “New Approach Methodologies,” which includes human-tissue models, computer simulations and AI-based approaches that reduce or avoid experimentation on animals such as mice, rats and monkeys.

Supporters of the methods, known as NAMs, argue that they can yield better outcomes because animals are not a perfect substitute for humans and can generate irrelevant findings. That can lead to costly, time-intensive development of drugs that fail to perform in people. Advocates also promote the alternatives as more humane.

The National Institutes of Health announced $84 million in September to build infrastructure for NAMs research at 10 sites nationwide.

“[T]he efforts signal a significant investment into human-based technologies as the future of preclinical science, aiming to make research faster, more predictive, and more directly relevant to human health,” the NIH said in a release.

Araceli has raised a total of $18.76 million from investors, and the new round was led by William Cortelyou and included Fluke Venture Partners, according to PitchBook. The company disclosed Thursday that businessman and animal welfare advocate Jeff Swickard was also an investor.

Swickard owns 54 auto dealerships across Western states and is a commercial real estate investor with $250 million invested in Portland properties.

Shawn MacDonald, chief financial officer for Araceli, said Swickard’s “support reflects a belief that we share, which is that better science and more humane science go hand-in-hand.”

Araceli launched in 2023 as a spinoff from Phoseon Technology, which makes UV LED units for industrial curing and life sciences applications.

The company says it has a strong presence in the Bay Area, Boston, London and Berlin, and that half of the world’s top 20 pharmaceutical companies use its technology. It has 38 employees, according to PitchBook.

The Secret Life of Hockney's Beverly Hills Housewife

📅 Nov 13, 2026  |  Category: World  |  Read Source →

Taking David Hockney’s painting of arts patron Betty Freeman as his subject, James Cahill tells the engrossing story of two friends who shaped the cultural landscape of 1960s Los Angeles.

In the summer of 1966, David Hockney began work on one of his most iconic paintings: “Beverly Hills Housewife,” a portrait of the arts patron Betty Freeman. She is depicted on her garden patio in front of her sleek, modernist house, which is framed by strips of blue sky and green lawn. A few status symbols surround her — a William Turnbull sculpture, a zebra-skin lounge designed by Le Corbusier, and a mounted antelope head— but the focal point is Freeman in her bright pink dress, gazing wistfully beyond the confines of the canvas.

Critic and novelist James Cahill uses the painting as a jumping off point to expound on the intertwined lives of both artist and muse, as well as to provide a fragmentary snapshot of Los Angeles in the 1960s, in his new book. With The Beverly Hills Housewife: Hockney’s Californian Muse and the World Beyond the Pool (2026), Cahill bounces between formal analysis of the painting, historical anecdotes, and inferences about the broader culture, zigzagging as if driving across LA without a map. Some of the detours prove less relevant to his central narrative than others, but they are nonetheless diverting pit stops on a thoroughly engrossing journey about the friendship between two complex individuals who played formative roles in shaping LA’s cultural landscape.

Morpho liquidators seized 4.18 million cbXRP as XRP-backed borrowers repaid $4 million in debt, with $53.5 million still outstanding.

XRP-backed borrowers surrendered about 4.18 million cbXRP in 883 liquidation events on Morpho, as liquidators repaid just over 4 million USDC of debt.

The activity took place in a lending market on Base where borrowers pledge cbXRP to borrow USDC.

CryptoSlate's analysis of Morpho's indexed transaction records covers Nov. 7 at 10:30:51 UTC through Nov. 13 before 13:00 UTC. The 883 events count individual liquidation logs, so one person can be represented more than once.

A later snapshot on Nov. 13 still showed 53.53 million USDC outstanding.

An earlier loan-stress coverage used Nov. 6 positions to estimate that a further 10% oracle-price decline could bring 8.84 million USDC of debt across 1,070 positions to liquidation eligibility, assuming balances stayed unchanged.

The market's liquidation threshold is 62.5% loan-to-value, which compares the debt with the collateral's value: falling collateral prices can push the same loan above the permitted ratio.

Under Morpho's liquidation mechanism, an outside liquidator can repay some or all of an eligible position's debt and receive collateral plus an incentive. Here, that means paying USDC and taking cbXRP.

Collateral leaves the position while the debt obligation shrinks, and the records alone do not fully capture each borrower's economic outcome.

The largest daily repayment total in the selected window was about 1.58 million USDC on Nov. 8, followed by about 1.06 million on Nov. 10. All 883 selected liquidation records reported zero bad debt, the debt left uncovered by collateral.

The decoded liquidation log for a Nov. 10 transaction shows about 202,394 USDC repaid, and 218,175 cbXRP seized in the same market.

The liquidator then sent that cbXRP amount to another address and received USDC and wrapped ETH from that address in the same transaction. A separate Nov. 7 log records about 1,050 USDC repaid against 1,029 cbXRP seized.

Establishing native XRP selling would require evidence linking the seized collateral to subsequent XRP trading, including any redemption or custody movement along the way.

Just how many games were born out of this silly Japanese commercial from 1986?

📅 Nov 13, 2026  |  Category: Politics  |  Read Source →

Does your mind get blown when you find out that the origin story of something you love is nothing like you pictured it? Like, how wild would it be to learn that the Final Fantasy series was inspired by a beverage commercial? Well, buckle up.

One of the most famous pieces of Final Fantasy trivia says SquareSoft picked that name because the company was in dire straits, knew it would be their last game, and wanted to go out with a bang. Cool story, bro; too bad it’s bunk. The original Japanese name for Final Fantasy actually translated to something closer to Fighting Fantasy in English, which immediately proves that whole story is rubbish. Also, why would a company expect to close after making a great, successful game? Final Fantasy wasn’t made in 2026, after all.

I’m reasonably well-versed in video game history, knowledgeable enough to know that many of the game development tales we’ve been hearing throughout the decades are completely fabricated, but a lot about Final Fantasy‘s origins remains a mystery, so I was pretty surprised when I stumbled upon a post on Bluesky putting forward the idea that a lot of Final Fantasy was actually inspired by a pretty silly energy drink commercial, starring Cindy Crawford, where the supermodel dons a set of armor to seemingly battle it out with an invisible enemy.

It's funny how much Final Fantasy 1 owes to this 1986 Japanese Cindy Crawford Pocari Sweat ad, art directed by Moebius

No offense to Crawford, who’s not really a fantasy knight, but her sword moves are so stiff they seem straight out of an old game. All meanness aside, even if that armor heavily resembles the outfits of Yoshitaka Amano’s era, it’s hard to tell whether the commercial itself inspired the people behind the popular RPG series. Still, we do know the commercial’s art direction was handled by the late French comic book artist Jean Giraud, aka Moebius, who is one of the greatest names in manga and anime. To give you an idea, the creators of Akira, Ghost in the Shell, and many of Studio Ghibli’s works cite Moebius as a main source of inspiration. I find it incredibly interesting that, even if unintentionally, his stint with commercials might’ve proved equally inspiring to a completely different type of media.

So, if you’re of the mind that the French completely ripped off Final Fantasy and Japanese RPGs to make Expedition 33, well, then think again, as they might’ve just been reaping what they’d sowed over 30 years ago.

To make matters even more interesting, the same thread revealed that Cindy Crawford’s adventure with games wasn’t over after that commercial, as she’d go on to model for none other than Samus Aran in an early iteration of Metroid Prime.

I had no idea about any of this. Regardless, did the aesthetics of Final Fantasy really originate from the commercial for a drink with a name as dumb as “Pocari Sweat”? I don’t know. I do know, however, that in the 2000s, SquareSoft made an actual soda commercial starring the characters of the most nostalgia-bait-y of all Final Fantasy games, so there’s some food for thought.

There’s no way of knowing for sure, just like you can’t count just how many other cool pieces of media might’ve gotten inspiration out of such a then-peculiar ad. What I do know for sure, however, is that Final Fantasy fans sure do like a good fan theory, even ones that have been thoroughly debunked by the game’s creators—which is not the case with this one—so good luck getting this one out of your heads whenever you see classic Final Fantasy popping up anywhere.

Beyoncé, Tems, Chris Brown & RAYE Lead 2027 R&B Grammys Race: Our Predictions

Will Queen Bey lock up her 100th Grammy nomination?

The past few Grammy ceremonies have been quite historic for Beyoncé. At the 2021 ceremony, she became the most-awarded woman in Grammy history; at the 2023 show, she became the most-awarded person, regardless of gender; and in 2025, she became the first Black artist to win best country album and the first Black woman to win album of the year as a lead artist since Lauryn Hill in 1999.

Will she make history once again with a 100th career nomination this year?

Bey certainly has a strong shot at pulling it off with “Morning Dew (Donk),” which is the leading contender for best R&B solo performance this year. A years-old leaked demo that found new life on TikTok and later became the lead single for Queen Bey’s 20th anniversary re-release of her sophomore studio album B’Day, “Morning Dew” is one of the year’s biggest R&B hits, reaching No. 26 on the Billboard Hot 100 and topping two radio charts.

From 1959-1968 and 2012-2026, this category combined female and male artists and honored both collaborative and solo performances. During that time, Beyoncé became the most-nominated (four, alongside Ledisi) and most-awarded (two) female artist in the category’s history. During the active years of the best female R&B vocal performance category (1968-2011), Beyoncé won twice from five nominations.

Three other female artists are sure bets for a nod here: Tems (“What You Need”), Jill Scott (“Pressha”) and Kehlani (“Out the Window”). Kehlani is the defending champion in this category; she took home the gold for “Folded” last year, and her name will likely appear across the R&B field come Nov. 16, thanks to her acclaimed self-titled set. “Out the Window” is her entry here, and the powerhouse ballad is practically catnip for this category.

Scott’s Adult R&B Airplay-topping “Pressha” will ride the momentum of her To Whom This May Concern LP, and the neo-soul icon earned several nominations in the now-retired categories of best female R&B vocal performance (five) and best urban/alternative performance (two wins). A two-time Grammy winner, Tems made her debut in the R&B field with her best R&B song nod for “Burning” at the 2025 ceremony. Already a leading rap field contender this year, thanks to her featured turn on Dave’s “Raindance,” Tems will likely show up in this category for “What You Need,” which hit No. 29 on the Hot 100 and topped a pair of radio charts.

Say those four ladies are locked — who gets the fifth slot? Kehlani’s “Folded” edged out Leon Thomas’ “Mutt (Live from NPR’s Tiny Desk)” in this category last year, and Thomas could very well return with “Just How You Are.” Coco Jones earned back-to-back nods here in 2024 (“ICU”) and 2025 (“Here We Go”), and she won the first time. She could reap a third bid for “Love Me Through” this year, though it lacked the commercial success of “ICU” or “Here We Go.” Victoria Monét was also nominated here in 2024, and it wouldn’t be a surprise to see her make the final lineup this year with “Let Me,” which spent three weeks atop Adult R&B Airplay. Daniel Caesar is a four-time nominee and previous winner here, so don’t count out his “Who Knows,” either.

Bruno Mars has won both times he’s been nominated here, whether as a soloist (“That’s What I Like” in 2018) or as a part of Silk Sonic (“Leave the Door Open” in 2022). Both of those songs were Hot 100 No. 1 hits — but this year, he’s competing with the Romantic deep cut “Why You Wanna Fight?” Jhené Aiko is also a two-time nominee in this category. PJ Morton, an R&B Grammy fave, picked up a nod here for “First Began” in 2019. He could return with “Protect My Heart” this year. Thailand’s Securities and Exchange Commission (SEC) has proposed a set of new rules to regulate stablecoin transactions conducted through licensed digital asset operators.

Announced on 12th November, the draft notification aims to prevent cryptocurrencies and stablecoins from being used for money laundering, cybercrime, and bypassing regulations governing international money transfers.

Here, it’s worth noting that this update came soon after September’s public consultation. During the same, the regulator collected feedback from industry participants.

As it stands, the SEC has incorporated those changes into the latest draft. It is now seeking further public comments until 26th November.

In this, one of the key proposals is to introduce a daily transfer limit of 5 million Thai baht per customer at each regulated digital asset operator. That said, the limit would apply separately to incoming and outgoing stablecoin transfers. However, the value of these transactions must also be consistent with the customer’s declared income and financial position.

However, it’s important to note that transfers between a customer’s own accounts at two SEC-supervised operators would be exempt from the limit, provided both operators follow the Travel Rule.

Simultaneously, the SEC also wants to strengthen wallet ownership verification. In this, the SEC has enabled customers to deposit stablecoins from their own verified wallets and withdraw them to wallets registered in their names.

Additionally, operators would also need to follow the Travel Rule, analyze customers, identify suspicious accounts, and track transactions connected to high-risk wallets.

These requirements would be done in order to make it harder for criminals to move illicit funds through regulated platforms.

Finally, the proposal also introduces stricter responsibilities for exchanges and brokers. Exchanges would have to publicly disclose their market makers and the digital assets for which they provide liquidity. Meanwhile, brokers would be prohibited from using liquidity providers for stablecoin-to-Thai baht trading pairs.

Besides that, brokers and dealers offering off-platform transactions would face a minimum transaction threshold of 3 million baht and would have to publish trading prices for transparency. Brokers would no longer be allowed to arrange off-platform trades directly between customers.

Taken together, these steps would help the SEC combat crimes that are concerning the stablecoin ecosystem in Thailand.

The Trump administration is giving states an optional six-month grace period for enforcing new Medicaid work requirements, which were due to go into effect January 1.

The Centers for Medicare & Medicaid Services (CMS) announced Friday an optional "hold harmless" period through June 30, 2027, to give states a transition period to further modernize systems, improve processes and streamline the applicant and beneficiary experience.

During the "hold harmless" period, states will still assess whether Medicaid applicants and beneficiaries meet the work requirements or qualify for an exclusion, but otherwise eligible people will not lose or be denied coverage solely because they fail to meet the requirement, CMS said.

CMS says the transition is intended to give states time to improve eligibility systems, automate verification, train workers and improve the beneficiary experience.

"States have made real progress, and they’ve been clear with us about what it takes to get this right for beneficiaries—at scale and for the long term," Dan Brillman, Deputy Administrator and Director of the Center for Medicaid and CHIP Services, said in a statement. "If the state can use data to show that someone is working or qualifies for an exclusion, that person should not have to track down paperwork to prove it. And when someone needs support, they should know exactly what to do, when to do it, and where to go. That’s how we build a Medicaid program that works better for people, connects them to work, education, and training opportunities, and supports paths to prosperity."

During the six-month enforcement pause, CMS encourages states to use existing data to automatically verify qualifying activities and exemptions before requesting additional information from beneficiaries.

States are also encouraged to simplify notices and make the process easy to navigate so beneficiaries understand what the state has already verified, what information is still needed and how to respond or get help. States should also test eligibility systems end-to-end, particularly how the systems handle missing, outdated or conflicting information, to resolve operational issues and minimize inappropriate coverage disruptions.

CMS also called on states to coordinate with community partners to connect applicants and beneficiaries who need assistance with opportunities for employment, education, job training and volunteering.

The work requirements were established as part of last summer's massive H.R. 1 legislation, or the One Big Beautiful Bill Act. Under the mandate, certain Medicaid applicants and beneficiaries ages 19 to 64 must meet a statutory work requirement, unless excluded. There are exemptions in place for the medically frail, disabled and others who are unable to meet the mandates.

CMS’ June 2026 interim final rule outlines qualifying activities, including 80 hours per month of work, community service or participation in a work program; enrollment in an educational program at least half-time; a combination of qualifying activities totaling at least 80 hours per month; or meeting the specified monthly income threshold.

State Medicaid administrators, health insurers, physicians and hospitals have pushed back on the Medicaid work requirements, citing concerns about financial and operational challenges, a spike in uncompensated care and the rule's strict "medical frailty" exemptions.

Industry groups argue the requirements impose onerous documentation burdens. The Alliance of Community Health Plans said that the final rule adds documentation requirements and tightens criteria for exemptions to the requirements, which will make it harder for individuals who need coverage to secure exemptions they may qualify for.

The organization also noted that most individuals enrolled in Medicaid are already working or in school.

Jennifer Tolbert, deputy director of the program on Medicaid and the uninsured at KFF, said in a quick take post back in June that "the rule adopts a restrictive definition of medical frailty that differs from states’ early expectations."

"Given the complexity of the provisions in the rule, states will likely face significant challenges in operationalizing the requirements in the next six months," Tolbert wrote. "With the short implementation timeline, states were already making implementation decisions that may not align with the rule."

Studies estimate that between 5 million and 10 million people could lose Medicaid coverage in 2028 following the rollout of new work requirements.

The Trump administration's tight definitions of medical frailty and documentation burdens have triggered multiple lawsuits from states, medical organizations and advocacy groups. A coalition of 26 states sued the Trump administration over its rollout of Medicaid work requirements. arguing that key provisions could cause eligible people to lose coverage and disrupt states’ implementation efforts. The states argue CMS’ new definition of who qualifies as “medically frail” is too restrictive and could make it harder for people with serious health conditions to receive exemptions.

In October, a Massachusetts judge sided with the states and rejected the Trump administration's definition of "medical frailty" for critical exemptions.

A group of physician groups, individual Medicaid enrollees and the city of Columbus also sued the federal government to challenge the work requirements.
