Zillow pushes Preferred agent program toward 100% of buyer connections

📅 Nov 04, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Zillow reported Q3 2026 traffic down 3% to 215 million monthly users and a $158 million net loss. It highlighted AI mode expansion, Preferred growth and gains in mortgage and rentals revenue.  AI Summary

Zillow may be facing calls for investigations into some of its practices by consumer advocacy organizations and federal lawmakers, but the real estate listing portal giant is doubling down on its belief that it is “building the faster, easier and more affordable moving experience.”

“As more consumers and professionals use Zillow to move from dreaming to transacting, the old real estate experience will feel slow, difficult and expensive by comparison,”  Zillow CEO Jeremy Waksman and Zillow COO and CFO Jeremy Hofmann wrote in a letter to shareholders published on Wednesday.

Despite this confidence, Zillow may be facing some headwinds. The company said that in the third quarter of 2026, visitor traffic to its apps and sites was down 3% during the quarter to 215 million average monthly unique users, while overall visits were down 5% annually to 2.3 billion according to Comscore data. Despite the declines, Comscore data shows that Zillow attracted 77% of average monthly unique visitors in the real estate category in Q3. In addition to this, the company said it has found that its AI search mode is deepening engagement, with visitors who use this option spending nearly three times as long as those who don’t on the site.

Zillow said that AI mode is now available to 100% of its signed-in users. The company said it was also exploring ways to use AI to make real estate professionals more productive and to help its internal engineers, who are using AI to ship 60% more changes to production than they did in 2024, according to Zillow.

“Buyers spend an average of two to three hours a week with us over about five months — and then for most, Zillow software and services power their transaction: the majority of residential real estate transactions in the U.S. run through our professional software,” the executives wrote. “Because of Zillow’s transaction infrastructure, our agentic AI does more than guide the shopping experience people love; it assists consumers and professionals throughout the transaction itself.

In addition to the decline in traffic, Zillow also reported a net loss of $158 million for the quarter, down from a $10 million net income generated in Q3 2025. This decline came even as revenue rose 7% annually to $724 million.

Zillow’s Preferred lead referral program is one of cornerstones of its revenue generation engine. The company reported that in Q3, two-thirds of buyer connections came to a Preferred agent partner in the integrated experience, roughly double what it was a year ago. Zillow said it was on track for Preferred to represent over three-quarters of connections by the end of the year, with it anticipating reaching 100% of connections in the first half of 2027. Zillow also noted that Preferred connections generate 35% more revenue per connection that its previous lead generation model.

Even with the growth of the Preferred program, Zillow’s for-sale revenue was flat year-over-year at $489 million. In addition to this, its residential segment recorded a 4% annual decline in revenue to $419 million, which the company attributed to higher-than-expected mortgage rates putting downward pressure on housing transaction volume.

Bright spots in Zillow’s Q3 earnings report occurred in its mortgage and rentals segments. Zillow’s mortgage rose 32% annually to $70 million, due to a 55% increase in purchase loan origination volume to $2.0 billion.

In markets where Zillow has integrated its mortgage product with its Preferred program the longest, Zillow said adoption rates of Zillow Home Loans is around 20%.

“We are also making Zillow Home Loans a trusted lender for Preferred agent partners’ entire book of business, not just for clients they connect with through Zillow,” Wacksman and Hofmann wrote.

Rentals revenue was up 26% year-over-year to $220 million, which the company attributed to multifamily revenue growing 35% year over year. Zillow said its rentals segment now accounts for roughly 30% of its total revenue, up from 21% two years ago. Additionally, the company noted that 81,000 multifamily properties were advertised on Zillow at the end of the third quarter, a 17% annual increase.

“Diversifying into this space has brought us a large growth opportunity and made our business more robust,” the executives wrote. “Our rentals strategy is to offer the broadest, most comprehensive inventory from single-family homes to large apartment buildings, and to streamline the transaction experience for consumers and housing providers.”

Executives added that they believe Zillow is still just scratching the surface of the opportunity presented by the rentals sector.

“We see a clear path to more than $1 billion in annual rentals revenue and a substantial opportunity to keep growing across both multifamily and longtail rentals well beyond that milestone,” Wacksman and Hofmann wrote.

The company also highlighted the performance of some of its other offerings, noting that its CRM product, Follow Up Boss, recorded roughly 141,000 monthly active users in Q3, up 16% annually. In addition to this, Zillow said over 800 brokerages had signed up for its pre-marketing product Zillow Preview and its listing enhancement product, Zillow Showcase, was on 5.4% of all new U.S. listings.

“Even as housing conditions deteriorated sharply, Zillow kept growing and outperforming, powered by our brand, audience, value to partners, disciplined cost management and strong growth levers across our well-diversified business,” Hofmann and Wacksman wrote. “These advantages are driving our performance through an exceptionally difficult macro environment today and position us to accelerate when conditions improve.”

Home Equity Conversion Mortgage (HECM) endorsements for August show that Atlantic Avenue Mortgage, loanDepot and Caliver Beach Mortgage remain the top brokers on a 12-month rolling basis, according to a report published Wednesday by HECMWorld.com based on data compiled by Reverse Market Insight (RMI).

The three leading brokers have remained intact for much of 2026, with Atlantic Avenue now at a rolling total of 1,002 endorsements, down slightly from 1,009 in July. loanDepot endorsed 43 loans in August, upping its rolling total to 476, while Caliver Beach produced 19 loans, dropping its rolling total slightly to 352.

The rest of the top 10 on a rolling basis includes West Capital Lending (194), C2 Financial Corp. (191), Barrett Financial Group (155), NEXA Lending (140), Carrington Mortgage Services (122), Senior Lending Corp. (115) and Integrity 1st Mortgage (107).

West Capital Lending had a strong month and jumped ahead of C2 Financial for #4, 194 to 191, after adding 15 loans in August. Caliver Beach Mortgage stays at #3 with 352.

HECMWorld.com noted in an email that it’s still waiting for data from the Department of Housing and Urban Development (HUD) to compile its HECM direct lender rankings for October. The data for the prior month is typically released in the first few days of each month.

September data published by RMI showed that HECM endorsements were down 6.7% from August, with the 1,790 loans in September representing the lowest total for any month since April 2020.

Last month, New View Advisors released the newest edition of its Proprietary Reverse Mortgage Production Index, which shows that private-label products are driving overall growth for reverse mortgage originations even as HECM production wanes. The company said that prop loans represented more than 60% of reverse mortgage volume in September — a share that is likely to rise further.

“Rising interest rates may challenge the reverse mortgage industry in the fourth quarter, but proprietary loans could still propel reverse mortgage industry totals to over $8 billion in 2026, versus $7.3 billion in total new originations of reverse mortgages in 2025,” New View explained.

In his weekly “Ribler on Rates” video series, Dan Ribler of Longbridge Financial said Wednesday that the 10-year Treasury yield, which closely connects with long-term mortgage rates, has offered some relief to capital markets and borrowers in recent weeks.

“In the last 30 days, the 10-year Treasury yield is down about 35 basis points at the same time as the S&P 500 is down about 2.5% to 3%. This is not a structural, massive repricing of anything,” Ribler said.

“This is just a 30-day cycle of risk-off — currencies, dollars moving out of riskier assets, whether it’s stocks, whether it’s AI infrastructure, into safer assets and Treasurys. As more dollars move into Treasurys, the price goes up and the yield comes down, and we get a little bit of rate love on our rate sheets.”

HousingWire‘s Mortgage Rates Center on Wednesday showed that 30-year fixed rates averaged 7.34%, although rates for reverse mortgage products typically run higher than that figure. Higher rates impact reverse mortgages differently than traditional forward mortgages as they eat into available proceeds for borrowers.

As lenders look to keep business flowing through the end of the year and beyond, they’re looking to technology as a potential spark plug.

On Wednesday, Nationwide Equities Corp. announced the launch of ReverseReach, which aims to help traditional mortgage brokers with lead generation efforts through their databases of past clients.

“We created ReverseReach to make uncovering those highly lucrative opportunities simple, efficient and accessible — even for brokers who have never closed a reverse mortgage before — so they can confidently and comfortably begin originating them,” CEO Paul Lamparillo said.

Agility Robotics announced on November 4, 2026 that it had completed its business combination with special-purpose acquisition company Churchill Capital Corp XI. The combined company’s common stock and warrants are expected to begin trading on Nasdaq on November 5, 2026.

Agility expects the listing to make it the only U.S. publicly listed pure-play humanoid robotics company with proven, active commercial deployments.

Churchill XI’s common stock, warrants and units will cease trading under their former symbols. Its outstanding units have been separated into their underlying securities and will no longer be listed following the closing.

All existing Agility shareholders will roll 100% of their equity into the combined company. Shares held by those shareholders and the Churchill sponsor will be subject to a 180-day restriction on sales after closing, with early release possible under certain stock-price performance measures. Agility will submit registrations to the Securities and Exchange Commission for resales of some shares, but those registrations will not lift the lock-up.

The closing followed Churchill XI shareholders’ approval of the combination and all related proposals on November 2, 2026, with more than 84% of votes cast supporting the transaction. An October 30, 2026 announcement put the expected gross capital raise at approximately $465 million before transaction expenses. It comprised approximately $164 million from Churchill XI’s trust account, approximately $201 million from a common-stock private investment committed at $10 per share and led by Foxconn, and $100 million of equity funding already received after the combination was announced. The capital was intended to support commercial expansion and accelerate deployments with enterprise customers.

Digit is designed to automate repetitive and physically demanding tasks and is commercially deployed in manufacturing, distribution and logistics with GXO, Schaeffler and Toyota Motor Manufacturing Canada. Agility reports more than 65,000 hours of real-world robot operation.

GXO has expanded its agreement to include Digit deployments at two additional U.S. facilities, expected to begin in early 2027. AGCO has signed a separate commercial agreement to pilot Digit at its manufacturing facility in Hesston, Kansas, beginning in 2027.

Digit 5, the next-generation model, is designed for cooperative safety so robots can work close to people in dynamic industrial environments. Agility reports more than $300 million in committed multi-year orders for Digit 5. That amount represents potential value expected over time, subject to contractual milestones, product features and specifications, and is not current-period revenue. Initial deliveries to early-access customers are targeted for the first half of 2027, with general availability targeted for the end of 2027.

Agility’s supporting infrastructure includes RoboFab, a U.S. manufacturing facility designed to produce up to 10,000 robots annually, and Agility Arc, a cloud-based enterprise software platform for deploying and managing robot fleets.

Agility enters its Nasdaq transition with operating customer deployments, while the scale of its next phase depends on converting conditional Digit 5 orders into 2027 deliveries and using RoboFab’s designed capacity and Agility Arc to support wider rollouts.

Amwell bets AI boom will boost demand for its unified care platform" style="max-width:100%; height:auto; border-radius:8px;" loading="lazy">

As health plans and employers contend with a growing array of virtual care and AI-enabled tools, Amwell is positioning its digital health platform as the infrastructure layer for AI-enabled care.

Executives at Amwell, formerly known as American Well, touted the company's platform strategy, government contracts and higher-value clinical services as the main drivers of future growth during the company's third-quarter earnings call Tuesday.

Rising healthcare costs are pushing health plans and employers to adopt tech-enabled care solutions, but they are looking for measurable engagement and outcomes from digital health vendors, not just access, according to Ido Schoenberg, M.D., Amwell chairman and CEO.

The proliferation of point solutions, accelerated by AI, is creating more vendor sprawl and member confusion, Schoenberg told investors and analysts during the call.

There's also a lack of proof with many digital health tools, he noted. "It is hard to see which programs work and harder still to replace one without disrupting members. That sprawl is accelerating. AI has made it faster and cheaper to build a clinical program. Investors have backed hundreds of new digital health companies this year, many of them built on AI. Our customers feel pressure to offer these programs, but each one brings its own cost, its own integration, and its own front door. The opportunity is real. So is the complexity," he said.

More patients are turning to AI for health guidance but those interactions don’t necessarily lead them to appropriate, covered care, creating the risk of poor clinical decisions and unnecessary costs for health plans, Schoenberg said.

"This is the problem Amwell is determined to solve for our customers. We help them improve clinical and financial outcomes. We do it through activation, getting more of their members into technology-enabled care in place of conventional care that often costs more. We give our customers one platform under their brand with one integration. It provides reliable, scalable rails to bring in a program, exchange data with it, and tests whether it works. The member's experience stays consistent, simple, and engaging. A common data structure proves the outcomes and improves performance over time," he said.

As AI adoption grows, a unified care platform becomes more valuable, Schoenberg asserted.

"We are not selling an AI feature. We are the trusted platform where AI-powered care runs with clinicians in the loop," he said during the Q3 call.

Amwell's customers represent more than 90 million covered lives, and the company also serves 9.6 million beneficiaries of the Military Health System.

Amwell's platform can help drive member engagement and utilization of digital health tools, executives said.

"Most of the health plans we talk to still see engagement in the single digits. We address that with always-on engagement, outreach to members through our customers' own channels within the rules they set. When a customer switches it fully on, members use the platform at more than three times the rate we see when it's off," Schoenberg said.

The platform runs on a common data structure, he noted. "It sends a member into a program with the right information. It collects outcomes back, and it reports value in the customer's own terms, whether that customer is a health plan or an employer that the plan serves. That is how a program proves its return on investment and how it gets better over time," he said.

In response to a question from an analyst, Schoenberg suggested pricing for the solution could evolve from fixed Software-as-a-Service (SaaS) fees toward outcomes-based arrangements in which Amwell shares in the savings it produces for health plans.

Amwell executives view that platform strategy as key to the company's growth.

In Q3, Amwell reported revenue that was slightly above Wall Street's consensus and a narrower adjusted loss than analysts expected. Amwell executives also reiterated confidence in reaching positive operating cash flow and breakeven to positive adjusted EBITDA in the fourth quarter.

Amwell brought in $49 million in Q3 revenue, according to financial results published Tuesday afternoon. Revenue was down approximately 12.9% year-over-year, but $1 million above the high end of management's guidance range, according to Chief Financial Officer Mark Hirschhorn.

Subscription revenue was $23.9 million, down approximately 22.5% year-over-year and 6.8% sequentially, Hirschhorn said. “The year-over-year decline continues to reflect the previously disclosed churn we've discussed on prior earnings calls,” he added.

Amwell Medical Group (AMG) visit revenue was $23.7 million, up approximately 11.7% year over year, while AMG paid visits totaled approximately 301,000 visits, essentially flat year over year, Hirschhorn said.

Virtual primary care visits were up 41% year over year, continuing that segment's strong growth trajectory. Total platform visits were approximately 760,000 visits, down approximately 30.5% year over year—though Hirschhorn noted the decline is consistent with previously discussed portfolio changes.

The telehealth platform reported a net loss of $11.7 million during the quarter, compared to a loss of $29.3 million the same period a year ago, Hirschhorn said. Adjusted EBITDA loss narrowed to $3.6 million from a loss of $12.7 million a year earlier.

Amwell executives are bullish on revenue growth from its own clinical services, including virtual primary care and behavioral health.

Executives also highlighted the company's government business as it delivered solid third-quarter financial performance. Amwell announced in September that the Department of Veterans Affairs (VA) issued a letter of intent to deploy its platform enterprise-wide.

"I want to be precise about what this is," Schoenberg said. "The letter is not a binding contract. A definitive agreement still has to be negotiated and executed under federal procurement rules, and that work is underway."

Schoenberg noted the letter followed a "comprehensive evaluation."

"It cited scalable video consultations, interoperability, cybersecurity compliance, and support for integrated care delivery," he said. "In plain terms, the capabilities the department evaluated are the ones we already run at scale for the military health system, and the department's own scale is considerable."

Amwell executives are optimistic the company will sign a contract with the VA "over the next couple of months," Hirschhorn said, with implementation beginning in 2027, but no definitive agreement exists yet.

Moreover, Schoenberg said the platform is continuing to negotiate a "direct, longer-term contract" with the Defense Health Agency (DHA).

The DHA, which oversees services for the Military Health System, renewed its contract with the telehealth platform in August 2025. In June, it called for sole contracts with Amwell and four other vendors.

DHA discontinued its payment for behavioral health and automated care services with Amwell last year. Schoenberg said the cessation was due to the Department of Defense’s cost-cutting measures—not the department’s satisfaction with the services.

Hirschhorn said Amwell is raising its full-year revenue guidance to between $204 million to $206 million, up from $195 million to $205 million. The company now expects full-year adjusted EBITDA to be a loss in the range of $6.9 million to $7.9 million, a meaningful improvement from its prior range of a loss of $12 million to $16 million.

Amwell forecasts full-year AMG visits in the range of 1.32 million to 1.37 million visits.

“In summary, during the third quarter, we exceeded our revenue guidance with stronger visit volumes, a stable subscription base, and a leaner cost structure that positions us to finish the year on a strong note,” Hirschhorn said.

Notably, she had been credited for heading Texas' response to a measles outbreak last year. Satish Pillai, M.D., is slated to fill the role in the interim as the agency seeks a replacement, the CDC said.

New research suggests that caffeinated energy drinks and stimulant supplements are associated with significantly higher risks of serious heart problems, including cardiac arrest and heart attack in young adults.

The findings, to be presented at the American Heart Association (AHA) Scientific Sessions 2026 November 6–9 in Chicago, are preliminary and can’t prove that energy drinks cause heart problems.

Still, the results highlight concerning cardiovascular effects of these popular products, particularly among adults under 35 who consume them regularly.

“These products are marketed as everyday pick-me-ups and sold next to soda and bottled water, so most people assume they carry no real risk,” study author Sai Samayamanthula, a fourth-year medical student at the University of Virginia School of Medicine in Charlottesville, VA, said in a news release.

“For many healthy people, if these products are consumed occasionally, the risk is probably small. However, individuals who consume energy drinks or other stimulant-containing supplements regularly can quickly exceed the recommended daily limit for caffeine, so it’s important for people to know that there may be a risk of cardiovascular harm with these products,” Samayamanthula continued.

Researchers from the University of Virginia School of Medicine analyzed 28,345 reports submitted to the Food and Drug Administration (FDA) between 2017 and 2025.

Of these, 854 involved caffeinated energy drinks or stimulant supplements, while the remaining reports involved non-stimulant products.

Compared with non-stimulant products, cardiac arrest was reported approximately 11 times more often with energy drinks and stimulant supplements. Heart attacks were reported 4.5 times more often, while ventricular arrhythmias and palpitations were reported about four times more frequently.

Nearly 45% of heart-related reports involved adults ages 18 to 34.

“Our findings support the case for more systematic safety monitoring of caffeine- and stimulant-containing products. In addition, healthcare professionals should remember to ask their younger patients who have palpitations, chest pain or fainting spells if they recently or regularly consume these products,” Samayamanthula said.

Gregory M. Marcus, MD, professor of medicine at the University of California, San Francisco School of Medicine, and chair of the 2026 AHA scientific statement on caffeine and cardiovascular disease, wasn’t involved in the study, but said the research “raises concerns that products with very high amounts of caffeine may be harmful to the cardiovascular system.”

“Although there are biological reasons why that connection could be possible, the study does not prove that caffeine products cause these events, because other factors may have influenced the results found in this analysis,” Marcus said in a news release.

“More research is needed to understand whether these stimulant products are truly responsible for the reported heart-related events.”

Cheng-Han Chen, MD, board certified interventional cardiologist and medical director of the Structural Heart Program at MemorialCare Saddleback Medical Center in Laguna Hills, CA, said the findings, although concerning, are not surprising. Chen wasn’t involved in the study.

“We know that energy drinks can have significant effects on the cardiovascular system, primarily because of their stimulant properties,” he told Healthline.

“Caffeine can increase blood pressure and heart rate, while other ingredients, such as guarana and taurine, may further contribute to cardiovascular stimulation and increased stress hormone levels. What is particularly concerning is the potential for these products to trigger serious complications, including abnormal heart rhythms, especially in people with underlying cardiovascular conditions.”

Chen agreed that occasional energy drink consumption is unlikely to have long-term consequences for heart health in most healthy individuals. However, he said that “frequent or excessive consumption can potentially increase the risk of hypertension and cardiac arrhythmias.”

An AHA scientific statement published in July 2026 notes that up to 400 milligrams of caffeine daily is generally considered safe for most healthy adults. That’s roughly equivalent to four or five 8-ounce cups of brewed coffee, depending on how it’s prepared.

However, some energy drinks contain up to three times as much caffeine per fluid ounce as brewed coffee.

Certain energy drinks may also contain stimulants, such as guarana, yerba mate, theobromine, synephrine, and yohimbine.

These ingredients may compound caffeine’s effects on the cardiovascular system.

“Caffeine stimulates the cardiovascular system, primarily by increasing sympathetic nervous system activity. This can cause the heart to beat faster, raise blood pressure, and increase the workload on the heart,” Chen said.

“Energy drinks may contain additional stimulants, such as guarana and taurine, which can further increase blood pressure and stress hormone levels. With excessive consumption, these effects can become more pronounced and potentially interfere with the heart’s normal electrical activity, increasing the risk of abnormal heart rhythms. People with underlying cardiovascular conditions, particularly hypertension or a predisposition to arrhythmias, may be especially vulnerable to these effects.”

Other research has echoed these concerns. A 2025 review suggests that high-volume energy drink consumption can prolong the heart’s QTc interval, increase blood pressure, and raise heart rate beyond the effects of caffeine alone.

“There is growing concern about the cardiovascular effects of energy drinks and stimulant-containing supplements, particularly among younger adults,” Chen said.

“One challenge is that younger individuals may not recognize that these products can have significant effects on their heart and blood pressure, especially when consumed frequently or in combination with other caffeinated products.”

Young adults accounted for a substantial portion of the FDA reports included in the study, highlighting potential cardiovascular concerns for this age group.

People with existing heart conditions, a history of irregular heart rhythms, or heightened caffeine sensitivity may face additional risks. Those with undiagnosed genetic heart conditions may also be vulnerable.

A 2024 study identified cases of sudden cardiac arrest occurring shortly after energy drink consumption, particularly among people with underlying inherited heart conditions.

“As a cardiologist, I am particularly concerned about people who may have underlying cardiovascular conditions that have not yet been diagnosed,” Chen said.

“Excessive stimulant intake can potentially trigger symptoms such as palpitations, chest discomfort, and lightheadedness. In susceptible individuals, it may also increase the risk of more serious complications, including abnormal heart rhythms.”

Additionally, combining energy drinks with strenuous exercise or alcohol may introduce additional cardiovascular risks.

The findings suggest an association between energy drinks, stimulant supplements, and reported cardiovascular events, but they are preliminary and do not establish cause and effect.

The FDA database also relied on voluntary adverse-event reports, meaning some events may never be reported, while others may receive disproportionate attention.

Researchers also do not yet know how many people consumed these products without experiencing any problems. Without this variable, the reports cannot establish actual risk.

Other factors, including underlying health conditions, medications, and lifestyle habits, may also influence the results. More systematic research and controlled clinical trials are needed.

For most healthy adults, consuming up to 400 milligrams of caffeine per day is generally considered safe.

However, Chen noted that individual tolerance for caffeine may vary, and people with underlying cardiovascular conditions, particularly high blood pressure or abnormal heart rhythms, may need to limit or avoid caffeine altogether.

“Energy drinks can vary considerably in their caffeine content and serving sizes,” Chen said.

“Depending on the product, a single can may contain anywhere from 80 to 300 milligrams of caffeine. This means that consuming even one or two energy drinks can put someone close to or above the recommended daily limit, particularly when combined with coffee, tea, or other caffeinated supplements.”

Data centers are learning to be flexible

Data centers are driving up electricity demand, but flexible power use could ease grid congestion and speed up connections

Picture a soccer club that bought a passenger van for its tournaments a few times a year. The van sits in a driveway the rest of the year. Buying it was the right call, because a van that wasn’t there on tournament weekend would have stranded the team.

The American power grid works the same way, and now we have numbers to show just how little of it gets used. This month, under a new state law, Virginia’s utilities reported theirs. Over the year ending in July, the distribution grid run by Dominion $D Energy, the utility behind the northern Virginia data center boom, was being used at 41% of its capacity on average.

Even in its busiest hours, the system averaged 80%, with some pockets running higher and others lower. In the state’s rural southwest, where demand has been falling, Appalachian Power reported 14% for the year and 54% at peak.

Grids are sized for the few hours a year when everyone runs the air conditioner at once, and building for those few hours is expensive. A data center that pulls full power around the clock raises that peak, and raising the peak means buying another van that mostly sits in the driveway, costing money while it gathers dust.

Researchers argued there might be a cheaper route than building, and it required something we prize in people: Flexibility. Duke $DUK researchers calculated that the existing grid could absorb nearly 100 gigawatts of new demand if the newcomers agreed to cut back its power use for roughly half a percent of the year.

The study caused a stir in energy circles, and Google $GOOGL hired its lead author. The idea came down to a trade. A customer accepts being throttled in the few hours when the local grid is stretched, and in return connects in months instead of years.

The clearest track record of this working belongs to California’s PG&E. EV charging companies, which draw heavily on the grid, saw the wait for power fall to four to eight months from one to three years before its Flex Connect pilot.

In the pilot’s first two years, the power participants asked for was there nine hours out of ten. In the hours it wasn’t, the limits cut into operations less than 1% of the time.

Batteries offered another way to ease the pressure at peak. In 2025, a data center company in Oregon secured a faster hookup from Portland General Electric $GE by agreeing to add a 31-megawatt battery, enough to run about 25,000 homes for two hours, that it could switch to if needed.

Others flexed the computing itself. Google started by putting off work that could wait whenever grids in Belgium and Taiwan ran tight. In 2024, it dialed back machine learning jobs three times when the grid around Omaha was under strain. This year it promised five utilities it would hold back as much as a gigawatt of data center demand when the grid was stretched.

Startups are selling software that slow, pause or relocate AI jobs when the grid is tight. There were plenty of ways to do it, and more kept coming.

Flexibility is already becoming required in some regions. A 2025 Texas law requires new large data centers to install equipment that lets the grid operator cut their power remotely in emergencies. Late last year, FERC, the federal agency that regulates the grid, directed the operator PJM to create interruptible transmission options for data centers built next to power plants. The Southwest Power Pool, which runs the grid across much of the Great Plains, made a similar move.

Of course, waiting is expensive, and data center queues in popular markets can run for years. Take an Nvidia $NVDA chip that costs $30,000 and is depreciated over three to five years, or $6,000 to $10,000 a year. Picture 100,000 of them in one cluster, waiting for power the way Microsoft $MSFT’s chief executive, Satya Nadella, said some of his company’s chips were last year. A year of waiting represents $600 million to $1 billion in potential computing value that goes unused.

The trade cost something on the other side too, although it was much less. For companies racing each other to build more powerful AI, even a few curtailed hours counted. If those 100,000 chips sat idle for the equivalent of 44 hours a year, the lost computing time would be worth $3 million to $5 million.

Flexibility will not fully replace the building the aging grid needs, because old equipment has to go regardless. But utilities planned to spend at least $1.4 trillion through 2030, with data centers and load growth among their top reasons, and a large transformer could take more than two years to arrive. Flexibility could shrink that bill, and that wait.

Mowbray was right to warn his Blackburn Rovers players of McGlynn’s abilities. The midfielder has been in excellent form for Stoke City, and his midweek goal takes him to four goals and two assists in 10 Championship games.

It was only Saturday that McGlynn scored one and set up the other in a 2-0 triumph over Rovers’ Lancashire rivals Preston North End. He was the scourge of Mowbray’s side too in another influential display.

McGlynn’s goal was the standout moment, but he also created two chances and completed 40 of his passes. He was heavily involved in the Potters’ play in possession with the second-most touches of their players (65).

His form to date more than justifies the fee paid for him, and may have some Championship rivals wishing they had pounced to bring him in themselves.

Blackburn Rovers have influential attacking midfielders, but Todd Cantwell has struggled for consistency in the past and Ryoya Morishita is out injured.
