Wolves are looking to bolster their attacking options this winter, as Portuguese head coach Cesar Peixoto has been working closely alongside his recruitment staff to identify potential incomings in January.

The Old Gold currently sit 3rd in the Championship table, one point behind both Middlesbrough and Birmingham City after an excellent run of form as of late, but Peixoto doesn't appear content with his options just yet.

According to Sky Sports' Lyall Thomas, Wolves are among numerous English clubs interested in signing former Celtic winger Liel Abada this winter, with the 25-year-old set to be out of contract in January.

Abada began his career in his native Israel with Maccabi Petah Tikva before moving to Glasgow with Celtic in the summer of 2021, going on to score 29 and assist 22 in 112 appearances across all competitions at Parkhead, winning the Scottish league three times, the Scottish Cup twice, and the Scottish League Cup twice.

In March 2024, Abada headed to the United States, linking up with North Carolina-based outfit Charlotte for around £8 million, and after a slow first couple of years with the club, has found his feet this season with ten goals and five assists in 27 MLS appearances so far.

Sky Sports report that he may not automatically meet the requirements for a work permit, as interested clubs may consider loaning him to a team in one of Europe's top five leagues for the remainder of the season so he can get the necessary points.

Whether that will be Wolves' plan remains to be seen, though Peixoto is showing few intentions of allowing the winter window to pass him by.

Abada can play anywhere across the final third, but is best used off either flank, not necessarily having a preference for which side he operates off.

Wolves' current wide options consist of Rodrigo Gomes, Mateus Mane, Bertrand Traore, Rafiki Said, and Jean-Ricner Bellegarde, with Peixoto certainly not struggling for depth. Despite this, the former Gil Vicente boss is eyeing an upgrade in the form of Abada.

On a free transfer, the 22-capped Israel international would be a solid pickup from a financial perspective, as only having turned 25 last month, the winger has his best years ahead of him.

His work permit issues may mean Old Gold supporters will have to wait to see him in action at Molineux, but if Wolves can loan him out for six months to gain the necessary qualifications, they could have a star on their hands ahead of the 2027/28 campaign for nothing.

Luca Williams-Barnett has disclosed that Tottenham boss Roberto De Zerbi teased him about an under-21s outing prior to naming him in the starting line-up for Saturday's 2-0 victory against Ipswich.

The 18-year-old academy product earned his full debut following two promising substitute appearances, delivering an impressive 64-minute performance that backed up the growing anticipation surrounding him.

The teenager opened the scoring inside 15 minutes before setting up Dominic Solanke's sliding finish following an intricate dribble and cross. Upon his substitution, he departed to a standing ovation, having become Tottenham's youngest Premier League goalscorer since Gareth Bale in 2007.

Speaking to SpursPlay, Williams-Barnett explained: "The day before, the gaffer obviously pulled me aside.

"He actually said, ‘you’re going to be playing with the 21s, 60 minutes to get your legs back,’ and I was like, ‘what?’ but obviously he told me the news and the whole day I was just buzzing. I couldn’t wait.

"Everything I’ve dreamed of. Obviously scoring and getting an assist, it’s like a dream come true basically."

Following the forward's fifth appearance for his boyhood club, De Zerbi offered high praise, suggesting the youngster possesses the potential to eventually match Barcelona's teenage star Lamine Yamal.

A buzz swirled around Tottenham Hotspur Stadium as soon as Williams-Barnett was named in Spurs’ line-up and it only took quarter of an hour for the skilful attacker to get in front of Abdoul Ouattara to bundle home Pedro Porro’s cross.

A first senior goal for Williams-Barnett continued his strong scoring record after 40 goals for Tottenham age-group teams since he made his debut for the under-18s aged 15 in 2024.

“The first ball into the box I wasn’t really there and I was quite annoyed because it was open and I could have had the goal,” Williams-Barnett explained.

“Once I saw it go back to Pedro, it was an instinct. Once I see he was able to cross the ball, I just had to get behind my man and put the ball in the goal.”

After this man-of-the-match showing, more starts are set to follow for Williams-Barnett, and it was understood he has agreed a new long-term contract with boyhood club Tottenham.

Quizzed about what comes next, Williams-Barnett said: “Stay humble, block out all the notifications and noise, but yeah I want to create myself into a starting XI player.”

French biotech rejects short seller’s cancer-risk claims after shares plunge 17%

📅 2026-11-23  |  Category: Business  |  Read Source →

French biotech Abivax's stock has partially recovered after the company responded to short seller Scorpion Capital accusing it of serious scientific and safety shortcomings in its experimental inflammatory bowel disease drug, obefazimod.

In a report published Friday, Scorpion alleged that Abivax's laboratory-commissioned tests indicated obefazimod may act through nonspecific toxic effects on cells, rather than the targeted biological mechanism the company described. Abivax's Paris-listed shares fell 17%.

After the closing bell, Abivax said Scorpion's report contained "numerous factually inaccurate and misleading statements, assumptions, and conclusions."

Shares rose 10% in Monday morning trading.

Obefazimod is Abivax's only drug. It has shown benefits for patients with ulcerative colitis in a late-stage trial. Abivax has said it aims to file for FDA approval by the end of the year, with a potential U.S. launch in 2027.

Obefazimod is also being tested for Crohn's disease, another inflammatory bowel disease, in a mid-stage trial.

Analysts have hailed the drug's potential as a best-in-class treatment for ulcerative colitis patients through its novel way of affecting multiple inflammatory pathways, as well as its potential multi-billion-dollar addressable market.

Scorpion Capital alleged that obefazimod may not work through the mechanism claimed by Abivax, but instead act as a nonspecific agent that could damage cells.

It also said its laboratory testing raised concerns about catalytic phototoxicity — where a light-absorbing molecule absorbs light energy and triggers potentially damaging chemical reactions — and alleged a possible connection to skin cancer cases reported in Abivax's phase 3 trials.

Scorpion also questioned the reliability of scientific research underpinning the treatment and suggested that its chemical properties could contribute to skin cancer risks.

In June, investors were spooked as cancer cases were reported in the study cohort in a late-stage trial of obefazimod in patients with ulcerative colitis. In a part-two dataset from the same trial, Abivax said malignancies were in line with expected rates, helping shares recover losses.

CEO Marc de Garidel has previously told CNBC that the medicine has "unmatched efficacy for the drug in a hard-to-treat population" and repeated the company's stance that it is safe.

"When you have ulcerative colitis, unfortunately, your immune system is depleted, and you are more susceptible to a certain type of cancer," he said in July.

Could $1,000 a Month in Dividend Stocks Make You Rich by 2040?

📅 Nov 23, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Dividends could shorten investors' paths to wealth.

By Keith Speights – Nov 23, 2026 at 4:44AM EST

10 stocks we like better than Enterprise Products Partners ›

Some investors thumb their noses at dividends because they're not interested in income. That's a mistake. Since 1926, more than 30% of the S&P 500's (^GSPC -3.70%) total return has come from reinvesting dividends. Granted, the percentage has been lower in recent years (due to the index's strong growth). However, dividends are an important component of wealth growth.

That raises a question, though: Can you become wealthy investing solely in dividend stocks? Let's narrow the question even further. Could investing $1,000 a month in dividend stocks make you rich by 2040?

Before we can answer that question, we must first address another critical one: How much money does it take to be rich? Different people will respond differently to that question.

The Barenaked Ladies tackled the question in 1992. The title of their song "If I Had a Million Dollars" gives away their answer: a cool $1 million. Many Americans might still agree that having $1 million would qualify a person as rich.

However, inflation has eroded the buying power of a dollar (or 1 million of them) over the last 34 years. Charles Schwab's (SCHW -2.13%) 2025 Modern Wealth Survey found that a net worth of $2.3 million was the threshold for being wealthy. But to be financially comfortable, the average response was $839,000. Answers varied considerably by region, though.

Let's go with The Barenaked Ladies' number for calculation purposes. Let's also use three very different dividend stocks to determine whether investing $1,000 per month could grow to $1 million by 2040.

Enterprise Products Partners (EPD -0.91%) ranks among the most attractive ultra-high-yield dividend stocks. The pipeline stock pays a distribution yield of around 6.3%. Enterprise has increased its distribution for 28 consecutive years. It's remarkably resilient, generating strong cash flow even during the financial crisis of 2008 and 2009 and the COVID-19 pandemic.

This midstream energy company has delivered a total return compound annual growth rate (CAGR) of roughly 19% over the past five years. What would buying $1,000 per month of Enterprise Products Partners' units (limited partnerships have units rather than shares) be worth after 14 years at that rate? Around $724,000. That's enough to make many Americans feel financially comfortable, but it's still well below our $1 million threshold to be rich.

Maybe turning to dividend royalty would get us closer to reaching the goal. AbbVie (ABBV +0.94%) has increased its dividend for an impressive 53 consecutive years, earning it a spot among the Dividend Kings -- a group of stocks with 50 or more consecutive years of dividend growth. The big drugmaker has successfully navigated a patent cliff and markets multiple blockbuster drugs.

AbbVie's forward dividend yield is slightly north of 2.5%, which is lower than historical levels primarily because the pharma stock has performed well. As a result, AbbVie's total return CAGR over the last five years is around 22%. If you bought $1,000 of the stock each month through 2040, you'd have almost $924,000 at that growth rate. We wouldn't quite reach the $1 million mark, but we'd be closer with AbbVie than with Enterprise Products Partners.

What if we focused more on growth than dividends? Google parent Alphabet (GOOG -2.59%) (GOOGL -2.70%) pays a minuscule dividend yield of 0.33%. However, the company is a leader in several key technologies shaping the future, including artificial intelligence (AI), cloud services, driverless cars, and quantum computing.

You might think all of this would be enough to vault Alphabet well past Enterprise Products Partners and AbbVie. However, that's not the case. Over the last five years, Alphabet's total return CAGR was around 12% -- a respectable but not jaw-dropping number. Investing $1,000 per month in the stock through 2040 at this CAGR would give you less than $414,000 at the end of the 14 years.

So what's the answer to our initial question? Investing $1,000 a month in dividend stocks could make you rich by 2040 (with "rich" defined as $1 million), but you would need to find stocks that deliver extraordinarily high total returns. That's easier said than done. None of the three stocks we evaluated would have achieved the goal.

Note, too, that the calculations above assumed that Enterprise Products Partners, AbbVie, and Alphabet would continue to grow at the same rate as they have over the past five years. That may or may not be the case.

There's another approach, though, that could be better for most investors. Don't worry about becoming rich in 14 years; focus on growing your wealth over the long term -- multiple decades. With a diversified portfolio of solid dividend stocks, that goal should be attainable.

GTA 6 boss explains why classic mini-games from previous games are missing

📅 Nov 23, 2026  |  Category: Other  |  Read Source →

Grand Theft Auto 6 boss Rob Nelson has explained why players aren’t able to play classic minigames like darts and bowling, despite there being some nods to them in-game.

After 13 years of waiting, Grand Theft Auto fans have finally been able to sink their teeth into GTA 6 and all that Leonida has to offer. Early on in the story, you’re introduced to one of the classic GTA mini-games: pool.

However, if you look around The Rusty Anchor and other bars around the state of Leonida, you will notice that darts is missing. Plus, there is the Kingpin bowling alley in Port Gellhorn, but you can’t relive your days as Niko and Roman, tossing strikes down the lanes.

Well, Rockstar’s Rob Nelson told VGC that a “lot of work” is needed to get the minigames and activities right, but they “didn’t have the resources” to bring Darts and Bowling back.

“The darts one is a contentious one,” Nelson said. “Even we… you hear they have unlimited money or unlimited time, even we do not and so sometimes you just have to admit we are not going to be able to make this to the level we feel we need to.”

Nelson noted that gym activities, like Weightlifting, extend beyond just button mashing. “It mimics what weightlifting is,” he added.

“A lot of work [is needed] to get it tuned right. Darts is one we just didn’t get to. It’s better not to do it than to do it and have it not be good enough.”

The Rockstar North co-studio head added that pool has “proper physics” and is a “game in its own right.”

Despite missing darts and bowling, Rockstar’s long-awaited release has paid off in spades. It has become the joint best-reviewed game of all time, matching Ocarina of Time.

If only there were a cheat code that could help you turn Jason into Luke Littler on a dartboard. That would have been a nice touch.
