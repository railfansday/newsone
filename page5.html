Florida’s invasive pythons don’t always stay on the ground, and about 1 in 7 adult sightings were up in trees

📅 Nov 21, 2026  |  Category: Science  |  Read Source →

A USGS study found that invasive Burmese pythons in Florida sometimes climb trees, especially in winter, raising concerns for nesting birds.

Burmese pythons invading southern Florida are not primarily tree-dwellers, but some of them climb. Among 188 radio-tracked adults, about 14 percent, or roughly 1 in 7, were found in a tree or shrub at least once, according to the U.S. Geological Survey (USGS).

For 168 juveniles, the share was about 17 percent. Across more than 4,000 observations, however, fewer than 3 percent placed a python in a tree or shrub.

Both age groups were most likely to climb in December and January, the cooler months of southern Florida’s dry season.

Each observation came from locating a snake through radio telemetry, not from a chance sighting. Tracking ran from November 2017 to December 2024 in Big Cypress National Preserve.

Lisa McBride, a biologist at the USGS Fort Collins Science Center, is the study’s first author.

A public USGS data release lists 112 tracking events in trees or shrubs, involving 56 different pythons.

The first python was observed in the Everglades in 1979, but no more were seen until 1995. The species now breeds in the preserve and is spreading through south Florida.

Burmese pythons, one of Florida’s invasive reptiles, are notoriously difficult to find. By some estimates, the probability of detecting an individual python can be less than 5 percent.

Adults in trees were seen basking, resting, brooding eggs or gathering with other pythons. Juveniles rested, basked or moved between tree limbs.

Most adults found in trees during the peak climbing months were basking, a behavior that lets reptiles absorb heat from their surroundings.

The researchers suggest pythons may use trees and shrubs at that time of year to maintain a high body temperature.

In February 2022, one adult male basked atop a Brazilian peppertree about 16 feet (5 meters) above the ground. It then climbed to the crown of a cabbage palm, about 23 feet (7 meters) up.

A female brooded her eggs in a fallen, hollow cypress tree about 6.5 feet (2 meters) above the ground in May 2022.

A juvenile female rested on a pond apple tree about 5 feet (1.5 meters) up that same May.

A USGS video shows a juvenile female moving across the branches of a dahoon holly tree in the preserve.

The higher share among juveniles may reflect an ontogenetic shift, a change in habitat use as an animal grows, the researchers suggest.

The probability of finding a python in a tree generally fell as its weight rose. Smaller snakes may move through branches more easily than heavy adults.

Still, trees with strong branches, such as Brazilian peppertrees and live oaks, supported some of the largest pythons tracked.

Adults caught in Florida average 6 to 9 feet (1.8 to 2.7 meters), according to the Florida Fish and Wildlife Conservation Commission (FWC). The species can reach 19 feet (5.8 meters), however.

Native from India to lower China, the Malay Peninsula and some islands of the East Indies, Burmese pythons are semi-aquatic and excellent climbers.

The exact route of their introduction is unknown. Pythons likely escaped from a breeding facility destroyed during Hurricane Andrew, and pet pythons have likely been released in and around the Everglades.

The months when adult pythons were most likely to be in trees overlap with the nesting season of several wading-bird species. Earlier studies documented pythons preying on nests of great egrets and white ibises in the Everglades.

No python was seen preying on birds in trees during the tracking. The USGS cautioned that the overlap does not mean tree use is causing bird declines.

Rather, it identifies another behavior that could bring an invasive predator into contact with vulnerable animals, and an area for continued research.

More than 25 percent of captured pythons have birds in their stomachs.

Before 2000, mammals were encountered frequently during nocturnal road surveys in Everglades National Park. Road surveys from 2003 to 2011 then recorded a 99.3 percent drop in raccoon observations as pythons spread.

Opossum observations fell by 98.9 percent and bobcat observations by 87.5 percent. The same surveys failed to detect rabbits.

Some reports put the south Florida python population anywhere from 5,000 to 180,000 snakes, though the best estimates so far place it closer to 30,000.

Almost 28,000 wild Burmese pythons have been removed from Florida’s environment and reported to the FWC since 2000. The 2026 Florida Python Challenge alone removed 280, with 907 participants from 30 U.S. states, Canada and Vietnam.

The South Florida Water Management District also pays agents an hourly rate to hunt pythons. They earn $50 for each snake up to 4 feet (1.2 meters), $25 for each additional foot (0.3 meters) and $200 for each verified active nest.

Seasonal data on tree use “could improve the effectiveness of removal efforts by management agencies,” the authors wrote.

Knowing when pythons climb could also help scientists understand how the snakes interact with the Greater Everglades ecosystem.

The study was published in the journal Journal of Herpetology.

Mariska Hargitay Made Her Acting Debut In A Cult Favorite '80s Horror Movie

📅 Nov 21, 2026  |  Category: Business  |  Read Source →

We may receive a commission on purchases made from links.

Mariska Hargitay has had one heck of a career as an actor. She'll forever be known as Detective Olivia Benson, the best "Law and Order: SVU" character of all time, and has, remarkably, appeared in several hundred episodes of the show. But decades before she joined the "Law & Order" franchise, she made her feature film debut in a cult 1980s horror movie.

Directed by Luca Bercovici, "Ghoulies" was released in 1984. It came out mere months after the stone-cold '80s classic that is "Gremlins" ruled the box office. Now, nobody can say that "Ghoulies" was as successful or as well-liked as "Gremlins," but releasing a film about little chaotic creatures around that time was undoubtedly a recipe for success.

The movie follows a young man and his girlfriend as they move into his parents' old mansion, where his satanic father is buried. When the young man performs a ceremony described in one of his father's black magic books, he unwittingly unleashes small, demonic creatures. Hargitay plays Donna, one of the guests at the house who encounters these little monsters.

"Ghoulies" is often accused of being a "Gremlins" rip-off, but, in actuality, the movies were competing to see which could make it to theaters first. In a 2018 interview with Love-It-Loud, Bercovici explained how it all went down.

"We were in production at the same time. We were curious about them, and they were curious about us. They were so curious about us, in fact, that Warner Bros. briefly sued to stop us from using the name — they lost. Part of the reason that 'Gremlins' came out first, is that Charlie Band ran out of money halfway through shooting, so a few months went by as we all scrambled for the money, allowing 'Gremlins' to open first."

While "Gremlins" had producer Steven Spielberg, director Joe Dante, and critics on its side, "Ghoulies" did not. Instead, it holds a terrible 7% critical approval rating on Rotten Tomatoes. But horror has never relied on critical opinion for success, particularly in the '80s. As a result, Mariska Hargitay's feature debut became a sizable hit.

Released in January 1985 and produced for a budget of just a few hundred thousand dollars, "Ghoulies" did big business (as much as $35 million at the box office, by some accounts). That paved the way for a string of sequels, including 1987's "Ghoulies II," 1991's "Ghoulies III: Ghoulies Go to College," and 1994's "Ghoulies IV." Good or bad, it made no difference. These movies were very successful.

Mind you, it's not remotely uncommon for well-known actors to get their start in less-than-prestigious horror movies. Oscar-winner Leonardo DiCaprio made his feature film debut in the 1990s creature feature "Critters 3," while Jennifer Aniston, who later broke out on "Friends," similarly made an early career appearance in "Leprechaun." It happens all the time.

Interestingly enough, Hargitay auditioned many times for a lead role on "Friends," only to be denied. She was also fired from the 1995 "Power Rangers" movie. The early '90s were not terribly kind to Hargitay. But she eventually made her way to "SVU" and, it almost goes without saying, that worked out pretty darn well for her.

Six products and services Google discontinued in 2026

📅 Nov 21, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Here's a rundown of some Google tools that ended up in the graveyard this year.

Google is inherently data-driven, and that extends to its products and services as well. The search giant has a tendency to shut down services when it feels they're not serving its broader goals, and this has led to the discontinuation of products as diverse as Google Reader, Google+, Stadia, Google Glass, Google Hangouts and dozens of other utilities over the last 15 years. 2026 is no different, so let's take a look at everything Google discontinued this year.

Google Assistant has been phased out in favor of Gemini on mobile devices. Google initially targeted the end of 2025 for the transition, but pushed the deadline to September, 2026. This means Assistant is no longer available on mobile devices, Wear OS smartwatches, headphones and earbuds, or vehicles using Android Auto projected from a user's phone. It's still usable on older devices like Nest Audio, Nest Mini, the original Google Home, Google Home Max and cars with Google built-in.

Google Assistant debuted in 2016 (in the short-lived chat app Allo, of all things), and was integrated into much of the broader Android ecosystem, including mobile phones, tablets, smart home devices and vehicles. It provided a hands-free way to control smart home lights, play music, call contacts, and look up information on the web. Those tasks will now be handled by Gemini.

The Pixel Tablet was discontinued in September 2026; the link to its listing now takes you to the Google Store's home page, and Google removed tablets as a category from its site navigation. Google's tablet ambitions have felt half-hearted at times, but the Pixel Tablet sought to change that. The tablet debuted in 2023 for $499, and it came with an 11-inch 60Hz LCD panel, decent hardware and reliable battery life. Google sold the tablet for as little as $250 during the 2025 Black Friday sale.

Its key selling point was the included Charging Speaker Dock, which effectively turned the tablet into a Nest Hub Max. The dock contained a dedicated speaker and served as a Cast target, allowing users to cast music and podcasts to the device. With the discontinuation of the Pixel Tablet, Google no longer has a tablet of its own; there were leaks pointing to a successor with a 120Hz panel, but that never materialized. With Google now focusing on Googlebooks, it may be a while before we see another Pixel Tablet.

Google ended production of the Nest Mini and Nest Audio in June 2026, with both products now discontinued. The Nest Mini proved particularly popular after Google announced it in 2019 for just $50, and the smart speaker offered an affordable way to interact with Google Assistant and play music or control smart home devices.

Nest Audio debuted in 2020 for $99, serving as the successor to the original Google Home. It featured a fabric design and surprisingly solid audio quality via two drivers — a 75mm woofer and 19mm tweeter — with only the much larger and more expensive Home Max rivaling its sound quality in Google's speaker portfolio.

Even though the Nest Audio and Nest Mini are discontinued, Google will continue providing software updates for the foreseeable future. Google is now positioning the $99 Google Home Speaker as the go-to choice for anyone looking for a smart speaker. The Home Speaker has 360-degree sound, but it comes with a smaller 58mm driver, and features Gemini instead of Google Assistant.

As part of a broader rebranding initiative centered around Google Health, Google turned the Fitbit app into a unified health portal in May 2026. Alongside the change, Google also indicated that it was migrating Google Fit data over to Google Health.

Google is positioning Health as its go-to choice to monitor health and fitness data from existing Fitbit wearables, and newer devices like the Fitbit Air and Fitbit Edge use it from the get-go. Fitbit's Web APIs have also been wound down, so if you're using a Fitbit fitness band or smartwatch, you will need to install and use Google Health to monitor metrics. While Health has a lot of features — including an AI-assisted Health Coach — it lacks the social features that made Fitbit's app stand out.

In January 2026, Google confirmed that it was ending the service, noting that it didn't "provide helpful next steps," and that it would instead focus on "tools that give you more clear, actionable steps to protect your information online." While there are other monitoring services such as Have I Been Pwned, Google's Dark Web Report was notable because it was integrated into your Google account.

Gmailify was a feature that let you use Yahoo, AOL or Outlook email accounts with Gmail and access spam protection, search, automatic sorting and other services available within Google's email service. The feature debuted 10 years ago, but it was discontinued in Q1 2026; existing users will still be able to use it until January 2027, and then it will be wound down.

That said, Google still lets you access email from third-party accounts within Gmail for Android and iOS, and it uses the standard Internet Message Access Protocol (IMAP) protocol to fetch mails. The only issue is that you don't get some of the extras, like spam protection. In a similar move, Google ended the ability to fetch emails from third-party accounts using the Post Office Protocol (POP); you'll have to use the newer IMAP standard to sync your mails going forward.

In addition to these, Google discontinued several lesser-known initiatives in 2026. Google announced in July 2026 that the legacy Google Earth Pro desktop app would no longer be available to download after June 25, 2027. If you've used Google Earth Pro in the past and want to continue using it, all you need to do is download and install the app before June 2027, and it will continue to work even after the wind-down. Google is moving its focus to Google Earth on the web and Android/iOS, and while you can still view satellite imagery and Street View for free, it is introducing tiered subscription plans for access to additional data layers.

Also, Steam for Chromebooks, a beta program that allowed users to install Steam games on ChromeOS machines, ended in January 2026. Google shut down Pixel Studio, a dedicated app that launched with the Pixel 9 series and let you create AI-assisted imagery using text prompts, in March 2026. With image generation moving to Gemini, Google is directing users to its latest AI assistant instead. Google clearly likes building new tools and features, and while that means there's always something exciting on the horizon, the downside is that there's no guarantee the service you use today will be available a year or two down the line.

The Best is Yet to Come for Caleb Williams and the Bears’ Offense

📅 Nov 21, 2026  |  Category: Sports  |  Read Source →

The Chicago Bears' bye week landed as close to the halfway point of the NFL season as possible, with 17 games on the schedule, giving us an opportunity to check back in with the team's offensive playmakers ahead of this week's matchup with New Orleans. A chaotic first few weeks saw slow starts from ascending pass-catchers Rome Odunze, Luther Burden, and Colston Loveland, absences from Caleb Williams and Kyle Monangai, and worst of all, a season-ending injury to Cole Kmet. Despite the waves created by the acquisition of defensive star Jeffery Simmons, this team was built with Ben Johnson's offensive identity at the forefront. This latest edition of Benchmarking Da Bears will serve as a reminder of just that:

Comp Player: 2022 Jared Goff (Detroit Lions)

Caleb Williams came out of the gate on fire in Carolina, leading the Bears to a 59-point offensive explosion in their 2026 debut. Unfortunately, the following week he faced a torrential downpour, Brian Flores' Vikings defense, and a hamstring injury that would keep him out for three games. Since his return, the Bears have not trailed. That launched Williams into the MVP conversation, and even had him near the front of the crowd until an ugly 4 interception game against Tampa Bay took a little wind out of his sails. Still, Williams has been playing extremely well, and there's reason to believe he can keep up this high-level of play in the latter half of the season.

Comp Player: 2025 D'Andre Swift (Chicago Bears)

One half of the Bears' admired tandem, Swift has been the lone back standing in recent weeks as Kyle Monangai continues to recover from his bout with turf toe. Swift hasn't looked completely healthy, which has reflected in his yards per carry numbers, but he's managed to power through and produced some much-needed scores for Chicago anyway. Swift has already set a new career high in rushing touchdowns (10), with a hat trick in Seattle serving as a monument to his and the offense as a whole's ability to commit to the run game.

Comp Player: 2023 Sam LaPorta (Detroit Lions)

Speaking of injuries, I'd be remiss not to mention the loss of Cole Kmet. Kmet went down for the year in the Bears' Thursday night victory over the New England Patriots, leaving them with gaps to fill in both the blocking and receiving game. It would be a stretch to say they've found a solution yet, but Colston Loveland could help if he's able to find some of the end-of-season form he showed last year. He's started slow in back-to-back seasons; can we expect him to elevate once again?

Comp Player: 2024 Jameson Williams (Detroit Lions)

Comp Player: 2022 Amon-Ra St. Brown (Detroit Lions)

Comp Player: 2022 Kalif Raymond (Detroit Lions)

The Bears' top trio of receivers have taken turns stepping up to the plate this season. Despite a disappointing start, Rome Odunze has shown a lot of flashes in recent weeks, particularly when it matters most. He's helped seal the game against New England and secure an early lead against Tampa Bay – which turned out to be pretty key. While his catch rate and lack of scores are less than ideal, his yardage is there, and as he grows more comfortable, we can hope to see continued improvements from Rome. In similar ways, Raymond started the year off as the Bears' go-to pass-catcher and Burden has had sporadic bursts throughout. If any or all of these three can find some consistency in the latter half of the year, the offense can certainly elevate to a new level at the right time.

The Saints are the first team up after the bye week for Chicago, but after that, they start what has shaped up to be the most intimidating stretch of any teams' schedule this season. In the NFC North, they may need to do exactly what Ben Johnson's preached and play their best football starting in November and December in order to even secure a spot in the playoffs.

Retired FBI agent warns NCIC breach could threaten police safety during traffic stops

📅 Nov 21, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Retired FBI Supervisory Special Agent Jason Pack breaks down what sits inside America’s law enforcement computers, how hackers get in and how to protect your data.

A retired FBI supervisory special agent warned Saturday that a cyber intrusion into the bureau's National Crime Information Center (NCIC) could endanger police officers during traffic stops if hackers altered records inside the database.

Jason Pack, a former FBI supervisory special agent who worked in cybercrime and public information, told Fox News Digital that the bureau's incident response team must quickly determine whether data was manipulated inside the system, which feeds criminal records to patrol cars nationwide.

"What they worry about is if any of this data has changed, then it might affect officers' safety on the side of the road," Pack said, noting there is no indication yet that any records were altered or that the system is experiencing an outage.

FBI PROBES SECOND CYBERATTACK IN MONTHS AS HACKERS BREACH KEY CRIME DATABASE, SOURCES SAY

Hackers have targeted the FBI for the second time in recent months. (Kevin Lamarque/Reuters, File)

Three sources familiar with the matter told Fox News that hackers breached the NCIC, marking the second cyberattack against the bureau in months. The FBI is investigating how the intruders gained entry and whether any information was compromised.

In an interview with Fox News' Kayleigh McEnany on Saturday morning, President Donald Trump said he had "just heard" about the intrusion, adding, "I will say that it’s not good. I don’t like that."

Pack described the NCIC as "America's computer for law enforcement," explaining that roughly 56 state terminals feed data out to hundreds of thousands of patrol car computers, so officers can check warrants, criminal histories, missing children, gang members and suspected terrorists.

FBI SAYS SOURCE OF ITS JOBS PORTAL BREACH STILL UNKNOWN AS HACKERS ALLEGE EMPLOYEE DATA COMPROMISED

While the database does not hold classified intelligence or sensitive FBI investigative case files, Pack said it stores personally identifiable information, including names, dates of birth and Social Security numbers.

Investigators will examine IP logs and metadata to determine whether attackers exploited a compromised password among the system's more than 100,000 users, breached a state terminal or infiltrated through a third-party vendor, Pack said, adding that cyberattacks have accelerated to "machine speed" as criminals increasingly use artificial intelligence to probe for vulnerabilities.

"You have this cat and mouse game where cybersecurity experts are trying to patch the vulnerabilities that they find. They're looking for their own vulnerabilities at the same time, they have to play defense against the criminals who are out there trying to steal information or do harm to our systems. I think we're just seeing now this speed of these intrusions ramp up a lot," Pack said.

TECH OUTAGE CASCADE EXPOSES US VULNERABILITIES TO FOREIGN CYBER THREATS

A cyber intrusion into the FBI's National Crime Information Center (NCIC) could endanger police officers during traffic stops, a retired agent says. (Getty Images, File)

The intrusion follows a September cyberattack in which the extortion group ShinyHunters breached an FBI job portal website and claimed to steal personal records belonging to bureau employees.

The latest incident also comes as a Government Accountability Office (GAO) report found 17 of 22 government agencies were not in compliance with federal cybersecurity regulations.

The FBI did not immediately respond to a request for comment on the NCIC breach.

5 Actors Who Should Play M In Robert Pattinson's James Bond Movies

📅 Nov 21, 2026  |  Category: Entertainment  |  Read Source →

The next era of the "James Bond" film franchise is finally taking shape. After years of wondering who will be suiting up as our new 007, Robert Pattinson has officially been cast as the next James Bond, taking over for Daniel Craig following his final entry with "No Time to Die," which came out way back in 2021. A lot has happened since then, including Amazon buying the rights to the property. Now, with a new lead actor in place, it's time to get the rest of the pieces of the puzzle locked in. That includes finding Bond's boss.

Historically, this has been the one they call M. Originally portrayed by Bernard Lee in the earliest Bond films, the codename given to the person behind the big desk at MI6 is an important one, as they head up the 00 program. Thus, M is the one who gives Bond his assignments and, at times, even helps him out in the field. It's an important role, one that has been played by the likes of Dame Judi Dench and Oscar-nominee Ralph Fiennes.

There's much to be done. Director Denis Villeneuve also has to find an actor to play the villain in Pattinson's "James Bond" movie, but M is a longer commitment, a multi-picture deal. It's a big responsibility. So, who should play the role alongside Pattinson? Who should be the one to help guide 007 through his next several missions for her majesty's secret service?

We're going to go over a handful of options, ranging from very obvious to unlikely yet intriguing. Let's get into it.

"The amount of time I have wanted to be M. I'm not sure who I need to call..."

Those are the words of Olivia Colman speaking to British Vogue in 2023. She wants the role. She's very well qualified for the role. If everyone wants to make this easy, why not just offer her the role? Sometimes, the easiest answer is also a very good answer. That not every actor who campaigns openly for a role should get it, but we're not just talking about any old actor here.

Colman is responsible for one of the most shocking Oscars upsets of all time when she took home Best Actress for "The Favourite." But it was more shocking because the Academy recognized Colman's impressive work over the rest of the pack. Ever since, she's been on a tear, turning in outstanding performances in everything from "The Lost Daughter" to "Paddington in Peru." Heck, she even ate up some screen time in Marvel's otherwise very disappointing series "Secret Invasion."

But Colman has been doing it at a very high level for years, long before she won an Oscar. "Hot Fuzz," "The Iron Lady," "Broadchurch." You name it, Colman is one of the best parts of everything she's in. Beyond that, she could bring both a seriousness to the role of M, which is required, while at the same time allowing for a playful dynamic with Bond. Colman feels like a slam dunk. It just so happens that the person campaigning for the role is very much right for the role. That's not always the case.

If Denis Villeneuve or Amazon want to go with a distinguished, male British actor who could be older and wiser than Robert Pattinson's Bond, one capable of being both convincingly stern and charming, Colin Firth is the man. An Oscar-winning actor who is almost too easy to picture sitting at a big desk behind a padded burgundy door, this is another "the obvious choice can also be the right choice" pick.

We have an actor here capable of delivering exposition, as M often has to, in a way that would be entertaining, as Firth could read the phone book and it would be engaging. But let's not forget that Firth politely destroyed fools in "Kingsman: The Secret Service," too, and that movie owes a lot to the "James Bond" franchise. What's more, it proved that Firth looks absolutely fantastic in an expensive suit, an important quality of a good M.

His resume speaks for itself. "A Single Man," "The King's Speech," and even "Mamma Mia!" prove that Firth is one of the finest actors of the last 30 years. There's zero question as to whether or not he's qualified to be M. So, it begs the question: Is he right for M? If we're looking to address that question in the traditional sense, then the answer is a resounding yes. The shoe fits and it fits well. Simple as that.

If it weren't for Judi Dench playing M so damn well for so damn long, we might have to have the utterly irritating "let's gender-swap M" conversation. Fortunately, that's not the world we live in. Bearing that in mind, it's going to be damn hard for anyone to argue against Tilda Swinton bossing around Robert Pattinson while dressed to the nines. What's not to like about that?

Swinton won a Best Supporting Actress Oscar for "Michael Clayton" so, much like many of the other performers on that list, she's Oscar-caliber. That almost feels like a must for M. Setting that aside, Swinton brings a domineering, powerful quality to so many of her best roles. Even so, she's a bit of a chameleon, which is partially what would make her an interesting M. It's not hard to imagine her playing an MI6 agent who used to work in the field before taking a desk job, yet she still has that darkness behind her eyes from yesteryear.

Oddly, it's Swinton's transformation into the Ancient One" in "Doctor Strange" that might make the best case here. She can do powerful yet quiet without having to be at the center of the action, and she can absolutely handle being a part of a giant, globally popular franchise. Really, there's nothing this role requires that she couldn't do, seemingly with ease.

Some might argue Swinton would be better as a "Bond" villain. That certainly wouldn't upset me, but it might be subverting expectations a bit to have her as Bond's buttoned-up boss. She could also help bring something fresh to the role for this new era.

And now we arrive at the most obvious answer. Does that make it the wrong answer? Using history as our guide, absolutely not. Ralph Fiennes made his entry into the "James Bond" franchise in 2012's "Skyfall" before fully taking over as M in "Spectre" following the death of Judi Dench's M. (May she rest in peace.) So, why replace a perfectly great actor in the role if we don't have to?

Let's not forget that the "James Bond" franchise is notorious for bringing actors back in roles even after reboots. Dench as M for both Pierce Brosnan and Daniel Craig's Bonds. Desmond Llewelyn was Q for every Bond except Craig. That's the nature of the beast as it exists. If Amazon doesn't totally buck tradition, it could easily bring back Fiennes for another go-around. And why not? He's already proven he can do it, he's a stellar actor, and he's arguably as popular as ever.

He's also an actor who seems to really understand the role. "Spectre" was going to make M the villain until Finnes refused. That, to this longtime fan, demonstrates a real understanding of who M is. On top of that, Fiennes is very familiar with shouldering the weight of a major movie franchise, having previously played Voldemort in the "Harry Potter" movies. The only reason to not do this is if Amazon and Denis Villeneuve truly and completely want to hit the big red reset button, which frankly seems likely.

But if not? The debate could be settled before it even gets started.

Last, but certainly not least, we arrive at the choice that's both decidedly out-of-the-box yet technically in the very box we're talking about. Pierce Brosnan previously played James Bond in four movies stretching from 1995 to 2002. His "GoldenEye" remains the most important box office hit in "James Bond" history, in addition to being a beloved entry in the franchise. But Brosnan's run was uneven and ended on a sour note with "Die Another Day." Perhaps playing M would give him a shot at redemption?

Let's get some things out of the way up top. Yes, it might be a little weird to have a former 007 play M, but it's not that much stranger than fully ignoring continuity in order to have the same actor play M for multiple Bonds. More importantly, Brosnan absolutely looks the part. The man, well into his 70s, still looks better in a suit than most of us could ever hope to. He's got an authoritative on-screen presence, but he's also got that charm that could work wonders for M.

Brosnan feels he was never good enough as James Bond. I would humbly argue that the scripts weren't always good enough for his James Bond, not the other way around. Why not use the past to help build a future for the franchise? Let Brosnan bring his experience to the role and mentor Robert Pattinson's 007. The meta nature of it all might add something to their dynamic.

The question of whether or not Brosnan would want to take on the role is another one entirely, but it's a wild card idea that's just inspired enough to be a whole lot of fun.
