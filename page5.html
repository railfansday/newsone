Third-quarter revenue of $9.5 million exceeds revenue generated during the entire first half of 2026

Acquired photonics and advanced packaging businesses contributed to commercial revenue growth while supporting QCi's quantum technology roadmap

Ends quarter with approximately $1.3 billion in cash, cash equivalents and investments

HOBOKEN, N.J., Nov. 9, 2026 /PRNewswire/ -- Quantum Computing Inc. ("QCi" or the "Company") (Nasdaq: QUBT), a vertically integrated quantum company pioneering photonics and semiconductor manufacturing, today announced financial results for the third quarter ended September 30, 2026.

QCi generated $9.5 million in revenue during the third quarter, exceeding the $9.2 million reported for the entire first half of 2026. Revenue growth was primarily driven by the Luminar Semiconductors, Inc. ("LSI"), NuCrypt, LLC ("NuCrypt") and NHanced Semiconductors, Inc. ("NHanced") businesses acquired during the first half of 2026, including acquired photonic components, quantum communications products and advanced packaging services sold to the aerospace, government and industrial markets. The Company continues to focus on expanding commercial relationships, integrating its manufacturing capabilities and advancing its proprietary quantum technologies as it moves into the next phase of its development.

"For several years, we've been building the technology and capabilities to make quantum more practical and accessible," said Dr. Yuping Huang, Chief Executive Officer of QCi. "We're starting to see those investments come together, and our focus is increasingly shifting from what we can build to what we can put to work for customers. We're encouraged by our progress, but recognize there's considerable work ahead. Our priorities are clear: growing our commercial business, scaling manufacturing and advancing our quantum roadmap. I've always believed that being early creates opportunity. Execution is what makes that opportunity last."

Csquare Reports Third Quarter 2026 Results and Business Highlights

📅 Nov 09, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

DALLAS, Nov. 9, 2026 /PRNewswire/ -- Csquare, Inc. (NYSE: CSQR) ("Csquare" or the "Company"), a leading provider of carrier-neutral data center solutions, today reported financial results for its fiscal third quarter ended September 30, 2026.

"Our third quarter results demonstrate strong and disciplined execution by our team and continued momentum across the business," said Spencer Mullee, Chief Executive Officer of Csquare. "We delivered strong revenue growth, record bookings and meaningful operating leverage while continuing to benefit from strong demand for our carrier-neutral digital infrastructure platform."

"These results reflect the solidity of our recurring revenue model and low net revenue churn, the strength of customer demand across our markets, and the operating leverage inherent in our business as Adjusted EBITDA margin expanded to 47.8% from 40.5% in the prior year period. Following our initial public offering in July, which resulted in $1.1 billion of debt reduction, our business is well positioned to continue executing on our strategy to deliver high-quality digital infrastructure across our urban core-focused portfolio."

"Demand for our offering remains strong and continues to accelerate. The strength of our platform is resonating in the market across traditional enterprise and higher density deployments as new and existing customers deploy and expand workloads on our platform. We believe our diversified portfolio, disciplined capital allocation strategy, and deep customer relationships position Csquare to continue capitalizing on a strong demand and pricing environment.  We remain focused on targeting high-quality customers, a diverse set of customers spanning multiple industries, and contracts that provide long-term, predictable cash flow."

RevenuesThird quarter total revenue increased 25.6% year-over-year to $302.3 million, compared to $240.8 million in the prior year period. Growth was driven by continued demand for the Company's colocation platform, customer deployments across existing and newly acquired facilities, and expansion of recurring infrastructure services. Revenue excluding metered power increased 22.1% to $276.2 million, underscoring the continued strength of the Company's recurring revenue base and customer expansion activity.

Commercial ActivityCsquare posted bookings of $66.1 million in the third quarter, representing the Company's 14th consecutive quarter of sequential bookings growth. The quarter was characterized by continued expansion of the commercial pipeline, strong pricing, and customers increasingly committing capacity further in advance of deployment requirements. Management believes these trends reflect ongoing supply constraints in many markets and growing customer focus on delivery certainty and long-term infrastructure planning.

Bookings were generated across enterprise customers, network providers, cloud-related deployments and higher-density infrastructure requirements. In addition, a significant portion of third-quarter bookings are expected to be deployed on shorter timelines than historical averages due to the utilization of existing capacity and targeted infrastructure upgrades rather than large-scale shell expansion projects.

Net Loss   Net loss for the third quarter of 2026 was $88.1 million, compared to a net loss of $29.8 million in the third quarter of 2025. The increase in net loss was primarily driven by higher year-over-year interest expense, IPO-related transaction costs, post-IPO stock-based compensation expenses, and costs associated with terminating certain pre-IPO compensation arrangements.

Adjusted EBITDAAdjusted EBITDA increased 44.3% year-over-year to $132.1 million, compared to $91.6 million in the third quarter of 2025. Adjusted EBITDA margin expanded to 47.8%, compared to 40.5% in the prior-year period, reflecting continued revenue growth, disciplined operating execution, and the inherent operating leverage of the Company's highly recurring business model.

A reconciliation of Adjusted EBITDA to Net Loss is included in the financial tables accompanying this release.

For the full year 2026, Csquare expects total revenues between $1.14 and $1.16 billion, Adjusted EBITDA between $490 and $500 million, recurring capital expenditures between $50 and $60 million, and non-recurring growth capital expenditures between $620 and $640 million.

The Company does not provide a reconciliation of forward-looking Adjusted EBITDA because certain items that affect the comparable GAAP measure cannot be reasonably provided without unreasonable effort.

IPO HighlightsDuring July 2026, Csquare successfully completed its initial public offering of 50.0 million shares of common stock at a public offering price of $21.00 per share and commenced trading on the New York Stock Exchange under the ticker symbol  NYSE: CSQR.

On July 27, 2026, the underwriters exercised their option to purchase an additional 7.499 million shares at the initial public offering price. In aggregate, the offering generated approximately $1.16 billion in net proceeds after underwriting discounts and commissions.

The IPO represents a significant milestone in the Company's evolution and strengthens its ability to execute its long-term growth strategy. As a public company, Csquare believes it is well positioned to benefit from enhanced access to capital markets, increased visibility among customers and investors, and greater strategic and financial flexibility.

The Company used the net proceeds to repay outstanding indebtedness, significantly reducing leverage and strengthening its balance sheet to support future investment opportunities.

Adjusted EBITDAWe define Adjusted EBITDA as net (loss) income, excluding (i) income taxes, (ii) interest expense, (iii) depreciation and amortization, (iv) gain on lease modification, (v) loss on extinguishment of debt, (vi) bargain purchase gain, (vii) stock-based compensation, (viii) other income (loss), net, and (ix) transaction and other costs. Transaction and other costs consist primarily of acquisition and integration costs, restructuring costs, costs associated with our initial public offering, and employee bonuses and loan extinguishment expenses directly attributable to specific transactions. The employee bonuses and loan extinguishment costs are recorded within Selling, marketing, general and administrative expenses in our Condensed Consolidated Statements of Operations. Management uses Adjusted EBITDA as a key measure of our operating performance and to assess the results of our business excluding certain items that we believe are not indicative of our core operating results. In addition, we believe Adjusted EBITDA is frequently used by securities analysts, investors, and other interested parties in the evaluation of data centers and other real estate companies. However, because Adjusted EBITDA is calculated before recurring cash charges, including interest expense and income taxes, which represent significant recurring cash charges necessary to operate our business, and is not adjusted for capital expenditures or other recurring cash requirements of our business, it should not be considered a measure of liquidity or an indicator of our cash flows and its utility as a measure of our performance is limited. Further, Adjusted EBITDA does not reflect our cash requirements or our ability to generate cash to meet those obligations. Other companies may calculate Adjusted EBITDA differently than we do and, as a result, Adjusted EBITDA may not be comparable to other companies' Adjusted EBITDA. Accordingly, Adjusted EBITDA should not be viewed in isolation or as a substitute for net (loss) income or any other performance measure calculated in accordance with U.S. GAAP.

Funds from OperationsManagement uses FFO, which is a non-GAAP financial measure commonly used in the real estate industry. This measure is used by management to evaluate performance corresponding to the retail colocation data center industry which has similarities to other real estate type companies. FFO is calculated in accordance with the standards approved by the Board of Governors of the National Association of Real Estate Investment Trusts. FFO represents net (loss) income (calculated in accordance with GAAP), excluding, when applicable (i) loss or gain from the disposition of real estate assets, (ii) depreciation and amortization and (iii) impairment write-downs of real estate assets and investments in entities when the impairment is directly attributable to decreases in the value of depreciable real estate held by the entity.

Management uses FFO as a supplemental performance measure because, in excluding the items identified in the calculation, it provides a performance measure that, when compared year over year, captures trends in utilization rates, pricing and operating costs. In addition, we believe FFO is frequently used by securities analysts, investors, and other interested parties in the evaluation of data centers and other real estate companies. However, because FFO excludes depreciation and amortization and does not capture the changes in the value of our data centers that result from use or market conditions, or the level of capital expenditures necessary to maintain the operating performance of our data centers, all of which have real economic effect and could materially impact our financial condition and results from operations, the utility of FFO as a measure of our performance is limited. Other companies may calculate FFO differently than we do and, as a result, FFO may not be comparable to other companies' FFO. Accordingly, FFO should not be considered in isolation or as a substitute for net (loss) income or any other performance measure calculated in accordance with U.S. GAAP.

Falling gasoline prices provided some relief to consumers in October, but Federal Reserve officials are increasingly concerned that inflation pressures elsewhere in the economy are proving harder to contain.

Prices at the pump declined by more than 4%, according to the Energy Information Administration, providing a respite from a surge that had added more than a dollar a gallon over the past year.

However, the consumer price index for the month, which the Bureau of Labor Statistics will release Tuesday at 8:30 a.m., is expected to show only a slight let-up.

Meanwhile, Fed officials say they are worried that inflation initially driven higher by President Donald Trump's tariffs and the Iran war is proving more persistent than expected, with price pressures emerging in sectors beyond those directly affected by the shocks.

At a time when the central bank is increasingly confident about the state of the labor market, concerns over the cost of living are what are top of mind for the Fed.

"We've been moving up to what historically we'd consider full employment, and the big problem has all been on the inflation side. We go around, we talk to people everywhere in this district, all we hear about is affordability on the business side, the cost," Chicago Fed President Austan Goolsbee said Monday during an event in his home district. "So we've got to come to terms with this cost-side and inflation-side failure."

The CPI reading for all items is expected to show an increase of 0.3%. Core inflation, which excludes volatile food and energy costs, is forecast to increase 0.2%, according to the Dow Jones consensus forecast. While both would be slower than the respective September monthly gains of 0.6% and 0.3%, they still represent further price pressures.

There will be no annual reading for October because of the government shutdown a year ago, which prevented the BLS from collecting some data and compiling a full index level. Because of the data issue, there isn't a comparable figure for the index.

Bank of America, using an assumed baseline interpolating data for the period last year, estimated the annual growth rates at 3.8% for headline and 2.7% for core, both 0.1 percentage point higher than September and well above the Fed's 2% goal.

Goolsbee said the current run on inflation is defying what normally could be expected from supply shocks and tariffs, with the current situation intensified by the hyperscaler build-out related to artificial intelligence.

"It was supposed to have gone away by now, and we're now starting to see the inflation kicking up in areas that probably weren't from oil prices and tariffs, like a bunch of services. Now this stuff with the AI data center build-out is, to my eyes, starting to look more just like old-fashioned demand overheating," he said.

The massive investment in data centers is creating demand for construction workers, electricity and equipment, potentially adding to price pressures even as AI promises longer-term productivity gains, he added.

Though the Fed in October voted to hold its benchmark interest rate in place, markets are pricing in a better than 80% probability that the central bank will raise rates by a quarter percentage point at its next meeting in December, according to CME FedWatch data.

Goolsbee, who doesn't vote on the rate-setting Federal Open Market Committee this year but will in 2027, didn't commit to which way he was leaning on additional rate hikes, though he said current conditions could warrant action.

"The conventional wisdom there is: Be aggressive up front, to never let the economy overheat, because once it gets overheated, it's extremely painful to get rid of that kind of overheating," he said. "So I think we've got to watch out in that space."

The CPI release comes with economic growth strong — 3.4% in the third quarter for gross domestic product and tracking at 3.5% for the final three months of the year, according to the Atlanta Fed — and the labor market seemingly in solid shape as well. Nonfarm payrolls expanded by a much better-than-expected 165,000 in October, while the unemployment rate edged lower to 4.1%, though inflation-adjusted wage growth has been negative.

That puts the inflation reading squarely in focus.

Bank of America said it sees tariff impacts fading but that goods prices could be pushed higher because of AI demand. Further, the firm is cautious about "sticky" services prices as well as pressures from health insurance and autos.

The bank has been hawkish in terms of Fed expectations and sees a December hike as likely so long as there isn't an escalated stock market sell-off that might dissuade the central bank.

"This will be the final inflation report before the Fed's December 8-9 meeting. If our forecast is correct, we think it would effectively lock in another 25bp hike, all else equal," BofA economist Stephen Juneau said in a client note. "Absent an indisputably soft 0.1% [month-over-month] print, we doubt the inflation data will dissuade the Fed from hiking in December. The bigger risk to our call comes from financial markets."

Wrought iron doors designed by Carla Fernández.

“When I began this project, I thought that we would be documenting a history, like the aura surrounding the artifacts in a museum, the apparitions of the past,” Wu writes in the book’s introduction. “Instead, what I’ve found is the vibrant, living presence of Indigenous ideas in Mexico.”

“In the guest bedroom, pink silk curtains dyed by Fernández cast a glow in the room. The bedspread is from her personal collection of Mesoamerican textiles, made of undyed wool in Teotitlán del Valle, Oaxaca.”—Su Wu

One space that exemplifies those ideas is Casa Flora, the colorful brutalist Mérida home and studio of the Mexican fashion designer Carla Fernández. As a designer, Fernández seeks to preserve the textile traditions of Indigenous and Mestizo communities through collaborations with artisans, bringing ancient practices to a contemporary audience. Fittingly, the home is full of Mesoamerican art and textiles.

Fernández led the renovation of the house, which dates back more than a century and now hosts workshops for Taller Flora, her design lab, which focuses on traditional Indigenous clothing techniques. (You can also stay in the home; the designer lists it for stays on Airbnb.)

Fernández’s husband, the artist Pedro Reyes, created several elements of the space, including a concrete outdoor shower, as well as some of the tiles throughout the space. His sculptures—including a prototype of Mitla Sillas, chairs inspired by the pyramids of the Mitla archaeological site in Oaxaca—are found throughout the home.

The outdoor shower designed by Pedro Reyes.

And in Carla’s home, I think there are many moments that exemplify this idea of the boundary between interior and exterior being not a physical one, but rather a marker of movement across psychological states.”
