Manchester United are emerging as the Premier League club showing the strongest interest in Como centre-back Jacobo Ramón as they begin planning the next stage of their defensive rebuild.

The 21-year-old has enjoyed an outstanding rise under Cesc Fàbregas in Italy, establishing himself as one of Serie A’s most promising young defenders.

His performances have now earned him a first senior Spain call-up, while United, Chelsea and Liverpool are all monitoring his progress.

According to AS, Man United have been the most persistent English club in their pursuit of Ramón, although Chelsea and Liverpool are also keeping the defender on their radar. Read the AS report on Jacobo Ramón

United have increased their monitoring of the Spaniard as they assess potential defensive additions for 2027.

The difficulty is that United do not control the situation. Madrid’s inexpensive buy-back clause gives them a huge advantage, while Ramón is attracting interest from several major clubs.

Still, United should remain in the conversation. If they want to build a younger, more technically secure defence, Ramón fits that strategy extremely well.

Kyle Whittingham Buyout Details in Contract Show Cost for Michigan to Fire Coach

📅 Nov 08, 2026  |  Category: Business  |  Read Source →

MINNEAPOLIS, MINNESOTA - OCTOBER 03: Head coach Kyle Whittingham of the Michigan Wolverines reacts during the second half against the Minnesota Golden Gophers at TCF Bank Stadium on October 03, 2026 in Minneapolis, Minnesota. (Photo by Tyler Clouse/Getty Images)

ANN ARBOR, MICHIGAN - SEPTEMBER 12: Head coach Kyle Whittingham of the Michigan Wolverines talks with the referees in the first half while playing the Oklahoma Sooners at Michigan Stadium on September 12, 2026 in Ann Arbor, Michigan. (Photo by Gregory Shamus/Getty Images)

Kyle Whittingham is in his first year with the Michigan Wolverines as their head football coach.

There will be fans calling for him not to get a second season and instead for Michigan to move on by paying the buyout amount in his contract.

It's been a bigger struggle this season for Michigan than anyone hoped. Whittingham didn't get enough out of Bryce Underwood, so he benched him for Tommy Carr, and it hasn't been smooth sailing for Carr, either.

That was before Michigan went out and didn't score through three quarters against Michigan State on Saturday, Nov. 7.

More and more, the Wolverines' fanbase is getting upset with Whittingham. Where exactly will that lead? Only time will tell — but it could be expensive.

GettyMINNEAPOLIS, MINNESOTA – OCTOBER 03: Head coach Kyle Whittingham of the Michigan Wolverines reacts during the second half against the Minnesota Golden Gophers at TCF Bank Stadium on October 03, 2026 in Minneapolis, Minnesota. (Photo by Tyler Clouse/Getty Images)

Whittingham came to Michigan to begin his coaching tenure with the 2026 season, and he got a hefty contract to do so.

He signed a five-year deal with a base salary of $41 million.

That began with an $8 million salary for 2026, going up by $100,000 each year, according to GBM Wolverine.

GettyANN ARBOR, MICHIGAN – SEPTEMBER 12: Head coach Kyle Whittingham of the Michigan Wolverines talks with the referees in the first half while playing the Oklahoma Sooners at Michigan Stadium on September 12, 2026 in Ann Arbor, Michigan. (Photo by Gregory Shamus/Getty Images)

Whittingham being fired without cause would result in him being paid 75% of his remaining salary, according to GBM Wolverine.

That means, for example, that he'd get paid 75% of $33 million if he was fired after the 2026 season. That works out to a $24.75 million buyout — which certainly isn't an unheard-of total in modern college football.

GBM Wolverine explained the outlook like this in an article earlier this season: "The good news for Michigan is that they aren't paying any other buyouts to former coaches. (Sherrone) Moore was fired for cause, and former head basketball coach Dusty May left for the NBA on his own. So, if Michigan wanted to pay the buyout, they probably have the money to do so."

It still would be a pretty rough look for Michigan to move on from its new coach after just a single season, although the Moore situation didn't set up the Wolverines to have the best head coach hiring cycle ever.

The reality is that Whittingham seemed like a quality hire based on his strong past, but it hasn't worked out the way they hoped in Ann Arbor.

Whittingham isn't necessarily a goner, but if he is at some point, Michigan can probably afford to make that happen.

Ciryl Gane Reacts to Josh Hokit Leapfrogging Alexander Volkov for UFC 334 Title Shot

📅 Nov 08, 2026  |  Category: Other  |  Read Source →

Ciryl Gane is just days away from his next heavyweight title defense, with Josh Hokit set to challenge him in the main event of UFC 334. The matchup came together after Tom Aspinall was forced to vacate the championship following further complications with his right eye, which had been injured again during his recovery.

Hokit will enter Madison Square Garden with an unbeaten 10-0 record and considerable momentum. His recent victories over Curtis Blaydes and a 41-year-old Derrick Lewis have helped propel him into the title picture.

However, his rapid rise to a championship opportunity has raised questions about whether he has done enough to leapfrog more established contenders. Alexander Volkov is among those who have every reason to feel overlooked.

The Russian heavyweight has won seven of his last eight fights, with his only defeat during that stretch coming against Gane at UFC 310. Even that result remains a point of contention among fans, many of whom believe Volkov deserved the decision.

Dana White had promised to address the situation following their controversial encounter, but the expected opportunity never materialized. Instead, Hokit has landed the next shot at Gane, leaving Volkov on the outside once again. Interestingly, even the champion appears puzzled by the promotion's decision.

During a recent interview with RMC Sport Combat, Gane was asked whether Hokit deserved to be fighting for the title ahead of Volkov. The Frenchman acknowledged that the American's personality may have played a significant role in securing the opportunity.

However, he also admitted that he would be frustrated if he were in the position of the contenders who had been overlooked.

He said, "If I put myself in the shoes of the guys in the top five, I wouldn't be happy. Naturally, I wouldn't be happy. I'd be thinking, 'How's this possible?' I've been here for a while, I've earned my spot."

Gane also brought up his own history with Volkov, acknowledging that their second meeting was far closer than the result suggested. He added, "You see the first Volkov fight I won, the second fight was super close, borderline controversial, practically a draw. I still deserve my shot to go there, you know? I understand the frustration and the desire to talk on social media. To defend yourself. But, you have to understand the game."

However, Gane believes that simply maintaining a strong position in the rankings is not always enough to secure opportunities in the UFC. According to the champion, fighters need spectacular finishes and memorable performances to strengthen their negotiating position.

He further explained, "Without that, you can't negotiate. If you have lackluster fights, kind of whatever, you need a great, pristine record or have lost to guys like that. It's all pawns. That's how it is."

Volkov's situation could have looked considerably different had the judges awarded him the split decision against Gane in their 2024 meeting. Instead, the Russian has spent considerable time trying to work his way back into title contention.

His recent victory over Rizvan Kuniev at UFC 333 has at least kept him in the conversation. With Gane and Hokit now preparing to settle the heavyweight championship at UFC 334, Volkov will be hoping for an opportunity to finally fight for undisputed gold against whoever emerges victorious at Madison Square Garden.

Liam Boyd's big boot brings Paul Bunyan back to Michigan State

📅 Nov 08, 2026  |  Category: Other  |  Read Source →

ANN ARBOR — Not all five-field-goal games are created equally.

Five against Toledo, nice. Five against Michigan, better. Much, much better.

"Yeah, for sure," fifth-year senior kicker Liam Boyd said with a shy smile following Michigan State's 15-6 win over Michigan at Michigan Stadium on Saturday, Nov. 7. "This means a ton. This game does.

"This is awesome. There's not really words for how I feel right now."

No worries. Boyd's boot did more than enough talking.

Boyd was 5-for-5 on field goals and accounted for every point as Michigan State pulled the upset, snapped a six-game losing streak and earned head coach Pat Fitzgerald his first Big Ten win in his first year with the Spartans.

Boyd was good from 21, 37, 38, 24 and 49 yards in the victory. Some of them, Fitzgerald could've gone for it on fourth down, but he played the situation game. Michigan State's defense was cooking — Michigan didn't score until there was 1 minute, 35 seconds left — and any points were going to be uber-valuable.

"I didn't want to kick any of those," Fitzgerald said afterward, to some laughter. "I told you guys I will be aggressive but not reckless. ... I thought we had the upper hand defensively. ... We were getting stops.

"If it was a shootout," Fitzgerald continued, "I'd probably play those plays a little differently."

He added, of Boyd, "Full confidence and belief in Liam."

From Boyd's perspective, he also stays with the mindset that he's going to get called in for the field goal, and even quipped that he gets a little disappointed when the offense decides to go for it on fourth down.

More: Michigan State safety Nikai Martinez dedicates rivalry win to late cousin

On Saturday, Michigan State decided to go with him, and he was prepared.

The team talked all week about how special teams would be a key.

"It's always a super tight game," said Boyd, who transferred to Michigan State this offseason, from Charlotte. "I knew going into it that myself and everybody in my room were going to have a big outcome on the game.

"I expect to make every kick when I go out there. I have that kind of conviction that it's going to go through."

Boyd got off to a fine start with Michigan State, making five of six field-goal attempts in the season-opening win against Toledo, including a 51-yarder. He hasn't had many chances since, going 6-of-8 the last seven games.

But his number (37, if you're scoring at home) got called over and over again against the Wolverines, and he led Michigan State to its first win in the rivalry since 2021.

His five field goals in a single game are one off the program record, set by Jonathan Kim in 2024. He's one of two Spartans to ever make five field goals in a single game twice, joining Paul Edinger, who also did it twice in the same season, in 1998. Edinger did it in a win against Northwestern. Nice. Then he did it the next week, in a win over No. 1 Ohio State. Better. Now, Boyd knows the feeling.

"I love this team and these coaches," said Boyd, who was 10-for-13 on field goals pre-Michigan State (at Charlotte and North Carolina), and now is 16-for-19 this season for the Spartans.

"It's just an awesome win for everybody."

InfoQ Homepage News Cloudflare Scales D1 Control Plane to Millions of Databases

Nov 08, 2026 2 min read

In a recent article, Cloudflare shared the redesign of the control plane of its D1 serverless SQL database to support millions of databases, avoiding centralized bottlenecks, implementing retries and idempotency, and using eventual consistency.

The D1 control plane architecture is built around Postgres for transactional metadata, ClickHouse for analytics, and Workers KV for globally replicated routing data. It keeps query processing at the edge, separate from centralized management operations, using distributed metadata caching, connection pooling, asynchronous workflows, and periodic health checks to improve scalability and recover from failures. Announced in 2022, Cloudflare D1 is a serverless SQL database built on SQLite and designed to run close to applications on Cloudflare’s global network. Over the past year, the number of daily active databases has grown 18-fold.

Cloudflare uses 30-second heartbeats, with per-database jitter, to reconcile each D1 Durable Object with Postgres, the source of truth for database existence and account limits. This mechanism makes failed creation or deletion operations self-correcting: orphaned objects are marked as deleted, and Workers KV routing metadata is repaired if best-effort updates fail. David Laban, full stack software engineer at Cloudflare, and Lambros Petrou, principal engineer at Cloudflare, write:

Heartbeats can tolerate slightly out-of-date data, meaning they can be served by Hyperdrive’s multiple caching layers or by a Postgres read replica. This heartbeat flow enables the Durable Object to self-correct to reflect the source of truth (Postgres) regarding the creation and deletion of databases.

Cloudflare keeps D1 queries off its centralized Postgres database by routing requests from an Edge API Worker through an Eyeball Worker directly to the relevant D1 Durable Object.

The Edge API Worker handles authentication, authorization, rate limiting, and request validation, while the Eyeball Worker trusts the database-specific authorization encoded in D1 Bindings, avoiding redundant checks in the query path. Laban and Petrou add:

You might also wonder why we use Workers KV when we already have Hyperdrive Caching. Workers KV is a durable datastore, not just a cache: if an item falls out of KV's edge cache, the read is served from KV's own storage rather than Postgres, keeping Postgres out of the data plane. In a catastrophic cache purge scenario, a million databases trying to fetch their routing information would lead to metastable failures.

Cloudflare enforces D1 storage limits using asynchronous usage updates, which are queued and batched to protect Postgres from write spikes. It maintains pre-aggregated account totals through incremental storage deltas, avoiding expensive scans and write contention for accounts with millions of databases. Durable Objects check these totals through Hyperdrive’s short-lived cache and block writes that would exceed the account limit. In a Reddit thread, user isaackogan comments:

It's an impressive architecture to be sure, but most of these gains are that it's an in-process database running on edge storage near you. The rest is orchestration around that. Cloudflare can leverage their massive global datacenter presence to do something that would be traditionally impossible for small shops, aka economies of scale at work.

In a previous popular thread, several Reddit users argued that D1 is unreliable, raising concerns about its availability and stability in production workloads.

Cloudflare D1 offers a permanent free tier that includes 5 GB of total storage, 5 million rows read per day, and 100,000 rows written per day. Beyond the free tier, storage costs 0.75 USD per GB-month, reads cost $0.001 per million rows, and writes cost 1 USD per million rows. D1 has no compute or data-transfer charges.

HBO's 36-Episode Sci-Fi Series Is A Masterclass In How Mystery Boxes Can Go Wrong

📅 Nov 08, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Few storytelling structures changed the TV landscape quite like the mystery box. The format drops viewers into a world filled with the unexplained, then spends several seasons delivering a drip-feed of answers. When done right, they’re incredibly satisfying. A strange detail in the premiere might seem like nothing until an episode two seasons later gives it a new meaning. Mystery boxes exploded in popularity in the 2000s thanks to addictive hits like Lost, though they’re far from a guaranteed recipe for success.

The intrigue that sets up a mystery box show in its opening episodes is easy to construct. Giving viewers a reason to want to keep revisiting it, however, is a different story. An opening episode or even season only needs a handful of strange clues to get the ball rolling, but the answers have to be worth the wait when they finally arrive. What’s more, the characters need lives and problems that make the central mystery one that audiences want to solve. Few shows proved how difficult a balancing act this is quite like HBO’s Westworld.

Westworld aired for four seasons from 2016 to 2022 before being abruptly canceled, and it's sadly remembered for all the wrong reasons. The sci-fi series begins its 36-episode run in a Wild West theme park where wealthy guests can indulge their fantasies at the expense of lifelike androids called hosts. However, all was not as it seemed thanks to the hosts being considerably more aware of their situation than they should have been. At first, Westworld seemed like it had all it needed to become the next Lost. Sadly, by the time it ended, the HBO show’s contribution to pop culture was being a case study in how not to do a mystery box story.

Westworld’s mystery box certainly was full of mystery. The problem was that mystery was pretty much all it was full of. The answers the show delivered were often simply new questions in disguise, and after a while it felt like the HBO show was more interested in keeping audiences guessing than it was in telling an enjoyable story. As the puzzles grew more and more elaborate, even figuring out where a scene fit into the timeline could take enough effort to make it borderline unwatchable. Non-chronological storytelling wasn't Westworld's greatest sin when it came to misunderstanding what makes its format appealing, either.

As Westworld went on, many reveals that were clearly meant to be impactful completely fell flat because viewers are still trying to figure out exactly what kind of show they’re watching and what the story was actually about. The cinematography, intense performances, and dramatic music underpinning a conversation would make it clear that it’s integral to the plot, but audiences couldn’t appreciate it because the show hadn’t made it clear enough why the scene mattered. Mystery box shows should leave their viewers asking questions, but the question should never be “why should I care”. Sadly, for Westworld, this increasingly became the biggest mystery surrounding its narrative.

Westworld fell into this trap because its compulsive need to construct a grand narrative puzzle came at the expense of character development. This meant that viewers inevitably cared less and less about solving the show’s mysteries with each passing episode. Understanding why a character reacts the way they do to a new reveal is integral to a mystery box being engaging viewing. Westworld’s underdeveloped characters removed this essential grounding, meaning that even its most shocking twists had minimal emotional impact.

This overreliance on the mysteries in its mystery box is why Westworld lost momentum so quickly after its admittedly solid first season. A new reveal or mysterious tease of things to come should make audiences want to see what happens next. When these twists and turns happen to characters viewers simply don’t care about in a story that’s too difficult to follow, however, the appeal of the mystery box format dwindles and dies incredibly quickly.

The increasingly elaborate puzzles and lack of substantial character development are only part of why Westworld was a failure. The other part was how dramatically HBO’s breakout hit changed from the intrigue of its central premise. The idea of a group of autonomous AI that exist as theme park attractions gradually gaining sentience was a fascinating one. Sadly, Westworld moved away from this setting and the narrative arcs that made its story followable after its first season. By the time it circled back to its original premise it was too little too late, as the show had already lost much of its audience.

A pivotal moment when it comes to Westworld’s downfall was in season 3, when the show flipped from being a uniquely distinctive sci-fi Western to a borderline-generic cyberpunk thriller. The park’s familiar Western routines had made the cruelty the AI hosts suffer at the hands of guests and their awakening after believing that they genuinely lived in the 19th century incredibly unsettling. Once the show moved to a futuristic city, Westworld felt like yet another near-future sci-fi show about the dangers of technology. The change meant the series lost everything unique about its identity.

Ultimately, HBO canceled Westworld after season 4, with its central story unresolved, then pulled it from HBO Max. All four seasons are available to buy digitally, but they’re not available to stream on any major platform. This speaks volumes about Westworld’s legacy as a blueprint for what not to do when it comes to mystery box storytelling. Many initially-divisive sci-fi shows manage to find pop-culture footholds years after they concluded thanks to arriving on a streaming service. Westworld’s legacy is the total opposite. It’s a show that HBO would clearly rather audiences forgot about, but its spectacular fall from grace ensures it will always be an infamous part of TV history.

25-Episode Netflix Adaptation Of A Classic Book Series Delivers Perfect Worldbuilding

📅 Nov 08, 2026  |  Category: Entertainment  |  Read Source →

Worldbuilding is one of the most important ingredients in any TV show, but it’s easier with some genres than others. For fantasy and sci-fi, spectacular elements like magical kingdoms and distant planets make for great foundations to build lore on. Comedies and dramas grounded in the real world, however, have much less to work with. Making the familiar feel distinctive is no easy task.

That makes one Netflix adaptation particularly deserving of recognition. Across three seasons between 2017 and 2019, it created a setting with more personality than many fictional universes that can boast elaborate maps and centuries of invented history. Its secretive world feels vast and governed by peculiar rules that make it seem quite unlike our own reality despite being set there. Even more impressively, it achieved all this across just 25 episodes. That show is Netflix’s A Series of Unfortunate Events.

Based on Daniel Handler’s 13 novels, published under the name Lemony Snicket, A Series of Unfortunate Events follows the three Baudelaire orphans as they bounce between adopted parents and foster families all while pursued by the murderous Count Olaf (Neil Patrick Harris). It has the sinister humor and theatrical atmosphere of a quirky fantasy, yet there’s no magic or supernatural elements to be found. Despite this, the world of A Series of Unfortunate Events feels distinct and different, and how the Netflix show turned everyday reality into somewhere so convincingly alien deserves celebration.

Netflix's A Series of Unfortunate Events may take place in the real world, but it doesn’t feel like it. It feels like a nightmare-like inversion of reality, both in terms of its aesthetics and in how the lives of the Baudelaire orphans play out following the death of their parents. Violet (Malina Weissman), Klaus (Louis Hynes), and baby Sunny (Presley Smith) are delivered from one incompetent caregiver to another, all while the authorities ignore the blatant schemes of Count Olaf and fail to keep them safe from him. They’re thrown into a system that definitely overlaps with the foster-care institutions of the real world, but operates with a disturbing incompetence that’s far removed from the genuine article.

As far as the aesthetics are concerned, A Series of Unfortunate Events creates its own world by making everything and everywhere look and feel slightly wrong despite being familiar. Its exaggerated sets make ordinary environments like a cheerily decorated living room seem capable of swallowing children whole. The unease this creates isn’t helped by the fact that the adults treat the obvious environmental dangers as perfectly reasonable.

The visual elements of A Series of Unfortunate Events’ worldbuilding are helped by the show’s deliberately scrambled relationship with history. There’s fashion, architecture, and technologies from various technologies, and this is once again presented as totally normal. Telegraphs and walkie-talkies, for example, are both treated like contemporary devices. The modern appears side-by-side with the antique in ways that technically could happen in the real world, but obviously aren’t the norm.

Patrick Warburton’s Lemony Snicket is the final masterstroke that ensures the reality presented by the Netflix show feels unique. He delivers his solemn narration like someone documenting an existing world, complete with personal regrets and information he cannot yet share. Every digression suggests another story happening beyond the children’s immediate predicament. Snicket’s musings make it clear that this world has a past just as rich as its present, making the absurdity of A Series of Unfortunate Events feel bizarrely plausible.

The convincing worldbuilding of A Series of Unfortunate events is definitely an achievement. However, the world of the Baudelaire orphans wasn’t created for the series. It came from Daniel Handler’s novels. This doesn’t make the creative vision of the show less noteworthy, however. Quite the opposite. Just as much as Netflix’s A Series of Unfortunate Events stands out for its small-screen worldbuilding, it’s also an example of a near flawless literary adaptation. The Netflix show completely understands why the A Series of Unfortunate Events books are so beloved.

Netflix’s A Series of Unfortunate Events succeeds as a show because it recognizes that the appeal of the books extends far beyond eccentric locations and elaborate misfortune (a mistake made by the divisive 2004 movie). The world of Handler’s books is a thematic exploration of children’s frustration with adults who possess authority without the intelligence or compassion to deserve it. It’s a setting whose central logic is that bureaucratic procedures are the governing power in all things, even when it blatantly puts the lives of children at risk. Any successful adaptation needs to bring this thematic core to screens, which the Netflix series definitely did.

The recurring failures of characters like Mr. Poe makes the world of both the A Series of Unfortunate Events Netflix show and books terrifying without requiring anything supernatural. The fact that those responsible for protecting Violet, Klaus, and Sunny repeatedly refuse to see danger that readers can is an essential part of why Handler’s novels are so engaging. The Netflix series recognized the importance of this, and that it was much more essential than the timeline of specific events or established visual details.

The show itself proves this, too, since the A Series of Unfortunate Events TV show makes multiple substantial changes to the timeline of the narrative. For example, it brings the Baudelaire’s connections to the shadowy V.F.D. organization forward so that their influence can be felt from the moment Violet, Klaus, and Sunny meet Count Olaf. Many other TV adaptations of books would have been lambasted for making such a huge alteration to the established story. A Series of Unfortunate Events gets away with it because the change helps to support a small-screen version of the narrative whose spiritual heart is a flawless recreation of everything that makes the source material so beloved.

Gli esterni alti di Fabregas giocano dentro il campo nei mezzi spazi ed è proprio lì che il Como devasta gli avversari".

Quali le chiavi del match che immagina? "Mi faccio delle domande. Come il Como riuscirà a presidiare le fasce? Come l'Inter difenderà il centro del campo alle spalle delle mezzali? Su questo facciamo attenzione, perché se si alzerà con gli interni di centrocampo lascerà spazi al Como… Quindi nasce un'altra domanda: l'Inter si snaturerà o accetterà il rischio?".

Quali gli aspetti che conteranno di più? "Conterà l'idea di gioco più che la scelta dei giocatori, sarà interessante capire cosa decideranno gli allenatori. Il Como a volte si abbassa e cerca i trigger point a metà campo e soprattutto è fortissimo sulla riaggressione, sulla riconquista delle seconde palle. Lì fa la differenza e stranamente fa pochissimi falli tattici, a differenza ad esempio della Roma. Per questo dico che l'Inter dovrà essere brava e attenta a non farsi sorprendere sulle seconde giocate. Contro il Como è più pericoloso perdere palla dopo un recupero che non recuperare proprio il pallone e lasciar finire l'azione. Per questo il Venezia ha fatto bella figura col Como, perché gli lasciava finire l'azione… Semplificando diciamo che l'Inter raramente potrà fare due tocchi col pallone".

Con Lautaro ancora out, meglio Pio Esposito o Bonny accanto a Thuram? "Non dimentichiamoci di un aspetto importante: all'Inter sta mancando tremendamente Lautaro, che è il giocatore che fa più tiri in Serie A, oltre 5 a partita. Thuram gioca meglio con Pio Esposito accanto, perché può fare la mezzaluna, può ruotare, può creare spazi. Bonny è meno compatibile, tocca tanti palloni e tende spesso ad allargarsi. Avrebbe più senso Pio, anche se c'è da dire che l'Inter è la squadra che fa meno lanci lunghi della Serie A e quindi sfrutta poco la sua fisicità. Un'altra chiave saranno comunque i cross, perché l'Inter è molto più forte fisicamente. Se ci sarà Pio Esposito potrà sfruttare i cross, anche dalla trequarti e non avrà bisogno di entrare in porta per segnare".
