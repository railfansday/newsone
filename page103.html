‘Seismic’ blue wave triggers the sinking of oil prices, energy stocks, and the chances of power permitting reform

📅 Nov 04, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Most energy stocks plunged on Wednesday—except for clean power and utilities in a few remaining reliably red states—as crude oil prices fell to one-month lows because of bullishness on Iran war peace talks and bearishness on the data center boom after Democratic blue wave wins in the midterm elections.

Even as Wall Street celebrated the anticipated gridlock from a splintered Congress and White House—the Dow Jones Industrial Average rose 3% Wednesday—most energy companies moved in the opposite direction, especially in states such as Texas that had the most shocking Democratic wins.

The other potential loser is the bipartisan legislation to expedite the construction of energy projects—and power the AI boom—that would benefit both renewable power and oil and gas pipelines. Anti-fossil fuel Democrats are now potentially motivated to take harder negotiating tactics. TD Cowen analysts slashed their projection for passing the Bipartisan American Affordability and Jobs Act (BAAJA) from 40% to 25% during the lame-duck session before the new Congress is sworn in.

Charles Hua, executive director of the PowerLines nonprofit, said the “seismic” blue wave from Congress to governors’ elections to public service commissions was a referendum on spiking utility bills, gasoline costs, and the inflationary ripple effects throughout the economy.

“You only get such seismicity in political results if something is fundamentally unsteady or unstable, and I think one of the big manifestations of that really has been energy costs,” Hua told Fortune.

In addition to voter discontent over the Iran war and fuel prices, there were more than 40 local ballot measure on restricting or regulating data centers. All but one of them—in North Dakota—passed. “That’s a staggering statistic,” Hua said.

As for Wall Street performance, there were clear winners and losers by utilities and independent power producers.

NRG Energy and Vistra, which are building out gas-fired power generation in Texas to help fuel the AI boom, saw their stock values drop precipitously by 12% and 15%, respectively. Gas turbine manufacturer GE Vernova dipped by 10%.

Analyst Julien Dumoulin-Smith of Jefferies specifically highlighted the “Texas shock,” noting that “the most impactful surprise is the magnitude by which Texas voted Democratic, imperiling the outlook for data center developments.”

Texas-based Tesla and SpaceX leader Elon Musk—after unsuccessfully spending hundreds of millions on GOP candidates—posted on X, “Data centers in space looking pretty appealing right now.”

On the other hand, utilities that operate in Florida, which remained Republican, were rewarded. Florida-based NextEra Energy jumped 5% and Duke Energy rose by 3%.

With more clean energy-loving Democrats coming into power, renewable energy companies also were rewarded. First Solar rose by over 4% and solar parts manufacturer Nextpower spiked by 13%.

After President Donald Trump downplayed the potential for an escalation in Iran and Vice President JD Vance blamed his party’s election losses on the war, the global oil benchmark fell by 8% to below $100 per barrel for the first time in nearly a month. As such, oil producers and refiners suffered on Wall Street too.

ExxonMobil and Chevron dipped by 4% and 5%, respectively. Refiners Marathon Petroleum and Valero Energy, which have reported record profits of late, plunged by 9% and 7%, respectively.

“We had a referendum on inflation, high oil prices, and Trump in general,” said oil forecaster Dan Pickering, founder of the Pickering Energy Partners consulting and research firm. “It feels like Trump will have more pushback and less maneuverability around how he wants to conduct the conflict in Iran.”

There may be optimism for peace talks in Iran, but this war likely is far from over, Pickering warned.

There is no clear compromise yet, and Trump could easily get frustrated and opt for escalation between now and January, which is what Trump himself predicted before the election.

“I think the Iran conflict gets more complicated for the U.S. We’ve been looking for an off ramp, but we want an off ramp that doesn’t leave the Middle East in chaos,” Pickering told Fortune. “I think that issue probably transcends the election. Even with more Democrats, I don’t think they’re going to want a bad outcome for the Iran crisis. Oil is down, but it’s not cratering.”

Domestically, much of the focus will remain on the data center backlash and permitting reform. Some of the biggest states for data center growth are Texas, Georgia, and Ohio and Democratic challengers all won the governor elections in those states while pushing for data center moratoriums.

“‘Moratorium’ certainly is the buzzword,” Hua said, but it’s not clear if those efforts equate to temporary pauses or outright opposition. “How high on the priory list is electricity? I suspect that electricity vis-a-vis data centers will remain a top-of-mind issue for these new administrations.”

The AI boom has brought both parties somewhat closer together in terms of supporting all-of-the-above energy solutions. That’s why a bipartisan permitting reform effort focused on expediting energy construction of all kinds was coming together. But a Democratic wave is expected to leave more of them arguing in favor of waiting until next year to propose a version that’s less friendly to fossil fuel projects. Such an effort could be risky because it would still require Trump’s signature.

“With greater leverage heading into 2027, Democrats have less incentive to accept pipeline provisions during the lame duck, pushing the debate toward a narrower clean energy-transmission package or no deal,” Dumoulin-Smith added.

The legislation would facilitate the growth of long-distance transmission lines, which most benefit renewable energy. As such, top transmission developer Quanta Services saw its stock fall by 12% Wednesday.

The legislation’s Democratic sponsor, Sen. Sheldon Whitehouse, D-R.I., implored his party to still support the bill on Wednesday.

“Democrats ran and won on affordability, and this bill lowers costs for families and will allow us to start delivering on our election promises,” Whitehouse told Fortune in a statement. “It’s a bird in the hand. We’d be fools not to take it.”

Jordan Blum is the Energy editor at Fortune, overseeing coverage of a growing global energy sector for oil and gas, transition businesses, renewables, and critical minerals.

Five Hacks Every Una Watch User Should Know

📅 Nov 04, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

The Una watch is pretty much made to be hackable, with its interchangeable parts and open development environment. But it’s also pretty barebones at launch, without many features available yet. However, here are five things I’ve discovered I can do with it—and I hope to expand this list quite a bit as the device and its community mature.

Because the Una watch has a regular USB-C port, you don’t need to remember any specific cable, dongle, or adapter to charge it. I knew that, but it didn’t occur to me until recently that I didn’t need a computer or a wall outlet to plug into the other end. I was able to take a USB-C to USB-C cable—the same kind I might carry around to charge my phone—and plug one end into my watch and the other into my phone. It charged!

(You can also charge some other devices from a phone, including an Apple Watch, but you’d need to carry around that specific adapter, which is why nobody bothers.)

The Una watch doesn’t have any built-in sleep tracking, but a few community-built apps aim to provide it. The Slumber app, for example, will let you know how long you slept and compare your most recent bedtime with when you usually get to bed. There are no sleep stages (honestly: refreshing, since watches are rarely good at this anyway), and the analysis is done on the watch itself.

But if what you’re really hoping for is a recovery score or an overnight HRV reading, the HabitLab app combines that with habit tracking. Every evening, the watch will buzz and ask you to check off which habits or factors played into your day: Alcohol and late meals are among the options, and the watch will automatically detect morning or evening workouts to add them to the list. In the morning, the watch asks you how you slept, and then it gives you a score based on your resting heart rate, HRV, and your subjective rating.

The app is supposed to be able to put together trends and insights from this data, but I haven’t managed to get it to do this. Maybe it’s waiting for more data (I’ve been using it a little over a week), or maybe it’s just buggy.

By contrast, the barcode app worked even better than I expected it to. It’s meant for a parkrun barcode; each participant in the free weekly 5K has a number that you can use to check your results online. But it works just as well for other barcodes.

I installed the Barcode app and entered the number for my library card. Perhaps because of the watch’s MIP screen, I found this barcode scanned more reliably than any barcodes I’ve previously tried to use on a watch. The library scanner picked it up just fine on the first try, and a phone-based scanner also had no problem with it. You can store up to six barcodes, so I’ve also entered a few others, like my grocery discount card.

The app supports QR codes as well as regular barcodes, and to enter a QR code, you just type in a shortened URL. (It needs to be 22 characters or less.) This means that the Barcode app is also a QR code creator, a nifty feature indeed.

Seeing how soon the sun sets is a bit of a downer as we leave daylight saving time and get into the dark winter months, but it’s also helpful in planning. The Sun & Moon Face puts a graphic on your wrist that shows how far along the sun is in its trajectory across the sky for the day. When evening is approaching, you’ll see just how little time you have left.

The app grabs your GPS location once a day, which means that for the first few hours I wore it, the sunrise and sunset times were incorrectly listed as 6 a.m. and 6 p.m. But as long as you aren’t flying to a drastically new location every day, the app does the job just fine. As a bonus, it changes the color of the screen’s sky to show daylight, nighttime, or sunrise/sunset colors.

If you want to hack this hackable watch, you might as well take the plunge and try to build something yourself. Instructions are here to download the development tools for Windows, Mac, or Linux, and there are tutorials for different features you might want to use and implement. There are also about a dozen example apps you can build on, including activity trackers, watch faces, and glances. I'm not much of a coder myself, but this is the friendliest setup I've seen for someone to get into building their own apps for a watch.

The Art of the Panoramic Aspect Ratio

📅 Nov 04, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Panoramic compositions offer another way to present a scene, preserving its horizontal breadth and giving the frame a different sense of scale, movement, and visual balance. They can be extremely powerful.

The proportions of a photograph influence how it is viewed. A conventional landscape frame provides a relatively balanced relationship between horizontal and vertical space, while a panoramic crop places considerably more emphasis on width. This changes the viewer’s experience, encouraging the eye to move across the image and take in the composition progressively.

That extended viewing experience is one of the format’s greatest strengths. Rather than concentrating attention within a relatively compact frame, a panoramic photograph gives visual information more room to unfold. The viewer can follow changes in light, examine relationships between foreground and background elements, and appreciate the full extent of the scene. This can make the photograph feel expansive without relying on dramatic processing or exaggerated perspective.

Panoramic images also have a distinctive sense of balance. Their proportions can create a feeling of openness and continuity, especially when the composition has a strong horizontal structure. The format can be quiet and understated or dramatic and imposing, depending on the subject, light, and arrangement of elements. It is not simply a way to include more of a scene. It changes which parts of the photograph receive emphasis and how the viewer moves through it.

The panoramic format works best when a scene’s horizontal breadth is an important part of its visual appeal. Landscape photography is an obvious application, but the format can also work well for architecture, cityscapes, environmental portraits, and other subjects where the surrounding space contributes to the image.

Before choosing a panoramic composition, consider what makes the original photograph compelling. If the subject depends on vertical elements or a strong relationship between foreground and sky, removing too much of the image’s height may weaken it. If the strongest visual information is distributed horizontally, however, a wider frame may create a more cohesive composition.

The format can also help simplify a photograph. Cropping away unnecessary sky or foreground reduces competing information and emphasizes the remaining elements. This is particularly useful when the original image contains substantial areas that contribute little to its visual impact. However, removing space simply to achieve a wider aspect ratio is not necessarily an improvement. Open areas can provide balance, establish scale, and give important details room within the frame.

It is worth comparing several aspect ratios before deciding on a final composition. A moderately wide image may preserve enough vertical information to maintain depth, while a more elongated format can create a stronger emphasis on horizontal movement. Neither is inherently better. The right proportions support the composition without sacrificing the information that matters.

A panoramic composition requires attention across the entire width of the frame. In a conventional photograph, the viewer may be drawn quickly toward a central subject. With a panoramic image, the extended proportions create more opportunity for visual movement, but they also make balance particularly important. A strong focal point should work with the rest of the frame rather than simply occupy the center.

Horizontal lines and directional elements can help establish that movement. The placement of the horizon, the alignment of natural or architectural features, and the distribution of contrasting areas all influence how the viewer moves through the photograph. Consider whether the composition has a clear visual starting point and whether the remaining elements support or interrupt that progression.

Negative space plays an equally important role. A wide frame does not need to be filled with detail from edge to edge. Open areas can provide visual relief, separate competing elements, and establish the atmosphere of the photograph. Removing too much space during cropping can make an image feel crowded, particularly when important subjects sit close to the boundaries.

Pay as much attention to the left and right edges as to the center. Small distractions become more noticeable when the composition is stretched horizontally, and an element that seems insignificant in the original frame may disrupt the finished image. Review the entire composition before releasing the shutter, and make small adjustments to camera position when necessary. The goal is not to fill every part of the frame, but to make each area feel intentional.

A wide aspect ratio can communicate a scene’s breadth, but width alone does not create depth. The relationship between elements at different distances remains essential. Overlapping forms, changes in contrast, and variations in light help establish separation and give the viewer a sense of how the scene extends into the distance.

Atmospheric conditions can be particularly effective in this regard. Haze, fog, rain, and changing light create tonal differences between near and distant elements. Preserving those transitions across a panoramic composition can give the photograph a strong sense of depth without requiring an exaggerated foreground.

Scale also benefits from thoughtful composition. Including recognizable elements within an expansive environment provides a reference point that helps viewers understand the size of the surrounding space. The challenge is to retain enough context for that relationship to be clear without allowing secondary details to compete with the main subject.

When editing, consider how much of the original foreground and background needs to remain. A panoramic crop that removes too much of either can flatten the photograph or change its sense of scale. The goal is to retain the relationships that give the image depth while letting the wider proportions emphasize the overall scene.

You can create panoramic photographs with almost any focal length. The choice depends on how much of the scene you need to include and how you want the different elements to relate to one another.

Wide-angle lenses capture a broad field of view and can preserve a strong relationship between foreground and background. They are useful when the composition depends on including substantial environmental context, although perspective becomes increasingly pronounced as the camera moves closer to foreground subjects. This can make nearby elements appear much larger in relation to the background.

Standard and telephoto lenses offer a different approach. Longer focal lengths isolate a narrower portion of the scene and can make distant elements appear more prominent within the composition. They are useful when the visual interest is concentrated in a particular section of the landscape or when the photographer wants to emphasize layers without including much foreground.

Camera position is just as important as focal length. Changes in height and direction can alter the alignment of elements, the amount of foreground visible, and the relationship between different parts of the scene. Take time to assess the composition before settling on a viewpoint, especially when working with a longer lens.

If the intention is to create a panoramic crop from a single exposure, leave sufficient room around important details. Cropping reduces the available resolution and may remove more of the original frame than expected. A little extra space gives you flexibility in editing and helps prevent important elements from being cut off.

There are two primary ways to create panoramic photographs: cropping a single exposure or stitching multiple frames together. Each approach has advantages, and the best choice depends on the scene, the available resolution, and whether anything within the frame is moving.

Cropping a single exposure is often the simplest option. It works particularly well when the camera’s native field of view already includes the necessary horizontal information and the resulting file retains enough resolution for the intended output. High-resolution cameras offer more flexibility, but it is still important to consider how much of the original image you’ll remove.

Stitching multiple photographs together can produce a much larger file with additional detail. This is useful when photographing a scene that extends beyond the camera’s field of view or when you intend to print a particularly large image. For consistent results, use a tripod when practical, keep the camera level, and overlap consecutive frames by approximately 25 to 30 percent. The overlap gives stitching software enough shared detail to align the images.

Manual exposure and a fixed white balance help maintain consistency throughout the sequence. If the camera changes exposure or white balance between frames, visible variations can appear in the finished image. Focus should also remain consistent. Consider focusing once and switching to manual focus before capturing the remaining frames, particularly when photographing distant subjects.

Stitching is not always the best choice. Movement within the scene can create alignment problems, duplicated details, or visible inconsistencies. Waves, foliage, clouds, and moving subjects may change position between exposures. In these situations, a single image may produce a more natural result. When stitching, also be mindful of nearby objects, since parallax can cause alignment errors if the camera rotates around the wrong point.

Post-processing shapes the final proportions and balance of a panoramic image. Most editing programs allow photographers to select a custom crop ratio or enter specific dimensions. Ratios such as 2:1 or 2.35:1 provide useful starting points, but there is no need to force every photograph into the same proportions.

Begin by identifying the visual information that needs to remain. Consider whether the sky, foreground, and surrounding details contribute to the photograph or simply occupy space. Adjust the crop gradually and compare different proportions. A relatively small change in height can have a significant effect on the image’s balance.

Check the position of the horizon and other prominent horizontal lines. Their placement should reflect the visual importance of the areas above and below them. Also review the edges for distractions, and make sure important details aren’t positioned awkwardly close to the boundaries.

For stitched panoramas, cropping also removes the uneven edges created during alignment. Leave enough room during capture to account for this, particularly when important details extend close to the edges of the frame. Once you’ve established the composition, inspect the full-resolution image for stitching errors, uneven transitions, and inconsistencies in exposure or color.

Avoid making the crop so tight that the image loses its sense of openness. One of the advantages of the panoramic format is the space it provides. Removing too much of that space can undermine the very quality that makes the format appealing.

Large prints are one of the most compelling applications of panoramic photography. The extended proportions make effective use of horizontal wall space, creating a presentation that feels distinct from a conventional landscape print. They are particularly well suited to areas where a standard rectangular photograph might appear too small or visually contained.

The viewing experience also changes with print size. A panoramic image encourages the viewer to move their attention across its length, taking in details and relationships that might be overlooked when viewing a smaller image on a screen. At a larger scale, subtle changes in tone, texture, and light become more apparent. The photograph can become something viewers return to rather than an image they take in at a glance.

The format is also versatile in interior spaces. Its proportions can work above furniture, along hallways, in offices, or across larger uninterrupted walls. A carefully composed panoramic print can provide a strong focal point without requiring an elaborate arrangement of multiple images. Its visual impact comes from the photograph itself and the space it occupies.

Preparing an image for a large print requires more consideration than simply selecting a wide crop. Resolution is particularly important. Cropping a single exposure reduces the available pixel dimensions, so check the final file against the print lab’s recommendations before ordering. If the intended print is especially large, stitching multiple frames may provide the additional resolution needed to retain fine detail.

The viewing distance should also inform the final output. A print intended to be seen from across a room does not necessarily require the same pixel density as one that will be examined from close range. Consult the printer’s specifications and consider the physical dimensions of the finished image before making final sharpening and output decisions.

The print surface influences how the photograph is presented. Matte papers reduce reflections and suit images with subtle tonal transitions, while luster and semi-gloss finishes add more surface sheen and can emphasize contrast. Canvas offers a different aesthetic, with visible texture and a softer rendering of fine detail. Choose a finish that complements the photograph rather than treating it as an afterthought.

Framing and presentation matter, too. Panoramic aspect ratios don’t always match standard frame sizes, so custom framing may be necessary. A mat can separate the photograph from its surroundings, while a frameless presentation gives the image a more continuous appearance. Consider the available wall space and the intended viewing distance when deciding on the final dimensions.

The strongest panoramic photographs aren’t necessarily the widest or most dramatic. Their success comes from the relationship between the subject, the composition, and the proportions of the finished image. How Aussie Bitcoiners can avoid 2027’s shock CGT hike: Part 1

📅 Nov 04, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

From setting up an SMSF or a company, to just leaving the country entirely, here are some strategies Australian Bitcoiners can employ to deal with 2027’s massive CGT hike.

The Australian government’s decision to scrap the 50% capital gains discount for all investments in the May budget came as a big shock for crypto owners. The new scheme effectively doubles the taxable component of a gain, minus a small discount for inflation.

But there are legal ways to minimize the amount of CGT you’ll have to pay — if you start prepping now.

Some Aussie crypto holders are taking drastic measures — like Finder founder and crypto king Fred Schebesta who is relocating to Singapore which offers 0% CGT, stating the tax changes were a “killer blow.”But there are less dramatic methods to reduce your CGT bills including setting up a self managed super fund (SMSF), a company to trade crypto at the company tax rate, or to just cross your fingers and hope the opposition will reverse the changes after the next election.

Since 1999, Aussie asset owners have only paid tax on half of their capital gain. From July 1, 2027 they’ll pay their full income tax rate — which means 30% CGT if your income and gains are between A$45,000 and $135,000, 37% for the portion between $135,000 and $190,000 and 47% above that. (All figures in this story are in Aussie dollars)

Adding insult to injury, low-income earners who aren’t on welfare like students and retirees will pay a minimum 30% CGT rate no matter how little the gain.

The only discount now available accounts for the effect of inflation on your cost basis. So if you buy a house for $1 million in 2027 and sell it for $1.5 million after 10 years, with inflation at 3%, you won’t have to pay tax on the $344,000 or so attributed to inflation.

“The challenge for cryptocurrency is that the CGT changes, indexation really helps slower growing assets not fast ones,” explains Andrea Yuen, Swyftx CEO.

So if you bought Bitcoin for $10,000 and sell it ten years later for $100,000, the inflation discount is only around $3,439. Under the old system you would pay tax on $45,000 of gains. Under the new system you’ll pay tax on $86,561 of gains.

Stockspot analyst Chris Bryki compared how ten different asset classes would fare under the new rules. “Crypto sits at the bottom because almost its entire return comes from capital growth,” he wrote in The Australian newspaper.

Here are a few different strategies to help crypto holders deal with the changes, ranging from little to no effort, to restructuring your entire life.

Any gain you’ve made before July 1 2027 will be taxed under the existing 50% discount scheme. So your best bet is to hope a raging bull market pushes the value of your portfolio as high as possible before that date.You should faithfully record the value of every crypto asset you have that day, as any gain from that value will be taxed at the higher rate.

Ben Simpson, founder of crypto advisory service Collective Shift, says Bitcoin’s performance since mid-year in a challenging macro environment bodes well for further gains before July.

But Simpson (who points out he’s not a financial advisor) adds, “I probably wouldn’t be betting the farm on the fact that we’re gonna have a huge bull run”

Yuen notes that prices aren’t within your control.  “A strategy isn’t a strategy if you’re just sort of hoping,” she says.

Both the official opposition The Coalition, and the insurgent opposition One Nation, have promised to reverse the changes. Crypto voters who feel strongly enough about this single issue, could vote for a change in government.However, the ruling ALP holds 94 out of 150 seats in the House of Representatives and will be difficult to dislodge from office. If the government does change — and the legislation can pass the Senate in time — it’s technically possible the changes could be reversed before anyone has to file taxes from July 1 2028.

But it’s probably more likely that any change to the system would occur after that date. Adrian Forza, director of Crypto Tax Australia, says this would make calculating gains very difficult.

Australian retirement funds are called superannuation funds, and once you are over 60 you can access your investments tax-free — even as they keep growing.Unfortunately none of the retail super funds allow clients to invest in crypto, so if you want to protect your crypto gains using this method, you’d have to set up an SMSF. About a third of Simpson’s high net worth clients invest in crypto via SMSFs.  “Buying Bitcoin and accumulating it there is a super solid idea,” he says, although he cautions against adding many altcoins. “You’re risking your retirement, which is an ultra long-term hold, on short-term altcoins,” he says.

Australian super fund AMP has invested in Bitcoin — but doesn’t allow its clients to do so.

The big drawback with SMSFs — explains the 27-year-old founder — is that younger crypto investors can’t access the money for decades.

Realistically then, it’s a strategy more suited to wealthier investors aged over 50, who have a shorter period to wait.

The government has also made running an SMSF a pain in the backside, with lots of compliance paperwork and audits, as well as a bunch of unavoidable fees that make it prohibitive for smaller balances. Annual costs typically range between $2,000 and $4,000, although online providers like Stake can reduce total annual costs to around $1,330, as long as you only buy Bitcoin or Ether ETFs.

You also have to sell any crypto and pay tax on it before you can rebuy it with your SMSF, and there are annual contribution limits to factor in, although Forza says you should be able to get almost $400,000 in there with a bit of creativity.

A strategy some billionaires employ to avoid paying tax is by borrowing against their assets, as repaying the loan often works out cheaper than paying tax on a sale.

Bitcoiners can now access a range of lenders, including BlockEarner, Veild and Swyftx, that let you employ a similar strategy by borrowing against your BTC as collateral.

“We like to bring that strategy to the masses, not just to billionaires,” laughs Yuen.

But it can be risky if you don’t have a billionaire’s deep pockets. Borrowers face high interest rates, relatively short loan periods (typically 12 months), and risk losing their Bitcoin if the market moves against them. You can’t borrow against the full value either — Swyftx has a loan-to-value ratio around 40%.”If your collateral drops below a certain amount, there will be a margin call,” she says.  “I would say it’s a bet on a price going up. I think you have to have a view that crypto will go up — or at least not go down so that you get margin-called... Otherwise, a crypto-backed loan wouldn’t make sense for you.”

Simpson doesn’t recommend this strategy due to the risks, but says it can pay off for some people. For example if you borrow at 10% interest, and Bitcoin compounds by 33% a year (as it has done in the past) you’d end up much better off than paying CGT.

But he warns it’s a leveraged bet that is best suited to sophisticated investors.

Bitcoin is down 28% over the past year, so anyone borrowing a year ago would have faced margin calls and possible liquidation as well as interest costs.

Borrowing against Bitcoin for a house deposit has become a more popular strategy. (BlockEarner)

Forza cautions that borrowing crypto can also be a CGT event in some circumstances, such as borrowing in DeFi. If you deposit ETH to Aave, it gets automatically swapped to WETH which  is a taxable event, and when you withdraw the ETH it’s another taxable event.

Borrowing from centralized lenders like BlockEarner or Swyftx is currently a grey area, and the ATO has not clarified if it’s a taxable event.”If you’re talking about [borrowing] ten grand fine. But if you’ve got three to five Bitcoin, I wouldn’t risk it because if it goes against you then you’re going to be paying tax.””Vield is the only one in Australia that’s got a private ruling that says their product is not a taxable event. No one else has got one.”Swftxy confirmed it has not got a private ruling and said clients should seek tax advice, while BlockEarner did not respond to a request for comment.

AMD's record quarter: Data center business doubles, while gaming craters

📅 Nov 04, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

AMD’s gaming revenue fell 40 percent in an otherwise record $13.4 billion quarter.

AMD reported third-quarter revenues on Tuesday with a record $13.4 billion, an increase of 45 percent year over year, as data center business nearly doubled and the company’s gaming segment cratered.

AMD CFO Jean Hu said, “We expect revenue to be approximately $17 billion, plus or minus 3%. The middle point of our guidance represents approximately 66% year-over-year revenue growth, primarily driven by the acceleration of our Data Center segment.” AMD says its Q4 revenue projections are up another 27 percent from this quarter’s results. As reported by Tom’s Hardware, Intel’s Q4 guidance places revenue estimates between $15 and $16 billion. That means that if both companies land where they expect to at the end of the fourth quarter, AMD would exceed Intel’s revenue for the first time in history.

The data center segment of AMD’s business was up 97 percent year over year, with a record $8.5 billion. AMD attributes the growth to demand for its EPYC line of enterprise server CPUs, along with the launch of the Instinct MI450 series GPUs. Sales of AMD Ryzen mobile chips led growth in the Client business, which includes desktop Ryzen processors and posted $2.95 billion in revenue, up 7 percent year over year.

Gaming revenue fell sharply for the company, posting a 40 percent drop in revenue at $773 million, which AMD attributes to “lower semi-custom and graphics revenue.” Operating margins in AMD’s data center business expanded from 25 percent to 34 percent, with operating income surging 168 percent to $2.88 billion.

Credit: AMD Credit: AMD

Embedded segment revenue, or all the SoCs, FPGAs, and boards under AMD’s umbrella that go into other hardware, was $1.1 billion, up 29 percent. AMD attributed this growth to gains made in the “aerospace and defense, automotive, communications, and industrial end markets.”

Credit: AMD Credit: AMD

The gaming segment of AMD’s business is composed of two main sources. There are semi-custom processors that AMD supplies to other manufacturers’ consoles, as well as its own Radeon graphics business. Where things can get confusing is that Ryzen processors, like those commonly installed in gaming PCs, are counted under the company’s client business, not gaming. According to AMD Chair and CEO Lisa Su, “In Gaming, revenue declined 40% year over year to $773 million, primarily due to lower semi-custom sales as expected at this stage of the console cycle.” The financial results do not break down the split between console chip sales and PC graphics cards.

Credit: AMD Credit: AMD

Along with the Q3 financial results, AMD used the occasion to highlight hardware still in the hopper. The new AMD Threadripper Halo Station, which AMD first showed at IFA in September, pairs a Threadripper 9995WX CPU with up to four Instinct MI350P GPUs and can run trillion-parameter LLMs locally. The system is slated for a release sometime in 2027, with pricing still to be announced.

AMD also announced that Windows-based systems running on its Ryzen AI Halo platform will be “coming soon.” Known as Project Zenith, the effort will support a developer-focused Windows experience on the current PRO 400 Series platform. Built on the Ryzen AI Max+ PRO 495 processor, the chip supports up to 192GB of unified system memory, with up to 160GB of that available to be allocated for graphics, and is capable of running 300 billion-parameter models at 4-bit quantization. On the call, Su stated that AMD “started shipping MI455X in volume in the quarter, and lead customers, including Anthropic, Meta, Microsoft, OpenAI, and Oracle, brought their first Helios racks online quickly.”

China Unveils Their Plan for a Crewed Moon Landing

📅 Nov 04, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Yesterday, on November 3rd, the deputy chief designer of China's crewed space program and chief designer of the lunar exploration program (Wang Xiang) unveiled an official statement detailing its plans for a crewed mission to the Moon. Under the proposed architecture, China will conduct two launches using the Long March 10 rocket, sending the Lanyue lunar lander and the Mengzhou crewed spaceship to the Moon, where they will rendezvous in orbit. Of the three taikonaut crew, two will then transfer from the spaceship to the lander and descend to the surface to conduct science operations.

The plans were outlined in a keynote speech Wang delivered at the 7th Manned Space Symposium, held from Oct. 31st to Nov.1st at Beihang University in Beijing. This year's theme was "Gathering Wisdom for the Galaxy: Digital and Intelligent Empowerment for High-Quality Development of Manned Spaceflight," and saw more than 800 experts and scholars from fields related to crewed spaceflight convene to discuss strategies, technologies, and the hurdles China's space program will face.

These plans echo the preliminary plan statements made at the 9th China (International) Commercial Aerospace Forum in July 2023. As Deputy chief engineer Zhang Hailian of the China Manned Space Engineering (CMSE) office said at the time, two Long March-10 rockets would launch the elements to the Moon separately. Zhang's slideshow also included a rendering of a lander depicted on a mural inside China's Manned Lunar Deep Exploration Project Office.

The CMSE unveiled the lander design to the world in September 2023 and officially named it Lanyue ("embracing the Moon") in early 2024. Since then, development of the Long March 10, Mengzhou spacecraft, and Lanyue lander has proceeded apace and reached several key milestones. This included a tethered test of the lander last year, which successfully simulated descent, guidance, control, and engine shutdown.

The Mengzhou spacecraft also successfully completed a series of zero-height abort and maximum dynamic pressure escape tests in June 2025 while the Long March-10 successfully completed a low-altitude demonstration and validation test earlier this year. These developmental milestones place China on track to complete a crewed lunar mission by 2030.

In an interview with China's state-controlled media company, CCTV, Wang said that their planning approach follows two principles and will consist of using different vehicle variants for different stages. To prepare for rendezvous in lunar orbit, China will use the Tiangong space station in Low Earth Orbit (LEO) to validate the rockets, lander and space capsule. An uncrewed flight mission of the Lanyue lander will also happen before a crew landing is attempted, which will gather critical environmental data on the luanr surface.

In addition, Wang highlighted how in preparation for crewed missions, the mission components will all be tested in an uncrewed state first:

We will use the near-Earth version of the Long March 10 to launch the near-Earth version of the Mengzhou spacecraft to perform space station missions in Low-Earth Orbit, validating the rocket's basic technologies and the critical re-entry and return phases of the spacecraft. Then we will use the lunar version of the Long March 10 to launch the lunar version of the Mengzhou spacecraft to execute the lunar landing mission. This is the validation process for the rocket: near-Earth first, then the Moon.

Artist's recreation of the two Chinese spacecraft that will put 'taikonauts' on the Moon. Credit: CSM

For the near-Earth phase of the plan, the Long March 10A (CZ 10A) will be used, a single stick variant with only two stages and no boosters. For the lunar mission, the standard, three-stage variant with additional boosters will be used, which has a payload capacity of 27 metric tons (~30 U.S. tons). This process is virtually identical to what NASA has been pursuing with its Artemis Program. Initially, NASA hoped to use the Block 2 variant of the Space Launch System (SLS) to deliver crew and payloads to the lunar surface (which has since been cancelled).

China's incremental "near-Earth first, then the Moon" approach is also very similar to the NASA's current launch plans. Before a crewed Orion spacecraft flew four astronauts around the Moon with Artemis II, NASA launched the uncrewed Artemis I capsule on a circumlunar flight to validate all the key systems. China's stated plans for an orbital rendezvous is also similar to NASA's revised plans for the Artemis III mission scheduled for next year, involving the seperate launch and an Orion spacecraft with a yet-to-be-determined Human Landing System (HLS).

"In an uncrewed state, we will validate many key technologies of the entire crewed process as possible, including launch, orbiting the Moon, docking with the Mengzhou spacecraft, landing and staying on the lunar surface, taking off again, and re-docking with the Mengzhou spacecraft," Wang added. "This is to ensure that the crewed flight is safe and reliable." Wang further indicated that the program has established system plans, technical standards and quality requirements under a single development framework.

China's long-term vision includes the creation of an International Lunar Research Station, the construction of which is currently planned to commence in 2031 using the super heavy-lift Long March 9 rockets. Roscosmo's involvement in the project is unclear at this time due to Russia's ongoing war in Ukraine and the way this has led to record lows in terms of annual launches and delays in the super-heavy Yenisei rocket. The details of this base are sitll somewhat vague, with the stated description claiming it will be located on the surface or in orbit.
