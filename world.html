Syfy Cancelled Its Ambitious Sci-Fi Series — Now It’s Prime Video’s Greatest Space Opera

📅 Nov 04, 2026  |  Category: World  |  Read Source →

After just three seasons, Syfy decided to pull the plug on an ambitious sci-fi series. Adapted from a bestselling book series, the show in question consistently landed in critics' books and even acquired a cult following. Yet, its run was cut short after three seasons. Fortunately, this is when Prime Video stepped in and renewed the series before it lasted for three more seasons.

Adapted from James S.A. Corey's acclaimed book series, The Expanse was first developed by Mark Fergus and Hawk Ostby for the Syfy Network. However, in 2018, Syfy prematurely ended the show's run in 2018. Sci-fi fans were devastated as The Expanse was easily one of the greatest additions to the genre in the 21st century.

Even some of the best sci-fi series, no matter how much potential they initially show, do not return after being canceled. Perhaps, in a parallel world, The Expanse, too, saw the same fate. Fate, however, had different plans for the series. After extensive campaigns from viewers, Amazon picked up the show and continued it for three more seasons, which cemented its "masterpiece" status.

Even in its first three seasons, The Expanse was undeniably an incredible sci-fi series. This explains why many relentlessly campaigned to bring it back. However, it was still short of becoming the masterpiece it eventually turned into. Everything from its portrayal of Newtonian physics to geopolitical conflicts between Earth, Mars, and the Belt were compelling in the first three seasons.

However, the show initially struggled with pacing issues. Especially in season 1, The Expanse felt inconsistent when it balanced Joe Miller's detective noir arc on Ceres with overarching political tension surrounding the Rocinante crew's escape. Syfy's network budget also limited the visual scope of the series, although its VFX team still did an incredible job.

Even main characters in The Expanse, like Holden, Amos, Naomi, and Alex, were yet to break free from their early archetypes in the show's initial chapters. The Expanse season 3 also had to race through Book 2 (Caliban's War) and significantly compress Book 3 (Abaddon's Gate) before ending its run. All in all, the sci-fi series was from its true potential after three installments.

The Expanse started putting its brilliance on full display after it returned for three more seasons on Prime Video. If it wasn't for its revival, it seems possible that The Expanse would have become another forgotten sci-fi series that was canceled a little too soon.

Landing on Prime Video after its revival not only allowed The Expanse to slightly extend its episode runtimes but also become more realistic. With no broadcast censors holding the show back from exploring the grit and violence of its world, The Expanse gradually became a more accurate and relatable representation of how space colonization works.

Going into season 5, The Expanse also finally got to split the Rocinante crew across different locations. This, in turn, helped the series explore and test the individual strengths and journey of its main roster. Even story beats surrounding the Ring Gate and the Marco Inaros and the Free Navy appeared much later, allowing the series to dive deeper into its portrayal of revolutionary stress and economic extinction.

All in all, if Prime Video had not given The Expanse a second chance, its legacy would have been far less long-lasting than it is today. While it is unfortunate that even Prime Video canceled the show before it could cover the complete length of its source material, it is hard not to be grateful that Syfy's cancellation did not mark the end of its run.

The Expanse is a sci-fi series set in the distant future where humanity has spread out across the solar system, but the alliances between the three most potent governing bodies have reached a state of cold war. A mixed-up crew finds themselves at the center of a dangerous intergalactic conspiracy that threatens to bring war to the colonized worlds.

Winter Container Inspiration From New Jersey

📅 Nov 04, 2026  |  Category: World  |  Read Source →

Hi GPOD! Today we’re returning to New Jersey to see more from Ellen Goldfarb and Tracy Buttrick. After sharing their beautiful fall containers with us, Ellen is back with some winter inspiration using colorful branches, evergreens, berries, pinecones, and more.

This beautiful antique lead planter in Ridgewood, New Jersey, always calls for a generous amount of greenery and nice height. We try to change what we do from year to year. This time we used curly willow, artificial white berry picks, magnolia leaves, red winterberry (Ilex verticillata, Zones 3–9), and frosted pinecones. Silver bell eucalyptus, silver brunia stems, noble fir (Abies procera, Zones 5–6), and incense cedar (Calocedrus decurrens, Zones 5–8) fill out the arrangement.

This is the same antique planter and house as in the previous photo, but from another winter. That year we used a combination of yellow twig dogwood (Cornus sericea ‘Flaviramea’, Zones 3–7) and red twig dogwood (Cornus sericea cvs., Zones 2–7) for lots of winter color. We also used artificial berry picks in pink and white and natural brown pine cones.

We like to use real berries, but sometimes the birds eat them before the season ends. Artificial berries give the client guaranteed color for longer. For greenery, we used desert juniper (Juniperus osteosperma, Zones 3–9), balsam fir (Abies balsamea, Zones 3–6), and incense cedar.

This slim planter sits on a small sliver of a step at an entry in Ho-Ho-Kus, New Jersey. It is simple but one of my favorites, with stunning red twig dogwood stems. We buy these from a farm that also grows and sells the more common darker-red stems.

Beneath the red is a froth of desert juniper. Dried lotus pods (Nelumbo cvs., Zones 4–10) and clusters of silver brunia punctuate the greenery. The finishing touch is a frilly skirt of bright chartreuse incense cedar.

This one sounds like an outfit, doesn’t it?

One of a pair of pots on the front porch of a Mission-style house in Teaneck, New Jersey, these had plenty of space for tall branches. We liked the combination of golden curly willow (Salix matsudana ‘Tortuosa’, Zones 4–8) and redtwig dogwood against the creamy tan stucco.

We tucked in shiny magnolia leaves with taller silver bell eucalyptus, then added natural brown pinecones and small clumps of silver brunia. A generous, draping swath of incense cedar finishes the bottom of the arrangement.

This is a window box in Englewood, New Jersey. A classic house calls for a classic planting. We used red winterberries, magnolia leaves, frosted pinecones, and silver brunia clusters. Some noble fir and our favorite evergreen, incense cedar, finish the arrangement.

This is a close-up detail of a planter in Franklin Lakes, New Jersey. Dark red twig dogwood stems are mixed with curly willow and branches of multiflora rose hips. We love using the rose hips, but they are sometimes hard to find.

This pot also had artificial white berry picks and Eastern white pine (Pinus strobus, Zones 3–8). Silver brunia on longer stems, noble fir, and incense cedar complete the arrangement.

Thank you for sharing more of your beautiful container designs with us, Ellen and Tracy! I love seeing how many different looks can come from a relatively small palette of winter materials. Changing the color and shape of the branches, berries, cones, and evergreens gives each container its own personality.

We have even more of Ellen and Tracy’s winter containers to share, so we’ll be back tomorrow with another round of inspiration!

‘Painful’ – Alexis Mac Allister doesn’t hold back about ‘unpleasant situation’ at Liverpool

📅 Nov 04, 2026  |  Category: World  |  Read Source →

Alexis Mac Allister has spoken out about his ‘painful, unpleasant situation’ at Liverpool in terms of not being offered a new contract.

The Argentine caused a stir in September when he said that he was ‘very sad‘ not to have been offered a new deal by the club, having seen fellow 2023 signings Ryan Gravenberch and Dominik Szoboszlai both pen contract extensions earlier this year.

The Telegraph’s Dominic King reported last month that Anfield chiefs aren’t in ‘any rush’ to begin talks over a new deal for the Reds’ number 10 (who’s contracted to June 2028), despite his resurgence in form since the start of this season.

Appearing on the Los Edul show in his homeland, Mac Allister spoke candidly about Liverpool’s decision not to offer him new terms over the past few months.

The 27-year-old said: “We’re in a situation where I have a year and a half left on my contract. Last season I was told that the club’s idea was not to renew it, at least for now, and obviously that was a painful, unpleasant situation.”

When asked if the situation was making him despondent, he replied: “Not that much, because at the end of the day, I still had – and still have – time left at this club, which is what I want most – to stay here.

“The reality is that the manager has now changed, the sporting director has changed, and new investors are coming into the club, so I think I’m now in a position where anything can happen. We’ll see what happens in the future. I’ve always said that I’m very happy here, that I really enjoy it, and we’ll see.”

Mac Allister added: “Look at Szoboszlai and Gravenberch. They’re two players who were in the same contractual situation as me, and when I was told that, they renewed their contracts.

“It was more a position taken by the club, and generally that’s how things are done. At the end of the day, as a club you don’t want to lose a lot of money, and you don’t want players leaving on free transfers either.”

That the Argentine has spoken out publicly about his contract situation once more gives a strong hint that privately he’s seething at not being offered a new deal in the same way that Szoboszlai and Gravenberch were.

Admittedly Mac Allister’s collapse in form last season may have triggered a reluctance from the club to commit to a contract extension for a player who’ll be 29 when his current terms expire, and he’s a bit older than his aforementioned midfield teammates.

However, the ex-Brighton man has been excellent in the first two months of this campaign, almost as though he’s motivated by a burning desire to earn a new deal at Anfield and show that the hierarchy have been mistaken in their reluctance to tie him down for the long-term.

The 2022 World Cup winner is currently on £180,000 per week at Liverpool, a figure exceeded by 10 of his teammates (Capology), and it could be the case that he’s seeking a higher amount than what the club are prepared to offer him.

Mac Allister could hardly have made his desire for a new contract any clearer. We don’t know exactly why his situation is taking longer to resolve than those of Szoboszlai and Gravenberch, but if he keeps performing on the pitch, then surely it’s just a matter of when and not if he’s offered an extension by the Anfield hierarchy.
