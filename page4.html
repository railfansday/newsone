Democrat Roy Cooper wins a North Carolina Senate seat

📅 Nov 04, 2026  |  Category: Politics  |  Read Source →

Former North Carolina Gov. Roy Cooper, the Democratic Senate nominee, speaks at a campaign event on Sunday in Concord, N.C. Allison Joyce/Getty Images hide caption

Midterms coverage: Live results | What to know | Stories around the countryStay connected: NPR Politics Podcast | NPR Politics newsletter | NPR app

Democrat Roy Cooper has won North Carolina's open U.S. Senate seat, according to a race call from the Associated Press, providing the party with a key Senate flip.

The former governor becomes the first Democrat elected to the Senate from North Carolina since 2008.

Cooper defeated Michael Whatley, a former Republican National Committee chair who President Trump picked to replace retiring GOP Sen. Thom Tillis.

Cooper and national Democratic Party groups significantly outspent Whatley and Republicans. A few weeks before the election, national GOP organizations pulled TV ads from North Carolina and redirected their money to other battlegrounds like Kansas.

Cooper, who served as governor from 2017 to 2024, focused his campaign on affordability issues. Each campaign stop he made across the state was promoted as the "Make Stuff Cost Less" tour.

Meanwhile, Whatley's campaign centered around crime and immigration issues. He criticized Cooper over a COVID-era legal settlement that released inmates from state prisons to address overcrowding during the pandemic. But polls indicated that the economy — not crime — was the top concern for North Carolina voters this year.

The race was Whatley's first run for elected office, and Cooper's campaign painted him as a lobbyist for Big Oil who would become a rubber stamp for Trump in the Senate.

Patrick Sebastian, a Republican strategist who did not work on the Whatley campaign, said his party's candidate should have made the economy a bigger focus.

"Really it's the economy is Issue A and then Issue B is probably government corruption, that people are following the most," Sebastian said. "So when we're not talking about those issues, it's going to be hard to connect with people that go to Walmart for their groceries and can only get 80% of what they used to buy because the prices have gone up."

The North Carolina Senate race was initially expected to be one of the most competitive — and most expensive — in the country, but Whatley never led in polling, and Cooper's lead widened over the course of the race.

Even as pro-Whatley TV ads largely disappeared in October, Trump and Vice President Vance came to North Carolina for multiple rallies in the final weeks of the campaign. Sebastian says those visits likely helped improve base turnout for GOP voters, but weren't enough to save Whatley's campaign.

Tillis announced in 2025 he wouldn't seek reelection, after Trump threatened him with a primary challenger for failing to support the president's tax cut and spending bill. Rather than campaign for Whatley, he's been promoting his new book criticizing fellow Republicans.

Should You Buy Alphabet Stock After Anthropic Raised Its Google Commitment to $121 Billion?

📅 Nov 04, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

The Claude maker just raised its minimum spending commitment to Google. Can it pay?

Anthropic, the artificial intelligence (AI) company behind Claude, updated its initial public offering (IPO) filing on Monday, Nov. 2, adding its third-quarter results. On Sept. 30, it had $95.6 billion in cash and cash equivalents and $36.3 billion in short-term investments -- around $132 billion combined, near its total at the end of June.

Against this, Anthropic has signed purchase commitments and leases worth about $895.4 billion, almost all for compute and infrastructure. The first filing, on Oct. 26, left that figure blank.

And one of the largest parts goes to Alphabet (GOOGL +0.37%)(GOOG +0.08%). Anthropic now says it committed to spend at least about $121.3 billion with Google between April 2026 and July 2033, no matter how much it uses. Last week's filing had that number at about $114.5 billion.

With Alphabet shares near $283 as I write, is this durable revenue for Google Cloud, or a bet on a customer that lost money from operations last quarter? I think it's mainly the former over the next couple of years.

Google Cloud's revenue backlog (contracted revenue not yet recognized) hit $560.8 billion on Sept. 30. Three months earlier, it stood at $513.9 billion. And a year before, Alphabet's total backlog, mainly Google Cloud, was just $157.7 billion, under an older count that left out contracts for a year or less. But growth has slowed lately, with around $47 billion added in the third quarter versus almost $52 billion in the second.

Anthropic's $121.3 billion minimum equals around 22% of that cloud backlog. Alphabet doesn't say how much of the number comes from Anthropic, and some of the minimum has probably been spent since April. But it's a big share tied to one customer.

Spread across a bit more than seven years, the minimum averages around $16.5 billion a year. Google Cloud's third-quarter revenue climbed 103% year over year to $30.7 billion, a rate of around $123 billion a year. This puts Anthropic's average minimum at about 13% of the cloud unit's current sales -- important, but not the whole business.

Anthropic also estimates it could spend another $125 billion to $150 billion on Google's custom AI chips, though some of that might go through third-party providers rather than Google.

For the next year, Anthropic looks able to pay. Around $69.8 billion of its commitments are due in the 12 months after Sept. 30. Its cash and investments cover it almost twice, not counting $14.8 billion of credit it hasn't tapped or any proceeds from its IPO.

The business is helping. Third-quarter revenue climbed around 47% from the second quarter to about $17.0 billion, more than 12 times its revenue for the year-ago quarter. Multiply that by four, and it's close to what comes due in the next 12 months.

Operating cash flow for the first nine months of 2026 was a positive $6.6 billion, versus negative $2.0 billion for the same period of 2025.

But growth cooled from the second quarter's 144% sequential jump. Anthropic had a third-quarter operating loss of $567 million, which included around $2.0 billion of non-cash charitable contributions. A year before, it lost $3.1 billion.

Still, compute and infrastructure costs rose back to 58% of revenue in the third quarter, against 48% in the second, but they were 148% of revenue a year ago.

Later on, the bills get far bigger. Around $447.5 billion of the commitments are expected before the end of 2030 -- about $105 billion a year, well over Anthropic's current revenue pace.

"We must grow our customer base and expand revenue rapidly in order to generate the cash flows necessary to satisfy these commitments," Anthropic wrote in the filing.

If Anthropic spends less than its minimum, it must pay Google the difference. Of course, that payment only helps Google if Anthropic has the cash. And Google has agreed to provide up to $20 billion of financing, in convertible notes or stock, subject to conditions, so part of what Anthropic pays Google might come from Google itself.

Alphabet doesn't need Anthropic to work out perfectly for its stock to make sense. Shares trade at around 19 times expected 2027 earnings, about 31% under their 52-week high. And Google Cloud earned $11.1 billion of operating income in the third quarter, around three times the $3.6 billion it made a year before.

Does the amended filing change my take on Alphabet? Not much. But I'd treat the next few years of the Google commitment as solid revenue and the later years as a bet on Anthropic's growth.

Overall, I think Alphabet stock is still worth a look at this price.

AI cloud startup Applied Compute raises $350M after reaching key revenue milestone

📅 Nov 04, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Applied Compute Inc., a fast-growing provider of artificial intelligence infrastructure, has raised $350 million in funding at a $3.25 billion valuation.

The startup announced the deal on Monday. It stated that prominent angel investor Elad Gil led the round with participation from Nvidia Corp., Shopify Ventures, HubSpot Ventures and over a dozen others.

Applied Compute operates a cloud platform that developers use to train large language models and perform inference. Additionally, it has a team of machine learning researchers that can lend customers a hand with their projects. The group takes on tasks such as creating quality evaluations for newly developed models.

The software stack that AI researchers use to train LLMs usually comprises two main components. One creates multiple copies of the model, assigns a different task to each one and turns the results into training data. The other component uses that training data to refine the LLM’s weights. The two components usually run synchronously, which means that each one has to wait for the other to complete its work before launching.

Applied Compute’s platform uses a more efficient training approach called asynchronous RL. It enables a training stack’s data generation and weight adjustment components to run at the same time, which speeds up LLM development. Additionally, the platform supports a second optimization method called self-distillation. It removes the need for teacher models, specialized neural networks that researchers use to supervise LLM training runs.

A built-in AI assistant automates repetitive aspects of customers’ work. It can, for example, summarize what new skills an LLM learned during a training run. Uncovering that information usually requires researchers to review lengthy reasoning logs.

Applied Computing’s platform also lends itself to running inference workloads. An autoscaling engine automatically adjusts the amount of hardware allocated to a model based on user traffic. A so-called online RL feature uses the prompts that the LLM receives to periodically fine-tune its weights, which keeps it aligned with customer requirements.

Applied Computing offers its training and inference features alongside a model router called Shift-1. It enables coding agents to send each user request to the LLM that can fulfill it in the most cost-efficient manner. Furthermore, it can split complex tasks among multiple LLMs.

Applied Computing disclosed in its funding announcement that its annualized recurring revenue has topped $100 million. The company, which launched less than 18 months ago, counts Microsoft Corp., DoorDash Inc. and other major tech firms as customers. It will use the newly raised capital to grow its market presence and buy more infrastructure.

“Training, inference, and routing are the first pieces of a much larger stack,” Applied Compute founders Rhythm Garg, Yash Patil and Linden Li wrote in a blog post. “We’re building the AI infrastructure every company will need to fully own their intelligence, from the models themselves to the systems that run, monitor, and secure them.”

AMD CEO Lisa Su said in the company’s quarterly earnings call on Tuesday that Q3 2026 was “was the largest and most profitable in our history.” AMD reported a record $13.4 billion in quarterly revenue, and it’s predicting it will hit another record high next quarter of $17 billion. The company’s data center business is driving that growth, with $8.5 billion in data center quarterly revenue for Q3 2026. Su said AMD’s expecting its revenue to “grow more than 80 percent in 2027,” adding that “data center will be the largest contributor.”

Eventually, most of AMD’s revenue might come from its data center business. In response to a question during Tuesday’s earnings call, CFO Jean Hu said, “Looking ahead of 2027, we actually think our data center business will be more than 80 percent of total company revenue.”

As of Q3 2026, roughly 63 percent of AMD’s net quarterly revenue was from its data center business. As AMD keeps smashing its data center revenue records, its gaming business dropped 40 percent compared to last year, dipping to $773 million. PCs, game consoles, and other tech have been hit with price hikes over recent months due to shortages of RAM and other components, driven by demand from AI data centers. The shortage might not end anytime soon — Intel said it’s not expecting the shortage to improve next year, and SK Hynix predicted it’ll last until 2030.

Su acknowledged the price hikes, saying, “Our view is, this is one of those times — when, you know, component prices go up, it affects overall system pricing. One would expect the consumer markets to be more sensitive to that, and so we have seen a bit of a slowdown in the sell-through.” Su added, “We are planning with the current environment for a down year for 2027.”

SURROUND Presents: Why Good Product Design Requires More Than a Good Idea

📅 Nov 04, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

A well-designed product can solve problems, but getting that product into the hands of the people who need it is another challenge entirely. Increasingly, designers are being asked to think beyond form and function to consider business models, manufacturing, marketing, and more.

In the latest episode of SURROUND Presents, METROPOLIS Editor-in-Chief Avi Rajagopal explores this evolving role with Souvik Paul, founder of medical device startup Aurie, and Sinclair Smith of the School of Visual Arts (SVA) MFA Products of Design program. Their conversation examines what it takes to move from identifying a problem to developing a viable solution—and why entrepreneurship is becoming an essential part of a designer’s education.

For Paul, the journey began on Wall Street, where he worked in finance before deciding to pursue a career in industrial design. Just two weeks before starting graduate school at SVA, his future sister-in-law sustained a spinal cord injury in a car accident. The experience fundamentally changed his approach to design, prompting him to dedicate his studies to understanding how products and services could improve the lives of people living with disabilities.

That work eventually led to Aurie, a medical device startup developing a reusable catheter system for people living with spinal cord injuries, spina bifida, and multiple sclerosis. Unlike conventional single-use catheters, Aurie’s catheter can be reused up to 100 times. The compact system automatically cleans, disinfects, and lubricates the catheter between uses. The approach reconsiders not only the physical product but also the economics of continued catheter use.

Yet designing the system was only one part of the challenge. After graduating, Paul worked as a design strategist at Johnson & Johnson, where he began to understand the difficulties of bringing new medical technologies to market. He eventually realized that if he wanted Aurie to exist, he would need to build the business behind it.

“If I believe that this product could—and should—exist in the world, I’m gonna have to do the legwork,” Paul recalled on SURROUND Presents. “I’m gonna have to take it across the finish line.”

That realization speaks to a broader shift in design education. At SVA, Smith has been rethinking how the MFA Products of Design program prepares students to develop ideas beyond the prototype stage. Drawing on his experience running the school’s Ground Floor Incubator, he has integrated business modeling and entrepreneurship more directly into the curriculum, allowing students to develop the commercial frameworks for their thesis projects alongside the products themselves.

Paul’s experience illustrates this broader approach. For him, the skills developed through design education continue to inform his work, whether he’s collaborating with other designers, communicating with investors, or navigating the regulatory requirements of medical device development.

The conversation ultimately highlights how designers increasingly need to understand the larger systems that determine how—and whether—their ideas can make a difference.

7 Greatest Netflix Miniseries That Are Worth Watching Over and Over

📅 Nov 04, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Content Warning: This list contains a brief mention of sexual assault.There's no question that Netflix has some fantastic miniseries, from Bodyguard to Bodies. But there are two types of great ones: ones you want to watch, let them sit with you, talk to others about them, and move on to the next. Others, however, call for a second watch. It might be to catch things you missed or view the story from a different perspective now that you know how it ends.

Whatever the reason, a handful of Netflix miniseries fall into the latter category, though for differing reasons. Interestingly, five of the six are powerful, heavy stories. But that's why watching them when you're in different frames of mind could cause you to see the stories in a different light.

There are a lot of "WTF" moments in Midnight Mass, especially if you go in without knowing what it's about. I watched after already knowing the big twist, but I can imagine that those who were thrown for a loop would want to go back and pick up on missed clues. The Gothic supernatural horror miniseries stars Zach Gilford as Riley Flynn, a young man struggling to acclimate to life in his small town after spending time in prison. Desperate to maintain his sobriety and find purpose, he is approached by the town's mysterious new priest, Father Paul Hill (Hamish Linklater).

The story in arguably the best Mike Flanagan TV show goes from small-town mundane life to scary religious story. Father Paul, it seems, is not your average man. And the reasons he gave for taking over for the town's long-time priest, Monsignor Pruitt, aren't exactly as he explained. The story heavily explores religious themes, including the idea of fanatical religion. It's a serious topic, and you may see things differently on a re-watch. Outside of that, Midnight Mass is thrilling with bumps in the night, creepy moments, and fantastically nuanced and emotional performances.

Unorthodox is an emotional tale of hope, and with just four episodes, it's an easy binge a second or third time around. Esty Shapiro (Shira Haas) is a young Jewish woman living in a strict Orthodox community in Brooklyn. She yearns for more, and though she is newly married to a man her family picked for her, and learns she is pregnant, she realizes there's no time like the present to escape and explore a secular lifestyle. So, she runs.

The story in this German drama, told in a mix of English, Yiddish, and German, follows both Esty's journey to self-discovery and the panic of her family members, including her husband Yaakov (Amit Rahav) to find her and bring her home, at all costs. It's a relatable story for anyone who has ever felt oppressed by their strict upbringing, whether by religious beliefs or something else. Knowing it's inspired by the true story of Deborah Feldman makes Unorthodox all the more impactful. For something uplifting that depicts a courageous woman at the center of the plot, Unorthodox will make you feel the same satisfying sense of relief every single time you watch.

Adolescence should come with a warning, because if you go into it like I did, just hearing from someone that you should watch without much context as to why, your jaw will be on the floor. That will happen again and again, no matter how often you watch the nearly perfect miniseries. But each time, you'll get something more out of it. The story in the psychological crime drama is that 13-year-old Jamie Miller (Owen Cooper) is arrested for the murder of a schoolmate. His parents are confused and horrified as they follow their son to the precinct for questioning. When they hear a story and see supposed evidence, it rocks them to their core.

The story continues to explore Jamie's journey as he sits with forensic psychologist Briony Ariston (Erin Doherty) and his parents, Eddie (Stephen Graham) and Manda (Christine Tremarco), do their best to continue on while dealing with the aftermath of this heinous crime. The episodes are hard to watch, every one of them filmed in a single take without anything but dialogue to drive them. Once you get over the initial shock, you'll want to listen carefully to the words, the guilt, the tone, in every scene to truly understand the damage that social media can have on young minds, and the impact on their loved ones. It's not a show that's just so good, it's worth watching again and again. It's one you'll want to watch again with your own teens, even the whole family, and use it as a point of discussion about difficult topics. Adolescence isn't about guilt or innocence, it's an examination of cyberbullying, the internet, and the manosphere in a way that seems almost too raw and real to be fiction.

Another show with high shock value, Baby Reindeer takes you on a journey with Donny Dunn (Richard Gadd) in a story purportedly based on Gadd's own experiences. There are two major things he's dealing with. A woman becomes obsessed with him after he's flirty at the bar where he works, and she relentlessly stalks him. He's also a struggling comedian looking to make it big and is taken advantage of by a TV writer. All the while, he's struggling with his own sexuality.

Once you get through the seven episodes, you may want to re-watch to dive into each separate story again. You'll want to try and view things through Donny's lens and those of other characters, like his parents, his transgender girlfriend Teri (Nava Mau), and even Martha (Jessica Gunning). Seeing all the moments that led to Donny's emotional breakdown hit harder the second time around as well. The third time? You'll still be grabbing for tissues.

Four Netflix characters, four completely different ways of showing up for somebody. This is the one who fits how you actually do friendship, and what having them in your life would be like.

You do not do friendship at half volume, and neither does he. What you want is somebody who says yes to the plan before you have finished describing the plan.

You are the friend who makes things happen, and so is she. Together you would be mildly exhausting to everyone around you and completely delighted with yourselves.

You have no patience for pleasant lies, and she has no ability to tell them. What looks like coldness from the outside is the most reliable friendship on this list.

You look after whoever is having the worst day. So does he. It is the least strategic way to move through the world and the best possible quality in a friend.

A story of hope, perseverance, and rising above, you might be able to watch The Queen's Gambit now with a clearer lens versus the one masked by COVID-19 stay-at-home orders when it first came out. And you will thoroughly enjoy the story through all seven episodes of the coming-of-age period drama. Set in the 1950s and based on the Walter Tevis novel, Beth Harmon (Anya Taylor-Joy) is a young orphan who discovers that she's a chess prodigy.

The show that makes for a perfect weekend binge follows Beth's rise up the ranks in the chess world while simultaneously dealing with her addiction to both drugs and alcohol. While Beth is proving herself a worthy player against all these men, she's also learning about her own worth as a person. The drugs that help her visualize the moves are a metaphor for the insecurities that hold Beth back and make her doubt her own abilities. It's a beautiful story of redemption and a young woman coming into her own, overcoming obstacles and fighting for her place.

In 1989, five Black and Latino young men were charged with the rape and assault of a young woman in Central Park. But years later, the convictions were all overturned when another man confessed to the crime. If you know the story of the Central Park Five, you may have been interested in watching this crime drama. If you didn't, the four episodes of When They See Us will walk you through everything. As a Netflix miniseries that will hook you in the first five minutes, you won't be able to resist binging your way through the entire thing. It's a gripping retelling of a story that rocked a city and country and continues to be talked about and sadly relevant to this day.

As one of the most talked about cases of wrongful conviction, The Central Park Five case, and in relation to this show, is a powerful commentary on racism and racial profiling. It doesn't get any easier to watch a second or third time around, especially knowing that When They See Us is inspired by real life events and real people deeply hurt by accusations at such tender, young ages. The powerful performances from the lead actors and the poignant messaging are worth exploring again and again, opening eyes to a terrible injustice that continues to repeat.

Looking for something lighter? Classic rom-coms are one of the most rewatchable genres, and while One Day, a romantic drama, hasn't been around long enough to be considered a classic, it may very well become one a decade or two from now. The lovely story in the romantic drama follows Emma Morley (Ambika Mod) and Dexter Mayhew (Leo Woodall) through 14 years of ups and downs in their relationship.

Based on the David Nicholls novel as well as the 2011 film adaptation, One Day is 14 episodes long, but it's the perfect binge when you're feeling romantic, even lonely. It will satiate your desire for an emotional, sweet, and well-delivered romantic drama that spans a decade and-a-half of time. It takes you on a long yet digestible journey through this likable couple's time together.

Binance stopped 57 North Korean cyber threats but fell for a fake job interview

📅 Nov 04, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

The exchange's defenses thwarted dozens of suspected attacks before hackers used a staged recruitment process to infiltrate its wallet systems.

Binance said it thwarted 57 suspected North Korea-linked cyber threats in 2026, yet a fake interview compromised one of its engineers.

The disclosure, in the exchange's Nov. 3 security report, reveals how attackers used a recruitment scam involving staged interviews, fabricated identities, and malicious software to access systems involved in the $6.34 billion cryptocurrency theft on Oct. 29.

“Throughout 2026, Binance's security team detected and prevented 57 suspected North Korea-linked cyber threat cases. These attempts reflect the persistent threat posed by state-sponsored actors targeting the cryptocurrency industry. The techniques include fraudulent job interviews, malicious software disguised as popular developer tools, compromised software packages and browser extensions, fake video conferencing applications, and social engineering campaigns designed to steal credentials or deploy remote access malware. We intercepted many of them, but the cost of failing to stop even one was severe—and this should never have happened.”

The operation succeeded despite Binance's security team identifying and preventing dozens of suspected North Korean intrusion attempts during the year, including campaigns using fraudulent job interviews and compromised developer tools.

The FBI attributed the theft to North Korea's TraderTraitor operation on Oct. 31, describing an ongoing effort to steal cryptocurrency and launder the proceeds through decentralized finance platforms.

Binance's findings expose a troubling gap between recognizing a persistent threat and preventing its execution.

The campaign's earliest identified stage occurred in June, when a Binance wallet infrastructure engineer downloaded a tampered Homebrew installer while attempting to install a popular AI coding tool.

Binance's security team detected the suspicious software, replaced the device, and rotated related credentials. However, the incident exposed information about the engineer's responsibilities and colleagues.

In September, someone impersonating a project director at a fictitious company approached another software engineer through a legitimate recruitment platform.

An individual posing as a human resources representative conducted a live video interview and shared a social media profile containing personal photographs and videos to establish credibility.

A second interview followed with someone claiming to be the company's chief executive.

Near the end of that conversation, the engineer was instructed to download a coding assignment onto his work computer. The repository contained malware that executed during setup, replacing a legitimate developer-tool component and establishing communication with an attacker-controlled server.

Binance said the previously unknown malware evaded its endpoint security software and concealed traces of the intrusion.

By Oct. 20, the attackers had used the compromised computer and an internal AI tool to gain control over two additional engineers, including the employee targeted in June.

They subsequently obtained a container deployment permission token. Nine days later, the attackers used stolen credentials to deploy a malicious container and reach Binance's wallet-signing infrastructure.

The exchange said the attackers initiated unauthorized transfers outside its normal withdrawal process, draining assets across several blockchain networks.

The tactics closely resemble a North Korean cyber operation that security researchers and US authorities have tracked for years.

In September 2024, the FBI warned that North Korean hackers were using highly personalized employment opportunities, elaborate fictional identities, and prolonged conversations to infiltrate cryptocurrency businesses.

The agency noted that even technically sophisticated employees could be deceived by campaigns tailored to their professional backgrounds.

That playbook remained active throughout 2026.

In June, Kudelski Security researchers documented North Korea's Contagious Interview operation, in which hackers impersonated recruiters on LinkedIn, WhatsApp and Discord before persuading developers to execute malicious code.

By September, Atlassian researchers had identified fake technical assessments hosted on established development platforms, where malicious instructions were hidden within otherwise legitimate-looking software projects.

The similarities raise questions about why Binance's existing threat detection measures failed to identify the successful recruitment campaign.

The exchange has not disclosed how many of the 57 prevented cases involved fake interviews or whether any were directly connected to the group behind the October breach.

Following the attack, Binance said it replaced compromised employee devices, revoked exposed credentials, and rebuilt affected wallet infrastructure. It also strengthened developer-environment protections and expanded AI-based behavioral monitoring.

US midterm election results: How Americans voted and what the data tells us

📅 Nov 04, 2026  |  Category: Business  |  Read Source →

Results are starting to come in from across the US, where people voted on their next Congress. DW takes a look at who voted Republican, who voted Democrat and what it all means.

We will update this article throughout the night as more data becomes available, so keep checking back.

In the 2026 midterms, Americans voted on all 435 members of the House of Representatives and 35 members of the Senate. Before election night, Republicans held the majority in both Congress chambers, if only by a hair. In 36 US states, voters also decided on who will become the next governor.

Midterm elections are held every four years, at the exact halfway point between presidential elections. The results will influence US politics for the next few years. Despite the significant executive power delegated to the presidency according to the US Constitution, the majority of federal political decisions have to be approved by Congress before they can be implemented. If the Democrats win the majority in one or both chambers of Congress, it will be harder for President Donald Trump to govern as he chooses.

Because the 50 US states lie across six time zones in total, results will trickle in over time, starting with the US East Coast and going west from there. DW follows the Associated Press (AP) for when a seat is called for Republicans or Democrats.

Polling stations in several eastern states closed at 1 a.m. local time. For more updates, go to our US midterms live blog.

In the US Senate, Democrats retained several of their seats on the East Coast, with Virginia being the first to be called. North Carolina was the first Republican Senate seat, which the Democrats were able to flip.

In the House of Representatives, all 435 seats are up for election.

The data on voter demographics and attitudes we use is based on a representative survey of more than 75,000 verified voters conducted by survey and market research firm SSRS on behalf of AP and other news organizations. It builds on interviews of both early and mail-in voters as well as exit polls of in-person voters on Election Day, November 3. Where percentages of Republican and Democratic voters don't add up to 100%, the remaining percent were voters who voted for third-party candidates.

One of the most important factors in this year's midterms is the economy. Prices for gas have increased significantly since the start of the Iran war, and everyday goods have become more and more expensive across the country as well. Almost half of all Americans say their family's financial situation is worse than two years ago.

That sentiment is not the same across the board though. Among those saying their family is better off than two years ago, 75% voted Republican and 23% Democrat. Among those saying they are worse off, only 24% voted Republican and 72% Democrat. President Trump has been saying that the country is on the upswing.

While not on the ballot, Trump is still a strong presence in the midterms: The midterms are considered a referendum on how he and his Republicans governed the country for the past two years. Most recently, Trump's approval ratings have fallen to the lowest he has ever had, with just under 30% of Americans saying they think he is doing a good job.

In recent history, only George W. Bush has fared worse, with 25% at the end of his second term. During the 2006 midterm election, Bush's Republican Party lost control of both chambers of Congress.

Exit polls showed that half of voters saw the 2026 midterm elections as an opportunity to rebuke the president. Three-quarters of US voters said they are "angry" or "dissatisfied" with the way the country is headed.

According to the AP Voter Poll, dissatisfaction was very high in Democratic-leaning states such as California and Massachusetts. However, even in Republican-leaning states, a majority of voters were at least "dissatisfied."

Before Tuesday's election, Republicans (and independents who caucus with them) held 53 of 100 seats in the Senate and 220 of 435 seats in the House. That means they only held two more seats than the 218 required for a majority. In recent years, such razor-thin majorities have been the lay of the land, especially in the House of Representatives.

Lucy Liu’s ‘The Pirate Queen’ Expands With Graphic Novel, New Game and International Tour as Creator Eloise Singer Joins BAFTA Breakthrough Cohort (EXCLUSIVE)

📅 Nov 04, 2026  |  Category: Entertainment  |  Read Source →

Lucy Liu and Eloise Singer are preparing to take “The Pirate Queen: No Safe Waters,” their immersive experience about Chinese pirate leader Cheng Shih, on an international tour next year, Singer tells Variety. The pair have also co-written a graphic novel and are developing a new game.

The news comes as Singer, the project’s writer and director, joins the 2026 BAFTA Breakthrough U.K. cohort. Related Stories Sega's 'Sonic the Hedgehog' to Launch Lingokids Games (EXCLUSIVE)

The franchise is built around a retelling of the stories about real life character Cheng Shih, who was a powerful female pirate in the South China Sea at the beginning of the 18th century.

“No Safe Waters” played in the Cannes Film Festival’s Immersive Competition this year, with Liu narrating and producing alongside Singer. It follows the original 2024 VR version of “The Pirate Queen,” which earned an Emmy nomination.

Liu says Cheng Shih was intelligent and strategically brilliant, and won respect through negotiation and leadership “in a world designed to exclude women entirely.” In Liu’s view, the pirate leader defies nearly every stereotype historically placed on Asian women in storytelling. “She was politically astute, ruthless when necessary, deeply strategic, and completely self-determined. Stories like hers remind us that history has always contained powerful, complex women – they were never centered in mainstream narratives,” Liu tells Variety. “That’s beginning to change, and I think audiences are hungry for stories that feel more expansive.”

Where the first outing was built for a headset, the new piece fills a shared room with projection, sound and movement. Audiences stand among storms and fleets as dragons move beneath their feet.

Liu does not appear on screen. She says voice carries a different kind of responsibility in immersive work, guiding atmosphere and movement at the same time. “In film, the camera captures a glance or physical gesture to communicate subtext, but here the voice can help create emotional architecture,” she says.

Singer has spent a decade on “The Pirate Queen,” the last three years of it with Liu, and says the pair have co-written a graphic novel, are preparing the international tour and are developing a new game. “I feel really lucky to be making it with Lucy. She’s thrown her heart and soul into this alongside me, and getting to build this world together has been one of the most special parts of the journey,” she says. “I love seeing how one story can take on a whole new life across different formats, each offering audiences a completely different way to experience it.”

Liu says the project’s ambition, and the importance of telling Cheng Shih’s story with nuance and scale, is something she and Singer both care about deeply. “We’re building ‘The Pirate Queen’ as a larger storyworld together, so it was never just about lending my voice to one experience,” she says. “As a producer, I wanted to help support the integrity of the story and the larger creative vision. I’m always drawn to work that challenges convention and creates space for voices and histories that haven’t been fully explored, and Cheng Shih’s story deserves that kind of scale.”

BAFTA named Singer to this year’s Breakthrough program, its Netflix-supported initiative for emerging screen talent, on Nov. 4. She was selected in the games category for “No Safe Waters.”

“It’s still a bit of a pinch-me moment. It’s something I’ve aspired to for such a long time, so to be selected is a little surreal, and to have BAFTA and Netflix recognize what we’ve created with ‘The Pirate Queen’ is incredibly special,” Singer tells Variety. “I’m especially proud that it’s for an immersive project. We started with a story we really believed in and wanted to bring it to life in a completely different way, something audiences could truly step inside and experience.”

A friend who went through the program before her told her to make the most of every opportunity. “So that’s what I intend to do! I’m really looking forward to meeting other filmmakers and finding ways to take over the world together,” she says.

Singer says this year is the first time she has really felt a scalable and sustainable future for immersive storytelling. “I’ve been genuinely encouraged by the level of distribution interest around ‘The Pirate Queen.’ It feels like there is a real appetite for immersive work now,” she says.

Liu sees the form as both an extension of filmmaking and something new. “It grows from cinematic language but it also asks the audience to participate differently. That changes the relationship between story and viewer,” she says. “We’re still discovering the language of immersive storytelling which is what makes this moment so exciting creatively.”

Singer’s background is in film and TV, and alongside new immersive work she is developing scripted projects. “I’ve always been drawn to stories about people who challenge expectations, whether they’ve been overlooked by history, underestimated by others, or simply refused to play by the rules,” she says. “Ultimately, I want to keep telling stories I really believe in and finding the most exciting ways to bring them to life.”
