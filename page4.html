Fantasy Football Updated Rookie Report: Fernando Mendoza gets his shot

📅 Nov 11, 2026  |  Category: Sports  |  Read Source →

Nine weeks into the NFL season, we've seen enough from this rookie class to know who's for real, who's still finding their footing and who could make some noise down the stretch. From Carnell Tate's impressive start in Tennessee to Fernando Mendoza getting his opportunity in Las Vegas, here's my midseason check-in on 15 rookies, and what I'm expecting for the rest of the season.

Carnell Tate has been everything we hoped he would be — and then some. Through seven games, he's hauled in 34 receptions for 413 yards and a touchdown despite playing in one of the more inconsistent passing offenses in football. Cam Ward and the Tennessee Titans haven't exactly made life easy for their rookie receiver, but Tate continues to produce.

The talent is undeniable, the opportunity is there and the chemistry with Ward continues to develop. I expect Tate to build on what's already been a successful rookie campaign. He's a weekly fantasy starter with plenty of room to grow.

Jonah Coleman might be the rookie running back I'm most excited about for the second half of the season. He's rushed for 191 yards and three touchdowns while adding 13 receptions for another 128 yards. More importantly, look at how Denver is using him. He handled 21 touches in Week 9, and the coaching staff clearly trusts him.

With R.J. Harvey on injured reserve and J.K. Dobbins getting older, the opportunity is sitting right in front of Coleman. He's already shown he can contribute in the passing game and handle meaningful carries. I wouldn't be surprised if Coleman finishes as one of the most valuable rookie running backs in fantasy.

Omar Cooper Jr. missed time early in the season, but the moment he got healthy, he made his presence felt. Through four games, Cooper has 20 receptions for 306 yards and two touchdowns, including back-to-back 100-yard performances. In Week 9, he followed those outings with eight receptions for 58 yards while Garrett Wilson was sidelined.

What I've really liked is the rapport he's developing with Geno Smith. Cooper looks comfortable; he's earning opportunities and he's showing he can win in different areas of the field. Wilson's return could impact his volume, but I'm buying what we've seen. Cooper has legitimate weekly flex appeal moving forward.

KC Concepcion Jr. has quietly been one of the most impressive rookie wide receivers in football. He's sitting at 37 receptions for 437 yards and a touchdown, including 251 receiving yards over his last three games. What jumps off the screen is his ability to create after the catch. Get this young man the football in space and let him work.

The Browns haven't exactly provided consistent quarterback play, but Concepcion continues to find ways to produce. He's dynamic, explosive and already showing flashes of a player who could eventually become a Pro Bowl-caliber receiver. For fantasy purposes, he's worked his way into weekly flex consideration.

And how about Denzel Boston? The rookie has 507 receiving yards and three touchdowns on 32 receptions. Boston gives me some young Mike Evans vibes with his size, downfield ability and willingness to go make contested catches. He's already producing explosive plays despite the limitations of this Browns offense.

What's encouraging is that both Boston and Concepcion are earning significant opportunities without cannibalizing each other's production. Boston has legitimate big-play upside every single week and continues to look like one of the steals of the rookie class. I love what we've seen and expect a strong finish.

Germie Bernard's rookie season has been a little up and down, but I've seen enough to remain encouraged. He's caught 32 passes for 284 yards and a touchdown, highlighted by a phenomenal 11-reception performance in Week 6. The following two games weren't nearly as productive, but I wouldn't let that discourage you.

Bernard has established himself as a trusted option for Aaron Rodgers in a Pittsburgh offense that's been better than advertised. His role makes sense. Get him the football quickly, let him operate in space and allow his natural ability to take over. Bernard feels like a buy-low candidate with a solid second-half outlook.

I can't believe I'm putting Fernando Mendoza on this list, but here we are. The No. 1 overall pick is taking over as the Raiders' starting quarterback after Kirk Cousins was traded to Minnesota. Mendoza has attempted just eight regular-season passes, completing six for 48 yards, so we have almost nothing to evaluate.

But here's what we do know: Cousins threw 20 touchdowns in nine games and Mendoza gets to operate in that same Klint Kubiak offense featuring Brock Bowers. Listen, any quarterback throwing to Brock Bowers gets a touchdown bonus in my book. Add Ashton Jeanty, Trey Tucker and a potentially more favorable schedule down the stretch, and there's legitimate streaming appeal here. I'm not expecting Mendoza to be a league winner, but don't be surprised if he becomes fantasy relevant sooner than people think.

Jadarian Price is one of those players where the box score doesn't tell the entire story. He's rushed for 172 yards and added 83 receiving yards on 10 catches without scoring a touchdown. The production hasn't been there consistently, but every time he touches the football, you see the explosiveness. Unfortunately, injuries and ball-security issues have limited his opportunities.

With Zach Charbonnet still working his way back from a knee injury, there's a path for Price to earn a larger role. Seattle's offensive line should also improve as it gets healthier. I'm cautiously optimistic, but I need to see him string together consistent performances before trusting him in fantasy lineups.

Makai Lemon has been better than his rookie numbers might suggest. Through nine games, he's caught 37 passes for 312 yards and one touchdown, including 19 receptions for 185 yards over his last three games. The Eagles offense has been difficult to watch at times, and Lemon has dealt with inconsistent quarterback play while occasionally being asked to operate as the team's top receiver with DeVonta Smith unavailable.

What I've loved is his ability to separate, particularly against zone coverage. He's consistently finding openings and earning targets. The big fantasy performances haven't arrived yet, but the underlying usage is encouraging. There's more here than the box score suggests.

We finally got our first look at Jordyn Tyson, and while it wasn't perfect, there were encouraging signs. In his NFL debut, Tyson caught four passes for 30 yards but also lost a fumble on his first reception. I'm not worried about that. What matters is that New Orleans immediately looked to get him involved.

This Saints offense ranks among the league leaders in neutral-situation pass rate, meaning the opportunity should continue to be there. Getting Calvin Banks back should help Tyler Shough operate more comfortably behind that offensive line. The biggest question remains Tyson's health. If that hamstring holds up, I'm expecting his role to expand significantly.

Mike Washington Jr. gave us one of the more surprising rookie performances of the season when he handled 25 carries for 83 yards. Then he turned around the following week and rushed six times for -3 yards. That's been the problem. Washington has 275 rushing yards and a touchdown this season, but his role remains difficult to project.

Neither Washington nor Ashton Jeanty has consistently found success running behind this Raiders offensive line. Washington clearly has some ability, but can this coaching staff establish a defined role for him? Until that happens, he's difficult to trust. He's an interesting stash with upside if Jeanty misses time.

There's no bigger fantasy disappointment from this rookie class than Jeremiyah Love. And before anybody gets upset, I'm not putting all of that on the player. Love has rushed for 268 yards, added just 36 receiving yards and scored two total touchdowns in six games. We've seen flashes of the talent that made him such a highly regarded prospect, but the situation in Arizona has been a disaster.

The offense has struggled, the quarterback situation has deteriorated and now Love is sitting on injured reserve. If you invested a second-, third- or fourth-round fantasy pick in him, there's no way around it — it's been disappointing. The talent hasn't disappeared, but the fantasy return simply hasn't been there.

Antonio Williams is one of those rookies who could become much more relevant over the second half of the season. He's caught 26 passes for 284 yards and a touchdown, including eight receptions for 79 yards over his last two games. The problem hasn't necessarily been Williams. It's been the Commanders' offense.

Jayden Daniels continues to deal with a significant elbow injury and Washington hasn't been particularly creative offensively. We've also seen Williams have some unfortunate plays wiped away. With Terry McLaurin out, there should be additional opportunities available. He's not a weekly fantasy starter yet, but there's a path for Williams to become a useful flex option.

Emmett Johnson has quietly put together 348 yards from scrimmage and two total touchdowns, including that ridiculous 128-yard receiving performance against Denver in Week 8. We saw a 73-yard touchdown reception and a player who can absolutely make things happen with the football in his hands.

The problem? Kenneth Walker III has been phenomenal since arriving in Kansas City.

Johnson isn't taking that starting job anytime soon. But what if the Chiefs can turn him into a Jerick McKinnon-type receiving weapon? There's potential for standalone fantasy value in that role. And if Walker were to miss time, Johnson immediately becomes one of the most valuable backups in football.

Kaelon Black has 303 rushing yards and a touchdown this season, and I don't think enough people are paying attention to what he's doing. That's more rushing production than Jeremiyah Love, Mike Washington Jr. and Jadarian Price. The biggest obstacle is obvious: Christian McCaffrey.

But we've seen San Francisco utilize multiple backs at times, and keeping McCaffrey fresh has to remain a priority. Black has demonstrated that he can be an effective runner when given opportunities. The receiving game hasn't been a major part of his role, so there are limitations to his standalone fantasy value. Still, this is a player I want rostered heading into the second half of the season.

Telegram Doesn’t Need to Convert Crypto Users: 1M Wallets in 48 Hours Reveal Its Massive Distribution Advantage

📅 Nov 11, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Ahmed Barakat is a journalist and copywriter based in Georgia with a growing focus on blockchain technology, DeFi, AI, privacy, digital assets, and fintech innovation.

The CryptoNews editorial team is composed of seasoned writers specializing in cryptocurrency and blockchain technology. Their expertise ensures comprehensive, accurate, and insightful content for...

Telegram is gaining crypto users without having to convince them to download another app. More than 1 million self-custodial wallets appeared on the messaging platform within 48 hours. Founder Pavel Durov says the growth reflects a simple feature: wallets activate automatically when users receive Grams. The milestone shows Telegram’s biggest advantage in crypto: distribution.

Unlike traditional crypto wallets, Telegram’s native wallet operates inside an application millions already use daily. Crypto traders, project founders, airdrop hunters, and blockchain communities have long relied on Telegram. That existing audience gives its wallet a distribution advantage that standalone crypto applications must spend years building.

The latest growth also raises an interesting question about blockchain adoption. Does crypto really need another wave of new users, or does it need easier access for people already participating? Telegram could provide exactly that bridge, provided wallet registrations translate into meaningful economic activity.

Durov reported more than 1million new self-custodial wallets over 48 hours in an X post. The dashboard accompanying his announcement showed more than 1 million registrations across the period.

1M+ self-custodial Gram wallets activated on Telegram in just 48 hours. $GRAM is up 10% in 24h, the #1 gainer in the CMC top 100. pic.twitter.com/VGVTT5AZQk

According to Durov, users do not need to manually create a wallet before receiving crypto. Their wallets activate when someone sends them Grams.

This creates a potentially powerful viral distribution mechanism. One user sends tokens to another, introducing the recipient to an on-chain wallet. The recipient can then hold or transfer assets without leaving Telegram.

This approach removes several familiar barriers to crypto adoption. New users do not necessarily need to understand seed phrases, download separate applications, or navigate an exchange. Telegram already provides the social environment where these transactions can begin.

The platform also has more than one billion monthly users, giving its crypto ambitions an enormous potential audience. Even a small conversion rate could produce substantial wallet growth.

The latest wallet milestone is not the only sign of expanding activity across The Open Network (TON), the blockchain powering Telegram’s wallet. TON averaged 4.06 million daily transactions during October. The network also added approximately 2.73 million wallets throughout the month, its strongest monthly addition since October 2024.

Stablecoin activity offers another indication of growing utility. TON averaged 37,693 daily stablecoin users in October, representing a 23.44% increase from September.

The stablecoin figures are particularly interesting because they point toward activity beyond token speculation. Payments and transfers could give Telegram crypto a more practical role in everyday digital interactions.

TON’s stablecoin supply stood at $585 million today. Its decentralized finance total value locked, however, remained comparatively modest at approximately $44 million.

Payments, stablecoin transfers, digital collectibles, and Telegram’s mini-app ecosystem could provide reasons to return. These services could gradually make blockchain transactions feel like another ordinary messaging feature.

Colorado Trail and Continental Divide Trail ‘Extensively Damaged’ After Severe Flooding: Here’s What We Know

📅 Nov 11, 2026  |  Category: Travel  |  Read Source →

This article was updated on November 11, 2026, at 8:35 a.m. with additional information regarding the condition of the Colorado Trail section 28.

In just over a week, two massive flooding events hit southwestern Colorado, destroying homes, businesses, and infrastructure across numerous mountain towns. But property is not all that the floods affected. Parts of both the Continental Divide Trail (CDT) and the Colorado Trail (CT) suffered damage that locals and trail organizations are calling “extensive” and “severe”, and which could take a very long time to repair.

“Rebuilding these damaged sections will not be quick, easy, or cheap,” Paul Talley, executive director of the Colorado Trail Foundation (CTF), said in a November 5 update on the floods. “But we are committed to rebuilding.”

As many of these areas are still inaccessible, there is very little confirmed information on their current state. Videos circulating online show missing bridges at key river crossings, and reports from the USFS and other agencies indicate that trailheads are damaged, and “heavy debris” is blocking some sections. But there has yet to be a full, official report on damage to these trails.

Here is what we know so far based on official updates, local reports, and videos.

Junction Creek is the CT’s southernmost section. Its trailhead, just outside of Durango, is also the CT’s southern terminus. Junction Creek Trail crosses multiple footbridges over the creek along its 19-mile stretch. A conditions report on the U.S. Forest Service webpage says that a “Huge rock and heavy debris” is currently blocking the trail about a half-mile from the trailhead. One video claims to show an area along Junction Creek Trail near Gudy’s Rest, where a footbridge has been washed away completely. The USFS posted its own video of the road to Junction Creek to Facebook, showing a new “20-foot waterfall” over the edge of the destroyed road.

“There is no doubt that trails across the forest have suffered severe damage,” the San Juan National Forest wrote. “For example, we know that the Colorado Trail along Junction Creek has largely been obliterated and that the trail bridge is gone. In some sections, the trail is buried underneath riverflow that was re-routed during the flooding.”

On November 10, a Facebook user posted to the Durango Area Trail Conditions Report Facebook page with information about the trail section above Junction Creek. According to the report and accompanying photos, the Walls Gulch Bridge was destroyed, and the trail following Flagler Fork Creek sustained “major damage.”

Authorities said that recovery work would likely not begin until spring and summer 2027 due to weather, and discouraged hikers from even attempting to set out on the area’s trails.

“Record flooding across Southwest Colorado has left San Juan National Forest roads, trails, and other infrastructure inaccessible, severely damaged, and, in many cases, impassable,” another post reads. “Don’t become a statistic — rescue will not be readily available due to the current demand for search and rescue services.”

Another video posted to Instagram shows the sign that many hikers use for their CT terminus photo now standing directly at the edge of a tall wash filled with flood debris.

The CTF said that it will be working over the winter with USFS, local partners, and volunteers to determine timelines and project budgets for the restoration efforts.

“It will be months before we can better understand what the next steps need to be on the sections damaged by climate events this season,” wrote Talley in his November 5 update.

There is even less information regarding damage to the CDT, which also passes through the flood-affected areas of Southwestern Colorado.

The Continental Divide Trail Coalition (CDTC) posted on Facebook on October 23, warning hikers that flooding had made “travel to and from the CDT trailhead at Wolf Creek Pass impossible,” and that “hikers may need to make alternate plans to avoid the area.”

On November 2, the CDTC posted another update for CDT travelers. “The extent of damage to the CDT, access roads, and CDT communities is still unknown,” it said. While the interactive map on its website shows no current closures of the trail due to flooding, the CDTC advised hikers to use “extreme caution and check with local authorities before visiting.”

Backpacker reached out to the CDTC for comment, but did not receive a response by the time of publication.

While the full extent of the damage to these trails remains to be seen, what’s certain is that the CTF, CDTC, USFS, and other state agencies have a great deal of restoration work to do next summer. Volunteers will be in high demand, and Talley, of the CTF, promised to be open about how hikers can help.

“While we do not yet know what it will take to rebuild, we do know what we will need,” Talley wrote. “We will need our supporters, we will need our dedicated volunteers, and we will need that community spirit that makes the CT so special.”

Apple's touchscreen MacBook Pro does not like to be taken apart

📅 Nov 11, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Easy lid swaps earn praise from iFixit, but panel removal ends in cracks and USB-C repairs require logic board surgery

Apple's first touchscreen MacBook got a mixed reception from iFixit's teardown specialists, who praised its easily replaced lid but found fans and USB-C ports buried beneath the logic board.

The MacBook Pro with PROLED XDR display was unveiled at the end of October. It features a tandem OLED screen that stacks two layers of organic light-emitting pixels, eliminating the need for a backlight, and adds touch capability to the MacBook line for the first time.

Although the vendor was patted on the back in recent years for improving the repairability of its famously locked-down products, this machine shows Apple still has some way to go.

The good? The entire lid comes off relatively easily. Its three-screw hinges closely resemble those on the M6 14-inch MacBook Pro, while streamlined display cable brackets simplify removal.

Apple's Repair Assistant tool also handled a full system board swap well. Despite concerns about Touch ID fingerprint authentication, everything went relatively smoothly when iFixit paired a touchscreen from one machine with a logic board from another. On the first boot, Repair Assistant recognized the battery, display, and Touch ID sensor, and touch control needed no separate calibration.

The bad? Replacing the panel alone is not possible. When iFixit tried to separate it from the lid, the glass delaminated, cracking the OLED and touch-sensitive layers beneath. That could make for a costlier repair than bargained for.

Under the bottom cover, the PROLED has a similar internal layout to the last few models of MacBook Pros, with the fans, board, speakers, and trackpad all in the same places.

That means the same annoyances persist. The MagSafe power connector can be removed via two screws and a bracket, yet the USB-C ports can't be extracted the same way because the logic board is in the way. The headphone jack and the fans also require removing the system board first, and the HDMI port and SDXC card reader are soldered to the board itself, as is the storage and memory.

Another gripe is that this MacBook's battery pack is hard to remove, though at least it is removable, unlike in many thin-and-light laptops. The tested Pro weighs 1.38 kg or about 3 pounds.

For these reasons, the MacBook Pro with PROLED XDR display earns a 5 out of 10 repairability score from iFixit, but this is actually a one point increase over the score it gave the M5 MacBook Pro.

The Apple logo has its own lighting. Older MacBooks with illuminated logos borrowed light from the LCD backlight shining through an apple-shaped window in the lid. This model uses a strip of LEDs along one edge of the logo, passing through a diffuser for an even glow. Because it sits behind the display panel, it would be nearly impossible to repair if it failed, especially if the panel is destroyed during removal.

Under a microscope, the PROLED XDR display looks very different from earlier MacBooks: the blue subpixel is significantly larger than the green and red ones.

The reason, we're told, is that blue OLED materials wear out faster, so a larger subpixel can emit the same amount of light while putting less strain on the material. ®
