Prior to her diplomatic appointment, Khera served nearly a decade in the Canadian Parliament in Ottawa. First elected in 2015 at the age of 26, she was one of the youngest women ever elected to the House of Commons. Before entering public life, Khera worked as a registered nurse in oncology.

Most teams have someone who thinks a bit differently. Many effective teams have a few. Sometimes that's good and sometimes it causes serious problems.

In a brainstorm, they might produce ten ideas while everyone else is still wondering how to structure their thoughts, or waiting to see what others say. Some are brilliant, some are baffling, or a combination of the two. They dress a little differently, follow their own passions unashamedly, and have a habit of questioning things everyone else doesn't notice or doesn't care to notice anymore.

In the right environment, and with some self-awareness and empathy, they can be the most interesting person in the room. They can also be the most frustrating, because despite a lot of good ideas, none of those ideas turns into reality. They have big ideas that they would love to see implemented by other people.

The unconventional style is one way of describing one of the "dark side" personality styles: everyday patterns that can help or hinder us at work, depending on how strongly they show up and where. Its extreme form resembles a clinical pattern of eccentric thinking and unusual perceptions. Most people with an unconventional style are nowhere near that. They simply have their own unique approach to life.

They tend to rely on their own understanding of how the world works, and it is often a rich, intense, and sometimes quirky one. For some, being different is unconscious. For others, it is a deliberate avoidance of anything conventional or ordinary. Either way, they are unlikely to change it to suit other people.

At their best, people with this style are interesting, different, and fun to be around. They are abstract and creative thinkers, and they can be remarkably tenacious, pursuing an idea long after others have given up, often with very little support.

They also perform an important function in teams. When everyone shares the same assumptions, they are the outsider on the inside. They ask the question that nobody else thought to ask, or offer an approach that breaks a deadlock. Think of the trainer whose odd analogy is the one that finally makes a complicated process click for a struggling new starter, or the analyst who notices that a problem everyone has been solving the same way for years could be approached from a completely different angle. A team without anyone like this risks drifting into comfortable groupthink.

There is evidence that unusual thinking and creativity overlap, but it is more nuanced than the stereotype of the eccentric genius. A meta-analysis by Acar and Sen (2013), combining results from 45 studies, found only a small overall link between creativity and personality dimensions that capture unusual perceptions, magical thinking, and eccentricity. However, there is some evidence that those who tend to experience life imaginatively and unusually can also be more creative.

The ability to put unconventional ideas into practice, though, can be more challenging, and the link with productivity is even more difficult to achieve.

The downside of this personality style is rarely a lack of ideas. The problem for team members is often the lack of follow-through. When someone's energy isn't channelled effectively, unconventional people can have lots of ideas, but nothing gets off the ground.

They can be easily distracted, drawn off on tangents, and quickly bored by tasks that don't fit their view of the world. Often, the routine parts of a project, such as documenting ideas, updating progress trackers, following templates, and managing a project through to completion, can feel unbearable. They can also be stubborn or disruptive when things don't go according to their plans, preferring their own way of doing things even when the team has agreed on another.

There is a social cost as well. People who are seen as eccentric are not always taken seriously, even when they are right. As I discussed in a post on how people enable dysfunction at work, the unconventional colleague may see a problem clearly but struggle to gain traction, because their protests are easily dismissed as another eccentric idea.

Distance can make this worse. In a remote team, the unconventional colleague can drift further into their own projects without anyone noticing until a deadline arrives. Left alone and out of the loop, people with this style can quickly spin off into their own world, building something fascinating that nobody asked for. In this case, the right adjustment is not stricter control, which they will resist, but more regular contact to make sure their passion and energy are pointed at the right target.

None of this means they lack motivation. As with people who are lower in conscientiousness, the motivation is there, but it may be challenging for them to apply it to the right priorities and connect with what the rest of the team needs to be productive.

Unconventional people can be successful, but they will be most effective in a workplace where people accept their quirks, and where they in turn make some effort to fit in and build relationships based on mutual respect. That works in both directions:

When they meet others halfway and align their talents with the team's priorities, unconventional people can be fun, vibrant, productive and innovative colleagues.

In a workplace, the challenge may be to direct that energy productively and prosocially. Part of doing that may mean remembering that they have plenty of time to pursue other creative pursuits outside of work.

Scientists Crack a Decades-Old Mystery That Could Unlock Faster Internet

📅 Nov 14, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

By untangling how the crystal barium titanate interacts with light, researchers have supplied data that could reduce costly trial and error in designing faster optical communication devices.

When light carries data through a communication device, absorption by the materials inside can weaken the signal. Engineers trying to build faster, more efficient devices need to predict how light will travel through those materials and how much will be lost before they commit to a design.

For a promising material called barium titanate, or BTO, those measurements have varied widely for decades. A KAIST-led research team has now clarified how it interacts with light, providing data that could help engineers design devices more reliably and reduce costly rounds of fabrication and redesign.

BTO offers a useful way to control light because applying an electric field changes its refractive index, a measure of how much light slows as it passes through a material. Its strong response to an electric field, known as the Pockels effect, makes it promising for optical modulators. These devices use electrical signals to control light and encode information onto it.

The difficulty is that a BTO crystal can contain small regions, called domains, with different crystal orientations. Those regions can interact with light differently, so measuring them together blends their responses. That makes it harder to determine the material’s underlying optical properties and predict how it will behave in a device.

To reduce that mixing, the team led by Professor Min Seok Jang of KAIST’s School of Electrical Engineering examined two types of single-crystal BTO samples with relatively uniform domain orientations. The researchers used Mueller-matrix spectroscopic ellipsometry, a technique that measures changes in light’s polarization, or the orientation of its electric-field vibrations, when it reflects from or passes through a material. Those changes provide information about how the material affects light.

Their analysis covered wavelengths from 190 nanometers (nm) to 40 micrometers (μm), spanning the ultraviolet to the far-infrared. Because BTO interacts with light differently across that range, the researchers divided it into three spectral regions and used an analytical approach suited to each. They determined how the refractive index and absorption properties vary with both wavelength and crystal orientation.

The team also developed a method for calculating the overall optical response of a crystal containing differently oriented domains. Using the orientations and relative proportions of those domains, the method accounts for the mixed internal structure that had made BTO’s properties difficult to establish.

Even in the wavelength range where BTO is nearly transparent, the researchers were able to quantify very weak absorption. They combined ellipsometry with separate transmittance measurements, which record how much light passes through a sample, to determine extinction coefficients as low as approximately 10⁻⁵ in the 2–10 μm range. The extinction coefficient describes how strongly a material absorbs light, with smaller values indicating weaker absorption.

Together, these measurements help answer two practical questions about a waveguide, a structure that guides light through a device. The refractive index helps designers predict how the light will travel, while the extinction coefficient helps them estimate how much will be lost to absorption. Engineers can use the data to assess devices of different sizes and shapes before fabrication. Potential applications include communications modulators, devices that generate light at new wavelengths, and circuits that process quantum information using light.

KAIST, led by President Choongsik Bae, announced the work on November 8. The study was conducted in collaboration with Professor Harry A. Atwater’s team at the California Institute of Technology (Caltech), the Korea Research Institute of Standards and Science (KRISS), and U.S.-based J.A. Woollam Co.

Aside from inflation, the economy overall isn’t great. The country isn’t adding a bunch of jobs, wages and salaries aren’t rising much, and people by and large don’t feel optimistic about their financial futures. So, combined with quickly rising costs, a very meh economy had voters wanting change.

As some CleanTechnica readers pointed out, if people were prospering financially and not being hit with severe inflation, many of them probably wouldn’t care about the corruption. However, also more broadly, Americans are fed up with forever wars in the Middle East, there’s never been a good and strong case for why Trump started this war (as tends to be the case with these wars in the Middle East), and many people really don’t like seeing innocent schoolchildren being bombed to death. There was clearly a huge blue shift in certain places as a result of this.

Many voters have gotten extremely concerned about Republicans under Trump’s leadership eroding the foundations of our democracy. While this became a major problem in Trump’s first term, he has gone much further in his second term. Apparently, more people have woken up to this problem and it has freaked them out.

A massive rollout of AI and data centers — often feeling completely forced on us — has upset a lot of Americans. This does come back to #1, too. Giant data centers have jacked up electricity prices a ton in several regions. Others are threatened with the same thing happening. The same thing has also happened from the crypto craze, which more and more people have also found to be a scam. Seeing billions upon billions trillions of dollars funneled toward AI companies while people are struggling, and then seeing electricity prices rising significantly — surprise, surprise — pisses people off and makes them vote for change.

No matter how you cut it, people vote based on money — in particular, their own money. Whether it’s food prices, gas prices, electricity prices, clothing prices, doll prices, computer prices, car prices, or other prices, people don’t like it when they have to pay a lot more money for stuff — especially when they aren’t making much more money, or more money at all. Simply put, this matter deserves extra weight, because the critical thing swing voters are often voting on is just that — money.

People voting for Democrats on climate and cleantech matters were already voting for Democrats based on these issues. It’s unlikely many people at all shifted from red to blue because of these factors.

Dutch Bros vs. Wendy's: Which Stock Is a Better Buy?

📅 Nov 14, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Dutch Bros is adding drive-thru shops at a rapid clip, while Wendy's relies on a franchise model built for steady cash flow. Their valuations tell very different stories.

Both Dutch Bros (BROS +6.50%) and Wendy's (WEN +1.22%) are different types of restaurant stocks, but each has the same challenge of creating more shareholder value. Dutch Bros is trying to build its future, while Wendy's is trying to improve its present.

Dutch Bros focuses on high-speed beverage service with a loyal following, while Wendy's leans on a massive global franchise network to deliver consistent results. Both compete for consumer dollars in a crowded market, but they offer very different risk and reward profiles for long-term investors.

Dutch Bros operates a rapidly growing network of drive-thru shops specializing in coffee, teas, and customizable energy drinks. In the competitive world of retail stocks, it relies on a unique culture and digital rewards to drive loyalty, particularly among younger consumers. The company sources its proprietary Rebel energy drinks through third-party partners.

In FY 2025, revenue reached nearly $1.6 billion, representing a significant increase of approximately 27.9% over the prior year. This growth is largely driven by the opening of new locations across the United States. The company also reported net income of roughly $117.3 million, showing a substantial rise from the previous fiscal period.

Wendy's operates a global quick-service restaurant system with over 7,000 locations. The business uses a system optimization strategy involving buying and selling restaurant units to maintain an efficient mix of company-owned and franchised stores.

In FY 2025, revenue reached approximately $2.2 billion, which was a decrease of roughly 3.1% compared to the previous year. Despite the lower top-line figure, the company produced net income of nearly $165.1 million. This resulted in a net margin of close to 7.6%, a figure showing how much of every dollar in revenue becomes profit after all expenses.

Dutch Bros faces risks from its geographic concentration in the Western United States, making it vulnerable to regional economic shifts. The business also relies on a small group of suppliers for key ingredients, which could lead to disruptions if those partners fail to deliver. Additionally, rapid store expansion requires flawless execution, and any failure to manage this growth could harm the brand reputation.

Wendy's operates in the fiercely competitive quick-service industry against other major chains and convenience outlets. The company carries significant leverage through its securitized financing facility, which requires consistent cash flow to service. It also depends heavily on independent franchisees to maintain standards, as any food safety incident can damage the global brand.

Wendy's has a lower forward price-to-earnings (P/E) using future earnings estimates; Dutch Bros carries a higher price-to-sales (P/S) ratio measuring market cap against sales over the past twelve months.

When comparing two stocks, it really comes down to which one will create more value for a shareholder, and each of these stocks has its own set of risks and rewards.

The risks and rewards with Dutch Bros largely come down to its expansion plans. It's opening new locations rapidly, opening 48 stores in the third quarter of 2026, bringing it to over 1,200 locations. It hopes to reach over 2,000 stores by 2029. But it's not in every state, which limits its brand recognition. In its third quarter, it also shared that as a percentage of company-operated shops revenues, occupancy costs increased by 100 basis points, as the company shifted more of its portfolio to build-to-suit leases. Higher utility and other operating expenses also played a role. That's worth paying attention to because higher rent and utility costs could weigh on the profitability of new locations compared to some of its legacy locations. That means, while expansion is one of the biggest opportunities for the company, that expansion could also be one of the biggest risks, as new locations may cost more to open and operate and be less successful than legacy locations.

For Wendy's, it's a company that's already shown its potential, but it's at a stage where it's largely focused on improving what it's built. For the first nine months of 2026, net income is down 49.2%. Still, there's promise in its breakfast offerings, which management says could unlock "up to $200 million in restaurant-level EBITDA across the system." In addition, while shareholders wait for the turnaround to take shape, Wendy's pays a dividend that yields a generous 3.4%.

Choosing between these two boils down to believing in Dutch Bros' growth plan or the improvement plan of Wendy's.

Cozy Bed to Selden Pass — John Muir Trail Day 15

📅 Nov 14, 2026  |  Category: World  |  Read Source →

After last night’s fabulous dinner, I expected to sleep like a baby.  And I did.  For a while.

Then a very disturbing event occurred, relating to my left wrist injury.  I woke up in the middle of the night and my thumb, index finger, third finger and fourth finger were completely and totally numb on my left hand, and the pinky was partially numb.

I hoped (fervently!) that this only meant I had slept on my injured wrist in a bad way.  The alternative would seem to be nerve damage of some kind.  Of the kind that says, “Get off the trail right away and see a doctor!!”

Gradually I managed to get feeling back.  As it slowly returned, I was cautiously relieved.  But I recognized that if the extreme numbness came back, then it was not a random misfortune, but something I really needed to worry about.  Aside from possible long-term damage, hiking with no feeling in my left hand was out of the question.

The other change overnight was temperature.  When I turned in, it was hot, even stuffy in my little cabin home, so I went to sleep with just my shirt on, with bare feet and bare legs.  There was no top sheet on the bed, just a sheet on the mattress and a blanket.  So, I slept between the sheet and the blanket.

Over the course of the night, it started to get ever chillier.  As the night went on, I added my little knit cap; then I added socks.  As it cooled further, I got out my sleeping bag liner and hopped in.  Finally, I got my puffy jacket and wrapped it around my legs and eventually that seemed to do the trick.  I was extremely resistant to pulling out my sleeping bag, I did not want to do that if I could help it.  Somehow that would have made the overnight feel less like a true break from the trail, I guess.

When I got up in the morning, my fingers all still had sensation, they were moving.  My wrist was still injured, but my fear of nerve damage abated.

I went up to breakfast and oh what a spread!  This is a place that serves hikers. They had scrapple, bacon, pancakes, eggs, fruit, yogurt, and coffee, of course.  The same crew from the night before all sat around and we stuffed our faces, understandably, as hungry hikers are prone to do.

Smiles showed me a picture of the inside of her cabin from the night before and it was hilarious!

With a great fear of SPIDERS, Smiles had set up and slept in her tent INSIDE her MTR cabin!

This picture gives a good sense of how she obtained her trail name!

So that’s what retired New York oncologists look like!

Lauralyn was there too, and she said, ”You know, you said you were going to recite that poem, how about it?”

Lauralyn encouraging me to recite The Raven

I warned them all it would take more than ten minutes and they said, “That’s okay, go ahead.”

As everyone was getting served, and kitchen staff were moving through the room, I recited The Raven.  They said they really enjoyed it, and I think they did.  With the distractions in the room, it was probably not my best performance, but I did all right.

It was time to get going, so I got my resupply bucket that was stashed in the kitchen under lock and key for bear protection.  Shlepping it to my cabin it felt very heavy.  Then I opened it and there was so much stuff I didn’t need.  I began sorting – everything I wanted went on the bed for packing, everything I did not went back in the bucket.

Smiles dropped by and told me, “I extended, I’m gonna stay here another day.”

“Okay, fine,” I replied, adding, “you know, I’m so tempted to go up to the dining area and fill one of my water bottles with their lemonade!”

She said, “Come on, I’m going that way anyway, let’s go.”

Together we went up.  Maybe my plan was perfectly acceptable, but it helped to have a partner in crime.  I filled up on lemonade, and it proved so delicious on the trail, I enjoyed it so much.

While near the dining room I used the flush toilet, the only one on their whole property.  It didn’t seem like a big deal, but I wanted to get my money’s worth.  (It was $375 a night, remember?)

Unfortunately, I never went in the hot spring, and the people who believe in those things were just raving about it.  The day before, when I might have done it, they had girls cleaning it with toothbrushes and I just didn’t feel like it any other time.

Back in the cabin, I finished packing, surprised that almost all my food fit in the bear canister.  It seemed I was getting a bit more efficient at that task.  My charger block almost got fully charged, finally, so that was good news too.

Before leaving Muir Trail Ranch, I dropped off my plastic five-gallon  bucket full of not-needed items (mostly food) at the reception location.   Just at that moment a German couple arrived and said, “Oh we forgot to send food, we have no food.”

I replied, “Well this is your lucky day, there’s a ton of food in here.  It’s good, expensive food too.”  It was, because a lot of it was the pricey freeze-dried stuff.  Somehow, I had badly miscalculated.  Whatever the reason, that couple made out like bandits.  The adage, “the trail provides” was certainly true for them that day!

I was facing a long uphill, over 3,000 feet to cross Selden Pass, and the very first stretch was one of the steepest.  After a break, when you first start walking, especially uphill, and when your pack is heavy from resupply, your legs can feel like lead.  Mine felt that way, for sure, but the good news was, it didn’t last long.

Pretty soon I was going up at a good pace, but I was stuck behind a horse being ridden by next-door-cabin Jay, who was behind a horse ridden by his guide.  Oddly, my pace was pretty close to that of the horses, but I didn’t want to get in front and hold them up.  At the same time, walking behind I was breathing the dust they were kicking up, so I kept having to drop back to get clear of that.

Finally, there was some separation between us.  The ascent included a lot of long switchbacks, so that the “crow fly” distance to MTR was not that great.  I could even still see it, just far, far beneath me now.  My blood was really pumping from all the uphill!

I was pleased at how I handled it.  True, it was at a lower elevation, but I thought at last I might be getting my trail legs.  Soon I crossed over some forests and meadows and was out of sight of the resort.  I spotted some wildflowers by the trail.

Perhaps it was the season, but I did not see the profusion of wildflowers on the JMT I have seen on other trails

In one of the meadows I was puzzled.  There was a path straight across, but someone had blocked it off.  As I skirted the glade, following the detour, I came across the team building the trail on more sustainable ground, to preserve the fragile meadow.   These folks do such an important job, making the trail better for all of us, and getting paid rather small salaries for doing it.  As always I stopped and thanked them profusely.  I wanted them to know that they and their work were deeply appreciated.

I came to the twin Sallie Keyes lakes.  As I arrived, I crossed some steppingstones over the exit creek and saw a beautiful snake swimming in the water; a garter snake, I think.  I even got its picture.

Right after this shot I watched the snake enter the water and swim away

Feeling hungry, I was tempted to have lunch there.  But then I looked at the map, and saw that the last lake before Selden Pass was called Heart Lake, because it’s shaped like a heart.

I thought, “That seems very appropriate. I will go to Heart Lake and have my lunch.”  At Muir Trail Ranch breakfasts they also lay out the fixings for making and packing a lunch.  I had prepared a sandwich and packed it into my Nalgene soaking container.  I also brought a fresh apple.

When I got to Heart Lake, I took off my shoes and socks, dunked my bare feet into the lake, and ate half of my sandwich.  Under the dry surface of the moss I was sitting on, the lower portion was very wet, meaning my shorts were quickly soaked.

The moss is where I sat and the water is where I swam

As I realized how wet my seat was, I also realized the thought of a swim was very attractive.  So, I took off my clothes, put on my water shoes, and walked into Heart Lake.  I plunged my whole body in, swam out my usual 10 yards, then swam back.  It refreshed me so much.  And yes, I was skinny dipping, except for my water shoes.

But nobody came by while I was swimming.  Afterwards I dried off a little bit and put my damp shorts back on.  Then I ate the rest of my sandwich and my apple and finished off the MTR lemonade.  It was so good.  I’d been drinking it all day, climbing and climbing.  Now it was gone.

A little hard to see, but those are the two upper lobes of the “heart” that names Heart Lake

Energized by food and swim, I continued on up to Selden Pass.  It was a little weird, because no one else was there.  Usually when you get to a pass, you are not alone.  It seems people get to a pass, and they often stay there for a while.  The are resting, or eating, or just enjoying the view and celebrating their accomplishment on top of the world.  Sam and I crossed Forrester Pass alone, but that day the weather was challenging, so nobody stayed up there.

On Selden Pass I was alone, and it was nice to be there by myself.  Then I started down, realizing it was now around 3.30 p.m., which meant my progress was faster than I expected.  That morning I envisioned arriving at closer to 5:00 p.m.  It seemed I was in better shape than I thought.

View north from Selden Pass, the lake is Marie Lake

Descending I was trying to balance going pretty fast, while also being careful to avoid another accident.  As I  proceeded down, I met a couple of women and, in the course of the discussion, one of them revealed their hike was a birthday trip.

So, I asked the birthday girl her name (Sydney) and I sang “Happy Birthday dear Sydney” to her.  That’s just what I do.

Further down I met a woman, an American of Korean origin, who was just finishing a (very late!) lunch at a stream.  She told me her trail name was Ninja Queen because she is allergic to UV light and therefore wears a ninja’s face mask and bright reflector sunglasses.

Just then another couple came going southbound and she too was Korean and was even carrying a Korean flag!   She said some Korean guys had a bunch of extra flags and offered  her one.  She felt patriotic and said, “Okay, sure!”

The guy she was with had the trail name Eminem.  It seemed they had met on trail and were just friends.  We took a selfie of the four of us meeting on the trail.

Papa George, Flag Girl, Eminem, and Ninja Queen hiking the High Sierra!

Such a random coincidence, right?  I said she should take “Flag Girl” as her trail name.  But it didn’t seem to really appeal to her.

I proceeded down and down and down, trying to make up as much distance as possible, so that tomorrow’s trip to VVR, Vermilion Valley Resort, is a relatively short one.  I did stop to refill my water at a stream crossing, and Ninja Queen passed me while I was there.  We exchanged greetings and found we both intended to overnight at VVR on the following day.

Finally, I got to the tent site that I was targeting.  To my disappointment, it appeared packed full.  I walked up to a tent to ask if there were any spots available nearby, and it was Sally!  The news about spaces was not hopeful, but I enjoyed stopping and chatting with her briefly.  That was very nice, especially when she said, “Yeah, all day I was asking, where is George? Where do you think George is?”

When I admitted I left MTR at 10:00 a.m. she went, “You left at 10 o’clock???”  Only then did I feel a little abashed at my late start.

That campsite was full, then the next campsite was full too.  The sun was getting lower and finally dropped behind the ridge.  It was still light enough to see, but it was quickly darkening.  Finally, the third place was empty!  I found a spot that seemed adequate in the fading light and set up camp.  I soaked and then ate an al pastor rice dish that was actually good, and I hid my bear cannister.  It was too dark by then to think about digging a cathole.

My faithful Big Agnes tent in the campsite near the river

Then I settled in for another night in the woods.
