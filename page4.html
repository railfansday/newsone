US Army Moves To Restart Washington DC Helicopter Training Flights 2 Years After Deadly Crash

📅 Nov 25, 2026  |  Category: Other  |  Read Source →

The US Army is moving to resume helicopter training flights in airspace near Ronald Reagan Washington National Airport (DCA) as soon as the week of December 6, according to a recent report by Politico. That timeline comes less than two years after the January 29, 2025 midair collision between an Army Sikorsky UH-60 Black Hawk and a PSA Airlines regional jet operating as American Airlines Flight 5342 over the Potomac River, a disaster that killed all 67 people aboard both aircraft.

Training operations in the corridor were halted in May 2025 after an Army Black Hawk came too close to Reagan National and forced approaching commercial airliners to abort landings. Resuming training requires Federal Aviation Administration (FAA) approval, and the service had not yet filed a formal request with regulators even as military officials briefed congressional staff on their plans.

Existing Federal Aviation Administration regulations explicitly exclude routine training from essential flight categories in the restricted zone around Reagan National. Under interim final rule 2026-01226, which took effect on January 23, 2026, and permanently closed Helicopter Route 4 between Hains Point and the Wilson Bridge, only rare essential flights may enter designated airspace up to 1,500 feet (457 meters) mean sea level. Those narrow exceptions cover lifesaving medical missions, active law enforcement responses to ongoing crimes, national defense or counterintelligence operations, continuity of government, and transport of the President or Vice President.

The rule specifies that proficiency evaluations, routine training, and non-essential personnel transport do not qualify as essential operations. Because the regulation bars routine training inside the designated Potomac zone, military leaders would need either a formal regulatory modification or an approved authorization pathway before standard pilot currency missions resume. Federal Aviation Administration officials opened public comment on the interim rule through February 23, 2026, indicated a final rule would follow after reviewing submissions, eliminated visual separation within five nautical miles (9.3 km) of the airport, increased staffing and oversight, and required air traffic controllers to manage commercial runway use when essential vertical-lift aircraft enter restricted airspace.

Investigations by the National Transportation Safety Board and the Army traced the January 2025 collision to overlapping operational, technical, and airspace vulnerabilities rather than a single error. National Transportation Safety Board findings and reporting by Task & Purpose highlighted tight vertical margins near low-altitude river routes, radio frequency congestion, altimeter instrumentation discrepancies, and bright urban lighting that complicated visual detection at night.

Investigators also noted that radio transmissions that might have helped crews confirm visual separation were missed or complicated by overlapping traffic. The National Transportation Safety Board issued 50 safety recommendations, including eight directed at the Army, and concluded that an Automatic Dependent Surveillance-Broadcast system could have alerted the airliner crew 59 seconds before impact. In response, the Army conducted fleet inspections, tested altimeters across Black Hawk variants, and reviewed crew separation procedures. These technical and procedural reviews reflect a broader effort to pair airframe checks with stricter altitude discipline across congested metropolitan corridors.

It is a very dangerous place for both commercial and non-commercial aircraft alike.

Pushback from aviation labor unions and pending legislation on Capitol Hill complicate the Army's push for a December restart. Association of Flight Attendants president Sara Nelson condemned plans to bring military training back near Reagan National so soon after the fatal crash.

Meanwhile, lawmakers remain divided over final statutory mandates governing shared airspace and traffic-alerting equipment. According to The Washington Sun and Senate Commerce Committee reporting, the Rotor Operations Transparency and Oversight Reform Act passed the Senate unanimously in December 2025 but stalled in the House, though leaders announced an agreement in principle in mid-November 2026 on a framework to reconcile competing bills before the end of the year. Negotiations involving the House Armed Services Committee remain central to settling how military helicopters and fixed-wing planes share crowded corridors, while commuters and airlines watch whether flight delays or runway adjustments could return. While Senators Ted Cruz and Maria Cantwell push to finish the legislation, unresolved details over military aircraft equipment requirements continue to draw scrutiny from safety advocates and families of the victims.

The 25 Best Video Games of All Time, Ranked

📅 Nov 25, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Especially compared to the likes of literature, theater, and film, whose histories go back over 100 years of history, video games are a much newer artform of creative expression that is still taking shape and evolving year by year. Still, the medium has had an undeniable growth since the early days of arcade shooters and 8-bit graphics, as games are able to tell rich and powerful stories all while being more immersive than any other artform. Especially with Grand Theft Auto VI's recent record-breaking release further cementing video games as the dominant entertainment medium of the modern age, it places an even greater legacy on the games designated as the greatest of all time.

What makes for a truly exceptional video game experience is going to vary and differ from each individual person, as there is no truly 100% correct list of the greatest video games of all time. For the sake of this list, the entries are a collection of games that consistently stand out and come up in conversation not just because of their high quality, but because of the standards they set when they released and their influence on the entire medium. Still, this only scratches the surface of exceptional video games that have been released since the inception of the industry.

While many people may think of the long-running television franchise or even the original comic when it comes to The Walking Dead, gamers will immediately sing the praises of Telltale's legendary point-and-click adventure game. An original story set in the comic book universe, the episodic game set a new standard for emotional storytelling and writing in video games, with a rich narrative influenced by player choice that amplifies the greatest strengths of Robert Kirkman's original series.

With stylish cell-shaded graphics and approachable gameplay simple enough for even casual gamers to enjoy and get wrapped up in the story, Telltale's The Walking Dead does a great job of making its central story as engaging and impactful as possible. While Telltale has continued the story with an array of subsequent sequels and continuing story, it's the original run of 5 episodes that made such a massive impact on audiences as one of the most poignant and beautiful gaming experiences of all time.

In the early days of 8-bit video games, the platformer genre reigned supreme as the most widely popular style of gaming, with no platformer franchise being as popular or immediately recognizable as Super Mario Bros. While the original NES classic is exceptional enough as it is, the brilliance and timeless appeal of the Mario franchise wouldn't be fully realized until Super Mario Bros. 3, which almost 40 years later is still one of the best 2d platformers ever made.

The game absolutely perfects the feeling and smooth controls required of a platformer of the era, being leaps and bounds ahead of its contemporaries in terms of precision and game feel. This is all on top of the overwhelming creativity in terms of the game's level designs and power-ups that truly bring the wild chaos of the Mario world to life. This classic platformer still stands as the absolute best game released on the Nintendo Entertainment System.

A striking monolith of the action-adventure genre from Team Ico, PlayStation 2 classic Shadow of the Colossus has stood as one of the quintessential examples of games as serious art ever since its release. The game features staggering visuals and a gargantuan sense of scale, seeing the player have to face off against various massive creatures across the land with the goal of resurrecting a young woman. Through traversing the world on horse and by foot, its exceptional visuals and minimalist design come together to create a defining gaming experience.

Especially in an era where action adventure gaming was continuously getting louder and more abrasive with its gameplay and visuals, the slow and methodical approach that Shadow of the Colossus took made the game that much more striking and memorable to 2000s gamers. It truly felt ahead of its time in terms of being an interactive work of art, with something meaningful and sorrowful to say in its storytelling instead of pure entertainment.

One of the premiere masterpieces of the PlayStation brand and one of the first games that come to mind for many when considering games that actively go for a cinematic feel, The Last of Us is one of the defining gaming masterpieces of the 2010s. It tells a dark post-apocalyptic story that primarily focuses on the loss of humanity at large, paralleling the unlikely connection that fosters between a smuggler who lost his daughter and a young girl who has only known this apocalyptic world.

While the emotional and groundbreaking storytelling is the primary element that people praise about The Last of Us, the core stealth-centric action gameplay is also worthy of praise, as it gets the player engaged and immersed in this heightened world where anything can go wrong. The game was also for many a massive leap forward in terms of technical craft in visuals, pushing the PlayStation 3 to its limits and ushering in a new age of realism in video games.

One of the great advents of gaming as an artform becoming more accessible in the digital era is that many more independent game developers are able to bring their visions to life, with a wide array of indie game masterpieces being released in the 21st century. One of the most defining and culturally dominant indie games has been Undertale, Toby Fox's quaint and quirky mixture of RPGs like Earthbound and bullet hell games like Touhou Project.

More than anything else, Undertale thrives thanks to its overwhelming charm and ingenuity, utilizing simplistic visuals and dynamic gameplay to create an experience that consistently delights and surprises the player. It certainly has a goofy tongue-in-cheek tone, satirizing many tropes of RPGs and video games as a whole, but there are also a lot of immaculate character writing and exceptional secrets that make Undertale one of the most influential indie games of all time.

An icon of the metroidvania genre so influential that the very genre is in part coined after its exceptional non-linear design, Castlevania: Symphony of the Night is the undeniable high point of a franchise that has delivered gaming greatness ever since the early days of 8-bit consoles. The game originally sold poorly due to being a 2D game released on a console that famously was at the forefront of early 3D gaming, the original PlayStation, but word of mouth about its quality transformed Symphony of the Night into one of gaming's greatest sleeper hits.

The game famously utilized a non-linear approach to the classic concept of exploring Dracula's Castle, allowing the player all sorts of freedom in the order in which they explore the castle and progress through the story. Combined with an incredibly addictive gameplay loop that has the player actively feel their character grow and get stronger as they play, Symphony of the Night has been the standard for non-linear metroidvanias for nearly 3 decades.

The Legend of Zelda series has continuously released some of the most important and widely influential adventures ever made, dating back 40 years of groundbreaking video game experiences. The Legend of Zelda: Breath of the Wild is the best-selling game in the series, as it completely reinvented and redefined what a Zelda game could be in the modern age and becoming one of the all-time greatest open world games.

Breath of the Wild was a game defined by its sense of pure freedom as an open world adventure, giving the player more control in terms of the order that they go about events compared to any other adventure game at the time, even able to go right to the final boss from the very beginning. Combined with a dynamic physics system and an overwhelming amount of charm and attention to detail, Breath of the Wild has been the quintessential Nintendo masterpiece of the past 10 years.

A story-based RPG that utilizes tabletop mechanics as the centric for its gameplay and setting, Disco Elysium is a masterclass of narrative and storytelling in the medium of video games. It's very abstract as far as RPGs are concerned, having very little combat and instead utilizing its RPG mechanics as skill checks for various actions and dialogue trees, much like a tabletop game would. This classic gameplay loop makes for one of the most immersive and limitless stories in gaming history, branching out into a wide array of different possibilities entirely dependent on player choice.

Its prospects as an indie game allow Disco Elysium not to hold back in terms of the symbolic themes and messaging of its story, commending on so many different aspects of life, government, and ideology in a game rich with perfectly written dialogue across the board. Disco Elysium is one of the greatest works of art that modern gaming has to offer, and is an absolute must-play for anyone who appreciates a great story in video games.

Once video games started to get larger in scale and complexity, the turn-based RPG inspired by games like Dungeons & Dragons became one of the best showcases for an immersive, long-lasting adventure; with Square being one of the best studios for RPGs in the classic era. One of several iconic Square RPGs was Chrono Trigger, a time traveling adventure sporting character designs from Dragon Ball creator Akira Toriyama that quickly became heralded as an RPG masterpiece.

The game has an immediately engaging story, following a group of adventurers from across time who work together to prevent a global catastrophe. While many other RPGs of the 20th century would largely focus on classic fantasy archetypes and elements, Chrono Trigger acted as this sprawling mixture of fantasy and sci-fi elements that was incredibly unique for the time. Mixed with immaculate storytelling and a perfect original soundtrack, to create one of the first times that a game fully enthralled and enchanted players as a complete experience.

Outer Wilds is one of the greatest examples of how, even in the modern age of video games, there are exciting and groundbreaking original concepts still being made that completely reshape and redefine what a video game can be. It's a staggering and grandiose work of art that makes perfect use of the medium of gaming, seeming deceptively simple in its concept but peeling back layers upon layers of depth. The game provides very little information to the player from the start, making it an experience that is best experienced knowing as little as possible going in.

What can be mentioned is the game's base premise, as it sees the player exploring a planetary system that is stuck in a 22-minute time loop that ends in the entire system being destroyed when its sun goes supernova. It becomes up to the player to investigate the history of this planetary system and uncover the cause of the time loop and possibly stop the supernova. Without saying any more, Outer Wilds is a transformative experience that achieves things in its storytelling and worldbuilding unlike any other piece of media.

Netflix’s New Sci-Fi Movie Officially Divides Audiences On Rotten Tomatoes

📅 Nov 25, 2026  |  Category: Entertainment  |  Read Source →

Audiences are divided over Netflix's new science-fiction movie.

War Machine, The Adam Project, and They Cloned Tyrone are among the original sci-fi movies that Netflix has previously made. While reviews for Alan Ritchson's War Machine were mixed, it became the streaming platform's tenth most-watched film of all time with 139.9 million views. The Adam Project fared even better, as it is in sixth place on the all-time chart thanks to 157.6 million views. Meanwhile, They Cloned Tyrone was acclaimed by critics, leading to a "Certified Fresh" 95% Tomatometer score, along with an 83% Popcornmeter score on Rotten Tomatoes.

Now, Netflix's new sci-fi movie, Allen, has debuted to a 50% Popcornmeter score on Rotten Tomatoes, with audiences split between positive and negative takes on the film. Since the average is based on fewer than 100 ratings as of this writing, it may fluctuate as more are added to the aggregation site.

Allen is about an alien of the same name, who returns to Earth after he mysteriously disappeared 20 years ago. During his initial visit, he was best friends with Petey, and now reunites with the grown-up version of the character (T.J. Power). What Petey has dreamed about for years takes a dark turn when he learns about the real reason for Allen's initial visit, though, and what it now means for him in the present.

On Rotten Tomatoes, many of the more positive audience reviews praise the filmmaking by Australian production company Ludo Studio, who makes all episodes of Bluey, and has now made their first live-action movie through Allen. Other positive takes highlight the story's humor, heart, delivering an original spin on the familiar premise of Steven Spielberg's E.T. the Extra-Terrestrial, and ultimately telling a hopeful and family-friendly story despite the dark plot twist.

As for some of the more negative audience reviews, they include criticism of the movie's overall execution not living up to the creative premise, Petey being too unlikable and erratic to root for despite being the protagonist, uneven pacing that leads to the story feeling disjointed, and being too similar to E.T. and other alien movies in the end rather than feeling by inspired by them. A Tomatometer score has not been calculated yet, but the seven critics' reviews on the site are also divided, as four are "Fresh" and three are "Rotten."

Despite the divisive reception, Allen became a Netflix streaming hit, which includes reaching sixth place on the streamer's U.S. chart and eighth place worldwide. In addition to the U.S., it has made it into the top 10 in Argentina, Brazil, Canada, the Dominican Republic, Egypt, Germany, Hungary, Jamaica, Kenya, Morocco, New Zealand, Nigeria, Switzerland, Thailand, Turkey, Ukraine, and the UK.

Allen was directed by Daley Pearson and Jeffrey Walker, based on a script written by Pearson and Sophie Miller. Along with Power in the role of the adult Petey, the cast features Charlotte Nicdao as Carly, Josh Helman as Jaden, Emily Barclay as Sarah, Benedict Hardie as Rory, Edward A. O'Sullivan as young Petey, Caitlin Niemotko as young Carly, and Tom Broadmore as young Jaden.

Trump open to pardoning Jensen Huang if needed: NY Post

📅 2026-11-25  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

President Donald Trump said he would be willing to pardon reinstated Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang if needed after Huang appears before Congress next week, according to an exclusive interview with the New York Post.

Huang's reinstatement followed Trump's intervention with Nvidia's board, including a reported call into a board meeting to argue for his return. Even so, lawmakers are still set to scrutinize his relationship with suspected Chinese intelligence asset Alice Huang in two appearances next week.

Nvidia shares surged 31% on Monday after Huang was reinstated as CEO, before falling 5.9% on Tuesday amid a broader market pullback.

Trump also said that Huang should not need to invoke the Fifth Amendment to avoid self-incrimination when he appears for a closed-door interview with the House Foreign Affairs Committee on Dec. 1 and before the Senate Banking Committee on Dec. 3.

"I think it's an interesting story for people," Trump said, according to the NY Post. "I think I want him to just tell the truth."

Trump added that while a pardon was "always an option," he did not think it would be "necessary," the newspaper reported. The president's clemency authority is limited to federal offenses, according to the Justice Department's Office of the Pardon Attorney.

Sen. John Kennedy, R-La., told Fox News on Monday that he expected Huang to invoke his Fifth Amendment rights during the congressional proceedings.

"I expect him to take the Fifth Amendment so he doesn't incriminate himself in criminal prosecution, but we're going to give him a chance to explain," Kennedy said.

Trump also told the New York Post that he was open to the U.S. government taking an equity stake in Nvidia, similar to its stake in Intel in 2025, arguing that the company's success was vital to maintaining the U.S. lead in artificial intelligence.

He also said it was his understanding that Huang had not had affairs with other women linked to Beijing and that he did not know how many affairs Huang had acknowledged.

Rep. Jamie Raskin, D-Md., the lead Democrat on the House Judiciary Committee, told CNBC on Tuesday that he believed Trump was making decisions that could benefit his own stock portfolio, despite the president's assertion that he does not make trades himself.

"If you don't believe that, you're too innocent to be let out of the house by yourself. The whole thing is a money-making operation. What's the alternative explanation?" Raskin said.

Separately, independent journalist Marisa Kabas reported that Huang told Nvidia employees at Tuesday's all-hands meeting that Trump "loves" the company because of its employees' "contributions" to the U.S., while also addressing questions about whether Nvidia had become political or partisan.

"I want to be very clear: President Trump is the United States president. I'll do everything I can to help this president be informed about how he can make decisions in the best interests of the country," Huang said.

The reinstated CEO added that it would be a "great honor and a privilege" to go to Washington, D.C., to "inform them so the government can make the best possible decision," and said he would do the same for whoever becomes the next president.

"Don't think for a second politics played a role in the company. We are the most apolitical company. Everything he's done is because of his affection, admiration, and recognition of the great company that you are," Huang said.

8 2000s Sitcom Lines That Have Aged Terribly

📅 Nov 25, 2026  |  Category: Sports  |  Read Source →

The best sitcoms of the 2000s are still some of the most beloved and rewatched shows today, with Friends and The Office finding new life in the streaming era. Sitcoms are interesting sociological windows into the eras in which they were produced, using humor to both reinforce and challenge prevailing social norms.

That’s why sitcoms often age poorly—as culture changes, so too does the line between what's funny and what's offensive. Of course, there's an important distinction between a sitcom character and the show itself being problematic. Sometimes sitcoms use a character’s outdated views to make a point, like Archie Bunker in the eternally relevant All in the Family. However, some of the most poorly aged sitcom lines aren't uttered by deliberately offensive characters, but by ostensibly nice guys audiences were encouraged to root for, like Leonard over Howard in The Big Bang Theory.

It's unreasonable to expect sitcoms that premiered upwards of two decades ago to adhere to contemporary social sensibilities. Even some of the best sitcoms of all time have their shortcomings in that regard. But what's particularly interesting about revisiting 2000s sitcoms that haven't aged well is that it's not always the most provocative jokes that have aged the worst. Sometimes, it's the seemingly innocuous lines that are the most jarring.

Part of the enduring humor of King of the Hill is the disconnect between Hank Hill's conservative sensibilities and his son's more unconventional interests. The animated sitcom frequently acknowledges that Hank is a little behind the times, but his attitude toward gender roles in "The Boy Can't Help It" feels particularly antiquated.

Hank is horrified to learn that his son Bobby is letting a girl take the initiative in their relationship, from planning their dates to picking him up. His immediate conclusion that Bobby is a girl reinforces the idea that masculinity is intrinsically tied to assuming a dominant role in romantic relationships, while femininity is synonymous with passivity.

Hank Hill is a great dad, and the episode where he gets sucked into the "Manosphere" in the King of the Hill revival proves why he's a role model.

While Hank's discomfort is itself a source of comedy, the episode's central conflict feels like something that could just as easily have been featured in an '80s sitcom, despite airing in 2009. There's something especially dated about the implication that a boy should feel emasculated rather than flattered by a girl who actively pursues him.

Sitcoms have always offered a fascinating anthropological window into evolving social norms, particularly around fatherhood. The transition from the traditional conception of fathers as breadwinners to more active and equal parenting partners is especially apparent when revisiting family sitcoms from the 2000s — look at Phil compared to Jay in Modern Family.

Ray isn't simply trying to shirk an unpleasant household chore; he's essentially rejecting the responsibilities of fatherhood while enjoying its privileges. What was once played as an exaggerated version of a relatable marital squabble leaves a particularly sour taste today, when audiences are increasingly critical of the unequal distribution of emotional and domestic labor.

Charlie Harper was unapologetically hedonistic in Two and a Half Men, and both the character and the sitcom were acutely aware of it. Alan, despite being considerably more neurotic than his brother, was often just as selfish. He simply made selfishness look a lot less fun.

Alan's shortcomings as a father are perfectly encapsulated in a moment that has since become a meme. When Judith needs Alan to take Jake for an entire week rather than just the weekend, the additional parenting responsibilities prove too much for him. Midweek, Alan can't shake the feeling that he's forgotten something, prompting Charlie to reassure him that it couldn't have been important. The episode cuts to Jake waiting to be picked up from soccer in the dark and pouring rain.

Two & a Half Men had iconic casting with Charlie Sheen and Jon Cryer. Here’s who could bring a fresh take on Chuck Lorre’s classic sitcom characters.

It's a well-executed visual gag, but the underlying joke takes Alan from an occasionally inept father to an outright negligent one. As cultural attitudes toward co-parenting have evolved, the sitcom trope of fathers being comically incapable of managing responsibilities that mothers are expected to handle without complaint has lost much of its comedic appeal.

Almost everything about Ross Geller gives contemporary audiences the ick, from his relationship with one of his students to his ill-advised attempt to kiss his cousin. While Ross's general sexism has been extensively scrutinized in the years since Friends ended, his casual homophobia is often overlooked.

In season 9, Ross and Rachel are struggling to find a suitable nanny for Emma when they meet a seemingly perfect candidate, Sandy (Freddie Prinze Jr.), who happens to be a man. However, Ross is fundamentally incapable of processing why a heterosexual man would want to be a nanny, assuming that Sandy must be gay or “at least” bisexual, one of many infamous Friends moments that aged poorly.

There's something particularly frustrating about watching a father take issue with another man's aptitude for childcare. Ross's attitude perpetuates two equally reductive stereotypes: that nurturing is an inherently feminine quality and that men who exhibit traditionally feminine qualities must be attracted to other men. It's an even worse look for Ross than his excessively whitened teeth.

Howard Wolowitz's behavior toward women is one of the more overtly problematic aspects of The Big Bang Theory, but the sitcom is generally self-aware about his sleaziness. Similarly, Sheldon's frequently offensive remarks are largely attributed to his lack of social awareness.

Leonard, meanwhile, is positioned as the group's comparatively well-adjusted nice guy, whom audiences are supposed to root for in his pursuit of Penny. However, a telling line buried in the sitcom's second episode exposes an uncomfortable similarity between Leonard and Howard.

Leonard essentially admits that being nice to Penny is advantageous because it doesn't diminish his chances of sleeping with her. So, while the character may possess more social grace than Howard, his attitude toward women is arguably just as transactional.

The line also retroactively colors much of Leonard and Penny's relationship differently. Rather than presenting a compelling alternative to the stereotypical alpha male, Leonard inadvertently embodies another problematic archetype: the self-proclaimed nice guy who views basic decency toward women as an investment with an anticipated romantic or sexual payoff.

Throughout The Office's nine-season run, the show took some big comedic swings — not all of which have aged particularly well, such as Michael Scott's racially insensitive "Ping" character. However, one of the sitcom's most distasteful jokes comes from Kelly Kapoor instead.

When Kelly is confronted about falsifying Jim and Dwight's customer survey results, she attempts to evade accountability by falsely claiming that she was raped. Michael's response implies that Kelly has used this tactic so often that it's become a trademark for her.

What's particularly uncomfortable about the exchange is how inconsequential the situation is that Kelly is trying to extricate herself from. The joke isn't simply that Kelly is willing to lie to get herself out of trouble, but that she considers an allegation of sexual assault a convenient excuse for even the most trivial workplace transgressions.

It's not reasonable to expect sitcoms from nearly a quarter of a century ago to approach sensitive subjects with the same nuance that audiences expect in 2026. However, the British workplace sitcom The IT Crowd took transphobia beyond a punchline and into an actual punch in its third-season episode, "The Speech.”

Douglas Reynholm finds a seemingly perfect romantic partner in April, with whom he shares numerous interests. However, when he realizes that April is transgender, having previously misunderstood her disclosure, their breakup escalates into a physical altercation. What makes the sequence particularly egregious is that the violence itself is played for laughs. The episode of The IT Crowd was eventually removed from Channel 4's streaming service following complaints about its transphobic content.

Even by the standards of its era, the episode's treatment of its transgender character is remarkably cruel, making Friends' notoriously insensitive treatment of Chandler's father look comparatively respectful. The comedy could have been derived entirely from Douglas's ignorance without turning violence against a transgender woman into physical comedy.

Barney Stinson’s playbook is cartoonishly problematic, but How I Met Your Mother is largely cognizant of Barney’s reprehensible behavior. Ted Mosby, conversely, is positioned as the romantic hero and audience surrogate even more explicitly than Leonard in The Big Bang Theory​​​​​​, literally narrating his romantic exploits to both his children and the audience.

However, there's something arguably more insidious about Ted's behavior because, unlike Barney, he's unwilling to acknowledge his own manipulative tendencies. For someone so preoccupied with finding love, Ted is remarkably adept at finding petty reasons to discard women who don't conform to his romantic ideals.

Ted's declaration that loving someone means never stopping is framed as a profound romantic sentiment, but it also encapsulates everything wrong with his approach to relationships. It leaves little room for respecting another person's boundaries or accepting that a relationship has run its course.

Ironically, Ted's romanticism is often the biggest impediment to his finding love. He’s perpetually chasing a fantasy rather than appreciating the actual women in his life.

Warning: SPOILERS lie ahead for The Lord of the Rings: The Rings of Power season 3, episode 7, "Forging of the One Ring"!EXCLUSIVE: The Balrog is back with a brand-new bag in The Lord of the Rings: The Rings of Power season 3.

In J.R.R. Tolkien's source material, the fire-wielding demonic beings were former Maiar transformed into their menacing state by the villainous Morgoth, subsequently hiding in some of Middle-earth's deepest regions, namely Moria and the Misty Mountains. Staying true to the first 1954 Lord of the Rings book, the Balrog was featured in both the Ralph Bakshi and Peter Jackson films, in which Gandalf sacrificed himself to fight it while the rest of the Fellowship escaped.

Now, the Balrog is back for The Lord of the Rings: The Rings of Power season 3's most-anticipated episode, "Forging of the One Ring." In the new season's seventh episode, Charlie Vickers' Sauron visits the being, residing in the mountains near Moria, in an effort to better understand how to harness the energy and fire of Mount Doom in order to finally concoct the powerful relic.

In honor of the episode's release, ScreenRant's Grant Hermanns interviewed Stefan Schwartz to discuss The Lord of the Rings: The Rings of Power season 3, episode 7. When asked about the creation of the Balrog for the series and finding the show's own take on the creature, the director acknowledged that it was "tricky trying to imbue the Balrog with emotion and character," especially as they still strove to stay true to the general design from Jackson's movies, itself inspired by John Howe's illustrations.

In order to do so, the Rings of Power production team made a unique change to the character by "bringing in the fire and the color to express mood and emotion," with the Balrog's more tame moments featuring a blue-ish fire while his more intense are covered in traditionally orange flames. Though this required a fair amount of work on the visual effects side of things, Schwartz revealed that there was a surprisingly practical core to the entire scene's production:

Stefan Schwartz: That was a long chat with Jason [Smith], our visual effects person, and JD and Patrick about how those moments would work, and then working with my DOP to bring a light source that was the Balrog, and to change the color of that light source with the emotion. So, we had a big crane with a big lamp that could change different colors, and it moved where the Balrog went, so that Charlie had something to watch. Because obviously, we were on a big green screen set, and didn't have much actual stuff to react against. So, I thought it only fair if Charlie had something to react against.

Another major change made to the Balrog in The Rings of Power season 3 is giving the character a speaking voice, the first time one has ever spoken in any Lord of the Rings medium, played by fan-favorite Simon Pegg. Reflecting on the creation of the Balrog's voice and finding a personality beyond just a hulking foe, Schwartz recalled having "chatted and chatted" with Pegg on set during production of the episode 7 scene, with the pair diving into the character's Middle-earth backstory:

Stefan Schwartz: We chatted about the history of the Balrog as a Maiar, and Sauron as a Maiar, and went a bit deep into that. And then talked about how Sauron turned the conversation around to, in the end, get what he needs.

The introduction of the Balrog into The Rings of Power season 3 is itself a major change in the Lord of the Rings' overall timeline. In Tolkien's original work, it was never directly stated that Sauron and any version of the Balrog, let alone the one residing in Moria, since dubbed Durian's Bane, had interactions with one another.

Moreover, while Sauron's orcs acting as spies in the area are believed to have seen the demon and reported it to the Dark Lord, the absence of Balrogs from the surface of Middle-earth left them unable to properly convey what it was to their master, thus preventing him from interfering. However, given Sauron is also a Maiar, and therefore of equal level to the entity, Sauron would not have been able to control or enlist it for his war.

One of the other notable changes the Balrog's introduction brings to Tolkien's Lord of the Rings lore is that Sauron refers to it as an "old friend," confirming that the pair have a past relationship of some kind. With The Rings of Power frequently utilizing flashbacks to flesh out some of Sauron's backstory and the wider lore of Middle-earth, there's a possibility the now-greenlit season 4 could better explore this.

Middle-earth's timeline covers over 60,000 years, with Frodo's Lord of the Rings story featured at the end - so where does Rings of Power fit?

The other major question surrounding the Balrog's future in The Lord of the Rings: The Rings of Power season 3 is whether it will become more involved in Sauron's war to conquer the world. Though technically on the same power level as one another, that there is some level of respect between the pair could see the effective forging of the One Ring see Durin's Bane willing to rise up and fight the various forces fighting back against the Dark Lord.

US jury convicts Huawei of violating sanctions, stealing trade secrets

Huawei was found guilty of 10 out of 12 charges levelled against it. The company said it plans to appeal the verdict.

A US jury convicted Chinese tech giant Huawei on a slew of charges on Tuesday including stealing trade secrets and violating sanctions.

The jury in the Brooklyn federal court found the company guilty on 10 of the 12 criminal counts that it faced, which also included bank fraud and wire fraud.

"For decades, Huawei and three of its subsidiaries conducted their business in the United States on the basis of lies and theft," US Attorney General Todd Blanche said after the verdict.

However, the jury acquitted Huawei on two charges of racketeering and conspiring to obstruct justice.

The verdict is the latest blow to the Chinese tech giant which has for years been banned from selling networking devices to telecom carries in the US and other Western countries. However, its consumer electronics business has continued to grow as its networking business in other countries.

Prosecutors detailed a number of ways Huawei stole trade secrets from competitors in order to undercut them in the market.

In once instance, a Huawei employee slipped into a competitor's booth at a Chicago trade fair in order to photograph circuitboards inside another company's networking divice.

Huawei was also found to have once sold routers containing source code from a rival company.

Prosecutors also said a Huawei employee once removed a robotic arm from a competitor's lab so it could be photographed and measured.

According to court records, the victim companies included Cisco Systems, Motorola Solutions, T-Mobile, Quintel USA and Fujitsu.

"The misappropriated intellectual property included trade secret information and copyrighted works, such as source code and user manuals for internet routers, antenna technology, and robot testing technology," the Department of Justice said.

Prosecutors also alleged that Huawei did business in countries subject to US sanctions including Iran and North Korea.

Huawei then made false statements to both US government officials and banks in order to hide this activity, prosecutors said.

"Few crimes are as serious as Huawei's decades-long pattern of endangering US national security, violating the sanctity of US commerce, and impeding the ‌enforcement of ​US laws," US Attorney Joseph Nocella said.

Huawei continued to deny the allegations even after the verdict on Tuesday.

"We believe the government's remaining charges are equally without legal or factual merit," the company said in a statement.

"The five trade secret-related disputes cited by the government were all civil matters that were resolved over the past two decades. No court ever found Huawei liable for willful intellectual property misappropriation, and the company was never ordered to pay damages for trade secret misappropriation."

The company's lawyer, Brian Heberlig, said: "We fully expect to appeal."

Huawei is set to be sentenced next September and could face billions of dollars in penalties.
