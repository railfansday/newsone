Rosalía Takes the Statement Hat Trend to the Skies

📅 Nov 21, 2026  |  Category: Business  |  Read Source →

Is it a bird? Is it a plane? No, it’s Rosalía, wearing one of the most spectacular headpieces of the season.

On Friday night, the Spanish singer arrived at the LOS40 Music Awards in Barcelona with an enormous white dove perched above her head, its wings dramatically outstretched as though caught mid-flight. The sculptural creation came courtesy of Robert Wun’s fall 2026 couture collection, and was paired with a custom ivory Maison Margiela gown, featuring a plunging halter neckline and delicate pleating. With her dark hair pulled back and her bleached blonde halo, the avian accessory was given plenty of room to spread its wings.

It’s a particularly pertinent choice of chapeau for the singer, whose Lux era has been steeped in religious imagery and otherworldly fashion. The dove, of course, is a traditional symbol of peace and the Holy Spirit. Rosalía has already experimented with all manner of heavenly headwear, from the Margiela nun-inspired headpiece on her album cover to the theatrical hats worn throughout her Lux tour. This might be her most divine accessory yet.

As it happens, the dove has been rather busy lately. Just days earlier, Whitney Peak wore a cobalt-blue gown from the same Robert Wun collection to the New York premiere of The Hunger Games: Sunrise on the Reaping. Her dress featured two sculptural white birds appearing to suspend the fabric in midair. Wun, whose fantastical couture designs regularly blur the line between fashion, costume, and sculpture, is becoming something of a go-to for stars seeking genuinely captivating red-carpet creations.

Consider Elle Fanning, who recently embraced the gloriously eccentric style of her Hunger Games character Effie Trinket in a pink Robert Wun couture gown, complete with a matching pillbox hat topped by a miniature ballerina. Fanning has also worn a saucy red satin creation by British milliner Rachel Trevor-Morgan.

And she’s in excellent company. Dua Lipa recently championed the artsy chapeau in a frayed Chanel couture number, while Anya Taylor-Joy opted for a gigantic pink Jacquemus hat that framed her head like a halo. Even winter’s more practical accessories have taken a more eccentric turn: Hunter Schafer has been championing furry, archival Undercover headgear, while Taylor Russell has collaborated with Ilana Blumberg on some particularly fabulous fuzzy hats.

For Wun, the theatricality is very much the point. His fall 2026 couture collection, titled Child's Play, explored childhood and fairytales through a procession of fantastical characters, from Snow White to the Big Bad Wolf. Working with milliner Awon Golding, the designer produced a series of elaborate hats and sculptural headpieces that seemed to defy the laws of gravity.

After seasons of slicked-back buns and minimalist accessories, fashion’s most adventurous dressers are evidently ready for something a little more playful. The message? There’s no such thing as too much drama up top. And as Rosalía has demonstrated, sometimes the best accessories have a life of their own—and take flight.

Conte wants to ‘take over a club that is already organised’

📅 Nov 21, 2026  |  Category: Sports  |  Read Source →

Syndicated reporting provided by Stats Perform and reviewed by the SoccerNews editorial team.

Antonio Conte revealed his ambition to "take over a club that is already organised" after receiving what he felt was unfair criticism during his time with Napoli.

The Italian head coach is currently without a role after leaving Napoli at the end of last season.

Conte guided Napoli to Serie A success in 2024-25 before helping them finish second last term, while they also won the Supercoppa Italiana.

Napoli have struggled this season under Massimiliano Allegri, sitting 10th in the league after 11 games, with a clash against Torino next up on Saturday.

Conte, who was reportedly considered for the Manchester United job, is hoping for a role at a club which is in a better position.

"There is one thing I'd love to do before ending my career as a coach, which is to take over a club that is already organised," Conte told the Cronache di Spogliatoio podcast.

"Often, it involved returning them to their history, a dimension that perhaps they had forgotten.

"It happened at Juventus, who got back to winning after seven years, Inter hadn't won the Scudetto for 10, and Chelsea were coming off a disastrous season when I arrived, finishing in 10th place, but then won the Premier League with me.

"The same was true at Napoli, where they had finished 10th, then I arrived and took them to victory.

"It is inevitable that when you arrive with a curriculum like this, you are accompanied by big expectations. People and the media expect you to win.

"I still remember when I arrived at Napoli, a team that finished 10th, a beautiful city, their objective was to qualify for the Champions League.

"I thought I could just enjoy my work and the city for a while, but the moment I signed, the media completely changed the message. I don't know how many other coaches would be treated that way."

Napoli are looking to get back to winning ways in Serie A, having failed to win their last two (D1 L1), and come up against a Torino side who are just one point and one place behind them in the table despite a three-game winless streak (D1 L2).

Scott McTominay has scored three goals in two Serie A games against Torino; only against Inter (four) has he netted more goals across Serie A and the Premier League.

Torino could become the first opponent against whom the Scottish player scores in three consecutive appearances in his career across these two leagues.

Giovanni Simeone made 83 Serie A appearances for Napoli between 2022 and 2025, his most for any club in the competition, but scored just six goals for them.

Simeone has also scored five goals against Napoli in the league, and only against Lazio (10), Juventus and Torino (both six) has he scored more in the competition.

Napoli are unbeaten in 20 of their last 22 Serie A matches against Torino (W15 D5), scoring 42 goals (1.9 per game on average); Torino's only two victories in this period both came at the Olimpico Grande Torino (1-0 in October 2025 and 3-0 in January 2024).

Torino's last away win against Napoli in Serie A was in May 2009, a 2-1 comeback with goals from Rolando Bianchi and Alessandro Rosina for the Granata, after Inacio Pia's opening goal.

Since then, Napoli have remained unbeaten in all 14 subsequent home games against Torino (W9 D5), and only against Genoa (16) and Udinese (15) do they boast a longer active home unbeaten run against current Serie A teams.

However, Napoli have won just five of their first 15 games in all competitions this season (W5 D3 L7 in 2026-27); the last time they had so few wins in their opening 15 matches of a single season was in 2003-04 (W2 D10 L3), when they were in Serie B.

Tufa Labs’ tufazip has compressed 1GB of information from Wikipedia into just 105MB of space, losslessly, without using any hand-built models, according to a research post. Researcher Tommy He built tufazip, and the full package, at 105.39MB after optimization, includes a small, 4.33MB pre-trained starter model — stored at low precision, or quantized, to save space — that’s needed to unpack the file. The full model is a 12-layer Transformer, which means there are 12 stages for each guess to go through. The starter stores just two already-trained stages, copying them back and forth to fill all 12 once the full model spins up in memory; each copy keeps learning on its own.

This doesn’t work like a traditional chatbot or cloud AI but is, rather, a self-contained language model that learns on the fly. Notable here is that this compression is lossless, which means there’s no risk of any data being lost in the process. This means that predictor updates must be “fully deterministic.”

Training happens in two stages. The starter, a 34-million-parameter model, is trained offline, before any compression begins. Then, the full model begins to learn online, by predicting each token and training on it, with the unpacking side repeating the same steps, with the model itself being rebuilt alongside the text. For this reason, the total size must include, besides the compressed text, only the unpacking program and the files it needs, including the starter, but not the full trained model.

There is no traditional, hand-built compression model that assists, setting it apart, Tufa Labs says, from all 12 entries that now rank above it on the benchmark, which mix a Transformer with hand-built models. Its first, online-only version ranked second when submitted in early September; the 105.39MB version was 13th as of the benchmark’s Nov. 19 update. Emphasis should be placed on this being a text-only benchmark, for those imagining media files.

While this sounds more exciting than, say, WinRAR, it’s for research purposes and not a product, and that’s underlined by what you’d need to actually run it. Currently, two Nvidia B200 GPUs — plus two Intel Xeon Platinum 8570 CPUs in the test machine — would need 631,548 seconds to compress and 645,748 seconds to decompress the 105.39MB version, or about 7.3 and 7.5 days, respectively, not counting the starter’s offline training.

The first version, which learned entirely online and came in at 105.98MB, took 647,595 and 630,136 seconds. Its GPUs peaked at 43.37 GiB of VRAM between them, and total memory use, GPUs included, peaked at roughly 68,525 MiB during compression and 73,748 MiB during decompression.

The experiment is fundamentally interesting because it shows, for example, that a smaller vocabulary can actually help: a 4K-word tokenizer beats both 8K and 16K. Bigger isn’t always better, and that goes for model size, too, as a 267-million-parameter model did slightly worse than a 188-million-parameter model on this data set. A run started from a bigger, six-layer offline model predicted better, with just a 99.17MB payload, but its chunkier weights made the total file larger than the online-only run.

Sony Bravia 9 II Review (2026): Adjustable and Bright

📅 Nov 21, 2026  |  Category: Sports  |  Read Source →

The current flagship television from Sony, the Bravia 9 II, is an RGB TV model (Sony calls it “True RGB”) that brings to life even the darkest scenes in Game of Thrones. Much brighter than the midrange Bravia 7 II, the 9 II's best qualities are related to its black levels, picture quality, and excellent viewing angles that allow me to see Jon Snow and the GoT dragons perfectly, even when I'm sitting off to the side.

This premium Sony television carries a premium price of $3,600 for the 65-inch model, but that’s still $1,400 less than the LG Micro RGB Evo, which retails for $5,000. I ended up preferring the overall picture quality of that LG model and some other OLED models, but—after my usual rigorous testing—I felt the Bravia 9 II came close to matching the high caliber of those other options. In fact, it’s just a notch or two lower than the absolute best television you might find.

The Sony Bravia 9 II is all black, sits on two feet, and includes four HDMI ports (only two are HDMI 2.1, though, which allows for higher-bandwidth streaming and sound at 48 Gbps). Setup was a breeze; Google TV was up and running in less than five minutes, which is an impressive feat, given that both Samsung and LG televisions typically add a few extra steps to the install process, such as authenticating your login (with Samsung) or relying on an app (with the LG ThinQ).

The premium Sony Bravia 9 II also offers exceptional sound. It includes two upfiring tweeters for more realistic Dolby Atmos than the Bravia 7 II, which lacks these extra channels. When I watched House of the Dragon, the room-filling audio made me feel like I was flying a dragon or piloting a ship.

Viewing angles on the Bravia 9 II are about the best I’ve seen on any television. I tested this element by camping out in a chair and playing video games off to the side, and there wasn’t a hint of screen glare. These perks are, again, a sign of its premium quality, verified through my usual benchmarks. Skin tones looked varied and vivid; one person had a little more red in her face than what I noticed on the LG Micro RGB Evo or the Samsung Micro RGB R95H.

In a demo-reel benchmark test I use for all televisions, I was shocked by the Bravia 9 II's stellar performance. I’ve watched the same short segment with a fence in front of a winter landscape countless times to test various models, but here, the green grass in the background looked supremely vivid and clear. It’s because the Bravia 9 II has such exceptional screen brightness, color accuracy, and absolutely no glare.

The Sony remote is just OK. I wish the Home button were centered; since it's not, I kept clicking the mic for the Google Gemini voicebot (which is in the center) by mistake. I prefer the bare-bones Samsung remote or the one included with an Apple TV 4K streaming device. Just give me a Netflix button and volume controls, and I’m good. Still, the layout of this remote mostly makes sense, and I like that it’s backlit.

The Bravia 9 II didn’t pass all of my tests for performance while I watched shows and movies, but it did allow me to tweak more settings than I’m used to. For example, the TV automatically calibrated colors and tones for Spider-Man: Brand New Day, but it still looked too dark. I was able to manually adjust the black level and used Vivid mode to make Spidey’s suit look extra red.

However, compared to the picture quality of an OLED like the Samsung S95H, the Bravia 9 II's deep blacks looked less realistic. That's because on OLED TVs, the LEDs can be turned off entirely. The Bravia 9 II performed OK for Toy Story 5, but in darker scenes late in the film, I noticed the Samsung had inky blacks that reminded me of a high-end projector.

My main takeaway is that it's nice that you can tweak settings on the Bravia 9 II to great effect, thanks to an RGB panel that’s super-bright. The movies Awake and The Creator tested my patience a little in that regard. I adjusted the black level to precisely 61 (out of 100) and could then see faces, a predawn mountain scene, and a beach landing with better clarity. Knowing you can achieve those results is nice; actually doing it does take time.

While playing Resident Evil: Requiem to test brightness, picture quality, and latency, I confirmed that it's possible to adjust the black level to liven up dark scenes. I also noticed that the latency, at a 120-Hz refresh rate when I used one of the HDMI 2.1 ports, was almost nonexistent. That helped with every slight twitch when a seven-headed monster from the dark underworld of hell started attacking me.

Similar games, like Senua's Saga: Hellblade II, looked too gray and washed out on the midrange Bravia 7 II and some LED models I’ve tested. I also played End of Abyss, a game that takes place almost entirely in dark hallways, and the Bravia 9 II rendered those scenes with inky blacks—granted, not as black as an OLED would, but better than many LED TVs.

Bravia 9 II lived up to its flagship-quality promise for sportscasts, making my beloved Minnesota Vikings' purple jerseys look vivid and clear and the grass bright green—all without displaying any glare at all. News programs were bright, with strong color accuracy, but not oversaturated—a good compromise. In fact, it was so clear, I felt as if I could reach out to clip an MS Now analyst's nose hairs.

The Bravia 9 II is near the top of the leaderboard of the best televisions to buy right now. You'll just want to consider the technology it uses. RGB models use red, green, and blue LEDs to create realistic colors that, in theory, improve color accuracy and vividness. Unlike an OLED TV, an RGB TV still shines those LED colors through an LCD panel. So you have to pick your poison. The Bravia 9 II looked super bright and vivid, especially during NFL games. It’s just a hair less colorful and vivid compared to the LG Micro RGB Evo.

Knowing you can tweak the settings for the results you want and that anyone in your household will be able to see America’s Got Talent from any angle in the room is a major plus. But is the Sony Bravia 9 II worth $3,600 when so many TVs cost around $2,000? I would say it depends on what you watch. I want to see dragons at night and monsters in dark hallways, but for some animated shows, sports, and network TV, you might just want your device to work well, right out of the box, without worrying about cinematic quality. Yes, a flagship lets you do a lot more, but at a much steeper price.
