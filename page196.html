Mocha Brown Hair-Color Season Is Here—and It’s Universally Flattering

📅 Nov 18, 2021  |  Category: Business  |  Read Source →

There’s something about this time of year that makes everybody want to go darker. From their nail polish to their wardrobe, it’s a turn toward rich, darker shades. So it’s only natural that hair color would follow suit, going deeper and darker too. This winter in particular, it’s all about mocha brown.

Also called mocha chocolate, the rich, glossy shade was first popularized by celebrity colorist Jenna Perry, whose multidimensional brunette wizardry has fans like Bella Hadid and Kaia Gerber. Since then, the smooth and chocolaty hue—which combines darker espresso tones with lighter, milkier brunette shades—has become popular with a much wider section of the A-list, with Hailey Bieber and Daisy Edgar-Jones among the latest devotees.

“It reminds me of a chocolate mousse or a rich chocolate cake,” Perry tells Vogue. “That’s where the name came from. I thought mocha chocolate resembled the decadence of this shade of brown.” In line with sweater weather, Perry has churned out individualized takes on what she calls the “universally flattering brunette hair color” for a slew of stars, including longtime client Emily Ratajkowski and Amelia Gray. “A deep, rich brunette is chic,” Perry says. “The color reflects warmly in the sun, so it doesn’t ever look drab.”

According to Stephen Buller and Anita Rice, founders of Buller and Rice salon, these cacao tones feel luxurious for winter, particularly when the color isn’t completely flat. The key? Having a varied tone. “This is important throughout the lengths and ends so it reflects light and feels expensive rather than looking like one solid block of dark color,” they say. “This also gives clients who have had years of balayage an opportunity to go darker without feeling as though they’re simply covering everything up.”

Celebrity hairstylist Lorraine Dublin says that these indulgent, chocolaty shades look especially beautiful on textured and curly hair: “Think deep espresso brown with subtle ribbons of mocha, creating a rich, multidimensional finish,” she previously told Vogue. “It works beautifully on textured hair because the tonal variations catch the light, highlighting the shape and movement of curls, coils, and kinks.” She recommends asking your colorist for a “rich espresso base” with “delicate mocha or cinnamon brown accents,” which will impart a soft, seamless color melt.

Another important part of mastering the mocha chocolate look is keeping hair strong and shiny. “What makes this hair color so incredible from the start is having healthy hair,” explains Perry, who adds that preserving pigments with a color-safe shampoo and conditioner is key. Try Matrix Total Results Brass Off Shampoo to nix brassy or red tones. You can also use an at-home gloss or color conditioner, like Glaze Supergloss Color Conditioning Hair Gloss Chocolate Gleam or John Frieda Brilliant Brunette Color Vibrancy Conditioner, to prolong your hair’s vibrancy.

15 Best Adventure Shows Like 'Avatar: The Last Airbender,' Ranked

📅 Sep 18, 2021  |  Category: World  |  Read Source →

Mike Thomas is the TV Resource Editor for Collider and is responsible for crafting TV guides and materials for fans who want to learn more about their favorite shows. He's also helped cover several shows with various reviews and interviews with the cast and crew behind hits such as One Piece, Loki, and X-Men '97.  His love for high fantasy, musicals, and comic books is why he can spend hours breaking down the genius of Bahubali or the lyrical artistry of the Disney hit Encanto. During his free time, Mike's passion for movies continues with his podcasts The Chatter After, The Chatter After Live, and The Amateur Otaku, all shows where he can dive deep into why all stories matter.

It’s rare to find a series that knocks it out of the park in nearly every category, but somehow Nickelodeon’s Avatar: The Last Airbender became one of the most beloved franchises in the world. Avatar is a fantasy series featuring strong world-building, fantastic characters, and an inspirational story that defined a generation.

The show follows Aang and his friends as they go on a life-changing journey to save the world from the reign of Fire Lord Ozai. The series is an anime-inspired fantasy adventure and blends both Eastern and Western philosophy into an action-packed series anyone can enjoy. Avatar: The Last Airbender is one-of-a-kind, but if you’re looking for more adventures and lore-filled worlds to explore, we have other similar shows for you to check out after the credits roll on Avatar.

Something that Avatar: The Last Airbender does well is blending fun, kid-like storytelling with mature themes and character arcs. Another series that does this excellently is the one and only Gravity Falls. The main trio of Dipper Pines (Jason Ritter), Mabel Pines (Kristen Schaal), Grunkle Stan (Alex Hirsch), and Soos Ramirez (Alex Hirsch) is abundantly lovable, very akin to how much people adore Team Avatar.

Gravity Falls is a show that loves good worldbuilding—just like Avatar: The Last Airbender—as Dipper and Mabel slowly discover that they are in a world that is not as natural as they once believed. They consistently rise to the occasion, have phenomenal character arcs, and experience thrilling action from episode to episode. —Eddie Possehl

Just as Avatar: The Last Airbender is one of the most popular animated shows out there, Adventure Time has the exact same reputation. Much like Gravity Falls, Adventure Time handles mature themes and journeys via childlike storytelling and humor. Jake the Dog (John DiMaggio) and Finn the Human (Jeremy Shada) are excellently written and have amazing growth over the course of the show's 8-year run.

They both slowly transition from episodic storytelling to serialized storytelling in a manner that is natural and flows well. There is deep world lore in each series that makes everything feel so expansive and lived-in. It's also another series that has a group of younger characters rising to the occasion and kicking butt, against all odds. —Eddie Possehl

Avatar: The Last Airbender features a group of youthful, yet powerful, characters that must go on a long cross-world journey to reach their destinations—physically and within their character arcs. The same can be said about Hunter X Hunter. Both shows also have young, pure-of-heart protagonists in Aang and Gon Freecss (Megumi Han and Erica Mendez).

Because of this, there's a feeling of hope and light that radiates through both shows, regardless of how genuinely dark they can get. These young men—both some of the strongest people in their respective universes—always lock in when they need to and are perfect heroes to follow. Not to mention, the power systems of Bending and Nen are well-developed, allowing for some great power-scaling and action. —Eddie Possehl

Another world-trekking adventure with a lovable, hopeful protagonist comes in the form of none other than One Piece—also known as one of the most famous television shows of all time. Within both shows, the characters go from island to island, city to city, to accomplish their goals. Aside from that, both of these amazing protagonists have loyal supporting casts that become perfect characters to follow.

You know you've written a good show when the supporting cast can be just as interesting, if not more so, than the protagonist themselves. This couldn't be more true for Avatar: The Last Airbender and One Piece. The Straw Hat Pirates and Team Avatar are abundantly similar, each with characters that are superbly developed. —Eddie Possehl

While it may not come to one's mind at first, Ben 10 is actually more similar to Avatar: The Last Airbender than one would think. It's got pretty much everything Avatar: The Last Airbender does: a young hero who can be a little irresponsible because he's a kid who holds the power of all the elements/aliens, has a close group of people around him to keep him on track, and has tough foes directly connected to his power.

Ben 10 is another series that is phenomenally famous. It's among the most popular and profitable franchises out there, and the way that Ben Tennyson (Tara Strong) grows by the end of the show is akin to how much Aang does — the airbender does it better, though, let's be honest. But the hero is far closer to Aang as a character than one would assume. —Eddie Possehl

If you just finished Avatar: The Last Airbender, then the next thing you should watch is its sequel series, The Legend of Korra. Here, we follow Avatar Korra, the successor to Avatar Aang. The world is long past the 100-year war, and Korra must learn what it means to be the Avatar in the modern age.

Here, the new Team Avatar faces off against anarchists, terrorists, and the spiritual imbalances that plague this new era. The Legend of Korra doesn’t follow in the footsteps of Avatar and instead forges its own path. One that is equally as engaging and deserves more recognition. To find the best dual-fuel generator for your home, campground, or worksite, consider the factors listed below, including fuel efficiency, fuel tank capacity, power output, and control panel configuration.

Dual-fuel generators have an isolated gasoline tank with a fuel capacity that ranges from as small as 2 gallons to more than 10 gallons. The larger the fuel tank on the generator, the longer the generator can provide gasoline-based power. But, as tank size increases, so does the size of the generator.

Fuel capacity does not affect how much propane you can use with your dual-fuel generator. Propane gas is stored under pressure in canisters, which attach to the generator with a hose and pressure regulator for direct fuel consumption.

The power output on dual-fuel generators is measured in watts. Manufacturers’ specs include peak power output and running power output. Peak power output refers to the maximum number of watts the generator can provide at any one time. Running power output equals the average number of watts produced during use.

Dual-fuel generators generally produce a power output from 1,000 watts to over 12,000 watts, depending on their size. Gasoline produces a higher wattage than propane, so many manufacturers of dual-fuel technology break wattage into propane peak and running outputs and gasoline peak and running outputs.

For greater power output needs, users may need to consider a standby generator rather than a portable dual-fuel option.

You can start your dual-fuel generator with an electric start switch or a recoil cord.

Dual-fuel generators are typically large and heavy, with hoses or tanks that make them difficult to carry and move. Thus, some manufacturers include wheel kits for tilting the generator back and rolling it to the desired location.

You may also be able to purchase a wheel kit separately to add to your existing generator or replace an older wheel kit that doesn’t work well anymore. Either way, a wheel kit makes transporting a portable dual-fuel generator much easier.

Lift hook bars are handy on jobsites, allowing workers to attach the generator to a hook-and-pulley system and raise or lower it instead of carrying it up or down stairs or ladders. In industrial settings, this feature helps raise the generator onto a walkway or platform.

A lift hook bar will increase the generator’s weight (and likely the price), so don’t look for a generator with this feature unless you truly plan to use it.

Most dual-fuel generators are between 22 inches long by 22 inches wide by 22 inches high and 35 inches long by 30 inches wide by 30 inches high. They typically weigh between 100 and 250 pounds.

Gasoline and propane each have their pros and cons as fuel options. Gasoline is easier to find, but propane costs less. Propane is a better fuel source in hot summer weather, while gas burns better in the cold. A dual-fuel generator lets you choose one or the other based on immediate circumstances.

Using both fuel sources, one after the other, greatly increases a generator’s runtime. Users can run to the gas station to refuel while the generator keeps providing power on propane.

A dual-fuel generator operates like a single-fuel generator, except it can burn propane and gasoline to produce electrical energy. It converts mechanical energy produced by the burning fuels to force the movement of electrical charges and generate electricity.

While some single-fuel generators can be adapted for use with natural gas, this isn’t the case with dual-fuel generators. Unless specially designed, dual-fuel generators do not run on natural gas and cannot be adapted for this purpose.

Propane gas is less expensive than gasoline, nearly impossible to spill, and burns cleaner, resulting in less wear on the engine over time. Running a dual-fuel generator on propane will save money but slightly decrease the machine’s peak and running watts.

Runtime is determined by fuel efficiency and fuel tank capacity. On average, a standard dual-fuel generator runs about 8 to 10 hours on propane.
