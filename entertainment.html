Will Ebenezer Be The Box Office Hit Johnny Depp's Hollywood Comeback Needs?

📅 Nov 04, 2026  |  Category: Entertainment  |  Read Source →

Johnny Depp is attempting to mount a Hollywood comeback. After years of being embroiled in controversy and starring in films you probably haven't heard of (like the colonial drama "Waiting for the Barbarians"), he's back in his first major studio movie in a long time. "Ebenezer" is a new take on Charles Dickens' "A Christmas Carol," with Depp playing Scrooge. The question is, can this be the box office hit that Depp needs?

Directed by Ti West ("X," "The Inkeepers"), "Ebenezer" is expected to pull in between $17 and $24 million domestically when it hits theaters next weekend, per Box Office Theory. Against a reported $38 million production budget, that would be a solid start for the Paramount release, especially since this one should be able to hang around through the holiday season. That is, assuming audiences like what West and Depp have cooked up. If it's eviscerated by critics and audiences alike? That's another conversation.

Competition shouldn't be a big issue, at least not right away. "Godzilla Minus Zero" could make box office history for the franchise topping the charts this weekend, but it shouldn't get in the way come its second frame. "The Cat in the Hat" is unlikely to be a major, direct threat, either. Rather, the biggest competition will come when "The Hunger Games: Sunrise on the Reaping" arrives on November 20.

The "Ebenezer" trailer positions Depp's "Christmas Carol" re-telling as being closer to a horror movie. Now, horror has been extremely hot at the box office lately, so that makes sense. Paramount is also selling the film with the tagline, "The name you know, the story you don't," and it has a star-studded supporting cast that includes Rupert Grint ("Harry Potter"), Andrea Riseborough ("Mandy"), Daisy Ridley ("Star Wars"), Trammell Tillman ("Severence"), and Ian McKellen ("X-Men").

There are obvious risks here. As mentioned, if "Ebenezer" is met with a bad response, all of these numbers could be meaningless, as word of mouth could wreck its ability to have legs. Still, it's likely to enjoy big returns on VOD and streaming, if for no other reason than curiosity from viewers.

The other issue is Johnny Depp himself. Depp dropped out of the "Fantastic Beasts" franchise after a court case verdict tied to tabloid The Sun in 2018. That, coupled with his highly-publicized courtroom battle with his ex-wife Amber Heard over allegations of abuse, derailed his career. Even before that, though, it wasn't going as well as it once had been.

Depp proved his best days were already behind him with the big box office bomb that was "Mortdecai" in 2015. He hasn't starred in a meaningful hit since 2018. Yes, the "Pirates of the Caribbean" movies made billions of dollars, but that was a long time ago. Do audiences still care about Depp? That's the big question Paramount seeks to answer. It's betting the answer is "yes."

Beyond that, Ti West has never directed a high-profile movie of this size. He helmed the "X" films rather successfully, with "MaXXXine" sending off the trilogy on a box office high note, but we're still talking about a movie that made $22 million in total. This is a big step up for West, and in light of the Depp of it all, there's a lot of pressure. Was the director able to tune out the noise and deliver a crowd-pleaser? We'll know soon enough.

"Ebenzer" hits theaters on November 13, 2026.

Lucy Liu’s ‘The Pirate Queen’ Expands With Graphic Novel, New Game and International Tour as Creator Eloise Singer Joins BAFTA Breakthrough Cohort (EXCLUSIVE)

📅 Nov 04, 2026  |  Category: Entertainment  |  Read Source →

Lucy Liu and Eloise Singer are preparing to take “The Pirate Queen: No Safe Waters,” their immersive experience about Chinese pirate leader Cheng Shih, on an international tour next year, Singer tells Variety. The pair have also co-written a graphic novel and are developing a new game.

The news comes as Singer, the project’s writer and director, joins the 2026 BAFTA Breakthrough U.K. cohort. Related Stories Sega's 'Sonic the Hedgehog' to Launch Lingokids Games (EXCLUSIVE)

The franchise is built around a retelling of the stories about real life character Cheng Shih, who was a powerful female pirate in the South China Sea at the beginning of the 18th century.

“No Safe Waters” played in the Cannes Film Festival’s Immersive Competition this year, with Liu narrating and producing alongside Singer. It follows the original 2024 VR version of “The Pirate Queen,” which earned an Emmy nomination.

Liu says Cheng Shih was intelligent and strategically brilliant, and won respect through negotiation and leadership “in a world designed to exclude women entirely.” In Liu’s view, the pirate leader defies nearly every stereotype historically placed on Asian women in storytelling. “She was politically astute, ruthless when necessary, deeply strategic, and completely self-determined. Stories like hers remind us that history has always contained powerful, complex women – they were never centered in mainstream narratives,” Liu tells Variety. “That’s beginning to change, and I think audiences are hungry for stories that feel more expansive.”

Where the first outing was built for a headset, the new piece fills a shared room with projection, sound and movement. Audiences stand among storms and fleets as dragons move beneath their feet.

Liu does not appear on screen. She says voice carries a different kind of responsibility in immersive work, guiding atmosphere and movement at the same time. “In film, the camera captures a glance or physical gesture to communicate subtext, but here the voice can help create emotional architecture,” she says.

Singer has spent a decade on “The Pirate Queen,” the last three years of it with Liu, and says the pair have co-written a graphic novel, are preparing the international tour and are developing a new game. “I feel really lucky to be making it with Lucy. She’s thrown her heart and soul into this alongside me, and getting to build this world together has been one of the most special parts of the journey,” she says. “I love seeing how one story can take on a whole new life across different formats, each offering audiences a completely different way to experience it.”

Liu says the project’s ambition, and the importance of telling Cheng Shih’s story with nuance and scale, is something she and Singer both care about deeply. “We’re building ‘The Pirate Queen’ as a larger storyworld together, so it was never just about lending my voice to one experience,” she says. “As a producer, I wanted to help support the integrity of the story and the larger creative vision. I’m always drawn to work that challenges convention and creates space for voices and histories that haven’t been fully explored, and Cheng Shih’s story deserves that kind of scale.”

BAFTA named Singer to this year’s Breakthrough program, its Netflix-supported initiative for emerging screen talent, on Nov. 4. She was selected in the games category for “No Safe Waters.”

“It’s still a bit of a pinch-me moment. It’s something I’ve aspired to for such a long time, so to be selected is a little surreal, and to have BAFTA and Netflix recognize what we’ve created with ‘The Pirate Queen’ is incredibly special,” Singer tells Variety. “I’m especially proud that it’s for an immersive project. We started with a story we really believed in and wanted to bring it to life in a completely different way, something audiences could truly step inside and experience.”

A friend who went through the program before her told her to make the most of every opportunity. “So that’s what I intend to do! I’m really looking forward to meeting other filmmakers and finding ways to take over the world together,” she says.

Singer says this year is the first time she has really felt a scalable and sustainable future for immersive storytelling. “I’ve been genuinely encouraged by the level of distribution interest around ‘The Pirate Queen.’ It feels like there is a real appetite for immersive work now,” she says.

Liu sees the form as both an extension of filmmaking and something new. “It grows from cinematic language but it also asks the audience to participate differently. That changes the relationship between story and viewer,” she says. “We’re still discovering the language of immersive storytelling which is what makes this moment so exciting creatively.”

Singer’s background is in film and TV, and alongside new immersive work she is developing scripted projects. “I’ve always been drawn to stories about people who challenge expectations, whether they’ve been overlooked by history, underestimated by others, or simply refused to play by the rules,” she says. “Ultimately, I want to keep telling stories I really believe in and finding the most exciting ways to bring them to life.”

Quentin Tarantino's Major New Netflix Movie Officially Releases In IMAX This Month

📅 Nov 04, 2026  |  Category: Entertainment  |  Read Source →

Nearly 35 years after bursting on the scene with Reservoir Dogs, Quentin Tarantino remains one of the most acclaimed and singularly talented modern filmmakers. The mastermind behind classics such as Pulp Fiction, Kill Bill, and Inglorious Basterds has not helmed a feature film for seven years but returns this month for a major release that ranks among 2026’s most talked about movies.

In 2019, Tarantino wrote and directed Once Upon a Time in Hollywood, a witty, intentionally meandering period piece about 1960s Hollywood, told through the perspective of a fading, alcoholic actor and his effortlessly cool stuntman across three fateful days. In addition to grossing over $400 million at the global box office, the film was lauded for its punchy dialogue, love letter to ‘60s L.A., and duo of stellar lead performances, in particular that of Brad Pitt, whose turn as Cliff Booth earned him an Academy Award.

After seven years, Tarantino follows Once Upon a Time in Hollywood with a grand return to cinema -- in the biggest premium format and on the biggest streaming service.

Quentin Tarantino’s eagerly anticipated return to movies comes in the form of The Further Mis-Adventures of Cliff Booth, a sequel to Once Upon a Time in Hollywood that positions Pitt’s former stuntman in the lead role. Interestingly, Tarantino opted against directing the film as he continues to adhere to a self-imposed limit of 10 feature-length directorial films. As he did not want his final project to be a sequel or a retread of covered ground, Tarantino passed the torch to another acclaimed filmmaker. However, he did write the screenplay for the movie.

Stepping into Tarantino’s lofty shoes is David Fincher, who previously collaborated with Brad Pitt on Seven, Fight Club, and The Curious Case of Benjamin Button. Fincher has had a long relationship with Netflix dating back to House of Cards and continuing with Mindhunter and Mank, making him a perfect choice to spearhead this next chapter in Cliff Booth’s story.

The Further Mis-Adventures of Cliff Booth takes place in 1977, roughly eight years after the events of Once Upon a Time in Hollywood and in the seminal year that saw George Lucas bring the original Star Wars to theaters.

The film picks up with Pitt’s Cliff Booth having transitioned away from full-time stunt work, now taking on odd jobs as a bartender and a cinema projectionist. In the shadows, however, he works as a studio fixer, handling murder, blackmail, extortion, and all manner of dirty secrets for the movers and shakers in Hollywood.

A surprise screening of the first 17 minutes of the film at AFI Fest revealed that Cliff gave up being Rick Dalton’s stuntman due to their growing physical dissimilarities. In more blunt terms, Cliff candidly says in the footage, “Rick got too fat for me to double him anymore.” It is currently unclear if Leonardo DiCaprio will reprise his role as Rick Dalton in the sequel.

Despite DiCaprio’s (presumed) absence, The Further Mis-Adventures of Cliff Booth bolsters an ensemble cast to back Pitt up. Timothy Olyphant will reprise his role as Lancer star James Stacy in the film, while no other cast members from Once Upon a Time in Hollywood are confirmed to appear.

Peter Weller, known for his role in RoboCop, will play Cliff’s father in the film. Yahya Abdul-Mateen II will play Roderick ‘Hot Rod’ Harrison, a Blaxploitation star who Pitt’s character crosses paths with. Rounding out the principal members of the cast are Elizabeth Debicki, Scott Caan, Carla Gugino, Matt Groove, JB Tadena, Corey Fogelmanis, and Karren Karagulian.

Plot details are largely under wraps for the film as promotional material has primarily focused on setting the atmosphere for the story.

On December 8, 2026, Tarantino will publish a companion novel titled The Adventures of Cliff Booth.

The Further Mis-Adventures of Cliff Booth will have a two-week limited IMAX theatrical run in select theaters on November 25, 2026. Tickets can be purchased here. The film will make its global streaming debut on Netflix on December 23, 2026.

'It Follows' Star Officially Addresses Her Return to Another Iconic 2010s Thriller Franchise

📅 Nov 04, 2026  |  Category: Entertainment  |  Read Source →

In a new interview, Monroe addressed the possibility of returning for a sequel

Maika Monroe has built a career as a modern scream queen. After a breakout 2014, when she starred in both It Follows and The Guest, she became a key figure in horror, continuing to revisit the genre in everything from Longlegs to Victorian Psycho. This year, both It Follows and The Guest have been a part of the horror conversation: It Follows just wrapped a sequel, wonderfully titled They Follow, while The Guest just received a quasi-sequel with Onslaught, which director Adam Wingard set in the same continuity as his previous 2014 film.

Monroe notably doesn't appear in Onslaught (there are no repeat characters), but will she ever return to The Guest universe? In a recent interview for her upcoming film, Victorian Psycho, Monroe sounded hopeful.

“I love Adam so much,” she tells Polygon. “We grabbed coffee not too long ago, and he's awesome, so I would love to work with him again, so we'll see.”

The Guest remains one of Wingard’s most beloved films (and this is the guy who directed Godzilla vs. Kong). Written by Simon Barrett and starring Monroe and Dan Stevens, the story follows a charming visitor named David (Stevens) who arrives at the Peterson family home. There, he reveals that he is an army veteran and was a close friend to their son, who died while on tour in Afghanistan. David soon integrates himself into the grieving family, coinciding with strange and violent events taking place in the town. Anna (Monroe), the family’s eldest daughter, begins to grow suspicious of his behavior and past. The Guest marked one of Stevens first freaky roles, an edge that has since become a defining aspect of his career.

Onslaught came out earlier this year and marked a reunion between Wingard and Barrett, who’ve built a long partnership throughout their careers, collaborating on films like A Horrible Way to Die, You’re Next, and the V/H/S franchise.

The film stars Adria Arjona as an army vet with PTSD, who’s forced to defend her community and her daughter against three Terminator-like army experiments who’ve escaped a nearby facility.

Onslaught also features Stevens as Dr. Hans Kammler, an aging German scientist with Nazi ties and the mastermind behind the super-soldiers stalking the protagonist. Wingard confirmed that the events of Onslaught take place near the town that’s featured in The Guest. Both films are a part of his and Barrett’s "KPG-verse," named after the shadowy organization that runs the super-soldier experiments and appears in several of their projects.

Even though Stevens plays different characters in both films, Wingard suggested there might be a connection between them. “I think he’s related to the genesis of the David character in some form [or] fashion, and I think that’s something that maybe we’ll more literally explore hopefully in future installments,” he told The Wrap.

Stevens, for his part, is happy to continue to explore that universe. “We’re definitely open to more KPG-verse. I don’t know whether a Guest 2, per se, is on the cards anytime soon, but you never know,” he told The Hollywood Reporter. “We’ve teed everything up for an Onslaught sequel as well, so watch this space.”

Whether we get a sequel to David and Anna’s stories or something new set in the same universe, the door is open. Monroe, Wingard, and Stevens all seem to agree: The world of The Guest is one they’d be happy to return to.
