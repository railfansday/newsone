MLB rumors: Corey Seager willing to waive his NTC, plus latest on Mets, Cubs, Blue Jays, Guardians, more

The uncertain 2026-27 Major League Baseball offseason is fully underway. The ongoing CBA negotiations and the near certainty of an owner-implemented lockout on Dec. 2 loom over all, and it may indeed mean a flurry of trades and free agent signings leading up to that date. That was certainly the case leading up to the last lockout of 2021-22, and this time around we've already seen some leading examples. Just recently, for instance, the New York Mets landed the biggest fish in the pond by signing ace Tarik Skubal to a record contract. Not long after, the Chicago Cubs fortified their rotation with Trevor Rogers and their outfield with Teruaki Sato, and Freddy Peralta is headed to the Baltimore Orioles. More's surely to come before lockout day arrives.

Thanks to that prospect of a hastened pace, offseason rumors are flowing. Speaking of which, we'll be here each day to round up what's out there. You can find Tuesday's supply just below.

The Philadelphia Phillies are open to moving Trea Turner off of shortstop given that he's now a pronounced defensive liability. Speaking of openness, veteran shortstop Corey Seager of the Texas Rangers is willing to waive his no-trade clause to join the Phillies and upgrade the position for them, NBC Sports Philadelphia reports.

Now 32, Seager will always have health and durability concerns, but he remains a plus defensive shortstop who produces well at the plate by positional standards. Signed to a 10-year, $325 million deal prior to the 2022 season (just before the last lockout, in fact), Seager is owed another $155 million through the 2031 season. The Rangers are reportedly looking to shake things up. The Phillies, meantime, are trying to keep their contention window open. It's a fit both ways, at least in theory. Such a move would likely mean a move to the outfield for Turner, who himself has very little trade value thanks to his ongoing decline and big contract.

The Toronto Blue Jays have thus far failed to make a big addition this offseason, but they still have targets on the board. The Jays, after missing out on Tarik Skubal, Freddy Peralta, and Trevor Rogers, have moved on to targets like right-hander Kaima Taira out of Japan and lefty Adrian Morejon, the Athletic reports. Taira, 27, ranked No. 2 on our list of notable international free agents this offseason. Here's part of that write-up:

"Listed at 5-foot-8, Taira gets outlier extension down the mound (close to seven feet despite his height), which creates a low release window. Hitters rarely see the ball come from where Taira releases it. He's not a gimmick pitcher either. Taira sits mid-90s with his four-seam fastball and backs it up with a cutter, sinker, sweeper, slider, changeup, and splitter. The sweeper and slider are distinct pitches, as are the changeup and splitter. The Seibu Lions used Taira as a reliever in 2024 and 2025, then started him in 2026, and he handled the assignment well. The breaking balls are just OK, and the fact of the matter is that pitchers this height don't often hold up under a starter's workload. I would assume the team that signs Taira will give him a chance to start. In the long run, though, he might be a reliever, perhaps even a multi-inning setup man. Taira was officially posted on Nov. 1."

Morejon, meanwhile, is particularly intriguing. He's young for a free agent at age 27, and he's coming off a dominant season in the San Diego Padres' bullpen. Thanks to his elite stuff and full repertoire, he's also a strong candidate to transition to the rotation moving forward, though there's reporting that he'd rather stay in the bullpen. That makes him a coveted presence on the market, and the Jays have the resources and willingness to land him.

As noted above, the Chicago Cubs have been energized on the market lately, but they still have work to do after losing so many contributors to free agency. On that front, the club may be eyeing the aforementioned Taira, who's making the leap from Japan, the Athletic reported before the latest flood of moves.

While Taira's role in MLB is TBD, the Cubs still have vacancies in the rotation for 2027 and beyond.

The Cleveland Guardians have agreed to a free-agent contract with left-hander Kris Bubic, Sportsnet reports. The contract is for $17.5 million over one year, according to ESPN. Bubic, 29, made nine starts for the Kansas City Royals last season, and after the trade to the Los Angeles Dodgers, he was used out of the bullpen. Bubic has an outlier fastball thanks to its rise and his unusual release point, and he's an interesting "ceiling" candidate in the rotation. However, his substantial history of injuries may mean he's best used out of the bullpen.

Even after achieving a reunion with veteran starting pitcher Sonny Gray, the Minnesota Twins are still looking to fortify their roster for a return to contention in 2027. The club still hopes to make one more significant addition this offseason and add another player who may not grab as many headlines, the Athletic reports. Atop the wishlist are a first baseman or a second baseman and another lefty reliever.

Even after the splash addition of Skubal, the Mets may not be done adding starting pitchers in the service of a rebound season in 2027. On that front, the Mets may be interested in adding a starting pitcher on a short-term deal for next season, the Athletic reports. That would likely limit them to the back half of the market, especially with so many bigger names reaching agreements.

Indian government has 'lost the plot', says 'cockroach' leader barred from UK flight

📅 Nov 24, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Watch: India's government is 'scared', cockroach movement leader says after travel ban

One of the leaders of India's "cockroach" movement has said the Delhi government has "completely lost the plot" after he was stopped from leaving the country for the UK.

Saurav Das said he was meant to board a flight on Tuesday morning but was told by immigration officers his passport had been "flagged" because of discrepancies involving his date of birth.

In an interview with the BBC, Das said there were no discrepancies in his documents, accusing the Indian government of using cheap tactics to stop activists from travelling and speaking on issues that matter to young people.

The BBC has reached out to the federal home ministry, Delhi police and airport officials for comment.

Saurav told the BBC World Service's Newshour programme the incident showed the government was "scared".

"They are scared of the cockroach movement. They're scared of young people asking them questions because they have never had to deal with a generation that wants to ask questions so head on. So that jitters them."

By not allowing him to travel, he said the government had lowered India's stature in the world.

"It's international news that I am unable to travel to the UK," he said. "And this shows Prime Minister Modi in such poor light.

"It's a way to harass, to target activists, young voices in India by the Modi government."

Das said he had been invited by the Oxford Union, as well as universities including Cambridge and Edinburgh, to speak about the movement.

Ratna Singh, another leader of the Cockroach Janta Party (CJP), wrote: "The manner in which Saurav was not allowed to board today raises deeply troubling questions about the state of freedom of expression and the space being allowed for young voices in this country."

On Tuesday, the London School of Economics Student Union's Amnesty International Unit, a student-led charity group, shared a post on Instagram saying that an event featuring Das had been "indefinitely postponed".

Saurav Das (L) speaking following the resignation of India's education minister in July

Das, a former journalist, became one of the faces of the CJP - a Gen Z-led movement that began as satire but has since emerged as a youth-led pressure group.

In July, protests spearheaded by the CJP over exam paper leaks led to the resignation of India's Education Minister Dharmendra Pradhan .

In October, CJP leaders including Das were among thousands detained by the police for trying to hold protests demanding the resignation of India's Chief Election Commissioner Gyanesh Kumar over alleged irregularities in electoral rolls. Kumar and the Election Commission have denied wrongdoing.

The same month, Das and Ashutosh Ranka, another CJP leader, were jailed for seven days after being arrested for trespassing at a banned protest site in Delhi.

Das insisted the CJP would continue protesting, but it did not want to be a political party "as of today".

"It's a long haul. We are not here for a short time. We are looking at India in the next 10 to 20 years. How do we develop India into a better country? That is the vision that we want to carry forward."

Abhijeet Dipke, the founder of CJP, wrote on X that if Das "had looted a bank or committed financial fraud", he may have been allowed to fly to London, referring to billionaire bank-loan defaulters who have left India.

There have been other instances in recent years of people being prevented from boarding flights.

In September 2025, Bhanu Tatak, an environmental and anti-dam activist from India's north-eastern Arunachal Pradesh state, was stopped from boarding a flight to Dublin.

In March 2022, Rana Ayyub, an investigative journalist and outspoken critic of Prime Minister Narendra Modi's government, was also stopped from boarding a flight to London at the Mumbai airport. She was due to speak at different events on media freedom and the targeting of journalists.

Google filed a patent application for an AI TV channel starring you

📅 Nov 24, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Ever since Google introduced its Veo 3 AI video model in March 2025, AI-generated video has started appearing in social feeds, ads, and even movies on free streaming services.

Now, Google has sketched out how AI could make the person watching TV the star of their own show. In a patent application published Nov. 19, the company describes a feature called "My Channel," which would use a viewer's watch history to generate videos for them. In one version, the system could use the viewer's likeness and voice to cast them as the lead of their very own TV show.

Patsnap, the database that hosts the filing, offers an AI-generated "technical title" that sums up the idea as a way of "enhancing user engagement."

The patent-tracking site Patentlyze first spotted the filing and shared it with 404 Media, which covered it on Nov. 23. Patent records show that Google filed the application on May 13, 2025, but the company has yet to announce a product.

One drawing in the application shows a TV menu called "John's Channel," with a crime comedy, a rom-com, a sci-fi adventure, and an action thriller. In a sample prompt, the filing tells the AI to make a video where "the user will play the protagonist." Another example takes that a step further. If a cricket fan has been watching clips about a particular player, the AI could put them in a scene with that player or show them "cheering for India in the stands." New videos could be made on request or on a schedule, such as once a week or once a month. To work out what to make, the system could look at the titles and genres a person watches and how long they watch them. It could also factor in the viewer's age group, the device's language setting, or its location. Those details would go into instructions for the AI about what kinds of stories to make. The application also describes an "advertising stitcher" that would insert ads into the finished video before it plays. Because even a show with an audience of one needs some sort of commercial break.

There are pieces of this idea in Google products already. Google TV Freeplay, for example, offers hundreds of free, ad-supported channels, and Google Photos can turn a picture someone selects into a short video.

Google's filing describes using photos or videos to create a digital likeness of the viewer. In one version, a TV assistant could send audio from voice commands to a server, where it could be stored "in association with the user." The system could then "synthesize the voice of the user for the dialogue of a protagonist."

The filing says viewers may have some say in what gets collected and "how that information is used." It leaves open whether someone could keep using voice commands to control the TV while opting out of having those recordings used to make a show, though.

For now, "My Channel" is still just an idea on paper. A patent examiner reportedly rejected Google's application, according to Patentlyze, for unknown reasons, and Google hasn't announced plans to launch it. The company can still seek to revise its claims or appeal. Mashable has reached out to Google for a comment.

BlackRock launches tokenized money market fund accepting stablecoins in Hong Kong

📅 Nov 24, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

The tokenized fund launched with approximately $100 million in assets, with Standard Chartered and Ant International participating as seed investors.

According to a Tuesday report by the South China Morning Post, the BlackRock HKD Digital Liquidity Fund launched with approximately $100 million in assets, with British banking group Standard Chartered and Ant International, a Singapore-based digital payments company spun out of China’s Ant Group, as seed investors.

The launch allows institutional investors to earn returns on their stablecoin holdings by investing directly in the fund, eliminating the need to convert their digital assets into traditional currency before investing.

The Hong Kong dollar, distinct from mainland China’s yuan, has been pegged to the US dollar since 1983.

Related: Hong Kong takes tokenized settlement to 24/7, plans stablecoin trials

BlackRock’s seed investors are also playing broader roles in the fund’s operations and adoption.

Subscriptions and redemptions can be carried out using HKDAP, a Hong Kong dollar-pegged stablecoin issued by Anchorpoint Financial, a joint venture between Standard Chartered, telecommunications company HKT and blockchain investment firm Animoca Brands. Standard Chartered also serves as the fund’s custodian, administrator, trustee and tokenization agent.

For Ant International, integrating the fund would support round-the-clock liquidity management across its global operations. According to the company’s disclosures, its payment services support more than 300 payment methods across 220 markets and process an average of over 20 million transactions daily.

BlackRock’s fund documentation shows that the fund began operations on Nov. 16, investing primarily in short-term securities issued or guaranteed by governments and high-grade bank deposits, with a net seven-day yield of 2.03% as of Tuesday. The fund may also accept tokenized deposits, while retail investors are limited to conventional Hong Kong dollars.

The fund’s launch follows HSBC’s Nov. 19 announcement that retail customers could invest in a tokenized money market fund using its Hong Kong dollar-backed stablecoin, RedCoin (RCHKD). HSBC and Anchorpoint Financial secured Hong Kong’s first two stablecoin issuer licenses in April, eight months after the city’s stablecoin regulatory framework took effect.

How to Deal With Holiday Arguments and Guilt Trips

📅 Nov 24, 2026  |  Category: Other  |  Read Source →

Posted November 24, 2026 | Reviewed by Michelle Quirk

Ideally, the holidays are a time of happiness, cheer, and positive relationship interactions. In practice, however, we often need to manage some bad with the good. There are usually a few “bones to pick” over the Thanksgiving turkey and “elephants in the room” along with the Christmas tree…

So, to maximize your holiday joy (and minimize your angst), I have compiled some strategies to deal with dysfunctional games, family patterns, arguments, and emotional manipulation. By the end, you should have a good idea why Aunt Ruth keeps giving you a guilt trip, how Uncle Stu keeps making you lose your cool, and what exactly you can do about all of it. Then, maybe, you can enjoy yourself, too!

To start, according to Eric Berne in his book Games People Play (1964), people generally try to manipulate us because they want something from us—but they don’t believe they can get it honestly or fairly. These negative beliefs (called existential positions) come in two forms. On one hand, they can believe that you’re not OK and try to bully you because they think you don’t deserve their fair treatment. On the other hand, they can believe that they’re not OK (i.e., I’m not okay) and try to guilt trip you into feeling that you owe them something for nothing.

Thus, people play games, argue, and manipulate when they believe they are not OK, other people are not OK, or both. This allows them to get what they want without giving back or risking being fair and trusting. Such manipulations can have secondary benefits for them, too, like being excused from standards or responsibilities (I'm not OK), or feeling morally superior (You're not OK). Unfortunately, those bad beliefs and manipulative games also cause many of our hurt feelings and dysfunctional relationship patterns in the process as well.

How these game-playing relatives and friends push your emotional buttons was explored by Niven, Totterdell, and Holman (2009) and defined as negative affect regulation strategies. Put simply, they use emotional manipulation tactics to make you feel bad, so you give them what they want. There are four general tactics they can employ to emotionally manipulate, but they all share one common theme—they reject you and hurt your feelings, while also putting their feelings first.

Beyond that, they might choose to emotionally bully you by being disrespectful and insulting or bragging and trying to make you feel bad by comparison. This would fall under the “You’re not OK” belief and allow them to feel superior at your expense. In contrast, they might decide to guilt-trip you by criticizing how you treat them or withdrawing until you feel bad and comply with their wishes. This would be part of an “I’m not OK” belief and allow them to be excused from standards and responsibilities—like doing something worthwhile for you back. In either case, unless you know how to defend yourself, you might be left trying to choke down negative emotions, while they munch on dessert!

Fortunately, there are things that you can do to defend yourself—and possibly even improve your relationship with that unruly relative, partner, or friend, too. To begin, though, you need to decide whether you want to deal with the manipulation indirectly by diverting attention and emotion away from the problem, or directly by engaging attention and emotion toward addressing the problem. In other words, can you make light of Cousin Bessie’s snotty comments and change the subject, or do you need to talk it out and come to a better arrangement?

To help you decide, consider some indirect and direct softening techniques suggested by Overall, Simpson, and Struthers (2013). They were initially devised for addressing problems with emotionally avoidant or argumentative romantic partners—and serve as a foundation for dealing with relationship problems in my book Attraction Psychology (Nicholson, 2022). Beyond that, I have found them helpful with family and friends as well, especially when they believe one of us is not “OK” and are emotionally hurtful about it.

Dealing with issues indirectly requires that you “soften” by temporarily downplaying the severity of the problem, until their bullying or guilt-tripping lessens. To do so, it helps to build positive rapport by communicating your care, acceptance, and regard—while also minimizing their emotional manipulation attempts with positive feelings and humor (i.e., kill them with kindness). From there, you can redirect them toward other positive aspects about your relationship with them and points of agreement. Essentially, this is a lot like dealing with a toddler having a tantrum—make light of the outburst and find a more pleasant topic to change their mind and improve their behavior.

Dealing with issues directly, however, requires that you “soften” by acknowledging some aspect of their point of view as a base of agreement—without surrendering to the negative emotions from their bullying or guilt trip. Pretend you’re a cool TV lawyer who says, “We can agree that there is a problem here, but it would help us both to keep working on a better solution.” From there, you can show optimism about solving the disagreement and having a good relationship with them. Where you are able, also acknowledge some of their positive efforts in the past toward connecting and compromising with you. This can help improve their emotional outlook and motivate them to find healthier ways to interact with you in the future as well.

Whatever strategy you choose, I hope you have Happy Holidays and a Good New Year, too!

Home-Price Growth Remains Outpaced as Inflation Continues to Rise

📅 Nov 24, 2026  |  Category: Business  |  Read Source →

As inflation remains elevated—with the latest Consumer Price Index (CPI) coming in hotter than economists’ expectations—“resilient” growth in home prices continues to be eroded.

The September S&P Cotality Case-Shiller Home Price Index was up 0.2% month-over-month, recording a 2.1% annual gain—the same as last month. This is compared to annual inflation readings of 3.4% in both the September CPI and Personal Consumption Expenditures price index.

“Nominal home prices remain resilient, but inflation is once again widening the gap between nominal and real housing returns,” said Rebecca Kaufman, associate director of Commodities at S&P Dow Jones Indices. She noted that this marks the “16th consecutive month in which U.S. home prices declined in real terms.”

Realtor.com® Senior Economist Anthony Smith also noted that this month’s data “reflects sales closing from July through September, a period when mortgage rates edged higher, with Freddie Mac’s 30-year fixed rate averaging 6.54% in July, near 6.7% in August, and 6.86% in September.”

The 10-City Composite was up 0.2% month-over-month to a 3.5% annual gain (down from 3.6% last month), while the 20-City Composite was up 0.1% month-over-month to a 2.6% annual gain (same as last month).

Kaufman also noted that this month’s report “revealed a widening gap between seasonal weakness and underlying price momentum,” as the monthly numbers pre-seasonal adjustment were declines across the board for the National, 10-City Composite and 20-City Composite Indices (-0.3%, -0.5% and -0.5%, respectively).

“That contrast suggests that some of the apparent monthly weakness reflects the normal fall slowdown rather than a broad deterioration in home prices,” she explained.

Smith agreed that this was reflective of “the normal fall slowdown,” as the “market’s momentum has weakened since September closed.”

Taking a look at the 20 cities that make up the index, Chicago once again led the pack (for the 6th consecutive month) with a 6.9% annual gain. Following behind were New York City at 5.8% and Miami at 4.6%.

Kaufman called out that Miami’s growth is “particularly notable,” as “after moving into fourth place among the 20 cities in July and August, it has now climbed into the top three, a sharp reversal from its comparatively weak performance in 2025.”

In terms of declines, Seattle was again the leader for the 4th consecutive month with an annual fall of 1.9%. Denver (-1.0%) and Dallas (-0.9%) followed behind.

For the picture ahead, Smith said that the “rate and demand backdrop has become more challenging since September closed,” as mortgage rates are now above 7% and inflation remains fairly elevated—with “expectations for a Fed rate hike in December.”

“If financing costs stay elevated into the winter, the plateau in price growth could give way to renewed softening, particularly in supply-heavy Southern and Western markets,” he continued.

To give stock, transfer the shares to the recipient’s brokerage account or a child’s custodial account. Your cost basis, what you paid, goes with them, so the recipient owes tax on your gain when they sell. Give appreciated shares to someone whose low income means 0% tax on long-term gains, and sell losing shares yourself to deduct the loss.

The gain you built is then taxed at the recipient’s rate, not yours, so the right recipient can owe nothing.

Say shares you bought years ago for $2,000 are worth $10,000 now, and you pay 15% on long-term gains, those on investments held over a year. Selling them yourself costs $1,200 in federal tax on the $8,000 gain.

Give them to your daughter in her first full year of work after college. The time you’ve held them counts as hers, so she gets long-term rates at once. If her taxable income, the gain included, stays low enough for the 0% long-term rate, she owes no federal tax on it. The kiddie tax limits this for anyone under 18, and for 18-year-olds and full-time students under 24 whose earnings cover no more than half their support. It taxes their investment income above a small yearly amount at a parent’s rate when that’s higher.

To make the gift, you’ll need the recipient’s brokerage name and account number for your broker’s online gift tool or form. For a child, open a custodial account, often called a UTMA or UGMA account, in their name. The shares become the child’s the day you give them, and the custodian manages them until the child comes of age.

Giving over $19,000 in a year to one person other than your spouse means filing a gift tax return, but you owe tax only once your lifetime gifts pass a multimillion-dollar exemption. Shares given to a charity or a donor-advised fund leave no one owing tax on the gain, since charities pay no income tax. Heirs who inherit shares in a taxable account get the shares’ value at death as their basis, so the gain before then goes untaxed.

Never give shares worth less than you paid. Neither of you can deduct the drop that came before the gift. Sell them, deduct the loss on your own return, and give the cash.

Before the transfer, open your broker’s cost basis page and send only the batches of shares that have gained.

Everything but the Plane: Here’s How One Brand Is Bringing Glamour Back to Travel

📅 Nov 24, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

PS’s private terminals are the epitome of real luxury.

Imagine a trip to the airport with no lines. No lines to check in, to drop your bag, to be told your first-class ticket is the wrong first-class ticket for the first-class lounge. Now, add to that no interpersonal friction. There are no snappy desk workers, no turbo TSA employees, and no graveyard shift customs agents that give you grief.

Airlines can only solve so many issues. These are terminal problems. PS, a Los Angeles-based company operating its own luxury private terminals, is solving them. The goal is to get you as close to the charter experience as possible, all while still flying commercial. If you ask around or sit in the common areas and eavesdrop long enough, you’ll likely hear something along the lines of “I usually fly private, but when I don’t, this is what I use.”

The company has locations in Atlanta, Dallas-Fort Worth, Miami, and Los Angeles, and the next order of business is expansion to the airports its clients use most—business hubs, mostly. But it started at LAX in 2017, and that’s the perfect airport for something like this. The horseshoe design and the traffic it creates, the small terminals, and the paparazzi culture do not cater to the A-list and executive frequent fliers that use it as a home airport. PS offers a way around that with on-tarmac connection from your flight to its terminal or vice versa. A chauffeur pulls you straight off the jet bridge and into a luxury fleet vehicle; that partnership is currently with BMW. If you don’t have any checked luggage, that 7 Series can (for an additional price) drive you straight to your destination within 35 miles of the airport. So if you are sitting in row one of first class, that’s about 50 steps between you and the i7 that takes you to your hotel. Or it can take you back to the PS terminal which is not a lounge, but acts as an über private lounge experience.

It ain’t cheap: $1,300 per head one-way for the Salon (a shared bar area), $4,950 for a private suite (essentially an airport hotel room with butler and spa service, this price is all-inclusive), and $1,650 for that PS Direct drive-you-straight-home service. There are discounts and perks if you opt for yearly membership, but this is all on top of your ticket, folks.

And it can lean comically luxurious with Champagne and caviar offered every time you enter—like before my 8 a.m. departure, to which I’m not opposed, but was caught a little off guard. There’s the jet-set marketing—tagline: The “back door” to the airport—and meant-for-the-timeline branded cocktail napkins: “You’ve arrived.”

But once you accept that PS could not afford to excuse itself from the flex-driven airport exclusivity economy, you can really admire its hospitality-inspired finishes. Its raison d’être is to get you through the airport with as little friction as possible, but it stands out in its commitment to hospitality within those confines.

For example, interior design and artwork are curated. The centerpiece artworks of each location are done in collaboration with local artists, made to fit the location they are in. The crown jewel thus far is in Miami, where PS took over the old Pan Am headquarters and flight training academy. The midcentury building is a Miami landmark, with geometric thermal walls acting as the façades and this pinkish sandy and limestone-heavy concrete throughout. Argentinian-born ceramicist Nina Surel matched this with tilework water features and a ceramic mural at the center of the property. Design like this, small and mostly unnoticed in the grand scheme of things, is what turns your mind back to a golden age of travel. It’s not credit card points and moneymaking schemes—though actually, you can buy any of the non-centerpiece artworks—just beauty for the sake of beauty.

On top of that, the food and beverage program is considered and regional. The blue corn cornbread with honey and a ricotta spread that chef Kelsey Kirwin (chefs’ names are on the menus, an indication of the atmosphere) is making at the DFW location overtook my mother’s as my all-time favorite. I would consider the signature PS martini—with (guess what) caviar-stuffed olives—worthy of a place on any hot big-city cocktail menu. And all the coffees, snacks, or water options are head and shoulders above both the general population terminals and the litany of airport lounges within them.

As far as service, yes, an agent handles your party’s check-in and keeps you abreast of delays so you don’t have to open an app—but that’s to be expected. It’s more in the interim. Servers don’t hover, they listen, and they’re backed by an extensive behind-the-scenes operation: Mention you prefer coffee, not bubbles, before a morning flight? A note is made on your profile for the next time you visit. Only ever ask for sparkling water when you fly out of LAX? They’ll know that in Atlanta. The entire operation seems to run on Guidara’s Unreasonable Hospitality, the same way all modern restaurants do. If you’re leaving Miami and mention to a business partner that you forgot to try a Cubano (on menu) and a cafecito (off menu), both will likely be delivered to your table, compliments of the house.

But every time I start to get wowed by the service details, I remember the price point and the level of clientele. That should be expected.

Standard terminals are airside, post-security, where everything (even a fancy and expensive airline lounge) is working within those confines. Since PS operates landside, with security the last stage of the terminal experience, it has the flexibility to be more hospitable to guests. For example, if you book a private suite, you can fly in, use it for a business meeting, and fly out. Or, your parents can accompany the family to departure, hang out in the suite, and squeeze every last second with the grandkids out of your visit. It is an airport experience actually designed around the ways we interact and what matters to us when we travel. That, more than the Champagne and caviar, is the definition of luxury.
