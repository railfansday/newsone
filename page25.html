I think your logic here is a bit faulty. You can't compare the unknowns of some future inheritance to the unknowns of your portfolio, because the risks are not equivalent.

You would not begin withdrawing 15% of your portfolio every year because the results might be catastrophic. But an inheritance is an addition to your own savings... it's not your baseline. So, yes, your inheritance might be 50% of what's expected, or it might be 10%, ir it might be 110%. But regardless, in either scenario you won't end up destitute, assuming you are careful with your own money. And, there's the black swan event: parent remarries, parent gets swindled, parent needs massive heathcare spending, and so on. If you overspend and get nothing, you're in trouble.

Is it reasonable to assume some (reduced) level of inheritance? Yes. By doing so, is it wise to begin drawing down your portfolio beyond what is prudent? I'd say no. If you want the money, there are ways to get it: The parent can gift you, your spouse, and each of your kids a lot of money, every year, forever. Probably more than you'd spend annually based on your "50% reduction" scenario.

The Wide-Leg Jeans That Every French Girl (And Kate Moss) Owns

📅 Nov 16, 2026  |  Category: Business  |  Read Source →

Last month, while taking in the spring/summer 2027 shows at Paris Fashion Week, I was eager to write about the street style trends bubbling up around the showgoers in town. Which proved harder than anticipated. For one thing, the flashiest dressers had been given full looks by the brands whose shows they were being paid to attend, and reporting on those felt like a somewhat dishonest exercise in journalism. For another, the consensus seems to be that we are living in a post-trend world, and fashion people in particular have little interest in dressing like anyone else. And so I turned my attention to real-life Parisians, among whom one item seemed to proliferate: jeans.

No, this isn’t going to be a trend report on something as perennially popular as denim, but on a particular high-waisted, flared style that I spotted so frequently, I was forced to dredge up what little French remains from my GCSEs to ask a woman where hers were from as I caught her climbing onto a Lime bike outside Le Saint-Gervais bar. “Excuse-moi, madame, votre pantalons… le brand?” She paused for a moment, swung her leg over the saddle, and unleashed a torrent of French from which I managed to decipher just two words: “Anna” and “Gérard Darel”, the latter needing no explanation.

Founded in Saint-Germain-des-Prés in the 1970s, the brand has long been a shorthand for what the rest of the world considers the tenets of French-girl style: discreet, easy-to-wear classics with a cultivated air. (So cultivated, in fact, that the brand acquired the original handwritten Proust Questionnaire in 2003, and recently collaborated with the philosopher Alain de Botton on a card-game version.) Which is all very Left Bank, of course, but perhaps the clearest evidence of its credentials is just how commonplace Gérard Darel is in everyday Parisian life. You can scarcely move for its iconic 24H bag—a soft, logo-free shoulder bag that Vogue once named among the bags of the century, alongside the Hermès Birkin – during the school rush on the Métro, where it is generally to be found stuffed with binders and tangled wired headphones.

But back to the jeans–an American invention, admittedly, but then again, this is the land of French fries. What made my startled Parisienne’s look so good? Much of it comes down to the cut–something Kate Moss, a woman who found fame in nothing more than a pair of jeans, understands, having been photographed in the brand’s Anna jeans as early as 2019. Designed by former creative director Valérie Gerbi and named after her daughter Anna – granddaughter of the brand’s founders, Danièle and Gérard Darel–the design braces at just the right point on the hips, fits snugly through the thighs and flares outwards just below the knee, skimming the top of a pair of shoes to create the illusion of longer legs. The double belt loops, meanwhile, criss-cross along the waistband to give the impression of a cinched waist, while the back pockets perform a similarly flattering service for the bum.

And yet the technicalities of a good fit can only explain so much. Perhaps the true appeal was in the way she wore them–with white ballet flats that I’m convinced were from Repetto’s men’s section (the Luciens) and a navy three-quarter-zip jumper with the sleeves pushed up–or the ease with which disappeared into the Marais, hair whipping in the wind.

U.S. Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent Just Issued a Blistering Warning to Nvidia

📅 Nov 16, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

During an interview with Fox News over the weekend, Bessent did not hold back about his concerns on the unfolding scandal at Nvidia.

The market seems to have mixed feelings about the departure of former Nvidia (NVDA -3.26%) CEO Jensen Huang.

An independent online petition requesting that Huang be reinstated now has over 180,000 signatures, including those from prominent business leaders like Elon Musk. President Donald Trump has also reportedly said he wouldn't mind if Huang returned as CEO.

During an interview with Fox News over the weekend, U.S. Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent expressed significant concerns about the situation and called for a "dramatic shakeup" at Nvidia. Make no mistake, Bessent just issued a blistering warning to the artificial intelligence (AI) chip giant.

Huang's resignation came after media outlets reported that the Nvidia co-founder had a relationship with a woman named Alice Huang (no relation), who previously was a controlling shareholder in a Singaporean data center company called MegaSpeed.

Other media outlets, such as The New York Times, reported that Huang also had alleged extramarital affairs with Nvidia employees. But the relationship with Alice raises national security concerns.

The Information reported that Alice created a network that illegally diverted $7.5 billion worth of Nvidia chips to China. The U.S. currently has export restrictions on the sale of certain Nvidia chips to China. Nvidia has claimed that it has had virtually zero Chinese chip sales since April 2025.

"I want to keep coming back to what was the culture at Nvidia, and were they pressing these seemingly illicit sales to goose their revenues and their earnings?" Bessent said during the Fox interview. "If this were a bank and they hadn't complied like this, the OCC (Office of the Comptroller of the Currency) would be in there right now, and a lot of the executives would be removed."

Bessent didn't hold back his views on the scandal, either, saying it was clear that some companies buying chips from Nvidia were shell companies working to advance AI interests in China.

Additionally, Bessent clearly does not want to see Huang return as CEO and believes Nvidia needs to get its ducks in a row quickly.

"This makes me think that as the board conducts the search for a new leader, that they need to go outside the company," he said. "I think we need answers in the next 10 days from the board and from the outside counsel."

Bessent has been a close confidant of Trump during his second term. The question is, how much weight does Bessent's warning carry within the administration?

Not only did Trump recently say he wouldn't mind seeing Huang return to Nvidia, but Bessent's questions also suggest divisions between Bessent and Howard Lutnick, another close confidant of Trump and the Secretary of the U.S. Commerce Department.

Bessent told CNBC that some federal agencies wanted to put MegaSpeed on an export blacklist. "But Commerce has the pen," Bessent said.

A White House spokesperson told Reuters last week that Trump has "complete confidence" in Lutnick. U.S. Attorney General Todd Blanche said on Nov. 12 that there is no confirmation yet that Alice was in fact a Chinese spy.

But questions remain about the extent to which Nvidia's chip sales, particularly in the Asia-Pacific region, have been diverted to China, if at all.

As Treasury Secretary, Bessent doesn't have direct authority to punish Nvidia, although historically he has been considered part of Trump's inner circle and has largely been in lockstep with the president. But with Trump seemingly supporting Huang, it's hard to know how much sway Bessent's words carry.

Still, it's clear that Bessent just issued a blistering warning to Nvidia, and I doubt he is alone among officials in the administration with concerns, so Bessent's warning shouldn't fall on deaf ears.

📅 Nov 16, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Anthropic's IPO will be a major event for the entire AI trade.

Anthropic will take center stage on Nov. 19 when it holds its initial public offering (IPO). It will be a critical day for the artificial intelligence (AI) trade, as how the stock is received could indicate whether the party is truly over or if it's full speed ahead.

While investors will be keeping an eye on a number of details connected to Anthropic, these are three of the most important details to monitor.

Companies like Nvidia (NVDA -3.26%) and Micron (MU -6.26%) have achieved excellent financial growth due to soaring hardware sales. The economics of selling semiconductors have been well established.

However, investors do not yet know as much about the economics of selling AI services. While companies like Alphabet (GOOG -2.88%) (GOOGL -2.96%) and Microsoft (MSFT -0.17%) have offered hints about their AI service revenues, these corporations were established long before AI became mainstream.

Anthropic will be the true test since its entire business revolves around AI services like Claude. The demand case for Claude is undeniable, but the big question is whether Anthropic can actually turn a profit with its services.

"We finally get to see what the other side of the equation looks like. What do the economics of selling AI look like? That's a great valuation marker. It's a great referendum," Direxion Head of Capital Markets Jake Behan said at Direxion's "Ahead of the Cycle" live briefing at the New York Stock Exchange.

The revenue growth is certainly there, and that has been the case for a while. Anthropic reported $17 billion in Q3 revenue. That was a 1,127% year-over-year jump, but a 47% sequential increase, which indicates that its growth rate is moderating.

The main concern is that Anthropic booked a $23.3 billion net loss in the third quarter. However, its operating loss of $567 million was more palatable. Investors should get more clarity about how Anthropic’s financial state is being viewed after the IPO, and later when the company reports its Q4 earnings.

The second key insight will be the level of appetite for the newly debuted stock. Anthropic’s IPO will essentially be a measure of whether investors are still willing to take risks as the AI trade continues its prolonged downturn.

That level of demand implies that investors are still eager to get more exposure to the AI trade, despite the current broadly negative sentiment. The amount of capital destined to flow into Anthropic may partially explain why AI stocks have been down in recent weeks. Anthropic is targeting a $1.4 trillion valuation, and all of that money has to come from somewhere.

Anthropic's IPO is generating plenty of buzz, and the oversubscription suggests that an early boom such as the one investors saw with SpaceX (SPCX -2.01%) is possible. The challenge will be whether Anthropic holds on to any early gains or deflates like a balloon.

Investors use metrics like the price-to-earnings ratio, price/earnings-to-growth (PEG) ratio, and price-to-sales ratio to value companies. While the last metric has its flaws, it’s the only one of those that can be applied here since Anthropic is still unprofitable.

While it's easy to weigh the values of chipmakers against each other with those metrics, Anthropic doesn't have publicly traded peers to measure its valuation against — and won’t until OpenAI's IPO, which most expect to happen next year.

Some investors can forgive a young company high net losses if its revenue growth is surging and it’s making progress toward consistent profitability. That generosity will be tested in the days and weeks after Anthropic's IPO.

This year’s previous blockbuster IPO, SpaceX, has been quite the roller coaster ride for shareholders. However, investors were quite happy with its Q3 earnings report, which came out on Oct. 20, and featured 161% year-over-year revenue growth and $4.4 billion in net income.

SpaceX owns the Grok large language model, so it may be a good gauge of how investors approach Anthropic. If Anthropic’s operating losses get more manageable or it transitions to delivering positive net income, that may be enough to offset any concerns that come up.

How Anthropic fares in the weeks following its IPO won't just matter for the company. It will be a vital indicator of the state of the entire AI trade.

'GTA 6' Hacker Pleads Guilty To Extorting Rockstar, Trolling In Prison

📅 Nov 16, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Arion Kurtaj’s hacking antics spoiled GTA 6 and reportedly cost companies millions

Arion Kurtaj, the hacker behind Grand Theft Auto 6's first major leak in 2022, just concluded a plea hearing in front of a London judge. The 21-year-old was accused of multiple counts of unauthorized operation of a computer, fraud, securing access to computer material with intent, and possession of a mobile phone while in prison. Kurtaj, who was a teenager when the crimes were committed, pleaded guilty on all counts.

Kurtaj's court plea may come to a surprise to some, as the hacker was initially deemed unfit to stand trial in 2023. After spending two years in a secure hospital, Kurtaj was reassessed in 2025 and soon moved to prison. The hacker called attention to himself while behind bars by uploading a number of photos on social media in which he mocked the authorities. The Crown Prosecution Service confirmed Kurtaj's possession of an illicit mobile phone in a press release on Monday, in which the prosecutor further detailed his case status.

The hacker published 50 minutes of GTA 6 gameplay split across 90 videos in 2022, all of which depicted a game that was not yet ready for public consumption. In addition to showing standard franchise gameplay mechanics, the leak was responsible for spoiling the series' first playable female character. At the time, Kurtaj threatened to release the game's source code if GTA 6 developer Rockstar did not contact him promptly. Kurtaj's access to this supposed source code is unknown, but more recent GTA 6 leaks have raised questions about hackers still potentially having access to an apparently playable build of the game.

Beyond targeting a major release, Kurtaj gained notoriety due to his hacking methods and overt circumstances. The breach was carried out using an Amazon Fire Stick connected to a hotel room television while the teenager was on bail for another offense. The hacker had reportedly used a SIM-swapping scheme to gain access to bank accounts and other sensitive information before targeting Rockstar Games. Kurtaj is associated with a larger hacking group, Lapsus$, and is accused of causing £10 million ($13.19 million) in damages.

His recent court appearance detailed more antics, like an extortion attempt against Uber in which he "posted a picture of a penis as part of his attempts to force the company to pay a ransom." The BBC's report also states that Kurtaj's custody has been rocky: He is accused of physically assaulting and biting staff caring for him.

"His offending was calculated and, even after being charged with serious offences, he continued to target organisations, demonstrating a persistent disregard for the harm he was causing and the law," wrote Robert Hutchinson, Specialist Prosecutor for the Crown Prosecution Service, in a press release.

Kurtaj will be sentenced on Nov. 23, four days after the official launch of Grand Theft Auto 6.
