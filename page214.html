The Reason Why Footballers Cut Holes in Their Socks

An increasing number of professional footballers have been playing with deliberately cut holes in their socks. It's become something of a growing trend in recent years, although not everyone is convinced it should be allowed.

Take legendary manager Jose Mourinho, for example. In February 2023, the Portuguese tactician criticised footballers who cut holes in their socks via an unexpected post on Instagram

The former Chelsea and Real Madrid boss, who is widely regarded as one of the greatest managers in football history, posted five black and white photos of unidentified players wearing ripped socks.He wrote:

"Beautiful socks for the Beautiful Game... Approved by the football authorities."

In November 2026, Mourinho then went viral again as he offered more recent thoughts on the matter. This time, he saw a young kid copying the trend and had the most typical response.

Jude Bellingham recalled the hilarious anecdote when speaking to Vogue:

“When [Mourinho] was coaching at AS Roma a few years ago, a kid at training had cut three holes in the back of his socks. Mourinho went over to him and said, ‘Why are you doing that?’ and he was like, ‘Bellingham does it. It’s meant to relieve pressure in your calf.’

"And Mourinho was like: ‘Kid, you’re nine. You don’t have calves.’”

More and more high-profile players appear to be following the new trend. Real Madrid and England midfielder Jude Bellingham has been seen playing with ripped socks for his country, including during the World Cup and Euro 2024, and while representing his former club Borussia Dortmund.

Bellingham's international teammate Bukayo Saka, has also been spotted playing with ripped socks at the aforementioned major tournaments.

Brazilian superstar Neymar has played with holes in his socks, as has former Manchester City star Kyle Walker. The number of holes cut in the socks varies from player to player. While some opt for one or two holes, others (like Walker) have played with multiple holes cut through the back of their socks.

One of the very first footballers spotted playing with ripped socks was former England full-back Danny Rose during the 2018 World Cup finals in Russia. Needless to say, the sight of Rose's socks sparked plenty of confusion and bemusement from football fans on social media at the time.

Football fans were baffled when they spotted Cristiano Ronaldo's black toenails, but there's a very good reason he paints them...

It's certainly an unusual look, isn't it? But tearing their socks apart prior to kick-off must have benefits - either real or psychological - for players, right? Otherwise, they surely wouldn't do it.

Players are generally cutting holes in their socks to reduce the pressure on the calf muscles, which are often restricted due to the garment's tightness.

Footballers with bulging leg muscles find socks particularly restrictive, resulting in effective blood flow and circulation becoming an issue, as well as breathability. Players are subsequently avoiding this problem by cutting holes in their socks.

Additionally, a Premier League club's senior member of medical staff confirmed to talkSPORT: “Many players feel the current socks are too tight and compressive on their legs, and some of them relate this to getting cramp in their calves."

That said, the medical expert suggested that the theory behind the slightly odd development in football kit is not backed up by 'hard evidence'. As a result, they claimed that it could merely be a fashion statement that has picked up traction over the years.

“There isn’t any hard evidence of this, though. It’s more to do with how they can feel uncomfortable. I think it then becomes more of a routine or fashion, as they’ve done it before and see other players do it. But the kit suppliers prefer they don’t, as it alters the look of the socks.

“Some kit suppliers have adapted their socks to try and change the level of compression around the calf area," the unnamed top flight staff member continued. "They’ll have designers looking at tweaks like this as they devise kits for the next season.”

Former Valencia player Ezequiel Garay was once ordered to change his socks after a La Liga referee decided his ripped pair did not befit the sporting dress code. But what do the laws of the game actually say when it comes to correct attire?

There's nothing in the Football Association's Laws of the Game stating that footballers must only wear socks without holes. Socks are listed among the basic compulsory equipment of every player, along with a jersey or shirt; shorts or truck suit trousers; shinguards, and footwear.

The referee has the power to ask a player to leave the playing area to correct his equipment if there are any infringements of this law.

Like Mourinho, TV host Richard Keys, who is best remembered for his long spell with Sky Sports in the UK, isn't a fan of footballers cutting holes in their socks.

In fact, during the Premier League clash between Chelsea and Manchester City, he called for a ban via X (formerly Twitter).

"Enough of this nonsense," Keys wrote. "There’s no need for it. The Spanish have outlawed it (although Bellingham gets away with it). It’s time we did." While the rules may eventually change, Premier League footballers are currently free to continue cutting their socks before games.

In an episode of Stick to Football, Gary Neville had his say on the matter and said he didn't believe the practice was necessary.

Neville ranted: "The one where they cut the actual holes in the back of their socks, which actually I think looks ugly to start with, doesn't make you look smart going onto a pitch. There was a thing at United - tuck your shirt in, make it look right. Tuck your shirt in and pull your socks up was actually a thing at United.

"I'm asking this question again. Why do players take the holes out of the back of their socks? Can I tell you why I'm a little bit sceptical about it... because in 2024, they have about 400 pairs of boots, they have everything made to measure. I'm not having that Nike or a sponsor of a kit don't make them a slightly bigger pair of socks. I don't get it."

Prince William and Kate Middleton’s Full Relationship Timeline

The last few years have seen a lot of enormous changes in the British Royal Family, an entity known more for tradition than rapid growth. After the loss of Queen Elizabeth II, who ruled over the U.K. for most of the 21st century, some people may be looking for some stability. If there’s any couple in the family who offers that, it’s Prince William and Kate Middleton.

William and Kate have had what appears on the outside to be a bit of a fairytale romance, from the storybook wedding to the picturesque family of five they have now. While they’ve faced hard times like everyone else, there’s something about them that has captivated people since the beginning of their relationship. Here’s a look back at their journey, from college sweethearts to royal power couple.

Prince William and Kate Middleton met while both studying at St. Andrews University in Scotland. Rumor has it that William was won over after seeing Kate modeling at a student fashion show for charity (a show for which he reportedly paid £200 for a front-row seat).

Other rumors suggest that her reason for being at St. Andrews was entirely related to meeting her future husband.

The Crown historian Robert Lacey wrote in his 2020 book Battle of Brothers: William and Harry—The Inside Story of a Family in Tumult, that Kate was trying to manifest her future relationship with William when she was only 19.

“Kate was very strategic,” Lacey said. “She gave up her place at one of Britain’s premier universities, Edinburgh, and opted for another one [St. Andrews] that probably didn’t have the same academic standard. She had already been to Edinburgh to get her flat accommodations sorted out but switched universities and switched her courses to history of art because that was William’s. She delayed her own education by a year and even takes the chance she won’t get into St. Andrews.”

She was not the only person making these choices. Applications to the school reportedly went up by 44 percent after it was announced that was William’s choice of university. They might not all have been marriage hopefuls, but some probably were like Kate.

Hello! magazine reported that Prince William was moving from his dorm into a flat with three college friends, one of them “a pretty 20-year-old brunette” who turned out to be Kate Middleton.

He allegedly started out as just her friend, before seeing her walk in a fashion show in this see-through dress on March 26, 2002.

“She’s there and about to walk down the runway on the fashion show, and she strips off an extra layer of clothing,” Lacey explained. “Here is a very strategic lady who knows what she wants and has got it.”

William and Kate were photographed together for the first time during a ski trip in Klosters, causing then-Prince Charles to threaten to sever ties with The Sun, the outlet that published the photos. Charles was said to have been “very disappointed” by the breach in an agreement between the press and Buckingham Palace that ensures royal children can continue their education without having their privacy intruded upon.

Both William and Kate graduated from St. Andrews. The Middletons later shared a photo of them together on the day.

The couple attended Camilla’s daughter Laura Parker-Bowles’ wedding, marking one of their first big public appearances together. It also sparked speculation that they were serious enough to one day walk down the aisle themselves.

In 2007, William and Kate took a time-out and broke up for a couple of months. Kate took a vacation to Ireland with her friends afterwards, while William awkwardly fielded questions about “how things are going” with Kate.

Kate later said in a 2010 interview after their engagement that in hindsight, she thinks it was healthy for them to have time apart.

“I think I at the time wasn't very happy about it, but actually it made me a stronger person...You find out things about yourself that maybe you hadn’t realized,” she said. “Or I think you can get quite consumed by a relationship when you’re younger. I really valued that time for me as well although I didn't think it at the time, looking back on it."

By July of that same year, rumors began to swirl about a possible reconciliation as Kate was seen attending a Princess Diana tribute concert—though she sat two rows behind William with her sister, Pippa.

The two were seen together again, back where it all began: the ski slopes. Photos show Kate and William together in Klosters as sources told newspapers that they were very much back together.

William graduated from his RAF training, with Kate at his side.

William proposed to Kate while on a trip to Kenya, with Princess Diana’s sapphire engagement ring. “We had a little private time away together with some friends, and I just decided it was the right time really,” William later said in their engagement interview. “We’d been talking about marriage or a while, so it wasn’t a massively big surprise, but I took her up somewhere nice in Kenya.”

William and Kate got married in a fairytale ceremony at Westminster Abbey witnessed by nearly 72 million people worldwide. The bride did her own makeup, the groom wore sweat pads so that he wouldn’t sweat out of his uniform, and Ellie Goulding performed at the reception.

In marrying William, Kate gained the title the Duchess of Cambridge, the Countess of Strathearn, and Lady Carrickfergus.

After the wedding, the married couple settled in Wales and adopted a dog, an English cocker spaniel named Lupo.

St. James’ Palace announced that Kate was pregnant with the couple’s first child. During her pregnancy, she was hospitalized for hyperemesis gravidarum, or severe morning sickness.

Kate gave birth to Prince George on July 22 and stepped out for post-baby photos.

Kensington Palace announced that Kate was pregnant with her and William’s second child. Kate had not reached the 12-week stage, but the Palace made the announcement because they felt they couldn’t keep the pregnancy under wraps from the press for much longer. Once again, Kate suffered from extreme morning sickness.

Kate gave birth to Princess Charlotte on May 2, who was then fourth in line for the throne after Charles, William, and her older brother, Prince George. She was also the first to take up the title of princess in 25 years. She is the highest-ranking female in line for the throne.

The couple made their red carpet debut at the British Academy Film Awards on February 12, where Prince William presented a BAFTA Fellowship award to Mel Brooks. Kate wore an off-the-shoulder tiered Alexander McQueen gown in a floral print.

Kensington Palace announced that Kate was pregnant with the couple’s third child. As with her previous two pregnancies, she suffered from hyperemesis gravidarum.

Kate gave birth to Prince Louis on April 23, who was then fifth in line for the throne. Though tradition dictated that Louis, as a male, would overtake Princess Charlotte for a place on the throne, the policy was amended prior to George’s birth with the Succession to the Crown Act 2013. That made Charlotte the first female royal to trump a younger male sibling.

Kate and William were front and center at the marriage of his little brother, Prince Harry, to Meghan Markle on May 19.

The couple helped Harry and Meghan celebrate on their wedding day at their nuptials at St. George’s Chapel in Windsor, England. “William was in a jokey, wonderful mood at the party,” a wedding guest told People at the time, adding that Kate was also in “great form.”

They added, “She and William were saying how thrilled they were to have their children there taking part.”

At Princess Eugenie’s October 12 wedding, Kate and William are seen showing each other a little affection. Eugenie was marrying her husband, Jack Brooksbank, when the royal couple were spotted holding hands in the pew.

Kate and William tend to be hands off when out in public.

After lockdown began in the U.K., the couple spoke with the BBC in a rare joint interview, discussing what it's like to parent through a pandemic.

“It's been ups and downs, like a lot of families self-isolating,” Kate said. “The children have got such stamina, I don't know how. Honestly, you get to the end of the day and you write down the list of all the things that you've done in that day. So, you pitch a tent, take the tent down again, cook, bake. You get to the end of the day—they have had a lovely time—but it is amazing how much you can cram into one day, that’s for sure.”

Nine years after welcoming their cocker spaniel, Ludo, into their lives, the couple announced they had bid him farewell. They shared a portrait of the dog on Instagram with a heartfelt goodbye.

“Very sadly last weekend our dear dog, Lupo, passed away,” the post read. “He has been at the heart of our family for the past nine years, and we will miss him so much. - W & C.”

April 29, 2021 marked the 10-year anniversary of the couple’s marriage, otherwise known as the tin anniversary. To celebrate, they shared some new portraits taken by Chris Floyd at Kensington Palace.

They also posted a video of themselves with their children walking through the sand dunes in Norfolk, England. They thanked everyone for the well wishes.

On September 28, the couple got dressed up for the No Time to Die premiere in London, posing with William’s father and stepmother. The royal family members were guests of honor at the event.

Kate wore a Jenny Packham capelet gown covered in gold sequins.

On October 20, Kate and William enjoyed their first Earthshot Awards at Alexandra Palace in London. The environmental initiative was launched by William, and he was tasked with giving a speech at the ceremony. A photo shows them sharing a sweet moment together behind the scenes.

Also at the event was Emma Thompson, Emma Watson, David Oyelowo, and Mo Salah. Ed Sheeran, Shawn Mendes, KSI, and Coldplay performed.

Enjoying another fun premiere, the couple hit the May 19 red carpet for Top Gun in the U.K.

Kate wore a black Roland Mouret gown, with an off-the-shoulders, white wraparound detail.

The couple’s first official portrait was unveiled at the University of Cambridge’s Fitzwilliam Museum on June 23. Originally commissioned in 2021 by the Cambridgeshire Royal Portrait Fund, it was painted by Jamie Coreth. Coreth is an award-winning portrait artist.

The Duke and Duchess take their three children to visit the Lambrook school, where they will be newly attending. The September 7 preview day offered the family a chance to get to know the facilities and teachers.

Prince George, Princess Charlotte, and Prince Louis were all wearing their school uniforms.

Queen Elizabeth II died on September 8. The then-Duke and Duchess of Cambridge became the Duke and Duchess of Cornwall and of Rothesay. On September 9, William’s father conferred the title of Prince and Princess of Wales.

In his address to the nation following the news of his mother’s death, King Charles III said, “With Catherine beside him, our new Prince and Princess of Wales will, I know, continue to inspire and lead our national conversations, helping to bring the marginal to the centre ground where vital help can be given.”

The Princess of Wales was a title once held by William’s mother, Princess Diana.

Along with Prince Harry and Meghan Markle, William and Kate visited Windsor Castle to view flowers and gifts left by citizens to honor the late Queen.

The couple attended Queen Elizabeth’s funeral at Westminster Hall after escorting her coffin from Buckingham Palace.

They posted their farewells to her on Twitter with a photo of the procession.

Kate and William attended the Festival of Remembrance on November 12, a ceremony dedicated to the day the Allies and Germany signed the armistice that ended World War I in 1918 for Europe.

Later that evening at a Royal Albert Hall concert, Kate paid tribute to the late Queen by wearing some of her famous jewelry: her Japanese pearl choker necklace along with her Bahrain diamond and pearl drop earrings.

I can only go by what I have heard from people that are much closer to the situation that I’d imagined a source for InTouch magazine is. I’ve not heard anything about a marriage being in trouble.”

I think it’s very lazy tabloid journalism at times because you can’t make money off people just being happy all the time. You need them to be in crisis for their lives to be more interesting, and I think sometimes two people can just be in a very safe and perhaps slightly dull marriage and that’s okay.”

In the endless iterating that goes into devising a menu for a holiday dinner, there’s one finishing touch that really makes or breaks the vibe: the drinks. The wine and beer will, of course, flow — at these festive events, a bottle of Champagne or sparkling rose is likely to make an appearance — and it’s easy enough to throw together a gin and tonic or screwdriver. But nothing makes a holiday gathering feel chic, totally put together, gloriously intentional, like a batch cocktail, made-to-order martini, or gussied-up punch.

Now, we all have our preferences when it comes to strength, spirit, and format. The host’s bandwidth is also a consideration; do you want to make a lot of one thing, or tailor your options to your guests’ preferences? Those questions are for you to answer, but our staff is here to help by providing this rundown of six tried, vetted, and loved drinks to make at your own celebration.

Angostura is a great option, delivering some classic holiday spice, but any style of cocktail bitters would probably work, as would an amaro. From set-it-and-forget-it backyard centerpieces to grab-and-go options for weekend camping trips, we tried well-regarded options from Solo Stove, Breeo, Blue Sky, and more.

Engineered to contain flames and burn fuel more completely, smokeless fire pits deliver ambient warmth with a lot less smoke in your eyes than a traditional campfire. They achieve this through two points of combustion: The first happens at the bottom, where airflow feeds burning wood; the second occurs at the top, where the fire’s smoke and gas output are burned off. And while no smokeless fire pit is 100 percent smokeless, if you use best practices (like burning dry wood) they come pretty darn close.

We tested smokeless fire pits in our New York and Pennsylvania backyards, building fires rather than judging them on specs alone. Along with smoke output, we considered size, which affects wood capacity and where the heat actually lands, as well as how easy each pit was to empty of ash and store once done, among other factors. We also used them repeatedly over time to see how their finishes held up, because a brand-new pit can look very different after several fires.

Solo Stove is the brand most people associate with smokeless fire pits (though Breeo was the first game in town). The latest joiner in Solo Stove’s lineup is the Summit, an upgraded take on the ever-popular Bonfire. Its 23-pound weight makes it just as easy to lift and store off-season, and there’s even less setup required. All-in-all, it’s the best smokeless fire pit for most people who want to enjoy a backyard fire pit that can be stored off-season.

Because the Summit is four to five inches shorter than the Bonfire, it does a better job of keeping your feet warm, one of the major gripes our Test Editor Brad Ford had of the earlier model. He says that while it holds less firewood, it undoubtedly produces a higher flame that extends well above the rim.

Another improvement he points out is the Summit’s integrated stand, which he says is sturdier and allows for better airflow than that of the Bonfire’s detached stand. There’s also a deeper ash pan, which is particularly helpful because the Summit burns through wood fast and needs frequent reloading. In terms of price, the 19.5-inch Summit is $100 more than a Bonfire of the same size, an extra cost that’s well worth the investment.

This eliminates the frustration I’ve had with some stainless-steel fire pits that discolor after a couple uses.”

Before lighting a single fire, we evaluated the things that can make or break a smokeless fire pit before you ever use them. We assessed the packaging and how well each pit was protected in transit, weighed every model, and evaluated assembly. We read the instructions, noting whether they were clear, complete, and actually useful.

We considered whether it could be disassembled for storage, and what came in the box versus what required an additional purchase. While We had plenty of outdoor space to work with, safety always came first: we set every pit on heavy-duty pavers and kept a fire extinguisher close at hand.

Then we lit them up. We used comparable loads of seasoned firewood to assess how easily each pit started, how much smoke it produced, how evenly it burned, and how much heat it put out. We paid attention to flame height and consistency, too, and whether the fire actually warmed people sitting around it—not just looked good from a distance. We measured logs to see exactly what each pit could accommodate and tested wood pellets where models supported them.

Finally, we evaluated the less glamorous but equally important details: how easy each pit was to tend, how much ash accumulated, how easily that ash could be removed, and what it took to clean and put each model away. And because a fire pit can be beautiful at 7 p.m. and a pain in the ass at 10 p.m., we tested for both.

Whether you want a smokeless fire pit to heat your outdoor space or be more of an ambient seasonal moment is an important consideration. For long nights by the fire with family and friends, you’ll likely want to invest in a larger model that measures 24 inches in diameter. In testing, models that size and up exuded warmth for several feet, and could easily warm four to six people.

They could also handle 14- to 16-inch-long logs. The trade-off: Models this size are a lot heavier and harder to move. If you’re looking for more of a warming accent, narrower fire pits work wonders. You can also maximize some models’ output by adding a heat deflector that sits above the flames and pushes heat out laterally, instead of letting it escape upward.

Ideally, the smokeless fire pit should work right out of the box, with limited-to-no setup, besides setting the pit on a base. It should also break down easily. If you’ll be storing it between fires or seasons, a model that’s easy to disassemble, carry, and put away is key.

The shallower and wider the smokeless fire pit, the easier it is to make a fire from scratch. You simply have more base room to work with to set up your paper, kindling, and wood, and less depth to maneuver to light your fire once ready. No matter your choice, you’ll definitely want a lighter with a long metal wand and a safety catch. None of the models we tested included one.

Burning pellets instead of wood was the closest we got to a truly smokeless fire during our testing. Made of compressed wood waste like sawdust, chips, and shavings, pellets are easy to light, always bone dry, and produce a hotter, more consistent flame than logs. There are trade-offs, though: They lack the classic crackle and pop of a wood-burning fire and that wonderful smell.

Bags of the stuff also typically cost more than a handful of logs at the hardware store, yet burn about the same length of time. But, if you’re allergic to smoke, don’t want your sweaters to smell of it, have wet wood drying out, or want a reliable and even fire every single time, pellets are an excellent way to go. Some fire pits work with them right out of the box; others need an adapter.

Every smokeless pit burns wood. Many smokeless fire pits can also use wood pellets right out of the box. Some, like Solo Stove, need an adapter, so the tiny pellets don’t drop right through the pit’s air holes into the ashcan below. Some portable options may only be wide or deep enough to handle twigs (pellets may be a better option for those).

Pro tip: Wet or green wood will always be slow to burn and emit a lot of smoke; use a moisture meter to suss out whether your logs are dry enough to light up before putting a match to them.

If you plan to use your fire pit regularly, easy ash removal should be a deciding factor. The last thing you want to have to do is pick up and tip a heavy steel drum over a trashcan in order to empty it of ash. Instead, look for a removable ash pan or tray that allows you to lift the ash out cleanly once the pit has fully cooled (usually 12 to 24 hours after a fire has ended). Otherwise, you’ll need a shovel to scoop out the ash.

If you don’t have space to leave your fire pit outside all year, think about who will move it and how often. Even a small pit designed for camping trips can weigh as much as 20 pounds. Fortunately, these options usually come with a carrying bag, so you can sling them over your shoulder. Meanwhile, larger models can top 45 pounds (the 24-inch Breeo weighs just over 60 pounds), which matters when you have to move it under cover or into the garage at the end of the season. Units that come apart make this easier; having a dolly or hand truck on hand helps, too.

Material affects how long your new pit will look the way it does on arrival. While stainless steel looks shiny and modern from the start, heat from the flames will discolor it, and potentially quite quickly, as some testing showed. (You can restore its original shine with a stainless-steel cleaner such as Bar Keepers Friend, but that’s extra work.) Corten-steel usually costs more but forms a protective rust-colored patina that looks attractively weathered over time. Painted or powder-coated steel usually costs less, but can scratch and chip with repeat use.

Whatever finish you choose, keeping your smokeless fire pit empty of ash and covered when it’s not in use will help extend its life. Rainwater sitting in a fire pit full of ash can create a corrosive, alkaline slurry that accelerates rust and can damage the metal. Some models come with a cover as part of their bundle; others charge extra for it. Our reviews note whether a cover is included in every pick.

Smokeless fire pits can be a lot more than a place to warm your hands on chilly nights. In some cases, you can turn them into an outdoor kitchen. Brands like Breeo, Solo Stove, and Cuisinart have grill grates, griddles, pizza ovens, or roasting sticks that let you cook over an open fire. Other add-ons also make a difference, like a cover, if you plan to leave it outside when not in use; a spark screen, particularly if you have toddlers and pets around (though, warning: they can also get hot to the touch); and fire pokers or ashtray lifters, so you don’t get your hands dirty. Definitely check the accessory lineup before you buy your pit; they’re often model-specific. A pair of fire-resistant gloves is always a good investment.

While product photos often depict smokeless fire pits sitting on wood decks or backyards, it’s worth noting that their bases can get hot enough to scorch or discolor lawns, wood decks, composite, and asphalt. Ideally, yours should sit on brick, concrete, stone, or sand. But in the absence of that, a dedicated base (sometimes included, other times sold separately) helps raise the unit off the ground. For an even safer setup, consider adding a fireproof mat underneath. You should also keep a bucket of water or a fire extinguisher nearby and check for local burn bans before lighting. All 11 Titan Types In Attack On Titan Explained

In Attack on Titan, the Titans are much more than oversized monsters lurking beyond humanity's walls. What initially appears to be a terrifying fight for survival gradually develops into a complicated history involving ancient powers, political conflicts, and the origins of the creatures themselves. As Eren Yeager and his fellow soldiers uncover more about their enemies, the true variety of Titan abilities becomes increasingly difficult to ignore.

From seemingly mindless Pure Titans to the extraordinary powers possessed by the Nine Titan Shifters, each form serves a different purpose and presents its own threats. Some rely on overwhelming strength and numbers, while others have specialized abilities capable of changing the outcome of an entire war. Understanding these distinctions is essential to appreciating the series' lore and worldbuilding, so here is a breakdown of all 11 major Titan types featured in Attack on Titan.

Pure Titans resemble humans but with varying levels of enlarged forms. The majority of the observed Titans, both in the manga and the anime, belong to this category, their overarching feature being their inability to form and process thoughts.

Despite being made similarly to other Titan types, Pure Titans are mindless and tend to attack without discrimination. They are dangerous due to their sheer numbers, formidable strength, and enormous size. Pure Titans have been used as weapons of war, first by Eldia and then by Marley, and can only be killed by slashing the nape of their neck.

A notable example of a Pure Titan would be the humanoid dubbed Bean, who first appeared when the gates of Trost District were broken by Attack on Titan's epic Colossal Titan.

Bean was one of the two Titans kept alive for the sake of experimentation in an attempt to better understand the nature of these beasts and their innate weaknesses.

The Abnormal Titans are one of the most bizarre Titan types. Abnormals are Pure Titans that display unusual behavior and intelligence, allowing them to perform unpredictable moves. They often tend to ignore nearby humans and move on to strategic locations to inflict more damage.

Abnormals are adept at performing tremendous leaps, running, speed-walking, and in a few cases, communicating in human tongues. An Abnormal Titan’s ability to form thoughts and speech has been chronicled in the diary of Ilse Langnar, who had run into a hesitant Titan amid an expedition massacre.

After Hange Zoë and Levi’s squad find the diary, it is revealed that the Titan addressed Ilse as “Lady Ymir”, mistaking her for Attack on Titan's Founder, Ymir Fritz.

Confused by the possibility that a Titan can experience emotions, Ilse became increasingly frustrated during her attempts at communication, ending with the Titan crushing her skull whilst shedding tears. The existence of the Abnormal Titans forces humans to approach the beasts with a more nuanced perspective. This revelation proved to the remaining soldiers that not all Titans are mindless killers.

As the name suggests, the Attack Titan is an offensive powerhouse, standing at 15 meters tall and characterized by a highly muscular build and flesh-less jaws.

Eren Yaeger would later improve the Attack Titan’s metrics by drinking the Armor serum that allowed him to harden the Titan’s skin during pivotal moments.

As his default Titan Form, Eren was able to receive memories from those who possessed the Attack Titan and those who had yet to inherit it. This would later come to help him manipulate his father, Grisha, to cause his own Titan creation subsequently.

Although these memories allow the user to gain insights, they are often incomplete and short snippets.

The Armored Titan has thick, hardened plates all over its body, acting as a natural defense against external attacks, including cannon hits, steel blades, and vertical maneuvering equipment, making it the most defensively strong of the Titan types.

In addition to this, these Attack on Titan Titans are able to harden their arms and feet, creating sharp claws in the process. The Armored Titan's only weakness seems to be the areas on the back of its exposed joints.

The armor is not capable of withstanding high-power weaponry, such as the Mid-East Allied Forces’ artillery.

Users of the largest Titan type, the Colossal Titan, can control the energy produced during their transformation to create various effects, such as when Bertolt attacks the Trost District, and his transformation triggers a powerful wind blast.

The Colossal Titan can also create a mushroom cloud of dust and rubble, leading to the death of many Survey Corps members during the Battle of Shiganshina District. As indicated by its name, the Colossal Titan is known for its enormous size, able to peer over the edge of Wall Mari and powerful enough to take down the outer gate with a single kick.

Due to his royal blood, Zeke’s Beast Titan holds the ability to turn Subjects of Ymir into Pure Titans.

Possessing greater endurance levels than Pure Titans and other members of the Nine Titans, the Cart Titan can partake in longer missions without having to pause for rest.

This Titan’s absorption resistance allows it to maintain its beastly form and wear armaments such as battle armor, mobile machine gun platforms, and cargo packs. As opposed to most bipedal Titans, the Cart Titan assumes a quadrupedal form, which potentially hinders the user from walking upright in human form.

To find the best dual-fuel generator for your home, campground, or worksite, consider the factors listed below, including fuel efficiency, fuel tank capacity, power output, and control panel configuration.

Dual-fuel generators have an isolated gasoline tank with a fuel capacity that ranges from as small as 2 gallons to more than 10 gallons. The larger the fuel tank on the generator, the longer the generator can provide gasoline-based power. But, as tank size increases, so does the size of the generator.

Fuel capacity does not affect how much propane you can use with your dual-fuel generator. Propane gas is stored under pressure in canisters, which attach to the generator with a hose and pressure regulator for direct fuel consumption.

The power output on dual-fuel generators is measured in watts. Manufacturers’ specs include peak power output and running power output. Peak power output refers to the maximum number of watts the generator can provide at any one time. Running power output equals the average number of watts produced during use.

Dual-fuel generators generally produce a power output from 1,000 watts to over 12,000 watts, depending on their size. Gasoline produces a higher wattage than propane, so many manufacturers of dual-fuel technology break wattage into propane peak and running outputs and gasoline peak and running outputs.

For greater power output needs, users may need to consider a standby generator rather than a portable dual-fuel option.

You can start your dual-fuel generator with an electric start switch or a recoil cord.

Many dual-fuel generators produce 50 to 90 dB of noise, depending on fuel type (gasoline runs louder than propane) and power output. The most common range is between 65 dB and 75 dB, comparable to the sound of normal conversation.

The quietest dual-fuel generators combine dual-fuel technology and inverter technology to create a dual-fuel inverter generator.

Dual-fuel generators are typically large and heavy, with hoses or tanks that make them difficult to carry and move. Thus, some manufacturers include wheel kits for tilting the generator back and rolling it to the desired location.

You may also be able to purchase a wheel kit separately to add to your existing generator or replace an older wheel kit that doesn’t work well anymore. Either way, a wheel kit makes transporting a portable dual-fuel generator much easier.

Lift hook bars are handy on jobsites, allowing workers to attach the generator to a hook-and-pulley system and raise or lower it instead of carrying it up or down stairs or ladders. In industrial settings, this feature helps raise the generator onto a walkway or platform.

A lift hook bar will increase the generator’s weight (and likely the price), so don’t look for a generator with this feature unless you truly plan to use it.

Most dual-fuel generators are between 22 inches long by 22 inches wide by 22 inches high and 35 inches long by 30 inches wide by 30 inches high. They typically weigh between 100 and 250 pounds.

Gasoline and propane each have their pros and cons as fuel options. Gasoline is easier to find, but propane costs less. Propane is a better fuel source in hot summer weather, while gas burns better in the cold. A dual-fuel generator lets you choose one or the other based on immediate circumstances.

Using both fuel sources, one after the other, greatly increases a generator’s runtime. Users can run to the gas station to refuel while the generator keeps providing power on propane.

A dual-fuel generator operates like a single-fuel generator, except it can burn propane and gasoline to produce electrical energy. It converts mechanical energy produced by the burning fuels to force the movement of electrical charges and generate electricity.

While some single-fuel generators can be adapted for use with natural gas, this isn’t the case with dual-fuel generators. Unless specially designed, dual-fuel generators do not run on natural gas and cannot be adapted for this purpose.

Propane gas is less expensive than gasoline, nearly impossible to spill, and burns cleaner, resulting in less wear on the engine over time. Running a dual-fuel generator on propane will save money but slightly decrease the machine’s peak and running watts.

Runtime is determined by fuel efficiency and fuel tank capacity. On average, a standard dual-fuel generator runs about 8 to 10 hours on propane.
