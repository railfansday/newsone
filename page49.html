That said, these gifts don't come free, so if you're hoping to take advantage of everything the Great Fairies have to offer, then you'll need to start saving up your Rupees for offerings now.

You don't need to find the Great Fairies in any particular order, and you don't have to accept every gift. The most natural order follows the locations you'll visit throughout the story, in order, but you can visit and accept the gifts in any order once you have access to the areas.

Found at the top of Death Mountain, after completing the Dodongo Cavern dungeon and helping Goron City.

Din's Fire: Create a fiery dome around you when you cast. Uses 2 Magic Power.

After passing through the gate on the path to Zelda, you'll see a short path off to the right with a breakable rock wall. Use a bomb to clear the way to this Great Fairy.

Improved Spin: Strike nearby foes with a mighty wave of magic power. You will be vulnerable while charging it up.

After you give King Zora the letter, you'll gain access to the Zora Fountain. In this area, head to the island at the southern end of the map to find the Great Fairy's entrance.

Farore's Wind: Create a waypoint you can warp to when in a dungeon. Uses 2 Magic Power.

After beating the Fire Temple, you can find this entrance in the Death Mountain Crater, south on the map of the path that leads back to Goron City.

Doubled Magic: Permanently doubles your pool of magic power.

Once you reach the Desert Colossus, look for the breakable wall on the northern part of the map.

Nayru's Love: Grants invulnerability when cast. Uses 4 Magic Power.

After you obtain the Golden Gauntlets inside Ganondorf's Castle, head back out through the entrance and look for the side path with the Great Fairy's entrance that you should now be able to clear.

Andri Gudjohnsen is one of the most clinical players in Europe, Blackburn Rovers must brace for interest: Opinion

Blackburn Rovers bounced back to winning ways on Saturday as they defeated West Ham 2-1 at Ewood Park. The result sees Tony Mowbray and co enter the international break on a high, having lost back-to-back games against Swansea City and Stoke City beforehand.

And, somewhat unsurprisingly, on the scoresheet for Rovers once again was Icelandic talisman Andri Gudjohnsen. The 24-year-old found space in the penalty area with smart movement and met Jayden Fevrier’s cross with a diving header, powering beyond Mads Hermansen in the Hammers’ goal.

The goal moved Gudjohnsen to 10 goals in 14 league matches, and 11 in 15 across all competitions. It marked a continuation of his stunning form, which has seen him become one of Europe’s most clinical scorers.

Gudjohnsen’s goal record is eye-catching, but the extent of his clinical finishing is highlighted by his goals per shot. The Blackburn Rovers man has taken only 20 shots in the Championship, with 12 on target and 10 of them ending in the back of the net (FotMob).

This equates to a goals per shot rate of 0.50, and this ranks him among the best in Europe. According to FBref, the only player better than Gudjohnsen in this metric is Stade Rennais man Boulaye Dia, who has taken nine shots and scored five Ligue 1 goals, equating to 0.56 goals per shot.

Brighton & Hove Albion midfielder Jack Hinshelwood is tied with Gudjohnsen on 0.50, but it is only him and Dia that better or equal that of the Rovers man in Europe’s top five leagues this season.

For comparison, this is better than goalscoring superstars like Erling Haaland (0.24 goals per shot), Kylian Mbappe (0.20), Lamine Yamal (0.16) and Alexander Isak (0.28).

Blackburn Rovers are in a good position with regard to Gudjohnsen’s contract. He only signed in the summer of 2025, and his deal runs until 2028 with an option to extend.

However, with such clinical finishing and goalscoring form, the Iceland international is bound to draw interest. Then, the Rovers hierarchy will have to decide whether they want to cash in big while he’s in form, or hold out for a bigger fee further down the line while Gudjohnsen plays a key role in Tony Mowbray’s side.

If Gudjohnsen continues at this level, his trajectory will soon be far steeper than that of his club. That will make exit opportunities both tempting and more feasible, with teams willing to pay more to try and pry him away from Ewood Park.

The numbers are difficult to ignore. A goalkeeper who isn’t making enough saves and is shipping goals at an alarming rate, 23 conceded in 15 games, is becoming a problem that cannot be overlooked.

One possible solution could be Alphonse Areola, who previously worked under Scott Parker at Fulham. There is also a potential opportunity for highly-rated youngster Finlay Herrick, who may be worth a closer look.

While Areola’s statistics last season hardly inspire confidence, conceding 37 goals in 20 appearances without recording a clean sheet, there is at least some context. By comparison, Hermansen managed seven clean sheets while conceding 28 goals in 18 matches.

The pair offer very different strengths and weaknesses! Areola is arguably the better shot-stopper but struggles when asked to play out from the back. Hermansen, meanwhile, has been preferred for his distribution and comfort in possession, yet questions remain over his save percentage and ability to keep the ball out of the net when it matters most.

Ex-Goalie Nuno failed to get the best out of Hermansen

Another concern is the absence of an established senior goalkeeping coach. That said, it’s worth remembering that the club’s former manager was a goalkeeper himself, while Rui Barbosa brought years of coaching and playing experience. Neither appeared able to significantly influence Hermansen’s development.

Currently, Billy Lepine, a former Dagenham & Redbridge youth player who has worked within the academy setup since 2008, occupies the role. Lukasz Fabianski is also assisting on a part-time basis with the academy.

And perhaps that’s where the story takes an even stranger twist.

Cast your mind back to 4th December 2024, when West Ham travelled to the King Power Stadium. Leicester won 3-1, ending a six-game winless run. Standing between the posts for Leicester that evening?

The previous year in the Championship he featured in the EFL team of the season. One of the most proactive ball playing keepers in the league, and completed 13 clean sheets.

If ever there was evidence of the “West Ham goalkeeper curse”, that might just be it.

The solution feels fairly straightforward. Bring Fabianski into the senior goalkeeping coaching setup, get Areola focused solely on football rather than the DJ decks, and bring in an experienced goalkeeper on loan in January to provide competition.

The ball is now firmly in Scott Parker’s court.

Crystal Palace should not have been awarded a late penalty against Liverpool.

That is the opinion of former head of PGMOL and FIFA referee Keith Hackett, speaking exclusively to Football Insider.

Andoni Iraola's side looked on course for a 3-0 win following goals from Alexis Mac Allister, Florian Wirtz and Alexander Isak.

All that was left for the Reds was to secure the clean sheet, but referee Tony Harrington put a dent in those plans by awarding a late penalty.

With it deemed it was the Hungarian who infringed on the Chilean, fouling him for a penalty.

Liverpool fans were less than happy with the penalty being awarded, and manager Iraola was left frustrated as well.

Hacket said: "That's exactly how I saw it, to be honest. You can't discount the fact that both were jumping.

"And I thought the Palace player was leading that assault on the opponent, and the referee got it wrong.

"I think what we're looking for as fans and as ex-referees and everybody connected with the game is an accurate interpretation of those types of challenges so that the outcome is the right decision."

In the end, VAR did not get involved, and the penalty decision stood, with the referee getting no second look at what had occurred.

Luckily, on this occasion, the outcome of the match was not altered, with Liverpool still picking up the win.

AI Slop FM radio experiment puts machines on trial, one dodgy tune at a time

📅 Nov 09, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Music fan from Wales lets listeners pit AI-generated songs against human bops – including toilet operas and HR protest songs

A Welsh music enthusiast has built an internet radio station that plays AI-generated tunes alongside human-made tracks without telling listeners which is which, to find out whether listeners really hate AI music or just the idea of it.

AI Slop FM is the creation of Mike Chaffin, who built and runs the station single-handedly from Ammanford, Carmarthenshire. By stripping away artist names, cover art, and any mention of AI, Chaffin hopes to find out what listeners really think when they have nothing but the music to go on.

The free 24/7 station asks listeners to rate tracks from S to E, tag how the music makes them feel, and leave written feedback. The most popular submissions will eventually make it into a weekly chart called the Slop 40, giving creators something that can be hard to come by in the age of streaming: an actual audience.

Anyone can submit a track, including human musicians, although Chaffin has already discovered that not all of them are particularly keen on the idea. He told The Register that he had floated the idea to an unsigned musician friend who had sold around 600 albums, but was met with a rather nervous response.

"Wh, wh, why would I want to do that?" the musician reportedly asked, according to Chaffin, who said he had never heard his friend stutter before.

"It seems that there is a deep rooted fear of AI music in the industry," Chaffin said.

The station is still in beta, with Chaffin waiting for enough submissions to fill its schedule across different genres before opening it fully. He's already been pleasantly surprised by the quality of the music submitted, although he still listens to every track to make sure nobody is trying to pass off Bohemian Rhapsody as their own work.

Among the early offerings are what Chaffin describes as a developing genre of slightly rude protest songs about HR departments, along with an operatic epic about a blocked toilet.

"Some of which are hilarious," he said of the HR songs. As for the toilet opera, it "runs through my head about once a day, whether I like it or not."

Chaffin believes the sheer volume of music uploaded to streaming platforms means plenty of perfectly decent tracks never find an audience, whether a person or a machine made them. He hopes AI Slop FM will give some of those overlooked creations a chance to be heard.

"You don't have to have published your creations to upload. Just to own the rights," he explained.

Tracks that don't make the Slop 40 could still get another outing, with plans for genre-specific stations offering unsuccessful submissions a second chance.

The bigger question is what happens when listeners start rating AI-generated music more highly than the human competition, but Chaffin isn't particularly worried about that.

"If the machines win then their creators clearly deserve to be heard, deserve an audience. If the musicians win, then that's reassuring for them."

He also wants to see how the station's eventual top five or top ten compares with the official music charts, although with submissions having only recently opened, there are no results to pore over just yet.

"Everyone has an opinion on music before a note has been heard, by the genre, the label, the name or the cover art," Chaffin said. "We strip all that away, tracks are judged purely on the music."

Whether listeners ultimately prefer human musicians or their algorithmically assisted rivals remains to be seen. Either way, somewhere in Carmarthenshire, an opera about a blocked toilet is proving rather difficult to flush from its listener's memory. ®

Mario's Next 'Adults Welcome' LEGO Set Is "Hatching Soon"

📅 Nov 09, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Nintendo and LEGO have teased the next entry in their 'Adults Welcome' Mario range, which will see Yoshi step into the limelight.

While we haven't seen the full set just yet, a new post on the Nintendo Today! app shows everything we need to know. We can see a close-up of the dino's eyes and snout in brick form, accompanied by the message, "Hatching soon".

Whether the set ends up being anything more than just Yoshi remains to be seen (we got that Super Mario World-inspired one a few years back, with a pixel art Mario in tow, let's not forget), but more Mario big builds are always a good thing.

The other sworded issue will be the price. The previous '18+' builds have come with a rather hefty price tag (the Mario and Luigi Kart sets will set you back £149.99 / £159.99 respectively), so we'd be surprised if the upcoming Yoshi isn't more of the same.

We'll be keeping an eye out for more price details and the all-important release date over the coming weeks.
