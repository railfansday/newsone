Is there something going on today? A lot of talk about committing crimes and the like, I just can't quite put my finger on why… Ah well, couldn't be important or interesting. What is interesting, however, is Keepsake County, a "midwestern stealth game" that has launched on this inauspicious day about stealing stuff from "hideous farmhouses in the middle of nowhere."

With the sarcastic intro out the way, I will address the elephant in the room: Vovly's Adventure: Reslimed is out today too. Fine, fine, yes I know GTA 6 is the game everyone's talking about, but Forward Instinct, the devs behind Keepsake County, seem well aware of that in their launch day post for the game: "Folks, thanks for your incredible patience. Keepsake County is finally OUT! And yes, it is deeply ironic that it wound up being today, of all days, that we got the green light to finally release, but here we are."

Keepsake Country was supposed to release earlier this month, but seemingly got caught up in the backlog Valve are faced with that's undoubtedly been caused by genAI games. In any case, the burgle-em-up does look quite delightful. I'm not sure if I can quite put my finger on exactly why I get this feeling, but it calls to mind a more complex version of the kind of game you'd find on Cartoon Network's website back in the day (which is a high point of praise, I'd like to be clear).

Each house in Keepsake Country is procedurally generated, meaning you never quite know what to expect when you enter. There's lockpicking, resident tie upping, and of course stealing to be done, but it doesn't just stop at the house. Parking your car, for instance, is an important thing to consider; do you want it hidden so you're less likely to be discovered, or right at the front door so you can make a speedy escape? Not to mention you have to deal with the fuzz showing up at some point if you aren't quick enough!

Gear and the like can be improved between runs, meaning you can lockpick faster or delay the cops, it all seems quite systemically thoughtful. Very much some immersive sim vibes from this one, just on a much smaller scale. And most certainly worth considering picking up on a release day like today! The 2027 Elantra will arrive first with a mostly carryover engine, but the 2028 Elantra Hybrid comes with upgrades to its gas-electric powertrain that improve its power and mpg figures.

We already saw the Elantra’s new sci-fi styling a few months ago when it was revealed globally, and the newly revealed U.S. version looks nearly the same. The 2027 Hyundai Elantra Is Bigger and Much, Much Bolder

📅 Nov 19, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

A sharp-edged “Art of Steel” design gives the Korean compact unmistakable panache.

The Hyundai Elantra continues to defy the apparent incipient death of the sedan, and the Korean automaker just gave it a major upgrade for 2027. Between a new “Art of Steel” design, a larger size, and upgraded infotainment technology, the 2027 Hyundai Elantra is more prepared for the future than ever before.

Revealed Thursday at the 2026 Los Angeles Auto Show, the 2027 Elantra's most obvious feature is its exterior design. Created under Hyundai's new Art of Steel design philosophy, which was first revealed on the Boulder concept, the Elantra is now a boxy beast with wide fenders, sharp headlights, and a square rear end. This new sedan was designed with a very clear three-box configuration and sport sedan dynamism in mind, according to Hyundai’s head of design.

“There is an inherent beauty in a sedan because its proportions are honest, intuitive and functional,” said SangYup Lee, executive vice president and head of the Hyundai and Genesis Global Design Center. “With the all-new Elantra, we returned to that essence, then pushed it forward. Wide, muscular fenders, a long hood, and refined three-box proportions give Elantra a confident, athletic presence that challenges the conventions of the compact sedan.”

In addition to the new exterior design, changes under the skin mean the latest Elantra is now 3.3 inches longer, 1.1 inches wider, and features a 1.1-inch longer wheelbase than its predecessor; all told, Hyundai says the 2027 Elantra has over one inch more rear headroom and legroom. So-called H-Edge lighting, futuristic grille styling, slim- neck mirrors, and lift-up flush door handles make up the rest of the visual changes.

But Hyundai didn’t stop its design renovations with the exterior. Inside the Elantra, the carmaker added a new Curve of Upholstery design and an asymmetrical front layout, which is meant to “gently embrace” the driver. A central 12.9-inch Pleos Connect infotainment system is standard on all Elantra models, while an available slim information display in the driver’s natural line of sight provides critical driving information. Other interior features include 15-kW dual wireless phone charging, Audio by Bang & Olufsen, Hyundai Digital Key 2, and 100-watt ultra-fast charging USB ports. Upholstery will be available in four black color combinations, as well as Burgundy/Rose Ivory and Chalk Beige.

Mechanically, the Elantra is a bit less exciting. The new model will offer two powertrain options: a 1.6-liter hybrid four-cylinder powerplant, and a naturally-aspirated 2.0-liter four-cylinder engine. The hybrid model will deliver 154 horsepower through a six-speed dual-clutch transmission, while the purely internal combustion model offers 146 horsepower routed through a continuously variable transmission. Hybridized Elantra models also feature a predictive drive system that intelligently anticipates driving patterns and road conditions to maximize efficiency, as well as a new “Stay Mode” that lets the climate control, stereo, charging ports and other non-motion-related functions operate for up to 30 minutes with the engine off.

The pure ICE version will arrive as a 2027 model year Elantra, while the new Hyundai Elantra Hybrid will debut as a 2028 model year vehicle. Specific on-sale timing, pricing, fuel economy ratings and other details will be revealed closer to the vehicle’s American launch.

2027 Hyundai Elantra Feels Too Nice To Be Treated Like An Economy Car

📅 Nov 19, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

For the most part, economy cars are treated as fairly disposable pieces of equipment meant mostly for getting folks from Point A to Point B. They're not exactly treated with much care during their lives, and when they're used up, they're scuttled off to the nearest junkyard. More likely than not, that same fate will befall the 2027 Hyundai Elantra and 2028 Elantra Hybrid, and I don't think that's fair. Top to bottom and front to back, this little sedan is just far too nice to deserve a fate like that.

As with the redesigned Tucson, Hyundai put its entire back into its eighth-generation compact sedan, giving it a distinct and decidedly upmarket look, a premium-feeling interior, a cutting-edge brain with stellar interior tech, and a hybrid powertrain that'll get over 50 mpg, all while maintaining the idea that "affordability is standard," according to Olabisi Boyle, Hyundai's Senior VP of product planning and mobility strategy. While I haven't driven it yet, the slightly larger new Elantra seems mighty impressive when it comes to the design, feel and specs.

Full Disclosure: Hyundai paid for my food and transportation so I could check out the new Elantra and Tucson at a warehouse in Jersey City, New Jersey.

The first thing about the new Elantra that will jump out at you about is undoubtedly how it looks. It's the latest example of Hyundai's "Art of Steel" design language that we've previously seen on the China-only Ioniq V, Boulder concept and recently revealed Tucson. Hyundai also told me that the Elantra's design pulls from the N Vision 74 concept, and even though this is a totally different kind of car, you can certainly see those cues, especially in the wide fenders of the rear three-quarter section. The high-performance N version of the Elantra has already been spied, too, and it's going to look sick as hell.

The Elantra is slightly bigger than it used to be; 3.4 inches longer and 1.2 inches wider, to be exact, with 1.2 inches added to the wheelbase. Hyundai says the majority of that extra space goes to the second row, with gains in legroom (1.1 inches), headroom (0.7 inches) and shoulder room for rear-seat occupants, and now folks who accidentally had a kid will be able to better fit a rear-facing car seat.

Despite the extra size and funky angles, the 2027 Elantra still cuts through the air with ease, achieving a 0.26 coefficient of drag. Apparently, H-shaped daytime running lights and taillights are very aerodynamic. Who knew? The wedgey front end, also reminiscent of the N Vision 74, doesn't hurt either, and neither do the flush-mounted door handles.

It all helps explain why the Elantra is a real fuel economy winner whether you're looking at the entry-level gas motor or the optional hybrid. Fitted with 16-inch wheels, the 147-horsepower 2.0-liter inline-4, with the help of a CVT, will return up to 35 mpg combined, according to Hyundai. The 155-hp hybrid's 1.6-liter four-cylinder is mated to a six-speed dual-clutch transmission and 1.49-kWh battery, and Hyundai says it's going to achieve up to 53 mpg combined, which puts it firmly in "really, really good" territory. There's no word on other performance metrics right now, but I wouldn't expect anything too exciting, if I'm honest. The hybrid also comes with a Stay mode that lets you run accessories off the hybrid battery rather than the ICE motor when stationary for longer periods of time.

While the exterior is full of hard edges and sudden cuts, the interior is a far softer, more inviting place, one that wouldn't look out of position on a car that costs far more than the Elantra's currently unannounced price would suggest. Hyundai calls the furniture-inspired design ethos "Curve of Upholstery," and it's supposed to be all about comfort, simplicity, and minimizing driver distractions. It not only looks pretty thanks to interesting styling and materials choices, but it also feels nice to the touch with solid build quality.

Hyundai says Elantra is a "software-defined vehicle," which is pretty uncommon for an internal-combustion-powered car. Mostly Hyundai's spokespeople talk about its Pleos Connect operating system and its capabilities, like next-gen navigation (and AI assistance crap), and it explains why the interior's focal point is the 12.9-inch infotainment touchscreen in the center of the dash. The left section of the screen serves as a driver display showing speed and other pieces of vehicle information, while the rest of it is a regular infotainment screen with configurable widgets for media, maps, and everything else you can imagine — including things you can't imagine, since Pleos allows owners to download apps created by others.

Beneath the center screen are dual wireless 15-watt Qi 2.0 chargers and 100-watt ultra-fast USB-C charging ports for your many important devices, and you can run audio from said devices into an optional 12-speaker Bang & Olufsen audio system. Not too shabby.

Then, behind the steering wheel is a slim 9.9-inch display that is meant to keep the driver's field of vision uncluttered and only display critical information like speed, as well as radio, map directions or a few other things via widgets. We first saw both of these screens in the Tucson, and they make the Elantra feel far more tech-forward than a usual compact sedan would. Adding to that tech-forwardness is Hyundai's SmartSense suite of driver-assistance systems, which includes the classics like lane-keeping assist, lane centering, adaptive cruise control, forward collision avoidance and blind-spot monitoring.

At launch, the gas-only Elantra will come in SE, SEL Sport, and Limited flavors. As you may imagine, features and materials get nicer as you move up the range, but the 12.9-inch screen, adaptive cruise control, push-button start and a few other niceties are all standard from the get-go. Coming a bit later (no word on how much later) will be the 2028 Elantra Hybrid, which will come in SE Blue, SEL Sport, and Limited trims. It's the same deal as the gas car, but there's even more as standard, including dual-zone climate control and Stay mode.

I know that right now the Elantra is shiny and new, with its covers just pulled off at the 2026 LA Auto Show, and soon it'll face the sorts of abuse that all budget-friendly cars do. Before that happens, enjoy the Elantra and what it has to offer the world, because it's not very often an automaker swings for the fences on a cheap vehicle like this. Yes, vehicles like the Corolla, Civic and Mazda3 are very solid, but they're never exactly inspiring — certainly not from a design perspective, at least.

The eighth-generation Elantra elevates the segment, and it might force other automakers to rethink their budget vehicles as well. It certainly looks polarizing from the outside, and it won't be for everyone, but it's also distinctive, and that's something that is very hard to come by on a budget. If you're the type of person who appreciates that, plus a comfortable interior and tech that punches well above its weight class, then it's best you start getting ready now, because the Elantra is set to hit U.S. dealers in early 2027.

Your Favorite THC Drinks Are About to Be Illegal

On Tuesday, more than two dozen veterans organizations sent an open letter to bipartisan congressional leaders demanding something that a few years ago might have been unheard of: better access to legal weed. “Veterans have sacrificed for our country, and they deserve safe, responsible options for managing chronic pain, PTSD, anxiety, sleep problems, and other health conditions,” it read. “Many veterans… rely on hemp products as an alternative to opioids. If Congress fails to act, the products they depend on will suddenly become illegal.”

That’s because on Dec. 11, a new law that limits the amount of THC allowed in hemp will go into effect, eradicating the cannabis market as Americans have come to know it. The colorful cans of THC sodas on Trader Joe’s shelves will be gone, as will the sleep-aid gummies from Target and and the bottles of THC “liquor” at Total Wines. “Anyone who produces the seed, grows a crop, processes the crop, makes a product, sells it to the shelf, and the people who buy it — all these people will be impacted,” explains Greg Serrao, co-founder of Willie’s Remedy, one of the country’s best-selling cannabis drinks.

The new definition will close an eight-year loophole that allowed the CBD market to take off, followed by the federally legal THC market. Veterans, farmers, and cannabis entrepreneurs agree that more regulations are needed to help finally wrangle what was, for a long time, a frontier industry. “A lot of people paint this picture of hemp as this wild west,” says Serrao. “That’s not true.”

What is true is that unless Congress acts to amend the new rules, in less than a month, a $28 billion industry will disappear. “Something like 65 percent of our customers are senior citizens, and 70 percent are over 45,” says Willie’s Remedy’s other co-founder, Forrest Dein.

But unless that advances soon, the businesses who rely on hemp — including the struggling farmers who were told hemp would be the crop to save them — will be shit out of luck.  “The collateral damage is that we axe this American commodity that has so much hope,” explains Morgan Tweet, who runs IND Hemp, a company that processes hemp into products like rope and fiber. “It’s really something special for American farmers, and for American businesses, and manufacturers. There’s nothing in our generation that comes close to being able to show the potential that hemp has with how diverse and how useful of a crop it is, and the magnitude of products you can make from it.”

It all started back in 2018, when Sen. Mitch McConnell accidentally legalized weed — kind of.

On Dec. 20, 2018, President Donald Trump signed the Agricultural Improvement Act, colloquially known as the Farm Bill, into law. In between protections for dairy farmers and changes to the federal food assistance program was a monumental change: for the first time since 1937, growing hemp would be legal in the United States. McConnell himself championed the change, positioning it as a boon to farmers in his home state of Kentucky. The text of the bill defined hemp as a cannabis plant that contained no more than .3 percent delta-nine THC, weed’s intoxicating compound, on a dry-weight basis. “I do not have any plans to endorse the legalization of marijuana,” McConnell said, noting that hemp and marijuana were “two separate plants.” Related Content Summer Walker Files $20 Million Cross-Complaint Against Interscope Julio Iglesias Under Investigation for Sexual Abuse Claims What I Learned About Genocide From the Rohingya Muslims Mullin Says Skipping LSU Game Amid FAA Crisis Would Have Caused ‘Confusion’

By then, hemp was already a popular crop in Europe, where it had long been used for everything from oil to rope. It was also at the center of the growing CBD industry. CBD, another cannabinoid found in hemp, was not intoxicating, like THC, but was starting to be used medicinally for things like epilepsy and pain management.

Once hemp was legalized, American farmers started planting the crop. Some varieties focused on bushy leaves and heavy stalks, great for industrial uses. But others had big flowers, perfect for extracting CBD. The CBD boom was expected; what wasn’t was that scientists quickly figured out a process to convert CBD into THC — and the hemp-derived THC market took off. Not only did they figure out how to make delta-9 THC, the cannabinoid that is known to get us high, they also started to make a wide variety of other kinds of cannabinoids, including delta-8 THC (a slightly less potent intoxicant that is present in trace amounts in the plant), as well as some that don’t actually occur naturally. It was all legal because the products were derived from hemp, not marijuana, which is a Schedule I controlled substance.

Many of these companies self-policed, testing the products themselves and going through established liquor frameworks in order to be sold at national chains. Others, however, skirted the rules, offering shady products made with questionable cannabinoids and no real testing or oversight. This so-called “gas station weed” started to make folks nervous — particularly McConnell. So when Congress was putting together a bill to reopen the government last fall, after a record-long shutdown, McConnell added an amendment to the 2018 definition of hemp to get rid of the market as we know it.

The new law will change the definition of legal hemp. Previously, hemp was allowed to have up to .3 percent THC as measured in its dry weight. Hemp farmers could grow crops that adhered to this, then processors could extract cannabinoids, and companies put them into products so that they had enough potency to see effects. The new law, however, looks at the final product, restricting anything with more than .4 milligrams of THC per container.

There are two parts to the law, explains Neil M. Willner, an attorney with Vicente LLP, a New York-based firm specializing in cannabis law. The first went into effect on Nov. 12, and banned the sale of all synthetic THC products, or THC compounds that are not found naturally in the cannabis plant. On Dec. 11, the low-dose sodas, liquors, and gummies that have become so popular — and usually contain five or 10 mg of THC per serving — will no longer be legal on a federal level. People in areas with state-legal cannabis programs can still go to a dispensary to buy THC products, but the companies built around hemp-derived cannabinoids — and the people in states without legal weed — will be out of luck.

It will also make it nearly impossible to grow hemp, too. “We’ve talked to a lot of hemp farmers, and it’s very difficult for them to manage the THC content of their crops,” says Dein. “It’ll push a lot of farmers out of business.” Tweet, owner of IND Hemp, the processing facility, adds that the new law doesn’t define what a “container” actually is. “I ship 55-gallon drums of hemp seed oil all over the country, and by definition, that’s a container, right?” she says. “Like, we do make a product that likely will go above that 0.4 milligram [limit] just because of that volume. Even though hemp seed oil doesn’t have a lot of cannabinoids in it, it does have some, and so there is some risk to our business.”

In one word, bad. Take, for example, Texas, where outgoing Lieutenant Governor Dan Patrick introduced a bill over the summer that would have banned all hemp products that contained THC. This was wildly unpopular with many of the 1.5 million veterans who live in the state, among others. Outgoing Governor Greg Abbott ended up vetoing the bill, instead establishing guidelines for age and marketing restrictions. In November, Patrick lost his bid for reelection, as did Abbott and other Republicans statewide. “I think that’s one of the reasons Texas went blue,” says Serrao. Tim Jensen, CEO of the consulting firm War Party Inc., which advises companies on how best to connect with veterans, as well as a signatory to the open letter sent to Congress, points out that it’s more than bad optics — there’s a very real health risk to veterans if the rug is pulled out from under the industry. “We have a large community of individuals of 55 and older that are experiencing a lot of inflammation and pain-related problems that are wanting to find alternative modalities that are not opioid related, and that is big,” he says. “You’re finding the veteran community really leaning into these opportunities to get away from alcohol.”
