New York State Senator Jeremy Cooney proposed a 10% surcharge on the net trading fee revenue that prediction market operators earn from transactions made in the state.

New York State Senator Jeremy Cooney proposed a 10% surcharge on the net trading fee revenue that prediction market operators earn from transactions made in the state. The bill, known as the Futures Act, was introduced Monday and referred to the Senate Rules Committee.

According to the Futures Act, proceeds from the 10% tax would be channeled to a newly created Futures Fund for Early Education. The fund aims to help achieve universal pre-kindergarten by supporting early education programs in New York for children ages 2 to 4.

Cooney's bill would designate the state commissioner of taxation and finance to administer the fund, with distributions to school districts made in consultation with the Division of the Budget and the State Education Department.

"I'm placing a bet on the future of UPK in New York," Cooney wrote on X. "When we invest in our youngest New Yorkers, we are investing in our state's future."

Outside of the tax, the bill would establish consumer protection requirements for prediction market platforms operating in New York. These include age and identity verification, tools allowing users to set deposit or trading limits, and options for voluntary self-exclusion.

Furthermore, the bill seeks to bar elected officials and policymaking staff from trading on such platforms, citing concerns over the use of non-public information. It would also establish penalties for election-related crimes carried out on prediction market platforms.

The proposal arrives as New York Attorney General Letitia James continues enforcement actions against major prediction market operators. James has sued both Kalshi and Polymarket, claiming that the platforms operate unlicensed gambling businesses in violation of state law.

In a recent development, a judge denied James' bid for a temporary restraining order (TRO) against Kalshi to block the platform from operating in New York. While Kalshi, headquartered in New York, said the TRO may disrupt its entire operations, the state attorney general has said that was not her goal.

The Commodity Futures Trading Commission has been clashing with states over prediction markets, asserting that its federal authority preempts state enforcement actions.

Meanwhile, the bill includes provisions intended to avoid conflict with federal law. It states that nothing in the measure shall be construed to authorize a state agency to regulate the listing, terms, or trading of event contracts on a designated contract market, or to exercise jurisdiction over matters reserved to the CFTC.

U.S. jury convicts Huawei in a criminal trial over fraud and trade secrets

📅 2026-11-25  |  Category: Business  |  Read Source →

Chinese technology giant Huawei was convicted Tuesday in New York on multiple federal criminal charges, including fraud, U.S. sanctions violations and conspiracy to steal trade secrets, following a three-month trial, according to the U.S. Department of Justice.

The verdict marks a major development in a long-running criminal case against Huawei, with charges first publicly disclosed in 2019.

A federal jury found Huawei and three of its subsidiaries guilty of multiple offenses, including bank and wire fraud and conspiracy to commit money laundering, the Justice Department said in a statement.

"No person or company is above the law," U.S. Attorney General Todd Blanche said, accusing Huawei and its subsidiaries of conducting business in the United States through deception and theft.

The Justice Department said Huawei misrepresented its activities in Iran to banks to secure billions of dollars and retain access to U.S. dollar-clearing services. The company also supplied surveillance cameras that enabled Iranian authorities to monitor protests, the department said.

Among the examples of intellectual property theft detailed by prosecutors were cases involving Cisco and T-Mobile.

Huawei copied source code used in Cisco routers, with its engineers referring to Cisco by spelling its name backward in an attempt to conceal the theft.

Huawei employees also sent photographs and technical details of T-Mobile's touchscreen-testing robot to China, with one employee later removing a component from T-Mobile's laboratory in a backpack, according to the Justice Department.

"For over two decades, Huawei built its global empire on a foundation of fraud, theft, and deception," said Assistant Attorney General Tysen A. Duva of the Justice Department's Criminal Division.

Huawei pushed back against the verdict in a statement issued Wednesday, while welcoming its acquittal on the racketeering conspiracy charge.

"We believe the government's remaining charges are equally without legal or factual merit," Huawei said, adding that the five trade secret-related disputes cited by prosecutors had previously been resolved as civil matters.

The company said it would review the verdicts and take necessary legal action to defend its interests, while remaining focused on maintaining uninterrupted business operations and investing in research and development.

Huawei and its subsidiaries face potential financial penalties, including fines, forfeiture and restitution, with sentencing scheduled for Sept. 14, 2027, according to the Justice Department.

U.S. prosecutors first publicly disclosed criminal charges against Huawei in January 2019, a month after the company's chief financial officer, Meng Wanzhou, was arrested in Canada.

Meng returned to China in September 2021 after entering into a deferred prosecution agreement with U.S. prosecutors.

Anthropic is trying to kill traditional desktop apps with Claude-generated desktop apps

📅 Nov 25, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Rarely a day goes by that Anthropic PBC doesn’t announce some kind of new capability, feature or model, and today wasn’t any different. Today came the ability to use Claude to create fully functional desktop applications for Windows and Mac computers with a single prompt.

It promises to be one of its more impactful updates, fanning the flames of those who argue that artificial intelligence is ultimately destined to eat the software world.

The company said today that users can now transform what it calls “artifacts” into standalone applications that run independently of Claude and can be accessed from the desktop. Artifacts have been around for a while, and they can be almost anything, such as a simple order tracker or a meeting dashboard that pulls information from the user’s calendar and email inbox.

However, until today, artifacts were a bit tricky to access. They could be opened up only from inside Claude itself, which meant users first had to open up the chatbot, login and then find the old conversation where they originally created the artifact they want to use.

Anthropic said people are using Claude artifacts all the time for various everyday tasks, and so it makes sense to mature the concept. “If you rely on a tool for work, it should open like the rest of your apps and remind you when something needs your attention,” the company said.

That’s why Anthropic is making it possible for people to use artifacts to create, run and share desktop applications with Claude’s new “Install as App” feature, which is available in beta now on Windows and macOS.

In a video posted on X, Anthropic showed how straightforward it is to create a functional desktop app from a simple prompt. The user simply describes the tool they want Claude to create, click confirm and then sit back and wait for a few seconds as it slaps together the required code to bring that app to life.

You can now install Claude artifacts as desktop apps on Mac and Windows.

Ask Claude to build an artifact, click "Install as app", and open it like any other app. It can use your connectors and, if you allow it, send notifications or add a menu bar item. pic.twitter.com/8CBgqe5OKq

— ClaudeDevs (@ClaudeDevs) November 24, 2026

Because these are ‘real’ apps, they come with their own app icons and they open in a dedicated Window, rather than running within the Claude interface. They also get the ability to send notifications to users, Anthropic said.

As an added benefit, Claude artifacts that are transformed into desktop apps will be able to use Claude and various third-party connectors, meaning that AI agents will be able to interact with them autonomously, Anthropic said. However, the company did warn that any requests made by an app to Claude will count towards their monthly credit usage, which means it could become expensive if an app is used excessively.

In a blog post Anthropic showcased a number of examples of what its internal teams have built, including a fully-featured image editing app called Linnet, a file manager with a cinematic design called Phosphor, and a terminal application that integrates with Claude, which is known as Tern.

There is one major caveat to Claude artifacts-as-apps that sets them apart from traditional desktop applications. According to Anthropic, “you need to be online and signed in to Claude to open an app.”

That could be a pretty big downside, for many desktop applications are designed to work even if the computer is not connected to the internet. But if someone lacks a Wi-Fi connection, they will be unable to access their Claude-generated apps, even if they’re already built and configured.

Houthi attack on Saudi Arabian sovereign wealth fund PIF's tower injures 11

📅 2026-11-25  |  Category: Business  |  Read Source →

Yemen's Iran-backed Houthis hit Saudi Arabian sovereign wealth fund Public Investment Fund Tower on Tuesday, injuring 11.

The kingdom's civil defense directorate did not name the tower, but posted an image showing damage to the PIF tower. Eyewitness reports by Al Jazeera also indicated that the building that was hit was the PIF Tower in Riyadh.

The injured included two Saudi citizens, two Indians, three Nepalis, and four Egyptians.

The attack comes as tensions have flared up again between Saudi Arabia, which is leading an international coalition to restore the internationally recognized government of Yemen after it was forced to flee in 2015, and the Houthis.

Turki al-Maliki, a spokesperson for the coalition, said one of the towers of the financial center was targeted in a "cowardly terrorist attack," referring to the financial center in the Saudi capital of Riyadh.

He added that authorities were conducting an on-site investigation at the scene of the attack.

This has been the second attack by the Iran-backed group in six days on the tower, although the previous attack did not cause any casualties.

On Wednesday, Houthi brigadier general Yahya Saree warned on X that the port of Jeddah and all Saudi ports along the Red Sea coast will become "targets for our forces," if Riyadh attacks the Yemeni Red Sea port of Hodeidah.

The coalition had asked civilians to evacuate the port "for their safety," on Nov. 20.

Attacks by the rebel Houthis have been escalating on Saudi Arabia, with the group targeting one of Saudi Arabia's oil refineries on Sunday, according to Reuters. The Houthis also launched drone and ballistic missiles against the country's airports, most recently targeting the King Khalid International Airport on Oct. 25, according to reports.

The new attacks come amid wider strikes by Tehran and Iran-linked forces in the Gulf, with Bahrain also reporting that it was attacked by Iran over the weekend. Bahrain is home to the U.S. Navy's Fifth Fleet.

"As a result of the heinous Iranian aggression, a civilian industrial facility was targeted, causing a fire at the site," Bahrain's Ministry of Interior said on X.

The ministry later said two people, a Bahraini citizen and a Pakistani resident, were injured in the attack.
