Marvel's Vision has a new codename, as Marvel announces an upcoming transformation into an even more powerful robotic form. Here's the new codename, and what it means for the fate of everyone's favorite synthezoid.

Debuting in 1968's Avengers #57, Vision was named for a classic Marvel character - the Golden Age hero known as Aarkus, an alien who fought alongside Captain America in WWII. In Marvel canon, it was coined by Janet van Dyne's Wasp (much like the 'Avengers' name itself) on seeing the mysterious figure for the first time, when Vision was sent to attack the Avengers.

Vision has undergone numerous transformations since then, from taking on his emotionless white form to being semi-recreated as the Young Avengers hero Jonas. Now, Vision undergoes his darkest transformation yet and a new name change.

Marvel recently revealed new details for the upcoming Hulk War: Infernal Rage #1, officially revealing the names of each of the 'Infernal Avengers' commanded by the titular villain. These monsters are all heroes corrupted by the Infernal Hulk's magic and possessed by the souls of ancient monsters.

The press release confirmed what fans had guessed - that the robotic being by Hulk's side is a transformed Vision. Patterned after a biblically-accurate angel, this new Vision is a floating metallic skull with a single eye and four wings, a gigantic, uncovered brain emerging from the top.

Marvel's new images name the being "The Diaboli Ex Machina," meaning "the devil from the machine." The reference is to an ancient Greek theatrical convention where a supernatural being would emerge to conclude the story, often appearing by a literal mechanism. "Diaboli Ex Machina" therefore speaks to a devil literally emerging from a mechanism, and also a supernatural being appearing suddenly to defeat Marvel's heroes.

Vision hasn't yet taken this form in Marvel Comics, though fans did glimpse it in an Amazing Spider-Man 1000/Queen in Black CGD 2026 #1 flash forward to the upcoming events of Hulk War.

The transformation is likely to happen in December 2's Infernal Hulk vs. Avengers #1, which will see Captain America, Iron Man, Captain Marvel, Vision and Scarlet Witch take on the Infernal Hulk, aka the demonic Eldest.

Because everyone but Wanda has a confirmed monster form in Hulk War - and due to their past romantic and familial bond - fans have speculated that The Diaboli Ex Machina is a combination of Scarlet Witch and Vision. This would go some way to explaining the religious iconography of Visions' new design, but others characters have undergone radical transformations without this kind of explanation.

It's more likely that the angelic elements of VIsion's transformation are connected to the demon soul implanted in him by the Infernal Hulk. It may even be linked to the android sent to defeat the Infernal Hulk by the One-Above-All (image above), which was destroyed but could be repurposed in transforming the Vision.

Marvel has been building to 2027's Hulk War since 2025, when the demonic Eldest defeated Bruce Banner and stole his gamma powers. Now, after resurrecting an army of monsters and corrupting heroes including Spider-Man, the Fantastic Four and iconic X-Men including Jubilee, the Hulk War is nearly upon us.

Before that point, the Avengers will embark on one last, desperate mission to stop the Infernal Hulk in his tracks, leading to the creation of the Iron Thing, the Ebon Sphinx, the War Dead and Vision's horrific Diaboli Ex Machina.

United Airlines' MileagePlus program launched on May 6, 1981, according to The Points Guy. The program, which celebrated its 45th anniversary this year, has undergone many iterations, particularly after the 2010 merger with Continental Airlines. According to NerdWallet, the program's most recent change took place on April 2, fine-tuning the mile-earning schedule for today's economy. Basic Economy officially no longer earns travelers miles, and it has reduced miles per dollar earned at each status level.

Premier 1K, United's top tier of earned status for passengers, grants a variety of benefits, amenities, and privileges onboard and in the airport. Premier 1K unlocks United's most comprehensive set of publicly available elite benefits, including priority pre-boarding, complimentary Premier Upgrades cleared as early as 96 hours before departure, three free checked bags, Economy Plus seating at booking for the member and up to eight companions, access to expanded award availability, a dedicated Premier phone line, and a bonus of 280 PlusPoints that can be used to request upgrades on eligible flights.

There are two main ways to achieve such status, including flying on United or United Express operated flights in a class other than Basic Economy, and with credit card spending with United's co-branded Chase credit card lineup. The exclusivity comes at a price, and not just anyone may qualify for Premier 1K, let alone United's entry level status, Premier Silver. For the 2026 qualification year, United Airlines illustrates on its website that earning Premier 1K status requires either 28,000 Premier Qualifying Points (PQP) or a combination of 22,000 PQP and 60 Premier Qualifying Flights (PQF).

PQPs are generally earned based on how much a traveler spends on United-operated flights, preferred seating, upgrades, and select partner airline tickets. The requirement makes Premier 1K one of the most expensive top-tier status levels among major US airlines.

A PQP measures the money you spend on airfare and eligible credit card purchases (typically earning 1 PQP per dollar spent on base fares). A PQF counts each individual flight segment you take on United, United Express, or eligible partner airlines. PQFs include layover segments. For instance, a round trip flight with a layover in each direction equals four PQFs earned.

The qualification requirements have steadily increased throughout the loyalty program's history. Following the merger with Continental Airlines and a broader airline industry trend toward revenue-based loyalty programs, United has increasingly prioritized spending over simply logging miles flown. Today, how much a passenger spends matters significantly more than how far they travel.

According to NerdWallet, one cannot simply earn Premier 1K status simply through spending on one of United's Chase cards. While a strong supplement in the pursuit of Premier 1K, it is not enough alone to suffice. United requires a four PQF minimum in order to qualify for any premier status, including the entry level Premier Silver.

While Premier 1K may traditionally be associated with road warriors flying hundreds of thousands of miles annually, United's current rules allow members to earn the vast majority of the required 28,000 PQP through credit card spending. The absolute maximum utilizing credit card spend requires 28,000 PQP, plus the four-flight minimum. Depending on the specific Chase card in use, one can earn 1 PQP per $20 spent or 1 PQP per $15 spent, translating to $560,000 or $420,000 in credit card spend annually.

In practical terms, a traveler pursuing status exclusively through credit card spending must supplement their spending with four PQF segments on United or an authorized partner airline. Business travelers whose employers pay for frequent premium cabin trips often reach the threshold naturally, whereas leisure travelers typically require a more deliberate strategy involving credit card spending and frequent travel.

This is certainly bad news for United MileagePlus members.

United's leadership has repeatedly emphasized that Premier 1K is intended to reward the airline's most loyal and highest-value customers. While MileagePlus has evolved significantly since its 1981 launch, the core philosophy remains centered around recognizing travelers who consistently choose United throughout the year. That philosophy is reflected in the program's modern qualification structure, where spending and engagement with the airline carry greater weight than mileage alone.

Jarad Fisher, President of United MileagePlus, joined United in February, having a proven track record as the Chief of Delta Air Lines' SkyMiles program, as well as being the creator of the Apple Card. The Points Guy has reported that Fisher describes Premier 1K earning in 2026 as majority PQP based, with United credit cards bridging the gap.

United continues moving toward revenue-based loyalty metrics. In October 2026, the carrier announced that Premier Qualifying Flights (PQF) would be removed from status qualification beginning in 2027, leaving Premier Qualifying Points (PQP) as the primary path to elite status. The change reinforces United's view that Premier status, particularly Premier 1K, should primarily reward customer value and spending rather than simply the number of flights taken.

Reaching Premier 1K is not solely about upgrades and priority boarding. United's broader loyalty ecosystem includes benefits such as PlusPoints, Million Miler status, and occasional status match opportunities from other airlines, all of which can influence whether pursuing 1K is worthwhile. For some travelers, these supplemental benefits may ultimately provide more value than the status title itself.

Possibly the most significant benefit is PlusPoints. Upon reaching Premier 1K, members receive a substantial allocation of upgrade credits that can be used on eligible United and partner-operated flights. Unlike complimentary upgrades, PlusPoints give travelers greater control over when and where they attempt to move into a premium cabin. However, upgrade inventory remains capacity controlled, meaning even top-tier members may not always secure their preferred seat.

Million Miler status adds another layer of value for long-term United customers. Unlike annual status qualifications, Million Miler recognition rewards cumulative lifetime flying with the airline. However, becoming a Million Miler is solely based on miles flown on a United Aircraft, not credit card spend. Overall, travelers considering a long-term loyalty strategy may therefore view Premier 1K as only one component of a broader relationship with United, especially compared with travelers who simply leverage status matches between competing carriers.

Of course, airline loyalty is not solely dictated by its frequent flyer program, but a range of other factors such as geography, service quality, and price. However, for regular visitors to New York, Los Angeles, or even Chicago, when multiple airlines and airports are of choice, is there a better top tier airline status to compare?

At Delta, travelers earn Diamond Medallion status exclusively through Medallion Qualification Dollars (MQDs), with a threshold of $28,000 MQDs during the qualification year. The airline's program is almost entirely revenue-based, allowing members to earn progress through flights, Delta Vacations purchases, and eligible credit card activity. Meanwhile, American Airlines awards its highest published tier, Executive Platinum, after earning 200,000 Loyalty Points, which can be accumulated through flights, co-branded credit card spending, shopping portals, hotel partners, and numerous non-flying activities. As a result, many loyalty experts consider American's program the easiest top-tier status to earn because it offers the greatest number of pathways that do not require extensive flying.

A traveler based near a United hub may find Premier 1K considerably more useful than an equivalent status elsewhere due simply to flight frequency and network coverage. Meanwhile, a traveler primarily flying through Atlanta may derive greater value from Delta Diamond Medallion, while someone based in Dallas-Fort Worth International Airport (DFW) may prefer American Airlines Executive Platinum. In each case, the best loyalty program is often the one aligned with a passenger's most frequently used airport.

United Premier 1K remains one of the most prestigious airline status levels available in North America, but it is also one of the most difficult to achieve. Travelers must accumulate significant Premier Qualifying Points through flying, spending, or a combination of both while maintaining an active relationship with the airline throughout the qualification year. Premier 1K status brings travelers select privileges, such as checked baggage, United Club access, upgrades, and more.

MileagePlus's evolution illustrates a broader industry trend toward rewarding revenue contribution rather than simply distance flown. Benefits such as PlusPoints, priority services, expanded award availability, and upgrade opportunities can provide substantial value for those who travel regularly. Premier 1K status provides extra value for travelers based in or frequently visiting any of United's hubs, with the presence of native United Airlines staff, a United Club, and with mainline United service with upgrade ability.

Looking ahead, the trend toward revenue-based qualification appears unlikely to reverse. You must be at least 62 or older to claim a spousal Social Security benefit. But even at this age, you can’t sign up if your partner is not yet receiving the partner's own retirement benefit. Arsenal 'have ingredients' but Europe's elite on another level

📅 Nov 19, 2026  |  Category: Sports  |  Read Source →

Arsenal goalkeeper Misa Rodriguez made six saves but was beaten twice in defeat by Lyon

It is only 18 months since Arsenal tasted European glory, but they are not a team on many people's list of favourites for this season's Women's Champions League.

That is because not only have the Gunners disappointed across their opening 15 matches of the season, but also the elite teams in Europe seem to have gone up another level.

In the space of seven days, Arsenal have witnessed first hand what it takes to reach those heights - and the gap they must bridge.

Last week, they were resilient at Barcelona to earn a very respectable 1-1 draw, but were fortunate the Spanish side were not clinical with their chances at Camp Nou.

On Thursday night, they were outplayed at home in losing 2-0 to an excellent Lyon side, who look on course to match or better their runner-up finish from last season.

One point from such games could be pleasing - but that only shows the work Arsenal need to do to be considered one of the top teams in Europe.

French giants Lyon outclass Arsenal in Women's Champions League

Arsenal came into Thursday's defeat by Lyon off the back of a fifth successive Women's Super League draw at Emirates Stadium against London City Lionesses.

In contrast, Lyon have been flying domestically, winning all nine of their matches, scoring 49 goals, and then not conceding any in their unbeaten Champions League run.

Such form sets the pace and it was clear at Meadow Park that they were superior to Arsenal in every department.

Vibrant Germany international Jule Brand gave them the lead midway through the first half and Melchie Dumornay sealed the win with a powerful strike in the 57th minute.

Arsenal defender Leah Williamson said the "better team won the game" and manager Renee Slegers highlighted the difference in physicality between the teams.

"Compared to last year when we played Lyon [in the semi-finals], they are even better now. They have standout individual quality," Slegers said.

"They dominated in the first half. We had solutions for the second half and had more presence. We wore them down better but it still didn't lead to open chances."

Despite playing at right-back, Jule Brand had the third most touches in the Arsenal penalty area for Lyon (five) after striker Marie-Antoinette Katoto and fellow goalscorer Melchie Dumornay

Lyon had a 3.33 expected goals ratio on Thursday, serving up 23 shots to Arsenal's seven. They created six big chances and 14 of their shots were inside the box.

Dumornay oozed quality, midfielder Lily Yohannes showed composure and Brand was very impressive both defensively and going forward for Lyon, deployed in a new position.

Those individuals have benefitted from the expertise of former Barcelona manager Jonatan Giraldez, who joined in June 2025 - the month after Arsenal won the Champions League - and has now had the time he needed to impose his style.

As a result, Lyon have gone up another level and the gap between these two teams has widened by Arsenal's drop-off this season.

Slegers also said Arsenal had "prepared all we could" for the intensity that Lyon brought in London - but it still did not have an impact.

Asked how far off Lyon they currently are, Slegers said: "It's 2-0 today and they are better than us but it doesn't mean if we were to play them again soon, this season, that it would be the same game again.

"We saw moments where we did well and we know from the last couple of years we can hurt Lyon.

"But for today, their pace and physicality helped them get too much dominance in the game and that is one of their strengths. Then it became hard for us."

Arsenal have not yet qualified for the knockout stages of the Champions League

Williamson said Arsenal "know what they need to do to be better" and that they are aware of "where the gaps are" to Europe's elite.

They are already 12 points off the pace in the Women's Super League title race and are unlikely to qualify automatically for the Champions League quarter-finals.

"The level rises year on year. Everybody cops goals - we have to try not to and we have to create," said Williamson following the defeat by Lyon.

"We're all focused and we're all together. That's the most comforting thing for me - but this has been a tough period."

It has not all been bad despite disappointing results - and context is important.

Over a gruelling seven days, they have faced European holders Barcelona, WSL high-flyers London City Lionesses and record Champions League winners Lyon.

They showed grit and resilience out in Barcelona last week - traits that have not been on show enough for Arsenal this season, but are clearly there.

Players like Williamson, striker Alessia Russo and summer signings Georgia Stanway and Ona Batlle know what it takes to compete at the highest level.

Arsenal invested in the summer transfer window and brought in proven, experienced players, but that has not translated into results - yet.

Giraldez said time with his players has helped improve Lyon and perhaps that is what Arsenal need - patience.

"I think they are changing now. They made a good game against Barcelona and it's very difficult to go there and get a point," said Giraldez.

"The game against London City was much better. It's not just about them, but also about us. Now we have improved and play better football than last year.

"I think it is a matter of time [for Arsenal]. They have a good coach and good players. In general, they have all the ingredients to have a good season."
