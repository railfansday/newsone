Guardrail en input y output

📅 Nov 13, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Posted on Nov 13 • Originally published at g.cloud

Los guardrails de entrada y salida son mecanismos técnicos que filtran, validan y transforman los datos antes de que un modelo de IA los procese (input) y antes de que sus respuestas sean entregadas al usuario (output), minimizando riesgos de contenido dañino, sesgado o no conforme.

Son controles técnicos integrados en el ciclo de inferencia de modelos de lenguaje. Los guardrails de entrada actúan en tiempo real sobre el prompt recibido: normalizan texto, identifican patrones de ataque (como prompt injection), verifican pertenencia al dominio autorizado y rechazan entradas ambiguas o potencialmente peligrosas. Los guardrails de salida, en cambio, operan tras la generación del texto: clasifican la respuesta según categorías de riesgo (odio, desinformación, privacidad), aplican filtros de tono y precisión, y pueden reescribir o suprimir fragmentos antes de la entrega final. Ambos son componentes esenciales de la arquitectura de seguridad de IA —no son módulos opcionales, sino capas obligatorias en entornos regulados o críticos.

Porque un solo punto de control es insuficiente. Un guardrail de entrada puede fallar ante entradas legítimas pero ambiguas que activan sesgos ocultos del modelo; un guardrail de salida, a su vez, no puede corregir una generación fundamentalmente errónea si no hay supervisión temprana. La combinación permite defensa en profundidad: entrada limpia → generación más predecible → salida auditada. Esto es especialmente relevante en aplicaciones brasileñas donde se requiere alineación con principios constitucionales (dignidad, não discriminação) y normativas sectoriales (ex. Resolução CFM nº 2.287/2021 para IA em saúde).

Con herramientas como IBM Granite Guardrails, que ofrecen APIs para integración nativa con modelos Granite (3.0 y superiores), soporte para reglas personalizables por sector y compatibilidad con pipelines RAG. No requieren reentrenamiento del modelo, sino configuración de políticas basadas en expresiones regulares, modelos ligeros de clasificación y listas de términos bloqueados o permitidos. Su eficacia depende de la calidad de los datos de entrenamiento de los clasificadores auxiliares y de la actualización continua de las reglas frente a nuevas tácticas de evasión.

Q: ¿Funcionan igual para todos los modelos de lenguaje?

A: No. Su efectividad varía según la arquitectura, tamaño y dominio del modelo. Requieren ajuste específico para cada caso de uso.

Q: ¿Pueden afectar el rendimiento del sistema?

A: Sí, aunque mínimamente: añaden latencia de milisegundos (típicamente <50 ms por capa), compensada por mayor confiabilidad operacional y reducción de incidentes post-despliegue.

Guardrail en el tribunal

📅 Nov 13, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Posted on Nov 13 • Originally published at g.cloud

El término guardrail en el tribunal no corresponde a un concepto jurídico reconocido ni regulado por el Consejo Nacional de Justicia (CNJ) ni por normativa procesal brasileña. No existe mención formal, doctrinal ni jurisprudencial al uso de “guardrails” como mecanismo institucional dentro de los tribunales brasileños.

“Guardrail” (barandilla de seguridad) es un término del diseño de sistemas de inteligencia artificial, usado principalmente por proveedores como IBM (ej. Granite models) para describir reglas predefinidas que restringen salidas inapropiadas —como contenido ilegal, discriminatorio o falso. En el ámbito judicial brasileño, no hay equivalente funcional ni normativo: los tribunales no despliegan modelos generativos autónomos en funciones decisivas, y las herramientas tecnológicas autorizadas (como sistemas de classificación documental o alerta de prazos) operan bajo estrictos protocolos de validación humana y auditoría —no bajo “guardrails” técnicos como tal.

El CNJ regula el uso responsable de tecnologías mediante la Resolução CNJ n.º 342/2020, que exige evaluación de impacto ético, transparencia en algoritmos usados para apoyo processual y prohibición expresa de automatización de decisiones judiciales. También se aplica la Lei Geral de Proteção de Dados (LGPD), especialmente en tratamiento de datos sensibles de partes. Sin embargo, ninguno de estos instrumentos menciona ni define “guardrails”.

Mediante auditorías periódicas (art. 12 da Res. CNJ 342/2020), registro obligatorio de sistemas na Plataforma Nacional de Governança Digital (PNGD) y exigencia de relatórios de viés e equidade para soluciones de IA —pero siempre con supervisión humana continua y sin delegación de competencias decisivas.

A: No. No existe guía, recomendação técnica ni documento oficial del CNJ que use o defina el término “guardrail”.

Q: ¿Pueden los tribunales usar modelos Granite de IBM con guardrails integrados?

A: Solo si cumplen la Res. CNJ 342/2020 y la LGPD; pero el uso de “guardrails” no sustituye los requisitos legales de auditoría, transparencia y control humano.

Q: ¿Hay casos jurisprudenciales que mencionen “guardrails”?

A: No. Búsqueda en RAGJur, STF e TJSP con los términos “guardrail”, “guard rail” y “barreira de segurança” (en contexto algorítmico) arroja cero resultados vinculantes.

Q: ¿Qué debe hacer un tribunal antes de adoptar una herramienta de IA?

A: Realizar avaliação de impacto ético, registrarla na PNGD, garantizar explicabilidade y someterla a revisão periódica por comissão de ética institucional —según arts. 9º y 12º da Res. CNJ 342/2020.

U.S. government grounds all flights across the United States ‘due to extraordinary cirumstances’

📅 Nov 13, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

The  Federal Aviation Administration said Friday afternoon that it has grounded all flights across the United States due to “extraordinary circumstances” and issues related to safety.

The FAA’s website said the cause is an “equipment outage.” The site itself was down for a brief period at the time of publication, with a note that the website was “experiencing technical difficulties.”

Per an alert sent by the Air Traffic Control System Command Center, or ATCSCC, the FAA said the ground stop means that aircraft cannot take off from departure gates. The message said that the disruption was “due to extraordinary circumstances and for reasons of safety,” but did not specify further. The alert was not visible on the ATCSCC website at press time.

The alert stated that all departing flights must remain on the ground “until further notice.” Inbound international flights will be “managed on a case-by-case basis,” it said.

Transportation Secretary Sean Duffy said in a post on X: “FAA technicians are investigating the issue.”

It’s not clear what caused the ongoing FAA equipment outage. The outage comes in the same week that the administration rolled out an AI-powered tool for air traffic controllers to save aircraft flight time and fuel costs — though it’s unclear if the ongoing outage is related.

A spokesperson for the Federal Aviation Administration did not immediately respond to a request for comment.

FAA Orders Nationwide Ground Stop Due To Air Traffic Control Communication Issues

📅 Nov 13, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

At 3:45 p.m. ET on Friday afternoon, the Federal Aviation Administration issued a nationwide ground stop, halting all flights that were set to take off from U.S. airports until further notice. An ATCSCC advisory was posted by Flightradar24 on Bluesky, which reads: "Due to extraordinary circumstances and for reasons of safety. Attention all aircraft operators, by order of the Federal Aviation Command Center all airports are ground stopped. All departing traffic should remain ground stopped until further notice. Inbound international flights will be managed on a case by case basis."

In a Twitter post, Transportation Secretary Sean Duffy said, "The FAA issued a nationwide ground stop for all U.S. airports following a communication issue impacting all Air Route Traffic Control Centers. FAA technicians are investigating the issue. We will provide immediate updates as we have them." There's no official word from the FAA yet; the organization has yet to post any statements on social media or its website. According to News9 Oklahoma, reports from aviation monitoring account OSINTdefender said the FAA's ground stop is due to communication frequency issues affecting the National Airspace System. As of right now the ground stop is scheduled to stay in effect until 11:00 p.m. ET, but that could change.

In about as funny a turn of events as you could possibly imagine, our team found out about this situation from a social media post by Flavor Flav, who seems to be one of the first people to post about it. This is a developing story, and we'll update you as we learn more.

A scan of the National Airspace System Status website shows major airports listed as "airport closure," "ground stop," "ground delay," or "departure delay." The last time the FAA enacted a ground stop was in January 2023, when all domestic departures were paused for less than two hours because of a pilot notification system went down. Before that, the last ground stop had been on September 11, 2001. A few days ago we told you about how the FAA is getting rid of ATC's floppy disks, and just yesterday it started rolling out a new AI air traffic tool, though of course there's nothing to suggest that has anything to do with today's issue.

Our very own Andy Kalmowitz is caught up in this mess, as he was about to take off from Chicago O'Hare International Airport on a connecting flight while on his way home from driving the new Subaru Forester Wilderness Hybrid. Right as they were getting ready to taxi the ground stop was issued, and his pilot came over the intercom and explained the situation: "We're in a nationwide ground stop right now. That's not ground delay, that's ground stop. Basically the FAA has said, 'look, we're having radio problems at multiple centers across the nation; it's not just United Airlines, and it's not just Chicago. If you're airborne, that's great, you're coming in to land,' but they are not allowing anyone else to take off and get out there into the system. Which is understandable, but I don't like it, and I know you don't either."

‘I Didn’t Want to Become Fat, Bloated, Naked Elvis’: Why KISS Called It Quits on Touring (And What They’re Up to Now)

📅 Nov 13, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Gene Simmons and Paul Stanley are gearing up for the release of the KISS: End of the Road documentary.

It’s been nearly three years since KISS played its last concert as a touring act — and in makeup. Now the group is giving fans a look behind the scenes of its End of the Road World Tour, a globe-spanning farewell trek that was bumpier than the KISS camp let on. The revealing KISS: End of the Road documentary drops Nov. 19 via KISSOnline.com, where it will be available for seven days before moving on to other platforms. The band will preview the film during its KISS Kruise: Land-Locked in Vegas convention Nov. 13-15 at the Virgin Hotel in Las Vegas, where it will also play acoustic and electric concerts and host a tribute to late original guitarist Ace Frehley, who passed away in October 2025 at the age of 74. KISS’ recent spate of activity also includes the release of box sets celebrating the 50th anniversary of the group’s fifth studio album, Rock and Roll Over, which spent 45 weeks on the Billboard 200 and peaked at No. 11 in 1976. At the same time, the KISS Army is awaiting an avatar show that’s been in the works for several years, as well as a biopic that’s also in process.

“It’s as it should be,” the group’s Paul Stanley tells Billboard via Zoom about KISS’ continuing life after reaching the End of the Road. “KISS is larger than life, and KISS isn’t about the four individuals. It’s about the four persons, the four characters. It’s a timeless thing, so I totally get those four going on. I can get older, but he [Stanley’s Starchild character] won’t.”

Gene Simmons, whom Stanley founded KISS with during 1973 in New York, adds that, “We’ve seen bands that wait too long to get off the road and it’s sad. I didn’t want to become fat, bloated, naked Elvis on the bathroom floor. So you have to know when to get off the stage as KISS. But that doesn’t mean (the band) has to end.”

The nearly two-and-a-half-hour KISS: End of the Road film shows in intimate detail that the conclusion of that phase of the band’s career was emotional, triumphant and trying.

Directed by DJ Viola, who also helmed the 2021 A&E documentary KISStory, the chronicle focuses on the final 2023 leg of the pandemic-interrupted End of the Road World Tour, which started in South America and played North America, Europe and Australia before wrapping up with homecoming performances Dec. 1-2 at Madison Square Garden in New York City.

Noting that KISStory was “what we call a ‘womb-to-tomb’ documentary,” Viola tells Billboard, “I wanted End of the Road to be almost the antithesis of that approach. Rather than having the guys tell us what happened, I wanted audiences to experience it as it was happening, from the first of their final 50 shows all the way to Madison Square Garden.” He and his crew became embedded flies on the wall during the trek, capturing planning meetings, backstage intrigue and plenty of onstage highlights, as well as interviews with band members, manager Doc McGhee, Simmons’ and Stanley’s wives and others.

“For 50 years,” Viola notes, “KISS has been protective of what happens behind the curtain. Everyone says they want to show the ‘warts and all,’ but very few actually mean it. This time, the band opened that curtain completely. What you discover is that beneath all the makeup, pyro and spectacle is an unwavering commitment to giving their fans the greatest show possible. After 50 years, that dedication hasn’t diminished one bit.”

There are warts aplenty, too, including: production difficulties early on that created significant tension in the touring party; Simmons being briefly incapacitated by dehydration during a concert in Brazil; and Stanley suffering from a severe flu that led to the cancellation of nine shows and put the last stretch of the tour in jeopardy. There are touching scenes as well, with the band and those around them talking about reaching the end of touring life. Stanley and Simmons both choke up during the final concert while talking from the stage about their parents and growing up in New York, and Simmons even shoos the camera crew away as he and Stanley exchange sentiments during that night’s sound check.

The film ends with the two men meeting, post-tour, for a convivial conversation at a deli in Bel Air, Calif.

“The trick to being unobtrusive in documentary filmmaking is to be ubiquitous,” Viola says. “You have to be there for the dullest moments as well as the most exciting ones…. The more you’re around, the more people stop thinking about the camera. Eventually, you’re just another person in the room. You have to become part of the team while remaining as invisible as possible. It’s a strange balance, but that’s where the best moments come from.”

He adds that, “The trust we’d established during KISStory was essential. KISS allowed me into virtually every corner of their world, from rehearsals and travel to putting on their makeup, standing onstage during the shows and being there afterward when the makeup came off and they dissected the night’s performance. They knew I wasn’t there to manufacture controversy. I was there to authentically capture what was happening, good or bad.”

Stanley says the band was not bothered by the constant camera presence, explaining that at this point, he and Simmons are more comfortable letting fans inside aspects of KISS that were previously kept under wraps — and deliberately so.

“It’s more appropriate to do now,” he says. “It’s famously remembered that for the early years of the band, nobody ever saw us without makeup and that was part of the mystique, part of building this larger-than-life persona. At this point, it’s interesting to let people go behind it and in some ways, it makes it bigger because it’s such a Herculean effort to do what we’ve done. To have a band go on stage 50 years later and people go, ‘They look the same as they did,’ well, that doesn’t happen by chance. It’s all very encompassing, and always has been…. I think people can appreciate that now and realize that there’s a real person behind Superman with a Marshall amp.”

The End of the Road film nods to the past with historical footage, and it also provides a glimpse into the future by showing the group working with Sweden’s Pophouse Entertainment — which acquired KISS’ catalog and trademarks for a reported $300 million during 2024 — on the digital avatar show, which was previewed at the final Madison Square Garden date. “They’re great new partners,” Simmons says. “They’re infusing so much money into KISS; you’re gonna have theme parks and cartoons and movies…just some amazing stuff coming. They’re very cognizant of the fact that the same-old isn’t the way to do it… and want to make it easier for fans who want to control the content.”

The KISS avatar show is still a work in progress, but Simmons predicts that it will arrive “in the next year to two years, at a place that is soon to be announced. They’re really putting a lot of effort  into it.” Stanley notes that “it’s far more complicated than I certainly anticipated. It’s quite complex. But the rewards are that much greater, too.” And he clarifies that it will likely be something different from what’s expected.

“The idea was never to replicate a concert. That’s not what this is about,” Stanley says. “It’s to create an experience and almost make us your tour guides into something unworldly, and yet totally based in the music. And the idea was never to do something that was appealing exclusively to a KISS fan; we wanted to create a must-see, so to speak, regardless of your taste in music. That’s what we’re creating.”

The KISS biopic is also moving forward, with Joseph McGinty “McG” Nichol directing and casting. “Anybody who’s ever been involved in film knows you can get old waiting for a film to get made,” Stanley says. “It’s still in motion, and that’s all I can say. It’s very slow going; you can rush something out but, frankly, I love being in a position where if we can’t do a film that I’m proud of, I don’t need to do a film. It just doesn’t matter that much. It would be a nice feather in the cap, but it better be a good-looking feather or I don’t want it.”

Simmons and Stanley may have more to reveal at the KISS Kruise. Both have been preparing for the band’s performances as well as the Frehley tribute, an all-star event during which Frehley’s band will back a roster of guests, including alumnus guitarist Bruce Kulick.

The Rock and Roll Over sets, meanwhile, feature a new mix by original album producer Eddie Kramer, along with outtakes, alternative mixes, demos and a live 1977 performance from Japan. “To have Eddie Kramer go in and rethink things was great,” Stanley says. “And I’m certainly open to looking into other people working on other albums and go in and reimagine them, rebirth them. I’m always in favor of making something better when it’s possible.”

Both men have their own projects as well: Simmons has dates booked into 2027 with his solo band and produced the film Deep Water; Stanley is actively painting “four or five days a week” and says his band Soul Station “will get back up and running soon.”

Meanwhile, KISS is also approaching the one-year anniversary (Dec. 7) of its Kennedy Center Honor — and also potentially being part of the last class to be feted in the actual venue, whose main building is closed to the public amid disputes over U.S. President Donald Trump’s efforts to rename and remodel the historic venue. (This year’s Honors will be presented at the Capital One Arena in Washington, D.C.)

None of that has affected the way Simmons and Stanley view their honor, however.

“All things must pass; nothing lasts forever,” Simmons notes. “I remember when Elvis (Presley) was invited by President Nixon to receive some kind of awards and stuff; I thought, ‘Cool.’ Do I agree with the politics of President Nixon? No, but he’s the duly elected president. President Trump is our president, and that’s it. What you think politically, that’s for the ballot box.”

Stanley adds that, “I don’t believe anyone ever went to any of the (honorees) and said, ‘Who did you vote for?’ That’s supposed to be a non-issue. For me, getting it was just part of the trifecta, so to speak. We got the Rock & Roll Hall of Fame. We got the Songwriters Hall of Fame, and then to get the Kennedy Center Honor…. I’m at a point in my life now where it really comes down to, if you love me, let me know. Don’t put it in an obituary or my eulogy. If there’s an accolade you want to give me, I’m here.”

Short answer: Zoho says PhoneBridge (Zoho Telephony) is not a separate app but an integration platform that connects CRM to either Zoho's built-in telephony, Zoho Voice or another Marketplace phone provider. Each route has its own trap: Zoho says built-in numbers without a call flow reject incoming calls, and Zoho Voice calls work in CRM only if numbers are assigned to Admin users and Telephony agents in Zoho Voice. Your fallback provider is the thing that breaks first

📅 Nov 13, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Anyone running an LLM feature for more than a few months has a fallback path: a second provider, a router, or a forty-line wrapper that tries the next model when the first one returns 529 or 503. The design work usually stops at "if the primary fails, use the secondary." That is the easy half.

The hard half is what happens to the secondary at the exact moment the primary trips.

When the primary starts returning overload errors, every client fails over within the same few seconds. The secondary does not receive a trickle; it receives its normal traffic plus the entire primary's traffic, arriving as one burst, with retry loops attached.

If the secondary was sized for its own load plus a small margin, it was sized for a number that never occurs during an incident. The result is a familiar pattern: the primary degrades, the fallback immediately degrades, and the incident looks like "both providers are down" when in fact the second one is being DDoS'd by its own customer.

With a secondary at 30% of primary capacity and a 2x retry multiplier, the fallback sees roughly 2.6x what it handles on a good day, in the first minute.

1. Treat Retry-After as authoritative, and jitter everything else. 503 and 529 carry the header inconsistently, so read it when present, and when absent use exponential backoff with jitter. Unjittered backoff turns a synchronised burst into a synchronised second burst, one interval later.

3. Instrument failover rate per provider, not just error rate. Error rate tells you what is failing now. Failover rate tells you how much of your traffic has already moved, which is the number that predicts whether the fallback is about to become the incident. In my experience it leads the user-visible symptom by minutes, which is the only window where you can act.

A fallback is not a spare tire. It is a second system that has to absorb a correlated spike at exactly the moment its sibling is unhealthy. Designing it as identical-but-idle guarantees it will be the first thing to fall over, and it will do so while every dashboard still shows the primary as the problem.

The cheap version of all this is a single line in the runbook: when the primary trips, cap fallback concurrency before you touch anything else. The expensive version is discovering it during the incident.

I Walked After Every Meal for Two Months, and My Digestion and Energy Levels Have Never Been Better

Experts weigh in on why sitting is the worst thing you can do after eating

It’s almost 9:00 p.m., and my work dinner in Manhattan’s SoHo neighborhood is finally coming to a close. As the people around me start to get up and call their Ubers, I head out the door to go home—on foot. After a lavish dinner, most people’s last thought is to get a brisk walk in, but for me, it’s crucial—not to hit a step count or burn calories, but as my version of a digestif, if you will.

My idea to walk after dinner came from a video I saw months ago from a TikTok nutritionist who touted a brisk 10-minute walk after meals as the key to boosting metabolism, regulating blood sugar levels, and helping your stomach digest food. My two favorite pastimes are eating and walking. I never thought the two would go hand in hand.

“Sitting briefly after eating isn’t inherently harmful, but prolonged, uninterrupted sitting can lead to a higher post-meal blood-sugar response compared with breaking it up with short walks,” says Elizabeth Sharp Edens, an internal medicine and obesity medicine physician, functional medicine practitioner, and founder of HMW Method. A 2022 meta-analysis in Sports Medicine found that light-intensity walking reduced post-meal glucose by almost 17 percent. The same study found that standing breaks after meals reduced glucose spikes by 9.5 percent on average. The TL;DR: Research says that any type of movement can improve glucose levels.

We know walking can do wonders—it can clear the mind, help you lean out, and even boost dopamine—but why is it a solution for feeling less full and avoiding afternoon crashes? “When you walk, your muscles use glucose for energy,” Edens explains. “Muscle contractions move glucose transporters called GLUT4 to the surface of muscle cells, helping draw glucose out of the bloodstream through a pathway that doesn’t depend on insulin.” Since your legs, the vehicle for walking, contain some of the biggest muscles in your body, it makes sense that walking effectively stimulates muscle contractions that move glucose transporters. “Walking breaks are a simple way to support blood sugar management while getting your steps in,” she adds.

While many rely on peptides, green powders, and “magic” supplements, walking is an easy, mindless (and free) way to improve digestion and prevent blood-sugar crashes. “Even two to five minutes after a meal can help, ideally before the glucose peak, typically 60 to 90 minutes after eating,” Kiran Krishnan, a research microbiologist, the founder and chief science officer of the Biome Learning Center, and Dose key opinion leader, adds.

I often joke after large meals that I will have to be rolled home. The good news: Walking can also reduce the overwhelming feeling of fullness. “Walking also helps the gut physically,” Krishnan says, citing a study that found that a slow walk after a meal significantly shortened the time it took the stomach to empty half its contents. “For people prone to bloating, mild [walking] cut intestinal gas retention from 45 percent to 24 percent and eased symptoms,” he adds. Plus, Krishnan says studies show reduced LDL cholesterol levels and improved liver function in those who walked after meals.

Then there’s good old gravity, says Caitlin Hosmer, a Maine-based registered dietitian and co-founder of MenoPantry. “Gravity helps too, which is part of why walking may help people who feel uncomfortably full or get reflux after meals,” she explains. “Gentle movement and staying upright help food move out of the stomach and along the digestive tract.”

Ten minutes isn’t long, but studies show it’s just as effective as a longer walk. A 2025 study published by PubMed Central found that a 10-minute walk lowered glucose measurements by 6 percent and a 30-minute walk lowered glucose levels by 5 percent, meaning just 10 minutes of walking is all you need to help reduce a post-meal glucose spike. If you can walk for 30 minutes, an hour, or even longer—that’s great. But just 10 minutes can work just as well.

What matters most is how quickly you get moving. “Glucose typically peaks 60 to 90 minutes after eating, and moving before that peak is what blunts the spike,” Hosmer says. “A walk puts your muscles to work just as glucose from the meal is flooding into the bloodstream.” At first, I questioned whether I had to walk after every meal. “Put your effort where it counts most: after your largest, most carbohydrate-rich meal,” Hosmer advises, adding that for most, that’s after dinner, but walking after all three meals is ideal.

It’s also not meant to feel daunting or like a chore. “Your post-dinner walk isn’t a workout. It’s a way of helping your body handle the meal,” Hosmer says. “The one real ‘wrong way’ is skipping it because you think it has to be long or perfect. I often say something is better than nothing, and that’s especially true here. Even a few minutes of walking, started soon after you finish eating, can blunt the blood sugar rise, and it may help your digestion too.”
