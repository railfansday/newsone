Nuxeo API authentication is only the first permission decision

📅 Nov 08, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Nuxeo is a document platform with an API-oriented architecture. Its REST API can retrieve documents, inspect ACLs, run workflows, and expose custom endpoints. Authentication tells the server who is calling. The ACL attached to a document, folder, repository, or workflow determines what that identity can do.

On 2026-11-08, ZoomEye count-only observations returned 4 matches for app="Nuxeo", 186 for title="Nuxeo", and 28,973 for http.body="Nuxeo". The query used sub_type=all, page 1, and page size 1. The body count is far larger and can include documentation, examples, and unrelated pages. The application count is small, while the title count gives a broader product-level lead to validate.

Nuxeo documentation describes a default ACL managed through the document interface and permissions that can be granted or denied. It also describes an anonymous user that can be referenced when a repository administrator gives certain folders or documents public access. Anonymous access is therefore an explicit grant, not an accidental HTTP state.

The practical question is which anonymous grants still exist. A public document may be intentional, but a folder that was made public for a temporary campaign can become a durable information leak. The ACL enricher and @acl adapter can help an authorized operator inspect inherited and explicit access decisions.

Inventory each path. Confirm which headers are trusted, whether direct access to the application bypasses the proxy, and whether a test client can supply an identity header that the server accepts. A header that is safe only when stripped by the proxy is not an authentication control on a directly reachable listener.

List repositories and their public entry points. Identify top-level folders with broad read permissions, then inspect documents that are directly accessible through the REST path. Check inherited ACLs and any explicit deny or grant that changes the expected result.

Review principals, groups, and service accounts. An account used by a workflow may have access far beyond the worker's business purpose. Rotate credentials that are shared by multiple applications, and verify that removing a user from a group removes access to documents that the group granted.

Choose two accounts: one that should have access and one that should not. Request the same document by ID, path, and search endpoint. Then request the ACL and confirm that the effective decision matches the intended policy. Repeat after revoking a permission to ensure that cached authorization state does not preserve access longer than policy permits.

Use the REST API's error behavior as evidence, not as the whole test. A 404 can mean the document is hidden, while a 403 can reveal that it exists. Record the result without assuming that an authentication success means the object was authorized.

Nuxeo's API is designed to be programmable. The same flexibility makes an ACL inventory necessary. The product finding is useful only when paired with repository-level authorization evidence.

Where To Spot The Airbus A380 In 2026: The 10 Airports With The Most Superjumbo Flights

The Airbus A380 remains one of the most recognizable aircraft in commercial aviation, and despite production having ended, the world's largest passenger airliner continues to operate an impressive international network. For enthusiasts hoping to catch the Superjumbo in action during 2026, some airports offer considerably better opportunities than others.

The Simple Flying Flight Tracker reveals where the aircraft makes the most appearances, which airlines operate the greatest number of services, and where spotters can find the widest variety of individual A380s. This article covers every Airbus A380 movement recorded between October 5 and November 3, 2026, providing a snapshot of the aircraft's continued global presence. Movements count each flight at both its departure and arrival airports, meaning a total of 14,140 movements represent 7,070 individual flights.

Manchester Airport (MAN) recorded 167 flights operated by the Airbus A380 between October 5 and November 3, with Emirates the sole operator of the type. Although its overall activity is considerably lower than that of the major international hubs, Manchester is notable because the Dubai-based carrier provides a regular Superjumbo presence, making it a convenient destination for those in northern England. Its position also gives British spotters an alternative to traveling to London to see the aircraft.

Meanwhile, New York John F. Kennedy International Airport (JFK) saw 284 Airbus A380 flights over the same period. These services were operated by the likes of Korean Air, Emirates, and Asiana Airlines, reflecting continuing demand for high-capacity international services connecting the US with major overseas destinations. This airport's presence in the list highlights how the A380 remains an important part of the transatlantic and transpacific aviation landscape.

Other airports also benefit from Emirates' continued commitment to the Airbus A380. Birmingham (BHX), Prague (PRG), Denpasar (DPS), and Christchurch (CHC) are among the destinations where enthusiasts can encounter the aircraft on daily services, while Glasgow (GLA) receives less frequent visits. These locations demonstrate that the Superjumbo's presence extends well beyond the world's largest connecting hubs, offering spotting opportunities at destinations that might otherwise appear unlikely to feature such a large aircraft.

According to the Simple Flying Flight Tracker, Paris Charles De Gaulle Airport (CDG) recorded 333 flights operated by the Airbus A380 between October 5 and November 3, 2026. Operators of the A380 to the French capital include Emirates, Qatar Airways, and Etihad Airways, demonstrating the continuing importance of Gulf-based airlines in maintaining the aircraft's European presence. Air France formerly operated a fleet of 10 A380s before retiring the aircraft type during the COVID-19 pandemic.

Meanwhile, Los Angeles International Airport (LAX) saw 342 Airbus A380 flights in the same period, making it one of the leading destinations in the United States. Its airline list includes Asiana Airlines, British Airways, Qantas, Korean Air, Emirates, and Lufthansa, giving it six different operators.

The airport's extensive long-haul connections explain its position in the ranking. Airlines deploy the A380 on selected routes linking Los Angeles with major international hubs across Europe, Asia, and the Middle East. Its role as a major gateway to the US ensures that several international carriers continue to bring the aircraft to California from destinations including London Heathrow Airport (LHR), Seoul Incheon International Airport (ICN), and Dubai International Airport (DXB).

Bangkok Suvarnabhumi Airport (BKK) recorded 356 Airbus A380 flights, operated by Asiana Airlines, Etihad Airways, Emirates, Qatar Airways, and Lufthansa, providing a healthy selection of operators to and from the Thai capital. The airport's position as a major international gateway to one of the world's most popular tourist destinations helps sustain its appeal among airlines operating busy long-haul routes.

Emirates, Qatar Airways and Etihad Airways provide important connections through their respective Gulf hubs, while Lufthansa and Asiana Airlines further diversify the airport's offering. Although Bangkok cannot match Dubai's flight frequency, its combination of aircraft numbers and airline variety makes it an attractive alternative. The presence of five operators also gives aviation enthusiasts a broader selection of aircraft to look out for on the Simple Flying Flight Tracker.

The US is a key long-haul market for superjumbo operators.

Sydney Kingsford Smith Airport (SYD) recorded 628 flights operated by the Airbus A380, placing it among the busiest airports in the world for the aircraft type. Airlines that operate the A380 to and from Sydney include Asiana Airlines, Singapore Airlines, Qantas, and Emirates, reflecting Australia's strong international travel market and demand for high-capacity long-haul services. The airport's extensive international connections make it an important destination for several of the world's remaining Superjumbo operators. Qantas is naturally an important part of the Sydney spotting experience, given its Australian headquarters and long-standing investment in the Airbus A380. Singapore Airlines and Emirates also contribute to the airport's activity, providing opportunities to see several different aircraft and liveries. This combination makes Sydney particularly interesting for those looking to compare the different configurations and appearances of international A380 operators.

British Airways, Qantas, Emirates, and Singapore Airlines all deploy their Superjumbos to the airport, reflecting its importance as a major destination for tourism, business, and transit flights. Singapore Airlines is particularly significant as the launch operator of the Airbus A380 back in 2007, and the aircraft remains part of its long-haul operations today, almost 20 years later.

Every now and then, a game with little to no fanfare pre-launch gets blessed by the algorithm and surges in popularity. Probably Stolen is the latest recipient of this; after being released on Oct. 28, it's continued to climb in popularity, peaking at 174,382 concurrent players on Nov. 4, according to SteamDB.

The debut game from indie studio Questing Goose, Probably Stolen is what you get if you mix the aesthetics and vibes of Cyberpunk 2077 with the gameplay systems of Papers, Please, with a little inventory management thrown in for good measure. You are the proprietor of a market stall in a dystopian space station, and you have one goal: turn the biggest profit you can.

There's just one caveat: most of your trading is going to come via illicit goods. "You’re a former scavenger who secured a storefront thanks to a generous loan and questionable timing," reads the Steam page. "Now rent is rising, factions are watching, and most of your best margins live in legal gray zones."

You can choose "which kind of trouble you're willing to monetize," from being a gunsmith or chemist to a farmer, water merchant, or fence. Each option brings its own problems though, such as addiction, inspections, extra hurdles to jump through such as machinery and permits. The list goes on. As your ethically dubious operation grows, so does your portfolio, and you can start running multiple businesses simultaneously. But you better make sure you don't miss a rent payment.

Reviews on Steam are currently aggregating to "overwhelmingly positive," and some of the anecdotes from players' time in the game are the best case of exactly why Probably Stolen is so popular. "Hid my storage chest with my most expencive [sic] stuff in the trashcan during a surprise popo-inspection and forgot to take it out. Energy Transfer Just Raised Its Distribution for the 20th Straight Quarter. Here's How Much $10,000 Invested Pays Annually.

📅 Nov 08, 2026  |  Category: Business  |  Read Source →

Don't let a past dividend cut scare you away from this 7%-yielding pipeline stock.

Energy Transfer (ET -1.41%), one of the largest pipeline-focused master limited partnerships (MLPs), recently announced its 20th consecutive increase in its quarterly cash distribution. Raising its quarterly distribution by more than 3%, the company now pays $0.3425 per unit quarterly, or $1.37 per unit annually.

That said, given how Energy Transfer has implemented a dividend cut in the recent past, management's touting of its "20th consecutive quarterly increase" is a bit misleading. Yet, while Energy Transfer may lack the dividend growth track record of other pipeline stocks, that doesn't mean you need to stick this name in your "stay away" pile.

Based on Energy Transfer's current stock price of around $19 per share, a $10,000 investment would buy you around 526 units. Multiply that by the annualized distributions, and the total amount of distributions from this investment is around $720 per year.

This equates to around a 7.1% forward distribution yield, well above the S&P 500 current forward dividend yield of around 1.1%. This also represents a higher forward yield than peers, such as 6.1%-yielding Enterprise Products Partners and 5.7%-yielding Enbridge.

Energy Transfer may have cut distributions in the past, but I wouldn't anticipate this happening again anytime soon. For one, Energy Transfer's distribution cut was due primarily to the COVID-19 outbreak, a black swan event. Moreover, consider Energy Transfer's latest quarterly results. For the quarter ending Sept. 30, the MLP reported distributable cash flow of $2.5 billion versus $1.18 billion in distributions.

Energy Transfer is also pursuing acquisitions it believes are accretive to its distributable cash flow, such as its recently announced $2.6 billion deal for Vaquero Midstream, and continues to invest billions organically to expand its pipeline network. Although future performance hinges heavily on the macro backdrop for energy, until signs of an unfavorable shift emerge, consider Energy Transfer another top choice among pipeline stocks for income-focused investors.

Why you shouldn't remove the screen protector film on your Nintendo Switch 2

📅 Nov 08, 2026  |  Category: Business  |  Read Source →

We're now a little over one year into the launch of the Nintendo Switch 2, which first hit the market back in the middle of 2025. It was priced in the same ballpark as its competitors, the PlayStation 5 and Xbox Series X/S, at $450 for the base console and $500 for a Mario Kart World bundle. Since then, it has increased to a standard $500, even for a base edition.

No one likes high prices, but if you paid either of those dollar amounts for the Switch 2, you've become keenly aware of your investment in this little machine. Keeping that investment safe is paramount, and one way to prevent damage is to avoid something that Nintendo has continuously warned us about even before launch: removing the pre-installed screen protector.

It may not seem like much, but it is a critical piece of the system's configuration. Removing it may seem tempting, but it's actually going to do quite a bit to protect your $500 investment.

When we buy a new piece of hardware or device, the satisfaction of peeling a film off is remarkably enjoyable. If you have a brand-new Switch 2, however, it's recommended that you absolutely not do that. It may seem like it's there for shipping needs, but its true purpose is rather important to the overall health and longevity of the system.

Calling it a "screen protector" isn't even entirely accurate, as it's described by Nintendo as an anti-scattering adhesive film. The main purpose of the film is to protect the console from glass shards that may shatter if the screen is broken. Needless to say, it plays a critical role in the system's lifespan.

Thankfully, actually removing it is quite difficult. Removing the film requires some intense force, which is probably the first and only clue one needs to determine that it shouldn't be taken off. If you do, somehow, manage to get it off, the system will be in severe danger. It is a foundational part of the console's hardware.

Now that you know why you shouldn't ever remove the adhesive film from the console, you may be curious to know if the Switch 2 needs any extra protection. While you will likely get by just fine with Nintendo's pre-installed protection, there are some extra steps you can take to give your Switch 2 a bit of extra life in the years ahead.

Nintendo sells an affordable screen protector that is manufactured by HORI, and there are so many third-party aftermarket versions available for purchase that it would be almost impossible for you to not be able to find one you like. Unlike the one applied by Nintendo, an actual screen protector will do exactly that, safeguarding the console from scratches and cosmetic damage.

Tempered glass protectors will cost slightly more, depending on the brand, but this is a part of the console's accessory lineup that is actually reasonably priced when compared to other items. If you're at all worried about the screen on your Switch 2, your best course of action is to do as instructed by Nintendo and leave the film on, then grab yourself a decently priced tempered glass screen protector.

TL;DR: Chainalysis said on November 6 it was restoring KYT and address screening in phases after a cyberattack. The firm earlier disclosed that names, usernames, password hashes and live tokens for users had been exposed. Customers were told to prepare password resets and API-key rotation; investigations data exposure has not been established.

A disruption at one of cryptocurrency’s best-known investigative and compliance providers is also a test of operational resilience for the companies that depend on it. In a security incident notice updated November 6, Chainalysis said it had begun restoring access to its Know Your Transaction, or KYT, and address-screening products through a phased process.

The restoration followed several days of service restrictions and increasingly detailed disclosures about a cyberattack. Because many crypto businesses use screening tools to investigate suspicious transactions or meet compliance obligations, this is a story about both information security and the ability of regulated operations to continue.

Chainalysis initially reported discovering unauthorised activity affecting its internal systems and tools. Its October 30 statement said it had found no evidence at that point of access to certain sensitive materials. But the November 4 update disclosed that user information, including names, usernames, password hashes and live tokens, had been exposed to an attacker.

That sequence is important. Early incident statements often reflect a limited investigation, and later forensic work can establish a wider impact. The responsible reading is the latest dated disclosure, not a permanently frozen version of the first announcement.

The company also reported abnormal activity around KYT and other screening tools, prompting additional products to be taken offline. Chainalysis said it had no evidence of access to investigation content or manipulation of its attribution data at the time of its updates. That is a narrower claim than declaring every possible effect ruled out.

Chainalysis told customers to submit trusted IP addresses for allowlisting and prepare to reset passwords and rotate API keys as access is restored. A password hash is not the same as a plaintext password, but exposed hashes create security concerns depending on how they were generated and the strength of the underlying credentials. Live tokens can present a separate risk because they may enable access while valid.

For organisations, the operational response should follow the vendor’s direct instructions and their own security teams’ assessment. Users should not click unexpected reset links in unsolicited emails; signing in through a verified service address is safer than relying on a message that could be spoofed.

The wider issue of controls and dependency is also present in NewsBTC’s coverage of Aave’s protective restrictions on new tBTC positions. Different systems and risks are involved, but both illustrate how a provider’s status can affect the options available to dependent services.

Compliance teams need accurate screening, audit trails and clear decisions about when they can accept funds or process an onboarding request. Temporarily losing a primary tool can complicate those processes even if the underlying blockchain continues operating. Backups, manual escalation and incident playbooks are therefore part of real crypto infrastructure.

Chainalysis said restoration would expand as it validated enhanced security controls. It had not, in the cited November 6 update, confirmed full restoration of every affected product. That distinction should guide reporting and customer decisions.

The key future questions are the complete scope of exposure, remedial actions, the timetable for Reactor and other affected products and independent assurance that compromised credentials have been invalidated. For now, the firm has acknowledged a serious security incident and begun a controlled recovery.

MINNEAPOLIS (AP) — Once Ty Dieffenbach saw the Minnesota secondary align and react the way he expected, it was time for the UCLA quarterback to let it fly.

Aidan Mizell was going to run it down. Dieffenbach had no doubt about that. And when the Bruins got the ball back about five minutes later, well, it was time to try it again.

With two 75-yard touchdown passes from Dieffenbach to Mizell on consecutive possessions in the fourth quarter, the Bruins sped away from the Gophers and showed the Big Ten — and the College Football Playoff selection committee — yet another strength of their unbeaten breakout team in the 34-20 victory on Saturday.

"We knew it was going to be a big finding-the-gaps-in-the-defense game, and we just worked all week on the timing," said Dieffenbach, who had a career-high 303 yards and three touchdowns on just eight completions.

Said Mizell: "We knew the whole game we were able to get behind the DB. We were just waiting for the right time to call it."

Mizell, a transfer from Florida who entered the week with only seven receptions all season developed a strong connection during training camp with Dieffenbach while they worked frequently with the backups. Dieffenbach, who took over last month for the injured Nico Iamaleava, quickly realized he could hardly overthrow the 6-foot-1 senior.

"He can really run, so you've just got to put it out there for him," Dieffenbach said.

Bruins coach Bob Chesney said offensive coordinator Dean Kennedy was trying to set up those plays to Mizell earlier in the game, and the strategy clearly worked on the Gophers — undoing more than 47 minutes of solid defense that had the game tied at 17 early in the fourth quarter.

"How we practice is how we play, and we knew by the way we practiced this week it was over with," Mizell said.

On the first one, the Gophers were in a two-high formation with quarters zone coverage underneath. But Mizell caught safety Kerry Brown flat-footed and blew by him on a post route, and safety Zack Harden — who was supposed to be playing deeper — bit on a crossing route in front of him.

On the second one, both Harden and safety Aidan Gousby lurched forward on the run fake by Dieffenbach. That left no safety help for cornerback Aydan West, who was badly beaten by Mizell on a post route from the other side of the field.

"When you have a blown coverage, you're going to get beat," Gophers coach P.J. Fleck said. "We've got to have depth to the defense, and we didn't have it."

Papua New Guinea’s Most Expensive Film, ‘Mussau: The First Song,’ Wraps Shoot, Unveils First Looks (EXCLUSIVE)

📅 Nov 07, 2026  |  Category: Sports  |  Read Source →

Indian filmmaker Bijukumar Damodaran directs the $4.5 million period drama, shot on remote Mussau Island and in Goroka, which is eyeing a 2027 festival premiere.

Papua New Guinea’s most expensive film to date, the $4.5 million historical drama “Mussau: The First Song,” has wrapped principal photography after a 40-day shoot across two provinces.

Filming took place on Mussau Island in New Ireland Province and in Goroka, in Eastern Highlands Province. PNG-based producer NAFA Productions celebrated the wrap at a Thanksgiving event in Port Moresby on Saturday. Government figures at the event included Belden Norman Namah, Minister for Arts, Culture and Tourism; Chief Secretary Ivan Pomaleu; and Chief Censor Jim Abani. Related Stories Taylor Swift's 'I Knew It, I Knew You' and 'La Bola Negra's 'La Nieve' Among Nominees for Hollywood Music In Media Awards

Indian director Bijukumar Damodaran, a three-time Indian National Film Award winner, is at the helm. Parul Agrawal produces, and Noelene Taula Wunum is executive producer.

Inspired by a true story and set in the 1930s, the film centers on Taula Livovoa, a young islander who walks away from tribal violence and takes up a faith that is new to him. His choice sets off a sweeping social change that the producers rank among the South Pacific’s most remarkable.

The dialogue is in six languages: Mussau, Emirau, Agarabi, Gadsup, Tok Pisin and English. Some of them are indigenous languages with small numbers of speakers that are at risk of dying out. The producers present the film as a way to protect the country’s linguistic and cultural heritage and bring it to a global audience.

Much of the cast was drawn from the local community, and many are acting on screen for the first time. Some had never watched a film before. Several roles are played by great-grandchildren of the real people whose lives inspired the story. Two British actors round out the ensemble.

Mussau Island has little infrastructure and patchy communications, which made the shoot hard to pull off. The 120-person crew, 20 of them technicians from India, spent 30 hours at sea crossing the Pacific to reach the island. Temporary lodging and facilities were set up there. Islanders helped throughout, including on sequences shot in the sea at high tide. The unit then moved on to Goroka to finish filming.

“This has been one of the most challenging and rewarding journeys of my filmmaking career,” Damodaran said. “The people of Mussau welcomed us into their lives, their culture and their history, and their contribution has been invaluable. Having descendants of the original historical figures portray some of these characters, alongside community members who had never seen a film before, made this journey even more profound.”

Hilda Tanimia wrote the story. The screenplay is by Jennifer Litau, who also served as translator, along with Joses Imona and Damodaran. The cinematographer is Yedhu Radhakrishnan and the editor is Davis Palakal Manuel. Arnold Laena handled production design and Gladice John designed the costumes. The music comes from Music Masters of PNG and the Murat Choir. The cast includes Freddy Sutchiff, Candy Ali, Daniel Lee Hird, Kevin Jackson, Jamie Napo, Calvin, Alan Balalua, Frank Eke and Dolvin Aite.

NAFA Productions wants the film to bow at a top-tier international festival in 2027. It also hopes the film will be Papua New Guinea’s official entry for best international feature at the 100th Academy Awards.

Damodaran has made that run once before. His earlier PNG–India co-production “Papa Buka” was the first film Papua New Guinea ever submitted to the Oscars’ international feature race.
