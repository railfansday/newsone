Clearpool’s move from CPOOL to CLEAR is reaching the point where a project timetable becomes an exchange-access issue. Its self-custody migration opens on November 17, while some trading venues have scheduled restrictions before that date.

The distinction matters because holders do not all follow the same process. A token in a personal wallet and a balance on an exchange may represent similar market exposure, but the organizations responsible for converting them are different.

Clearpool’s November 12 migration guide sets a one-for-one conversion and a February 17, 2027 deadline. It identifies CLEAR as an ERC-20 token on Ethereum. The guide separates holders using their own wallets from those whose exchanges will handle the migration.

That separation is more useful than treating the announcement as a universal instruction to transfer tokens. Exchange support, snapshot rules and the blockchain on which a balance is held can affect the appropriate process.

The guide is the informational starting point for verifying those arrangements. The existence of a public migration should not make unsolicited messages or lookalike websites credible. A legitimate project announcement can be copied by an impersonator without changing its appearance very much.

KuCoin’s notice schedules CPOOL deposits and withdrawals to close at 04:00 UTC on November 16, followed by trading bots at 07:00 and the CPOOL/USDT market at 08:00. The exchange says it will convert eligible balances automatically.

Its notice includes specific eligibility conditions: a minimum of 0.1 CPOOL and covered spot-account balances. Pending deposits or withdrawals at the snapshot are excluded. It also warns that later deposits of the old token will not be supported.

These details show why the project deadline alone is insufficient. An exchange can stop accepting an asset months before the broader self-custody window ends. Its reopening schedule for the replacement asset is another separate milestone, which KuCoin says will be announced after the swap.

A holder exchanging 1,000 old tokens for 1,000 new tokens preserves the number of units in that transaction. The conversion does not establish the new market price, total supply or the holder’s percentage interest in the network’s token economy.

Clearpool’s September expansion proposal paired the migration with treasury recapitalization and additional allocations. That document is background to the economic design; the November migration guide supplies the operational dates. The two should be read for their different purposes.

NewsBTC’s examination of Aave’s proposed division of lending revenue illustrates a related analytical point: an administrative parameter or token count is only one part of the underlying economics. Funding, incentives and realized activity still determine how a protocol develops.

Clearpool has linked its broader plans to institutional credit and expansion toward the XRP Ledger. Completing a token conversion would be an operational milestone, while launching products and attracting borrowers would require separate evidence.

That distinction also appears in NewsBTC’s reporting on financing tokenized collateral. A working token or interface is one component; enforceable claims, usable liquidity and dependable servicing make the financial product useful.

For this week’s transition, the immediate questions are concrete: whether exchanges apply their published rules, when replacement balances appear and when the new markets reopen. Those answers will reveal the quality of the migration more clearly than a change of ticker alone.

Paul Rudd And Seann William Scott's R-Rated Comedy Is Worth Revisiting 18 Years Later

📅 Nov 16, 2026  |  Category: Entertainment  |  Read Source →

Studios abandoned big comedies years ago. But in the 2000s and early 2010s, we really had it good. This was the age of the Judd Apatow comedy, in which the director not only delivered such modern classics as "The 40-Year-Old Virgin" and "Knocked Up," but influenced almost every comedy released during the era. Alongside the main Apatow offerings you had those which he produced such as "Anchorman: The Legend of Ron Burgundy," or those made by his collaborators, such as "Superbad." Then, there were those films that featured Apatow associates but were made without his direct involvement. The brilliant "I Love You, Man" is one example which, despite being criminally overlooked, remains one of Paul Rudd's best films.

As does 2008's "Role Models," which saw Rudd team up with Seann William Scott for a film that, like pretty much every comedy movie released at the time, was defined by the improvisational, R-rated humor that had made Apatow's films so successful. This particular movie, however, paired Apatow allies Rudd and Christopher Mintz-Plasse with former members of sketch comedy troupe The State in director David Wain and writer/actor Ken Marino.

As someone who watched "Role Models" when it came out, I simply cannot believe this movie is 18 years old. Despite being less well known than the aforementioned "Knocked Up" and its ilk, "Role Models" became instantly beloved by me and my friends upon its release. Almost two decades later, it's strange to think that an entire generation has grown up without films such as this shaping their sense of humor. If you happen to be part of that group, now's the time to acquaint yourself with this  oft-overlooked comedy. If, like me, you remember "Role Models" fondly, you'll be happy to hear it holds up.

Like so many of the best comedies of the 2000s, "Role Models" succeeds because despite being an R-rated comedy with frequent gross-out gags and unapologetically immature humor, it's also a surprisingly tender story with its heart in the right place.

Paul Rudd plays Danny who, alongside Seann William Scott's Anson Wheeler, works as a promoter for the energy drink "Minotaur." Danny feels deeply unfulfilled and has become deeply cynical as a result, spending most of his time correcting people on their speech. This causes his girlfriend, Beth (Elizabeth Banks), to break up with him, after which Danny loses it during a presentation at a high school. His freak out leads to both he and Anson being assigned to community service at Sturdy Wings, a big brother-style program where the pair become mentors to two "littles."

Anson is paired with Ronnie (Bobb'e J. Thompson) a foul-mouthed kid with no friends, while Danny is given Augie Farks (Christopher Mintz-Plasse), a teenager obsessed with his medieval LARP group. At first, Danny and Anson are merely going through the motions. But over time they develop a bond with their "littles" — one which just might melt Danny's cold, cynical heart.

The secret star of "Role Models" is Joe Lo Truglio, who plays Kuzzik, a particularly dedicated LARP-er who, if you watch the bloopers, made it almost impossible for Rudd to get through any of his scenes without breaking. But there are a ton of standout performances here, from A.D. Miles as overly earnest Sturdy Wings counsellor Martin Gary to Ken Jeong as the self-serious LARP king Argotron and Jane Lynch as a former addict turned Sturdy Wings leader.

"Role Models" hit theaters in November 2008 and grossed a solid $92.6 million on a $28 million budget. It also garnered positive reviews, earning a 77% "certified fresh" rating on Rotten Tomatoes. Dana Stevens of Slate found "Role Models" to be "strewn with enough serendipities to make it a worthy variation on the dudes-grow-up genre," while the San Francisco Chronicle's Peter Hartlaub noted how, while Seann William Scott and Paul Rudd were "from two different moviemaking cliques" they ultimately "get along fine in a Bing Crosby/David Bowie kind of way."

Meanwhile, Rafer Guzman of Newsday noted how Rudd, who co-wrote the script with David Wain, Timothy Dowling, and Ken Marino, had "clearly learned a few things working on Judd Apatow comedies." By that point, Rudd had appeared in Apatow's "The 40-Year-Old Virgin" and "Knocked Up," as well as several Apatow-produced projects including "Anchorman" and "Forgetting Sarah Marshall." As such, he was well-established as one of the most prominent figures of the 2000s R-rated comedy boom.

But The State boys certainly brought their own flair to the film and the pairing of theirs and Rudd's sensibility along with Scott's lovable knucklehead charm made for a hilarious and surprisingly heartwarming experience that deserves to be remembered as fondly as some of the more well-known comedies of the era. If you fancy giving one of the most underrated buddy comedies of the 2000s another go, or are yet to see the "Role Models," the movie is available to watch for free on Pluto TV.

OpenAI releases a new image classifier and ChatGPT warnings to combat CSAM

📅 Nov 16, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

OpenAI today announced new tools to combat child sexual abuse material (CSAM) amid growing safety concerns around the use of AI tools. These features include a new image classifier to detect CSAM, warnings and temporary blocks to prevent users from creating or accessing content that sexualizes children, a new guideline for developers, and a set of tools for law enforcement.

The company unveiled a new image classifier that can detect CSAM images, including the ones generated by AI. OpenAI is giving select companies access to its classifier through its API at no cost. The tool looks for visual signs of abuse in images not seen before and assigns a score that can give an indicative direction to reviewers about dealing with CSAM.

OpenAI said that it developed this classifier with the National Center for Missing and Exploited Children (NCMEC), and it is working with Tech Coalition and ROOST to share access to the tool. In its early testing with partners, the AI company found that the classifier flags 48% fewer images while finding the same number of abusive images, reducing the work of reviewers. Plus, images flagged by the classifier are twice as likely to be abusive images.

What’s more, the company is adding new warning screens in ChatGPT to prevent users from accessing material that sexualizes children. ChatGPT will show an alert to users attempting such prompts, direct users towards specialized support to stop harmful behaviour, and also suspend the account for 24 hours as a warning. The app already blocks CSAM content and requests related to it. It said that repeated offences could lead to the account being removed permanently.

OpenAI noted that it formed the message and guidelines around this behavior with organizations and researchers, including Protect Children, the Lucy Faithfull Foundation, and Joel Scanlan at the University of Tasmania, who independently evaluated a trial of deterrent messages.

The company is also publishing guidelines for developers using OpenAI tools to prevent, detect, and help users report CSAM content. It said that in early testing, the guidelines have helped a number of developers adopt child safety tooling and register to report CSAM to NCMEC. OpenAI is also equipping law enforcement dealing with child safety with AI-powered features like dedicated plugins, investigative tools, and complex data analysis, with access managed by program administrators. It is also providing training to investigators along with a field guide on how to use AI.

OpenAI has had several lawsuits against the company blaming its technology for harming users, especially children. Saftey-focused nonprofit Common Sense Media labeled OpenAI’s tech as “unacceptable risk” for teens. Other AI chatbots — specifically Grok — have been used to create nonconsensual sexualized imagery, including of children.

The AI startup had made changes in the last 12 months to its technology including releasing a safety blueprint, updating content guidelines to bar inappropritate content, retuning its model to prevent them from generating harmful content, and releasing a special version of ChatGPT for teens.
