Meta is testing Direct, a standalone messaging app for Instagram

📅 Nov 17, 2026  |  Category: Other  |  Read Source →

It’s only available in Turkey and Thailand for now.

Meta is working on a dedicated messaging app for Instagram that could also expand the reach of the app's DMs. The company is starting to test Direct, a new standalone app that brings Instagram's inbox to a separate app, but doesn't require an existing Instagram account to use. Direct will only be available in Thailand and Turkey for now, Meta said in an update.

Direct looks pretty similar to Instagram's inbox at first glance. It has an almost identical layout, with existing chats carrying over from the main app if you log in with your Instagram credentials. It even has some of the same personalization features, like the ability to set custom backgrounds for individual threads and Instagram's status update feature, Notes.

But while Instagram users can log in with their existing accounts, Direct doesn't actually require an Instagram login. People can also sign up for Direct with their email address or phone number, and they'll still be able to message friends in the app. However, it sounds like messaging without a linked Instagram account won't be quite as convenient. Meta notes that users without Instagram credentials will need to manually add contacts to the app via their Instagram username or a QR code in order to start conversations.

Meta says it's also bringing some of Instagram's safety features to Direct. The app won't allow people to start chats with accounts they aren't already connected with via their contacts. And parents will be able to monitor who their teen is chatting with and how much time they spend in the app if they use the company's parental control features. Additionally, Meta says it will "use technology to detect and act on potentially inappropriate interactions with young people," though it's vague about what exactly that will entail.

The test of Direct is an interesting move for Meta. Instagram head Adam Mosseri often notes that messaging is Instagram's most-used feature. But he was also reluctant to give Threads its own inbox because it seemed "redundant" to use multiple apps to reach the same people when Meta briefly considered bringing Instagram's inbox to Threads. He said at the time that mirroring the Instagram inbox could cause "notification routing weirdness" and generally seemed like a less than ideal experience for most people.

The app also raises questions about whether Direct would compete with WhatsApp in the countries where it's rolled out. It's unlikely an Instagram-centric app would replace WhatsApp for many people, but it could eat into that app's engagement if non-Instagram users can use Direct to message friends instead.

That may also be why Direct seems fairly limited for now. Meta describes it as a test that will only be available to people in Turkey and Thailand for the time being. But if the app is a success, it's likely the company will consider expanding it to more places.

The important Gmail security settings you should be using

📅 Nov 17, 2026  |  Category: Other  |  Read Source →

Having your Google account compromised can be a huge deal.

Gmail is no longer just an email service. Plenty of websites and apps let you log in with your Google account, and many people use that one login for almost everything they do online. It makes signing in a lot easier. It also means that whoever holds the keys to your account holds a lot more than just your private emails.

Google is very aware of that, which is why it offers a variety of security and privacy settings to keep your account safe and keep third parties out of it. These are some of the most important ones you should be using.

Start with Security Checkup; it's a page in your Google Account that gives you personalized recommendations, like adding a recovery phone number and email. Those might sound like very basic steps, but they matter more than you might think: Google uses them to alert you to suspicious activity and to help you get back in if someone locks you out. From google.com, click your profile icon in the upper right, then click Manage your Google Account. Click Security & sign-in. At the top of the page, you'll see an icon. A green shield means nothing needs your attention right now. A blue, yellow or red exclamation mark means there's something to fix.

The single biggest fix is two-step verification, also known as two-factor authentication. With it on, a stolen password isn't enough, because signing in also takes something you physically have on you, like your phone or your own computer. To switch it on, go to Security & sign-in, find the How you sign in to Google section, select Turn on 2-Step Verification and follow the prompts.

Then there are passkeys, which let you sign in with your fingerprint, face scan or screen lock instead of a password. A passkey can't be shared, copied or written down, so it holds up against phishing. To create one, go to your account's passkey page and select Create a passkey. Only do it on devices you own; once a passkey exists, anyone who can unlock that device can get into your account.

Locking the front door doesn't help if someone already has a key, so check who and what is connected. Go to myaccount.google.com, open Security & sign-in and scroll down to the linked apps section, then select See all linked apps. You'll see the apps and sites you use Sign in with Google for, plus anything that has been given access to your account data. Anything you don't recognize or no longer use can go: select Remove access, then Confirm.

Next, look at Gmail itself. An attacker who gets in often changes a few quiet settings so they can keep reading your mail later. Open Gmail on your computer, click the gear icon in the top right and select See all settings. Then check for anything unfamiliar under automatic forwarding, filters, labels, scheduled emails, your vacation responder, and IMAP or POP access. Mail delegation, which lets someone else read, send and delete email in your account, has its own spot: go to the Accounts and Import tab and look under Grant access to your account. If a name is there that you didn't add, click Delete next to it. You can't manage delegates from the Gmail app, only on the desktop site. Automatic forwarding is the nasty one. It sends a copy of every new message, except spam, to another address, and you'd never notice. If you find a forwarding rule you didn't set up, change your password right away and turn it off.

The devices page is the other place to look. It lives in your Google Account, not in Gmail's settings: go to myaccount.google.com, open Security & sign-in (on a computer it may show up as just Security in the left-hand menu), find Your devices and select Manage all devices. You'll see everywhere you're signed in or have been in the last few weeks. Watch for a device you don't recognize, a location you weren't in or a browser you don't normally use. Take a moment to think about it, though: an airport, a library computer or a phone you recently reset can all look odd. If something really isn't yours, select the device and tap Sign out.

Once the settings are in place, a few habits keep them working. Keep two-step verification on, and never share a verification code with anyone. Google guarantees it won't call you to ask for one. Run Security Checkup again every now and then, since recommendations change and old devices and apps pile up.

In general, keep an eye out for any suspicious activity, or even lack of activity. For example, you stop receiving mail, friends tell you they got spam from you, there are emails sent that you didn't write or messages vanish from your inbox without landing in Trash. If any of that sounds familiar, change your password first, then work through the devices, apps and Gmail settings above.

What Do We Do With Leftover Love?

📅 Nov 16, 2026  |  Category: Other  |  Read Source →

After years or decades, problems and patterns can become deeply entrenched. Be a fly on the wall during a sample couples therapy session and witness the slow, deliberate work of changing those patterns for a more enduring love.

Self Tests are all about you. Are you outgoing or introverted? Are you a narcissist? Does perfectionism hold you back? Find out the answers to these questions and more with Psychology Today.

Posted November 16, 2026 | Reviewed by Ekua Hagan

He was everything you had ever wanted. Kind, gentle, thoughtful. Almost too good to be true. You imagined a future together, a home, a family, a lifetime of ordinary mornings. Then, one day, while making eggs for breakfast, you saw the messages. The pan was still warm. The sun was coming through the window. Nothing around you had changed, yet it's all gone. In a matter of seconds, the man you thought you knew disappeared. The future you had imagined vanished with him. But the love you felt still lingers.

You can throw the eggs into the bin, but the feelings cannot disappear as quickly. Love involves several interconnected brain systems. Dopamine-rich reward pathways contribute to desire and anticipation (Fisher et al., 2010); the brain's natural opioid system helps make closeness comforting and social rejection distressing (Hsu et al., 2013; Nummenmaa et al., 2016); and limbic regions, particularly the amygdala, help connect emotional experiences with memory (Cahill et al., 1996). The truth can change what you think in seconds, but it cannot instantly erase what your brain has learned over months or years, so do not let people tell you that you are "co-dependent" or "weak." You have a normal brain that might play tricks on you.

You have leftover love, and that leftover love can be weaponised against you (Lesiak & Gelsthorpe, 2025). He may use the feelings that remain to persuade you to let him stay, asking you to ignore what you have discovered, silence your judgment, and compromise the very values that define who you are.

He has already betrayed you. Now he is asking you to betray yourself for him.

You are the one expected to swallow your anger, silence your judgment, and call it "forgiveness." Look at the bargain. He gets to keep the woman who loves him. And you are expected to give up the woman who knows the truth.

Decades ago, psychiatrist Judith Herman wrote about it in Trauma and Recovery (1992):

His ultimate goal appears to be the creation of a willing victim. Hostages, political prisoners, battered women, and slaves have all remarked upon the captor’s curious psychological dependence upon his victim. George Orwell gives voice to the totalitarian mind in the novel 1984:

“We are not content with negative obedience, nor even with the most abject submission. When finally you surrender to us, it must be of your own free will. We do not destroy the heretic because he resists us; so long as he resists us we never destroy him. We convert him, we capture his inner mind, we reshape him. We burn all evil and all illusion out of him; we bring him over to our side, not in appearance, but genuinely, heart and soul.”

Read those last words again. Heart and soul.

It is not enough that you stay. You must want to stay. It is not enough that you forgive him. You must learn to see his betrayal as something you can live with. You must look at the evidence of your own eyes and persuade yourself that it no longer matters.

And if keeping him means abandoning everything you believe in, what exactly are you trying to save—and what's left—when you do that?

Some wounds are easier to bear because you know you stood by your values while receiving them. In Kraków, officers Jakub Kulik and Artur Morański were seriously wounded confronting a mass shooter. Poland calls them heroes, and people across the country are grateful for their courage and service.

Then there is another kind of wound. One that no surgeon can stitch. Amy Winehouse sings it in "You Know I'm No Good": “I cheated myself, like I knew I would.” By the end of the song, she is no longer simply confessing what she did. She is passing judgment on herself.

Imagine waking up beside a man you no longer respect, living a life you never wanted, having persuaded yourself to accept things you once knew were unacceptable. You kept him, perhaps. But somewhere along the way, you lost the ability to trust your own judgment.

That is the deeper cruelty of weaponised love (Lesiak & Gelsthorpe, 2025). He does not need you to believe he is innocent. He needs you to stop caring that he isn't.

And if manipulation or coercion brought you to that point, the shame belongs to the person who engineered that surrender, not to you.

Name the man, not the fantasy. The betrayal wasn't simply another woman. It was the months of lies that made you believe you were building a future together while he was living another life. He needed you to trust him because, had you known the truth, you might have walked away. So he made sure you didn't.

That is what a programme of deception does. It robs you of the freedom to make informed choices about your own life. When used to dominate and subordinate, deception becomes a tool of coercive control and psychological abuse. Stop reaching for words that make him sound better. A liar is a liar. A traitor is a traitor. A backstabber is a backstabber. Grieve the man he pretended to be, the image he created to win your love and use it to control you. But don't defend the man behind the mask.

The eggs went into the bin that morning. The relationship ended. The feelings are disappearing. You'll be ok! Probably you are already more ok now than when you were cheated on, lied to, and abused.

When many of us think "hot breakfast," we tend to naturally gravitate toward thoughts of scrambled eggs. Aside from the fact that, when prepared correctly, they are tender, delicate, and fluffy (or custardy, depending on how you like them), eggs are considered the gold standard for protein, so they are quite wholesome as well. On their own, though, they can get a little monotonous, especially if you're making them every day, so it does help to add an ingredient here or there. And if you happen to enjoy omega-3-rich cold-smoked salmon, well, it pairs seamlessly with a classic scramble, boosting both the flavor and the protein content.

Once considered an expensive delicacy, smoked salmon is quite popular now in the U.S., and as a result, it's much easier to find at your local grocery store (even Aldi carries its own version, and it can be found at Walmart, too). Cold-smoked salmon, despite the fact that it tends to be packed in super-thin slices, is buttery and velvety. While it does taste fishy, there are also light, smoky notes that give it a deeply savory flavor, which works beautifully when combined with the mild egg scramble. Additionally, a serving (which will vary in size) might contain anywhere from 12 to 18 grams of protein, which is great if you have daily goals you want to meet or you're just looking to get more of the macronutrient in general.

Truly, the most difficult part about incorporating smoked salmon into your scramble is cooking the eggs to the right consistency without burning or browning them. All you have to do with the salmon is slice the ultra-thin pieces into slivers or small squares. Once your eggs have reached the texture you desire, you'll take the pan off the heat and fold in the salmon. That's literally it; you don't have to actually cook the salmon over direct heat because it's so delicate that the residual warmth of the scrambled eggs will gently bring it up to temperature.

Now, because smoked salmon is so light and thin, you might want your eggs to match that energy, so create a fluffy, soft texture with a bit of cream whisked in. Alternatively, you could take a page out of Bobby Flay's book and fold in a dollop of crème fraîche, or really up the decadence and melt in some cubes of cream cheese (and take care not to overcook anything, because smoked salmon with burnt, rubbery eggs? Also, because all of the elements involved are either rich or thick, a sprinkling of something green, like fresh chives or dill, or even a little parsley or tarragon, would brighten things up, both aesthetically and in terms of flavor.

The simple and convenient Apple Maps feature every CarPlay user should know about

📅 Nov 16, 2026  |  Category: Other  |  Read Source →

Let your friends know what time you'll be arriving.

For many iPhone users, Apple CarPlay has become synonymous with a quality driving experience, from playing their favorite tunes to navigating to their next destination. And although Apple continues to upgrade its popular infotainment system, one of its most useful features has been hidden in Apple Maps for years and is critical to letting your family, friends, and, if you are like me, enemies know when you will arrive at your destination.

I am, of course, referring to sharing your estimated time of arrival (ETA) through Apple Maps. Most users are probably familiar with Apple's Find My and location-sharing functions, but CarPlay's Share ETA feature is woefully underappreciated. Both simple to use and highly customizable, the feature is a godsend for worried parents and surprise parties everywhere.

To enable Share ETA, go to Settings > Apps > Maps. From here, scroll down to Share ETA and enable the feature. You can also enable location sharing in Messages, Contacts and Find My. You may also want to turn on Siri to make sharing an ETA more convenient. To do so, go to your iPhone's Settings, tap Siri and enable Turn on Siri.

Sharing your ETA is a simple way to improve your CarPlay experience. If you're using Siri, you can initiate a voice command when you're following Maps directions, either by holding the voice command button on your steering wheel or saying "Hey Siri" if your car supports voice activation. Next, say "share my ETA with (contact name)."

If you would rather not use Apple's voice assistant, you can share your ETA directly in Maps. After you've set off, tap the route card at the bottom of the screen, then Share ETA and choose a recipient. If the contact isn't on the list, you can find them under Open Contacts. To share with more than one person, tap Sharing with at the bottom of the screen, Open Contacts and select your intended recipients. You can also stop sharing your ETA by opening the route card again, tapping Sharing with, selecting Stop Sharing ETA and choosing the contact you want to stop sharing with.

You can also automatically share your ETA with someone by adding their contact to a pinned location. First, go to Maps > Places > Pinned. Next, tap the blue information button > Add Person. Next, tap the desired contact and confirm the selection. Now, whenever you select the pinned location in Apple Maps and tap Go, it will automatically share your ETA.

Grammy Awards: All the Artists Who’ve Been Nominated in the Big 4 Categories in the Same Year

📅 Nov 16, 2026  |  Category: Other  |  Read Source →

Remarkably, 13 of these 17 artists are women.

When the nominations for the 69th annual Grammy Awards were announced on Monday (Nov. 16), Ella Langley became the 17th artist to receive nominations in each of the so-called “Big Four” Grammy categories – album, record and song of the year plus best new artist – in the same year. Remarkably, 13 of those 17 artists are women. How to account for that? Grammy voters seem to like to give extra encouragement to talented young women artists.

Langley is the first country artist to be nominated in each of the Big Four categories since Bobbie Gentry in 1968, who was the first artist from any genre to achieve the feat.

Three times in recent years, two artists achieved the feat in the same year. Billie Eilish and Lizzo both achieved the feat in 2020. Olivia Rodrigo and FINNEAS both achieved it in 2022. Chappell Roan and Sabrina Carpenter both achieved it in 2025.

This has happened more often in recent years because the number of nominees in each of the Big Four categories has expanded. Until the Grammy ceremony held in 2019, there were generally five nominees in each of those categories. The upcoming 69th annual Grammy Awards, set for Sunday (Feb. 7), will mark the ninth year in a row there have been eight or more nominees in each of those categories. There were 10 at the ceremonies in 2022 and 2023, but the Recording Academy (mercifully) brought it back down to eight in 2024.

All but one of the 16 previous artists to receive nods in each of the Big Four categories had a spotlight performance slot on the Grammy telecast. The exception was FINNEAS, and even he was featured in sister Eilish’s performance of “Happier Than Ever” on the 2022 telecast.

Here’s a complete list of artists who have been nominated in each of the top four Grammy categories in the same year. The years shown are the years of the Grammy ceremonies.

Gentry, who was 25 at the time, was nominated for her debut album Ode to Billie Joe and the intriguing story song that was its title track. She won best new artist and in two other categories – best vocal performance, female and best contemporary female solo vocal performance. Gentry hasn’t won another Grammy since that night, though she was nominated for her 1970 album Fancy.

Cross, then 29, was nominated for his eponymous debut album and his hypnotic ballad “Sailing.” He won in all four categories and for best arrangement accompanying vocalist(s). Cross received three more nominations the following year for “Arthur’s Theme (Best That You Can Do),” though he hasn’t added to his trophy collection since his big night.

Lauper, then 31, was nominated for her first solo album, She’s So Unusual — and, in a unique occurrence, two different works for record and song of the year, respectively. The zesty “Girls Just Want to Have Fun” was nominated for record; the poignant “Time After Time,” which Lauper co-wrote with Rob Hyman, was nominated for song. She won best new artist. She won a second Grammy 29 years later – best musical theater album for Kinky Boots.

Chapman, then 24, was nominated for her eponymous debut album and her surprise hit, “Fast Car.” Chapman, the first Black artist to be nominated in each of the Big Four categories in one year, won best new artist and in two other categories – best pop vocal performance, female and best contemporary folk recording. She won a fourth Grammy eight years later for “Give Me One Reason.”

Carey, then 20, was nominated for her eponymous debut album and her sleek ballad “Vision of Love,” which she co-wrote with Ben Margulies. She won best new artist and best pop vocal performance, female. She has since won three additional Grammys.

Cole, then 29, was nominated for her debut album, This Fire, and her hit “Where Have All the Cowboys Gone?” She won best new artist. She had seven nominations that night, including producer of the year, non-classical, but hasn’t even been nominated since that night.

The singer, then 26, was nominated for her debut album, Acoustic Soul, and her single, “Video.” She co-wrote the song with Carlos “Six July” Broady, Reginald Harris and Shannon Sanders. India.Arie was the first artist to receive nods in each of the Big Four categories but not win in any of them. She went 0-7 that year (though she came back to win two Grammys the following year and two more in the years since then).

Winehouse, then 24, was nominated for her second (and final) studio album Back to Black and her retro-sounding hit “Rehab.” Winehouse, the first English artist to be nominated in each of the Big Four categories in the same year, won for record and song of the year, best new artist and in two other categories – best pop vocal album and best female pop vocal performance. Winehouse won a sixth Grammy four years later for “Body and Soul,” a duet with Tony Bennett. Winehouse died in 2011 at age 27.

The trio — the only group to be nominated in each of the Big Four categories in the same year — was nominated for its second (and still most recent) album, Some Nights, and its power ballad “We Are Young,” featuring Janelle Monáe. The trio, consisting of Jack Antonoff, Andrew Dost and Nate Ruess, co-wrote the smash with its producer, Jeff Bhasker. fun. won song of the year and best new artist. Antonoff has since won 12 additional Grammys.

Smith, then 22, was nominated for their debut album In the Lonely Hour and for the “Darkchild Version” of the smash hit “Stay with Me” (which Smith co-wrote with James Napier and William Phillips). Smith won record and song of the year, best new artist and best pop vocal album. Eight years later, they won a fifth Grammy for “Unholy,” a record-setting collab with Kim Petras.

Eilish, then 17, was nominated for her first full-length album, When We All Fall Asleep, Where Do We Go? and her quirky hit “bad guy” (which she co-wrote with her producer/brother, FINNEAS). Eilish was the youngest artist to receive nods in each of the Big Four categories in the same year. She won in all four categories, the first person to do so since Christopher Cross 39 years earlier. Eilish won a fifth Grammy that night, best pop vocal album, and has since won five additional Grammys.

Lizzo, then 31, was nominated for the deluxe edition of her third album, Cuz I Love You, and her genre-blending smash “Truth Hurts” (which she co-wrote with her producer Ricky Reed, Steven Cheung and Jesse Saint John). She lost in each of the Big Four categories to Eilish but still managed to win three awards that night – best pop solo performance (in which she managed to beat Eilish), best urban contemporary album and best traditional R&B performance. Three years later, she won record of the year for “About Damn Time.”

FINNEAS, then 24, was nominated as a writer and producer on Eilish’s sophomore album, Happier Than Ever, and its title track and also for his work on the deluxe “Triple Chucks” edition of Justin Bieber’s Justice. He was nominated for best new artist for work on his debut studio album Optimist and its lead single “What They’ll Say About Us.” He didn’t win in any of the Big Four categories that year, but not to worry: He has amassed 11 Grammys, one more even than his superstar sister.

Rodrigo, then 18, was nominated for her debut album Sour and its smash hit “Driver’s License.” She won for best new artist, as well as best pop vocal album and best pop solo performance.

Carpenter, then 25, received an album of the year nod for Short n’ Sweet, a record of the year nod for “Espresso” and a song of the year nod for “Please Please Please.” Short n’ Sweet was also nominated for best pop vocal album. “Espresso” was also nominated for best pop solo performance. Carpenter won in the latter two categories.

Roan, then 26, received an album of the year nod for her first full-length album, The Rise and Fall of a Midwest Princess, and record and song of the year nods for its breakout hit “Good Luck, Babe!.” The Rise and Fall… was also nominated for best pop vocal album. “Good Luck, Babe!” was also nominated for best pop solo performance.

Langley, 27, received an album of the year nod for her sophomore set, Dandelion, and record and song of the year nods for its megahit “Choosin’ Texas,” which set a new record for most weeks at No. 1 on the Billboard 200. Langley is also nominated for best country solo performance, best country song and best contemporary country album.

Instagram will let creators add Amazon affiliate links directly to their posts and Reels

📅 Nov 16, 2026  |  Category: Other  |  Read Source →

The app will become even more of a shopping mall, just in time for Black Friday.

Meta has a new gift for Instagram creators just in time for Black Friday and the holiday shopping season. The company is making it easier for influencers to promote Amazon affiliate products directly in their posts without having to rely on a link in bio service.

US creators who are also in Amazon's influencer program will now be able to add dozens of affiliate links directly to their Reels and Instagram posts, Meta announced. The links will take viewers directly to the Amazon app and creators will earn their usual commission from any purchase made via their content.

Instagram has enabled product tagging and shoppable posts for some time. But the company previously required that tagged products in Reels already exist in Meta's commerce catalog. Now, creators can instead link their Amazon account directly to Instagram and add up to 30 products in a single video (there's a 20-product limit for feed posts). The Instagram app will also show creators what commission to expect for each item.

For creators, the timing of the new feature is particularly good. Commission from affiliate links is a central part of how many Instagram influencers earn money from their accounts. And posts with products linked directly can generate twice as many clicks as bio links, according to Meta. And while the company won't take a cut of creators' commissions, these links are no doubt a valuable source of data to Meta about what their users are interested in.

The update is the latest way Meta has made it easier for Instagram influencers to direct users off-platform. The company has also started allowing creators to add clickable links to captions and Reels if they pay for certain Meta One subscription plans. Those paid tiers also come with expanded link pages. Up to now, the company's restrictions on link-sharing have led many creators to depend on separate "link in bio" services. Now, it seems Meta is increasingly interested in offering those tools to creators directly.

For now, Amazon affiliate linking is only available to creators in the United States who have a professional account on Instagram and are 18 or older. The company hasn't said if it plans to expand the feature to creators in more countries.

Thomas Frank returns to the Premier League with Crystal Palace after being sacked by Tottenham in February; he will replace Pierre Sage at Selhurst Park; Palace are bottom of the table with just seven points from their first 10 games

Crystal Palace have appointed Thomas Frank as their new manager.

The Dane replaces Pierre Sage, who was dismissed on November 12 following a seventh league defeat of the season to Liverpool.

Frank has signed a deal until the summer of 2029.

Frank was among a list of names that Palace considered in the summer before Sage was appointed to the role.

Other candidates that Palace were interested in included Frank Lampard, Sean Dyche, Vitor Pereira, Kim Hellberg, Vitor Matos, Tony Popovic, Jens Berthel Askou and Ole Werner.

Frank had great success at Brentford during his seven-year spell between 2018 and 2025, getting the Bees into the Premier League and then establishing them as a top-flight club, reaching a ninth-place finish in 2023.

He then moved to Tottenham last season to replace Ange Postecoglou, but was sacked after less than eight months in charge with the club in 16th place, five points above the relegation zone.
