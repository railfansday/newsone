Ciryl Gane Reacts to Josh Hokit Leapfrogging Alexander Volkov for UFC 334 Title Shot

📅 Nov 08, 2026  |  Category: Other  |  Read Source →

Ciryl Gane is just days away from his next heavyweight title defense, with Josh Hokit set to challenge him in the main event of UFC 334. The matchup came together after Tom Aspinall was forced to vacate the championship following further complications with his right eye, which had been injured again during his recovery.

Hokit will enter Madison Square Garden with an unbeaten 10-0 record and considerable momentum. His recent victories over Curtis Blaydes and a 41-year-old Derrick Lewis have helped propel him into the title picture.

However, his rapid rise to a championship opportunity has raised questions about whether he has done enough to leapfrog more established contenders. Alexander Volkov is among those who have every reason to feel overlooked.

The Russian heavyweight has won seven of his last eight fights, with his only defeat during that stretch coming against Gane at UFC 310. Even that result remains a point of contention among fans, many of whom believe Volkov deserved the decision.

Dana White had promised to address the situation following their controversial encounter, but the expected opportunity never materialized. Instead, Hokit has landed the next shot at Gane, leaving Volkov on the outside once again. Interestingly, even the champion appears puzzled by the promotion's decision.

During a recent interview with RMC Sport Combat, Gane was asked whether Hokit deserved to be fighting for the title ahead of Volkov. The Frenchman acknowledged that the American's personality may have played a significant role in securing the opportunity.

However, he also admitted that he would be frustrated if he were in the position of the contenders who had been overlooked.

He said, "If I put myself in the shoes of the guys in the top five, I wouldn't be happy. Naturally, I wouldn't be happy. I'd be thinking, 'How's this possible?' I've been here for a while, I've earned my spot."

Gane also brought up his own history with Volkov, acknowledging that their second meeting was far closer than the result suggested. He added, "You see the first Volkov fight I won, the second fight was super close, borderline controversial, practically a draw. I still deserve my shot to go there, you know? I understand the frustration and the desire to talk on social media. To defend yourself. But, you have to understand the game."

However, Gane believes that simply maintaining a strong position in the rankings is not always enough to secure opportunities in the UFC. According to the champion, fighters need spectacular finishes and memorable performances to strengthen their negotiating position.

He further explained, "Without that, you can't negotiate. If you have lackluster fights, kind of whatever, you need a great, pristine record or have lost to guys like that. It's all pawns. That's how it is."

Volkov's situation could have looked considerably different had the judges awarded him the split decision against Gane in their 2024 meeting. Instead, the Russian has spent considerable time trying to work his way back into title contention.

His recent victory over Rizvan Kuniev at UFC 333 has at least kept him in the conversation. With Gane and Hokit now preparing to settle the heavyweight championship at UFC 334, Volkov will be hoping for an opportunity to finally fight for undisputed gold against whoever emerges victorious at Madison Square Garden.

Liam Boyd's big boot brings Paul Bunyan back to Michigan State

📅 Nov 08, 2026  |  Category: Other  |  Read Source →

ANN ARBOR — Not all five-field-goal games are created equally.

Five against Toledo, nice. Five against Michigan, better. Much, much better.

"Yeah, for sure," fifth-year senior kicker Liam Boyd said with a shy smile following Michigan State's 15-6 win over Michigan at Michigan Stadium on Saturday, Nov. 7. "This means a ton. This game does.

"This is awesome. There's not really words for how I feel right now."

No worries. Boyd's boot did more than enough talking.

Boyd was 5-for-5 on field goals and accounted for every point as Michigan State pulled the upset, snapped a six-game losing streak and earned head coach Pat Fitzgerald his first Big Ten win in his first year with the Spartans.

Boyd was good from 21, 37, 38, 24 and 49 yards in the victory. Some of them, Fitzgerald could've gone for it on fourth down, but he played the situation game. Michigan State's defense was cooking — Michigan didn't score until there was 1 minute, 35 seconds left — and any points were going to be uber-valuable.

"I didn't want to kick any of those," Fitzgerald said afterward, to some laughter. "I told you guys I will be aggressive but not reckless. ... I thought we had the upper hand defensively. ... We were getting stops.

"If it was a shootout," Fitzgerald continued, "I'd probably play those plays a little differently."

He added, of Boyd, "Full confidence and belief in Liam."

From Boyd's perspective, he also stays with the mindset that he's going to get called in for the field goal, and even quipped that he gets a little disappointed when the offense decides to go for it on fourth down.

On Saturday, Michigan State decided to go with him, and he was prepared.

The team talked all week about how special teams would be a key.

"It's always a super tight game," said Boyd, who transferred to Michigan State this offseason, from Charlotte. "I knew going into it that myself and everybody in my room were going to have a big outcome on the game.

"I expect to make every kick when I go out there. I have that kind of conviction that it's going to go through."

Boyd got off to a fine start with Michigan State, making five of six field-goal attempts in the season-opening win against Toledo, including a 51-yarder. He hasn't had many chances since, going 6-of-8 the last seven games.

But his number (37, if you're scoring at home) got called over and over again against the Wolverines, and he led Michigan State to its first win in the rivalry since 2021.

His five field goals in a single game are one off the program record, set by Jonathan Kim in 2024. He's one of two Spartans to ever make five field goals in a single game twice, joining Paul Edinger, who also did it twice in the same season, in 1998. Edinger did it in a win against Northwestern. Nice. Then he did it the next week, in a win over No. 1 Ohio State. Better. Now, Boyd knows the feeling.

"I love this team and these coaches," said Boyd, who was 10-for-13 on field goals pre-Michigan State (at Charlotte and North Carolina), and now is 16-for-19 this season for the Spartans.

"It's just an awesome win for everybody."

Ripple's Schwartz Speaks Out About His Influence Over XRP Price

📅 2026-11-08  |  Category: Other  |  Read Source →

David Schwartz, former chief technology officer of Ripple and one of the original XRP Ledger architects, has responded to claims that he is partially to blame for XRP's poor performance.

In a recent interaction on the X social media platform, David Schwartz was challenged about his possible impact on the price of the Ripple-linked cryptocurrency.

"Don't run from this. You can honestly say you don't have anything to do with it? It's not unfounded anger," the user wrote.

Although David Schwartz does not entirely deny that he can have an impact on the price of XRP, he is clear that it is minimal.

"Can I honestly say that I have nothing to do with the price of XRP? I guess probably not nothing. But very little," David Schwartz responded.

The response from Schwartz comes in the wake of mounting anger within the XRP community about the token's long-running price slump.

Indeed, XRP has seen roughly a 50% decline in value over the past year, per data from CoinMarketCap. During this period, the token has seen several temporary spikes, but the overall trend has been downward.

Of course, XRP is not the only token experiencing such a dramatic drop in value.

Stellar (XLM), another prominent cryptocurrency that seeks to enable payments, has seen a very similar price trend over the same time period.

The interaction came on the back of a long post in which Schwartz lamented the rise in hostile replies he receives on X.

Schwartz, a Ripple veteran, disclosed that some users are actually following him into other discussions and then proceeding to either label him a fraud or criticize him for his work on XRP.

The Ripple veteran noted that he has now made the decision to block such users, as irrelevant crypto-related posts are disrupting his discussions with other users.

Nonetheless, he stressed that the decision affects both critics and fans. Even if someone makes a positive comment about XRP but in an unsuitable place, they might also get blocked.

He also emphasized that he has no issue with honest criticism or accusations that make sense and come with good arguments.

He is mostly annoyed with users who keep hijacking unrelated discussions to vent their frustration.

In fact, Schwartz even offered to unblock users who commit to not doing so in the future.

Schwartz stepped away from his day-to-day duties at Ripple at the end of 2025. He had worked at the firm for over a decade. However, he has not completely left Ripple.

Earlier this month, Schwartz clarified that he's still on Ripple's board of directors and holds the honorary title of CTO Emeritus.

Furthermore, he disclosed that he still meets with his old team twice a month.

ANALYSIS | How Miro Muheim and Albert Grønbæk were catalysts for Hamburg’s 3-1 win over Borussia Mönchengladbach

📅 Nov 08, 2026  |  Category: Other  |  Read Source →

Hamburg finally won again. On a cold Friday night at the Volksparkstadion, they beat Borussia Mönchengladbach 3–1. These three vital points, coming just before the international break, put them on 12 points, three points adrift of Werder Bremen, who occupy a European spot.

HSV also put in an impressive performance throughout the game. Some might argue that the result was influenced by the fact that Gladbach played with 10 men from the 49th minute onwards.

This did indeed impact the game. However, some important moments in the game may have been evident from the outset.

Kou Itakura’s red card actually seems predictable from the very beginning. The reason is simple. He repeatedly struggled when Albert Grønbæk tried to pass him on the right. Whether it was his own decision or that of the coaching staff, Itakura’s tendency to play as a close defender was only causing him trouble.

The problem is that Itakura is not a fast defender, yet he was playing on the side where HSV were targeting their attack. Unlike Philipp Sander, who played in the centre and used his close proximity and constant pressing to Patson Daka to good effect, Itakura is playing in the right space where his decision to press is not necessarily important.

This was especially the case when they couldn’t find a way to build up play with short passes, as HSV tended to send long passes directly forward, with Daka usually becoming the target. Constant pressing became the right approach.

With such an aggressive approach, it was only a matter of time before Itakura received his second yellow card after getting his first in the first half. He was sent off after just 49 minutes.

Miro Muheim’s goal also seemed destined to happen. He has always had a special talent for shooting, but in this game it was his positioning that made the difference.

From the early game, Muheim tried to move around to find the right space in which to receive the ball from his teammates. He always tried to become the free man for HSV.

He moved diagonally (inside or outside) and was constantly looking to become available in the final third, and so it was no surprise when he had the space to unleash his beautiful shot that it put HSV in the lead for the first time in the match.

Alongside Albert Grønbæk, Muheim was also the key to Merlin Polzin’s strategy of overloading HSV’s left side in the second half. This triggered the second goal – not to mention the fact that they were playing with an extra man.

This overload, combined with intelligent diagonal play, was key to HSV’s second goal. This diagonal play was also something that had already been signalled in the first half. The Red Shorts constantly moved the ball from the outside to the inside, especially when they were able to pull Gladbach’s centre-back out wide, opening up space in the half-space.

For that purpose of diagonal play and exploiting the half-space, Grønbæk was their specialist. He knew exactly how to position himself in the half-space to receive the ball and create opportunities. Their second goal came from a similar situation, with Grønbæk receiving a beautiful diagonal pass from Albert Sambi Lokonga before assisting the homegrown Shafiq Nandja.

6 moments that defined Rob Gronkowski’s Patriots career

📅 Nov 07, 2026  |  Category: Other  |  Read Source →

Rob Gronkowski is a New England Patriots Hall of Famer, and it is not hard to see why. Not only was he an integral part of the organization's Dynasty 2.0 in the 2010s, he also performed at the highest level throughout his nine seasons with the team.

When he left the Patriots after the 2018 season — one capped by his third championship — he did so as the owner of 15 NFL records. While some of those have been broken over the last seven years, his impact on the Patriots and the NFL as a whole is undeniable.

Breaking it down to a handful of moments is therefore a challenging task. This collection is therefore more of a "greatest hits" than a comprehensive journey through his oeuvre.

Despite questions about a back injury and even though he fell asleep during his pre-draft visit to Gillette Stadium, the Patriots forced their luck in the second round of the 2010 NFL Draft. Trading up two spots to leap-frog the Baltimore Ravens, they secured Gronkowski with the 42nd overall selection. That moment, which saw him celebrate on stage with his entire immediate family, already set the tone for what was about to come.

Coming off a solid rookie season, Gronkowski fully broke out in Year 2 to establish himself as the best tight end in the NFL and a player with sky-high potential. There were multiple plays during his impressive 2011 campaign that can be picked as examples, but one stands out above the rest: a 49-yard catch-and-run against Washington that showed his rare blend of speed, power and elusiveness. It was one of the first times in his career that he showed that he was a different animal.

Even with Peyton Manning gone, Patriots vs. Colts was still one of the NFL's best rivalries in the mid-2010s. And one particular matchup stands out in regards to Gronkowski: Week 11 of the 2014 season. He finished the game with a non-conspicuous stat-line of four catches, 71 yards and a touchdown. That score, however, was arguably the most spectacular one of his nine-year career: like a "runaway track," as Al Michaels said on the broadcast, he ran over defenders on his way to turning a short third-down conversion into a 26-yard score. Of course, his performance that day was also remembered for something else.

A true tight end, Gronkowski was a dual-impact player both due to his immense receiving prowess and his abilities as a blocker. His contributions in the second area oftentimes go undervalued, but they also define who he was as a player: a powerful and physical presence at the point of attack. Him blocking standout edge Terrell Suggs 1-on-1 in the 2011 AFC Championship Game is a prime example of that, and so is one of the most re-watched moments of his career, when he threw Colts safety Sergio Brown "out of the club" in 2014.

A large part of Gronkowski's success as a Patriots was not just his physical superiority over his opponents, but also his ability to be on the same page as his quarterback; it is why Tom Brady made sure to recruit him to Tampa Bay to close out both players' careers. The chemistry between the two was on constant display for nine years, but if we had to choose one moment as an example — despite the eventual unsatisfactory outcome — it would be the 2015 AFC Championship Game in Denver. Going against a generational defense, Gronkowski converted two massive fourth downs in the late fourth quarter.

Gronkowski's Patriots story cannot be told without also mentioning the injuries he had to overcome, and his Super Bowl history reflects that. He was severely limited against the Giants in 2012 and unable to play against the Falcons in 2017; he also did have a profound impact on the game in his three other appearances, including, in Super Bowl LIII. That particular game put a perfect bow on his career with the team. Slowed by years of physical challenges, he still managed one last hurrah against the Rams: going up the seam as part of the New England's famous Hoss-Y-Juke concept, he hauled in the biggest catch of the game — one that helped set up the only touchdown in the Patriots' eventual 13-3 victory. It was Gronkowski's final catch as a Patriot, and a worthy send-off.

10 Classic Rock Albums You Must Listen to Before You Die

📅 Nov 04, 2026  |  Category: Other  |  Read Source →

Classic rock is maybe definable as the kind of rock you're likely to hear on classic rock radio stations (unsurprisingly), but a better definition could be narrowing it down to rock released during a certain period. That period was when rock was at its most popular and culturally dominant, and arguably, during that time, rock music was also at its best, so it’s classic rock, and then it also happens to be rock that is classic (in terms of quality and historical importance).

There’s an attempt to single out some particularly great classic rock albums below, all of them worth listening to before you die. Or, less morbidly, listen to them all while you're still breathing; before the bucket’s kicked. That’s still pretty morbid. Sorry. But these albums are amazing, and enough so that you’ve probably heard most of them before, but if you haven’t, they're essential to give a spin at least once before… you know…

It feels uncool to call them Creedence Clearwater Revival instead of just “Creedence,” but either way, here's Cosmo’s Factory, which is probably the band’s best album. Creedence (yeah, let’s be cool for a bit; for fun) is entirely classic rock in sound, really being demonstrative of this particular era of rock, and so sure, Cosmo’s Factory is blunt, certainly straightforward, and pretty easy to listen to quite passively.

Sometimes it’s just what you need, though? Something loud, catchy, and very of its time, but all of those things in good ways. Cosmo’s Factory is strong enough that you might not love Creedence, or the particular kind of rock they made, but you're still likely going to, at the very least, tolerate this particular album. And if you are fond of classic rock and/or Creedence, then it’s safe to say you're going to go well beyond tolerating this, since you'll probably love it.

If there’s one flaw with Damn the Torpedoes, it’s that it starts in a way strong enough that you could argue the momentum isn't entirely made, yet that’s a nitpick, since the album after the first three tracks is still strong. It’s more just that the run from “Refugee” to “Here Comes My Girl” to “Even the Losers” is especially great, with three high-quality songs on their own all feeling perfectly sequenced, and thereby made even better.

But Damn the Torpedoes is not a long album, so once you're three tracks down, there are only six to go, with “Don't Do Me Like That” and “Louisiana Rain” among those remaining songs. Tom Petty and the Heartbreakers had a knack for pumping out punchy rock music early on, so the two albums before Damn the Torpedoes and the one following it are also great, yet it’s Damn the Torpedoes that’s the most consistent and memorable of the bunch.

Make no mistake: The Rolling Stones had a great run throughout a good chunk of the 1960s, but there’s an argument to be made that it was their 1970s run that demonstrated the band at their strongest. Sticky Fingers is especially strong, and the one being singled out here, but it wouldn’t be wise to overlook the 1960s, and then ‘80s-wise, the band was still going fairly strong there, even if their music in that decade wasn’t quite as “classic rock” in sound.

Sticky Fingers is great, and most of it holds up incredibly well over half a century later.

Anyway, Sticky Fingers was the album that helped demonstrate The Rolling Stones could well be the new kings of rock, making a great start for the whole decade, right around a time when another legendary British band had broken up, leaving the future of rock temporarily uncertain, to some extent. More on that other band later, by the way. But yes, Sticky Fingers is great, and most of it holds up incredibly well over half a century on from its initial release.

Easily one of the best double albums of all time, Electric Ladyland is probably The Jimi Hendrix Experience’s greatest album, which is a big take, even without too many albums by the band. It comes down to all those albums being phenomenal and influential, and it’s just the case that Electric Ladyland, with its size and also all the wild things it’s going for – and successfully trying to cram into – a single album.

Electric Ladyland is still such a wonderful and strange album to dive into, even today, with it coming up on nearly six decades since it first came out (safe to assume it would've been even more mind-blowing to hear back in 1968). There’s an undeniably interesting atmosphere to the album overall, and it’s surprisingly consistent, all the while saving two of the best songs until the very end: a cover of Bob Dylan’s “All Along the Watchtower” and then “Voodoo Child (Slight Return).”

This spot could’ve easily gone to Who’s Next, which has the singles and perhaps more historical/cultural importance, so consider that an honorable mention for that possibly easier pick (the one that’s slightly more straightforward, not to mention potentially simpler to praise). But Quadrophenia is even better, and maybe ever so slightly underrated, being like Electric Ladyland in the way it sustains itself for twice as long as a normal album, but then it’s also more of a concept album.

Broadly, Quadrophenia is about struggling to fit in while growing up and dealing with intense isolation, and lots of what it tackles lyrically still packs a punch. The story might not be as comprehensible as was the case with The Who’s Tommy, but it doesn’t really matter when there’s so much to feel throughout Quadrophenia, since it’s very emotional, cathartic at times, and then also a bit intense at times thanks to how full-on the introspection gets at other points.

Bruce Springsteen had a remarkable run throughout the 1970s and 1980s, since it’s that period of time that saw the release of the majority of his all-time great albums. There are albums after that era that are still pretty great, but then again, it’s also hard for anything to be close to Born to Run in terms of quality. And even compared to the other Springsteen albums of those two decades, Born to Run is special.

It does a lot to condense everything great about Springsteen into just eight largely phenomenal tracks, and while they'd all be worth mentioning, it’s especially worth mentioning the title track (an all-timer), “Thunder Road,” and “Jungleland,” the former kicking off Born to Run expertly, and the latter being one essentially perfect closing track. Everything here works, and it’s 40-ish perfect minutes of classic rock, in the end.

It says something about how great Led Zeppelin were, as a band, that you could probably put an album of theirs without “Stairway to Heaven” in a ranking like this and it would still feel right. The first half-dozen albums are all very hard to fault, but among greatness, it’s probably Led Zeppelin IV that well and truly is the greatest, and sure, “Stairway to Heaven” helps, but the other songs here are also fantastic.

Technically, this album’s untitled, but it’s easier to call it Led Zeppelin IV after the band put out three self-titled albums in the couple of years before Led Zeppelin IV. There’s also a great variety of tracks here, with some not being particularly rock-heavy, but then other parts do indeed get very energetic and loud. The slower songs make for a nice contrast, and Led Zeppelin IV transitions from song to song incredibly well, with the pacing and overall flow being impeccable.

There are arguments to be made for several other albums by The Beatles that aren’t Abbey Road to be worthy of being called the best, but it’s Abbey Road that feels like a culmination of the band’s entire run. No shade to those other albums, though, since the band was remarkably consistent and boundary-pushing, especially through the last half of the 1960s (see also Sgt. Pepper’s Lonely Hearts Club Band, the self-titled or "White" Album, Revolver, Rubber Soul, and Magical Mystery Tour, if you want to count that one).

Abbey Road has some all-time great tracks on it, and the whole thing flows incredibly well, too, especially all those short songs in the second half that make up a medley. It’s got almost everything great about The Beatles packed into one album, and is also significant for being the final one the band recorded, even if Let It Be, largely recorded earlier, was the last album of the band’s to be released (and the only one that didn’t come out in the 1960s, since it dropped in 1970).

Pink Floyd is known for making albums that you can really get lost in, and The Dark Side of the Moon is especially dense and immersive in a mostly good way. “Mostly” because this album is pretty heavy-going at times, so maybe it’s not for every mood, yet it’s for a lot of them, and it is overall about life quite broadly, helping it be a little easier to get into than some of the band’s other great (and also ambitious) rock albums.

So, it’s great within Pink Floyd’s discography, and then when judged on its own, The Dark Side of the Moon is still phenomenal and everything. It’s just such a quintessential album – rock or otherwise – that it’s hard to know how to even praise it or convince someone who’s yet to listen to it that they really need to get on it. Also, it’s probably the kind of thing almost everyone’s heard at this point, so maybe it’s an odd recommendation for present purposes. Still, uh, maybe re-listen to it before you die? Yeah, there we go. That works.

If you want to find great David Bowie songs, you can go as far back as the 1960s, since “Space Oddity” came out then and is an obvious classic, but if you want essential albums of his, it’s best to start in the 1970s. He kept going beyond that decade, of course, with his final album (Blackstar) even being a masterpiece, but the most masterful of his masterpieces is probably The Rise and Fall of Ziggy Stardust and the Spiders from Mars.

And since no one has really ever done it better than Bowie, why not also call it the best classic rock album of all time? The Rise and Fall of Ziggy Stardust and the Spiders from Mars is just too good, with everything being perfect to the extent that it’s hard to know what else to say. Why bother saying anything when you can just listen? Thankfully, The Rise and Fall of Ziggy Stardust and the Spiders from Mars never really gets old, either.

I don't see a difference between trad IRA and Roth in terms of threat to disability benefits. Either the trust is making qualified distributions and paying 3rd parties or it isn't.
