US Army Moves To Restart Washington DC Helicopter Training Flights 2 Years After Deadly Crash

📅 Nov 25, 2026  |  Category: Other  |  Read Source →

The US Army is moving to resume helicopter training flights in airspace near Ronald Reagan Washington National Airport (DCA) as soon as the week of December 6, according to a recent report by Politico. That timeline comes less than two years after the January 29, 2025 midair collision between an Army Sikorsky UH-60 Black Hawk and a PSA Airlines regional jet operating as American Airlines Flight 5342 over the Potomac River, a disaster that killed all 67 people aboard both aircraft.

Training operations in the corridor were halted in May 2025 after an Army Black Hawk came too close to Reagan National and forced approaching commercial airliners to abort landings. Resuming training requires Federal Aviation Administration (FAA) approval, and the service had not yet filed a formal request with regulators even as military officials briefed congressional staff on their plans.

Existing Federal Aviation Administration regulations explicitly exclude routine training from essential flight categories in the restricted zone around Reagan National. Under interim final rule 2026-01226, which took effect on January 23, 2026, and permanently closed Helicopter Route 4 between Hains Point and the Wilson Bridge, only rare essential flights may enter designated airspace up to 1,500 feet (457 meters) mean sea level. Those narrow exceptions cover lifesaving medical missions, active law enforcement responses to ongoing crimes, national defense or counterintelligence operations, continuity of government, and transport of the President or Vice President.

The rule specifies that proficiency evaluations, routine training, and non-essential personnel transport do not qualify as essential operations. Because the regulation bars routine training inside the designated Potomac zone, military leaders would need either a formal regulatory modification or an approved authorization pathway before standard pilot currency missions resume. Federal Aviation Administration officials opened public comment on the interim rule through February 23, 2026, indicated a final rule would follow after reviewing submissions, eliminated visual separation within five nautical miles (9.3 km) of the airport, increased staffing and oversight, and required air traffic controllers to manage commercial runway use when essential vertical-lift aircraft enter restricted airspace.

Investigations by the National Transportation Safety Board and the Army traced the January 2025 collision to overlapping operational, technical, and airspace vulnerabilities rather than a single error. National Transportation Safety Board findings and reporting by Task & Purpose highlighted tight vertical margins near low-altitude river routes, radio frequency congestion, altimeter instrumentation discrepancies, and bright urban lighting that complicated visual detection at night.

Investigators also noted that radio transmissions that might have helped crews confirm visual separation were missed or complicated by overlapping traffic. The National Transportation Safety Board issued 50 safety recommendations, including eight directed at the Army, and concluded that an Automatic Dependent Surveillance-Broadcast system could have alerted the airliner crew 59 seconds before impact. In response, the Army conducted fleet inspections, tested altimeters across Black Hawk variants, and reviewed crew separation procedures. These technical and procedural reviews reflect a broader effort to pair airframe checks with stricter altitude discipline across congested metropolitan corridors.

It is a very dangerous place for both commercial and non-commercial aircraft alike.

Pushback from aviation labor unions and pending legislation on Capitol Hill complicate the Army's push for a December restart. Association of Flight Attendants president Sara Nelson condemned plans to bring military training back near Reagan National so soon after the fatal crash.

Meanwhile, lawmakers remain divided over final statutory mandates governing shared airspace and traffic-alerting equipment. According to The Washington Sun and Senate Commerce Committee reporting, the Rotor Operations Transparency and Oversight Reform Act passed the Senate unanimously in December 2025 but stalled in the House, though leaders announced an agreement in principle in mid-November 2026 on a framework to reconcile competing bills before the end of the year. Negotiations involving the House Armed Services Committee remain central to settling how military helicopters and fixed-wing planes share crowded corridors, while commuters and airlines watch whether flight delays or runway adjustments could return. While Senators Ted Cruz and Maria Cantwell push to finish the legislation, unresolved details over military aircraft equipment requirements continue to draw scrutiny from safety advocates and families of the victims.

Paulson Adebo gets shot at Giants redemption after up-and-down season

📅 Nov 25, 2026  |  Category: Other  |  Read Source →

Paulson Adebo looked out of place at practice last week as the NFL's highest-paid "don't-forget-about-me" trying to find his place on the punt team.

Two days later, Adebo improbably looked totally at home as a shutdown cornerback whose wild ride this season is about to accelerate further because he has risen from purgatory to again be considered one of the Giants' most important players.

"Obviously, it's not where I wanted to be going into the season," Adebo said after Tuesday's meetings. "You prepare every week like you're going to play. If you're the third [cornerback], you're one play away from going in every game, so you owe it to yourself and you owe it to your teammates to be prepared."

Adebo, whose $24.1 million salary cap charge is the highest on the Giants, lost his starting job to Deonte Banks and Greg Newsome II in training camp. He was picked on off the bench in Week 1, missed the next five games due to injury, returned to play well against the Texans as Banks' fill-in, was a healthy scratch against the Commanders and then saved the day with four passes defended and an interception after Newsome was injured against the Jaguars.

With Newsome now expected to miss four to six weeks due to ankle surgery, Adebo will start opposite Banks on Sunday against the Colts. The Giants will face three of the NFL's top six passing attacks in that six-game window.

New York Giants cornerback Paulson Adebo (21) runs off the field after an interception against the Jacksonville Jaguars in East Rutherford, N.J., Nov. 22, 2026. IMAGN IMAGES via Reuters Connect

"I know what type of player I am," Adebo said. "I know what I can do on the field. I know what type of person I am, so I don't think it's anything new necessarily that I've learned about myself, but certainly just staying prepared and ready to go when you're called upon."

Only 20 months after signing a three-year, $54 million contract as the prize of the Giants' 2025 free agent class, Adebo was inactive against the Commanders because backup cornerbacks Colton Hood and Nic Jones offered more special teams value. Adebo played 13 total special teams snaps from 2022-25 as he developed into a starting corner for the Saints and then the Giants' No. 1 option against elite receivers.

Head coach John Harbaugh pulled aside Adebo last week to keep him engaged with a message that turned out to be prescient.

New York Giants' Greg Newsome II (20) runs during training camp at the Greenbrier in White Sulphur Springs, W.Va., Saturday, Aug. 1, 2026. Corey Sipkin for the NY POST

"I said, 'In my mind, you're a starter in this league. And when you started here, you've done really well. And you're going to have to start again,' " Harbaugh said Nov. 18. "So when that time comes, he's in a good place."

The time came Sunday when Adebo allowed two catches on eight targets. His fourth-quarter interception at the 28-yard line secured three points (to extend to a 14-point lead) at a time when the offense couldn't move the ball.

"I mean, I'm glad that [Harbaugh] thinks that," Adebo said. "I certainly think highly of myself. So, kind of been thinking that way the whole season. So, it wasn't anything new per se."

The stretch run of the season suddenly becomes a proving ground for Adebo, who recently looked like a lock to be a salary cap casualty ($14 million saved against $6.3 million dead money) in 2027. Could the Giants feel comfortable with Adebo and Newsome as starters next year if Banks leaves in free agency?

"He's a baller," safety Jevón Holland said. "He hasn't been playing that much this year, but it's just how the game goes. When they called his name, he went out and balled. I'm excited for him."

People snickered when Newsome prematurely declared the Giants cornerback room the best in the NFL during the preseason.

It's become harder to argue with his assessment as time has passed. The performance against Trevor Lawrence was a definitive argument because Banks added an interception and Dru Phillips made three of the most important plays (all breakups) of the game.

"I think I said it in training camp about how we have three, four guys that are really starters in this league," Adebo said. "So, there really is that mindset that we all know we can compete. So, just keep on executing, keep on doing what we've been doing."

Wildfires Closed 189 Miles of the Pacific Crest Trail in 2026: What 2027 Hikers Need To Know

📅 Nov 24, 2026  |  Category: Other  |  Read Source →

According to the end-of-year wildfire recap from the Pacific Crest Trail Association (PCTA), seven fires closed 189 miles of the trail in 2026, burning directly across six miles of the trail itself.

California experienced a below-average fire year, while Oregon suffered a record-setting fire year. However, the fires in Oregon largely avoided the PCT corridor. In California, 329,000 acres burned, with the PCT seeing brief closures from the Corral and Talbot fires totaling 32.5 miles of trail.

In Oregon, over 2 million acres burned, but only 6.1 miles of trail were closed from the Badger Butte and Lemiti fires in Mount Hood National Forest.

The Badger Butte Fire in Oregon. Photo via PCTA

Washington, in contrast, experienced the brunt of this year’s closures, especially later into the summer. Trail crews are still working to evaluate the extent of the damage along the trail.

Statewide, over 650,000 acres burned, leading to 150 miles of closed trail from the Ptarmigan, Miners, Three Queens, King, and Sisi fires. Most importantly, the Ptarmigan Fire led to the closure of the northernmost 50 miles of trail, including the Northern Terminus.

Fire has occurred across landscapes along the PCT for thousands of years, often burning at low intensity every few years to a few decades. While low-intensity burns clear brush, replenish soil nutrients, and support new growth, burned trail corridors take years to recover.

Impacts from the older Dixie and Bear fires continue to drop dead timber and sprout heavy brush each year. Across 2025 and 2026, PCTA staff and volunteers cut 3,100 fallen logs in the Bear Fire scar alone, part of more than 23,000 downed trees cleared across the entire PCT since 2024.

Anyone mapping out a 2027 hike on the PCT should prepare for hazards that last well beyond the timeline of an active fire. Closure orders sometimes extend through winter and into the next season, so hikers need to check the PCTA closures page when making plans. Inside these scars, burned timber can topple without warning, especially from wind, making it unsafe to camp or take long breaks under dead trees.

Burned timber is prone to toppling. Photo via Katie Jackson

Winter weather can also reshape recent burn scars before summer arrives. Scorched slopes are unstable, making them much more prone to landslides. Heavy winter rains could still wash out additional sections before the start of the 2027 hiking season.

Hikers should expect downed trees, overgrown brush, and missing or rough tread. Lost forest canopy also brings more sun exposure and can change water sources, so carrying extra water and checking updated water reports will still be very important.

Even with the ill-timed, unfortunately placed closures, a whole group of 2026 hikers still put together rewarding seasons by staying flexible, taking detours, or planning return trips for the missing miles.

Bringing that same adaptability to the Pacific Crest Trail in 2027 will make navigating the post-fire terrain much easier.

VANCOUVER, BC, Nov. 24, 2026 /PRNewswire/ -- Forecasters expect a warmer-than-usual winter across much of North America thanks to a strong El NiÃ±o, but Ideal Siding, North America's largest siding renovation company, says that's no reason for homeowners to relax. A winter that hovers around freezing can be just as hard, if not harder, on siding than a steady deep freeze, because every thaw lets water into small gaps and every refreeze pushes them wider.

"People hear 'mild winter' and assume their house gets a break," said Alex Filipuk, CEO of Ideal Siding. "For siding, it's often the opposite. A winter that bounces between rain, snow and thaw gives water dozens of chances to get behind the panels and freeze."

Ideal Siding suggests walking the outside of the house after every major storm or thaw. Here's what to look for:

1.     The roofline: Icicles behind the gutters or ice building on the eaves are signs that meltwater may be backing up toward the walls.2.     Windows, doors and vents: Cracked or shrinking caulk and dark streaks beneath openings.3.     The siding itself:

4.     The bottom rows: Snow piled up and pressing against the siding. Keep it cleared away from the walls.5.     Inside: New cold spots, moisture on walls or unexpectedly high heating bills, which can signal hidden winter damage.

A mild stretch is also a good window for prep work to reseal or repaint aging wood siding, patch small gaps in the exterior, trim branches that could snap under ice or snow, and wash the outside so moisture doesn't stay trapped against it.

For homeowners weighing an upgrade, fiber cement and engineered wood siding continue to lead in cold climates thanks to their durability and moisture resistance. High-quality vinyl can also hold up well when it's installed with proper expansion clearances.

"A mild start to winter is a gift if you use it," added Filipuk. "That's extra time to get an inspection and fix small problems while the weather still cooperates. The harsher cold can still hit later in the season, and you want your siding sealed tight if it does."

This Classic Italian Sandwich Is Extra Crispy & Delicious

📅 Nov 24, 2026  |  Category: Other  |  Read Source →

Everybody celebrates Italy for pizza, pasta, and gelato, and while they're classics for a reason, we implore you to explore Italy's unrivaled gastronomic tapestry a little deeper. Trust us, there are some serious gems hidden just beneath the surface. One of our favorite Italian street foods that you need to try is porchetta, Italy's answer to crispy pork belly. Irresistibly crunchy and bursting with indulgent flavors, it makes for a seriously delicious sandwich filler.

Porchetta is a style of pork roast, but is completely unique in its preparation. It's typically made from a whole deboned pig or a large cut of pork belly, which is laid flat and heavily rubbed on the inside with salt, garlic, and black pepper, alongside quintessential Italian seasonings like rosemary, sage, and fennel seeds. The pork is then rolled back together and slow-roasted for several hours, allowing the interior to turn soft and tender while the skin develops a beautiful crackling.

Porchetta is a meal in itself, but goes even better in a sandwich. Start out with a thick Italian white bread, something that won't turn soggy when paired with the pork's rich meat juices and sauces — ciabatta or focaccia work perfectly here. To balance the richness, add something bright with a little zing. We think salsa verde is an excellent choice, since the lemon juice and vinegar bring a sharp acidity, while fresh parsley keeps the heavy roast from feeling cloying — it pairs perfectly with a Cuban interpretation of the recipe as well. Rather than piling on thick slabs of porchetta, slice it thinly, so it stays tender and folds nearly into the bread, and make sure every sandwich gets a few pieces of the crackling skin for a satisfying crunch. As for what else to put on the sandwich, draw inspiration from Bobby Flay's favorite Roman porchetta sandwich.

You should have no problem finding pre-rolled porchetta at the butcher, but why not save your money and make it at home instead? We promise it's not as intimidating as it looks. For the juiciest cut that's also easiest to roll and inexpensive, pick up a skin-on pork belly. Its exceptionally high fat content keeps the roast moist and makes it incredibly forgiving to work with, so even the least confident cooks won't have to worry about overcooking it.

Porchetta is the star of the sandwich, and it's the crackling skin that really steals the show, so getting it earth-shatteringly crispy is essential. Here, the secret to a crunchy crust is dryness, so before you get to work on seasoning, leave the pork uncovered in the fridge overnight to let the surface dry out. Many cooks also salt the meat generously before its rest, which draws out even more moisture. And before roasting, it's good practice to pat the skin completely dry, since any lingering moisture turns to steam and keeps the skin from crisping.

To keep the sandwich from turning into a soggy mess, it's essential to let your porchetta rest for around 15 to 30 minutes after roasting. It might be tempting to carve it right away and admire your hard work, but slicing too soon sends the flavorful juices flooding into the chopping board and then straight into the bread, which even the sturdiest ciabatta can't hold back. The logic is the same as with steak because resting gives the juices time to redistribute through the muscle, leaving the meat deliciously tender and flavorful.

How to Deal With Holiday Arguments and Guilt Trips

📅 Nov 24, 2026  |  Category: Other  |  Read Source →

Posted November 24, 2026 | Reviewed by Michelle Quirk

Ideally, the holidays are a time of happiness, cheer, and positive relationship interactions. In practice, however, we often need to manage some bad with the good. There are usually a few “bones to pick” over the Thanksgiving turkey and “elephants in the room” along with the Christmas tree…

So, to maximize your holiday joy (and minimize your angst), I have compiled some strategies to deal with dysfunctional games, family patterns, arguments, and emotional manipulation. By the end, you should have a good idea why Aunt Ruth keeps giving you a guilt trip, how Uncle Stu keeps making you lose your cool, and what exactly you can do about all of it. Then, maybe, you can enjoy yourself, too!

To start, according to Eric Berne in his book Games People Play (1964), people generally try to manipulate us because they want something from us—but they don’t believe they can get it honestly or fairly. These negative beliefs (called existential positions) come in two forms. On one hand, they can believe that you’re not OK and try to bully you because they think you don’t deserve their fair treatment. On the other hand, they can believe that they’re not OK (i.e., I’m not okay) and try to guilt trip you into feeling that you owe them something for nothing.

Thus, people play games, argue, and manipulate when they believe they are not OK, other people are not OK, or both. This allows them to get what they want without giving back or risking being fair and trusting. Such manipulations can have secondary benefits for them, too, like being excused from standards or responsibilities (I'm not OK), or feeling morally superior (You're not OK). Unfortunately, those bad beliefs and manipulative games also cause many of our hurt feelings and dysfunctional relationship patterns in the process as well.

How these game-playing relatives and friends push your emotional buttons was explored by Niven, Totterdell, and Holman (2009) and defined as negative affect regulation strategies. Put simply, they use emotional manipulation tactics to make you feel bad, so you give them what they want. There are four general tactics they can employ to emotionally manipulate, but they all share one common theme—they reject you and hurt your feelings, while also putting their feelings first.

Beyond that, they might choose to emotionally bully you by being disrespectful and insulting or bragging and trying to make you feel bad by comparison. This would fall under the “You’re not OK” belief and allow them to feel superior at your expense. In contrast, they might decide to guilt-trip you by criticizing how you treat them or withdrawing until you feel bad and comply with their wishes. This would be part of an “I’m not OK” belief and allow them to be excused from standards and responsibilities—like doing something worthwhile for you back. In either case, unless you know how to defend yourself, you might be left trying to choke down negative emotions, while they munch on dessert!

Fortunately, there are things that you can do to defend yourself—and possibly even improve your relationship with that unruly relative, partner, or friend, too. To begin, though, you need to decide whether you want to deal with the manipulation indirectly by diverting attention and emotion away from the problem, or directly by engaging attention and emotion toward addressing the problem. In other words, can you make light of Cousin Bessie’s snotty comments and change the subject, or do you need to talk it out and come to a better arrangement?

To help you decide, consider some indirect and direct softening techniques suggested by Overall, Simpson, and Struthers (2013). They were initially devised for addressing problems with emotionally avoidant or argumentative romantic partners—and serve as a foundation for dealing with relationship problems in my book Attraction Psychology (Nicholson, 2022). Beyond that, I have found them helpful with family and friends as well, especially when they believe one of us is not “OK” and are emotionally hurtful about it.

Dealing with issues indirectly requires that you “soften” by temporarily downplaying the severity of the problem, until their bullying or guilt-tripping lessens. To do so, it helps to build positive rapport by communicating your care, acceptance, and regard—while also minimizing their emotional manipulation attempts with positive feelings and humor (i.e., kill them with kindness). From there, you can redirect them toward other positive aspects about your relationship with them and points of agreement. Essentially, this is a lot like dealing with a toddler having a tantrum—make light of the outburst and find a more pleasant topic to change their mind and improve their behavior.

Dealing with issues directly, however, requires that you “soften” by acknowledging some aspect of their point of view as a base of agreement—without surrendering to the negative emotions from their bullying or guilt trip. Pretend you’re a cool TV lawyer who says, “We can agree that there is a problem here, but it would help us both to keep working on a better solution.” From there, you can show optimism about solving the disagreement and having a good relationship with them. Where you are able, also acknowledge some of their positive efforts in the past toward connecting and compromising with you. This can help improve their emotional outlook and motivate them to find healthier ways to interact with you in the future as well.

Whatever strategy you choose, I hope you have Happy Holidays and a Good New Year, too!

Ways you can help your video doorbell stay charged in the winter

📅 Nov 24, 2026  |  Category: Other  |  Read Source →

Winter can be tough on battery-powered video doorbells, but a few adjustments may help yours cope when temperatures drop.

While video doorbells aren't a necessity per se, it can be hard to imagine living without them once you own one. Knowing who's at the door saves you run-ins with unwanted salesmen. More seriously, they may help deter burglars — or help you catch an offender in the act if deterrence fails. Of course, your ability to capture that activity will depend in part on the distance at which your video doorbell stops detecting motion.

Another catch is that they're perpetually outdoors. That might not be such a big deal if you live somewhere warm like Miami or San Antonio. But in places with prolonged cold weather, battery life can suffer to the point that a video doorbell becomes more of a burden than an aid. It's inherent to the chemistry of lithium-ion technology, unfortunately.

The main way to alleviate this issue is by bringing your doorbell or its battery in from the cold. There may be other tactics you can employ, though, such as strategic positioning.

The critical point here is that an ideal charging range for lithium-ion batteries is between 50 and 86 degrees Fahrenheit (10 to 30 Celsius). When charging below the freezing point, there's a risk that metallic lithium will start plating onto electrodes, permanently reducing capacity. And even if the weather is slightly above freezing, charging will be less efficient than it could be. Cold-weather performance is one of the differences between sodium and lithium-ion batteries.

Before you charge a doorbell battery, make sure to let it warm up to room temperature first. This should not only preserve capacity but also help avoid cold-related charging delays. That's significant, considering that a Ring battery can take as long as 10 hours to top up. Let the battery charge fully before putting it back outside to maximize the time between recharges. If your doorbell has a removable battery, keeping a charged spare will minimize downtime. Some models in Ring's updated battery-powered doorbell lineup feature quick-release battery packs to make swaps easier.

Some doorbells don't offer removable batteries, however. One example is the battery-powered Google Nest Doorbell. In that case, you'll need to become familiar with removing your doorbell and bringing it indoors. Be sure to time this properly. Since a recharge might take several hours, it's probably wise to charge overnight unless you're worried about security and don't have any other cameras covering the front of your home.

In climates with borderline winters — that is, where the temperature only occasionally dips below freezing — you may be able to extend battery life by aiming or sheltering your doorbell in just the right way. If you can block the wind, for example, or maximize sunlight, your doorbell will naturally run warmer than it might otherwise.

A vital point here is that in extreme cold, it's possible for a doorbell to stop functioning for the duration. Anker, Ring, Google and others all agree on a minimum operating temperature of -4 degrees Fahrenheit (-20 Celsius). Between that and the freezing point, devices may still refuse to charge in order to prevent the plating we mentioned earlier.

Honestly, then, the best choice if you live in a frostbitten region may be to switch to a wired doorbell, as long as you can find one compatible with your home's design. While wired doorbells may still encounter problems around the -4 degrees Fahrenheit mark, they're otherwise immune to the cold, since they draw on the same electricity as the rest of your house.

Dr. Edith Eva Eger, sometimes called the female Viktor Frankl, died this year at age 98. At age 16, Edith and her family were deported to Auschwitz. She and her mother stood in line before the Angel of Death, Dr. Josef Mengele. Mengele directed the mother to go toward the gas chamber, and pointed Edith in a different direction to live. As Edith watched the smoke rise from the gas chamber, she knew her separation from her mother was permanent. That night, Mengele forced Edith, a gymnast and ballerina, to dance for him. In the depths of her numbness and grief, she willed herself to dance, then kindly shared the bread that Mengele disdainfully tossed her with other prisoners.

In facing this experience and other childhood adversities, Edith showed remarkable courage and resilience. Her mother was critical, being stuck in grief and anger for losing her mother as a child. Edith felt like the invisible sister, only gaining a sense of worth when she was able to rescue her parents from their loneliness and pain. She was cruelly shamed for being Jewish and teased for her awkwardness and crossed eyes. She coped with her sense of shame by becoming a perfectionist in gymnastics and dance to gain approval.

In overcoming her pain, she learned wisdom that she shared well into her 90s as a practicing psychologist. For example, she taught of the peace of having a clear conscience, relating that Mengele would always have to live with what he’d done; he was more of a prisoner than she was.

Edith taught that one survives adversity by committing to a cause outside of self and focusing on what one still has. In the concentration camp, she participated in mock beauty pageants and fantasized about a delightful future and a loving marriage. Although the circumstances were depressing, she realized that she had a purpose and that only she could do certain things in the way that she could.

Rather than becoming embittered, she clung to her faith, reasoning that it wasn’t God who ran the death camps, but hateful people. Even among the Germans, she found people who were secretly kind. She experienced, and chose to believe, that imperfect people are redeemable. She even forgave her German captors, whom she believed were themselves prisoners.

Understandably, Edith suffered long after her liberation. Yet she came to believe that suffering can reveal inner strengths and grow the confidence that one can endure intense adversity. She chose to believe in hope, love, and her ability to find joy in nature and little children.

She also encouraged people to tell their story in safe and appropriate ways, to release pain’s grip. She courageously accepted an invitation to return to Germany, where Hitler had once stood, and returned to Auschwitz to come to peace with her haunting memories.

Edith showed us how to overcome crushing adversity by reclaiming one’s sense of worth and purpose. In her healing journey, she gained hard-earned wisdom that she generously shared with others, still high-kicking into her 90s.

Her life reminds us that suffering crosses everyone’s path. That suffering can be a stumbling block or a stepping stone to greater resilience and life satisfaction (Schiraldi, 2021; 2017). We thank her for her inspiring example. Rest in peace, Edith Eva Eger.

T-Wolves' Donte DiVincenzo cleared for basketball activity, 7 months after rupturing his Achilles

📅 Nov 24, 2026  |  Category: Other  |  Read Source →

Donte DiVincenzo is one step closer to returning to play, seven months after rupturing his right Achilles tendon.

The Minnesota Timberwolves announced on Tuesday that the 29-year-old guard has been cleared for team basketball activities. DiVincenzo suffered the injury during Game 4 of the opening round of the Western Conference playoffs in April.

According to The Athletic's Jon Krawczynski, DiVincenzo is aiming to return to play in January. No official timeline has been announced.

DiVincenzo tore his Achilles on April 25 and underwent surgery one day later. A return nine months later would beat that of Jayson Tatum of the Boston Celtics (298 days).

"I'm going to push, push, push," DiVincenzo said in October. "That was the biggest thing for me. They've had to tell me let's wait another week or two versus, 'c'mon buddy, let's get going.'"

DiVincenzo played in all 82 regular-season games in 2025-26 and averaged 12.2 points, 4.1 rebounds, 3.8 assists and 1.3 steals per game.
