Marvel Announces Vision's New Codename After Historic Transformation

📅 Nov 20, 2026  |  Category: Other  |  Read Source →

Marvel's Vision has a new codename, as Marvel announces an upcoming transformation into an even more powerful robotic form. Here's the new codename, and what it means for the fate of everyone's favorite synthezoid.

Debuting in 1968's Avengers #57, Vision was named for a classic Marvel character - the Golden Age hero known as Aarkus, an alien who fought alongside Captain America in WWII. In Marvel canon, it was coined by Janet van Dyne's Wasp (much like the 'Avengers' name itself) on seeing the mysterious figure for the first time, when Vision was sent to attack the Avengers.

Vision has undergone numerous transformations since then, from taking on his emotionless white form to being semi-recreated as the Young Avengers hero Jonas. Now, Vision undergoes his darkest transformation yet and a new name change.

Marvel recently revealed new details for the upcoming Hulk War: Infernal Rage #1, officially revealing the names of each of the 'Infernal Avengers' commanded by the titular villain. These monsters are all heroes corrupted by the Infernal Hulk's magic and possessed by the souls of ancient monsters.

The press release confirmed what fans had guessed - that the robotic being by Hulk's side is a transformed Vision. Patterned after a biblically-accurate angel, this new Vision is a floating metallic skull with a single eye and four wings, a gigantic, uncovered brain emerging from the top.

Marvel's new images name the being "The Diaboli Ex Machina," meaning "the devil from the machine." The reference is to an ancient Greek theatrical convention where a supernatural being would emerge to conclude the story, often appearing by a literal mechanism. "Diaboli Ex Machina" therefore speaks to a devil literally emerging from a mechanism, and also a supernatural being appearing suddenly to defeat Marvel's heroes.

Vision hasn't yet taken this form in Marvel Comics, though fans did glimpse it in an Amazing Spider-Man 1000/Queen in Black CGD 2026 #1 flash forward to the upcoming events of Hulk War.

The transformation is likely to happen in December 2's Infernal Hulk vs. Avengers #1, which will see Captain America, Iron Man, Captain Marvel, Vision and Scarlet Witch take on the Infernal Hulk, aka the demonic Eldest.

Because everyone but Wanda has a confirmed monster form in Hulk War - and due to their past romantic and familial bond - fans have speculated that The Diaboli Ex Machina is a combination of Scarlet Witch and Vision. This would go some way to explaining the religious iconography of Visions' new design, but others characters have undergone radical transformations without this kind of explanation.

It's more likely that the angelic elements of VIsion's transformation are connected to the demon soul implanted in him by the Infernal Hulk. It may even be linked to the android sent to defeat the Infernal Hulk by the One-Above-All (image above), which was destroyed but could be repurposed in transforming the Vision.

Marvel has been building to 2027's Hulk War since 2025, when the demonic Eldest defeated Bruce Banner and stole his gamma powers. Now, after resurrecting an army of monsters and corrupting heroes including Spider-Man, the Fantastic Four and iconic X-Men including Jubilee, the Hulk War is nearly upon us.

Before that point, the Avengers will embark on one last, desperate mission to stop the Infernal Hulk in his tracks, leading to the creation of the Iron Thing, the Ebon Sphinx, the War Dead and Vision's horrific Diaboli Ex Machina.

Hulk War: Infernal Rage #1 is coming February 3 from Marvel Comics.

Florida Set To Execute 71-Year-Old Man Who Claims He Was Framed For Killing Pregnant Woman, Husband

📅 Nov 19, 2026  |  Category: Other  |  Read Source →

Florida is gearing up for a record-breaking 20th execution this year ... but the man set to be put to death Thursday evening has spent decades claiming he was framed for the brutal murders of a pregnant woman and her husband.

Thomas Mitchell Overton, 71, is scheduled to be executed by lethal injection right now at Florida State Prison ... more than 35 years after Michael and Susan "Missy" MacIvor were found murdered inside their Florida Keys home.

And here's the twist ... Overton has insisted for years that he didn't commit the murders, claiming police planted the DNA evidence used to convict him. His attorneys have also raised some questions about how that evidence was handled.

The case dates back to August 22, 1991, when Michael, 30, and Susan, 29, were found dead inside their home in Tavernier, Florida. She was eight months pregnant at the time.

According to court documents, Michael had been hit in the head and strangled, with masking tape covering his face. Susan was found bound with tape and strangled in the couple's bedroom, and investigators determined she had also been sexually assaulted. The couple's unborn baby also died.

Investigators say they discovered the home's telephone wires had been cut and a ladder was positioned against the balcony.

The murders went unsolved for years ... until investigators connected Overton to the crime through DNA evidence.

According to court records, investigators recovered semen from the couple's bedding and developed a DNA profile in 1993, but didn't have a suspect to match it to.

That changed in 1996, when investigators arrested Overton in an unrelated burglary case. Although he initially refused to provide a blood sample, investigators eventually obtained a court order to collect his blood for testing. Two separate laboratories then reported DNA results linking him to the semen found at the crime scene.

But Overton has always insisted he was innocent.

During his original sentencing proceedings, he told the judge, "I didn't commit the crime." But he didn't present evidence that could have helped him avoid the death penalty because he said he believed his conviction would eventually be overturned on appeal and didn't want to put his family and friends through the ordeal.

In his appeal to the Florida Supreme Court, Overton's attorneys argued that more DNA testing could potentially clear his name or identify someone else involved in the murders.

Could Earth have been collecting dark matter for billions of years?

📅 Nov 19, 2026  |  Category: Other  |  Read Source →

Heat measurements from dead stars helped physicists narrow down how dark matter might interact with ordinary matter.

Physicists have spent decades building dark matter detectors deep underground, and they still haven’t caught a single particle.

A new study suggests that Earth itself, along with the Sun, Jupiter and a handful of dead stars, might trap certain kinds of dark matter better than any lab instrument.

These bodies could have been collecting slow-moving particles for billions of years.

The researchers used neutrino data and heat measurements to rule out some combinations of particle mass and interaction strength that laboratory experiments couldn’t probe.

The work was led by Alejandro Ibarra from the Technical University of Munich, Merlin Reichard from the Korea Institute for Advanced Study and Gaurav Tomar from the Sogang University in Seoul.

About 85% of the matter in the universe is dark matter. We know it’s there because galaxies spin faster than their visible matter can explain and light bends more than it should as it passes them.

Beyond that, very little is known about it. Physicists are stuck in a frustrating loop.

You can’t identify something you can’t see, and you can’t design a way to see it without some idea of what it is. So they make a guess, build an experiment to test it, and so far the answer has always been no.

One guess is that dark matter isn’t completely dark. Its particles might respond to electricity and magnetism very faintly, carrying a tiny fraction of an electron’s charge or acting like microscopic magnets.

The paper describes five such properties. One of them, the “anapole moment,” is a doughnut-shaped kind of magnetism.

Any of the five would let a dark matter particle occasionally bump into an atom.

Underground detectors wait for those bumps. A slow particle taps an atom so gently that the instrument doesn’t notice.

In practice, these detectors are more sensitive to faster dark matter particles.

Planets and stars work the other way around. A fast particle shoots straight through them. A slow one only needs to lose a little speed in a collision before gravity grabs it and won’t let go.

Over billions of years, trapped particles would sink toward the center and crowd together. Once packed tightly enough, they could meet and annihilate, turning into other particles.

Some of those would be neutrinos, which pass through rock almost as if it weren’t there. The rest would deposit their energy as heat.

“In this work, we considered four celestial bodies: Earth, the Sun, Jupiter and white dwarfs,” Tomar said.

“There are dedicated experiments, for example IceCube at the South Pole, looking for neutrinos from the Sun and Earth. For Jupiter and white dwarfs, we considered the heating possibilities.”

For the Sun and Earth, the team used limits from IceCube, a detector built into the Antarctic ice, and from its smaller companion detector, DeepCore.

That included ten years of IceCube data on neutrinos coming from Earth’s core. There was no excess.

Neither Jupiter nor the white dwarfs generate heat through nuclear fusion.

The Cassini spacecraft measured how much heat Jupiter leaks from its interior.

The white dwarfs are burnt-out stellar cores in M4, a cluster of ancient stars. Each one holds about 1.4 times the Sun’s mass in a ball with a radius of roughly 1,250 kilometers.

If a candidate type of dark matter would make these objects give off more heat than they actually do, it can be crossed off.

Earth turned out to be the best test for dark matter with a tiny electric charge. For particles heavier than about ten protons, it set the strongest limits yet.

There are two reasons. First, a charged particle’s pull reaches a long way, so it is easily drawn in. Second, Earth’s core is full of iron, whose atoms each carry 26 protons, which makes collisions more likely.

The white dwarfs set leading limits on two of the more exotic properties, the charge radius and the anapole moment, across a wide range of particle masses.

For very heavy particles, they also beat earlier limits on dark matter’s magnetic dipole moment.

The anapole limits matter for one popular candidate whose only possible electromagnetic property is the anapole moment.

For that candidate, the white dwarfs reach places that particle colliders and underground detectors struggle to get to.

Jupiter was a letdown. In general, it didn’t improve on limits that already existed. None of the four bodies beat earlier limits on the electric dipole moment either.

Underground labs sit beneath a kilometer or more of rock to block cosmic rays. If dark matter interacts strongly with ordinary matter, that same rock could slow it down or stop it before it reaches the detector.

The shield could be hiding the very particles the experiment is looking for.

Planets and stars don’t have this problem. The more strongly dark matter interacts, the better they catch it.

There is one important caveat. The more dark matter surrounds a star, the more of it the star can capture, and nobody has measured how much dark matter M4 contains.

The team had to rely on estimates from models.

“We do not know the dark matter density there. We used estimated values, so if those values change, our limits could become weaker or stronger,” Tomar noted.

Tomar suggests two places to look next. One is even colder white dwarfs in regions with more dark matter, where any extra heat would be easier to detect.

The other is beyond the Solar System, where exoplanets could serve as dark matter traps too.

The study is published in the Journal of Cosmology and Astroparticle Physics.

How to switch camera views in GTA 6

📅 Nov 19, 2026  |  Category: Other  |  Read Source →

The game only offers a first-person perspective in very limited circumstances.

Grand Theft Auto 6 gives players lots of ways to customize the experience, from the clothes the protagonists wear to granular user interface controls. Being able to shift your perspective of the action is part of that. Rockstar has long offered GTA players the ability to reposition the camera, and you can do that this time around as well.

In the camera tab of the settings menu, you can adjust the height of the camera while you're on foot, as well as the horizontal and vertical follow speeds. You have similar options for land and water vehicles, along with specific follow speeds based on whether you're the driver or a passenger. When it comes to aircraft, you can adjust the follow speed and determine whether the camera follows your plane when you do a barrel roll.

When you're out there exploring the map in a land or water vehicle, you can change the height of the camera on the fly. To switch between the three options, hold the down button on the D-pad, pick the height you want and confirm. You'll also be able to switch to the cinematic camera mode here. This makes things like high-speed chases look cool, but you might find it harder to steer your vehicle.

In addition, there are some very limited ways in which you can switch to a first-person perspective. While you're on foot, you can hold the left trigger on your controller and click the right thumbstick. This lets you take a closer look at your surroundings.

Going back to the camera section of the settings menu, you can enable a first-person perspective when your character is looking at their phone. If you look at your phone while you're driving (in GTA 6, never in real life), you'll see your device and the road from a first-person view as well. Perhaps Rockstar will add a proper first-person mode to GTA 6 down the line, as it did with the previous game.
