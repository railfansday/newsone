Dopamine-rich reward pathways contribute to desire and anticipation (Fisher et al., 2010); the brain's natural opioid system helps make closeness comforting and social rejection distressing (Hsu et al., 2013; Nummenmaa et al., 2016); and limbic regions, particularly the amygdala, help connect emotional experiences with memory (Cahill et al., 1996). The truth can change what you think in seconds, but it cannot instantly erase what your brain has learned over months or years, so do not let people tell you that you are "co-dependent" or "weak." You have a normal brain that might play tricks on you.

You have leftover love, and that leftover love can be weaponised against you (Lesiak & Gelsthorpe, 2025). He may use the feelings that remain to persuade you to let him stay, asking you to ignore what you have discovered, silence your judgment, and compromise the very values that define who you are.

He has already betrayed you. Now he is asking you to betray yourself for him.

You are the one expected to swallow your anger, silence your judgment, and call it "forgiveness." Look at the bargain. He gets to keep the woman who loves him. And you are expected to give up the woman who knows the truth.

Decades ago, psychiatrist Judith Herman wrote about it in Trauma and Recovery (1992):

His ultimate goal appears to be the creation of a willing victim. Hostages, political prisoners, battered women, and slaves have all remarked upon the captor’s curious psychological dependence upon his victim. George Orwell gives voice to the totalitarian mind in the novel 1984:

“We are not content with negative obedience, nor even with the most abject submission. When finally you surrender to us, it must be of your own free will. We do not destroy the heretic because he resists us; so long as he resists us we never destroy him. We convert him, we capture his inner mind, we reshape him. We burn all evil and all illusion out of him; we bring him over to our side, not in appearance, but genuinely, heart and soul.”

Read those last words again. Heart and soul.

It is not enough that you stay. You must want to stay. It is not enough that you forgive him. You must learn to see his betrayal as something you can live with. You must look at the evidence of your own eyes and persuade yourself that it no longer matters.

And if keeping him means abandoning everything you believe in, what exactly are you trying to save—and what's left—when you do that?

Some wounds are easier to bear because you know you stood by your values while receiving them. In Kraków, officers Jakub Kulik and Artur Morański were seriously wounded confronting a mass shooter. Poland calls them heroes, and people across the country are grateful for their courage and service.

Then there is another kind of wound. One that no surgeon can stitch. Amy Winehouse sings it in "You Know I'm No Good": “I cheated myself, like I knew I would.” By the end of the song, she is no longer simply confessing what she did. She is passing judgment on herself.

Imagine waking up beside a man you no longer respect, living a life you never wanted, having persuaded yourself to accept things you once knew were unacceptable. You kept him, perhaps. But somewhere along the way, you lost the ability to trust your own judgment.

That is the deeper cruelty of weaponised love (Lesiak & Gelsthorpe, 2025). He does not need you to believe he is innocent. He needs you to stop caring that he isn't.

And if manipulation or coercion brought you to that point, the shame belongs to the person who engineered that surrender, not to you.

Name the man, not the fantasy. The betrayal wasn't simply another woman. It was the months of lies that made you believe you were building a future together while he was living another life. He needed you to trust him because, had you known the truth, you might have walked away. So he made sure you didn't.

That is what a programme of deception does. It robs you of the freedom to make informed choices about your own life. When used to dominate and subordinate, deception becomes a tool of coercive control and psychological abuse. Stop reaching for words that make him sound better. A liar is a liar. A traitor is a traitor. A backstabber is a backstabber. Grieve the man he pretended to be, the image he created to win your love and use it to control you. But don't defend the man behind the mask.

The eggs went into the bin that morning. The relationship ended. The feelings are disappearing. You'll be ok! Probably you are already more ok now than when you were cheated on, lied to, and abused.

For many iPhone users, Apple CarPlay has become synonymous with a quality driving experience, from playing their favorite tunes to navigating to their next destination. And although Apple continues to upgrade its popular infotainment system, one of its most useful features has been hidden in Apple Maps for years and is critical to letting your family, friends, and, if you are like me, enemies know when you will arrive at your destination.

I am, of course, referring to sharing your estimated time of arrival (ETA) through Apple Maps. Most users are probably familiar with Apple's Find My and location-sharing functions, but CarPlay's Share ETA feature is woefully underappreciated. Both simple to use and highly customizable, the feature is a godsend for worried parents and surprise parties everywhere.

To enable Share ETA, go to Settings > Apps > Maps. From here, scroll down to Share ETA and enable the feature. You can also enable location sharing in Messages, Contacts and Find My. You may also want to turn on Siri to make sharing an ETA more convenient. To do so, go to your iPhone's Settings, tap Siri and enable Turn on Siri.

Sharing your ETA is a simple way to improve your CarPlay experience. If you're using Siri, you can initiate a voice command when you're following Maps directions, either by holding the voice command button on your steering wheel or saying "Hey Siri" if your car supports voice activation. Next, say "share my ETA with (contact name)."

If you would rather not use Apple's voice assistant, you can share your ETA directly in Maps. After you've set off, tap the route card at the bottom of the screen, then Share ETA and choose a recipient. If the contact isn't on the list, you can find them under Open Contacts. To share with more than one person, tap Sharing with at the bottom of the screen, Open Contacts and select your intended recipients. You can also stop sharing your ETA by opening the route card again, tapping Sharing with, selecting Stop Sharing ETA and choosing the contact you want to stop sharing with.

You can also automatically share your ETA with someone by adding their contact to a pinned location. First, go to Maps > Places > Pinned. Next, tap the blue information button > Add Person. Next, tap the desired contact and confirm the selection. Now, whenever you select the pinned location in Apple Maps and tap Go, it will automatically share your ETA.

Grammy Awards: All the Artists Who’ve Been Nominated in the Big 4 Categories in the Same Year

📅 Nov 16, 2026  |  Category: Other  |  Read Source →

Remarkably, 13 of these 17 artists are women.

When the nominations for the 69th annual Grammy Awards were announced on Monday (Nov. 16), Ella Langley became the 17th artist to receive nominations in each of the so-called “Big Four” Grammy categories – album, record and song of the year plus best new artist – in the same year. Remarkably, 13 of those 17 artists are women. How to account for that? Grammy voters seem to like to give extra encouragement to talented young women artists.

Langley is the first country artist to be nominated in each of the Big Four categories since Bobbie Gentry in 1968, who was the first artist from any genre to achieve the feat.

Three times in recent years, two artists achieved the feat in the same year. Billie Eilish and Lizzo both achieved the feat in 2020. Olivia Rodrigo and FINNEAS both achieved it in 2022. Chappell Roan and Sabrina Carpenter both achieved it in 2025.

This has happened more often in recent years because the number of nominees in each of the Big Four categories has expanded. Until the Grammy ceremony held in 2019, there were generally five nominees in each of those categories. The upcoming 69th annual Grammy Awards, set for Sunday (Feb. 7), will mark the ninth year in a row there have been eight or more nominees in each of those categories. There were 10 at the ceremonies in 2022 and 2023, but the Recording Academy (mercifully) brought it back down to eight in 2024.

All but one of the 16 previous artists to receive nods in each of the Big Four categories had a spotlight performance slot on the Grammy telecast. The exception was FINNEAS, and even he was featured in sister Eilish’s performance of “Happier Than Ever” on the 2022 telecast.

Here’s a complete list of artists who have been nominated in each of the top four Grammy categories in the same year. The years shown are the years of the Grammy ceremonies.

Gentry, who was 25 at the time, was nominated for her debut album Ode to Billie Joe and the intriguing story song that was its title track. She won best new artist and in two other categories – best vocal performance, female and best contemporary female solo vocal performance. Gentry hasn’t won another Grammy since that night, though she was nominated for her 1970 album Fancy.

Cross, then 29, was nominated for his eponymous debut album and his hypnotic ballad “Sailing.” He won in all four categories and for best arrangement accompanying vocalist(s). Cross received three more nominations the following year for “Arthur’s Theme (Best That You Can Do),” though he hasn’t added to his trophy collection since his big night.

Lauper, then 31, was nominated for her first solo album, She’s So Unusual — and, in a unique occurrence, two different works for record and song of the year, respectively. The zesty “Girls Just Want to Have Fun” was nominated for record; the poignant “Time After Time,” which Lauper co-wrote with Rob Hyman, was nominated for song. She won best new artist. She won a second Grammy 29 years later – best musical theater album for Kinky Boots.

Chapman, then 24, was nominated for her eponymous debut album and her surprise hit, “Fast Car.” Chapman, the first Black artist to be nominated in each of the Big Four categories in one year, won best new artist and in two other categories – best pop vocal performance, female and best contemporary folk recording. She won a fourth Grammy eight years later for “Give Me One Reason.”

Carey, then 20, was nominated for her eponymous debut album and her sleek ballad “Vision of Love,” which she co-wrote with Ben Margulies. She won best new artist and best pop vocal performance, female. She has since won three additional Grammys.

Cole, then 29, was nominated for her debut album, This Fire, and her hit “Where Have All the Cowboys Gone?” She won best new artist. She had seven nominations that night, including producer of the year, non-classical, but hasn’t even been nominated since that night.

The singer, then 26, was nominated for her debut album, Acoustic Soul, and her single, “Video.” She co-wrote the song with Carlos “Six July” Broady, Reginald Harris and Shannon Sanders. She went 0-7 that year (though she came back to win two Grammys the following year and two more in the years since then).

Winehouse, then 24, was nominated for her second (and final) studio album Back to Black and her retro-sounding hit “Rehab.” Winehouse, the first English artist to be nominated in each of the Big Four categories in the same year, won for record and song of the year, best new artist and in two other categories – best pop vocal album and best female pop vocal performance. Winehouse won a sixth Grammy four years later for “Body and Soul,” a duet with Tony Bennett. Winehouse died in 2011 at age 27.

Meanwhile, Georgetown was reliving John Thompson's golden era, hitting every shot they launched at the rim. Basketball is a game of runs, and the Hoyas found themselves in the midst of an epic heater. When Clemson finally cleared their head and started playing basketball again, the score was 45-27 with 1:49 remaining in the half before Clemson hit a little run before halftime, giving the team the faintest whiff of momentum and making the score 47-32 heading into the break.

The Tigers flipped the switch in the second half, thanks to Cole Certa and Ace Buckner's scoring and a more consistent defensive and rebounding effort. With 13:18 remaining, the Tigers cut Georgetown's 15-point halftime lead to 4 with a David Faulkner dunk, turning a game that looked like a lost cause in the first half into something worth watching. Unfortunately for Clemson, every time it looked like they were taking control, Georgetown's veteran backcourt of Elmarko Jackson and Jaland Lowe had an answer. When Clemson took a 62-61 lead on a Cole Certa 3 with 8 minutes remaining, the Hoyas went down to the other end and matched it with a Jaland Lowe 3 to retake the lead. When a Cole Certa layup at the 6:23 mark put the Tigers up by 2, Elmarko Jackson responded with a mid-range jumper to tie it at 66-66 with 5:49 remaining. Jackson then proceeded to take the game on his shoulders for the Hoyas. He scored 8 of their next 10 points while Clemson's hot shooting cooled off. Jackson's layup with 3:00 minutes remaining, at which point he handed the scoring baton off to Jaland Lowe, who scored 9 of Georgetown's final 11 points. A Chase Thompson 3 with 26 seconds got the Tigers to 5 points at 81-76, but the Hoyas managed to handle the Clemson press (with the help of a couple of questionable calls from the refs), finishing the game off with a Vince Iwucjukwu dunk as time expired.

Georgetown was led in scoring by Jaland Lowe, who scored 21 points, including a 3-for-6 from beyond the arc. Vince Iwuchukwu contributed a 20-point, 10-rebound double-double while towering over the Clemson front court, and Elmarko Jackson added 17 points, many of which came at the exact right moment for Ed Cooley's crew.

Clemson was led in scoring by Ace Buckner with 20 points, with Cole Certa adding an additional 14 points. Liutauras Lelevicius was the only other Tiger in double digits with 10.
