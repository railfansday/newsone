Moore eyes Kane shirt as Spurs' past and future stars meet

📅 Nov 20, 2026  |  Category: Sports  |  Read Source →

Moore is Tottenham's youngest-ever Premier League player, having made his debut for the club's senior side at just 16 years old.

Cologne loanee Mikey Moore says he hopes to exchange shirts with former Tottenham team-mate Harry Kane when they face Bayern Munich in the Bundesliga on Friday.

The 19-year-old England Under-21 international is on a season-long loan from Spurs, while Three Lions captain and Ballon d'Or winner Kane left north London to join Bundesliga giants Bayern in 2023.

"I haven't actually spoken to him. When he was leaving Tottenham I was just coming into the first team so we don't really know each other too well," said Moore.

"But we've got Bayern on Friday so I'm looking forward to hopefully getting a chat with him and maybe his top."

The 19:30 GMT kick-off will be shown live on BBC Sport and iPlayer.

Kane was still at Tottenham when Moore was first invited to train with the senior squad during the 2021-22 season.

"He's been a massive idol of mine since I was really young," Moore told the Euro Leagues podcast last month.

"When I first went up to train with the first team under Antonio Conte, Harry Kane was over there, and [I saw] some finishing that [I have] never seen anything like again.

"It will be a great experience to play against him and share a pitch with him."

The pair may not have crossed paths in Germany yet but Moore did bump into the England skipper while on international duty last month.

"I'll try my best to get my hands on that shirt," Moore added. "I saw him on the last international break, actually, and went and said hello.

"Hopefully, I'll be coming back to Koln with that in my hand."

How much German has Mikey Moore learned so far?

Moore says the success of Jadon Sancho and Jude Bellingham in the Bundesliga influenced his decision to join Cologne on loan.

Moore's move came almost a decade after a 17-year-old Sancho left Manchester City to join Borussia Dortmund.

During his time in Germany, Sancho established himself as one of Europe's most exciting wingers, making more than 150 appearances for Dortmund and helping the club win the DFB-Pokal in 2021. He subsequently secured a Â£73m move to Manchester United.

Bellingham followed a similar path in 2020, leaving boyhood club Birmingham City to join Dortmund. The midfielder quickly developed into one of the most sought-after players in world football before joining Real Madrid for an initial Â£88.5m in 2023.

"Over the years you've seen so many young players come out here, and there are good examples of world-class players like Jude Bellingham and Jadon Sancho doing really well," Moore said.

"It's a really attractive place to play football, especially for a young attacker. It's a great platform to play, and that was sold to me by the people at the club and the manager.

"They've put real trust in me, and the amount of game time I've played so quickly in my Koln [Cologne] career has been nice. It's been good to have that trust from all the staff and people at the club.

"I'm already happy with my decision. Now I can really kick on, make my own story and build my own path in German football."

Although Moore only turned 19 in August, he is no stranger to stepping outside his comfort zone.

Last season, he made 47 appearances in all competitions, including the Champions League qualifying rounds, during a loan spell at Scottish Premiership side Rangers.

It was the first time Moore had left his boyhood club Tottenham, and he rose to the challenge, scoring six goals and providing two assists in 28 Scottish Premiership appearances.

The experience offered a different test to the one he now faces in Germany, but it provided valuable exposure to the demands of first-team football.

"I had options to stay in England and maybe stay somewhere a little more comfortable," Moore said.

"But the conversations came down to me picking a team, and I wanted to take myself a little bit out of my comfort zone to test myself in one of the top five leagues.

"When we were speaking, everyone said they thought it would be a really good experience for me to experience this type of football.

"It's really intense, and there are some great teams in the league.

"For me, it was a no-brainer. I made the decision early that I wanted to come out here, and now I just need to push on."

Having now found an apartment close to fellow England youth internationals Reigan Heskey and Jahmai Simpson-Pusey, who also joined the club this summer, the teenager has turned his attention to learning German.

Moore had his first lesson last month and, at his mother's request, is hoping to expand his vocabulary in the language over the coming weeks.

"I wasn't the brightest at school, so I might take a while to learn the language," Moore joked.

"At least I'll be able to learn a few words so I can order my food in German."

Worse Than 2008? Peter Schiff Issues Terrifying Market Crash Warning

📅 2026-11-20  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Noted economist and longtime Bitcoin critic Peter Schiff has sounded the alarm about an upcoming financial apocalypse, warning that the crisis could be worse than the historic 2008 market crash.

On Nov. 20, Peter Schiff shared a series of troubling X posts warning about the potential for significant losses in the U.S. stock market, real estate, and cryptocurrencies amid escalating financial system stress.

The economist shared a video with a very alarming headline: "The Next Crash Will Be Worse Than 2008."

In one of his most recent posts on X, Schiff argued that the recent gold price downtrend might signal a grim outlook for other key asset classes.

"It's probably a bad sign that gold is falling this much. It probably means that the stock and real estate markets are going to fall even more in percentage terms," Schiff cautioned.

According to Peter Schiff, gold's recent price behavior could be signaling deflation. As a result, this might lead to a more severe financial crisis.

However, Peter Schiff is convinced that the Federal Reserve will step in to prevent this financial apocalypse.

"Gold is signaling deflation. But asset deflation would bust the government and the banking system, so the Fed will pivot to avoid it," the economist explained.

Simply put, Schiff assumes that the Federal Reserve will pivot and introduce monetary stimulus to prevent a financial meltdown.

In another startling prediction, the economist warned that the U.S. stock market might wipe out all its gains since President Donald Trump took office.

"There's a good chance that the U.S. stock market is about to give back all the gains since Trump took office," the economist predicted.

He also warned that there might be a recession. As a result, Trump will likely point to Democrats and their wins in the recent midterm elections as the reason for this recession.

Interestingly enough, Peter Schiff's bearish outlook also applies to Bitcoin (BTC), which has already suffered significant losses amid the overall market crash.

On Nov. 18, Peter Schiff shared several alarming market indicators. According to his data, the Nasdaq has dropped 12.25%. AI infrastructure company CoreWeave has plummeted 64%.

Bitcoin, on the other hand, has plunged 33.5% from its 2026 peak.

Additionally, the economist noted that the U.S. housing market index had dropped to 25, its lowest level since 2012. This shows that the economic issues go well beyond AI stocks.

"Still think this is just about AI?" he asked.

UnitedHealth Group has sued the federal government over its lowered Medicare Advantage star ratings for next year, further plunging the program’s ratings system into disarray.

UnitedHealth marks at least the 15th Medicare Advantage plan to file a lawsuit against the Centers for Medicare and Medicaid Services for how the agency calculated 2027 star ratings. The government rates Medicare Advantage plans on a scale from one to five stars, based on health screens, customer satisfaction, and other measures that get mixed into a complicated formula. The insurers argue in their lawsuits that the government’s measurement is inconsistent and is inappropriately dropping their scores below the bonus thresholds.
