It had been, for a time, one of our favourite bits of heavily caveated nonsense which straddled this and last season. It was, with one of the final kicks of an invigorating, liberating, and most importantly first of the millennium Leeds Premier League win at Stamford Bridge, obliterated in the most fitting way.

From a last-gasp Sean Longstaff equaliser against Bournemouth in April to the stoppage-time strike which crowned a 4-1 thrashing of Chelsea, Leeds scored 16 Premier League goals in as many matches.

Aaronson had played 534 minutes across those 16 games, yet somehow managed to avoid actually being on the pitch for any of those goals.

It was a run which included a couple of stints as an unused substitute, as well as full appearances or cameos in Daniel Farke’s attacking football vacuum, such as the 3-0 thrashing by West Ham in May.

But contained within it were some stunning near-misses which Aaronson must have struggled not to take entirely personally: Anton Stach’s winner against Nottingham Forest on the opening weekend, six minutes after the American came off; all four goals against Newcastle coming before his introduction, as with all three against Burnley last campaign.

The goal which kicked it all off was even laced with the sense of a specific vendetta against Aaronson: Longstaff replaced him in the 84th minute of that Bournemouth draw before snatching a point.

It was the sort of apparent individual curse which confirmed a certain bias within the Leeds fanbase. The sense that Aaronson benefits from Farke’s favouritism is not new – the Chelsea game marked his 100th appearance under the manager, a mark only six others have reached during his tenure – and the American’s presence in the team has touched a Red Bull nerve since he signed four years ago.

Aaronson, for many, is the stepchild Leeds ended up in custody of after the messy Jesse Marsch divorce.

Farke himself has taken aim at his “challenges in his offensive game” before, lamenting his “polarising” and “hectic” nature. But that usually comes as part of a ham-fisted defence of a player he feels faces “more than unfair” criticism from the supporters.

The Chelsea game was a case in point: Leeds had a commanding and deserved 3-1 lead, yet the reaction to Aaronson being the first substitute, especially in place of goalscoring hero Noah Okafor, was defined by mild frustration.

Those bristling at the sight of Aaronson receiving his tactical instructions on the touchline might ordinarily have had a point, but against this opponent in that situation, he was the best player Farke could have called on.

Set free on the counter by Harry Wilson, Aaronson made Malo Gusto and Levi Colwill look foolish before sliding an unduly composed finish through the keeper’s legs, having netted in the nine-goal Carabao Cup thriller at this stadium in September.

Adding Emi Martinez to his absurdly targeted list of Chelsea-based former The Best FIFA Men’s Goalkeeper award-winning victims alongside Edouard Mendy was a fine riposte – and end to a statistic we’ll always cherish.

WooshPay's AI payment assistant understands the request, matches available cards and guides users through card selection and SMS verification before executing payment and returning results in real time. "WooshPay produced a working demonstration less than a month after we signed, which reflects our shared goal of bringing AI into real payment scenarios. We look forward to testing the security and efficiency of AI payments in cross-border use cases with WooshPay during the pilot."

WooshPay plans to open the pilot to merchants and AI platforms in Q1 2027.

Asking Mom and Dad for Down Payment Help This Thanksgiving? Here’s How Much To Ask For

📅 Nov 23, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Thanksgiving. A time for turkey, football, and the gathering of family.

It can be a stressful day for many, from the demands of traveling to the obligations of hosting at home. But hopefully, for most, it’s a day to be spent being thankful for having the loved ones gathered around the table.

And what better time to ask those loved ones for a bit of help to achieve the American dream than on a quintessential American holiday?

Jokes aside, if it’s on your holiday agenda to hit up Mom, Dad, Grandma, Grandpa, or even cool Aunt Janet for some money to buy a home next year, there’s a few things you should know before making the trip.

Like how much you’re actually going to need for that dream home.

The reality is, young people really do need help with down payments these days—and many are getting it.

In the past year, 25% of first-time homebuyers used gifts or loans from family and friends to help fund their down payment, according to the National Association of Realtors® 2026 Profile of Home Buyers and Sellers.

Furthermore, whether they’re going it alone or with help, all buyers are strapped for the cash for down payments as overall payments are on the decline.

The typical down payment share dropped for four straight quarters, softening to 12.8% in the first quarter of 2026, down 1.1 percentage points from a year earlier, according to the most recent Realtor.com® down payment report.

In dollar terms, down payments sank 19% year over year to their lowest point since the third quarter of 2021.

Before sitting down with your parents to talk numbers, it’s important to know just how much money you’ll actually need.

For context, the typical U.S. down payment in April 2026 reached $25,000, or an average of 13.2% of the purchase price, down from $27,500 and 13.8% in April 2025.

But calculating the right amount you’ll need starts with being honest about how much money you already have—and where you plan to buy.

"Every buyer’s situation and every market is different, so there’s no single number," Realtor.com senior economist Hannah Jones says. Still, there are benchmarks to keep in mind.

"The most useful targets are the 10% and 20% thresholds, and the right ask depends on how far a buyer is from the next one. In lower-cost metros, help reaching 10% down could be just a few thousand dollars rather than tens of thousands. In pricey California metros, the typical down payment is well into six figures, which is very hard to save without a windfall (see the AI boom) or parental help.”

Framing the request around those specific benchmarks should make it easier to explain to your parents why a certain dollar amount matters. Hitting each tier unlocks tangible savings on the loan itself, reducing ongoing housing costs long after closing day.

"Getting to 10% down can pay off significantly in the form of a lower mortgage rate, and getting to 20% eliminates the need for private mortgage insurance," Jones explains.

Still, asking for help is tricky, and if you’re concerned your parents will be hesitant, remember that any help they can offer will go a long way.

“A smaller loan still lowers the monthly payment. More is better if the goal is minimizing the payment, but reaching one of these thresholds is a great place to start the conversation."

Finally, if there is some hesitation from your family about offering a helping hand due to the market conditions and mortgage rates, there’s some hope on the horizon that should sway them.

"Nationally, the market has shifted in a more buyer-friendly direction," Jones says. But location truly makes all the difference.

"Supply is still catching up in many Northeast and Midwest metros, so sellers hold more of the cards there and buyers should put their best foot forward. In the South and parts of the West, buyers are in the driver’s seat.”

Elon Musk spends about an hour a week on his $16 billion foundation. Most of its 2025 giving went to another Musk nonprofit

📅 Nov 23, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Elon Musk’s foundation gave away nearly $400 million in 2025, and more than three-quarters of it went to another nonprofit he founded, according to the Musk Foundation’s latest tax filing, first reported by Bloomberg.

That total is down from the record $474 million the foundation gave in 2024. Over the same year, its assets grew by more than $1 billion, to about $16 billion, helped by a $64 million gift of Tesla shares. That makes it one of the world’s largest private foundations.

The gap between what the foundation holds and what it gives matters because private foundations must generally pay out about 5% of their investment assets each year or face IRS excise taxes. The Musk Foundation has fallen short of that minimum before, Forbes reported after reviewing its 2024 filing. Foundations can make up a shortfall the following year, so one year’s giving doesn’t settle whether the rule was met.

The foundation runs with almost no staff. Its tax return lists three directors: Musk, his family-office head Jared Birchall, and Matilda Simon. Musk, who is also listed as president, reported working for the foundation about an hour a week, Bloomberg reported.

More than $310 million went to The Foundation, a nonprofit Musk set up to run a STEM-focused primary and secondary school in Bastrop, Texas, outside Austin, with plans to eventually add a university.

Another $20 million went to a donor-advised fund at Fidelity Charitable over which Musk has advisory control. Donors to these funds can take a tax deduction when they contribute and later recommend grants to charities. Once the money is in, the sponsoring organization legally controls it, while the donor keeps advisory privileges, according to the IRS.

That structure has drawn scrutiny before. In 2022, Fortune reported philanthropy experts’ concerns that Musk’s giving could lower his tax bill without quickly moving money to working charities.

Other gifts went to Memphis-area organizations, where xAI, the AI company Musk founded and SpaceX acquired in February, runs the Colossus data centers it is racing to expand. Slingshot Memphis, a nonprofit that uses data, research, and evaluations to measure the effectiveness of poverty-fighting programs, received $5.9 million. Regional One Health Foundation received $9.4 million for patient care.

Musk signed the Giving Pledge in 2012, committing to give away the majority of his wealth during his lifetime or in his will. Bloomberg reported that he gave roughly $1.4 billion from 2021 through 2024, even as his net worth grew by hundreds of billions of dollars.

Forbes estimates Musk’s net worth at about $812 billion as of Nov. 21. The foundation’s 2025 giving equals about 0.05% of that, though most of his wealth is tied up in SpaceX and Tesla stock rather than cash.

That number swings hard. Musk became the world’s first trillionaire in June after SpaceX went public, and his fortune has crossed back and forth over that line since.

Musk has argued that giving well is harder than it looks. “It is very difficult to give money away for the reality of goodness,” he has said.

The 7 Most Common Mistakes Home Cooks Make When Cooking Mashed Potatoes

📅 Nov 23, 2026  |  Category: Food  |  Read Source →

The simplest Thanksgiving side deserves better.

In a traditional Thanksgiving meal, mashed potatoes are a seemingly simple side dish that does a lot of heavy lifting. They're the perfect vessel for a puddle of gravy, and they’re a creamy complement to everything else on your plate—yes, even your aunt's questionable green bean casserole.

Mashed potatoes prove you can transform even the humblest ingredients into something magical. And yet, we've all somehow still eaten bad ones. With a recipe this basic, even the smallest mistakes have nowhere to hide.

Thankfully, those mistakes are 100% avoidable. We rounded up the most common ways to mess up mashed potatoes so that yours turn out as delicious as possible this holiday season.

It doesn’t matter if you cut your potatoes into a fine dice or rough chunks. Whatever size you pick, stick with it. Making sure your potato pieces are roughly the same size will help them all cook at the same rate. If your knife work is haphazard, you may end up with some pieces that are perfectly cooked, others that are waterlogged and mushy, and some that are still firm in the center.

Like pasta, potatoes need a good amount of seasoning to really shine—which is why salting the cooking water is so important. You can be generous with the salt, since much of it stays in the water and ends up going down the drain. When you start seasoning early in the cooking process, it’s easier to build flavor and adjust as you go.

In the hustle of cooking all of your Thanksgiving dishes, you may be tempted to boil your potatoes ahead of time and mash them later in the day. But it’s always better to work with potatoes while they’re still hot and easier to mash. Waiting until they’re cool will likely require far more elbow grease—and you’ll still end up with uneven chunks.

A smooth, creamy mash is the height of decadence. To make sure the potatoes are lump-free, some people may reach for a blender—but this is one of the most consequential mistakes you can make. Potatoes are naturally starchy, and the more you work them, the more starch gets released from their cell walls. Breaking out the blender may be well-intentioned, but it can leave you with potatoes that are gummy and gluey. Instead, opt for the right tool, like a hand masher, food mill, or potato ricer.

Another important thing to consider is that potato starches continue absorbing moisture as mashed potatoes sit. They can soak up the milk you’ve added, leaving your potatoes dry and dense. Don’t be afraid to add more liquid—or at least keep some extra on hand to loosen up your mash before serving.

Anthony Bourdain famously pointed to generous amounts of salt and butter as part of what makes restaurant food taste so good. That’s especially true for mashed potatoes. You may be scared to add more than a light sprinkle of salt, but don’t forget to taste and adjust the seasoning before serving. A little extra salt can make all the difference for your Thanksgiving dinner.

Whether you’re feeding your immediate family or a small army, you should always make a big batch of mashed potatoes. This side is one of the most popular components of a Thanksgiving menu, so it never hurts to make extra. It’s also one of the most versatile dishes for repurposing leftovers. You can transform them into gnocchi, shepherd’s pie, or crispy fried croquettes. It may require more mashing in the moment, but your future self will thank you.

Have there been any new developments regarding Everbank? For example, have they addressed any of the issues that Volstagg identified above? Have they made any other changes (good or bad)?
Nothing has really changed, other than they did increase the ACH limits a bit. It's now up to $10,000 per day / $30,000 per month. I think they got hit last year with some or the Check kiting / ACH fraud, like other banks were having and reacted by restricting pretty hard. They are owned by Private Equity, so that's kind of your answer on what is most important to their senior management. Also, EB bought another bank (WaFd / Washington Federal) recently.

https://www.everbank.com/about/news/09- ... gic-merger

Things are set to close and merge sometime next year, and it's not clear what that actually means for existing customers of EB or WaFd.

There are some good things and some not so good things (see my above messages). After playing around with them for a few months, I only ended up keeping 1 savings account open. I keep a chunk of my Emergency Fund there, about 4 months of expenses as an alternate to Alliant (where I continue to do my primary day-to-day banking) and Fidelity (4 week TBills on AutoRoll for short term cash). Their interest rate is reasonably good (currently 4.2% for new customers, ~3.7% for existing customers, which is better then Alliant but worse then 4 week TBills are currently paying) and they were quick to adjust their rate (1-2 days later), when the Fed did their recent 0.25% increase (unlike Alliant).

Would I use them for my day-to-day bank, no. Are they fine for a HYSA where you plan to park cash and pretty much ignore it and let it earn interest, I think so.
Why would you use them at all for HYSA given that you already have 4 week t-bills at Fidelity and the t-bills pay more plus are not subject to state tax?
